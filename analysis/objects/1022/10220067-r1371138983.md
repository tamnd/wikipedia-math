# Crystalline cohomology

## o1 subject: Crystalline cohomology

In mathematics, crystalline cohomology is a Weil cohomology theory for schemes $X$ over a base field $k$. Its values $H^n(X/W)$ are modules over the ring $W$ of Witt vectors over $k$. It was introduced by Alexander Grothendieck and developed by Pierre Berthelot.

## Applications
For schemes in characteristic $p$, crystalline cohomology theory can handle questions about $p$-torsion in cohomology groups better than $p$-adic étale cohomology. This makes it a natural backdrop for much of the work on $p$-adic $L$-functions.
Crystalline cohomology, from the point of view of number theory, fills a gap in $\ell$-adic cohomology, which occurs exactly where there are "equal characteristic primes". Traditionally the preserve of ramification theory, crystalline cohomology converts this situation into Dieudonné module theory, giving an important handle on arithmetic problems. Conjectures with wide scope on making this into formal statements were enunciated by Jean-Marc Fontaine, the resolution of which is called $p$-adic Hodge theory.
