## Proof
In the Jacobson density theorem, the right $R$-module $U$ is simultaneously viewed as a left $D$-module where $D = \operatorname{End}(U_{R})$, in the natural way: $g \bullet u = g(u)$. It can be verified that this is indeed a left module structure on $U$. As noted before, Schur's lemma proves $D$ is a division ring if $U$ is simple, and so $U$ is a vector space over $D$.
The proof also relies on the following theorem proven in p. 185:
Theorem. Let $U$ be a simple right $R$-module, $D = \operatorname{End}(U_{R})$, and $X \subset U$ a finite set. Write $I = \operatorname{ann}_{R}(X)$ for the annihilator of $X$ in $R$. Let $u$ be in $U$ with $u \bullet I = 0$. Then $u$ is in $XD$; the $D$-span of $X$.
### Proof of the Jacobson density theorem
We use induction on $|X|$. If $X$ is empty, then the theorem is vacuously true and the base case for induction is verified.
Assume $X$ is non-empty, let $x$ be an element of $X$ and write $Y = X\,\backslash\{x\}.$ If $A$ is any $D$-linear transformation on $U$, by the induction hypothesis there exists $s \in R$ such that $A(y) = y \bullet s$ for all $y$ in $Y$. Write $I = \operatorname{ann}_{R}(Y)$. Now $x \bullet I$ is a submodule of $U$. If $x \bullet I = 0$, then the previous theorem implies that $x$ would be in the $D$-span of $Y$, contradicting the $D$-linear independence of $X$, therefore $x \bullet I \ne 0$. Since $U$ is simple, we have: $x \bullet I = U$. Since $A(x) - x \bullet s \in U = x \bullet I$, there exists $i$ in $I$ such that $x \bullet i = A(x) - x \bullet s$.
Define $r = s + i$ and observe that for all $y$ in $Y$ we have:
$\begin{aligned}
y \cdot r &= y \cdot(s + i) \\
&= y \cdot s + y \cdot i \\
&= y \cdot s && (\text{since } i\in \text{ann}_R(Y)) \\
&= A(y)
\end{aligned}$
Now we do the same calculation for $x$:
$\begin{aligned}
x \cdot r &= x \cdot (s + i) \\
&= x \cdot s + x \cdot i \\
&= x \cdot s + \left ( A(x) - x \cdot s \right )\\
&= A(x)
\end{aligned}$
Therefore, $A(z) = z \bullet r$ for all $z$ in $X$, as desired. This completes the inductive step of the proof. It follows now from mathematical induction that the theorem is true for finite sets $X$ of any size.
