# Rigid transformation

## o1 subject: Rigid transformation

In mathematics, a rigid transformation (also called Euclidean transformation or Euclidean isometry) is a geometric transformation of a Euclidean space that preserves the Euclidean distance between every pair of points.

## o8 statement: Distance formula

## Distance formula
A measure of distance between points, or metric, is needed in order to confirm that a transformation is rigid. The Euclidean distance formula for $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ is the generalization of the Pythagorean theorem. The formula gives the distance squared between two points $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ as the sum of the squares of the distances along the coordinate axes, that is
$$d\left(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}\right)^2 = \left(X_1 - Y_1\right)^2 + \left(X_2 - Y_2\right)^2 + \dots + \left(X_n - Y_n\right)^2 = \left(\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{Y}\right) \cdot \left(\mathbf{X} - \mathbf{Y}\right).$$
where $\mathbf{X} = (X_{1}, X_{2}, ..., X_{n})$ and $\mathbf{Y} = (Y_{1}, Y_{2}, ..., Y_{n})$, and the dot denotes the scalar product.
Using this distance formula, a rigid transformation $g : \mathbf{R}^{n} \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ has the property,
$$d(g(\mathbf{X}), g(\mathbf{Y}))^2 = d(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y})^2.$$
