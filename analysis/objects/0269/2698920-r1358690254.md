# Raychaudhuri equation

## o1 subject: Raychaudhuri equation

In general relativity, the Raychaudhuri equation, or Landau–Raychaudhuri equation, is a fundamental result describing the motion of nearby bits of matter.

## o8 statement/theorem: Focusing theorem

## Focusing theorem
Suppose the strong energy condition holds in some region of our spacetime, and let $\vec{X}$ be a timelike geodesic unit vector field with vanishing vorticity, or equivalently, which is hypersurface orthogonal. For example, this situation can arise in studying the world lines of the dust particles in cosmological models which are exact dust solutions of the Einstein field equation (provided that these world lines are not twisting about one another, in which case the congruence would have nonzero vorticity).
Then Raychaudhuri's equation becomes
$\dot{\theta} = - \frac{\theta^2}{D-1} - 2 \sigma^2 - {E[\vec{X}]^a}_a$
Now the right hand side is always negative or zero, so the expansion scalar never increases in time.
Since the last two terms are non-negative, we have
$\dot{\theta} \leq - \frac{\theta^2}{D-1}$
Integrating this inequality with respect to proper time $\tau$ gives
$\frac{1}{\theta} \geq \frac{1}{\theta_0} + \frac{\tau}{D-1}$
If the initial value $\theta_0$ of the expansion scalar is negative, this means that our geodesics must converge in a caustic ($\theta$ goes to minus infinity) within a proper time of at most $(D-1)/|\theta_0|$ after the measurement of the initial value $\theta_0$ of the expansion scalar. This need not signal an encounter with a curvature singularity, but it does signal a breakdown in our mathematical description of the motion of the dust.

## o11 application: Applications

### Applications
The event horizon is defined as the boundary of the causal past of null infinity. Such boundaries are generated by null geodesics. The affine parameter goes to infinity as we approach null infinity, and no caustics form until then. So, the expansion of the event horizon has to be nonnegative. As the expansion gives the rate of change of the logarithm of the area density, this means the event horizon area can never go down, at least classically, assuming the null energy condition.
