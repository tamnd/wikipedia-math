# Casas-Alvero conjecture

## o1 subject: Casas-Alvero conjecture

In mathematics, the Casas-Alvero conjecture is a problem about polynomials which have factors in common with their derivatives, proposed by Eduardo Casas-Alvero in 2001.

## o2 section: Formal statement

## o3 statement: Formal statement

## Formal statement
Let f be a polynomial of degree d defined over a field K of characteristic zero. If f has a factor in common with each of its derivatives f (i), i = 1, ..., d − 1, then the conjecture predicts that f must be a power of a linear polynomial.

## o6 statement/generalization: Special cases

## Special cases
The conjecture is known to hold in characteristic zero for degrees of the form pk or 2pk where p is prime and k is a positive integer. Similarly, it is known for degrees of the form 3pk where p ≠ 2, for degrees of the form 4pk where
p ≠ 3, 5, 7, and for degrees of the form 5pk where p ≠ 2, 3, 7, 11, 131, 193, 599, 3541, 8009. Similar results are available for degrees of the form 6pk and 7pk. It is later established for d = 12, making d = 20 the smallest open degree.
