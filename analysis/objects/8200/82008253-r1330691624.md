# Berk-Jones test

## o1 subject: Berk-Jones test

The Berk-Jones test (or BJ test) refers to a class of non-parametric goodness-of-fit statistical tests used to determine whether a set of observed data follow a specific probability distribution. Introduced by Robert H. Berk and Douglas H. Jones in 1979, the test is specifically designed to be more powerful than the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test in the tails of the distribution.

| Test Method | Sensitivity Focus | Best Use Case
| Kolmogorov–Smirnov | Median / Center | General deviations in the bulk of the data.
| Anderson–Darling | Weighted Tails | Moderate tail sensitivity; common in engineering.
| Berk–Jones | Extreme Tails | Sparse signals; likelihood-ratio based optimality.
| Higher Criticism | Extreme Tails | Sparse signals; $Z$-score based optimality.

## Applications
The Berk-Jones test is widely used in:
- Genomics: for identifying differentially expressed genes where only a small fraction of genes are expected to show a signal.
- Signal detection: for detecting weak, sparse signals against a background of noise.
- Meta-analysis: for combining p-values from multiple independent studies to detect a global effect.
