# Polynomial SOS

## o1 subject: Polynomial SOS

In mathematics, a form (i.e. a homogeneous polynomial) h(x) of degree 2m in the real n-dimensional vector x is sum of squares of forms (SOS) if and only if there exist forms $g_1(x),\ldots,g_k(x)$ of degree m such that
$$h(x) = \sum_{i=1}^k g_i(x)^2 .$$

## o12 example: Examples

### Examples
- Consider $m = 2$ and the form $h(x)=x_1^4-x_1^2x_2^2+x_2^4$ of degree 4 in two variables. We have
$$x^{\{m\}} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1^2\\x_1x_2\\x_2^2\end{pmatrix}\!,
~H+L(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix}
1&0&-\alpha_1\\0&-1+2\alpha_1&0\\-\alpha_1&0&1
\end{pmatrix}\!.$$
Since there exists α such that $H+L(\alpha)\ge 0$, namely $\alpha=1$, it follows that h(x) is SOS.
- Consider $m = 2$ and the form $h(x)=2x_1^4-5x_1^3x_2/2+x_1^2x_2x_3-2x_1x_3^3+5x_2^4+x_3^4$ of degree 4 in three variables. We have
$$x^{\{m\}}=\begin{pmatrix}x_1^2\\x_1x_2\\x_1x_3\\x_2^2\\x_2x_3\\x_3^2\end{pmatrix},
~H+L(\alpha) = \begin{pmatrix}
2&-5/4&0&-\alpha_1&-\alpha_2&-\alpha_3\\
-5/4&2\alpha_1&1/2+\alpha_2&0&-\alpha_4&-\alpha_5\\
0&1/2+\alpha_2&2\alpha_3&\alpha_4&\alpha_5&-1\\
-\alpha_1&0&\alpha_4&5&0&-\alpha_6\\
-\alpha_2&-\alpha_4&\alpha_5&0&2\alpha_6&0\\
-\alpha_3&-\alpha_5&-1&-\alpha_6&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.$$
Since $H+L(\alpha)\ge 0$ for $\alpha=(1.18,-0.43,0.73,1.13,-0.37,0.57)$, it follows that $h(x)$ is SOS.
