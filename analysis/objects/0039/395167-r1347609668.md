# Froude number

## o1 subject: Froude number

In continuum mechanics, the Froude number ($\mathbf{Fr}$, after William Froude, ) is a dimensionless number defined as the ratio of the flow inertia to the external force field (the latter in many applications simply due to gravity). The Froude number is based on the speed–length ratio which he defined as:
$$\mathrm{Fr} = \frac{u}{\sqrt{g L}}$$
where $u$ is the local flow velocity (in m/s), $g$ is the local gravity field (in m/s2), and $L$ is a characteristic length (in m).

## o21 pointer-item: Critical flow

### Shallow water waves
For shallow water waves, such as tsunamis and hydraulic jumps, the characteristic velocity $U$ is the average flow velocity, averaged over the cross-section perpendicular to the flow direction. The wave velocity, termed celerity $c$, is equal to the square root of gravitational acceleration $g$, times cross-sectional area $A$, divided by free-surface width $B$:
$$c = \sqrt{g \frac{A}{B}},$$
so the Froude number in shallow water is:
$$\mathrm{Fr} = \frac{U}{\sqrt{g \dfrac{A}{B}}}.$$
For rectangular cross-sections with uniform depth $d$, the Froude number can be simplified to:
$$\mathrm{Fr} = \frac{U}{\sqrt{gd}}.$$
For $\operatorname{Fr} < 1$ the flow is called a subcritical flow, further for $\operatorname{Fr} > 1$ the flow is characterised as supercritical flow. When $\operatorname{Fr} \approx 1$ the flow is denoted as critical flow.
