# Graph of groups

## o1 subject: Graph of groups

In geometric group theory, a graph of groups is an object consisting of a collection of groups indexed by the vertices and edges of a graph, together with a family of monomorphisms of the edge groups into the vertex groups.
There is a unique group, called the fundamental group, canonically associated to each finite connected graph of groups. It admits an orientation-preserving action on a tree: the original graph of groups can be recovered from the quotient graph and the stabilizer subgroups. This theory, commonly referred to as Bass–Serre theory, is due to the work of Hyman Bass and Jean-Pierre Serre.

## o2 section: Definition

## o3 definition: Definition

## Definition
A graph of groups over a graph $Y$ is an assignment to each vertex $x$ of $Y$ of a group $G_{x}$ and to each edge $y$ of $Y$ of a group $G_{y}$ as well as monomorphisms $\varphi_{y,0}$ and $\varphi_{y,1}$ mapping $G_{y}$ into the groups assigned to the vertices at its ends.

## o8 statement/theorem: Structure theorem

## Structure theorem
Let $\Gamma$ be the fundamental group corresponding to the spanning tree $T$. For every vertex $x$ and edge $y$, $G_{x}$ and $G_{y}$ can be identified with their images in $\Gamma$. It is possible to define a graph with vertices and edges the disjoint union of all coset spaces $\Gamma/G_{x}$ and $\Gamma/G_{y}$ respectively. This graph is a tree, called the universal covering tree, on which $\Gamma$ acts. It admits the graph $Y$ as fundamental domain. The graph of groups given by the stabilizer subgroups on the fundamental domain corresponds to the original graph of groups.

## o9 section: Examples

## o10 example: Examples

## Examples
- A graph of groups on a graph with one edge and two vertices corresponds to a free product with amalgamation.
- A graph of groups on a single vertex with a loop corresponds to an HNN extension.
