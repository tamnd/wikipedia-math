# Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture

## o1 subject: Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture

In mathematics, Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture is a conjecture made by that the pair correlation between pairs of zeros of the Riemann zeta function (normalized to have unit average spacing) is

## o2 citation

## o3 section: Conjecture

## o4 statement/conjecture: Conjecture

## Conjecture
Under the assumption that the Riemann hypothesis is true.
Let $\alpha\leq \beta$ be fixed, then the conjecture states
$\begin{aligned}
\lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{\#\{(\gamma,\gamma') :0 < \gamma,\gamma' \leq T \text{ and }2\pi\alpha/\log(T)\leq \gamma -\gamma' \leq 2\pi\beta/\log(T)\}}{\frac{T}{2\pi}\log{T}}
\\
= \int\limits_\alpha^\beta 1-\left(\frac{\sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}\right)^2 \mathrm{d}u + \delta(\alpha, \beta),
\end{aligned}$
where each $\gamma, \gamma'$ is the imaginary part of the non-trivial zeros of Riemann zeta function, that is $\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma$, and $\delta(\alpha, \beta) = 1$ if $0 \in [\alpha, \beta]$ and $\delta(\alpha, \beta) = 0$ otherwise.

## o13 statement/conjecture: F(α) conjecture or strong pair correlation conjecture

## F(α) conjecture or strong pair correlation conjecture
Let again $\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma'$ stand for non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Montgomery introduced the function
$F(\alpha):=F_T(\alpha)=\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\log(T)\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}T^{i\alpha(\gamma-\gamma')}w(\gamma-\gamma')$
for $T>2,\;\alpha\in \mathbb{R}$ and some weight function $w(u):=\tfrac{4}{(4+u^2)}$.
Montgomery and Goldston proved under the Riemann hypothesis, that for $|\alpha|\leq 1$ this function converges uniformly
$F(\alpha)=T^{-2|\alpha|}\log (T)(1+\mathcal{o}(1))+|\alpha|+\mathcal{o}(1),\quad T\to \infty.$
Montgomery conjectured, which is now known as the F(α) conjecture or strong pair correlation conjecture, that for $|\alpha|> 1$ we have uniform convergence
$F(\alpha)=1+\mathcal{o}(1),\quad T\to \infty$
for $\alpha$ in a bounded interval.
