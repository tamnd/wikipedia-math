# Equidiagonal quadrilateral

## o1 subject: Equidiagonal quadrilateral

In Euclidean geometry, an equidiagonal quadrilateral is a convex quadrilateral whose two diagonals have equal length. Equidiagonal quadrilaterals were important in ancient Indian mathematics, where quadrilaterals were classified first according to whether they were equidiagonal and then into more specialized types.

## Special cases
Examples of equidiagonal quadrilaterals include the isosceles trapezoids, rectangles and squares.
Among all quadrilaterals, the shape that has the greatest ratio of its perimeter to its diameter is an equidiagonal kite with angles π/3, 5π/12, 5π/6, and 5π/12.

## Characterizations
A convex quadrilateral is equidiagonal if and only if its Varignon parallelogram, the parallelogram formed by the midpoints of its sides, is a rhombus. An equivalent condition is that the bimedians of the quadrilateral (the diagonals of the Varignon parallelogram) are perpendicular.
A convex quadrilateral with diagonal lengths $p$ and $q$ and bimedian lengths $m$ and $n$ is equidiagonal if and only if
$pq=m^2+n^2.$
