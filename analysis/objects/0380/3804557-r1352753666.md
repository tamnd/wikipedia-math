# Inverse hyperbolic functions

## o1 subject: Inverse hyperbolic functions

In mathematics, the inverse hyperbolic functions are inverses of the hyperbolic functions, analogous to the inverse circular functions. There are six in common use: inverse hyperbolic sine, inverse hyperbolic cosine, inverse hyperbolic tangent, inverse hyperbolic cosecant, inverse hyperbolic secant, and inverse hyperbolic cotangent. They are commonly denoted by the symbols for the hyperbolic functions, prefixed with arc- or ar- or with a superscript ${-1}$ (for example $\operatorname{arcsinh}$, $\operatorname{arsinh}$, or $\sinh^{-1}$).

## Notation
The earliest and most widely adopted symbols use the prefix arc- (that is: $\operatorname{arcsinh}$, $\operatorname{arccosh}$, $\operatorname{arctanh}$, $\operatorname{arcsech}$, $\operatorname{arccsch}$, $\operatorname{arccoth}$), by analogy with the inverse circular functions ($\arcsin$, etc.). For a unit hyperbola ("Lorentzian circle") in the Lorentzian plane (pseudo-Euclidean plane of signature $(1, 1)$) or in the hyperbolic number plane, the hyperbolic angle measure (argument to the hyperbolic functions) is indeed the arc length of a hyperbolic arc.
Also common is the notation $\sinh^{-1},$ $\cosh^{-1},$ etc., although care must be taken to avoid misinterpretations of the superscript −1 as an exponent. The standard convention is that $\sinh^{-1} x$ or $\sinh^{-1}(x)$ means the inverse function while $(\sinh x)^{-1}$ or $\sinh(x)^{-1}$ means the reciprocal $1 / \sinh x.$ Especially inconsistent is the conventional use of positive integer superscripts to indicate an exponent rather than function composition, e.g. $\sinh^{2} x$ conventionally means $(\sinh x)^2$ and not $\sinh(\sinh x).$
Because the argument of hyperbolic functions is not the arc length of a hyperbolic arc in the Euclidean plane, some authors have condemned the prefix arc-, arguing that the prefix ar- (for area) or arg- (for argument) should be preferred. Following this recommendation, the ISO 80000-2 standard abbreviations use the prefix ar- (that is: $\operatorname{arsinh}$, $\operatorname{arcosh}$, $\operatorname{artanh}$, $\operatorname{arsech}$, $\operatorname{arcsch}$, $\operatorname{arcoth}$).
In computer programming languages, inverse circular and hyperbolic functions are often named with the shorter prefix a- (asinh, etc.).
This article will consistently adopt the prefix ar- for convenience.
