# Congruent isoscelizers point

## o1 subject: Congruent isoscelizers point

In geometry, the congruent isoscelizers point is a special point associated with a plane triangle. It is a triangle center and it is listed as X(173) in Clark Kimberling's Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers. This point was introduced to the study of triangle geometry by Peter Yff in 1989.

## o3 citation

## o4 section: Definition

## o5 definition: Definition

## Definition
An isoscelizer of an angle $A$ in a triangle $\triangle ABC$ is a line through points $P_{1}$ and $Q_{1}$, where $P_{1}$ lies on $AB$ and $Q_{1}$ on $AC$, such that the triangle $\triangle AP_{1}Q_{1}$ is an isosceles triangle. An isoscelizer of angle $A$ is a line perpendicular to the bisector of angle $A$.
Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Let $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ be the isoscelizers of the angles $A, B, C$ respectively such that they all have the same length. Then, for a unique configuration, the three isoscelizers $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ are concurrent. The point of concurrence is the congruent isoscelizers point of triangle $\triangle ABC$.

## o6 section: Properties

## o7 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
- The trilinear coordinates of the congruent isoscelizers point of triangle $\triangle ABC$ are
$$\begin{array}{ccccc}
\cos\frac{B}{2} + \cos\frac{C}{2} - \cos\frac{A}{2} &:& \cos\frac{C}{2} + \cos\frac{A}{2} - \cos\frac{B}{2} &:& \cos\frac{A}{2} + \cos\frac{B}{2} - \cos\frac{C}{2} \\[4pt]
= \quad \tan\frac{A}{2} + \sec\frac{A}{2} \quad \ \ &:& \tan\frac{B}{2} + \sec\frac{B}{2} &:& \tan\frac{C}{2} + \sec\frac{C}{2}
\end{array}$$
- The intouch triangle of the intouch triangle of triangle $\triangle ABC$ is perspective to $\triangle ABC$, and the congruent isoscelizers point is the perspector. This fact can be used to locate by geometrical constructions the congruent isoscelizers point of any given $\triangle ABC$.
