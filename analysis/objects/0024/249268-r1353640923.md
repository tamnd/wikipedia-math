# Glossary of group theory

## o1 subject: Glossary of group theory

A group is a set together with an associative operation that admits an identity element and such that there exists an inverse for every element.

## o2 section: A

## o3 entry: abelian group

abelian group
A group $(G, \bullet)$ is abelian if $\bullet$ is commutative, i.e. $g \bullet h = h \bullet g$ for all $g, h \in G$. Likewise, a group is nonabelian if this relation fails to hold for any pair $g, h \in G$.

## o4 entry: ascendant subgroup

ascendant subgroup
A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is ascendant if there is an ascending subgroup series starting from $H$ and ending at $G$, such that every term in the series is a normal subgroup of its successor. The series may be infinite. If the series is finite, then the subgroup is subnormal.

## o5 entry: automorphism

automorphism
An automorphism of a group is an isomorphism of the group to itself.

## o6 section: C

## o7 entry: center of a group

center of a group
The center of a group $G$, denoted $Z(G)$, is the set of those group elements that commute with all elements of $G$, that is, the set of all $h \in G$ such that $hg = gh$ for all $g \in G$. $Z(G)$ is always a normal subgroup of $G$. A group $G$ is abelian if and only if $Z(G) = G$.

## o8 entry: centerless group

centerless group
A group $G$ is centerless if its center $Z(G)$ is trivial.

## o9 entry: central subgroup

central subgroup
A subgroup of a group is a central subgroup of that group if it lies inside the center of the group.

## o10 entry: centralizer

centralizer
For a subset $S$ of a group $G$, the centralizer of $S$ in $G$, denoted $C_{G}(S)$, is the subgroup of $G$ defined by
: $\mathrm{C}_G(S)=\{ g \in G \mid gs=sg \text{ for all } s \in S\}.$

## o11 entry: characteristic subgroup

characteristic subgroup
A subgroup of a group is a characteristic subgroup of that group if it is mapped to itself by every automorphism of the parent group.

## o12 entry: characteristically simple group

characteristically simple group
A group is said to be characteristically simple if it has no proper nontrivial characteristic subgroups.

## o13 entry: class function

class function
A class function on a group $G$ is a function that it is constant on the conjugacy classes of $G$.

## o14 entry: class number

class number
The class number of a group is the number of its conjugacy classes.

## o15 entry: commutator

commutator
The commutator of two elements $g$ and $h$ of a group $G$ is the element $[g, h] = g^{-1}h^{-1}gh$. Some authors define the commutator as $[g, h] = ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ instead. The commutator of two elements $g$ and $h$ is equal to the group's identity if and only if $g$ and $h$ commutate, that is, if and only if $gh = hg$.

## o16 entry: commutator subgroup

commutator subgroup
The commutator subgroup or derived subgroup of a group is the subgroup generated by all the commutators of the group.

## o17 entry: complete group

complete group
A group $G$ is said to be complete if it is centerless and if every automorphism of $G$ is an inner automorphism.

## o18 entry: composition series

composition series
A composition series of a group $G$ is a subnormal series of finite length
: $1 = H_0\triangleleft H_1\triangleleft \cdots \triangleleft H_n = G,$
with strict inclusions, such that each $H_{i}$ is a maximal strict normal subgroup of $H_{i+1}$. Equivalently, a composition series is a subnormal series such that each factor group $H_{i+1} / H_{i}$ is simple. The factor groups are called composition factors.

## o19 entry: conjugacy-closed subgroup

conjugacy-closed subgroup
A subgroup of a group is said to be conjugacy-closed if any two elements of the subgroup that are conjugate in the group are also conjugate in the subgroup.

## o20 entry: conjugacy class

conjugacy class
The conjugacy classes of a group $G$ are those subsets of $G$ containing group elements that are conjugate with each other.

## o21 entry: conjugate elements

conjugate elements
Two elements $x$ and $y$ of a group $G$ are conjugate if there exists an element $g \in G$ such that $g^{-1}xg = y$. The element $g^{-1}xg$, denoted $x^{g}$, is called the conjugate of $x$ by $g$. Some authors define the conjugate of $x$ by $g$ as $gxg^{-1}$. This is often denoted ${}^{g}x$. Conjugacy is an equivalence relation. Its equivalence classes are called conjugacy classes.

## o22 entry: conjugate subgroups

conjugate subgroups
Two subgroups $H_{1}$ and $H_{2}$ of a group $G$ are conjugate subgroups if there is a $g \in G$ such that $gH_{1}g^{-1} = H_{2}$.

## o23 entry: contranormal subgroup

contranormal subgroup
A subgroup of a group $G$ is a contranormal subgroup of $G$ if its normal closure is $G$ itself.

## o24 entry: cyclic group

cyclic group
A cyclic group is a group that is generated by a single element, that is, a group such that there is an element $g$ in the group such that every other element of the group may be obtained by repeatedly applying the group operation to $g$ or its inverse.

## o25 section: D

## o26 entry: derived subgroup

derived subgroup
Synonym for commutator subgroup.

## o27 entry: direct product

direct product
The direct product of two groups $G$ and $H$, denoted $G \times H$, is the cartesian product of the underlying sets of $G$ and $H$, equipped with a component-wise defined binary operation $(g_{1}, h_{1}) \cdot (g_{2}, h_{2}) = (g_{1} \cdot g_{2}, h_{1} \cdot h_{2})$. With this operation, $G \times H$ itself forms a group.

## o28 section: E

## o29 entry: exponent of a group

exponent of a group
The exponent of a group $G$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $g^{n} = e$ for all $g \in G$. It is the least common multiple of the orders of all elements in the group. If no such positive integer exists, the exponent of the group is said to be infinite.

## o30 section: F

## o31 entry: factor group

factor group
Synonym for quotient group.

## o32 entry: FC-group

FC-group
A group is an FC-group if every conjugacy class of its elements has finite cardinality.

## o33 entry: finite group

finite group
A finite group is a group of finite order, that is, a group with a finite number of elements.

## o34 entry: finitely generated group

finitely generated group
A group $G$ is finitely generated if there is a finite generating set, that is, if there is a finite set $S$ of elements of $G$ such that every element of $G$ can be written as the combination of finitely many elements of $S$ and of inverses of elements of $S$.

## o35 section: G

## o36 entry: generating set

generating set
A generating set of a group $G$ is a subset $S$ of $G$ such that every element of $G$ can be expressed as a combination (under the group operation) of finitely many elements of $S$ and inverses of elements of $S$. Given a subset $S$ of $G$. We denote by $\langle S\rangle$ the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $S$. $\langle S\rangle$ is called the subgroup of $G$ generated by $S$.

## o37 entry: group automorphism

group automorphism
See automorphism.

## o38 entry: group homomorphism

group homomorphism
See homomorphism.

## o39 entry: group isomorphism

group isomorphism
See isomorphism.

## o40 section: H

## o41 entry: homomorphism

homomorphism
Given two groups $(G, \bullet)$ and $(H, \cdot)$, a homomorphism from $G$ to $H$ is a function $h : G \to H$ such that for all $a$ and $b$ in $G$, $h(a \bullet b) = h(a) \cdot h(b)$.

## o42 section: I

## o43 entry: index of a subgroup

index of a subgroup
The index of a subgroup $H$ of a group $G$, denoted $\lvert G : H\rvert$ or $[G : H]$ or $(G : H)$, is the number of cosets of $H$ in $G$. For a normal subgroup $N$ of a group $G$, the index of $N$ in $G$ is equal to the order of the quotient group $G / N$. For a finite subgroup $H$ of a finite group $G$, the index of $H$ in $G$ is equal to the quotient of the orders of $G$ and $H$.

## o44 entry: isomorphism

isomorphism
Given two groups $(G, \bullet)$ and $(H, \cdot)$, an isomorphism between $G$ and $H$ is a bijective homomorphism from $G$ to $H$, that is, a one-to-one correspondence between the elements of the groups in a way that respects the given group operations. Two groups are isomorphic if there exists a group isomorphism mapping from one to the other. Isomorphic groups can be thought of as essentially the same, only with different labels on the individual elements.

## o45 section: L

## o46 entry: lattice of subgroups

lattice of subgroups
The lattice of subgroups of a group is the lattice defined by its subgroups, partially ordered by set inclusion.

## o47 entry: locally cyclic group

locally cyclic group
A group is locally cyclic if every finitely generated subgroup is cyclic. Every cyclic group is locally cyclic, and every finitely-generated locally cyclic group is cyclic. Every locally cyclic group is abelian. Every subgroup, every quotient group and every homomorphic image of a locally cyclic group is locally cyclic.

## o48 section: N

## o49 entry: no small subgroup

no small subgroup
A topological group has no small subgroup if there exists a neighborhood of the identity element that does not contain any nontrivial subgroup.

## o50 entry: normal closure

normal closure
The normal closure of a subset $S$ of a group $G$ is the intersection of all normal subgroups of $G$ that contain $S$.

## o51 entry: normal core

normal core
The normal core of a subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is the largest normal subgroup of $G$ that is contained in $H$.

## o52 entry: normal series

normal series
A normal series of a group $G$ is a sequence of normal subgroups of $G$ such that each element of the sequence is a normal subgroup of the next element:
: $1 = A_0\triangleleft A_1\triangleleft \cdots \triangleleft A_n = G$
with
: $A_i\triangleleft G$.

## o53 entry: normal subgroup

normal subgroup
A subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ is normal in $G$ (denoted $N \text{◅} G$) if the conjugation of an element $n$ of $N$ by an element $g$ of $G$ is always in $N$, that is, if for all $g \in G$ and $n \in N$, $gng^{-1} \in N$. A normal subgroup $N$ of a group $G$ can be used to construct the quotient group $G / N$.

## o54 entry: normalizer

normalizer
For a subset $S$ of a group $G$, the normalizer of $S$ in $G$, denoted $N_{G}(S)$, is the subgroup of $G$ defined by
: $\mathrm{N}_G(S)=\{ g \in G \mid gS=Sg \}.$

## o55 section: O

## o56 entry: orbit

orbit
Consider a group $G$ acting on a set $X$. The orbit of an element $x$ in $X$ is the set of elements in $X$ to which $x$ can be moved by the elements of $G$. The orbit of $x$ is denoted by $G \cdot x$

## o57 entry: order of a group

order of a group
The order of a group $(G, \bullet)$ is the cardinality (i.e. number of elements) of $G$. A group with finite order is called a finite group.

## o58 entry: order of a group element

order of a group element
The order of an element $g$ of a group $G$ is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $g^{n} = e$. If no such integer exists, then the order of $g$ is said to be infinite. The order of a finite group is divisible by the order of every element.

## o59 section: P

## o60 entry: perfect core

perfect core
The perfect core of a group is its largest perfect subgroup.

## o61 entry: perfect group

perfect group
A perfect group is a group that is equal to its own commutator subgroup.

## o62 entry: periodic group

periodic group
A group is periodic if every group element has finite order. Every finite group is periodic.

## o63 entry: permutation group

permutation group
A permutation group is a group whose elements are permutations of a given set $M$ (the bijective functions from set $M$ to itself) and whose group operation is the composition of those permutations. The group consisting of all permutations of a set $M$ is the symmetric group of $M$.

## o64 entry: p-group

p-group
If $p$ is a prime number, then a $p$-group is one in which the order of every element is a power of $p$. A finite group is a $p$-group if and only if the order of the group is a power of $p$.

## o65 entry: p-subgroup

p-subgroup
A subgroup that is also a $p$-group. The study of $p$-subgroups is the central object of the Sylow theorems.

## o66 section: Q

## o67 entry: quotient group

quotient group
Given a group $G$ and a normal subgroup $N$ of $G$, the quotient group is the set $G / N$ of left cosets $\{aN : a \in G\}$ together with the operation $aN \bullet bN = abN$. The relationship between normal subgroups, homomorphisms, and factor groups is summed up in the fundamental theorem on homomorphisms.

## o68 section: R

## o69 entry: real element

real element
An element $g$ of a group $G$ is called a real element of $G$ if it belongs to the same conjugacy class as its inverse, that is, if there is a $h$ in $G$ with $g^{h} = g^{-1}$, where $g^{h}$ is defined as $h^{-1}gh$. An element of a group $G$ is real if and only if for all representations of $G$ the trace of the corresponding matrix is a real number.

## o70 section: S

## o71 entry: serial subgroup

serial subgroup
A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is a serial subgroup of $G$ if there is a chain $C$ of subgroups of $G$ from $H$ to $G$ such that for each pair of consecutive subgroups $X$ and $Y$ in $C$, $X$ is a normal subgroup of $Y$. If the chain is finite, then $H$ is a subnormal subgroup of $G$.

## o72 entry: simple group

simple group
A simple group is a nontrivial group whose only normal subgroups are the trivial group and the group itself.

## o73 entry: subgroup

subgroup
A subgroup of a group $G$ is a subset $H$ of the elements of $G$ that itself forms a group when equipped with the restriction of the group operation of $G$ to $H \times H$. A subset $H$ of a group $G$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if it is nonempty and closed under products and inverses, that is, if and only if for every $a$ and $b$ in $H$, $ab$ and $a^{-1}$ are also in $H$.

## o74 entry: subgroup series

subgroup series
A subgroup series of a group $G$ is a sequence of subgroups of $G$ such that each element in the series is a subgroup of the next element:
: $1 = A_0 \leq A_1 \leq \cdots \leq A_n = G.$

## o75 entry: subnormal subgroup

subnormal subgroup
A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is a subnormal subgroup of $G$ if there is a finite chain of subgroups of the group, each one normal in the next, beginning at $H$ and ending at $G$.

## o76 entry: symmetric group

symmetric group
Given a set $M$, the symmetric group of $M$ is the set of all permutations of $M$ (the set all bijective functions from $M$ to $M$) with the composition of the permutations as group operation. The symmetric group of a finite set of size $n$ is denoted $S_{n}$. (The symmetric groups of any two sets of the same size are isomorphic.)

## o77 section: T

## o78 entry: torsion group

torsion group
Synonym for periodic group.

## o79 entry: transitively normal subgroup

transitively normal subgroup
A subgroup of a group is said to be transitively normal in the group if every normal subgroup of the subgroup is also normal in the whole group.

## o80 entry: trivial group

trivial group
A trivial group is a group consisting of a single element, namely the identity element of the group. All such groups are isomorphic, and one often speaks of the trivial group.
