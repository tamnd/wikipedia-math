# Square of opposition

## o1 subject: Square of opposition

In term logic (a branch of philosophical logic), the square of opposition is a diagram representing the relations between the four basic categorical propositions. The origin of the square can be traced back to Aristotle's tractate On Interpretation and its distinction between two oppositions: contradiction and contrariety. However, Aristotle did not draw any diagram; this was done several centuries later.

## o3 table: The four Aristotelian propositions

The four Aristotelian propositions
| Name | Symbol | Latin | English* | Mnemonic | Modern form
| Universal affirmative | $A$ | Omne $S$ est $P$. | Every $S$ is $P$. ($S$ is always $P$.) | (I affirm) | $\forall x (Sx \rightarrow Px)$
| Universal negative | $E$ | Nullum $S$ est $P$. | No $S$ is $P$. ($S$ is never $P$.) | (I deny) | $\forall x (Sx \rightarrow \neg Px)$
| Particular affirmative | $I$ | Quoddam $S$ est $P$. | Some $S$ is $P$. ($S$ is sometimes $P$.) | (I affirm) | $\exists x (Sx \land Px)$
| Particular negative | $O$ | Quoddam $S$ nōn est $P$. | Some $S$ is not $P$. ($S$ is not always $P$.) | (I deny) | $\exists x (Sx \land \neg Px)$
