# Priestley space

## o1 subject: Priestley space

In mathematics, a Priestley space is an ordered topological space with special properties. Priestley spaces are named after Hilary Priestley who introduced and investigated them. Priestley spaces play a fundamental role in the study of distributive lattices. In particular, there is a duality ("Priestley duality") between the category of Priestley spaces and the category of bounded distributive lattices.

## o6 section: Definition

## o7 definition: Definition

## Definition
A Priestley space is an ordered topological space $(X,\tau,\le)$, i.e. a set $X$ equipped with a partial order $\le$ and a topology $\tau$, satisfying
the following two conditions:
- $(X,\tau)$ is compact.
- If $\scriptstyle x\,\not\le\, y$, then there exists a clopen up-set $U$ of $X$ such that $x\in U$ and $y\notin U$. (This condition is known as the Priestley separation axiom.)

## o9 section: Connection with spectral spaces

## o10 statement/theorem: Theorem

Theorem:
If $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, then both $(X,\tau^{u})$ and $(X,\tau^{d})$ are spectral spaces.
Conversely, given a spectral space $(X,\tau)$, let $\tau^{\#}$ denote the patch topology on $X$; that is, the topology generated by the subbasis consisting of compact open subsets of $(X,\tau)$ and their complements. Let also $\le$ denote the specialization order of $(X,\tau)$.

## o11 citation

## o12 statement/theorem: Theorem

Theorem:
If $(X,\tau)$ is a spectral space, then $(X,\tau^{\#},\le)$ is a Priestley space.
In fact, this correspondence between Priestley spaces and spectral spaces is functorial and yields an isomorphism between Pries and the category Spec of spectral spaces and spectral maps.

## o14 section: Connection with bitopological spaces

## o15 statement/theorem: Theorem

Theorem:
If $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, then $(X,\tau^{u},\tau^{d})$ is a pairwise Stone space. Conversely, if $(X,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})$ is a pairwise Stone space, then $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, where $\tau$ is the join of $\tau_{1}$ and $\tau_{2}$ and $\le$ is the specialization order of $(X,\tau_{1})$.
The correspondence between Priestley spaces and pairwise Stone spaces is functorial and yields an isomorphism between the category Pries of Priestley spaces and Priestley morphisms and the category PStone of pairwise Stone spaces and bi-continuous maps.
Thus, one has the following isomorphisms of categories:
$\mathbf{Spec}\cong \mathbf{Pries}\cong \mathbf{PStone}$
One of the main consequences of the duality theory for distributive lattices is that each of these categories is dually equivalent to the category of bounded distributive lattices.
