# Conditional disclosure of secrets

## o1 subject: Conditional disclosure of secrets

Conditional disclosure of secrets (CDS) is a primitive, studied in information-theoretic cryptography, that allows distributed, non-communicating parties to coordinate the release of information to a third party. CDS was initially introduced for use in the context of private information retrieval, and has been related to communication complexity and non-local quantum computation. It is also related to the private simultaneous message passing setting.

## o5 definition: Definition of conditional disclosure of secrets

## Definition of conditional disclosure of secrets
The conditional disclosure of secrets setting involves three players; Alice, Bob and the referee. Alice receives an input $x\in \{0,1\}^n$ and a secret $z\in \{0,1\}$, and Bob receives a string $y\in \{0,1\}^n$. A choice of Boolean function $f:\{0,1\}^{2n}\rightarrow \{0,1\}$ is fixed in advance and known to all players. Alice and Bob cannot communicate with one another, but share a string of random bits which we label $r$. Alice and Bob compute messages $m_A=m_A(x,z,r)$ and $m_B=m_B(y,r)$, which they send to the referee. The referee knows $(x,y)$. A CDS protocol consists of the encoding maps applied by Alice and Bob.
A protocol is said to be $\epsilon$-correct if, for all $(x,y)\in f^{-1}(1)$, the referee can determine $z$ with probability $1-\epsilon$.
A protocol is said to be $\delta$-secure if, for all $(x,y)\in f^{-1}(0)$ the distribution of the messages is $\delta$ close to a simulator distribution (in total variation distance), where the simulator distribution is independent of $z$.
The communication complexity of a CDS protocol P is the total number of bits of message sent by Alice and Bob. The CDS communication cost of a function, $CDS_{\epsilon,\delta}(f)$ is the minimal communication cost of an $\epsilon$-correct, $\delta$ secure protocol that implements $f$. The randomness complexity and randomness cost of implementing a function in the CDS model are defined similarly, but consider the number of bits of shared random bits held by Alice and Bob.

## o9 statement/theorem: Theorem (Amplification)

Theorem (Amplification). A CDS protocol for f which supports a single-bit secret with privacy and correctness error of 1/3 can be transformed into a new CDS protocol with privacy and correctness error of $2^{-\Omega(k)}$ and communication/randomness complexity which are larger than those of the original protocol by a multiplicative factor of O(k).
In fact, somewhat more than the above theorem is true in that the size of the secret can also be made to be of length $k$, while simultaneously reducing the correctness and privacy errors as above. The proof involves first encoding the secret $z$ into a secret sharing scheme, and then running the original CDS protocol on each share of the resulting scheme.

## o10 section: Closure

## o11 statement/theorem: Theorem (CDS is closed under complement)

Theorem (CDS is closed under complement). Suppose that f has a CDS protocol with randomness cost of $\rho$ bits, communication complexity of $t$ bits, and privacy and correctness errors $\delta=\epsilon=2^{-k}$. Then $\neg f$ has a CDS scheme with similar privacy and correctness errors, and randomness and communication complexity of $O(k^3\rho^2t + k^3\rho^3)$.
The cost of a CDS protocol is also closed under formula's, in the following sense. Consider two functions $f_1$ and $f_2$.
Then, the communication and randomness costs of $f_1\wedge f_2$ as well as $f_1\vee f_2$ are not much larger than the sum of the costs for $f_1$ and $f_2$. See Applebaum et al. for a precise statement.
