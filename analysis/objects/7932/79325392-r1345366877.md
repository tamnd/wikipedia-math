# Assembly (realizability)

## o1 subject: Assembly

In theoretical computer science and mathematical logic, assemblies can be informally described as sets equipped with representations for elements. Realizability toposes are completions of assembly categories.

## o3 section: Definition

## o4 definition: Definition

## Definition
Let $A$ be a fixed partial combinatory algebra.
An assembly $S$ (over $A$) is simply a set $|S|$ equipped with a relation $\Vdash_S$, read “realizes”, between $A$ and $|S|$, such that every element of $|S|$ has a realizer, i.e., for all $s \in |S|$, there exists $a \in A$ with $a \Vdash_S s$.
Assemblies are equipped with a categorical structure as follows. A morphism $f : S \to T$ between assemblies $S$ and $T$ is a function $f : |S| \to |T|$ between their underlying sets, for which there exists an element $p$ such that for all element $s \in |S|$ realized by $a \in A$, the application $p\,a$ is defined in the pca $A$ and the element $f(s) \in |T|$ is realized by $p\,a$. The category of assemblies over $A$ is denoted by $\operatorname{Asm}(A)$.
An assembly $S$ is said to be modest when elements do not share realizers, i.e., for all $s, s' \in S, a \in A$, if $a \Vdash_S s$ and $a \Vdash_S s'$ then $s = s'$. The category of modest assemblies over $A$ is denoted $\operatorname{Mod}(A)$ and is a full subcategory of $\operatorname{Asm}(A)$.

## o5 section: Examples

## o6 example: Examples

## Examples
The unit assembly $1$ is carried by a singleton $\{\star\}$, where $\star$ is realized all elements of the pca (alternatively, giving $\star$ any non-zero number of realizers gives an isomorphic object).
The empty assembly $0$ is carried by the empty set.
The assembly of natural numbers is carried by $\mathbb{N}$ where each natural number $n$ is realized only by its associated Curry numeral $\bar{n}$.
At the extreme opposite of modest assemblies, the constant assembly $\nabla X$ is carried by the set $X$, where all elements of $X$ are realized by all elements of the pca.
The assembly of classical truth values is the constant assembly on a two-element set, i.e., $\nabla \{0, 1\}$. The assembly of decidable truth values or booleans, is carried by $\{0, 1\}$, where 0 is realized by $\bar{0}$ and 1 is realized by the $\bar{1}$.
An intermediate between decidable and classical truth values is the assembly of semidecidable truth values. It is carried by $\{0, 1\}$, and all realizers are programs which compute infinite sequences of bits, i.e., elements $p$ from the pca such that for all $n$ the application $p\,\bar{n}$ is defined and has value $\bar{0}$ or $\bar{1}$. The realizers of 1 are those which compute a sequence that contains the bit 1, and the realizers of 0 are the others.
Given two assemblies $S$ and $T$, we may form their binary product $S \times T$ as follows: its carrier is the Cartesian product of the carriers $|S| \times T$, and a pair $(a, b) \in |S| \times |T|$ is realized by elements $p$ such that $\mathsf{fst}\,p$ realizes $a$ and $\mathsf{snd}\,p$ realizes $b$. Here, $\mathsf{fst}$ and $\mathsf{snd}$ are the projection functions in some encoding of pairs inside the pca, e.g., $\mathsf{pair} := \langle x y z \rangle z x y, \mathsf{fst} := \langle p \rangle p (\langle x y \rangle x), \mathsf{snd} := \langle p \rangle p (\langle x y \rangle y)$.)
We may also form the binary coproduct $S + T$, whose carrier is the disjoint union $|S| \sqcup |T|$ and where a realizer of $s \in S$ is $\mathsf{inl}\,a$ for $a$ a realizer of $s$, while a realizer of $t$ is $\mathsf{inr}\,b$ for $b$ a realizer of $t$. Here, $\mathsf{inl}$ and $\mathsf{inr}$ are the constructors for some encoding of disjoint unions, e.g., $\mathsf{inl} := \langle x \rangle \mathsf{pair}\,\bar{0}\,x, \mathsf{inr} := \langle x \rangle \mathsf{pair}\,\bar{1}\,x$.
