## Properties
The lexicographic product is in general noncommutative: $G \bullet H \ne H \bullet G$. However it satisfies a distributive law with respect to disjoint union: $(A + B) \bullet C = A \bullet C + B \bullet C$.
In addition it satisfies an identity with respect to complementation: $C(G \bullet H) = C(G) \bullet C(H)$. In particular, the lexicographic product of two self-complementary graphs is self-complementary.
The independence number of a lexicographic product may be easily calculated from that of its factors:
$\alpha(G \bullet H) = \alpha(G)\alpha(H)$.
The clique number of a lexicographic product is as well multiplicative:
$\omega(G \bullet H) = \omega(G)\omega(H)$.
The chromatic number of a lexicographic product is equal to the b-fold chromatic number of G, for b equal to the chromatic number of H:
$\chi(G \bullet H) = \chi_{b}(G)$, where $b = \chi(H)$.
The lexicographic product of two graphs is a perfect graph if and only if both factors are perfect.
