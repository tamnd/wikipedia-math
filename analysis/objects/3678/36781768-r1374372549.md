# Heavy traffic approximation

## o1 subject: Heavy traffic approximation

In queueing theory, a discipline within the mathematical theory of probability, a heavy traffic approximation (sometimes called heavy traffic limit theorem or diffusion approximation) involves the matching of a queueing model with a diffusion process under some limiting conditions on the model's parameters. The first such result was published by John Kingman, who showed that when the utilisation parameter of an M/M/1 queue is near 1, a scaled version of the queue length process can be accurately approximated by a reflected Brownian motion.

## o16 statement/theorem: Theorem 1

Theorem 1. Consider a sequence of G/G/1 queues indexed by $j$.
For queue $j$ let $T_j$ denote the random inter-arrival time, $S_j$ denote the random service time;
let $\rho_j=\frac{\lambda_j}{\mu_j}$ denote the traffic intensity with $\frac{1}{\lambda_j}=E(T_j)$ and $\frac{1}{\mu_j}=E(S_j)$;
let $W_{q,j}$ denote the waiting time in queue for a customer in steady state;
Let $\alpha_j=-E[S_j-T_j]$ and $\beta_j^2=\operatorname{var}[S_j-T_j];$
Suppose that $T_j\xrightarrow{d} T$, $S_j\xrightarrow{d} S$, and $\rho_j \rightarrow 1$. then
$\frac{2\alpha_j}{\beta_j^2}W_{q,j}\xrightarrow{d} \exp(1)$
provided that:
(a) $\operatorname{Var}[S-T]>0$
(b) for some $\delta > 0$, $E[S_j^{2+\delta}]$ and $E[T_j^{2+\delta}]$ are both less than some constant $C$ for all $j$.

## o20 statement/theorem: Theorem 2

Theorem 2. Let $X$ be a Brownian motion with drift $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ starting at the origin, and let $M_t = \sup_{0\leq s\leq t} X(s)$
if $\mu \leq 0$
$\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty}P(M_t > x)=\exp(2\mu x/\sigma^2 ), x \geq 0;$
otherwise
$\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} P(M_t\geq x)=1, x \geq 0.$

## Example
Consider an M/G/1 queue with arrival rate $\lambda$, the mean of the service time $E[S]=\frac{1}{\mu}$, and the variance of the service time $\operatorname{var}[S]=\sigma_{B}^2$. What is average waiting time in queue in the steady state?
The exact average waiting time in queue in steady state is given by:
$W_q=\frac{\rho^2+\lambda^2\sigma_B^2}{2\lambda(1-\rho)}$
The corresponding heavy traffic approximation:
$W_q^{(H)}=\frac{\lambda(\frac{1}{\lambda^2}+\sigma_B^2)}{2(1-\rho)}.$
The relative error of the heavy traffic approximation:
$\frac{W_q^{(H)}-W_q}{W_q}=\frac{1-\rho^2}{\rho^2+\lambda^2\sigma_B^2}$
Thus when $\rho\rightarrow 1$, we have :
$\frac{W_q^{(H)}-W_q}{W_q} \rightarrow 0.$
