# Jyā, koti-jyā and utkrama-jyā

## o1 subject: Jyā, koti-jyā and utkrama-jyā

sa, sa and sa are three trigonometric functions introduced by Indian mathematicians and astronomers. The earliest known Indian treatise containing references to these functions is Surya Siddhanta. These are functions of arcs of circles and not functions of angles. sa and sa are closely related to the modern trigonometric functions of sine and cosine. In fact, the origins of the modern terms of "sine" and "cosine" have been traced back to the Sanskrit words sa and sa.

## o3 section: Definition

## o4 definition: Definition

## Definition
Let '$\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB}$' denote an arc whose two extremities are $A$ and $B$ of a circle with center $O$. If a perpendicular $\operatorname{BM}$ is dropped from $B$ to $\operatorname{OA}$, then:
- sa of $\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} = \operatorname{BM}$
- sa of $\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} = \operatorname{OM}$
- sa of $\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} = \operatorname{MA}$
If the radius of the circle is $R$ and the length of $\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB}$ is $s$, the angle subtended by $\operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB}$ at $O$ measured in radians is $\theta = s / R$. The three Indian functions are related to modern trigonometric functions as follows:
- $\operatorname{sa} ( \operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} ) = R \sin ( s / R )$
- $\operatorname{sa} ( \operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} ) = R \cos ( s / R )$
- $\operatorname{sa} ( \operatorname{arc} \operatorname{AB} ) = R ( 1 - \cos ( s / R ) ) = R \operatorname{versin} ( s / R )$

## o6 definition/notation: Terminology

## Terminology
An arc of a circle is like a bow and so is called a sa or sa which in Sanskrit means "a bow".
The straight line joining the two extremities of an arc of a circle is like the string of a bow and this line is a chord of the circle. This chord is called a sa which in Sanskrit means "a bow-string", presumably translating Hipparchus's with the same meaning.
The word sa is also used as a synonym for sa in geometrical literature.
At some point, Indian astronomers and mathematicians realised that computations would be more convenient if one used the halves of the chords instead of the full chords and associated the half-chords with the halves of the arcs. The half-chords were called sas or sas. These terms were again shortened to sa by omitting the qualifier sa which meant "half of".
The Sanskrit word sa has the meaning of "point, cusp", and specifically "the curved end of a bow".
In trigonometry, it came to denote "the complement of an arc to $90^{\circ}$". Thus
sa is "the sa of the complementary arc". In Indian treatises, especially in commentaries, sa is often abbreviated as sa. The term sa also denotes "the side of a right angled triangle". Thus sa could also mean the other cathetus of a right triangle, the first cathetus being the sa.
sa means "inverted", thus sa means "inverted chord".
The tabular values of sa are derived from the tabular values of sa by subtracting the elements from the radius in the reversed order. This is really the arrow between the bow and the bow-string and hence it has also been called sa, sa or sa all meaning "arrow".
An arc of a circle which subtends an angle of $90^{\circ}$ at the center is called a sa (a quadrat of a circle). Each zodiacal sign defines an arc of $30^{\circ}$ and three consecutive zodiacal signs defines a sa. The sa of a sa is the radius of the circle. The Indian astronomers coined the term sa to denote the radius of the base circle, the term sa being indicative of "the sa of three signs". The radius is also called sa, sa, sa, etc., all meaning "semi-diameter".
According to one convention, the functions sa and sa are respectively denoted by "Rsin" and "Rcos" treated as single words. Others denote sa and sa respectively by "Sin" and "Cos" (the first letters being capital letters in contradistinction to the first letters being small letters in ordinary sine and cosine functions).
