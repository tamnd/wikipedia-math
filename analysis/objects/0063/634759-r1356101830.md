# Hausdorff paradox

## o1 subject: Hausdorff paradox

The Hausdorff paradox is a paradox in mathematics named after Felix Hausdorff. It involves the sphere ${ S^2}$ (the surface of a 3-dimensional ball in ${ \mathbb{R}^3 }$). It states that if a certain countable subset is removed from ${ S^2 }$, then the remainder can be divided into three disjoint subsets ${ A,B }$ and ${ C }$ such that ${ A, B, C }$ and ${ B \cup C }$ are all congruent. In particular, it follows that on $S^2$ there is no finitely additive measure defined on all subsets such that the measure of congruent sets is equal (because this would imply that the measure of ${ B \cup C }$ is simultaneously $1/3$, $1/2$, and $2/3$ of the non-zero measure of the whole sphere).
