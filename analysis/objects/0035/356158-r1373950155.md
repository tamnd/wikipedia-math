# Annulus (mathematics)

## o1 citation

## o2 subject: Annulus

In mathematics, an annulus (: annuli or annuluses) is the region between two concentric circles. Informally, it is shaped like a ring or a hardware washer. The word "annulus" is borrowed from the Latin word anulus or annulus meaning 'little ring'. The adjectival form is annular (as in annular eclipse).

## Complex structure
In complex analysis an annulus $\operatorname{ann}(a; r, R)$ in the complex plane is an open region defined as
$r < |z - a| < R.$
If $r = 0$, the region is known as the punctured disk (a disk with a point hole in the center) of radius $R$ around the point $a$.
As a subset of the complex plane, an annulus can be considered as a Riemann surface. The complex structure of an annulus depends only on the ratio $\tfrac{r}{R}$. Each annulus $\operatorname{ann}(a; r, R)$ can be holomorphically mapped to a standard one centered at the origin and with outer radius 1 by the map
$z \mapsto \frac{z - a}{R}.$
The inner radius is then $\tfrac{r}{R} < 1$.
The Hadamard three-circle theorem is a statement about the maximum value a holomorphic function may take inside an annulus.
The Joukowsky transform conformally maps an annulus onto an ellipse with a slit cut between foci.
