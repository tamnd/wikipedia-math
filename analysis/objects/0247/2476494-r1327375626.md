## o4 subject: Equiangular polygon

In Euclidean geometry, an equiangular polygon is a polygon whose vertex angles are equal. If the lengths of the sides are also equal (that is, if it is also equilateral) then it is a regular polygon. Isogonal polygons are equiangular polygons which alternate two edge lengths.

## Construction
An equiangular polygon can be constructed from a regular polygon or regular star polygon where edges are extended as infinite lines. Each edges can be independently moved perpendicular to the line's direction. Vertices represent the intersection point between pairs of neighboring line. Each moved line adjusts its edge-length and the lengths of its two neighboring edges. If edges are reduced to zero length, the polygon becomes degenerate, or if reduced to negative lengths, this will reverse the internal and external angles.
For an even-sided direct equiangular polygon, with internal angles θ°, moving alternate edges can invert all vertices into supplementary angles, 180-θ°. Odd-sided direct equiangular polygons can only be partially inverted, leaving a mixture of supplementary angles.
Every equiangular polygon can be adjusted in proportions by this construction and still preserve equiangular status.

## o10 section: Equiangular polygon theorem

## o11 statement: Equiangular polygon theorem

## Equiangular polygon theorem
For a convex equiangular p-gon, each internal angle is 180(1−2/p)°; this is the equiangular polygon theorem.
For a direct equiangular p/q star polygon, density q, each internal angle is 180(1−2q/p)°, with $1 < 2q < p$. For $w = \gcd(p,q) > 1$, this represents a w-wound $\tfrac{p/w}{q/w}$ star polygon, which is degenerate for the regular case.
A concave indirect equiangular $(p_{r}+p_{l})$-gon, with $p_{r}$ right turn vertices and $p_{l}$ left turn vertices, will have internal angles of $180(1-2/\lvert p_{r}-p_{l}\rvert))^{\circ}$, regardless of their sequence. An indirect star equiangular $(p_{r}+p_{l})$-gon, with $p_{r}$ right turn vertices and $p_{l}$ left turn vertices and q total turns, will have internal angles of $180(1-2q/\lvert p_{r}-p_{l}\rvert))^{\circ}$, regardless of their sequence. An equiangular polygon with the same number of right and left turns has zero total turns, and has no constraints on its angles.

## o12 section: Notation

## o13 definition/notation: Notation

## Notation
Every direct equiangular p-gon can be given a notation $\langle p\rangle$ or $\langle p/q\rangle$, like regular polygons {p} and regular star polygons {p/q}, containing p vertices, and stars having density q.
Convex equiangular p-gons $\langle p\rangle$ have internal angles 180(1−2/p)°, while direct star equiangular polygons, $\langle p/q\rangle$, have internal angles 180(1−2q/p)°.
A concave indirect equiangular p-gon can be given the notation $\langle p-2c\rangle$, with c counter-turn vertices. For example, $\langle 6-2\rangle$ is a hexagon with 90° internal angles of the difference, $\langle 4\rangle$, 1 counter-turned vertex. A multiturn indirect equilateral p-gon can be given the notation $\langle {}^{p-2c}/_{q}\rangle$ with c counter turn vertices, and q total turns. An equiangular polygon <p−p> is a p-gon with undefined internal angles $\theta$, but can be expressed explicitly as $\langle p-p\rangle$θ.

## o22 pointer-item: Equiangular quadrilaterals

### Equiangular quadrilaterals
Direct equiangular quadrilaterals have 90° internal angles. The only equiangular quadrilaterals are rectangles, $\langle 4\rangle$, and squares, {4}.
An equiangular quadrilateral with integer side lengths may be tiled by unit squares.
