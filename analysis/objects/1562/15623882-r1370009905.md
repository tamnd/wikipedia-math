# Four factor formula

## o1 subject: Four factor formula

The four-factor formula, also known as Fermi's four factor formula, is used in nuclear engineering to determine the multiplication of a nuclear chain reaction in an infinite medium.

## o2 table: Four-factor formula: k_{\infty} = \eta f p \varepsilon

Four-factor formula: $k_{\infty} = \eta f p \varepsilon$
| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Formula | Typical thermal reactor value
| $\eta$ | Reproduction factor (eta) | $\tfrac{\operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{produced}\text{ from } \operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{fissions}}{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{absorption}\text{ in } \operatorname{fuel} \operatorname{isotope}}$ | $\eta = \frac{\nu \sigma_f^F}{\sigma_a^F} = \frac{\nu \Sigma_f^F}{\Sigma_a^F}$ | 1.65
| $f$ | Thermal utilization factor | $\tfrac{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{absorbed}\text{ by }\text{ the } \operatorname{fuel} \operatorname{isotope}}{\operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{absorbed} \operatorname{anywhere}}$ | $f = \frac{\Sigma_a^F}{\Sigma_a}$ | 0.71
| $p$ | Resonance escape probability | $\tfrac{\operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons} \operatorname{slowed}\text{ to } \operatorname{thermal} \operatorname{energies} \operatorname{without} \operatorname{absorption}}{\operatorname{total} \operatorname{fission} \operatorname{neutrons}}$ | $p \approx \exp \left( -\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N} N_i I_{r,A,i}}{\left( \overline{\xi} \Sigma_p \right)_{mod}} \right)$ | 0.87
