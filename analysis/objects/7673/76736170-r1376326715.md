# Sensitivity theorem

## o1 subject: Sensitivity theorem

In computational complexity, the sensitivity theorem, proved by Hao Huang in 2019, states that the sensitivity of a Boolean function $f: \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ is at least the square root of its degree, thus settling a conjecture posed by Nisan and Szegedy in 1992. The proof is notably succinct, given that prior progress had been limited.

## o22 section: Statement

## o23 statement: Statement

## Statement
Every Boolean function $f : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ can be expressed in a unique way as a multilinear polynomial over the real numbers. The degree of $f$ is the degree of this unique polynomial, denoted $\deg(f)$.
The sensitivity of the Boolean function $f$ at the point $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ is the number of indices $i \in [n]$ such that $f(x^{\oplus i}) \neq f(x)$, where $x^{\oplus i}$ is obtained from $x$ by flipping the $i$-th coordinate. The sensitivity of $f$ is the maximum sensitivity of $f$ at any point $x \in \{0,1\}^n$, denoted $s(f)$.
Trivially, we have the upper bound $s(f)\leq n$. Tal, improving on an earlier bound of Nisan and Szegedy, showed that
$s(f) \leq \deg(f)^2.$
Note that for this upper bound, it is important that we are working over the field of real numbers, as opposed to the field of two elements; on the field of two elements, the polynomial $\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$ (the sum of coordinates, i.e., the function that calculates the parity of the number of 1s) has degree 1 but full sensitivity $n$.
The sensitivity theorem states that
$s(f) \ge \sqrt{\deg(f)}.$
The sensitivity theorem is tight for the AND-of-ORs function
$\bigwedge_{i=1}^m \bigvee_{j=1}^m x_{ij},$
which has degree $m^2$ and sensitivity $m$.

## o27 section: Proof

## o28 proof: Proof

## Proof
Let $f : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ be a Boolean function of degree $d$. Consider any maxonomial of $f$, that is, a monomial of degree $d$ in the unique multilinear polynomial representing $f$. If we substitute an arbitrary value in the coordinates not mentioned in the monomial then we get a function $F$ on $d$ coordinates which has degree $d$, and moreover, $s(f) \geq s(F)$. If we prove the sensitivity theorem for $F$ then it follows for $f$. So from now on, we assume without loss of generality that $f$ has degree $n$.
Define a new function $g : \{0,1\}^n \to \{0,1\}$ by
$g(x_1,\dots,x_n) = f \oplus x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus x_n.$
It can be shown that since $f$ has degree $n$ then $g$ is unbalanced (meaning that $|g^{-1}(0)| \neq |g^{-1}(1)|$), say $|g^{-1}(1)| > 2^{n-1}$. Consider the subgraph $G$ of the hypercube (the graph on $\{0,1\}^n$ in which two vertices are connected if they differ by a single coordinate) induced by $S = g^{-1}(1)$. In order to prove the sensitivity theorem, it suffices to show that $G$ has a vertex whose degree is at least $\sqrt{n}$. This reduction is due to Gotsman and Linial.
Huang constructs a signing of the hypercube in which the product of the signs along any square is $-1$. This means that there is a way to assign a sign to every edge of the hypercube so that this property is satisfied. The same signing had been found earlier by Ahmadi et al., who were interested in signings of graphs with few distinct eigenvalues.
Let $A$ be the signed adjacency matrix corresponding to the signing. The property that the product of the signs in every square is $-1$ implies that $A^2=nI$, and so half of the eigenvalues of $A$ are $\sqrt{n}$ and half are $-\sqrt{n}$. In particular, the eigenspace of $\sqrt{n}$ (which has dimension $2^{n-1}$) intersects the space of vectors supported by $S$ (which has dimension $>2^{n-1}$), implying that there is an eigenvector $v$ of $A$ with eigenvalue $\sqrt{n}$ which is supported on $S$. (This is a simplification of Huang's original argument due to Shalev Ben-David.)
Consider a point $x \in S$ maximizing $|v_x|$. On the one hand, $Av = \sqrt{n}v$.
On the other hand, $Av$ is at most the sum of absolute values of all neighbors of $x$ in $S$, which is at most $\deg_G(x) \cdot |v_x|$. Hence $\deg_G(x) \geq \sqrt{n}$.
### Constructing the signing
Huang constructed the signing recursively. When $n=1$, we can take an arbitrary signing. Given a signing $\sigma_n$ of the $n$-dimensional hypercube $Q_n$, we construct
a signing of $Q_{n+1}$ as follows. Partition $Q_{n+1}$ into two copies of $Q_n$. Use $\sigma_n$ for one of them and $-\sigma_n$ for the other, and assign all edges between the two copies the sign $1$.
The same signing can also be expressed directly. Let $(x,y)$ be an edge of the hypercube. If $i$ is the first coordinate on which $x,y$ differ, we use the sign $(-1)^{x_1 + \cdots + x_{i-1}}$.

## o36 statement/generalization: Extensions

## Extensions
The sensitivity theorem can be equivalently restated as
$\deg(f) \leq s(f)^2.$
Laplante et al. refined this to
$\deg(f) \leq s_0(f)s_1(f),$
where $s_b(f)$ is the maximum sensitivity of $f$ at a point in $f^{-1}(b)$.
They showed furthermore that this bound is attained at two neighboring points of the hypercube.
Aaronson, Ben-David, Kothari, Rao and Tal defined a new measure, the spectral sensitivity of $f$, denoted $\lambda(f)$. This is the largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of the sensitivity graph of $f$, which is the subgraph of the hypercube consisting of all sensitive edges (edges connecting two points $x,y$ such that $f(x) \neq f(y)$). They showed that Huang's proof can be decomposed into two steps:
- $\deg(f) \leq \lambda(f)^2$.
- $\lambda(f) \leq s(f)$.
Using this measure, they proved several tight relations between complexity measures of Boolean functions: $\deg(f) = O(Q(f)^2)$ and $D(f) = O(Q(f)^4)$. Here $D(f)$ is the deterministic query complexity and $Q(f)$ is the quantum query complexity.
Dafni et al. extended the notions of degree and sensitivity to Boolean functions on the symmetric group and on the perfect matching association scheme, and proved analogs of the sensitivity theorem for such functions. Their proofs use a reduction to Huang's sensitivity theorem.
