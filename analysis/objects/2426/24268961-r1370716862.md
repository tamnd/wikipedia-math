# Hyperoctahedral group

## o1 subject: Hyperoctahedral group

The hyperoctahedral groups are a family of mathematical groups that arise as the group of symmetries of the square, the cube, and their higher-dimensional counterparts (the hypercubes), as well as the corresponding dual polytopes (the regular octahedron and its higher-dimensional counterparts, the cross-polytopes). There is one hyperoctahedral group for each dimension $n$.

## Subgroups
The hyperoctahedral group has several notable families of subgroups.
### Center
The center of $S^{\pm}_n$ consists solely of the longest element $-1$ and the identity.
### Index-$2$ subgroups
For $n \geq 2$, the group $S^{\pm}_n$ has three subgroups of index $2$ (that is, subgroups that include exactly half the elements in $S^{\pm}_n$): the orientation-preserving symmetries of the hypercube, the even-signed permutation group (the Coxeter group of type D), and the generalized alternating group.
The alternating subgroup or even subgroup of the hyperoctahedral group is the subgroup consisting of elements of determinant $1$ in the matrix representation, that is, the elements that can be written as a product of an even number of reflections. They are also the orientation-preserving symmetries of the hypercube.
A second index-$2$ subgroup is formed by those elements whose signed permutation has an even number of minus signs. This group is again a Coxeter group, of type D. One possible set of generators for this subgroup is $s_0s_1s_0 = (1 \ -2)(-1 \ 2), s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_{n - 1}$, where the $s_i$ are the Coxeter generating set for $S^{\pm}_n$. It is also the symmetry group of the demihypercube, the polytope that one gets by taking the convex hull of every other vertex of the hypercube $\mathcal{H}$.
When $n$ is odd, the hyperoctahedral group $S^{\pm}_n$ is the direct product of its even subgroup and its center, and also the product of the type-D subgroup with its center. Moreover, in this case the even subgroup and type-D subgroup are isomorphic and are further isomorphic to the quotient of $S^{\pm}_n$ by its center.
The third index-$2$ subgroup is the wreath product $C_2 \wr A_{n}$ of the two-element group with the alternating group of even permutations (a generalized alternating group).
### Commutator subgroup
For $n \geq 2$, the commutator subgroup of $S^\pm_n$ has index $4$; it is equal to the commutator subgroup of the even-signed subgroup of type D.
### Parabolic subgroups
The standard parabolic subgroups of $S_n$ are the Young subgroups: for each tuple $(a_1, \ldots, a_k)$ of positive integers such that $a_1 + \ldots + a_k = n$, the Young subgroup associated to the tuple is the subgroup isomorphic to $S_{a_1} \times \cdots \times S_{a_k}$ that arises by permuting the values in each of the sets $\{1, 2, \ldots, a_1\}$, $\{a_1 + 1, a_1 + 2, \ldots, a_1 + a_2\}$, etc. among themselves. The standard parabolic subgroups of $S^\pm_n$ are the subgroups of the form $S^\pm_{a_0} \times S_{a_1} \times \cdots S_{a_k}$ where $a_0$ is a nonnegative integer and $a_1, \ldots, a_k$ are positive integers such that $a_0 + a_1 + \ldots + a_k = n$. In particular, the maximal standard parabolic subgroups of the hyperoctahedral group $S^{\pm}_n$ are the setwise stabilizers of the set $\{k, \ldots, n\}$ for $k = 1, \ldots, n$. That is, each maximal standard parabolic is isomorphic to the direct product $S^{\pm}_{k - 1} \times S_{n + 1 - k}$ of a smaller hyperoctahedral group and a symmetric group. When $k = 1$, we have as a special case that the symmetric group $S_n$ is a maximal parabolic subgroup of $S^{\pm}_n$. Meanwhile, the hyperoctahedral group $S^{\pm}_k$ is a parabolic subgroup of $S^{\pm}_n$ for all $k \leq n$.
