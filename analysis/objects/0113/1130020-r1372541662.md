# Maximum parsimony

## o1 subject: Maximum parsimony

In evolutionary biology, maximum parsimony is a method used to build phylogenetic trees based on the principle of simplicity. Under the maximum-parsimony criterion, the best tree will minimize the amount of homoplasy (i.e., convergent evolution, parallel evolution, and evolutionary reversals). In other words, under this criterion, the optimal tree has the shortest graph using the fewest evolutionary steps required to explain its genetic and physical data.
Some of the basic ideas behind maximum parsimony were presented by James S. Farris in 1970 and Walter M. Fitch in 1971.
