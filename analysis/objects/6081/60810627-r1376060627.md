# Newton–Gauss line

## o1 subject: Newton–Gauss line

In geometry, the Newton–Gauss line (or Gauss–Newton line) is the line joining the midpoints of the three diagonals of a complete quadrilateral.

## o12 statement: Theorem

#### Theorem
If the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{BF}$ is $P$, the Newton–Gauss line of the complete quadrilateral $ABCDEF$ and the line $PM$ determine an angle $\angle PMN$ equal to $\angle EFD$.
##### Proof
First show that the triangles $\triangle NPM, \triangle EDF$ are similar.
Since $BE \parallel PN$ and $FC \parallel PM$, we know $\angle NPM = \angle EAC$. Also, $\tfrac{\overline{BE}}{\overline{PN}} = \tfrac{\overline{FC}}{\overline{PM}} = 2.$
In the cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$, these equalities hold:
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle EDF &= \angle ADF + \angle EDA, \\
&= \angle ACB + \angle ABC, \\
&= \angle EAC.
\end{aligned}$$
Therefore, $\angle NPM = \angle EDF$.
Let $R_{1}, R_{2}$ be the radii of the circumcircles of $\triangle EDB, \triangle FCD$ respectively. Apply the law of sines to the triangles, to obtain:
$$\frac {\overline{BE}}{\overline{FC}}=\frac {2R_1\sin \angle EDB}{2R_2\sin \angle FDC}=\frac {R_1}{R_2}=\frac {2R_1\sin \angle EBD}{2R_2\sin \angle FCD} = \frac{\overline{DE}}{\overline{DF}}.$$
Since $\overline{BE} = 2 \cdot \overline{PN}$ and $\overline{FC} = 2 \cdot \overline{PM}$, this shows the equality $\tfrac{\overline{PN}}{\overline{PM}} = \tfrac{\overline{DE}}{\overline{DF}}.$ The similarity of triangles $\triangle PMN, \triangle DFE$ follows, and $\angle NMP = \angle EFD$.
##### Remark
If $Q$ is the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{FC}$, it follows by the same reasoning that $\angle NMQ = \angle EFA$.

#### Theorem
The line through $E$ parallel to the Newton–Gauss line of the complete quadrilateral $ABCDEF$ and the line $EF$ are isogonal lines of $\angle BEC$, that is, each line is a reflection of the other about the angle bisector. (Figure 2)
##### Proof
Triangles $\triangle EDF, \triangle NPM$ are similar by the above argument, so $\angle DEF = \angle PNM$. Let $E'$ be the point of intersection of $BC$ and the line parallel to the Newton–Gauss line $NM$ through $E$.
Since $PN \parallel BE$ and $NM \parallel EE',$ $\angle BEF = \angle PNF$, and $\angle FNM = \angle E'EF$.
Therefore,
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle CEE' &= \angle DEF - \angle E'EF, \\
&= \angle PNM - \angle FNM, \\
&= \angle PNF = \angle BEF.
\end{aligned}$$

## o22 section: Lemma

## o23 statement/lemma: Lemma

#### Lemma
Let $G$ and $H$ be the orthogonal projections of the point $F$ on the lines $AB$ and $CD$ respectively.
The quadrilaterals $MPGN$ and $MQHN$ are cyclic quadrilaterals.
##### Proof
$\angle EFD = \angle PMN$, as previously shown. The points $P$ and $N$ are the respective circumcenters of the right triangles $\triangle BFG, \triangle EFG$. Thus, $\angle PGF = \angle PFG$ and $\angle FGN = \angle GFN$.
Therefore,
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle PGN + \angle PMN
& = (\angle PGF + \angle FGN) + \angle PMN \\[4pt]
& = \angle PFG + \angle GFN + \angle EFD \\[4pt]
&= 180^\circ
\end{aligned}.$$
Therefore, $MPGN$ is a cyclic quadrilateral, and by the same reasoning, $MQHN$ also lies on a circle.

## o26 section: Theorem

## o27 statement: Theorem

#### Theorem
Extend the lines $GF, HF$ to intersect $EC, EB$ at $I, J$ respectively (Figure 4).
The complete quadrilaterals $EFGHIJ$ and $ABCDEF$ have the same Newton–Gauss line.
##### Proof
The two complete quadrilaterals have a shared diagonal, $\overline{EF}$. $N$ lies on the Newton–Gauss line of both quadrilaterals. $N$ is equidistant from $G$ and $H$, since it is the circumcenter of the cyclic quadrilateral $EGFH$.
If triangles $\triangle GMP, \triangle HMQ$ are congruent, and it will follow that $M$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of the line $HG$. Therefore, the line $MN$ contains the midpoint of $\overline{GH}$, and is the Newton–Gauss line of $EFGHIJ$.
To show that the triangles $\triangle GMP, \triangle HMQ$ are congruent, first observe that $PMQF$ is a parallelogram, since the points $M, P$ are midpoints of $\overline{BF}, \overline{BC}$ respectively.
Therefore,
$$\begin{aligned}
& \overline{MP} = \overline{QF} = \overline{HQ}, \\
& \overline{GP} = \overline{PF} = \overline{MQ}, \\
& \angle MPF = \angle FQM.
\end{aligned}$$
Also note that
$$\angle FPG = 2 \angle PBG = 2 \angle DBA = 2 \angle DCA = 2 \angle HCF = \angle HQF.$$
Hence,
$$\begin{aligned}
\angle MPG &= \angle MPF + \angle FPG, \\
&= \angle FQM + \angle HQF, \\
&= \angle HQF + \angle FQM, \\
&= \angle HQM.
\end{aligned}$$
Therefore, $\triangle GMP$ and $\triangle HMQ$ are congruent by SAS.
##### Remark
Due to $\triangle GMP, \triangle HMQ$ being congruent triangles, their circumcircles $MPGN, MQHN$ are also congruent.

## o33 statement/generalization: Generalization

## Generalization
Dao Thanh Oai showed a generalization of the Newton–Gauss line.
For a triangle $ABC$, let $l$ an arbitrary line and $A_{0}B_{0}C_{0}$ the Cevian triangle of an arbitrary point $P$. $l$ intersects $BC, CA$, and $AB$ at $A_{1}, B_{1},$ and $C_{1}$ respectively. Then $AA_{1}\cap B_{0}C_{0}, BB_{1}\cap C_{0}A_{0}$, and $CC_{1}\cap A_{0}B_{0}$ are colinear.
If $P$ is the centroid of the triangle $ABC$, the line is Newton–Gauss line of the quadrilateral composed of $AB, BC, CA,$ and $l$.
