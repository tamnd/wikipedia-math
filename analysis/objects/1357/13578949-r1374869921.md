# Physical unclonable function

## o1 subject: Physical unclonable function

In computing and cryptography, a physical unclonable function or PUF is a physical object whose operation cannot be reproduced ("cloned") in a physical way (by making another system using the same technology), such that for a given input and conditions (challenge), provides a physically defined "digital fingerprint" output (response) that serves as a unique identifier—most often for a semiconductor device such as a microprocessor or a material producing an optical signal. PUFs are often based on unique physical variations occurring naturally during semiconductor manufacturing.

## Chemical unclonable function
Chemical unclonable functions (CUFs) are analogous to physical unclonable functions but are based on chemical reactions. As of May 2026 only one type of CUF has been presented, a DNA-based function by Luescher et al. This CUF is based on pools of partially random DNA molecules that produce a consistent output (DNA sequence reads) when presented with an input (performing PCR with specific primers). Due to chemical modifications applied to the ends of the molecules, the DNA pool is neither clonable nor able to be sequenced, and even given perfect knowledge about the sequences comprising the pool it would not be feasible to re-synthesize it given the number of unique sequences (100 million in the current implementation). Thus reverse-engineering is not realistically possible.
Proposed applications include authentication of art works and anti-counterfeit tagging of medications by applying the DNA pools to the individual doses of medications themselves. Since the verification of the sample requires laboratory equipment and takes several hours to complete, the DNA-pool based CUF is not currently applicable to time-sensitive areas like digital encryption.
