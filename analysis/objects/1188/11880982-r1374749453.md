# Material nonimplication

## o1 subject: Material nonimplication

Material nonimplication or abjunction (la) is a term referring to a logic operation used in generic circuits and Boolean algebra. It is the negation of material implication. That is to say that for any two propositions $P$ and $Q$, the material nonimplication from $P$ to $Q$ is true if and only if the negation of the material implication from $P$ to $Q$ is true. This is more naturally stated as that the material nonimplication from $P$ to $Q$ is true only if $P$ is true and $Q$ is false.

### Logical equivalences
Material nonimplication may be defined as the negation of material implication.
| $P \nrightarrow Q$ | $\Leftrightarrow$ | $\neg (P \rightarrow Q)$
| | $\Leftrightarrow$ | $\neg$
In classical logic, it is also equivalent to the negation of the disjunction of $\neg P$ and $Q$, and also the conjunction of $P$ and $\neg Q$
| $P \nrightarrow Q$ | $\Leftrightarrow$ | $\neg($ | $\neg P$ | $\lor$ | $Q)$ | $\Leftrightarrow$ | $P$ | $\land$ | $\neg Q$
