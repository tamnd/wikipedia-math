# Information dimension

## o1 subject: Information dimension

In information theory, information dimension is an information measure for random vectors in Euclidean space, based on the normalized entropy of finely quantized versions of the random vectors. This concept was first introduced by Alfréd Rényi in 1959.

## Definition and Properties
The entropy of a discrete random variable $Z$ is
$\mathbb{H}_0(Z)=\sum_{z \in supp(P_Z)}P_Z(z)\log_2\frac{1}{P_Z(z)}$
where $P_Z(z)$ is the probability measure of $Z$ when $Z=z$, and the $supp(P_Z)$ denotes a set $\{z|z \in\mathcal{Z},P_Z(z)>0\}$.
Let $X$ be an arbitrary real-valued random variable. Given a positive integer $m$, we create a new discrete random variable
$\langle X\rangle_m=\frac{\lfloor mX\rfloor}{m}$
where the $\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$ is the floor operator which converts a real number to the greatest integer less than it. Then
$\underline{d}(X)=\liminf_{m \rightarrow \infty}\frac{\mathbb{H}_0(\langle X \rangle_m)}{\log_2m}$
and
$\bar{d}(X)=\limsup_{m \rightarrow \infty}\frac{\mathbb{H}_0(\langle X \rangle_m)}{\log_2m}$
are called lower and upper information dimensions of $X$ respectively. When $\underline{d}(X)=\bar{d}(X)$, we call this value information dimension of $X$,
$d(X)=\lim_{m \rightarrow \infty}\frac{\mathbb{H}_0(\langle X \rangle_m)}{\log_2m}$
Some important properties of information dimension $d(X)$:
- If the mild condition $\mathbb{H}(\lfloor X\rfloor)<\infty$ is fulfilled, we have $0\leq\underline{d}(X)\leq\bar{d}(X)\leq1$.
- For an $n$-dimensional random vector $\vec{X}$, the first property can be generalized to $0\leq\underline{d}(\vec{X})\leq \bar{d}(\vec{X})\leq n$.
- It is sufficient to calculate the upper and lower information dimensions when restricting to the exponential subsequence $m=2^l$.
- $\underline{d}(X)$ and $\bar{d}(X)$ are kept unchanged if rounding or ceiling functions are used in quantization.

## o7 definition: An equivalent definition of Information Dimension

## An equivalent definition of Information Dimension
In 1994, Kawabata and Dembo in proposed a new way of measuring information based on rate distortion value of a random variable. The measure is defined as
$d_R(X)=-2\frac{R(X, D)}{\log D},$
where $R(X, D)$ is the rate-distortion function that is defined as
$R(X, D) = \min_{\|X-\hat{X}\|_2\leq D} I(X, \hat{X}),$
or equivalently, minimum information that could lead to a $D$-close approximation of $X$.
They further, proved that such definition is equivalent to the definition of information dimension. Formally,
$d_R(X) = d(X).$

### Example
Consider a signal which has a Gaussian probability distribution.
We pass the signal through a half-wave rectifier which converts all negative value to 0, and maintains all other values. The half-wave rectifier can be characterized by the function$f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,& \text{if } x\geq 0\\
0,&x<0
\end{cases}$
Then, at the output of the rectifier, the signal has a rectified Gaussian distribution. It is characterized by an atomic mass of weight 0.5 and has a Gaussian PDF for all $x>0$.
With this mixture distribution, we apply the formula above and get the information dimension $d$ of the distribution and calculate the $d$-dimensional entropy.$d(X)=\rho=0.5$The normalized right part of the zero-mean Gaussian distribution has entropy $h(P_{Xc})=\frac{1}{2}\log_2(2\pi e\sigma^2)-1$, hence
$\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{H}_{0.5}(X)&=(1-0.5)(1\log_21)+0.5h(P_{Xc})+\mathbb{H}_0(0.5)\\
&=0+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}\log_2(2\pi e\sigma^2)-1)+1\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\log_2(2\pi e\sigma^2)+\frac{1}{2}\,\text{ bit(s)}
\end{aligned}$
