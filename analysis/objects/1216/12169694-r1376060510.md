# Geometric measure theory

## o1 subject: Geometric measure theory

In mathematics, geometric measure theory (GMT) is the study of geometric properties of sets (typically in Euclidean space) through measure theory. It allows mathematicians to extend tools from differential geometry to a much larger class of surfaces that are not necessarily smooth.

## Examples
The Brunn–Minkowski inequality for the n-dimensional volumes of convex bodies K and L,
$$\mathrm{vol} \big( (1 - \lambda) K + \lambda L \big)^{1/n} \geq (1 - \lambda) \mathrm{vol} (K)^{1/n} + \lambda \, \mathrm{vol} (L)^{1/n},$$
can be proved on a single page and quickly yields the classical isoperimetric inequality. The Brunn–Minkowski inequality also leads to Anderson's theorem in statistics. The proof of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality predates modern measure theory; the development of measure theory and Lebesgue integration allowed connections to be made between geometry and analysis, to the extent that in an integral form of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality known as the Prékopa–Leindler inequality the geometry seems almost entirely absent.
### Gauss curvature measure
Consider the Gauss map, which sends each point on a smooth surface to the unit vector orthogonal to its local tangent plane. This can be generalized. Let $K$ be a closed surface in $\mathbb{R}^n$ containing the origin, and enclosing a convex region, then each point on $K$ has one or more local supporting planes. For any $z \in K$, define $N_K(z)$ to be the set of orthogonal directions to all local supporting planes to $K$ at $z$.
Now we define the multivalued Gauss map $G_K: \mathbb S^{n-1} \to \mathbb S^{n-1}$ defined by
$$G_K(x) = N_K(\rho(x) x)$$
where $\rho(x) x$ is the intersection of the ray $0 \to x$ with $K$. This map then maps the uniform measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb S^{n-1}$ to another measure $\mathbb S^{n-1}$ by
$$\mu_K (E) := \mu(G_K(E))$$
for any Borel set. This is the Gaussian curvature measure associated with $K$. It is a Borel measure for any $K$.
This then induces the inverse problem: Given a Borel measure on the sphere, is it a Gaussian curvature measure? Alexandrov showed the following:
- Given a Borel measure $\nu$, there exists some such $K$ such that $\nu = \mu_K$ iff
$$\begin{cases}
v\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right) \\
v\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-1} \backslash \omega\right)>\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\omega^*\right),\;\; \forall \omega \subseteq \mathbb{S}^{n-1} \text { compact and convex. }\end{cases}$$
- If such a $K$ exists, then it is unique up to dilation.
Such $K$ can be constructed as follows: Approximate $\nu$ as the weak limit of a sequence of point-mass measures. Each is the Gaussian curvature measure of a polytope, and the sequence of polytopes converge to $K$.
If $\mu_K$ has a bounded probability density function (in the sense of a Radon–Nikodym derivative):
$$\frac{d\mu_K}{d\mu}(y) = \rho(y), \quad\rho: \mathbb S^{n-1} \to [0, c]$$
then $K$ is continuously differentiable (that is, its tangent planes are unique and varies continuously over its surface).
### Surface area measure
Similarly, we define the surface area measure of $K$ by
$$\nu_K (E) := \mu(N_K^{-1}(E))$$
for any Borel set. It is a Borel measure for any $K$. This has an intuitive picture in as follows: Consider an open set $U \subset \mathbb S^{n-1}$ with a single connected continuous boundary $\partial U$. For each $\hat n \in \partial U$, produce the supporting plane of $K$ perpendicular to $\hat n$. As $\hat n$ runs over $\partial U$, the supporting planes rub all over $K$ and the contact points produces a $(n-2)$-dimensional closed subset of $K$ that encloses a $(n-1)$-dimensional region of $K$. Then $\nu_K(U)$ is its measure.
For example, if $K$ is a rectangle of side lengths $2a, 2b$ in the plane, then $\mu_K$ is the sum of four equal point mass measures
$$\frac 14 (\delta_{(a, b)/\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} + \delta_{(-a, b)/\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} + \delta_{(a, -b)/\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}} + \delta_{(-a, -b)/\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}})$$
but $\nu_K$ is the sum of four point mass measures $2b \delta_{(1, 0)} + 2b \delta_{(-1, 0)} + 2a \delta_{(0, 1)} + 2a \delta_{(0, -1)}$.
### Others
Given a convex function $u: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ where $\Omega$ is an open subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$, its associated Monge–Ampère measure $\mu_u$ on $\Omega$ is defined as $\mu_u(E) := \mu(\cap_{x \in \Omega} \partial u(x))$, where $\partial$ is the subdifferential function, and $\mu$ is the Lebesque measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$. This has applications in the study of weak solutions to the Monge–Ampère equation. It is a nonnegative, locally finite, Borel measure.
