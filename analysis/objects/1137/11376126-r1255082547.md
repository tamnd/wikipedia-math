# Principal axis theorem

## o1 subject: Principal axis theorem

In geometry and linear algebra, a principal axis is a certain line in a Euclidean space associated with a ellipsoid or hyperboloid, generalizing the major and minor axes of an ellipse or hyperbola. The principal axis theorem states that the principal axes are perpendicular, and gives a constructive procedure for finding them.

## Formal statement
The principal axis theorem concerns quadratic forms in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ which are homogeneous polynomials of degree 2. Any quadratic form may be represented as
$$Q(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}^\textsf{T} \mathbf{Ax}$$
where $\mathbf{A}$ is a symmetric matrix.
The first part of the theorem is contained in the following statements guaranteed by the spectral theorem:
- The eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$ are real.
- $\mathbf{A}$ is diagonalizable, and the eigenspaces of $\mathbf{A}$ are mutually orthogonal.
In particular, $\mathbf{A}$ is orthogonally diagonalizable, since one may take a basis of each eigenspace and apply the Gram-Schmidt process separately within the eigenspace to obtain an orthonormal eigenbasis.
For the second part, suppose that the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}$ are $\lambda_{1}, ..., \lambda_{n}$ (possibly repeated according to their algebraic multiplicities) and the corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis is $\mathbf{u}_{1}, ..., \mathbf{u}_{n}$. Then,
$$\mathbf{c} = [\mathbf{u}_1, \ldots,\mathbf{u}_n]^\textsf{T} \mathbf{x},$$
and
$$Q(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda_1 c_1^2 + \lambda_2 c_2^2 + \dots + \lambda_n c_n^2,$$
where $c_{i}$ is the $i$-th entry of $\mathbf{c}$. Furthermore,
The $i$-th principal axis is the line determined by equating $c_{j} = 0$ for all $j = 1, ..., i - 1, i + 1, ..., n$. The $i$-th principal axis is the span of the vector $\mathbf{u}_{i}$.
