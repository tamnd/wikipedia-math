# Ball covariance

## o1 subject: Ball covariance

Ball covariance is a statistical measure that can be used to test the independence of two random variables defined on metric spaces. The ball covariance is zero if and only if two random variables are independent, making it a good measure of correlation. Its significant contribution lies in proposing an alternative measure of independence in metric spaces. Prior to this, distance covariance in metric spaces could only detect independence for distance types with strong negative type. However, ball covariance can determine independence for any distance measure.

## Definition
### Ball covariance
Next, we will introduce ball covariance in detail, starting with the definition of a ball. Suppose two Banach spaces: $(\mathbf{X}, \rho)$ and $(\mathbf{Y}, \zeta)$, where the norms $\rho$ and $\zeta$ also represent their induced distances. Let $\theta$ be a Borel probability measure on $\mathbf{X} \times \mathbf{Y}, \mu, \nu$ be two Borel probability measures on $\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}$, and $(X, Y)$ be a $B$-valued random variable defined on a probability space such that $(X, Y) \sim \theta, X \sim \mu$, and $Y \sim \nu$. Denote the closed ball with the center $x_1$ and the radius $\rho\left(x_1, x_2\right)$ in $\mathbf{X}$ as $\bar{B}\left(x_1, \rho\left(x_1, x_2\right)\right)$ or $\bar{B}_\rho\left(x_1, x_2\right)$, and the closed ball with the center $y_1$ and the radius $\zeta\left(y_1, y_2\right)$ in $\mathbf{Y}$ as $\bar{B}\left(y_1, \zeta\left(y_1, y_2\right)\right)$ or $\bar{B}_\zeta\left(y_1, y_2\right)$. Let $\left\{W_i=\left(X_i, Y_i\right), i=1,2, \ldots\right\}$ be an infinite sequence of iid samples of $(X, Y)$, and $\omega=\left(\omega_1, \omega_2\right)$ be the positive weight function on the support set of $\theta$. Then, the population ball covariance can be defined as follows:
$\operatorname{BCov}_\omega^2(X, Y)=\int[\theta-\mu \otimes \nu]^2\left(\bar{B}_\rho\left(x_1, x_2\right) \times \bar{B}_\zeta\left(y_1, y_2\right)\right) \omega_1\left(x_1, x_2\right) \omega_2\left(y_1, y_2\right) \theta\left(d x_1, d y_1\right) \theta\left(d x_2, d y_2\right)$
where$[ \theta-\mu \otimes \nu ]^2 (A \times B):=[\theta(A \times B)-\mu(A) v(B)]$ for $A\in\mathbf{X}$ and $B\in\mathbf{Y}$.
Next, we will introduce another form of population ball covariance. Suppose $\delta_{i j, k}^X:=I\left(X_k \in \bar{B}_\rho\left(X_i, X_j\right)\right)$ which indicates whether $X_k$ is located in the closed ball $\bar{B}_\rho\left(X_i, X_j\right)$. Then, let $\delta_{i j, k l}^X=\delta_{i j, k}^X\delta_{i j, l}^X$ means whether both $X_k$ and $X_l$ is located in $\bar{B}_\rho\left(X_i, X_j\right)$, and $\xi_{i j, k l s t}^X=\left(\delta_{i j, k l}^X+\delta_{i j, s t}^X-\delta_{i j, k s}^X-\delta_{i j, l t}^X\right) / 2$. So does $\delta_{i j, k}^Y$, $\delta_{i j, k l}^Y$ and $\xi_{i j, k l s t}^Y$ for $Y$. Then, let $(X_i,Y_i)$, $i=1,2,\dots,6$ be iid samples from $\theta$. Another form of population ball covariance can be shown as
$\operatorname{BCov}_\omega^2(X, Y)=E\left\{\xi_{12,3456}^X \xi_{12,3456}^Y \omega_1\left(X_1, X_2\right) \omega_2\left(Y_1, Y_2\right)\right\}$
Now, we can finally express the sample ball covariance. Consider the random sample $(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}={X_k,Y_k}, k=1,\ldots,n)$. Let $\hat{\omega}_{1,n}$ and $\hat{\omega}_{2,n}$ be the estimate of ${\omega}_{1}$ and ${\omega}_{2}$. Denote
$\Delta_{i j, n}^{X Y}=\frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \delta_{i j, k}^X \delta_{i j, k}^Y, \Delta_{i j, n}^X=\frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \delta_{i j, k}^X, \Delta_{i j, n}^Y=\frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^n \delta_{i j, k}^Y,$
the sample ball covariance is
$\mathbf{B C o v}_{\omega, n}^2(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}):=\frac{1}{n^2} \sum_{i, j=1}^n\left(\Delta_{i j, n}^{X Y}-\Delta_{i j, n}^X \Delta_{i j, n}^Y\right)^2 \times \hat{\omega}_{1, n}\left(X_i, X_j\right) \hat{\omega}_{2, n}\left(Y_i, Y_j\right) .$
### Ball correlation
Just like the relationship between the Pearson correlation coefficient and covariance, we can define the ball correlation coefficient through ball covariance. The ball correlation is defined as the square root of
$\operatorname{BCor}_\omega^2(X, Y):=\operatorname{BCov}_\omega^2(X, Y) / \sqrt{\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(X) \mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(Y)},$
where
$\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(X)=\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(X,X)= E\left(\xi_{12,3456}^X \omega_1\left(X_1, X_2\right) \right)^2 ,$
and
$\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(Y)=\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(Y,Y)= E\left(\xi_{12,3456}^Y \omega_1\left(Y_1, Y_2\right) \right)^2 .$
And the sample ball correlation is defined similarly,
$\operatorname{BCor}_{ \omega, n }^2(X, Y):=\operatorname{BCov}_{ \omega, n }^2(X, Y) / \sqrt{\mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(X) \mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(Y)},$
where
$\mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(X)=\mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(X,X),$
and
$\mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(Y)=\mathbf{B C o v}_{ \omega, n }^2(Y,Y).$

## Properties
1.Independence-zero equivalence property: Let $S_\theta$, $S_\mu$ and $S_\nu$ denote the support sets of $\theta$, $\mu$ and $\nu$, respectively. $\operatorname{BCov}_\omega(X, Y)=0$ implies $\theta = \mu \otimes \nu$ if one of the following conditions establish:
(a).$\mathbf{X} \times \mathbf{Y}$ is a finite dimensional Banach space with $S_\theta=S_\mu \times S_\nu$.
(b).$\theta = a_1 \theta_d + a_2 \theta_a$, where $a_1$ and $a_2$ are positive constants, $\theta_d$ is a discrete measure, and $\theta_a$ is an absolutely continuous measure with a continues Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to the Gaussian measure.
2.Cauchy–Schwarz type inequality: $\operatorname{BCov}_\omega^2(X, Y) \leq \operatorname{BCov}_\omega(X) \operatorname{BCov}_\omega(X)$
3.Consistence: If $\hat{\omega}_{1,n}$ and $\hat{\omega}_{2,n}$ uniformly converge ${\omega}_{1}$ and ${\omega}_{2}$ with $E(\omega_1 \omega_2)<\infty$ respectively, we have $\operatorname{BCov}_{\omega, n}(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \underset{n \rightarrow \infty} {\stackrel{a . s .} {\longrightarrow}} \operatorname{BCov}_\omega(X, Y)$ and $\operatorname{BCor}_{\omega, n}(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \underset{n \rightarrow \infty} {\stackrel{a . s .} {\longrightarrow}} \operatorname{BCor}_\omega(X, Y)$.
4.Asymptotics: If $\hat{\omega}_{1,n}$ and $\hat{\omega}_{2,n}$ uniformly converge ${\omega}_{1}$ and ${\omega}_{2}$ with $E(\omega_1 \omega_2)<\infty$ respectively,
(a)under the null hypothesis, we have $n \mathbf{B C o v}{ }_{\omega, n}^2(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y}) \xrightarrow [n \rightarrow \infty]{d} \sum_{v=1}^{\infty} \lambda_v Z_v^2$, where $Z_v$ are independent standard normal random variables.
(b)under the alternative hypothesis, we have $\sqrt{n}\left(\mathbf{B C o v}_{\omega, n}^2(\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{Y})-\mathbf{B C o v}_\omega^2(X, Y)\right)
\xrightarrow [n \rightarrow \infty]{d} N(0, \Sigma)$.
