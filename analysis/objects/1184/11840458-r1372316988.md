# John ellipsoid

## o1 subject: John ellipsoid

In mathematics, the John ellipsoid or Löwner–John ellipsoid $E(K)$ associated to a convex body $K$ in $n$-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ can refer to the $n$-dimensional ellipsoid of maximal volume contained within $K$ or the ellipsoid of minimal volume that contains $K$.

## o5 section: Properties

## o6 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
The inner Löwner–John ellipsoid $E(K)$ of a convex body $K \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a closed unit ball $B$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ if and only if $B \subseteq K$ and there exists an integer $m \ge n$ and, for $i = 1, ..., m$, real numbers $c_{i} > 0$ and unit vectors $u_i \in S^{n-1} \cap \partial K$ (where $S$ is the unit n-sphere) such that
$$\sum_{i = 1}^m c_i u_i = 0$$
and, for all $x \in \mathbb{R}^n:$
$$x = \sum_{i = 1}^m c_i (x \cdot u_i) u_i.$$

## Computation
In general, computing the John ellipsoid of a given convex body is a hard problem. However, for some specific cases, explicit formulas are known. Some cases are particularly important for the ellipsoid method.
Let $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ be an ellipsoid in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ defined by a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and center $\mathbf{a}$. Let $\mathbf{c}$ be a nonzero vector in $\mathbb{R}^n.$ Let $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c})$ be the half-ellipsoid derived by cutting $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ at its center using the hyperplane defined by $\mathbf{c}$. Then, the Lowner-John ellipsoid of $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c})$ is an ellipsoid $E(\mathbf{A}, \mathbf{a})$ defined by:
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{a'} &= \mathbf{a} - \frac{1}{n+1} \mathbf{b} \\
\mathbf{A'} &= \frac{n^2}{n^2-1} \left(\mathbf{A} - \frac{2}{n+1} \mathbf{bb}^\mathrm{T} \right)
\end{aligned}$$
where $\mathbf{b}$ is a vector defined by:
$$\mathbf{b} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbf{c}^\mathrm{T} \mathbf{Ac}}} \mathbf{Ac}$$
Similarly, there are formulas for other sections of ellipsoids, not necessarily through its center.

## o11 section: Applications

## o12 application: Applications

## Applications
The computation of Löwner–John ellipsoids (and in more general, the computation of minimal-volume polynomial level sets enclosing a set) has found many applications in control and robotics. In particular, computing Löwner–John ellipsoids has applications in obstacle collision detection for robotic systems, where the distance between a robot and its surrounding environment is estimated using a best ellipsoid fit.
Löwner–John ellipsoids has also been used to approximate the optimal policy in portfolio optimization problems with transaction costs.
