# Diamond principle

## o1 subject: Diamond principle

In set theory, the diamond principle, denoted $\Diamond$, is a combinatorial principle introduced by Ronald Jensen that holds in the constructible universe and that implies the continuum hypothesis. Jensen extracted the diamond principle from his proof that the axiom of constructibility implies the existence of a Suslin tree.

## Definitions
The diamond principle $\lozenge$ says that there exists a ◊-sequence; that is, a family of sets $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$ for $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ such that for any subset $A$ of ω1 the set of $\alpha$ with $A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}$ is stationary in $\omega_{1}$.
There are several equivalent forms of the diamond principle. One states that there is a countable collection $\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ for each countable ordinal $\alpha$ such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is a stationary subset $C$ of $\omega_{1}$ such that for all $\alpha$ in $C$ we have $A \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ and $C \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$. Notice that, a weaken form which states that, there exist sets $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$ for $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is at least one infinite $\alpha$ with $A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}$ , is equivalent to the Continuum Hypothesis.
More generally, for a given cardinal number $\kappa$ and a stationary set $S \subseteq \kappa$, the statement $\lozenge_{S}$ (sometimes written $\lozenge(S)$ or $\lozenge_{\kappa}(S)$) is the statement that there is a sequence $\langle A_{\alpha} : \alpha \in S\rangle$ such that
- each $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$
- for every $A \subseteq \kappa$, $\{\alpha \in S : A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}\}$ is stationary in $\kappa$
The principle $\lozenge_{\omega_{1}}$ is the same as $\lozenge$.
The diamond-plus principle $\lozenge^{+}$ states that there exists a $\lozenge^{+}$-sequence, in other words a countable collection $\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ for each countable ordinal α such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is a closed unbounded subset $C$ of $\omega_{1}$ such that for all $\alpha$ in $C$ we have $A \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ and $C \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$.
