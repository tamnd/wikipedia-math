# Yff center of congruence

## o1 subject: Yff center of congruence

In geometry, the Yff center of congruence is a special point associated with a triangle. This special point is a triangle center and Peter Yff initiated the study of this triangle center in 1987.

## o2 citation: Kimberling

## o3 section: Isoscelizer

## o4 pointer-item: Isoscelizer

## Isoscelizer
An isoscelizer of an angle $A$ in a triangle $\triangle ABC$ is a line through points $P_{1}, Q_{1}$, where $P_{1}$ lies on $AB$ and $Q_{1}$ on $AC$, such that the triangle $\triangle AP_{1}Q_{1}$ is an isosceles triangle. An isoscelizer of angle $A$ is a line perpendicular to the bisector of angle $A$. Isoscelizers were invented by Peter Yff in 1963.

## o6 section: Yff central triangle

## o7 pointer-item: Yff central circle

## Yff central triangle
Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Let $P_{1}Q_{1}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $A$, $P_{2}Q_{2}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $B$, and $P_{3}Q_{3}$ be an isoscelizer of angle $C$. Let $\triangle A'B'C'$ be the triangle formed by the three isoscelizers. The four triangles $\triangle A'P_{2}Q_{3}, \triangle Q_{1}B'P_{3}, \triangle P_{1}Q_{2}C',$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are always similar.
There is a unique set of three isoscelizers $P_{1}Q_{1}, P_{2}Q_{2}, P_{3}Q_{3}$ such that the four triangles $\triangle A'P_{2}Q_{3}, \triangle Q_{1}B'P_{3}, \triangle P_{1}Q_{2}C',$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ are congruent. In this special case $\triangle A'B'C'$ formed by the three isoscelizers is called the Yff central triangle of $\triangle ABC$.
The circumcircle of the Yff central triangle is called the Yff central circle of the triangle.

## o10 section: Properties

## o11 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
- The trilinear coordinates of the Yff center of congruence are
$$\sec\frac{A}{2} : \sec\frac{B}{2} : \sec\frac{C}{2}$$
- Any triangle $\triangle ABC$ is the triangle formed by the lines which are externally tangent to the three excircles of the Yff central triangle of $\triangle ABC$.
- Let $I$ be the incenter of $\triangle ABC$. Let $D$ be the point on side $BC$ such that $\angle BID = \angle DIC$, $E$ a point on side $CA$ such that $\angle CIE = \angle EIA$, and $F$ a point on side $AB$ such that $\angle AIF = \angle FIB$. Then the lines $AD, BE, CF$ are concurrent at the Yff center of congruence. This fact gives a geometrical construction for locating the Yff center of congruence.
- A computer assisted search of the properties of the Yff central triangle has generated several interesting results relating to properties of the Yff central triangle.

## o14 section: Generalization

## o15 statement/generalization: Generalization

## Generalization
The geometrical construction for locating the Yff center of congruence has an interesting generalization. The generalisation begins with an arbitrary point $P$ in the plane of a triangle $\triangle ABC$. Then points $D, E, F$ are taken on the sides $BC, CA, AB$ such that
$$\angle BPD = \angle DPC, \quad \angle CPE = \angle EPA, \quad \angle APF = \angle FPB.$$
The generalization asserts that the lines $AD, BE, CF$ are concurrent.
