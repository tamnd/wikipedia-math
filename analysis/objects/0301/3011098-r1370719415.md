# Euler method

## o1 subject: Euler method

In mathematics and computational science, the Euler method (also called the forward Euler method) is a first-order numerical procedure for solving ordinary differential equations (ODEs) with a given initial value. It is the most basic explicit method for numerical integration of ordinary differential equations and is the simplest Runge–Kutta method. The Euler method is named after Leonhard Euler, who first proposed it in his book Institutionum calculi integralis (published 1768–1770).

### Example
If we have the differential equation $y' = 1+ ( t-y)^2$, and the exact solution $y = t+ \frac{1}{t-1}$, and we want to find $M$ and $L$ for when $2 \le t \le 3$.
$$\begin{aligned}
L &= \max \left| \frac{d}{dy} f(t,y) \right|
= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| \frac{d}{dy} \left[1 + \left( t-y\right)^2 \right] \right| \\[1ex]
&= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| 2 \left( t - y\right) \right|
= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| 2 \left( t - \left[t + \frac{1}{t-1}\right]\right) \right| \\[1ex]
&= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| - \frac{2}{t-1} \right| = 2
\end{aligned}$$
$$\begin{aligned}
M &= \max \left|\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\left[y(t)\right]\right| \\
&= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| \frac{d^2}{dt^2} \left( t + \frac{1}{1-t} \right) \right| \\
&= \max_{2 \le t \le 3} \left| \frac{2}{\left(-t+1\right)^3} \right| = 2
\end{aligned}$$
Thus we can find the error bound at t=2.5 and h=0.5:
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{error bound} &= \frac{hM}{2L}\left(e^{L(t_i-t_0)}-1\right) \\[1ex]
&= \frac{0.5 \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 2}\left(e^{2(2.5-2)}-1\right) = 0.42957
\end{aligned}$$
Notice that t0 is equal to 2 because it is the lower bound for t in $2 \le t \le 3$.
