# Karamata's inequality

## o1 subject: Karamata's inequality

In mathematics, Karamata's inequality, named after Jovan Karamata, also known as the majorization inequality, is a theorem in elementary algebra for convex and concave real-valued functions, defined on an interval of the real line. It generalizes the discrete form of Jensen's inequality, and generalizes in turn to the concept of Schur-convex functions.

## o5 statement: Statement of the inequality

## Statement of the inequality
Let $I$ be an interval of the real line and let $f$ denote a real-valued, convex function defined on $I$. If $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}$ are numbers in $I$ such that $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ majorizes $(y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n})$, then
:
$f(x_1)+\cdots+f(x_n) \ge f(y_1)+\cdots+f(y_n).$
Here majorization means that $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, \ldots, y_{n}$ satisfies
:
$x_1\ge x_2\ge\cdots\ge x_n$ and $y_1\ge y_2\ge\cdots\ge y_n,$
and we have the inequalities
:
$x_1+\cdots+x_i\ge y_1+\cdots+y_i$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$.
and the equality
:
$x_1+\cdots+x_n=y_1+\cdots+y_n$
If $f$ is a strictly convex function, then the inequality ((1)) holds with equality if and only if $y$ is a permutation of $x$.
### Weak majorization case
$x \preceq_w y$ if and only if $\sum g\left(x_i\right) \leq \sum g\left(y_i\right)$ for any continuous increasing convex function $g: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$.

## o14 section: Example

## o15 example: Example

## Example
The finite form of Jensen's inequality is a special case of this result. Consider the real numbers $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n} \in I$ and let
$a := \frac{x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n}{n}$
denote their arithmetic mean. Then $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ majorizes the $n$-tuple $(a, a, \ldots, a)$, since the arithmetic mean of the $i$ largest numbers of $(x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n})$ is at least as large as the arithmetic mean $a$ of all the $n$ numbers, for every $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. By Karamata's inequality ((1)) for the convex function $f$,
$f(x_1)+f(x_2)+ \cdots +f(x_n) \ge f(a)+f(a)+\cdots+f(a) = nf(a).$
Dividing by $n$ gives Jensen's inequality. The sign is reversed if $f$ is concave.

## o17 proof: Proof of the inequality

## Proof of the inequality
We may assume that the numbers are in decreasing order as specified in ((2)).
If $x_{i} = y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, then the inequality ((1)) holds with equality, hence we may assume in the following that $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for at least one $i$.
If $x_{i} = y_{i}$ for an $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, then the inequality ((1)) and the majorization properties ((3)) and ((4)) are not affected if we remove $x_{i}$ and $y_{i}$. Hence we may assume that $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$.
It is a property of convex functions that for two numbers $x \ne y$ in the interval $I$ the slope
$\frac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}$
of the secant line through the points $(x, f\,(x))$ and $(y, f\,(y))$ of the graph of $f$ is a monotonically non-decreasing function in $x$ for $y$ fixed (and vice versa). This implies that
:
$c_{i+1}:=\frac{f(x_{i+1})-f(y_{i+1})}{x_{i+1}-y_{i+1}}\le\frac{f(x_i)-f(y_i)}{x_i-y_i}=:c_i$
for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. Define $A_{0} = B_{0} = 0$ and
$A_i=x_1+\cdots+x_i,\qquad B_i=y_1+\cdots+y_i$
for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$. By the majorization property ((3)), $A_{i} \ge B_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$ and by ((4)), $A_{n} = B_{n}$. Hence,
:
$\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i=1}^n \bigl(f(x_i) - f(y_i)\bigr)
&=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i (x_i - y_i)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i \bigl(\underbrace{A_i - A_{i-1}}_{=\,x_i}{} - (\underbrace{B_i - B_{i-1}}_{=\,y_i})\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i (A_i - B_i) - \sum_{i=1}^n c_i (A_{i-1} - B_{i-1})\\
&=c_n (\underbrace{A_n-B_n}_{=\,0}) + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\underbrace{c_i - c_{i + 1}}_{\ge\,0})(\underbrace{A_i - B_i}_{\ge\,0}) - c_1(\underbrace{A_0-B_0}_{=\,0})\\
&\ge0,
\end{aligned}$
which proves Karamata's inequality ((1)).
To discuss the case of equality in ((1)), note that $x_{1} > y_{1}$ by ((3)) and our assumption $x_{i} \ne y_{i}$ for all $i \in \{1, \ldots, n - 1\}$. Let $i$ be the smallest index such that $(x_{i}, y_{i}) \ne (x_{i+1}, y_{i+1})$, which exists due to ((4)). Then $A_{i} > B_{i}$. If $f$ is strictly convex, then there is strict inequality in ((6)), meaning that $c_{i+1} < c_{i}$. Hence there is a strictly positive term in the sum on the right hand side of ((7)) and equality in ((1)) cannot hold.
If the convex function $f$ is non-decreasing, then $c_{n} \ge 0$. The relaxed condition ((5)) means that $A_{n} \ge B_{n}$, which is enough to conclude that $c_{n}(A_{n}-B_{n}) \ge 0$ in the last step of ((7)).
If the function $f$ is strictly convex and non-decreasing, then $c_{n} > 0$. It only remains to discuss the case $A_{n} > B_{n}$. However, then there is a strictly positive term on the right hand side of ((7)) and equality in ((1)) cannot hold.
