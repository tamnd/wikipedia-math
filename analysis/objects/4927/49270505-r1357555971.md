# Hafnian

## o1 subject: Hafnian

Hafnium
In mathematics, the hafnian is a scalar function of a symmetric matrix that generalizes the permanent.

## o2 citation

## o3 section: Definition

## o4 definition: Definition

## Definition
The hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$ is defined as
$\operatorname{haf}(A) = \sum_{\rho \in P_{2n}^2} \prod_{\{i,j\} \in \rho} A_{i,j},$
where $P_{2n}^2$ is the set of all partitions of the set $\{1, 2, \dots, 2n\}$ into subsets of size $2$.
This definition is similar to that of the Pfaffian, but differs in that the signatures of the permutations are not taken into account. Thus the relationship of the hafnian to the Pfaffian is the same as relationship of the permanent to the determinant.

## Basic properties
Besides its definition as a sum over perfect pairings, the hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$ can equivalently be written as
$\operatorname{haf}(A)=\frac{1}{n!2^n}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}A_{\sigma(2i-1),\sigma(2i)},$
where $S_{2n}$ is the symmetric group on $\{1,2,\dots,2n\}$.
An equivalent Levi-Civita representation holds for any even-dimensional symmetric matrix $V=(V_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}$:
$\operatorname{haf}(V)=\frac{1}{2^{N/2}(N/2)!}\sum_{i_1,\dots,i_N=1}^{N}|\epsilon^{i_1\cdots i_N}|V_{i_1 i_2}\cdots V_{i_{N-1}i_N},$
where $N$ is even.
The hafnian also admits a fermionic (Berezin integral) representation. If $V$ is a symmetric $2L\times 2L$ matrix and $\chi_i,\bar{\chi}_i$ are Grassmann variables, then
$\int D(\chi,\bar{\chi})\,\exp\!\left[\frac{1}{2}(\chi\bar{\chi})^{\mathsf T}V(\chi\bar{\chi})\right]=\operatorname{haf}(V),$
where
$(\chi\bar{\chi})^{\mathsf T}=\bigl(\bar{\chi}_1\chi_1,\dots,\bar{\chi}_{2L}\chi_{2L}\bigr)$
and
$D(\chi,\bar{\chi})=d\chi_1\,d\bar{\chi}_1\cdots d\chi_{2L}\,d\bar{\chi}_{2L}.$
For low-dimensional examples,
$\operatorname{haf}\!\begin{pmatrix}V_{11}&V_{12}\\V_{12}&V_{22}\end{pmatrix}=V_{12},$
and for a $4\times 4$ symmetric matrix,
$\operatorname{haf}\!\begin{pmatrix}
V_{11}&V_{12}&V_{13}&V_{14}\\
V_{12}&V_{22}&V_{23}&V_{24}\\
V_{13}&V_{23}&V_{33}&V_{34}\\
V_{14}&V_{24}&V_{34}&V_{44}
\end{pmatrix}
=
V_{12}V_{34}+V_{13}V_{24}+V_{14}V_{23}.$
A useful scaling property is the following. If $V=(V_{ij})$ and $S=(S_{ij})$ are symmetric matrices such that
$V_{ij}=g_i g_j S_{ij},$
then, for even $N$,
$\operatorname{haf}(V)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N} g_i\right)\operatorname{haf}(S).$
In particular, if $V=gS$ for a scalar $g$, then
$\operatorname{haf}(gS)=g^{N/2}\operatorname{haf}(S).$
The hafnian of the adjacency matrix of a graph counts its perfect matchings (also known as 1-factors). Indeed, a partition of $\{1,2,\dots,2n\}$ into subsets of size 2 is equivalent to a perfect matching of the complete graph $K_{2n}$.
The hafnian is closely related to the permanent. In particular,
$\operatorname{per}(C)=\operatorname{haf}\begin{pmatrix}
0 & C \\
C^\mathsf{T} & 0
\end{pmatrix}$.
Thus, just as the hafnian counts perfect matchings in a graph from its adjacency matrix, the permanent counts matchings in a bipartite graph from its biadjacency matrix.
The hafnian is also related to moments of multivariate Gaussian distributions. By Wick's probability theorem, for a real $2n\times 2n$ symmetric matrix $A$,
$\operatorname{haf}(A)=\mathbb E_{\left(X_1,\dots,X_{2n}\right)\sim\mathcal N\left(0,A+\lambda I\right)}\left[X_1\cdots X_{2n}\right],$
where $\lambda$ is any number large enough that $A+\lambda I$ is positive semi-definite. Since the hafnian does not depend on the diagonal entries of the matrix, the expectation on the right-hand side is independent of the choice of $\lambda$.

## Computation
Computing the hafnian of a (0,1)-matrix is #P-complete, because computing the permanent of a (0,1)-matrix is #P-complete.
The hafnian of a $2n\times 2n$ matrix can be computed in $O(n^3 2^n)$ time.
If the entries of a matrix are non-negative, then its hafnian can be approximated to within an exponential factor in polynomial time.
