## o31 statement: Euler's identity and roots of unity

### Euler's identity and roots of unity
Euler's identity, $e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$, sometimes claimed to be "the most beautiful theorem in mathematics", becomes $e^{i\tau/2} + 1 = 0$ in terms of $\tau$. Hartl has asserted that $e^{i\tau} = 1$ (which he also called "Euler's identity") is a more fundamental and meaningful equation.
John Conway noted that Euler's identity is a specific case of the general formula of the $n$th roots of unity, $\sqrt[n]{1} = e^{i\tau k/n} (k = 1, 2, ..., n)$, which he maintained is preferable and more economical than Euler's identity.
