# Landau–Mignotte bound

## o1 subject: Landau–Mignotte bound

In algebra, a Landau–Mignotte bound (sometimes only referred to as Mignotte's bound) is one of a family of inequalities concerning a univariate integer polynomial f(x) and one of its factors h(x). A basic version states that the coefficients of h(x) are bounded independently of h(x) by an exponential expression involving only the degree and coefficients of f(x), i.e. only depending on f(x).

## o6 definition/notation: Notation

## Notation
$f,g,h\in\mathbb{C}[x]$ will be univariate complex polynomials which later will be restricted to be integer polynomials, i.e. in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. Explicitly
$f=\sum\limits_{i=0}^nf_ix^i,\ \ \
g=\sum\limits_{i=0}^mg_ix^i,\ \ \
h=\sum\limits_{i=0}^kh_ix^i.$
$n,m,k$ are the degrees, the leading coefficients are $f_n,g_m,h_k$.
Define norms by considering the coefficients as vectors, explicitly
$\|f\|_{\infty}=\max_{0\leq i\leq n}|f_i|,\ \ \
\|f\|_{2}=\left(\sum\limits_{i=0}^n|f_i|^2\right)^{1/2},\ \ \
\|f\|_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^n|f_i|.$
By the fundamental theorem of algebra $f$ has $n$ roots $z_1,z_2,\ldots,z_n$ (with multiplicity). Set the Mahler measure of $f$ to be
$M(f)=|f_n|\prod\limits_{i=1}^n\max\{1,|z_i|\}.$
Similarly define $\|g\|_{2}$, $M(h)$, etc.

## o7 section: Landau's inequality and other basic properties

## o8 statement: Landau's inequality and other basic properties

## Landau's inequality and other basic properties
Landau proved in 1905 a key inequality linking the Mahler measure of a polynomial to its Euclidean norm.
$M(f)\leq\|f\|_{2}$
In general norms obey the following inequalities
$\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\|f\|_{2}\leq(n+1)\|f\|_{\infty}.$
The Mahler measure satisfies $M(f)\geq|f_n|$ which for non-trivial integer polynomials implies $M(f)\geq 1$. See also Lehmer's conjecture.
The Mahler measure is multiplicative, i.e. if $f=gh$ then
$M(f)=M(g)M(h).$

## o19 statement/generalization: Generalizations

## Generalizations
Usually the Mignotte bounds are only stated for complex or integer polynomials. They are equally valid for any subring $R\subset\mathbb{C}$, in particular when considering only monic polynomials for which $\frac{|h_k|}{|f_n|}=1$.
Any abstract number field and its ring of integers can be considered a subring of $\mathbb{C}$, however there can be multiple embeddings which are inequivalent with respect to absolute values. The Mignotte bounds are abstract and general enough that they hold independent of the chosen embedding. This may be taken as a hint that they are not as tight as possible in principle, as can indeed be seen from competing bounds that are sometimes better.

## o20 section: Applications

## o21 application: Applications

## Applications
In computer algebra when doing effective computations with integer polynomials often the following strategy is applied. One reduces a polynomial $f$ modulo a suitable prime number $p$ to get $f_p$, solves a related problem over $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ instead of $\mathbb{Z}$ which is often simpler, and finally uses Hensel lifting to transfer the result for $f_p$ back to $f$.
Hensel lifting is an iterative process and it is in general not clear when to stop it. The Landau–Mignotte bounds can supply additional a priori information that makes it possible to give explicit bounds on how often Hensel lifting has to be iterated to recover the solution for $f$ from a solution for $f_p$.
In particular this can be applied to factoring integer polynomials or for computing the gcd of integer polynomials. Although effective, this approach may not be the most efficient, as can be seen in the case of factoring.
