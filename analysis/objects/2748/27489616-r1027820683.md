# Wiener algebra

## o1 subject: Wiener algebra

In mathematics, the Wiener algebra, named after Norbert Wiener and usually denoted by $A(\mathbf{T})$, is the space of absolutely convergent Fourier series. Here $\mathbf{T}$ denotes the circle group.

## o5 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
The sum of an absolutely convergent Fourier series is continuous, so
$A(\mathbb{T})\subset C(\mathbb{T})$
where $C(\mathbf{T})$ is the ring of continuous functions on the unit circle.
On the other hand an integration by parts, together with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Parseval's formula, shows that
$C^1(\mathbb{T})\subset A(\mathbb{T}).\,$
More generally,
$\mathrm{Lip}_\alpha(\mathbb{T})\subset A(\mathbb{T})\subset C(\mathbb{T})$
for $\alpha>1/2$ (see ).
