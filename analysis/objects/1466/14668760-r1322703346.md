# Alternating-direction implicit method

## o1 subject: Alternating-direction implicit method

In numerical linear algebra, the alternating-direction implicit (ADI) method is an iterative method used to solve Sylvester matrix equations. It is a popular method for solving the large matrix equations that arise in systems theory and control, and can be formulated to construct solutions in a memory-efficient, factored form. It is also used to numerically solve parabolic and elliptic partial differential equations, and is a classic method used for modeling heat conduction and solving the diffusion equation in two or more dimensions. It is an example of an operator splitting method.

## o10 definition/construction: The method

### The method
The ADI method is a two step iteration process that alternately updates the column and row spaces of an approximate solution to $AX - XB = C$. One ADI iteration consists of the following steps:1. Solve for $X^{(j + 1/2)}$, where $\left( A - \beta_{j +1} I\right) X^{(j+1/2)} = X^{(j)}\left( B - \beta_{j + 1} I \right) + C.$ 2. Solve for $X^{(j + 1)}$, where $X^{(j+1)}\left( B - \alpha_{j + 1} I \right) = \left( A - \alpha_{j+1} I\right) X^{(j+1/2)} - C$.
The numbers $(\alpha_{j+1}, \beta_{j+1})$ are called shift parameters, and convergence depends strongly on the choice of these parameters. To perform $K$ iterations of ADI, an initial guess $X^{(0)}$ is required, as well as $K$ shift parameters, $\{ (\alpha_{j}, \beta_{j})\}_{j = 1}^{K}$.

## o35 statement/generalization: Generalizations

### Generalizations
The usage of the ADI method as an operator splitting scheme can be generalized. That is, we may consider general evolution equations
$\dot u = F_1 u + F_2 u,$
where $F_1$ and $F_2$ are (possibly nonlinear) operators defined on a Banach space. In the diffusion example above we have $F_1 = {\partial^2 \over \partial x^2}$ and $F_2 = {\partial^2 \over \partial y^2}$.
