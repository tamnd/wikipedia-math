## o2 subject: Bijection, injection and surjection

In mathematics, injections, surjections, and bijections are classes of functions distinguished by the manner in which arguments (input expressions from the domain) and images (output expressions from the codomain) are related or mapped to each other.

## Examples
It is important to specify the domain and codomain of each function, since by changing these, functions which appear to be the same may have different properties.
Injective and surjective (bijective)
- The identity function idX for every non-empty set X, and thus specifically $\mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R} : x \mapsto x.$
- $\mathbf{R}^+ \to \mathbf{R}^+ : x \mapsto x^2$, and thus also its inverse $\mathbf{R}^+ \to \mathbf{R}^+ : x \mapsto \sqrt{x}.$
- The exponential function $\exp \colon \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}^+ : x \mapsto \mathrm{e}^x$ (that is, the exponential function with its codomain restricted to its image), and thus also its inverse the natural logarithm $\ln \colon \mathbf{R}^+ \to \mathbf{R} : x \mapsto \ln{x}.$ Here, $\mathbf{R}^+$ denotes the positive real numbers.

## o14 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
- For every function $f$, let $X$ be a subset of the domain and $Y$ a subset of the codomain. One has always $X \subset f^{-1}(f(X))$ and $f(f^{-1}(Y)) \subset Y$, where $f(X)$ is the image of $X$ and $f^{-1}(Y)$ is the preimage of $Y$ under $f$. If $f$ is injective, then $X = f^{-1}(f(X))$, and if $f$ is surjective, then $f(f^{-1}(Y)) = Y$.
- For every function $h: X \to Y$, one can define a surjection $H : X \to h(X) : x \mapsto h(x)$ and an injection $I : h(X) \to Y : y \mapsto y$. It follows that $h = I \circ H$. This decomposition as the composition of a surjection and an injection is unique up to an isomorphism, in the sense that, given such a decomposition, there is a unique bijection $\varphi:h(X)\to H(X)$ such that $H(x)=\varphi(h(x))$ and $I(\varphi(h(x)))=h(x)$ for every $x\in X.$
