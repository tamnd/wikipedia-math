# Presentation of a monoid

## o1 subject: Presentation of a monoid

In algebra, a presentation of a monoid (or a presentation of a semigroup) is a description of a monoid (or a semigroup) in terms of a set $\Sigma$ of generators and a set of relations on the free monoid $\Sigma^{\ast}$ (or the free semigroup $\Sigma^{+}$) generated by $\Sigma$. The monoid is then presented as the quotient of the free monoid (or the free semigroup) by these relations. This is an analogue of a group presentation in group theory.

## Construction
The relations are given as a (finite) binary relation $R$ on $\Sigma^{\ast}$. To form the quotient monoid, these relations are extended to monoid congruences as follows:
First, one takes the symmetric closure $R \cup R^{-1}$ of $R$. This is then extended to a symmetric relation $E \subset \Sigma^{\ast} \times \Sigma^{\ast}$ by defining $x \sim_{E} y$ if and only if $x$ = $sut$ and $y$ = $svt$ for some strings $u, v, s, t \in \Sigma^{\ast}$ with $(u,v) \in R \cup R^{-1}$. Finally, one takes the reflexive and transitive closure of $E$, which then is a monoid congruence.
In the typical situation, the relation $R$ is simply given as a set of equations, so that $R=\{u_1=v_1,\ldots,u_n=v_n\}$. Thus, for example,
$\langle p,q\,\vert\; pq=1\rangle$
is the equational presentation for the bicyclic monoid, and
$\langle a,b \,\vert\; aba=baa, bba=bab\rangle$
is the plactic monoid of degree 2 (it has infinite order). Elements of this plactic monoid may be written as $a^ib^j(ba)^k$ for integers i, j, k, as the relations show that ba commutes with both a and b.
