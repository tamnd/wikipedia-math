# Entropy rate

## o1 subject: Entropy rate

In the mathematical theory of probability, the entropy rate or source information rate of a stochastic process is, informally, the time density of the average information in a stochastic process. For stochastic processes with a countable index, the entropy rate $H(X)$ is the limit of the joint entropy of $n$ members of the process $X_k$ divided by $n$, as $n$ tends to infinity:

## Definition
A process $X$ with a countable index gives rise to the sequence of its joint entropies $H_n(X_1, X_2, \dots X_n)$. If the limit exists, the entropy rate is defined as
$H(X) := \lim_{n \to \infty} \tfrac{1}{n} H_n.$
Note that given any sequence $(a_n)_n$ with $a_0=0$ and letting $\Delta a_k := a_k - a_{k-1}$, by telescoping one has $a_n={\textstyle \sum_{k=1}^n}\Delta a_k$. The entropy rate thus computes the mean of the first $n$ such entropy changes, with $n$ going to infinity.
The $n$th entropy change is itself the conditional entropy $H(X_n | X_{n-1}, X_{n-2}, ...)$. The entropy rate is thus the average entropy of the distribution of the $n$th variable once the previous ones are known. The behaviour of joint entropies from one index to the next is also explicitly subject in some characterizations of entropy.
