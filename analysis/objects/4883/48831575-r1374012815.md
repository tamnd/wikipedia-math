# Fueter–Pólya theorem

## o1 subject: Fueter–Pólya theorem

The Fueter–Pólya theorem, first proved by Rudolf Fueter and George Pólya, states that the only quadratic polynomial pairing functions are the Cantor polynomials.

## o5 section: Statement

## o6 statement: Statement

### Statement
If $P$ is a real quadratic polynomial in two variables whose restriction to $\mathbb{N}^2$ is a bijection from $\mathbb{N}^2$ to $\mathbb{N}$ then it is
$P(x,y) := \frac{1}{2} ((x+y)^2+3x+y)$
or
$P(x,y) := \frac{1}{2} ((y+x)^2+3y+x).$

## o7 section: Proof

## o8 proof: Proof

### Proof
The original proof is surprisingly difficult, using the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem to prove the transcendence of
$e^a$ for a nonzero algebraic number $a$.
In 2002, M. A. Vsemirnov published an elementary proof of this result.

## o12 statement/conjecture: Fueter–Pólya conjecture

## Fueter–Pólya conjecture
The theorem states that the Cantor polynomial is the only quadratic pairing polynomial of $\mathbb{N}^2$ and $\mathbb{N}$. The conjecture is that these are the only such pairing polynomials, of any degree.
