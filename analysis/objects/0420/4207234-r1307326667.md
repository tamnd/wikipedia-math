# Dehn plane

## o1 subject: Dehn plane

In geometry, Max Dehn introduced two examples of planes, a semi-Euclidean geometry and a non-Legendrian geometry, that have infinitely many lines parallel to a given one that pass through a given point, but where the sum of the angles of a triangle is at least $\pi$. A similar phenomenon occurs in hyperbolic geometry, except that the sum of the angles of a triangle is less than $\pi$. Dehn's examples use a non-Archimedean field, so that the Archimedean axiom is violated. They were introduced by and discussed by .
