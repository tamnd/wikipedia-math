# Qvist's theorem

## o1 subject: Qvist's theorem

In projective geometry, Qvist's theorem, named after the Finnish mathematician Bertil Qvist, is a statement on ovals in finite projective planes. Standard examples of ovals are non-degenerate (projective) conic sections. The theorem gives an answer to the question How many tangents to an oval can pass through a point in a finite projective plane? The answer depends essentially upon the order (number of points on a line −1) of the plane.

## Statement and proof of Qvist's theorem
Qvist's theorem
Let $\Omega$ be an oval in a finite projective plane of order $n$.
(a) If $n$ is odd,
every point $P \notin \Omega$ is incident with 0 or 2 tangents.
(b) If $n$ is even,
there exists a point $N$, the nucleus or knot, such that, the set of tangents to oval $\Omega$ is the pencil of all lines through $N$.
Proof
(a) Let $t_{R}$ be the tangent to $\Omega$ at point $R$ and let $P_{1}, ... , P_{n}$ be the remaining points of this line. For each $i$, the lines through $P_{i}$ partition $\Omega$ into sets of cardinality 2 or 1 or 0. Since the number $\lvert \Omega\rvert = n + 1$ is even, for any point $P_{i}$, there must exist at least one more tangent through that point. The total number of tangents is $n + 1$, hence, there are exactly two tangents through each $P_{i}$, $t_{R}$ and one other. Thus, for any point $P$ not in oval $\Omega$, if $P$ is on any tangent to $\Omega$ it is on exactly two tangents.
(b) Let $s$ be a secant, $s \cap \Omega = \{P_{0}, P_{1}$} and $s= \{P_{0}, P_{1},...,P_{n}$}. Because $\lvert \Omega\rvert = n + 1$ is odd, through any $P_{i}, i = 2,...,n$, there passes at least one tangent $t_{i}$. The total number of tangents is $n + 1$. Hence, through any point $P_{i}$ for $i = 2,...,n$ there is exactly one tangent. If $N$ is the point of intersection of two tangents, no secant can pass through $N$. Because $n + 1$, the number of tangents, is also the number of lines through any point, any line through $N$ is a tangent.

the projective closure of the parabola $y = x^{2}$, is an oval with the point $N = (0)$ as nucleus (see image), i.e., any line $y = c$, with $c \in K$, is a tangent.

## o9 proof: Proof

Proof
(a) Let $t_{R}$ be the tangent to $\Omega$ at point $R$ and let $P_{1}, ... , P_{n}$ be the remaining points of this line. For each $i$, the lines through $P_{i}$ partition $\Omega$ into sets of cardinality 2 or 1 or 0. Since the number $\lvert \Omega\rvert = n + 1$ is even, for any point $P_{i}$, there must exist at least one more tangent through that point. The total number of tangents is $n + 1$, hence, there are exactly two tangents through each $P_{i}$, $t_{R}$ and one other. Thus, for any point $P$ not in oval $\Omega$, if $P$ is on any tangent to $\Omega$ it is on exactly two tangents.
(b) Let $s$ be a secant, $s \cap \Omega = \{P_{0}, P_{1}$} and $s= \{P_{0}, P_{1},...,P_{n}$}. Because $\lvert \Omega\rvert = n + 1$ is odd, through any $P_{i}, i = 2,...,n$, there passes at least one tangent $t_{i}$. The total number of tangents is $n + 1$. Hence, through any point $P_{i}$ for $i = 2,...,n$ there is exactly one tangent. If $N$ is the point of intersection of two tangents, no secant can pass through $N$. Because $n + 1$, the number of tangents, is also the number of lines through any point, any line through $N$ is a tangent.

## o12 example: Example

Example
For a projective plane over a finite field $K, \lvert K\rvert = n$ even and $n > 4$, the set
$\Omega_{1} = \{(x, y) | y = x^{2}\} \cup \{(\infty)$} is an oval (conic section) (see image),
$\overline{\Omega}_{1} = \{(x, y) | y = x^{2}\} \cup \{(0), (\infty)$} is a hyperoval and
$\Omega_{2} = \{(x, y) | y = x^{2}\} \cup \{(0)$} is another oval that is not a conic section. (Recall that a conic section is determined uniquely by 5 points.)
