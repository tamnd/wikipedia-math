# Spherical wedge

## o1 subject: Spherical wedge

In geometry, a spherical wedge or ungula is a portion of a ball bounded by two plane semidisks and a spherical lune (termed the wedge's base). The angle between the radii lying within the bounding semidisks is the dihedral $\alpha$. If $AB$ is a semidisk that forms a ball when completely revolved about the z-axis, revolving $AB$ only through a given $\alpha$ produces a spherical wedge of the same angle $\alpha$. Beman (2008) remarks that "a spherical wedge is to the sphere of which it is a part as the angle of the wedge is to a perigon." A spherical wedge of $\alpha = \pi$ radians (180°) is called a hemisphere, while a spherical wedge of $\alpha = \pi/2$ radians (90°) is sometimes called a semihemisphere (two octants); a spherical wedge of $\alpha = 2\pi$ radians (360°) constitutes a complete ball.
