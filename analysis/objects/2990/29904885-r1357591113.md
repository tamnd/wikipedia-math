# Double origin topology

## o1 subject: Double origin topology

In mathematics, more specifically general topology, the double origin topology is an example of a topology given to the plane $\mathbf{R}^{2}$ with an extra point, say $0*$, added. In this case, the double origin topology gives a topology on the set $X = \mathbf{R}^{2} \sqcup \{0*\}$, where $\sqcup$ denotes the disjoint union.

## o2 section: Construction

## o3 definition/construction: Construction

## Construction
Given a point $x$ belonging to $X$, such that $x \ne 0$ and $x \ne 0*$, the neighbourhoods of $x$ are those given by the standard metric topology on $\mathbf{R}^{2}-\{0\}.$ We define a countably infinite basis of neighbourhoods about the point 0 and about the additional point $0*$. For the point $0$, the basis, indexed by $n$, is defined to be:
$\ N(0,n) = \{ (x,y) \in {\mathbf R}^2 : x^2 + y^2 < 1/n^2, \ y > 0\} \cup \{0\} .$
In a similar way, the basis of neighbourhoods of $0*$ is defined to be:
$N(0^*,n) = \{ (x,y) \in {\mathbf R}^2 : x^2 + y^2 < 1/n^2, \ y < 0\} \cup \{0^*\} .$

## o5 section: Properties

## o6 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
The space $\mathbf{R}^{2} \sqcup \{0*\}$, along with the double origin topology is an example of a Hausdorff space, although it is not completely Hausdorff. Since it has a base of
regular open sets, it is an example of a semiregular space that is not regular. In terms of compactness, the space $\mathbf{R}^{2} \sqcup \{0*\}$, along with the double origin topology fails to be either compact, paracompact or locally compact, however, $X$ is second countable. Finally, it is an example of an arc connected space.
