# Biconjugate gradient method

## o1 subject: Biconjugate gradient method

In mathematics, more specifically in numerical linear algebra, the biconjugate gradient method is an algorithm to solve systems of linear equations

## o2 section: The Algorithm

## o3 definition/construction: The Algorithm

## The Algorithm
- Choose initial guess $x_0\,$, two other vectors $x_0^*$ and $b^*\,$ and a preconditioner $M\,$
- $r_0 \leftarrow b-A\, x_0\,$
- $r_0^* \leftarrow b^*-x_0^*\, A^*$
- $p_0 \leftarrow M^{-1} r_0\,$
- $p_0^* \leftarrow r_0^*M^{-1}\,$
- for $k=0, 1, \ldots$ do
$\alpha_k \leftarrow {r_k^* M^{-1} r_k \over p_k^* A p_k}\,$
$x_{k+1} \leftarrow x_k + \alpha_k \cdot p_k\,$
$x_{k+1}^* \leftarrow x_k^* + \overline{\alpha_k}\cdot p_k^*\,$
$r_{k+1} \leftarrow r_k - \alpha_k \cdot A p_k\,$
$r_{k+1}^* \leftarrow r_k^*- \overline{\alpha_k} \cdot p_k^*\, A^*$
$\beta_k \leftarrow {r_{k+1}^* M^{-1} r_{k+1} \over r_k^* M^{-1} r_k}\,$
$p_{k+1} \leftarrow M^{-1} r_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot p_k\,$
$p_{k+1}^* \leftarrow r_{k+1}^*M^{-1} + \overline{\beta_k}\cdot p_k^*\,$
In the above formulation, the computed $r_k\,$ and $r_k^*$ satisfy
$r_k = b - A x_k,\,$
$r_k^* = b^* - x_k^*\, A^*$
and thus are the respective residuals corresponding to $x_k\,$ and $x_k^*$, as approximate solutions to the systems
$A x = b,\,$
$x^*\, A^* = b^*\,;$
$x^*$ is the adjoint, and $\overline{\alpha}$ is the complex conjugate.
### Unpreconditioned version of the algorithm
- Choose initial guess $x_0\,$,
- $r_0 \leftarrow b-A\, x_0\,$
- $\hat{r}_0 \leftarrow \hat{b} - \hat{x}_0A^*$
- $p_0 \leftarrow r_0\,$
- $\hat{p}_0 \leftarrow \hat{r}_0\,$
- for $k=0, 1, \ldots$ do
$\alpha_k \leftarrow {\hat{r}_k r_k \over \hat{p}_k A p_k}\,$
$x_{k+1} \leftarrow x_k + \alpha_k \cdot p_k\,$
$\hat{x}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{x}_k + \alpha_k \cdot \hat{p}_k\,$
$r_{k+1} \leftarrow r_k - \alpha_k \cdot A p_k\,$
$\hat{r}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{r}_k- \alpha_k \cdot \hat{p}_k A^*$
$\beta_k \leftarrow {\hat{r}_{k+1} r_{k+1} \over \hat{r}_k r_k}\,$
$p_{k+1} \leftarrow r_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot p_k\,$
$\hat{p}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{r}_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot \hat{p}_k\,$

## o7 statement/fact: Properties

## Properties
- If $A= A^*\,$ is self-adjoint, $x_0^*= x_0$ and $b^*=b$, then $r_k= r_k^*$, $p_k= p_k^*$, and the conjugate gradient method produces the same sequence $x_k= x_k^*$ at half the computational cost.
- The sequences produced by the algorithm are biorthogonal, i.e., $p_i^*Ap_j=r_i^*M^{-1}r_j=0$ for $i \neq j$.
- if $P_{j'}\,$ is a polynomial with $\deg\left(P_{j'}\right)+j<k$, then $r_k^*P_{j'}\left(M^{-1}A\right)u_j=0$. The algorithm thus produces projections onto the Krylov subspace.
- if $P_{i'}\,$ is a polynomial with $i+\deg\left(P_{i'}\right)<k$, then $v_i^*P_{i'}\left(AM^{-1}\right)r_k=0$.
