# Albertson index

## o1 subject: Albertson index

The Albertson index (also known as irregularity or Albertson irregularity index) is a graph invariant that measures the irregularity of a graph by summing the imbalances of all its edges. It was introduced by Michael O. Albertson in 1997 as a single parameter to capture how "non-regular" a graph is.

## o2 citation: albertson1997

## o3 section: Definition

## o4 definition: Definition

## Definition
For a simple undirected graph $G = (V, E)$, the imbalance of an edge $e = {u,v}$ is defined as:
$\operatorname{imb}(e) = |\deg(u) - \deg(v)|$
where $\deg(u)$ and $\deg(v)$ denote the degrees of vertices $u$ and $v$.
The Albertson index of $G$, denoted $\operatorname{Alb}(G)$ (sometimes $\operatorname{I}(G)$ or $\operatorname{irr}(G)$ in the literature), is the sum of the imbalances of all edges:
$\operatorname{Alb}(G) = \sum_{uv \in E(G)} |\deg(u) - \deg(v)|$
A graph is regular if and only if its Albertson index is zero.
