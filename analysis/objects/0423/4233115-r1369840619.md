# Scottish Book

## o1 subject: Scottish Book

The Scottish Book () was a thick notebook used by mathematicians of the Lwów School of Mathematics in Poland for jotting down unsolved problems. The notebook was named after the Scottish Café, where it was kept.

## Continuity
After World War II, an English translation annotated by Ulam was published by Los Alamos National Laboratory in 1957. After World War II, Hugo Steinhaus at the University of Wrocław revived the tradition of the Scottish book with The New Scottish Book from 1945 to 1958.
The tradition of the Scottish Book continues to inspire not only mathematicians but also educators in other fields. Piotr Kowzan proposed a "goose method" as a pedagogical tool for marking open problems and encouraging future research. Inspired by the eccentric rewards in the Scottish Book, this approach aims to foster curiosity and knowledge-building across generations.
