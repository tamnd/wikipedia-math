# Integration by substitution

## o1 subject: Integration by substitution

In calculus, integration by substitution, also known as u-substitution, reverse chain rule or change of variables, is a method for evaluating integrals and antiderivatives. It is the counterpart to the chain rule for differentiation, and can loosely be thought of as using the chain rule "backwards." This involves differential forms.

## o9 proof: Proof

### Proof
Integration by substitution can be derived from the fundamental theorem of calculus as follows. Let $f$ and $g$ be two functions satisfying the above hypothesis that $f$ is continuous on $I$ and $g'$ is integrable on the closed interval $[a,b]$. Then the function $f(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$ is also integrable on $[a,b]$. Hence the integrals
$$\int_a^b f(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\ dx$$
and
$$\int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\ du$$
in fact exist, and it remains to show that they are equal.
Since $f$ is continuous, it has an antiderivative $F$. The composite function $F \circ g$ is then defined. Since $g$ is differentiable, combining the chain rule and the definition of an antiderivative gives:
$$(F \circ g)'(x) = F'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x).$$
Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus twice gives:
$$\begin{aligned}
\int_a^b f(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)\ dx
&= \int_a^b (F \circ g)'(x)\ dx \\
&= (F \circ g)(b) - (F \circ g)(a) \\
&= F(g(b)) - F(g(a)) \\
&= \int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} F'(u)\, du = \int_{g(a)}^{g(b)} f(u)\, du,
\end{aligned}$$
which is the substitution rule.

## o17 statement/theorem: Theorem

Let $U$ be an open set in $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ and $\varphi : U \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ an injective differentiable function with continuous partial derivatives, the Jacobian of which is nonzero for every $x$ in $U$. Then for any real-valued, compactly supported, continuous function $f$, with support contained in $\varphi(U)$:
$$\int_{\varphi(U)} f(\mathbf{v})\, d\mathbf{v} = \int_U f(\varphi(\mathbf{u})) \left|\det(D\varphi)(\mathbf{u})\right|\,d\mathbf{u}.$$

## o21 statement/theorem: Theorem

Let $U$ be a measurable subset of $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ and $\varphi : U \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ an injective function, and suppose for every $x$ in $U$ there exists $\varphi'(x)$ in $\mathbf{R}^{n,n}$ such that $\varphi(y) = \varphi(x) + \varphi'(x)(y - x) + o(\lVert y - x\rVert)$ as $y \to x$ (here $o$ is little-o notation). Then $\varphi(U)$ is measurable, and for any real-valued function $f$ defined on $\varphi(U)$:
$$\int_{\varphi(U)} f(v)\, dv = \int_U f(\varphi(u)) \left|\det \varphi'(u)\right| \,du$$
in the sense that if either integral exists (including the possibility of being properly infinite), then so does the other one, and they have the same value.

## o23 statement/theorem: Theorem

Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space equipped with a finite Radon measure $\mu$, and let $Y$ be a σ-compact Hausdorff space with a σ-finite Radon measure $\rho$. Let $\varphi : X \to Y$ be an absolutely continuous function (where the latter means that $\rho(\varphi(E)) = 0$ whenever $\mu(E) = 0$). Then there exists a real-valued Borel measurable function $w$ on $X$ such that for every Lebesgue integrable function $f : Y \to \mathbf{R}$, the function $(f \circ \varphi) \cdot w$ is Lebesgue integrable on $X$, and
$$\int_Y f(y)\,d\rho(y) = \int_X (f\circ \varphi)(x)\,w(x)\,d\mu(x).$$
Furthermore, it is possible to write
$$w(x) = (g\circ \varphi)(x)$$
for some Borel measurable function $g$ on $Y$.

## o24 statement/theorem: Theorem

Let $U$ be an open subset of $\mathbf{R}^{n}$ and $\varphi : U \to \mathbf{R}^{n}$ be a bi-Lipschitz mapping. Let $f : \varphi(U) \to \mathbf{R}$ be measurable. Then
$$\int_{\varphi(U)} f(x)\,dx = \int_U (f\circ \varphi)(x) \left|\det D\varphi(x)\right|\,dx$$
in the sense that if either integral exists (or is properly infinite), then so does the other one, and they have the same value.
