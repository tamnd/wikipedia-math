# Sand table

## o1 subject: Sand table

A sand table, or sandboard, uses constrained sand for writing, modelling, or educational purposes. The original version of a sand table may be the grc used by early Greek students. They were later used throughout the Arab world. In the modern era, one common use for a sand table is to make terrain models for military planning and wargaming.

## o2 section: Abax

## o3 pointer-item: Sand abacus

## Abax
An grc () was a table covered with sand commonly used by students, particularly in Greece, to perform studies such as writing, geometry, and calculations.
The grc was the predecessor to the abacus. Objects, such as stones, were added for counting and then columns for place-value arithmetic. The demarcation between an grc and an abacus seems to be poorly defined in history; moreover, modern definitions of the word universally describe it as a frame with rods and beads and, in general, do not include the definition of "sand table".
The sand table may well have been the predecessor to some board games. ("The word ar, or abacus, is used both for the reckoning-board with its counters and the play-board with its pieces, "). grc is from the old Greek for "sand table".
