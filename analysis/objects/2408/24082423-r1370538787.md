# Mass–luminosity relation

## o1 subject: Mass–luminosity relation

In astrophysics, the mass–luminosity relation is an empirical and theoretical relationship between a star's mass and its luminosity The relationship is represented by the equation:
$$\frac{L}{L_{\odot}} = \left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)^a$$
where $L_{\text{☉}}$ and $M_{\text{☉}}$ are the luminosity and mass of the Sun and $1 < a < 6$. The value $a = 3.5$ is commonly used for main-sequence stars. This equation and the usual value of $a = 3.5$ only applies to main-sequence stars with masses $2M_{\text{☉}} < M < 55M_{\text{☉}}$ and does not apply to red giants or white dwarfs. As a star approaches the Eddington luminosity then $a = 1$.
