# Return period

## o1 subject: Return period

A return period, also known as a recurrence interval or repeat interval, is an average time or an estimated average time between events such as earthquakes, floods, landslides, or river discharge flows to occur.
The reciprocal value of return period is called the frequency of occurrence.

## o12 example: Example

### Example
If the return period of occurrence $T$ is 243 years ($\mu = 0.0041$) then the probability of exactly one occurrence in ten years is
$\begin{aligned}
P(r;t) & = \frac{(\mu t)^r}{r!} e^{-\mu t}\\[6pt]
P(r=1;t=10) & = \frac{(10/243)^1}{1!} e^{-10/243} \approx 3.95\%
\end{aligned}$

## o15 example: Example

### Example
Given that the return period of an event is 100 years,
$p={1\over 100}=0.01.$
So the probability that such an event occurs exactly once in 10 successive years is:
$\begin{aligned}
P(X = 1) & =\binom{10}{1} \times 0.01^1 \times 0.99^9 \\[4pt]
& \approx 10 \times 0.01 \times 0.914 \\[4pt]
& \approx 0.0914
\end{aligned}$
