# Abe Sklar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abe Sklar"}

**Abe Sklar** (November 25, 1925 – October 30, 2020) was an American mathematician and a professor of applied mathematics at the [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology) (Illinois Tech) and the inventor of [copulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_%28probability_theory%29) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sklar was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) to Jewish parents who immigrated to the United States from [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine). He attended [Von Steuben High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Steuben_High_School) and later enrolled at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1942, when he was only 16. Sklar went on to become a student of [Tom M. Apostol](wikipedia://en/2196529) at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), where he earned his Ph.D. in 1956. His students at IIT have included geometers [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421) and [Marjorie Senechal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjorie_Senechal).[^o6]

In 1959, Sklar introduced the notion of and the name of "[copulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_%28probability_theory%29)" into [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and proved the theorem that bears his name, [Sklar's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_%28probability_theory%29#Sklar's_theorem).[^o7][^o8] That is, that [multivariate cumulative distribution functions](wikipedia://en/5793#Multivariate_case) can be expressed in terms of copulas.[^o9] This representation of distribution functions, which is valid in any dimension and unique when the margins are continuous, is the basis of [copula modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_modeling), a widespread data analytical technique used in statistics; this representation is often termed Sklar's representation. [Schweizer–Sklar t-norms](wikipedia://en/9823717#Schweizer–Sklar_t-norms) are also named after Sklar and Berthold Schweizer, who studied them together in the early 1960s.
