# Itô–Nisio theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Itô–Nisio theorem"}

The **Itô–Nisio theorem** is a theorem from [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) that characterizes [convergence](wikipedia://en/50723) in [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces). The theorem shows the equivalence of the different types of convergence for sums of independent and symmetric random variables in Banach spaces. The Itô–Nisio theorem leads to a generalization of Wiener's construction of the Brownian motion.[^o2] The symmetry of the [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) in the theorem is needed in infinite spaces.

:::

The theorem was proven by Japanese mathematicians [Kiyoshi Itô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyoshi_Itô) and Makiko Nisio in 1968.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $(E,\|\cdot\|)$ be a real [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) Banach space with the norm induced topology, we use the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_σ-algebra) and denote the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) as $E^*$. Let $\langle z,S\rangle:={}_{E^*}\langle z,S\rangle_E$ be the [dual pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair) and $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358). Let

- $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be an infinite sequence of independent and symmetric $E$-valued random variables defined on the same probability space,
- $$S_n=\sum_{k=1}^n X_k,$$
- $\mu_n$ be the [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) of $S_n$,
- $S$ some $E$-valued random variable.

The following are equivalent:[^o3]

1. $S_n\to S$ almost surely.
1. $S_n\to S$ in probability.
1. $\mu_n \to \mu$ in the [Lévy–Prokhorov metric](wikipedia://en/6789891).
1. $\{\mu_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is [uniformly tight](wikipedia://en/6553354).
1. $\langle z, S_n\rangle\to \langle z, S\rangle$ in probability for every $z\in E^*$.
1. There exists a probability measure $\mu$ on $E$ such that for every $z\in E^*$

$$\mathbb{E}[e^{i\langle z,S_n\rangle}]\to \int_{E}e^{i\langle z,x\rangle} \, \mu(\mathrm{d}x).$$

**Remarks:** Since $E$ is separable, item 3 (i.e. convergence in the Lévy–Prokhorov metric) is the same as convergence in distribution $\mu_n\implies \mu$. If we remove the symmetric-distribution condition:

- in a finite-dimensional setting equivalence is true for all except item 4 (i.e. the uniform tighness of $\{\mu_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$),[^o3]
- in an infinite-dimensional setting $1\iff 2 \iff 3$ is true but $6\implies 3$ does not always hold.[^o3]

:::
