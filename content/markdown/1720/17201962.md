# Helen Abbot Merrill

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helen Abbot Merrill"}

*Image: Helen Abbott Merrill with Students.jpg.* Helen Abbott Merrill with math students at Wellesley College

**Helen Abbot Merrill** (March 30, 1864 – May 1, 1949) was an American mathematician, educator and textbook author. She was chair of the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) department at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College), where she taught from 1893 to 1932.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Merrill was born on March 30, 1864, in [Llewellyn Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llewellyn_Park,_New_Jersey).[^o4] Her father George Merrill was a [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey) insurance claims adjustor; her mother was Emily Dodge Abbot Merrill. She lived in [Newburyport, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newburyport,_Massachusetts), for some of her childhood.[^o5] She entered Wellesley College in 1882; she intended to major in Greek and Latin, but switched to mathematics after one year, and graduated in 1886.[^o4] She pursued further studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and in Germany.[^o5] In 1903 she earned a PhD in mathematics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) under the direction of [James Pierpont](wikipedia://en/2594993). Her thesis was "On Solutions of Differential Equations which possess an Oscillation Theorem."[^o6]

Her younger brother [William P. Merrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_P._Merrill) was a noted Presbyterian minister, hymn writer, and pacifist.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Merrill taught at girls' schools in New York and Pennsylvania after college.[^o5] She taught mathematics at Wellesley College beginning in 1893, and she was chair of the mathematics department from 1916 to 1932.[^o5][^o9] She rebuilt Wellesley's collection of cardboard mathematical models, after a fire destroyed the original set.[^o10] Upon her retirement in 1932 from Wellesley, she was given the title [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita).[^o10]

She became a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1911.[^o11] In 1920 she was appointed vice-president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o12]

## Publications {#Publications}

Merrill wrote two textbooks with her Wellesley colleague [Clara Eliza Smith](wikipedia://en/57372963): *Selected Topics in Higher Algebra* (Norwood, 1914) and *A First Course in Higher Algebra* (Macmillan, 1917).[^o14][^o15] She also wrote a book titled *[Mathematical Excursions](wikipedia://en/63480777)* in 1933, meant to explain some mathematical concepts to a young audience.[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Merrill died in 1949, at the age of 85, in [Wellesley, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley,_Massachusetts).[^o18]
