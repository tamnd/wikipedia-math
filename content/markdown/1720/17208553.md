# Simply connected at infinity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simply connected at infinity"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of mathematics, a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is said to be **simply connected at infinity** if for any [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subset *C* of *X*, there is a compact set *D* in *X* containing *C* so that the induced map on [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004)

:::

$$\pi_1(X-D) \to \pi_1(X-C)$$

is the zero map. Intuitively, this is the property that loops far away from a small subspace of *X* can be collapsed, no matter how bad the small subspace is.

The [Whitehead manifold](wikipedia://en/1192013) is an example of a 3-[manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) that is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible) but not simply connected at infinity. Since this property is invariant under [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660), this proves that the Whitehead manifold is not homeomorphic to **R**<sup>3</sup>.

However, it is a theorem of [John R. Stallings](wikipedia://en/18729795)[^o2] that for $n \geq 5$, a contractible *n*-manifold is homeomorphic to **R**<sup>*n*</sup> precisely when it is simply connected at infinity.
