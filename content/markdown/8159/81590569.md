# Wiesław Żelazko

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wiesław Żelazko"}

**Wiesław Tadeusz Żelazko** (16 February 1933 – 6 November 2025) was a Polish mathematician who specialised in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and the theory of topological and [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665). His name is attached to well-known theorems in [Banach algebra theory](wikipedia://en/4665), he held a full professorship at the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302), received major national and scientific awards, and served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Żelazko was born in [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź), Poland on 16 February 1933.[^o5] He was a finalist in the second Polish Mathematical Olympiad in 1951. He studied mathematics at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), graduating in 1955 under the supervision of [Roman Sikorski](wikipedia://en/1983335). He joined the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302) (IMPAN) soon afterwards and completed his Ph.D. there in 1960 under [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) with a dissertation on locally bounded and m-convex rings.[^o6]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Żelazko spent his entire professional career at IMPAN, where he became *docent* in 1965, *extraordinary professor* in 1971, and *full professor* in 1976.[^o8] His research focused on the structure theory of topological algebras, automatic continuity, and spectral properties of Banach algebras.[^o9]

He supervised several doctoral students, including Zbigniew Słodkowski and Jaroslav Zemánek, both of whom later became prominent researchers in operator theory and Banach algebras.[^o6]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Żelazko was married to Jowita Koncewicz.[^o11] He died on 6 November 2025, at the age of 92.[^o12]

### Legacy {#Legacy}

A short note that several theorems bear his name and that his results remain standard references. Several theorems in functional analysis bear his name. The **Gleason–Kahane–Żelazko theorem**, independently obtained by three authors, provides a geometric characterization of multiplicative linear functionals and has since become a standard result in Banach algebra theory. The **Hirschfeld–Żelazko theorem** offers another spectral criterion that found broad use in automatic continuity problems.

## Professional service {#Professional_service}

Żelazko was a member of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society) and of the [Mathematics Committee of the Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences). From 1983 to 1985, he served as president of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791)[^o15].

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Żelazko received the **Stefan Banach Prize** (1967)[^o17] and the **Stefan Banach Medal** (2000). He was also decorated with the [Knight's Cross of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) (1979), [Officer's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) (1988), and [Commander's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) (2009) of the **[Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta)** for his contributions to Polish science.
