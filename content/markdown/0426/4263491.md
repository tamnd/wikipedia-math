# Cavity method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cavity method"}

The **cavity method** is a mathematical method presented by [Marc Mézard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Mézard), [Giorgio Parisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Parisi) and [Miguel Angel Virasoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Ángel_Virasoro_%28physicist%29) in 1987[^o2] to derive and solve some [mean field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_field)-type models in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), specially adapted to disordered systems. The method has been used to compute properties of [ground states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) in many [condensed matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter) and [optimization problems](wikipedia://en/1126536).

:::

Initially invented to deal with the [Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherrington–Kirkpatrick_model) of [spin glasses](wikipedia://en/148555), the cavity method has shown wider applicability. It can be regarded as a generalization of the [Bethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Bethe)–[Peierls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Peierls) iterative method in tree-like graphs, to the case of a graph with loops that are not too short. The cavity method can solve many problems also solvable using the [replica trick](wikipedia://en/3885155) but has the advantage of being more intuitive and less mathematically subtle than replica-based methods.

The cavity method proceeds by perturbing a large system with the addition of a non-thermodynamic number of additional constituents and approximating the response of the entire system [perturbatively](wikipedia://en/102140). The application of the resulting approximation, along with an assumption that certain observables are [self-averaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-averaging), yields a self-consistency equation for the statistics of the added constituents. The added constituents are then considered to be the mean-field variables.

The cavity method has proved useful in solving [optimization problems](wikipedia://en/1126536) such as [k-satisfiability](wikipedia://en/4715) and [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743). It has yielded not only ground states energy predictions in the average case but has also inspired algorithmic methods.

## See also {#See_also}

The cavity method originated in the context of [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), but is also closely related to methods from other areas such as [belief propagation](wikipedia://en/800010).
