# Walter Buckingham Carver

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Buckingham Carver"}

**Walter Buckingham Carver** (January 11, 1879, [Town Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Hill_%28Maryland−Pennsylvania%29), [Fulton County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton_County,_Pennsylvania) – July 4, 1961, [Ithaca, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca,_New_York)) was an American mathematician, noteworthy as the president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for a two-year term in 1939 and 1940.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Carver was an undergraduate at [Gettysburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_College) and then at [Dickinson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson_College), where he graduated in 1899.[^o3] In 1904 he received in his Ph.D. in mathematics from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). His thesis *On the Cayley-Veronese Class of Configurations* was supervised by [Frank Morley](wikipedia://en/589167). For two academic years from 1904 to 1906, Carver was a professor at [Ursinus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursinus_College).[^o3] In 1906 joined the faculty of Cornell University and held a professorship there until he retired as professor emeritus in 1948. He dedicated his career to [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471), primarily of college undergraduates.[^o6] He chaired Cornell's mathematics department from 1929 to 1932 and again from 1938 to 1940.[^o6] In retirement he was active in mathematical teaching and [problem solving](wikipedia://en/1467948) until shortly before his death. His last article in the MAA's *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* was published about a week before his death. At professor emeritus, he occasionally taught and counseled Cornell students and conducted summer sessions in mathematical programs.[^o7]

Carver was a founding member in 1915 of the Mathematical Association of America (MAA) and the MAA's 18th president. As president, he was the successor to [Aubrey J. Kempner](wikipedia://en/36825600) and the predecessor of [Raymond Woodard Brink](wikipedia://en/34278566). Carver was from 1943 to 1948 the MAA's secretary-treasurer, as the successor to Williams D. Cairns.[^o3]

Carver served as the editor-in-chief of the *American Mathematical Monthly* for 4 years from 1932 to 1936.[^o8] His contributions of problems and solutions to the *American Mathematical Monthly* spanned about 58 years, longer than any other contributor in his lifetime.[^o7] In 1937 at the MAA's annual meeting, he gave an invited address *Thinking versus manipulation*, criticizing the teaching of formal manipulations without sufficient emphasis on mathematical reasoning.[^o9]

In 1929 Dickinson College awarded him an honorary doctorate.[^o10]
