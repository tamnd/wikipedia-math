# Swinnerton-Dyer polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Swinnerton-Dyer polynomial"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Swinnerton-Dyer polynomials** are a family of polynomials, introduced by [Peter Swinnerton-Dyer](wikipedia://en/2105540), that serve as examples where [polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization) algorithms have worst-case runtime. They have the property of being [reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reducible_polynomial) [modulo](wikipedia://en/1352428) every prime, while being irreducible over the rational numbers. They are a standard counterexample in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

Given a finite set $P$ of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666), the Swinnerton-Dyer polynomial associated to $P$ is the polynomial:

$$f_P(x) = \prod \left(x + \sum_{p\in P} (\pm) \sqrt{p}\right)$$

where the product extends over all $2^{|P|}$ choices of sign in the enclosed sum. The polynomial $f_P(x)$ has degree $2^{|P|}$ and integer coefficients, which alternate in sign. If $|P|>1$, then $f_P(x)$ is [reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reducible_polynomial) modulo $p$ for all primes $p$, into linear and quadratic factors, but irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of $f_P(x)$ is $\mathbb Z_2^{|P|}$.

The first few Swinnerton-Dyer polynomials are:

$$f_{\{2\}}(x) = x^2-2 = (x-\sqrt 2)(x+\sqrt 2)$$

$$f_{\{2,3\}}(x) = x^4-10x^2+1 = (x-\sqrt 2-\sqrt 3)(x-\sqrt 2+\sqrt 3)(x+\sqrt 2 -\sqrt 3)(x+\sqrt 2+\sqrt 3)$$

$$f_{\{2,3,5\}}(x) = x^8-40x^6+352x^4-960x^2+576.$$
