# Michelle Schatzman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michelle Schatzman"}

**Michelle Véra Schatzman** (8 December 1949 – 20 August 2010) was a French [analyst](wikipedia://en/48396) and mathematician, specializing in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), particularly in topics related to [nonlinear analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_analysis) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).[^o6][^o12] She combined her role as a [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) research director with teaching as a professor at the [Claude Bernard University Lyon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard_University_Lyon_1). In 1996, in collaboration with S. Jon Chapman and Jacob Rubinstein, Schatzmann developed a [mean-field theory](wikipedia://en/555768) of the vorticity density of [superconductivity vortices](wikipedia://en/4389619), now called the Chapman–Rubinstein–Schatzman model.[^o3][^o4] In physics, the model is used to describe [type-II superconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type-II_superconductor).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Michelle Véra Schatzman was born in a secular Jewish family. Her father was French [astrophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysicist) [Evry Schatzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evry_Schatzman), who also was president of the [Rationalist Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_Union). Her mother Ruth Schatzman (née Fisher) took a keen interest in Russian folk tales. She was an associate of Russian in high schools in Lille and Paris, then a lecturer at the [Paris VIII University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_8_University).[^o6]

Schatzman entered the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles) in 1968 and was top of the class.[^o7] She earned the [agregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) and a PhD in 1971, under the leadership of [Haïm Brezis](wikipedia://en/18329570) and a state doctorate in 1979 under the direction of [Jacques Louis Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Louis_Lions).[^o6]

Michelle Schatzman married Yves Pigier in 1975. They had two children. They divorced in 1988.[^o6] Her daughter is married to Dan Mangoubi, an Israeli mathematician and a professor at the [Albert Einstein Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein_Institute_of_Mathematics) of the [University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Schatzman led something of a hybrid career, dividing her time between research and teaching.[^o6][^o12]

She was first an attaché, then a research assistant from 1972 to 1984 at the Laboratoire d'analyse numérique in Paris 6, now Laboratoire Jacques-Louis Lions, then from spring 1981, at the Center of Applied Mathematics of the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique).[^o6]

She became a professor at the [Claude Bernard University Lyon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard_University_Lyon_1) in 1984, in the Lyon-Saint-Étienne [digital analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) team, which in 1995 became the Laboratory for Applied Mathematics in Lyon (MAPLY), which Schatzman headed for eight years.[^o7] This laboratory merged in 2005 with other laboratories in Lyons, leading to the establishment of the Institut Camille Jordan.[^o6]

In 1996, S. Jon Chapman, Jacob Rubinstein and Schatzmann developed a [mean-field theory](wikipedia://en/555768) of the vorticity density of [superconductivity vortices](wikipedia://en/4389619), now called the Chapman–Rubinstein–Schatzman model.[^o10][^o11]

She returned to the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) in 2005 as a research director while continuing to teach, especially master students.[^o6]

Schatzman spent extended stays in foreign universities including the [University of California at Berkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkley), the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) in Minneapolis, and the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) in New York City.[^o6]

After suffering from cancer, Michelle Schatzman died in Lyon on 20 August 2010, aged 60.[^o6][^o12]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

- [Knight of the Legion of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_of_the_Legion_of_Honor) (2008).[^o14]
- Prize of Mme Claude Berthault awarded by the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2006.[^o15]

In 2026, Schatzman was announced as one of [72 historical women in STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women's_names_for_the_Eiffel_Tower) whose names were to be added to the [72 men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower) already celebrated on the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower). The plan was conceived by a student and tour guide named Benjamin Rigaud[^o16] and it was announced by the Mayor of Paris, [Anne Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) following the recommendations of a committee led by [Isabelle Vauglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Vauglin) of [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences) and Jean-François Martins, representing the operating company which runs the Eiffel Tower.[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

Over the years, Schatzman published over 70 papers, many of which continue to be cited today.\<ref>\<ref name=smf>\</ref>

Her books include:

- *Numerical Analysis, A Mathematical Approach*, first published by Masson in 1991 and reprinted by Dunod in 2001
- *Numerical Analysis: A Mathematical Introduction* (2002), Clarendon Press, Oxford. .
