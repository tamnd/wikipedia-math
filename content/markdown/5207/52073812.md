# Jackie Stedall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jackie Stedall"}

**Jacqueline Anne** "**Jackie**" **Stedall** (4 August 1950 – 27 September 2014)[^o3] was a British [mathematics historian](wikipedia://en/14220). She wrote nine books,[^o3] and appeared on radio on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4)'s *[In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_%28radio_series%29)* programme.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Stedall was born in [Romford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romford), [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), and attended [Queen Mary's High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary's_High_School) in [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall).[^o3] Her academic achievements included a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Girton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge), an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) from the [University of Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kent), a [PGCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Graduate_Certificate_in_Education) from [Bristol Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Polytechnic) (now the [University of the West of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_of_England)), and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) from the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University).[^o3][^o5] Her PhD focused upon [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290)' 1685 work *Treatise of Algebra*.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After her MSc degree, Stedall worked for three years as a [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), and four years as an administrator for [War on Want](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Want). Subsequently, she worked as a [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher) for eight years.[^o5] Stedall's academic career began in 2000, when she became a Clifford Norton student at [The Queen's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_College,_Oxford), studying the history of science.[^o3][^o5] She later became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the college, and created a third-year module on the history of mathematics at the University of Oxford.[^o3][^o5] In 2002, Stedall became the managing editor of the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793)'s newsletter, which later became the [BSHM Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSHM_Bulletin) journal. She worked alongside fellow mathematical historian [Eleanor Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Robson).[^o6]

Stedall appeared multiple times on the [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4) programme *[In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_%28radio_series%29)*. Topics that she discussed on the programme included [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), whether [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) or [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) were the founder of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918), [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) in finance, and [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) era mathematics.[^o8][^o9]

## Books {#Books}

Stedall wrote a 2008 book *Mathematics Emerging*[^o11] which was used as the primary textbook for her course.[^o3][^o5] She also co-edited and published the *Oxford Handbook of the History of Mathematics*.[^o16] With [Janet Beery](wikipedia://en/59100464), she co-edited *[Thomas Harriot’s Doctrine of Triangular Numbers: the 'Magisteria Magna'](wikipedia://en/63072640)* (European Mathematical Society, 2009).[^o12]

In 2012, Stedall wrote the book *[The History of Mathematics: A Very Short Introduction](wikipedia://en/68366991)*, part of the [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press)' *Very Short Introduction* series of books. The book focused on "what mathematical historians do and how they do it".[^o13] It won the 2013 [Neumann Prize](wikipedia://en/7151472) for the best English-language book on the history of mathematics.[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Stedall was married and had two children.[^o16]

Whilst suffering from [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer), Stedall joined the Painswick Friends' meeting house, which "helped her find peace with her illness".[^o3][^o16] In March 2014, she was robbed by a Romanian fraud gang, who stole her bank card.[^o16]

Stedall died of cancer on 27 September 2014.[^o3][^o5] In her will, she donated money to [Queen's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_College,_Oxford) Library for the preservation of mathematical history books.[^o17] In 2015, the [Canadian Society for History and Philosophy of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1806503) held a special session to remember Stedall, and in 2016, the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793) held a two-day meeting at Queen's College on sixteenth- and seventeenth-century algebra, which they dedicated to Stedall.[^o18][^o19]
