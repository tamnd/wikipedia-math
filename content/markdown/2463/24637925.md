# Sun-Yung Alice Chang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sun-Yung Alice Chang"}

**Sun-Yung Alice Chang** (, Chông Sṳn-yùng, ; born 1948) is a [Taiwanese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_American) mathematician specializing in aspects of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) ranging from [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) to [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). She is the [Eugene Higgins Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Department_of_Mathematics) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Chang was born in [Xian, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian,_China), in 1948 and grew up in [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan). She received her [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) (B.S.) degree in 1970 from [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) and her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1974 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o5] At Berkeley, Chang wrote her thesis on the study of bounded [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions). Chang became a full professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1980 before moving to [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) in 1998.[^o6]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Chang's research interests include the study of geometric types of [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376) and problems in isospectral geometry. Working with her husband Paul Yang and others, she produced contributions to [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) in relation to geometry and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).[^o6]

She teaches at Princeton University as of 1998. Before that, she held visiting positions at University of California-Berkeley; Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, N.J.; and Swiss Federal Institute of Technology, Zurich, Switzerland.[^o6] She served at Swiss Federal Institute of Technology as a visiting professor in 2015.[^o8]

In 2004,[^o9] she was interviewed by Yu Kiang Leong for *Creative Minds, Charmed Lives: Interviews at Institute for Mathematical Sciences, National University of Singapore*, and she declared:

> *«In the mathematical community, we should leave room for people who want to do work in their own way. Mathematical research is not just a scientific approach; the nature of mathematics is sometimes close to that of art. Some people want individual character and an individual way of working things out. They should be appreciated too. There should be room for single research and collaborative research».*[^o10]

Chang's life was profiled in the 2017 documentary film *Girls who fell in love with Math*.[^o11]
