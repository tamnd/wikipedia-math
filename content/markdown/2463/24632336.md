# Buchholz's ordinal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Buchholz's ordinal"}

In mathematics, **ψ<sub>0</sub>(Ω<sub>ω</sub>)**, widely known as **Buchholz's ordinal**, is a [large countable ordinal](wikipedia://en/4376814) that is used to measure the [proof-theoretic strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof-theoretic_strength) of some mathematical systems. In particular, it is the [proof-theoretic ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084282) of the subsystem $\Pi_1^1$-CA<sub>0</sub> of [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454);[^o2][^o3] this is one of the "big five" subsystems studied in [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365) (Simpson 1999). It is also the proof-theoretic ordinal of $\mathsf{ID_{<\omega}}$, the theory of finitely [iterated inductive definitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchholz's_ID_hierarchy), and of $KP\ell_0$,[^o4] a fragment of [Kripke–Platek set theory](wikipedia://en/1124695) extended by an axiom stating every set is contained in an [admissible set](wikipedia://en/5138993). Buchholz's ordinal is also the order type of the segment bounded by $D_0D_\omega0$ in Buchholz's ordinal notation $\mathsf{(OT, <)}$.[^o2] Lastly, it can be expressed as the limit of the sequence: $\varepsilon_0 = \psi_0(\Omega)$, $\mathsf{BHO} = \psi_0(\Omega_2)$, $\psi_0(\Omega_3)$, ...

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

- $\Omega_0 = 1$, and $\Omega_n = \aleph_n$ for *n* > 0.
- $C_i(\alpha)$ is the closure of $\Omega_i$ under addition and the $\psi_\eta(\mu)$ function itself (the latter of which only for $\mu < \alpha$ and $\eta \leq \omega$).
- $\psi_i(\alpha)$ is the smallest ordinal not in $C_i(\alpha)$.
- Thus, *ψ*<sub>0</sub>(Ω<sub>*ω*</sub>) is the smallest ordinal not in the closure of $1$ under addition and the $\psi_\eta(\mu)$ function itself (the latter of which only for $\mu < \Omega_\omega$ and $\eta \leq \omega$).

:::
