# Harry Kesten

::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Kesten"}

**Harry Kesten** (November 19, 1931 – March 29, 2019) was a Jewish American mathematician best known for his work in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), most notably on [random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) on [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) and [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765), [branching processes](wikipedia://en/377202), and [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Harry Kesten was born in Duisburg, Germany in 1931,[^o9][^o10] and grew up in the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Netherlands), where he moved with his parents in 1933 to escape the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi). Surviving [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_the_Netherlands), Kesten initially studied chemistry, and later theoretical physics and mathematics, at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). He moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1956 and received his PhD in mathematics in 1958 at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) under the supervision of [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425). He was an instructor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) before returning to Cornell in 1961.[^o9]

Kesten died on March 29, 2019, in [Ithaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca,_New_York) at the age of 87.[^o10]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Kesten's work includes many fundamental contributions across almost the whole of probability,[^o9][^o12][^o13] including the following highlights.

- *[Random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks) on [groups](wikipedia://en/19447).* In his 1958 PhD thesis, Kesten studied symmetric random walks on countable groups *G* generated by a jump distribution with support *G*. He showed that the spectral radius equals the exponential decay rate of the return probabilities.[^o14] He showed later that this is strictly less than 1 if and only if the group is [non-amenable](wikipedia://en/657430).[^o15] The last result is known as *Kesten's criterion for amenability*. He calculated the spectral radius of the *d*-regular tree, namely $\frac{2\sqrt{d-1}}{d}$.
- *Products of [random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrices).* Let $Y_n=X_1 X_2\cdots X_n$ be the product of the first *n* elements of an ergodic stationary sequence of random $k \times k$ matrices. With [Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152) in 1960, Kesten showed the convergence of $n^{-1}\log^+\|Y_n\|$, under the condition $E (\log^+\|X_1\|)<\infty$.[^o16]
- *[Self-avoiding walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-avoiding_walks).* Kesten's ratio limit theorem states that the number $\sigma_n$ of *n*-step self-avoiding walks from the origin on the integer lattice satisfies $\sigma_{n+2}/\sigma_n \to \mu^2$ where $\mu$ is the [connective constant](wikipedia://en/36246682). This result remains unimproved despite much effort.[^o17] In his proof, Kesten proved his pattern theorem, which states that, for a proper internal pattern *P*, there exists $\alpha$ such that the proportion of walks containing fewer than $\alpha n$ copies of *P* is exponentially smaller than $\sigma_n$.[^o18]
- *[Branching processes](wikipedia://en/377202).* Kesten and Stigum showed that the correct condition for the convergence of the population size, normalized by its mean, is that $E(L\log^+ L)<\infty$ where *L* is a typical family size.[^o19] With Ney and [Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Spitzer), Kesten found the minimal conditions for the asymptotic distributional properties of a critical branching process, as discovered earlier, but subject to stronger assumptions, by [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov) and [Yaglom](wikipedia://en/28210480).[^o20]
- *[Random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) in a random environment.* With Kozlov and [Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Spitzer), Kesten proved a deep theorem about random walk in a one-dimensional random environment. They established the limit laws for the walk across the variety of situations that can arise within the environment.[^o21]
- *[Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558).* In 1966, Kesten resolved a conjecture of [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and Szűsz on the discrepancy of irrational rotations. He studied the discrepancy between the number of rotations by $\xi$ hitting a given interval *I*, and the length of *I*, and proved this bounded if and only if the length of *I* is a multiple of $\xi$.[^o22]
- *[Diffusion-limited aggregation](wikipedia://en/1312515).* Kesten proved that the growth rate of the arms in *d* dimensions can be no larger than $n^{2/(d+1)}$.[^o23][^o24]
- *[Percolation](wikipedia://en/187805).* Kesten's most famous work in this area is his proof that the critical probability of bond percolation on the square lattice equals 1/2.[^o25] He followed this with a systematic study of percolation in two dimensions, reported in his book *Percolation Theory for Mathematicians*.[^o26] His work on scaling theory and scaling relations[^o27] has since proved key to the relationship between critical percolation and [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108).[^o28]
- *[First passage percolation](wikipedia://en/44370787).* Kesten's results for this growth model are largely summarized in *Aspects of First Passage Percolation*.[^o29] He studied the rate of convergence to the time constant, and contributed to the topics of [subadditive](wikipedia://en/745789) stochastic processes and [concentration of measure](wikipedia://en/788497). He developed the problem of [maximum flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_flow) through a medium subject to random capacities.

A volume of papers was published in Kesten's honor in 1999.[^o30] The Kesten memorial volume of [Probability Theory and Related Fields](wikipedia://en/30852593)[^o31] contains a full list of the dedicatee's publications.
