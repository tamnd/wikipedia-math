# Eli Ben-Sasson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eli Ben-Sasson"}

**Eli Ben-Sasson** (Hebrew: אלי בן-ששון) is an Israeli computer scientist known for his work on [zero-knowledge proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_proofs). He is also the co-founder and CEO of [StarkWare Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarkWare_Industries), a Founding Scientist of Zcash, and a former Professor of Computer Science at [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Ben-Sasson studied [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) at [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), earning his PhD in 2001 under the supervision of Prof. [Avi Wigderson](wikipedia://en/6570979).[^o4][^o5] He then completed his Postdoctoral Studies at Harvard and MIT.[^o6][^o4] In 2005, Ben-Sasson joined the faculty of the Faculty of Computer Science at the Technion as Senior Lecturer (Assistant Professor). In 2009, he received the Henry Taub Award for Academic Excellence.[^o7] In 2010, Ben-Sasson was promoted to Associate Professor at the Technion and in 2015 was promoted again to Full Professor, a position he held till he left Technion in 2020. He researched theoretical computer science and [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543).[^o6]

In the early 2000s, Ben-Sasson published a series of articles on short, efficiently testable proofs, including [quasi-linear length PCP proofs](wikipedia://en/3001241),[^o8] He later published theoretical and practical improvements of these methods.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]

In 2013, Ben-Sasson lectured about his research on Zero-knowledge proof at a Bitcoin conference and introduced the potential benefit of Zero-knowledge to Increase Bitcoin scalability.[^o13] He then met with early crypto pioneers, including [Vitalik Buterin](wikipedia://en/41729424) and Greg Maxwell, and raised awareness for the use of Zero-knowledge proof in crypto. Following that, he began working towards the practical realization of his research in the field of crypto and blockchain.[^o6]

In 2014, Ben-Sasson and other researchers collaborated on Zerocash, a design for a private [cryptocurrency](wikipedia://en/36662188) based on zero-knowledge proofs.[^o14] This research won the 2024 IEEE Test of Time Award.[^o15] Ben-Sasson later became a founding scientist of [Zcash](wikipedia://en/51849458), which was based on the Zerocash design.[^o16]

In 2018, Ben-Sasson and other researchers invented STARKs and FRI protocols, which together created a type of [zero-knowledge proof](wikipedia://en/450541) with several unique traits: (1) protection against quantum computers, (2) quasi-linear proof time, (3) polylogarithmic verification time, and (4) transparency, which means no dependence on secret keys.[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20] Later that year, Ben-Sasson co-founded [StarkWare Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarkWare_Industries) alongside Uri Kolodny (who served as its first CEO), Michael Riabzev (his PhD student) and [Alessandro Chiesa](wikipedia://en/78187002). StarkWare offers scaling solutions for blockchains using STARK techniques. Since February 2024 Ben-Sasson has been both CEO and president of the company.[^o21][^o22]

In April 2019, Technion sued Ben-Sasson along with his PhD student and co-founder Michael Riabzev and others, alleging that StarkWare Industries was based on intellectual property developed at the university.[^o23] The institute demanded 50% of Ben-Sasson's stake in the company. Ben-Sasson maintained that the company used employees' knowledge and open source code, but no invention belonging to the Technion.[^o24] No patents or other IP claims were submitted by Techion since then, and in 2020 the two sides reached an agreement before the case reached the court.
