# Integral of a correspondence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integral of a correspondence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **integral of a correspondence** is a generalization of the [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) of [single-valued functions](wikipedia://en/185427) to [correspondences](wikipedia://en/12506030) (i.e., set-valued functions).

:::

The first notion of the integral of a correspondence is due to [Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann) in 1965,[^o2] with a different approach by [Debreu](wikipedia://en/474724) appearing in 1967.[^o3] Integrals of correspondences have applications in [general equilibrium theory](wikipedia://en/45938) in [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255),[^o4][^o5] [random sets](wikipedia://en/5308916) in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542),[^o6][^o7] [partial identification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_identification) in [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390),[^o8] and [fuzzy numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_numbers) in [fuzzy set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzzy_set_theory).[^o9]

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

### Correspondences {#Correspondences}

A *correspondence* $\varphi: X \rightrightarrows Y$ is a function $\varphi : X \rightarrow \mathcal{P} (Y)$, where $\mathcal{P} (Y)$ is the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $Y$. That is, $\varphi$ assigns each point $x \in X$ with a set $\varphi(x) \subset Y$.

### Selections {#Selections}

A [*selection*](wikipedia://en/1432664#Choice_function_of_a_multivalued_map) $f$ of a correspondence $\varphi: X \rightrightarrows Y$ is a function $f: X \rightarrow Y$ such that $f(x) \in \varphi(x)$ for every $x \in X$.

If $X$ can be seen as a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) $(X, \mathcal X, \mu)$ and $Y$ as a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $(Y, || \cdot ||)$, then one can define a *measurable selection* $f$ as an $\mathcal X$-[measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$ such that $f(x) \in \varphi(x)$ for [μ-almost all](wikipedia://en/44688) $x \in X$.[^o6]

::: {#o14 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### The Aumann integral {#The_Aumann_integral}

Let $(X, \mathcal X, \mu)$ be a measure space and $(Y, ||\cdot ||)$ a Banach space. If $\varphi : X\rightrightarrows Y$ is a correspondence, then the *Aumann integral* of $\varphi$ is defined as

$$\int_X \varphi d \mu := \left\{\int_X f d \mu : f \text{ is a measurable selection of } \varphi\right\}$$

where the integrals $\int_X f d \mu$ are [Bochner integrals](wikipedia://en/3675277).

::: {#o16 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: let the underlying measure space be $([0,1], \mathcal L, \lambda)$, and a correspondence $\varphi: [0,1] \rightrightarrows \mathbb R$ be defined as $\varphi(x) = \{2, 3\}$ for all $x \in [0,1]$. Then the Aumman integral of $\varphi$ is $\int_X \varphi d \lambda = [2, 3]$.

:::

### The Debreu integral {#The_Debreu_integral}

Debreu's approach to the integration of a correspondence is more restrictive and cumbersome, but directly yields extensions of usual theorems from the integration theory of functions to the integration of correspondences, such as [Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue)'s [Dominated convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/351853).[^o4] It uses [Rådström's embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/81880778) to identify convex and compact valued correspondences with subsets of a real Banach space, over which [Bochner integration](wikipedia://en/3675277) is straightforward.[^o3]

Let $(X, \mathcal X, \mu)$ be a measure space, $(Y, ||\cdot ||)$ a Banach space, and $\mathcal K \subset \mathcal P(Y)$ the set of all its convex and compact subsets. Let $\varphi: X \to \mathcal K$ be a convex and compact valued correspondence from $X$ to $Y$. By [Rådström's embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/81880778), $\mathcal K$ can be [isometrically embedded](wikipedia://en/52119#Metric_spaces) as a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) $C$ in a real Banach space $(\mathcal Y, || \cdot ||_{\mathcal Y})$, in such a way that addition and multiplication by nonnegative real numbers in $\mathcal Y$ induces the corresponding operation in $\mathcal K$.

Let $\varphi^*: X \rightrightarrows C$ be the "image" of $\varphi$ under the embedding defined above, in the sense that $\varphi^*(x) \subset C$ is the image of $\varphi(x)$ under this embedding for every $x \in X$. For each pair of $\mathcal X$-simple functions $f, g : X \to C$, define the metric $\Delta(f, g) = \int_X ||f - g||_{\mathcal Y} d \mu$.

Then we say that $\varphi$ is integrable if $\varphi^*$ is integrable in the following sense: there exists a sequence of $\mathcal X$-simple functions $(f_n)_n$ from $X$ to $C$ which are [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/6085#In_a_metric_space) in the metric $\Delta$ and [converge in measure](wikipedia://en/7996261) to $\varphi^*$. In this case, we define the integral of $\varphi^*$ to be

$$\int_X \varphi^* d \mu := \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n d \mu \in C$$

where the integrals $\int_X f_n d \mu$ are again simply [Bochner integrals](wikipedia://en/3675277) in the space $(\mathcal Y, || \cdot ||_{\mathcal Y})$, and the result still belongs $C$ since it is a convex cone. We then uniquely identify the *Debreu integral of $\varphi$* as[^o6]

$$\int_X \varphi d \mu := E \in \mathcal K$$

such that $E^* = \int_X \varphi^* d \mu \in C$. Since every embedding is injective and surjective onto its image, the Debreu integral is unique and well-defined.

:::
