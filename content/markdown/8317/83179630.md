# Normal cone (convex analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal cone"}

In [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) and [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29), the **normal cone** to a set at a point is a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) consisting of vectors that make a non-acute angle with every feasible direction from the point. For a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292), it is the [polar cone](wikipedia://en/10063937) of the [tangent cone](wikipedia://en/1416915) and gives a geometric form of first-order optimality conditions.[^n1][^n2]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $C$ be a convex subset of a finite-dimensional real inner product space $V$, and let $x\in C$. The **normal cone** to $C$ at $x$ is

$$N_C(x)=\{v\in V:\langle v,y-x\rangle\leq 0\text{ for all }y\in C\}.$$

If $x\notin C$, the normal cone is often defined to be empty. The elements of $N_C(x)$ are called [normal vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) to $C$ at $x$. The sign convention above gives outward normals.

If $x$ is an interior point of $C$, then $N_C(x)=\{0\}$. If $C$ is a smooth full-dimensional convex body and $x$ is a boundary point, then $N_C(x)$ is the ray generated by the outward normal vector at $x$.

:::

## Subgradients {#Subgradients}

Normal cones are closely related to [subgradients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgradient). If $f:V\to\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$ is a proper [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568), then its [epigraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigraph)

$$\operatorname{epi} f=\{(x,t):t\geq f(x)\}$$

is a convex set. A vector $p\in V$ is a subgradient of $f$ at $x$ if and only if[^n1][^n3]

$$(p,-1)\in N_{\operatorname{epi} f}(x,f(x)).$$

Equivalently,

$$f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle p,y-x\rangle\quad\text{for all }y.$$

### Sublevel sets {#Sublevel_sets}

Still with $f$ a proper convex function, consider the [sublevel set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublevel_set) through a point $x$:

$$C=\{y:f(y)\leq f(x)\}.$$

Every subgradient of $f$ at $x$ determines a normal vector to $C$ at $x$. Indeed, if $p\in\partial f(x)$ and $y\in C$, then

$$\langle p,y-x\rangle\leq f(y)-f(x)\leq 0.$$

Under standard regularity hypotheses, for example when $x\in\operatorname{core}(\operatorname{dom} f)$ and $x$ is not a minimizer of $f$, the converse also holds:

$$N_C(x)=\mathbb R_+\,\partial f(x),$$

or equivalently the normal cone to the sublevel set is the closed convex cone generated by the subdifferential.[^n1][^n4]
