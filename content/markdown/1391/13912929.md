# Goldilocks principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goldilocks principle"}

*Image: The Three Bears - Project Gutenberg eText 17034.jpg.* Illustration for "Goldilocks and the Three Bears"

The **Goldilocks principle** is named by analogy to the children's story "[Goldilocks and the Three Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldilocks_and_the_Three_Bears)", in which a young girl named Goldilocks tastes three different bowls of [porridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porridge) and finds she prefers porridge that is neither too hot nor too cold but has just the right temperature.[^o2] The concept of "just the right amount" is easily understood and applied to a wide range of disciplines, including [developmental psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_psychology), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology),[^o3] [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics)[^o4] and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering).

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: The-Goldilocks-Effect-Human-Infants-Allocate-Attention-to-Visual-Sequences-That-Are-Neither-Too-pone.0036399.s002.ogv.* An animation used to study the Goldilocks effect in visual attention of infants

In [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science) and [developmental psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_psychology), the Goldilocks effect or principle refers to an [infant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant)'s preference to attend events that are neither too simple nor too complex according to their current representation of the world.[^o7] This effect was observed in infants, who are less likely to look away from a visual sequence when the current event is moderately probable, as measured by an idealized learning model.

In [astrobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrobiology), the Goldilocks zone refers to the [habitable zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitable_zone) around a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star). As [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148) put it, "Like Goldilocks, the development of intelligent life requires that planetary temperatures be 'just right.[^o8] The [Rare Earth hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_Earth_hypothesis) uses the Goldilocks principle in the argument that a [planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) must be neither too far away from nor too close to a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star) and [galactic centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_massive_object) to support life, while either extreme would result in a planet incapable of supporting life.[^o9] Such a planet is colloquially called a "Goldilocks planet".[^o10][^o11] [Paul Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Davies) has argued for the extension of the principle to cover the selection of our universe from a (postulated) [multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse): "Observers arise only in those universes where, like Goldilocks' porridge, things are by accident 'just right.[^o12]

In economics, a [Goldilocks economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldilocks_economy) sustains moderate economic growth and low inflation, which allows a market-friendly [monetary policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_policy). Goldilocks pricing, also known as [good–better–best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good–better–best) pricing, is a marketing strategy that uses [product differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_differentiation) to offer three versions of a product to corner different parts of the market: a high-end version, a middle version, and a low-end version.

In communication, the Goldilocks principle describes the amount, type, and detail of [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication) necessary in a system to maximise effectiveness while minimising redundancy and excessive scope on the "too much" side and avoiding incomplete or inaccurate communication on the "too little" side.[^o13]

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the "Goldilocks Fit" references a [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) model that represents the perfect flexibility to reduce the error caused by bias and variance.

In the [design sprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_sprint), the "Goldilocks Quality" means to create a prototype with just enough quality to evoke honest reactions from customers.[^o14]

In [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), the Goldilocks [learning rate](wikipedia://en/59969558) is the learning rate that results in an algorithm taking the fewest steps to achieve minimal [loss](wikipedia://en/442137). Algorithms with a learning rate that is too large often fail to converge at all, while those with too small a learning rate take too long to converge.[^o15]
