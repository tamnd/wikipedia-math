# Goro Nishida

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goro Nishida"}

**Goro Nishida** was a Japanese mathematician. He was a leading member of the [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan) school of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), following in the tradition of [Hiroshi Toda](wikipedia://en/13710063).[^o2]

:::

Nishida received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University) in 1973, after spending the 1971–72 academic year at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England). He then became a professor at Kyoto University in 1990.[^o3] His proof in 1973 of Michael Barratt's conjecture (that positive-degree elements in the [stable homotopy](wikipedia://en/2946655) ring of spheres are [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/31480741)) was a major breakthrough: following [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959)' [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams-Novikov_spectral_sequence) of the [Hopf invariant](wikipedia://en/4417198) one problem, it marked the beginning of a new global understanding of algebraic topology.

His contributions to the field were celebrated in 2003 at the NishidaFest[^o4] in [Kinosaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinosaki,_Hyōgo), followed by a satellite conference at the [Nagoya Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_Institute_of_Technology); the proceedings were published in *[Geometry and Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_and_Topology)*'s monograph series. In 2000 he was the leading organizer for a concentration year at the Japan–US Mathematics Institute[^o5] at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University).

Nishida's earliest work grew out of the study of infinite [loop spaces](wikipedia://en/2174332); his first paper (in 1968, on what came eventually to be known as the Nishida relations) accounts for interactions between [Steenrod operations](wikipedia://en/2146220) and Kudo–Araki (Dyer–Lashof) operations. Some of his later work concerns a circle of ideas surrounding the [Segal conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segal_conjecture), transfer homomorphisms, and stable splittings of [classifying spaces](wikipedia://en/923556) of groups. The ideas in this series of papers have by now grown into a rich subfield of homotopy theory; it continues today in (for example) the theory of [*p*-compact groups](wikipedia://en/3298657).
