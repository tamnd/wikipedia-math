# Moishezon manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moishezon manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **[Moishezon](wikipedia://en/32854935) manifold** $M$ is a compact [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) such that the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) on each component $M$ has [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) equal to the [complex dimension](wikipedia://en/2783513) of the component:

:::

$$\dim_\mathbf{C}M=a(M)=\operatorname{tr.deg.}_\mathbf{C}\mathbf{C}(M).$$

Complex [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) have this property, but the converse is not true: [Hironaka's example](wikipedia://en/39648291) gives a smooth 3-dimensional Moishezon manifold that is not an algebraic variety or [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754). (Moishezon 1967, Chapter I, Theorem 11) showed that a Moishezon manifold is a [projective algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_algebraic_variety) if and only if it admits a [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric). (Artin 1970) showed that any Moishezon manifold carries an [algebraic space](wikipedia://en/2394027) structure; more precisely, the category of Moishezon spaces (similar to Moishezon manifolds, but are allowed to have singularities) is equivalent with the category of algebraic spaces that are proper over $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{C})$.
