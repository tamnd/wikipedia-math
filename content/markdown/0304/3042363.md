# Daihachiro Sato

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daihachiro Sato"}

**Daihachiro Sato** was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who was awarded the [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._R._Ford) Award in 1976 for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), specifically on his work in the [Diophantine](wikipedia://en/9109) representation of [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers).[^o5] His doctoral supervisor at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) was [Ernst G. Straus](wikipedia://en/6920898).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sato was an only child born in [Fujinomiya, Shizuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujinomiya,_Shizuoka), Japan on June 1, 1932. While still attending high school, Sato published his first mathematics research paper, which led to his acceptance at the [Tokyo University of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tsukuba). There, Sato earned a B.S. in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), a popular academic field at the time due to the recent [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics) awarded in 1949 to [Hideki Yukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Yukawa). Later, in 1965, [Shin'ichirō Tomonaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin'ichirō_Tomonaga), one of Dr. Sato's professors at this university, was also awarded a [Nobel Prize in Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize_in_Physics).

Following his undergraduate degree in Japan, he switched his studies to [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), earning a M.Sc. and a Ph.D. from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA),[^o4][^o3] and eventually became tenured at the [University of Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan), Regina campus in [Regina, Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina,_Saskatchewan), Canada. Following his retirement in 1997 he was granted the position Professor Emeritus at the [University of Regina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Regina) which is what the Regina campus became in 1974. Subsequently, he further taught at the [Tokyo University of Social Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Social_Welfare) from 2000 until 2006, after which he returned to Canada. He died at [Ladner, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladner,_British_Columbia) on May 28, 2008.

Sato's interests included integer valued entire functions, generalized interpolation by analytic functions, prime representing functions, and function theory. It is in the field of prime representing functions that Sato co-authored a paper with James P. Jones, Hideo Wada, and [Douglas Wiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Wiens) entitled "Diophantine Representation of the Set of Prime Numbers", which won them the [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._R._Ford) Award in Mathematics in 1976.[^o5]
