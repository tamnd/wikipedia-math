# Moduli scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moduli scheme"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **moduli scheme** is a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) that exists in the [category of schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_schemes) developed by French mathematician [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). Some important [moduli problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem) of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) can be satisfactorily solved by means of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) alone, while others require some extension of the 'geometric object' concept ([algebraic spaces](wikipedia://en/2394027), [algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377) of [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821)).

:::

## History {#History}

Work of Grothendieck and [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594) (see [geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003)) opened up this area in the early 1960s. The more algebraic and abstract approach to moduli problems is to set them up as a [representable functor](wikipedia://en/844461) question, then apply a criterion that singles out the representable [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) for schemes. When this programmatic approach works, the result is a *fine moduli scheme*. Under the influence of more geometric ideas, it suffices to find a scheme that gives the correct [geometric points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_point). This is more like the classical idea that the moduli problem is to express the [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) naturally coming with a set (say of isomorphism classes of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225)).

The result is then a *coarse moduli scheme*. Its lack of refinement is, roughly speaking, that it doesn't guarantee for families of objects what is inherent in the fine moduli scheme. As Mumford pointed out in his book *[Geometric Invariant Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Invariant_Theory)*, one might want to have the fine version, but there is a technical issue ([level structure](wikipedia://en/47016783) and other 'markings') that must be addressed to get a question with a chance of having such an answer.

[Teruhisa Matsusaka](wikipedia://en/47886466) proved a result, now known as [Matsusaka's big theorem](wikipedia://en/42982817), establishing a necessary condition on a [moduli problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_problem) for the existence of a coarse moduli scheme.[^o3]

## Examples {#Examples}

Mumford proved that if *g* > 1, there exists a coarse moduli scheme of smooth curves of genus *g*, which is [quasi-projective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-projective).[^o6] According to a recent survey by [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár), it "has a rich and intriguing intrinsic geometry which is related to major questions in many branches of mathematics and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028)."[^o7] Braungardt has posed the question whether [Belyi's theorem](wikipedia://en/3144280) can be generalised to varieties of higher dimension over the [field of algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_algebraic_numbers), with the formulation that they are generally birational to a finite [étale covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_covering) of a moduli space of curves.[^o8]

Using the notion of [stable vector bundle](wikipedia://en/18909129), coarse moduli schemes for the vector bundles on any smooth [complex variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variety) have been shown to exist, and to be quasi-projective: the statement uses the concept of [semistability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_vector_bundle).[^o9] It is possible to identify the coarse moduli space of special [instanton bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instanton_bundle), in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), with objects in the classical geometry of conics, in certain cases.[^o10]
