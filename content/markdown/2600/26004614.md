# Computably inseparable

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computably inseparable"}

In [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), two [disjoint sets](wikipedia://en/39359) of natural numbers are called **computably inseparable** or **recursively inseparable** if they cannot be "separated" with a [computable set](wikipedia://en/332264).[^o2] These sets arise in the study of computability theory itself, particularly in relation to [$\Pi^0_1$ classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi01_class). Computably inseparable sets also arise in the study of [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The natural numbers are the set $\mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$. Given disjoint subsets $A$ and $B$ of $\mathbb{N}$, a **separating set** $C$ is a subset of $\mathbb{N}$ such that $A \subseteq C$ and $B \cap C = \emptyset$ (or equivalently, $A \subseteq C$ and $B \subseteq C'$, where $C' = \mathbb{N} \setminus C$ denotes the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of $C$). For example, $A$ itself is a [separating set](wikipedia://en/13972359) for the pair, as is $B'$.

If a pair of disjoint sets $A$ and $B$ has no [computable](wikipedia://en/332264) separating set, then the two sets are **computably inseparable**.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

If $A$ is a non-computable set, then $A$ and its complement are computably inseparable. However, there are many examples of sets $A$ and $B$ that are disjoint, non-complementary, and computably inseparable. Moreover, it is possible for $A$ and $B$ to be computably inseparable, disjoint, and [computably enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computably_enumerable).

- Let $\varphi$ be the standard indexing of the [partial computable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_computable_function). Then the sets $A = \{ e : \varphi_e(0) = 0 \}$ and $B = \{ e : \varphi_e(0) = 1 \}$ are computably inseparable ([William Gasarch](wikipedia://en/47580612)1998, p. 1047).
- Let $\#$ be a standard [Gödel numbering](wikipedia://en/207790) for the formulas of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic). Then the set $A = \{ \#(\psi) : PA \vdash \psi \}$ of provable formulas and the set $B = \{ \#(\psi) : PA \vdash \lnot\psi \}$ of refutable formulas are computably inseparable. The inseparability of the sets of provable and refutable formulas holds for many other formal theories of arithmetic (Smullyan 1958).

:::
