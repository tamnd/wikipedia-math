# Rigid cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rigid cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **rigid cohomology** is a [$p$-adic cohomology](wikipedia://en/46927724) theory introduced by [Pierre Berthelot](wikipedia://en/2378544) in 1986.[^o2] It extends [crystalline cohomology](wikipedia://en/10220067) to [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) that need not be proper or [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819), and extends [Monsky–Washnitzer cohomology](wikipedia://en/26005931) to non-[affine varieties](wikipedia://en/320468).

:::

For a scheme $X$ of finite type over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088) $k$, there are rigid cohomology groups $H^i_{\textrm{rig}}(X/K)$ which are finite dimensional vector spaces over the field $K$ of fractions of the ring of Witt vectors of $k$. More generally, one can define rigid cohomology with compact supports, or with support on a closed subscheme, or with coefficients in an overconvergent [isocrystal](wikipedia://en/830034). If $X$ is smooth and proper over $k$, the rigid cohomology groups are the same as the crystalline cohomology groups.

The name "rigid cohomology" comes from its relation to [rigid analytic spaces](wikipedia://en/5828941).

In 2006, [Kiran Kedlaya](wikipedia://en/31688876) used rigid cohomology to give a new proof of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705).[^o3]
