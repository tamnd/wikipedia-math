# Graceful labeling

*Image: Graceful labeling.svg.* A graceful labeling. [Vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) labels are in black, edge labels in red.

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Do all trees admit a graceful labeling?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Graceful labeling"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **graceful labeling** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with $m$ edges is a [labeling](wikipedia://en/1609861) of its [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) with some subset of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) from 0 to $m$ inclusive, such that no two vertices share a label, and each edge is uniquely identified by the [absolute difference](wikipedia://en/28021681) between its endpoints, such that this magnitude lies between 1 and $m$ inclusive.[^o3] A graph which admits a graceful labeling is called a **graceful graph**.

:::

The name "graceful labeling" is due to [Solomon W. Golomb](wikipedia://en/1860707); this type of labeling was originally given the name **β-labeling** by Alexander Rosa in a 1967 paper on graph labelings.[^o4]

A major open problem in graph theory is the **graceful tree conjecture** or **Ringel–Kotzig conjecture**, named after [Gerhard Ringel](wikipedia://en/1571968) and [Anton Kotzig](wikipedia://en/25648858), and sometimes abbreviated **GTC** (not to be confused with [Kotzig's conjecture](wikipedia://en/77390020) on regularly path connected graphs).[^o5] It hypothesizes that all [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_%28graph%29) are graceful. It is still an open conjecture, although a related but weaker conjecture known as "Ringel's conjecture" was partially proven in 2020 by [Montgomery](wikipedia://en/83003164), [Pokrovskiy](wikipedia://en/83165784) and [Sudakov](wikipedia://en/35862803).[^o6][^o7][^o8]

Kotzig once called the effort to prove the conjecture a "disease".[^o9]

Another weaker version of graceful labelling is **near-graceful labeling**, in which the vertices can be labeled using some subset of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) on $[0, m + 1]$ such that no two vertices share a label, and each edge is uniquely identified by the [absolute difference](wikipedia://en/28021681) between its endpoints (this magnitude lies on $[1, m + 1]$).

Another conjecture in graph theory is **Rosa's conjecture**, named after Alexander Rosa, which says that all [triangular cacti](wikipedia://en/9944209#Triangular_cactus) are graceful or nearly-graceful.[^o10]

A graceful graph with edges 0 to $m$ is conjectured to have no fewer than $\left\lceil \sqrt{3 m+\tfrac{9}{4}} \right\rfloor$ vertices, due to [sparse ruler](wikipedia://en/22809664) results. This conjecture has been verified for all graphs with 213 or fewer edges. A related conjecture is that the smallest 2$m$-valence graceful graph has $3 m^2$ edges, with the case for 6-valence shown below.

## Selected results {#Selected_results}

- In his original paper, Rosa proved that an [Eulerian graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulerian_graph) with number of edges *m* ≡ 1 (mod 4) or *m* ≡ 2 (mod 4) cannot be graceful.[^o4]
- Also in his original paper, Rosa proved that the cycle *C<sub>n</sub>* is graceful if and only if *n* ≡ 0 (mod 4) or *n* ≡ 3 (mod 4).
- All [path graphs](wikipedia://en/4587190) and [caterpillar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_graph) are graceful.[^o4]
- All [lobster graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster_graph) with a [perfect matching](wikipedia://en/44031) are graceful.[^o12]
- All trees with at most 27 vertices are graceful; this result was shown by Aldred and [McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_McKay_%28mathematician%29) in 1998 using a computer program.[^o13][^o14] This was extended to trees with at most 29 vertices in the Honours thesis of Michael Horton.[^o15] Another extension of this result up to trees with 35 vertices was claimed in 2010 by the Graceful Tree Verification Project, a [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing) project led by Wenjie Fang.[^o16]
- All [wheel graphs](wikipedia://en/1655876), web graphs, [helm graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helm_graph), [gear graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_graph), and [rectangular grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_graph#Square_grid_graph) are graceful.[^o13]
- All *n*-dimensional [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/39783) are graceful.[^o17]
- All [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) [connected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) with four or fewer vertices are graceful. The only non-graceful simple connected graphs with five vertices are the 5-[cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) ([pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014)); the [complete graph *K*<sub>5</sub>](wikipedia://en/85816#Examples); and the [butterfly graph](wikipedia://en/4372111).
