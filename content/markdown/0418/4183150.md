# Gamma process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gamma process"}

*Image: Gamma-Process.png.* Two different gamma processes from time 0 until time 4. The red process has more occurrences in the timeframe compared to the blue process because its *shape* parameter is larger than the blue shape parameter.

A **gamma process**, also called the *Moran-Gamma subordinator*,[^n1] is a two-parameter [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) which models the accumulation of *effort* or *wear* over time. The gamma process has [independent](wikipedia://en/877315) and [stationary increments](wikipedia://en/22497977) which follow the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079), hence the name. The gamma process is studied in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [stochastics](wikipedia://en/292222), with particular applications in [deterioration modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterioration_modeling)[^n2] and [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680).[^n3]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

The gamma process is often abbreviated as $X(t)\equiv\Gamma(t;\gamma, \lambda)$ where $t$ represents the time from 0. The shape parameter $\gamma$ (inversely) controls the jump size, and the rate parameter $\lambda$ controls the rate of jump arrivals, analogously with the gamma distribution.[^n4] Both $\gamma$ and $\lambda$ must be greater than 0. We use the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079) in this article, so the reader should distinguish between $\Gamma(\cdot)$ (the gamma function), $\Gamma(\gamma, \lambda)$ (the gamma distribution), and $\Gamma(t;\gamma, \lambda)$ (the gamma process).

:::

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The process is a pure-jump [increasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increasing) [Lévy process](wikipedia://en/1025748) with intensity measure $\nu(x)=\gamma x^{-1} \exp(-\lambda x),$ for all positive $x$. It is assumed that the process starts from a value 0 at $t=0$ meaning $X(0)=0$. Thus jumps whose size lies in the interval $[x,x+dx)$ occur as a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with intensity $\nu(x)\,dx.$

The process can also be defined as a stochastic process $X(t), t \leq 0$ with $X(0) = 0$ and independent increments, whose [marginal distribution](wikipedia://en/506077) $f$ of the random variable $X(t) - X(s)$ for an increment $l = t-s \geq 0$ is given by[^n4]

$$f(x;l, \gamma, \lambda) = \Gamma(\gamma l, \lambda) = \frac {\lambda^{\gamma l}}{\Gamma (\gamma l)} x^{\gamma l-1}e^{-\lambda x}.$$

### Inhomogenous process {#Inhomogenous_process}

It is also possible to allow the shape parameter $\gamma$ to vary as a function of time, $\gamma(t)$.[^n4]

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Mean and variance {#Mean_and_variance}

Because the value at each time $t$ has mean $\gamma t/\lambda$ and variance $\gamma t/\lambda^2,$[^n5] the gamma process is sometimes also parameterised in terms of the mean ($\mu$) and variance ($v$) of the increase per unit time. These satisfy $\gamma = \mu^2/v$ and $\lambda = \mu/v$.

### Scaling {#Scaling}

Multiplication of a gamma process by a scalar constant $\alpha$ is again a gamma process with different mean increase rate.

$$\alpha\Gamma(t;\gamma,\lambda) \simeq \Gamma(t;\gamma,\lambda/\alpha)$$

### Adding independent processes {#Adding_independent_processes}

The sum of two independent gamma processes is again a gamma process.

$$\Gamma(t;\gamma_1,\lambda) + \Gamma(t;\gamma_2,\lambda) \simeq \Gamma(t;\gamma_1+\gamma_2,\lambda)$$

### Moments {#Moments}

The [moment function](wikipedia://en/368684) helps mathematicians find expected values, variances, skewness, and kurtosis.

$$\operatorname E(X_t^n) = \lambda^{-n} \cdot \frac{\Gamma(\gamma t+n)}{\Gamma(\gamma t)},\ \quad n\geq 0 ,$$

where $\Gamma(z)$ is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

### Moment generating function {#Moment_generating_function}

The [moment generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function) is the expected value of $\exp(tX)$ where X is the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685).

$$\operatorname E\Big(\exp(\theta X_t)\Big) = \left(1- \frac\theta\lambda\right)^{-\gamma t},\ \quad \theta<\lambda$$

### Correlation {#Correlation}

[Correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) displays the statistical relationship between any two gamma processes.

$$\operatorname{Corr}(X_s, X_t) = \sqrt{\frac s t},\ s<t$$

, for any gamma process $X(t) .$

:::

## Related processes {#Related_processes}

The gamma process is used as the distribution for random time change in the [variance gamma process](wikipedia://en/20809590). Specifically, combining Brownian motion with a gamma process produces a variance gamma process,[^n6] and a variance gamma process can be written as the difference of two gamma processes.[^n3]
