# Conjugacy-closed subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conjugacy-closed subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **conjugacy-closed** if any two elements of the subgroup that are [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) in the group are also conjugate in the subgroup.[^o2]

:::

An alternative characterization of conjugacy-closed [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) is that all [class automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_automorphism) of the whole group [restrict](wikipedia://en/1589032) to class automorphisms of the subgroup.

The following facts are true regarding conjugacy-closed subgroups:

- Every central factor (a subgroup that may occur as a factor in some [central product](wikipedia://en/4842988)) is a conjugacy-closed subgroup.
- Every conjugacy-closed normal subgroup is a [transitively normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/4938714).
- The property of being conjugacy-closed is [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463), that is, every conjugacy-closed subgroup of a conjugacy-closed subgroup is conjugacy-closed.

The property of being conjugacy-closed is sometimes also termed as being **conjugacy stable**. It is a known result that for finite [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634), the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) of the base [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is a conjugacy-closed subgroup of the general linear group over the extension field. This result is typically referred to as a **stability theorem**.

A subgroup is said to be **strongly conjugacy-closed** if all intermediate subgroups are also conjugacy-closed.
