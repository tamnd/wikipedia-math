# Fractal curve

*Image: Gosper 6.gif.* Construction of the [Gosper curve](wikipedia://en/3918047)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fractal curve"}

A **fractal curve** is loosely a mathematical [curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve_%28mathematics%29) whose [shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_%28geometry%29) retains the same general pattern of [irregularity](wikipedia://en/372742), regardless of how high it is magnified or [scaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_%28mathematics%29), that is, its graph is either space-filling or takes the form of a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913).[^o2]

:::

In general, fractal curves are nowhere [rectifiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifiable_curve) — that is, they do not have finite [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curve_length) — and every [subarc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarc) longer than a single [point](wikipedia://en/593693) has [infinite length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_length).[^o3] A famous example is the boundary of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562).

## In nature {#In_nature}

Fractal curves and fractal patterns are widespread in [nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature), found in such places as [broccoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broccoli), [snowflakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflakes), feet of [geckos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geckos), [frost crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_crystals), and [lightning bolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_strike).[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8]

See also [Romanesco broccoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesco_broccoli), [dendrite crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrite_%28crystal%29), [trees, fractals](wikipedia://en/35659147#Trees,_fractals), [Hofstadter's butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter's_butterfly), [Lichtenberg figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichtenberg_figure), and [self-organized criticality](wikipedia://en/718855).

## Dimension {#Dimension}

Mathematical curves are [one dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_dimensional) spaces. However, fractal curves have different [fractal dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) or [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294)[^o10] (see [list of fractals by Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/2506864)).

*Image: Mandelbrot sequence new.gif.* Zooming in on the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562)

## Relationship to other fields {#Relationship_to_other_fields}

Starting in the 1950s, [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999) and others have studied [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782) of fractal curves, and have applied theory of fractals to modelling [natural phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_phenomena). Self-similarity occurs, and analysis of these patterns has found fractal curves in such diverse fields as [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), [geomorphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology), [human physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_physiology) and [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics).

As examples, "landscapes" revealed by [microscopic views](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscopic_view) of [surfaces](wikipedia://en/50328015) in connection with [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), [vascular networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_network), and shapes of [polymer molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer_molecule) all relate to fractal curves.[^o2]
