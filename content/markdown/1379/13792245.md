# Polychoric correlation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polychoric correlation"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **polychoric correlation**[^o2] is a technique for estimating the [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) between two hypothesised [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) continuous [latent variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latent_variable), from two observed [ordinal variables](wikipedia://en/821148). [Tetrachoric correlation](wikipedia://en/61567850) is a special case of the polychoric correlation applicable when both observed variables are [dichotomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichotomous_variable). These names derive from the polychoric and tetrachoric series which are used for estimation of these correlations.

:::

## Applications and examples {#Applications_and_examples}

This technique is frequently applied when analysing items on self-report instruments such as [personality tests](wikipedia://en/609194) and [surveys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_survey) that often use [rating scales](wikipedia://en/5216974) with a small number of response options (e.g., strongly disagree to strongly agree). The smaller the number of response categories, the more a correlation between latent continuous variables will tend to be attenuated. Lee, Poon & Bentler (1995) have recommended a two-step approach to [factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492) for assessing the factor structure of tests involving ordinally measured items.[^o4] Kiwanuka and colleagues (2022) have also illustrated the application of polychoric correlations and polychoric [confirmatory factor analysis](wikipedia://en/11391242) in nursing science.[^o5] This aims to reduce the effect of statistical artifacts, such as the number of response scales or [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) of variables leading to items grouping together in factors. In some disciplines, the statistical technique is rarely applied; however, some scholars have demonstrated how it can be used as an alternative to the Pearson correlation.[^o6]
