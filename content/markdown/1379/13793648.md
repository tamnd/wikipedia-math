# Alexey Ivakhnenko

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexey Ivakhnenko"}

**Alexey Ivakhnenko** (Олексíй Григо́рович Іва́хненко; 30 March 1913 – 16 October 2007) was a Soviet and Ukrainian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) most famous for developing the [group method of data handling](wikipedia://en/13793747) (GMDH), a method of inductive statistical learning, for which he is considered as one of the founders of [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning).[^o3][^o24]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Aleksey was born in [Kobelyaky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobeliaky), [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate) in a family of teachers.[^o5] In 1932 he graduated from Electrotechnical college in Kyiv and worked for two years as an engineer on the construction of the largest power plant in [Berezniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezniki). Then in 1938, after graduation from the [Leningrad Electrotechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Electrotechnical_University), Ivakhnenko worked in the [All-Union Electrotechnical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Union_Electrotechnical_Institute) in Moscow during [wartime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). There he investigated the problems of [automatic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_control) in the laboratory, led by [Sergey Lebedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Lebedev_%28scientist%29).

He continued research in other institutions in Ukraine after his return to Kyiv in 1944. In that year he received the Ph.D. degree and later, in 1954 received the D.Sc. degree. In 1964, he was appointed as a Head of the Department of Combined Control Systems at the Institute of Cybernetics. Simultaneously working at first as a Lecturer, and from 1961, as a Professor of Automatic Control and Technical Cybernetics at the [Kyiv Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sikorsky_Kyiv_Polytechnic_Institute).

## Research {#Research}

Ivakhnenko is known to be the founder of Inductive modelling, a scientific approach used for pattern recognition and complex systems forecasting.[^o7] He had used this approach during development of the [group method of data handling](wikipedia://en/13793747) (GMDH). In 1968 the journal "Avtomatika" had published his article "Group Method of Data Handling – a rival of the method of stochastic approximation",[^o8] marking the beginning of a new stage in his scientific work. He led the development of this approach, with a professional team of mathematicians and engineers at the Institute of Cybernetics.

### Group method of data handling {#Group_method_of_data_handling}

The [GMDH](wikipedia://en/13793747) method presents a unique approach to solving problems in [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) and even a new philosophy to [scientific research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_research), which became possible using modern computers.[^o10] A researcher may not adhere precisely to traditional [deductive](wikipedia://en/61093) way of building models "from general theory – to a particular model": monitoring an object, studying its structure, understanding the principles of its operation, developing [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) and testing the [model](wikipedia://en/20590) of an object. Instead, the new approach is proposed "from specified data – to a general model": after the input of data, a researcher selects a class of models, the type of models-variants generation and sets the [criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_criterion_%28statistics%29) for model selection. As most routine work is transferred to a computer, the impact of human influence on the objective result is minimised. In fact, this approach can be considered as one of the implementations of the artificial intelligence thesis, which states that a computer can act as powerful advisor to humans.

The development of GMDH consists of a synthesis of ideas from different areas of science: the cybernetic concept of "[black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box)" and the principle of successive [genetic selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_%28genetic_algorithm%29) of pairwise [features](wikipedia://en/1179950), [Godel's incompleteness theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godel's_incompleteness_theorems) and the [Gabor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Gabor) principle of "freedom of decisions choice",[^o11] the [Adhémar's](wikipedia://en/373812) incorrectness and the [Beer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Stafford_Beer) principle of external additions.[^o12]

GMDH is the original method for solving problems for structural-parametric [identification](wikipedia://en/552466) of models for [experimental data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_data) under [uncertainty](wikipedia://en/63778).[^o13] Such a problem occurs in the construction of a [mathematical model](wikipedia://en/20590) that approximates the unknown pattern of investigated object or process.[^o14] It uses information about it that is implicitly contained in data. GMDH differs from other methods of modelling by the active application of the following [principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle): automatic models generation, inconclusive decisions, and consistent selection by external criteria for finding models of optimal complexity. It had an original multilayered procedure for automatic models structure generation, which imitates the evolutionary process of biological selection with consideration of pairwise successive features. Such procedure is currently used in [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) networks.[^o15] To compare and choose optimal models, two or more subsets of a data sample are used. This makes it possible to avoid preliminary assumptions, because sample division implicitly acknowledges different types of uncertainty during the automatic construction of the optimal model.

In the early 1980s Ivakhnenko had established an organic analogy between the problem of constructing models for noisy data and signal passing through the [channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_%28communications%29) with [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_%28electronics%29).[^o28] This made possible to lay the foundations of the theory of noise-immune modelling.[^o13] The main result of this theory is that the complexity of optimal predictive model depends on the level of uncertainty in the data: the higher this level (e.g. due to noise) - the simpler must be the optimal model (with less estimated parameters). This initiated the development of the GMDH theory as an [inductive](wikipedia://en/211399) method of automatic adaptation of optimal model complexity to the level of information in [fuzzy data](wikipedia://en/56601). Therefore, GMDH is often considered to be the original information technology for [knowledge extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_extraction) from [experimental data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_data).

### Results {#Results}

Alongside to GMDH, Ivakhnenko had developed the following set of results:

- Principle of construction of self-organizing deep learning networks.[^o7]
- Theory of models self-organization according to experimental data.[^o31]
- Method of control with forecast optimization.[^o29]
- New principles of [automatic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) of speed for [AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_motor) and [asynchronous electric motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induction_motor).[^o18]
- Theory of invariant systems for [adaptive control](wikipedia://en/2020708) with compensation of measured disturbances.[^o19] He had developed the principle of indirect measurement of disturbances, called as "differential fork" that was used later in practice.
- Principle of combined control (with negative feedback for the controlled variables and positive feedback for the controlled disturbances).[^o20] A number of such systems, for the speed control of electric motors had been implemented in practice. That proved the practical feasibility of invariant conditions in a combined control systems that unite the advantages of closed systems for control by deviation (high precision) and open systems (performance).
- The non-searching extreme regulators on the basis of situations recognition.[^o22]
- Principle of self-learning pattern recognition. It was demonstrated at first in the cognitive system "Alpha",[^o14] created under his leadership.
- Basis for the construction of cybernetic prediction devices.[^o21]
- Noise-immune principles of robust modelling for data with noises.[^o13]
- Design of multilayered [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network) with active neurons, where each neuron is an algorithm.

Ivakhnenko is well known for his achievements in the theory of invariance and theory of combined automatic control systems, that operates on the principle of measured disturbances compensation. He had developed devices and methods for the adaptive control of systems with magnetic amplifiers and motors.

He is the author of the first Soviet monograph on [engineering cybernetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_cybernetics),[^o22] which was published worldwide in seven languages.[^o23] In his study, a further development of the principles of combined control was connected with the implementation of methods of [evolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) [self-organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organisation), [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706) and [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074) in [control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_system).

In recent years, his main innovation - the GMDH method - was developed as a method of inductive modelling, complex processes, and systems [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074). His ideas are utilised now in [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) networks.[^o24] The effectiveness of the method was confirmed repeatedly during the solution of real complex problems in [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology), which aided increase its popularity among the international scientific community.[^o25] In parallel, there were conducted developments of evolutionary self-organising [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) in a related field - [clustering](wikipedia://en/669675) problems of pattern recognition.[^o26] Advances in the modelling of environmental processes reflected in the monographs,[^o27][^o28] economic processes - in the books.[^o29] [^o30] The results of exploration of [recurrent](wikipedia://en/1706303) multilayered GMDH algorithms are described in the books.[^o31][^o7]

## Scientific school {#Scientific_school}

From 1963 to 1989 Ivakhnenko was the editor of the specialized scientific journal "Avtomatika" (later "Problems of management and computer science"), that was important for the formation and development of the Ukrainian school of inductive modelling. Throughout these years the magazine was translated and reprinted in the United States as "Soviet Automatic Control" (later "Journal of Automation and Information Sciences").

Ivakhnenko maintained an active teaching career, at first as the Assistant Professor at the Department of Theoretical Mechanics, and then at the Control Systems faculty. Since 1960, as Professor of the Department of Technical Cybernetics in [Kyiv Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Polytechnic_Institute), he lectured at the university, and had multiple graduate students. In 1958-1964 he was an organiser of the All-Union Conferences of Invariance in Kiev, where the development of the invariant control systems theory was restored after prohibition.[^o33]

More than 220 young scientists prepared and successfully defended their candidate dissertations under his leadership in the KPI and the Institute of Cybernetics and nearly 30 of his students defended their post-doctoral dissertations. Furthermore, his students V.M.Kuntsevych, V.I.Kostyuk, V.I.Ivanenko, V.I.Vasiliev, A.A.Pavlov and others had created their own scientific schools.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Ivakhnenko is the Honorary Scientist of the USSR (1972), two-time winner of the State Prize (1991, 1997) for his works on the theory of invariant automatic systems and set of publications on Information technology in the field of Artificial intelligence. Author of 40 books and over 500 scientific articles. Honorary Doctor of [National Technical University "KPI"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sikorsky_Kyiv_Polytechnic_Institute) (2003) and [Lviv Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_Polytechnic) (2005). He was the Corresponding Member of Academy of Sciences USSR (1961) and Academician of [NAS of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine) (2003).
