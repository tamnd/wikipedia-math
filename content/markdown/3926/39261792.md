# Louis Rosenhead

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis Rosenhead"}

**Louis Rosenhead** [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE) (1 January 1906 – 10 November 1984) was a British mathematician noted for his work on [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), and was head of the Department of Applied Mathematics at [Liverpool University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_University) from 1933 to 1973.[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Rosenhead was born in [Mabgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabgate), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), on 1 January 1906 to parents from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), the first of three children.[^o3] His parents Abram Rozenkopf (born 1879) and Chaja Nagacz (born 1884) came from adjacent villages in Poland. They were married in Leeds in 1905, adopting the Anglicized versions of their names: Abraham and Ellen Rosenhead. Abraham was a tailor who did his national service in Russia and came to England in 1903; Ellen came in 1902.[^o3] They were [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish).[^o4]

Rosenhead married Esther Brostoff in Leeds in 1932. Together they had two sons, Martin and [Jonathan](wikipedia://en/62161979).[^o3]

Rosenhead died 10 November 1984.[^o3]

## Education {#Education}

Rosenhead matriculated from Leeds High School and went to the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) to study medicine, but after four weeks changed his studies to mathematics. His decision was influenced by [Selig Brodetsky](wikipedia://en/4601432). Rosenhead graduated with first class honours in 1926, and continuing, eventually to attain a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1928, studying under Brodetsky. He then earned another Ph.D. at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), studying under [Harold Jeffreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Jeffreys). This was followed by spending the academic year 1930-31 in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) where [Ludwig Prandtl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Prandtl) was active. Rosenhead spent time there along with [Sydney Goldstein](wikipedia://en/6346312) and [H. B. Squire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Brian_Squire), all working on theoretical fluid mechanics.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

In 1931 Rosenhead became assistant lecturer in applied mathematics at [University College, Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Swansea). In 1933 he replaced [Joseph Proudman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Proudman) as professor of applied mathematics at Liverpool.[^o3]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Rosenhead was superintendent of [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics) at the Projectile Development Establishment and collected a team of mathematicians to work on rocket weapons.

Rosenhead was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1946.[^o3]

In 1947 he was instrumental in developing statistics and in the appointment of [Robin Plackett](wikipedia://en/16157376). His colleagues also included [Maurice Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Bartlett), [P. A. P. Moran](wikipedia://en/6876839) and [D. G. Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._G._Kendall).
