# Robert Lawson Vaught

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Lawson Vaught"}

**Robert Lawson Vaught** (April 4, 1926 – April 2, 2002) was an American [mathematical logician](wikipedia://en/19636) and one of the founders of [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Vaught was a musical prodigy in his youth, in his case playing the piano. He began his university studies at [Pomona College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona_College), at age 16. When [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) broke out, he enlisted into the [US Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Navy), which assigned him to the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California)'s [V-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-12_Navy_College_Training_Program) program. He graduated in 1945 with an AB in physics.

In 1946, he began a Ph.D. in mathematics at Berkeley. He initially worked under the supervision of the topologist [John L. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Kelley), writing on [C\* algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*_algebra). In 1950, in response to [McCarthyite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism) pressures, Berkeley required all staff to sign a [loyalty oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalty_oath). Kelley declined and moved his career to [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) for three years. Vaught then began afresh under the supervision of [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), completing in 1954 a thesis on [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), titled *Topics in the Theory of Arithmetical Classes and Boolean Algebras*. After spending four years at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), Vaught returned to Berkeley in 1958, where he remained until his 1991 retirement.

In 1957, Vaught married Marilyn Maca; they had two children.

## Work {#Work}

Vaught's work is primarily focused on [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). In 1957, he and Tarski introduced [elementary submodels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_submodel) and the [Tarski–Vaught test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarski–Vaught_test) characterizing them. In 1962, he and [Michael D. Morley](wikipedia://en/2921675) pioneered the concept of a [saturated structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_structure). His investigations on countable models of first-order theories led him to the [Vaught conjecture](wikipedia://en/13797188) stating that the [number](wikipedia://en/6174) of countable models of a complete first-order theory (in a countable language) is always either finite, or countably infinite, or equinumerous with the real numbers. Vaught's ["Never 2" theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaught's_theorem) states that a complete first-order theory cannot have exactly two nonisomorphic countable models.

He considered his best work his paper "Invariant sets in topology and logic", introducing the [Vaught transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaught_transform). He is known for the Tarski–Vaught test for elementary substructures, the [Feferman–Vaught theorem](wikipedia://en/69955049), the [Łoś–Vaught test](wikipedia://en/22229525) for completeness and decidability, the Vaught two-cardinal theorem, and his conjecture on the nonfinite axiomatizability of totally [categorical theories](wikipedia://en/1096396) (this work eventually led to [geometric stability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_stability_theory)).
