# Axiomatic quantum field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Axiomatic quantum field theory"}

**Axiomatic quantum field theory** is a mathematical discipline which aims to describe [quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field_theories) in terms of rigorous axioms. It is strongly associated with [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987), but has also been studied in recent years from a more geometric and functorial perspective.

:::

There are two main challenges in this discipline. First, one must propose a set of axioms which describe the general properties of any mathematical object that deserves to be called a "quantum field theory". Then, one gives rigorous mathematical constructions of examples satisfying these axioms.

## Analytic approaches {#Analytic_approaches}

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Wightman axioms"}

### Wightman axioms {#Wightman_axioms}

The first set of axioms for quantum field theories, known as the [Wightman axioms](wikipedia://en/293622), were proposed by [Arthur Wightman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Wightman) in the early 1950s. These axioms attempt to describe QFTs on flat Minkowski spacetime by regarding quantum fields as operator-valued distributions acting on a Hilbert space. In practice, one often uses the Wightman reconstruction theorem, which guarantees that the operator-valued distributions and the Hilbert space can be recovered from the collection of [correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Osterwalder–Schrader axioms"}

### Osterwalder–Schrader axioms {#Osterwalder–Schrader_axioms}

The correlation functions of a QFT satisfying the Wightman axioms often can be [analytically continued](wikipedia://en/80842) from [Lorentz signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_signature) to [Euclidean signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_signature). (Crudely, one replaces the time variable $\;t\;$ with imaginary time $\;\tau = -\sqrt{-1\,}\,t~;$ the factors of $\;\sqrt{-1\,}\;$ change the sign of the time-time components of the metric tensor.) The resulting functions are called [Schwinger functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwinger_functions). For the Schwinger functions there is a list of conditions — [analyticity](wikipedia://en/80842), [permutation symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_symmetry), [Euclidean covariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_covariance), and [reflection positivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_positivity) — which a set of functions defined on various powers of Euclidean space-time must satisfy in order to be the analytic continuation of the set of correlation functions of a QFT satisfying the Wightman axioms.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Haag–Kastler axioms"}

### Haag–Kastler axioms {#Haag–Kastler_axioms}

The [Haag–Kastler axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haag–Kastler_axioms) axiomatize QFT in terms of nets of algebras.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Euclidean CFT axioms"}

### Euclidean CFT axioms {#Euclidean_CFT_axioms}

These axioms (see e.g.[^o11]) are used in the [conformal bootstrap](wikipedia://en/47273717) approach to [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774) in $\mathbb{R}^d$. They are also referred to as **Euclidean bootstrap axioms**.

:::
