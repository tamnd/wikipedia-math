# Lénárt sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lénárt sphere"}

*Image: Istvan Lenart and spheres.jpg.* István Lénárt demonstrating a number of Lénárt spheres.

A **Lénárt sphere** is an educational [manipulative](wikipedia://en/6791734) and [writing surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_material) for exploring [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), invented by [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) István Lénárt as a modern replacement for a spherical [blackboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboard).[^o2][^o3] It can be used for visualizing the geometry of [points](wikipedia://en/593693), [great and small circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_a_sphere), [triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_triangle), [polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_polygon), [conics](wikipedia://en/68189121), and other objects on a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), and comparing spherical geometry to [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) as drawn on a flat piece of paper or blackboard. The included spherical [ruler](wikipedia://en/65593) and [compass](wikipedia://en/492445) support [synthetic](wikipedia://en/267484) [straightedge and compass construction](wikipedia://en/61229) on the sphere.

:::

## Products {#Products}

*Image: Lenart sphere equilateral triangle.jpg.* Equilateral triangle constructed on a Lenart sphere.

The Lénárt sphere and accessories are produced by the company Lénárt Educational Research and Technology.

The basic set includes:[^o5]

- An eight-inch transparent plastic [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859)
- A [torus](wikipedia://en/74800)-shaped support to place under the sphere
- Hemispherical [transparencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_%28projection%29) that fit over the sphere for students to draw on with [marker pens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marker_pen) or cut out shapes with [scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scissors)
- A spherical ruler with two scaled edges for drawing [great-circle arcs](wikipedia://en/48082) and measuring [spherical angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_angle) and [great-circle distances](wikipedia://en/379733)
- A spherical compass and [center](wikipedia://en/4850775) locator for drawing [small circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_circle)
- A set of [wet-wipe markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet-wipe_marker) for writing and drawing on the sphere and transparencies
- A hanger for displaying spherical constructions and designs
- A 16-page booklet of suggested activities, "Getting Started on the Lenart Sphere"
- A four-color [polyconic projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyconic_projection) of the earth that one can cut out and transform into a globe

The company also sells replacement parts, extra transparency sheets, [wet-wipe markers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet-wipe_marker), and Lénárt's book *Non-Euclidean Adventures on the Lenart Sphere*, which describes more activities for students.

## Related tools {#Related_tools}

[Spherical Easel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Easel) is an [interactive geometry software](wikipedia://en/1196020) tool for exploring spherical geometry (see #External links). Other interactive geometry software is typically limited to the flat plane.

## History {#History}

[Spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) is fundamental to ancient [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology), [celestial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_navigation), and [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608) and [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography), and it used to be a standard part of undergraduate [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471). In recent decades [hand computations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) have been replaced by [electronic computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer) and spherical trigonometry has been pushed out of the typical mathematics curriculum by other topics.[^o2]

The Lénárt sphere was invented by István Lénárt in Hungary in the early 1990s and its use is described in his 2003 book comparing planar and spherical geometry.[^o8]

The Lénárt sphere is widely used throughout Europe in university courses on [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) and [geographic information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_system) (GIS).
