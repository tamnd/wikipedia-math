# Isoperimetric point

*Image: Isoperimetric point.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isoperimetric point"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **isoperimetric point** is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) — a special point associated with a plane [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). The term was originally introduced by G.R. Veldkamp in a paper published in the [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) in 1985 to denote a point $P$ in the plane of a triangle $\triangle ABC$ having the property that the triangles $\triangle PBC, \triangle PCA, \triangle PAB$ have equal perimeters, that is, having the property that[^o2][^o3]

:::

$$\begin{aligned} & \overline{PB} + \overline{BC} + \overline{CP}, \\ =\ & \overline{PC} + \overline{CA} + \overline{AP}, \\ =\ & \overline{PA} + \overline{AB} + \overline{BP}. \end{aligned}$$

Isoperimetric points in the sense of Veldkamp exist only for triangles satisfying certain conditions. The isoperimetric point of $\triangle ABC$ in the sense of Veldkamp, if it exists, has the following [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790).[^o4]

$$\sec\tfrac{A}{2} \cos\tfrac{B}{2} \cos\tfrac{C}{2} - 1 \ : \ \sec\tfrac{B}{2} \cos\tfrac{C}{2} \cos\tfrac{A}{2} - 1 \ : \ \sec\tfrac{C}{2} \cos\tfrac{A}{2} \cos\tfrac{B}{2} - 1$$

Given any triangle $\triangle ABC$ one can associate with it a point $P$ having trilinear coordinates as given above. This point is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) and in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) (ETC) it is called the isoperimetric point of the triangle $\triangle ABC$. It is designated as the triangle center *X*(175).[^o5] The point *X*(175) need not be an isoperimetric point of triangle $\triangle ABC$ in the sense of Veldkamp. However, if isoperimetric point of triangle $\triangle ABC$ in the sense of Veldkamp exists, then it would be identical to the point *X*(175).

The point $P$ with the property that the triangles $\triangle PBC, \triangle PCA, \triangle PAB$ have equal perimeters has been studied as early as 1890 in an article by [Emile Lemoine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Lemoine).[^o5][^o6]

## Existence of isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp {#Existence_of_isoperimetric_point_in_the_sense_of_Veldkamp}

*Image: Isoperimetric point 02.svg.* A triangle $\triangle ABC$ in which the triangle center *X*(175) is not the isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp.

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Let the sidelengths of this triangle be $a, b, c$. Let its circumradius be $R$ and inradius be $r$. The necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp can be stated as follows.[^o2]

The triangle $\triangle ABC$ has an isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp if and only if

$$a + b + c > 4R + r.$$

For all acute angled triangles $\triangle ABC$ we have $a + b + c > 4R + r$, and so all acute angled triangles have isoperimetric points in the sense of Veldkamp.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $P$ denote the triangle center *X*(175) of triangle $\triangle ABC$.[^o5]

- $P$ lies on the line joining the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) and the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) of $\triangle ABC$.
- If $P$ is an isoperimetric point of $\triangle ABC$ in the sense of Veldkamp, then the [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of triangles $\triangle PBC, \triangle PCA, \triangle PAB$ are pairwise tangent to one another and $P$ is their radical center.
- If $P$ is an isoperimetric point of $\triangle ABC$ in the sense of Veldkamp, then the perimeters of $\triangle PBC, \triangle PCA, \triangle PAB$ are equal to

$$\frac{2 \triangle}{\bigl| 4R + r - (a+b+c) \bigr|}$$

where $\triangle$ is the area, $R$ is the circumradius, $r$ is the inradius, and $a, b, c$ are the sidelengths of $\triangle ABC$.[^o11]

:::

## Soddy circles {#Soddy_circles}

*Image: Inner and outer Soddy points 02.svg.* Inner and outer Soddy circles in the case where the outer Soddy point is an isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp.

*Image: Inner and outer Soddy points 01.svg.* Inner and outer Soddy circles in the case where the outer Soddy point is not an isoperimetric point in the sense of Veldkamp.

Given a triangle $\triangle ABC$ one can draw circles in the plane of $\triangle ABC$ with centers at $A, B, C$ such that they are tangent to each other externally. In general, one can draw two new circles such that each of them is tangential to the three circles with $A, B, C$ as centers. (One of the circles may degenerate into a straight line.) These circles are the [Soddy circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soddy_circles) of $\triangle ABC$. The circle with the smaller radius is the *inner Soddy circle* and its center is called the *inner Soddy point* or *inner Soddy center* of $\triangle ABC$. The circle with the larger radius is the *outer Soddy circle* and its center is called the *outer Soddy point* or *outer Soddy center* of triangle $\triangle ABC$. [^o11][^o12]

The triangle center *X*(175), the isoperimetric point in the sense of Kimberling, is the outer Soddy point of $\triangle ABC$.
