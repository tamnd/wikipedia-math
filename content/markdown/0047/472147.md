# Divisible group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Divisible group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **divisible group** is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) in which every element can, in some sense, be divided by positive integers, or more accurately, every element is an *n*th multiple for each positive integer *n*. Divisible groups are important in understanding the structure of abelian groups, especially because they are the [injective](wikipedia://en/473775) abelian groups.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

An abelian group $(G, +)$ is **divisible** if, for every positive integer $n$ and every $g \in G$, there exists $y \in G$ such that $ny=g$.[^o4] An equivalent condition is: for any positive integer $n$, $nG=G$, since the existence of $y$ for every $n$ and $g$ implies that $n G\supseteq G$, and the other direction $n G\subseteq G$ is true for every group. A third equivalent condition is that an abelian group $G$ is divisible if and only if $G$ is an [injective object](wikipedia://en/473779) in the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748); for this reason, a divisible group is sometimes called an **injective group**.

An abelian group is $p$-**divisible** for a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ if for every $g \in G$, there exists $y \in G$ such that $py=g$. Equivalently, an abelian group is $p$-divisible if and only if $pG=G$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb Q$ form a divisible group under addition.
- More generally, the underlying additive group of any [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over $\mathbb Q$ is divisible.
- Every [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a divisible group is divisible. Thus, $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is divisible.
- The *p*-[primary component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_component) $\mathbb Z[1/p]/\mathbb Z$ of $\mathbb Q/ \mathbb Z$, which is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the *p*-[quasicyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicyclic_group) $\mathbb Z[p^\infty]$, is divisible.
- The multiplicative group of the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb C^*$ is divisible.
- Every [existentially closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existentially_closed) abelian group (in the [model theoretic](wikipedia://en/19858) sense) is divisible.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If a divisible group is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of an abelian group then it is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of that abelian group.[^o9]
- Every abelian group can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in a divisible group.[^o10] Put another way, the category of abelian groups [has enough injectives](wikipedia://en/473779#Enough_injectives_and_injective_hulls).
- Non-trivial divisible groups are not [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/58527).
- Further, every abelian group can be embedded in a divisible group as an [essential subgroup](wikipedia://en/19202990) in a unique way.[^o11]
- An abelian group is divisible if and only if it is *p*-divisible for every prime *p*.
- Let $A$ be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). If $T$ is a divisible group, then $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{Z}\text{-Mod}} (A,T)$ is injective in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of $A$-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410).[^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Structure theorem of divisible groups"}

## Structure theorem of divisible groups {#Structure_theorem_of_divisible_groups}

Let *G* be a divisible group. Then the [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052) Tor(*G*) of *G* is divisible. Since a divisible group is an [injective module](wikipedia://en/473775), Tor(*G*) is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of *G*. So

$$G = \mathrm{Tor}(G) \oplus G/\mathrm{Tor}(G).$$

As a quotient of a divisible group, *G*/Tor(*G*) is divisible. Moreover, it is [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/3039330). Thus, it is a vector space over **Q** and so there exists a set *I* such that

$$G/\mathrm{Tor}(G) = \bigoplus_{i \in I} \mathbb Q = \mathbb Q^{(I)}.$$

The structure of the torsion subgroup is harder to determine, but one can show[^n1][^n2] that for all [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* there exists $I_p$ such that

$$(\mathrm{Tor}(G))_p = \bigoplus_{i \in I_p} \mathbb Z[p^\infty] = \mathbb Z[p^\infty]^{(I_p)},$$

where $(\mathrm{Tor}(G))_p$ is the *p*-primary component of Tor(*G*).

Thus, if **P** is the set of prime numbers,

$$G = \left(\bigoplus_{p \in \mathbf P} \mathbb Z[p^\infty]^{(I_p)}\right) \oplus \mathbb Q^{(I)}.$$

The cardinalities of the sets *I* and *I*<sub>*p*</sub> for *p* ∈ **P** are uniquely determined by the group *G*.

:::

## Injective envelope {#Injective_envelope}

As stated above, any abelian group *A* can be uniquely embedded in a divisible group *D* as an [essential subgroup](wikipedia://en/19202990). This divisible group *D* is the **injective envelope** of *A*, and this concept is the [injective hull](wikipedia://en/665821) in the category of abelian groups.

::: {#o19 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Reduced abelian group"}

## Reduced abelian groups {#Reduced_abelian_groups}

An abelian group is said to be **reduced** if its only divisible subgroup is {0}. Every abelian group is the direct sum of a divisible subgroup and a reduced subgroup. In fact, there is a unique largest divisible subgroup of any group, and this divisible subgroup is a direct summand.[^o20] This is a special feature of [hereditary rings](wikipedia://en/5123176) like the integers **Z**: the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of injective modules is injective because the ring is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129), and the quotients of injectives are injective because the ring is hereditary, so any submodule generated by injective modules is injective. The converse is a result of (Matlis 1958): if every module has a unique maximal injective submodule, then the ring is hereditary.

A complete classification of countable reduced periodic abelian groups is given by [Ulm's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm's_theorem).

:::

::: {#o23 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

Several distinct definitions generalize divisible groups to divisible modules. The following definitions have been used in the literature to define a **divisible [module](wikipedia://en/276410)** *M* over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R*:

1. *rM* = *M* for all nonzero *r* in *R*.[^n3] (It is sometimes required that *r* is not a zero-divisor, and some authors[^n4] require that *R* is a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627).)
1. For every principal left [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *Ra*, any [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/474118) from *Ra* into *M* extends to a homomorphism from *R* into *M*.[^n5][^n6] (This type of divisible module is also called *principally injective module*.)
1. For every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) left ideal *L* of *R*, any homomorphism from *L* into *M* extends to a homomorphism from *R* into *M*.

The last two conditions are "restricted versions" of the [Baer's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baer's_criterion) for [injective modules](wikipedia://en/473775). Since injective left modules extend homomorphisms from *all* left ideals to *R*, injective modules are clearly divisible in sense 2 and 3.

If *R* is additionally a domain then all three definitions coincide. If *R* is a principal left ideal domain, then divisible modules coincide with injective modules.[^n7] Thus in the case of the ring of integers **Z**, which is a principal ideal domain, a **Z**-module (which is exactly an abelian group) is divisible if and only if it is injective.

If *R* is a [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) domain, then the injective *R* modules coincide with the divisible *R* modules if and only if *R* is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132).[^n7]

:::
