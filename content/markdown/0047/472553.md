# Argumentum ad crumenam

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Argumentum ad crumenam"}

An ***argumentum ad crumenam*** [argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_argument), also known as **an argument to the purse**, is the [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450) of drawing conclusions based on the speaker's financial status.[^o2][^o3] The term generally refers to the assumption that having wealth is indicative of insight or virtue, and that poverty denotes the opposite.[^o2]

:::

The opposite is the *[argumentum ad lazarum](wikipedia://en/472557)*.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- "If you're so smart, why aren't you rich?"
- "This new law is a good idea. Most of the people against it are riff-raff who make less than \$20,000 a year."
- "[Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett) is hosting a seminar. This seminar is better than others, because Warren Buffett is richer than most people."

:::
