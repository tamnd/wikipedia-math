# Partition of an interval

*Image: Integral Riemann sum.png.* A partition of an interval being used in a [Riemann sum](wikipedia://en/176478). The partition itself is shown in grey at the bottom, with the norm of the partition indicated in red.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Partition of an interval"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **partition** of an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $[a, b]$ on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) is a finite [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $x_{0}, x_{1}, x_{2}, \ldots, x_{n}$ of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) such that

:::

$$a = x_{0} < x_{1} < x_{2} < \ldots < x_{n} = b.$$

In other terms, a partition of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) interval $I$ is a strictly increasing sequence of numbers (belonging to the interval $I$ itself) starting from the initial point of $I$ and arriving at the final point of $I$.

Every interval of the form $[x_{i}, x_{i + 1}]$ is referred to as a **subinterval** of the partition *x*.

## Refinement of a partition {#Refinement_of_a_partition}

Another partition $Q$ of the given interval \[a, b\] is defined as a **refinement of the partition** $P$, if $Q$ contains all the points of $P$ and possibly some other points as well; the partition $Q$ is said to be “finer” than $P$. Given two partitions, $P$ and $Q$, one can always form their **common refinement**, denoted $P \lor Q$, which consists of all the points of $P$ and $Q$, in increasing order.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Mesh of a partition"}

## Norm of a partition {#Norm_of_a_partition}

The **norm** (or **mesh**) of the partition

$$x_{0} < x_{1} < x_{2} < \ldots < x_{n}$$

is the length of the longest of these subintervals[^o6][^o7]

$\{\{\operatorname{math}|\max x_{i} - x_{i-1}$ : *i* = 1, … , *n* }}}.

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Partitions are used in the theory of the [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390), the [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491) and the [regulated integral](wikipedia://en/6818116). Specifically, as finer partitions of a given interval are considered, their mesh approaches zero and the [Riemann sum](wikipedia://en/176478) based on a given partition approaches the [Riemann integral](wikipedia://en/26390).[^o10]

:::

## Tagged partitions {#Tagged_partitions}

A **tagged partition** or Perron Partition is a partition of a given interval together with a finite sequence of numbers $t_{0}, \ldots, t_{n - 1}$ subject to the conditions that for each $i$,

$$x_{i} \le t_{i} \le x_{i + 1}.$$

In other words, a tagged partition is a partition together with a distinguished point of every subinterval: its mesh is defined in the same way as for an ordinary partition.[^o12]
