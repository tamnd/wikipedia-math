# David Wheeler (computer scientist)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Wheeler"}

**David John Wheeler** (9 February 1927 – 13 December 2004)[^o12][^o13][^o14] was an English [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) and professor of computer science at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o15][^o16][^o17]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Wheeler was born in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), England, the second of the three children of (Agnes) Marjorie, *née* Gudgeon, and Arthur Wheeler, a press tool maker, engineer, and proprietor of a small shopfitting firm.[^o19] He was educated at a local primary school in Birmingham and then went on to [King Edward VI Camp Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_Camp_Hill_School_for_Boys) after winning a scholarship in 1938. His education was disrupted by [World War&nbsp;II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War&nbsp;II), and he completed his sixth form studies at [Hanley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanley_High_School,_Stoke-on-Trent).[^o19] In 1945 he gained a scholarship to study the [Cambridge Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), graduating in 1948. He was awarded the world's first[^o21] PhD in computer science in 1951.[^o21][^o22]

## Career {#Career}

Wheeler's contributions to the field included work on the [Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Delay_Storage_Automatic_Calculator) (EDSAC) in the 1950s[^o24] and the [Burrows–Wheeler transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrows–Wheeler_transform) (published 1994). Along with [Maurice Wilkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Wilkes) and [Stanley Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Gill), he is credited with the invention around 1951 of the [subroutine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subroutine) (which they referred to as the *closed subroutine*), and gave the first explanation of how to design software libraries;[^o9] as a result, the *jump to subroutine* instruction was often called a [Wheeler Jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheeler_Jump). Wilkes published a paper in 1953 discussing relative [addressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addressing_mode) to facilitate the use of subroutines.[^o25] (However, [Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) had discussed subroutines in a paper of 1945 on design proposals for the NPL [ACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_Computing_Engine), going so far as to invent the concept of a return address stack.[^o26])

He was responsible for the implementation of the [CAP computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAP_computer), the first to be based on security capabilities. In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), he was the designer of [WAKE](wikipedia://en/599287) and the co-designer of the [TEA](wikipedia://en/528720) and [XTEA](wikipedia://en/530334) encryption algorithms together with [Roger Needham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Needham). In 1950, with Maurice Wilkes, he used EDSAC to solve a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) relating to [gene frequencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele_frequency) in a paper by [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824).[^o27] This represents the first use of a computer for a problem in the field of [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology).

He became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of [Darwin College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_College,_Cambridge) in 1964 and formally retired in 1994, although he continued to be an active member of the [University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge_Computer_Laboratory) until his death.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On 24 August 1957 Wheeler married astrophysics research student Joyce Margaret Blackler, who had used EDSAC for her own mathematical investigations as a research student from 1955. Together they had two daughters and a son.

Wheeler died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack) on 13 December 2004 while cycling home from the Computer Laboratory.[^o19]

## Recognition and legacy {#Recognition_and_legacy}

Wheeler:

- In 1981 was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)
- In 1985 received a [Computer Pioneer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Pioneer_Award) for his contributions to [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) programming
- In 1994 was inducted as a Fellow of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery)
- In 2003 was named a [Computer History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_History_Museum) Fellow Award recipient "for his invention of the closed subroutine, and for his architectural contributions to ILLIAC, the Cambridge Ring, and computer testing"[^o11]

The Computer Laboratory at the University of Cambridge annually holds the "Wheeler Lecture", a series of distinguished lectures named after him.[^o30]

## Quotes {#Quotes}

Wheeler is often quoted as saying "All problems in computer science can be solved by another level of [indirection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirection)."[^o32] or "All problems in computer science can be solved by another level of indirection, except for the problem of too many layers of indirection."[^o33] This has been called the [fundamental theorem of software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_software_engineering).

Another quotation attributed to him is "[Compatibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_compatibility) means deliberately repeating other people's mistakes."[^o34]
