# Modus ponendo tollens

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modus ponendo tollens"}

***Modus ponendo tollens*** (**MPT**;[^o2] [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): "mode that denies by affirming")[^o3] is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) for [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). It is closely related to *[modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900)* and *[modus tollendo ponens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modus_tollendo_ponens)*.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

MPT is usually described as having the form:

1. Not both A and B
1. A
1. Therefore, not B

For example:

1. Ann and Bill cannot both win the race.
1. Ann won the race.
1. Therefore, Bill cannot have won the race.

As [E. J. Lemmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._J._Lemmon) describes it: "*Modus ponendo tollens* is the principle that, if the negation of a conjunction holds and also one of its conjuncts, then the negation of its other conjunct holds."[^o5]

In [logic notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_logic_symbols) this can be represented as:

1. $$\neg (A \land B)$$
1. $$A$$
1. $$\therefore \neg B$$

Based on the [Sheffer Stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffer_Stroke) (alternative denial), "|", the inference can also be formalized in this way:

1. $$A\,|\,B$$
1. $$A$$
1. $$\therefore \neg B$$

## Proof {#Proof}

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| *Step* | *Proposition* | *Derivation* |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\neg (A \land B)$ | Given |
| 2 | $A$ | Given |
| 3 | $\neg A \lor \neg B$ | [De Morgan's laws](wikipedia://en/64669) (1) |
| 4 | $\neg \neg A$ | [Double negation](wikipedia://en/379833) (2) |
| 5 | $\neg B$ | [Disjunctive syllogism](wikipedia://en/7963) (3,4) |

:::

## Strong form {#Strong_form}

*Modus ponendo tollens* can be made stronger by using [exclusive disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_disjunction) instead of non-conjunction as a premise:

1. $$A \underline\lor B$$
1. $$A$$
1. $$\therefore \neg B$$

:::
