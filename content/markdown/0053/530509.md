# Horst Feistel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Horst Feistel"}

**Horst Feistel** (January 30, 1915[^o3] – November 14, 1990[^o8]) was a German-American [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) who worked on the design of [ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciphers) at [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), initiating research that culminated in the development of the [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) (DES) in the 1970s. The structure used in DES, called a [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network), is commonly used in many [block ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_ciphers).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Feistel was born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) in 1915, and moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in 1934. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was placed under house arrest, but gained US citizenship on 31 January 1944. The following day he was granted a security clearance and began work for the US [Air Force Cambridge Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Cambridge_Research_Center) (AFCRC) on [Identification Friend or Foe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identification_Friend_or_Foe) (IFF) devices until the 1950s. He was subsequently employed at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology)'s [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Laboratory), then the [MITRE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MITRE) corporation. In 1968, Feistel became a Research Staff Member at the [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) [T.J Watson Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center).[^o8] During his time there he received an award for his cryptographic work. In 1971, he patented the [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) [cryptographic system](wikipedia://en/506383) at IBM.[^o8] His research at [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) led to the development of the [Lucifer](wikipedia://en/364242) and [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) (DES) ciphers. Feistel was one of the earliest non-government researchers to study the design and theory of [block ciphers](wikipedia://en/4594).

Feistel lent his name to the [Feistel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feistel_network) construction, a common method for constructing block ciphers (for example DES).

Feistel obtained a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and his [master's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), both in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). He married Leona (Gage) in 1945, with whom he had a daughter, Peggy.
