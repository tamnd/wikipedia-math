# On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude

*Image: Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse.pdf.* The article

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude"}

"**Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse**" (usual [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) translation: "**On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude**") is a seminal 9-page paper by [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) published in the November 1859 edition of the *Monatsberichte der Königlich Preußischen Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Berlin*.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

This paper studies the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) using [analytic methods](wikipedia://en/48396). Although it is the only paper Riemann ever published on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), it contains ideas which influenced thousands of researchers during the late 19th century and up to the present day. The paper consists primarily of [definitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitions), [heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristic) [arguments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arguments), sketches of [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285), and the application of powerful analytic methods; all of these have become essential [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept) and tools of [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_history) [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).

Among the new definitions, ideas, and notation introduced:

- The use of the [Greek letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) [zeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta) (*ζ*) for a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) previously mentioned by [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler)
- The [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of this [zeta function *ζ*(*s*)](wikipedia://en/25809) to all [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) *s* ≠ 1
- The [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) [*ξ*](wikipedia://en/5396799)(*s*), related to the zeta function through the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) (or the [Π function](wikipedia://en/12316#Pi_function), in Riemann's usage)
- The discrete function *J*(*x*) defined for *x* ≥ 0, which is defined by *J*(0) = 0 and *J*(*x*) jumps by 1/*n* at each prime power *p*<sup>*n*</sup>. (Riemann calls this function *f*(*x*).)

Among the proofs and sketches of proofs:

- Proof of the [Hankel contour representation of the zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809#Mellin-type_integrals)[^o3]
- Two proofs of the [functional equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_equation_%28number_theory%29) of *ζ*(*s*)
- Proof sketch of the product representation of *ξ*(*s*)
- Proof sketch of the approximation of the number of roots of *ξ*(*s*) whose imaginary parts lie between 0 and *T*.

Among the conjectures made:

- The [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125), that all (nontrivial) zeros of *ζ*(*s*) have real part 1/2. Riemann states this in terms of the roots of the related *ξ* function, That is, (He was discussing a version of the zeta function, modified so that its roots are real rather than on the critical line.)

New methods and techniques used in number theory:

- Functional equations arising from [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146)
- [Analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) (although not in the spirit of Weierstrass)
- [Contour integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methods_of_contour_integration)
- [Fourier inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_inversion).

Riemann also discussed the relationship between *ζ*(*s*) and the distribution of the prime numbers, using the function *J*(*x*) essentially as a measure for [Stieltjes integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue-Stieltjes_integration). He then obtained the main result of the paper, a formula for *J*(*x*), by comparing with ln(*ζ*(*s*)). Riemann then found a formula for the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) *π*(*x*) (which he calls *F*(*x*)). He notes that his equation explains the fact that *π*(*x*) grows more slowly than the [logarithmic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_integral), as had been found by [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and [Carl Wolfgang Benjamin Goldschmidt](wikipedia://en/36846598).

The paper contains some peculiarities for modern readers, such as the use of [Π](wikipedia://en/12316#Pi_function)(*s* − 1) instead of Γ(*s*), writing *tt* instead of *t*<sup>2</sup>, and using the [bounds](wikipedia://en/42693) of ∞ to ∞ as to denote the [Hankel contour](wikipedia://en/1268560).
