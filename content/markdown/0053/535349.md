# Chebotarev density theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chebotarev density theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **Chebotarev density theorem**, named after [Nikolai Chebotarev](wikipedia://en/3033696), statistically describes the splitting of [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) in a given [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) $K$ of the field $\mathbb{Q}$ of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). Generally speaking, a prime integer will factor into several [ideal primes](wikipedia://en/523968) in the ring of [algebraic integers](wikipedia://en/98981) of $K$. There are only finitely many patterns of splitting that may occur. Although the full description of the splitting of every prime $p$ in a general Galois extension is a major unsolved problem, the Chebotarev density theorem says that the frequency of the occurrence of a given pattern, for all primes $p$ less than a large integer $N$, tends to a certain limit as $N$ goes to infinity. It was proved by Chebotarev in his thesis in 1922.[^o2]

:::

A special case that is easier to state says that if $K$ is an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) which is a Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $n$, then the prime numbers that completely split in $K$ have density $1/n$ among all primes. More generally, splitting behavior can be specified by assigning to (almost) every prime number an invariant, its [Frobenius element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element), which is a representative of a well-defined [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) in the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$. In this case, the theorem says that the asymptotic distribution of these invariants is uniform over the group, so that a conjugacy class with $k$ elements occurs with frequency asymptotic to $k/n$.

## History and motivation {#History_and_motivation}

When [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) first introduced the notion of [complex integers](wikipedia://en/48628) $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, he observed that the ordinary prime numbers may factor further in this new set of integers. He distinguished three cases: if a prime $p$ is congruent to 1 mod 4, then it factors into a product of two distinct prime Gaussian integers, or "splits completely"; if $p$ is congruent to 3 mod 4, then it remains prime, or is "inert"; and if $p$ is 2 then it becomes a product of the square of the prime $(1+i)$ and the invertible Gaussian integer $-i$; we say that 2 "ramifies". For instance,

$5 = (1 + 2i)(1-2i)$ splits completely;

$3$ is inert;

$2 = -i(1+i)^2$ ramifies.

From this description, it appears that as one considers larger and larger primes, the frequency of a prime splitting completely approaches $50%$, and likewise for the primes that remain primes in $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. [Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/101453) demonstrates that this is indeed the case. Even though the prime numbers themselves appear rather erratically, splitting of the primes in the extension $\mathbb{Z}\subset \mathbb{Z}[i]$ follows a simple statistical law.

Similar statistical laws also hold for splitting of primes in the [cyclotomic extensions](wikipedia://en/30872462), obtained from the field of rational numbers by adjoining a primitive [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) of a given order. For example, the ordinary integer primes group into four classes, each with probability $25%$, according to their pattern of splitting in the ring of integers corresponding to the 8th roots of unity. In this case, the [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) has degree 4 and is [abelian](wikipedia://en/174448), with the Galois group isomorphic to the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928). It turned out that the Galois group of the extension plays a key role in the pattern of splitting of primes. [Georg Frobenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Frobenius) established the framework for investigating this pattern and proved a special case of the theorem. The general statement was proved by [Nikolai Grigoryevich Chebotaryov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Grigoryevich_Chebotaryov) in 1922.

## Relation with Dirichlet's theorem {#Relation_with_Dirichlet's_theorem}

The Chebotarev density theorem may be viewed as a generalisation of [Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/101453). A quantitative form of Dirichlet's theorem states that if *$n \geq 2$* is an integer and $a$ is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to *$n$*, then the proportion of the primes $p$ congruent to $a$ mod *$n$* is asymptotic to *$1/\phi(n)$*, where *$\phi$* is the [Euler totient function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_totient_function).

This is a special case of the Chebotarev density theorem for the *$n$*-th [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) *$K$*. Indeed, the Galois group of *$K/\mathbb{Q}$* is abelian and can be canonically identified with the group of invertible [residue classes](wikipedia://en/20087) mod *$n$*. The splitting invariant of a prime *$p$* not dividing *$n$* is simply its residue class because the number of distinct primes into which $p$ splits is *$\phi(n)/m$*, where *$m$* is [multiplicative order](wikipedia://en/344542) of $p$ modulo *$n$;* hence, by the Chebotarev density theorem, primes are asymptotically uniformly distributed among different residue classes coprime to *$n$*.

:::

## Formulation {#Formulation}

In their survey article, (Lenstra Stevenhagen 1996) give an earlier result of Frobenius in this area: let $P(t)$ be a monic integer polynomial and $K$ is a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) of $P(t)$; then $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064). It makes sense to factorise $P(t)$ modulo a prime number $p$. Its "splitting type" is the list of degrees of irreducible factors of $P(t)$ mod $p$; i.e. $P(t)$ factorizes in some fashion over the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) $\mathbb{F}_p$. If *$n$* is the degree of $P(t)$, then the splitting type is a [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_an_integer) *$\Pi$* of *$n$*. Now each $g$ in $G = \operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})$ is a permutation of the roots of $P(t)$ in $K$; in other words, by choosing an ordering of a root and its [algebraic conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_conjugate), $G$ is [faithfully represented](wikipedia://en/2617872) as a subgroup of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$. We can write $g$ by means of its [cycle representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_representation), which gives a "cycle type" *$c(g)$*, again a partition of *$n$*.

The theorem of Frobenius states that for any given choice of *$\Pi$* the primes $p$ for which the splitting type of $P(t)$ mod $p$ is *$\Pi$* has a [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) $\delta$, with $\delta$ equal to the proportion of $g$ in $G$ that have cycle type *$\Pi$*.

The statement of the more general Chebotarev density theorem is in terms of the [Frobenius element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element) of a prime (ideal), which is in fact an associated [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) $C$ of elements of the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $G$. If we fix $C$ then the theorem says that, asymptotically, a proportion $|C|/|G|$ of primes have associated Frobenius element as $C$. When $G$ is abelian, the classes each have size 1. For the case of a [non-abelian group](wikipedia://en/4791400) of order 6 they have size 1, 2 and 3, and there are correspondingly (for example) $50%$ of primes $p$ that have an order 2 element as their Frobenius. Thus, these primes have residue degree 2, and so they split into exactly three prime ideals in a degree 6 extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ with it as Galois group.[^o9]

:::

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $L$ be a finite Galois extension of a number field $K$ with Galois group $G$. Let $X$ be a subset of $G$ that is stable under conjugation. The set of primes $v$ of $K$ that are unramified in $L$ and whose associated Frobenius conjugacy class $F_v$ is contained in $X$ has density $\#X/\#G$.[^o12] The statement is valid when the density refers to either the natural density or the analytic density of the set of primes.[^o13]

### Effective version {#Effective_version}

The [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567) implies an [effective version](wikipedia://en/250237)[^o15] of the Chebotarev density theorem: if $L/K$ is a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$, and $C$ a union of conjugacy classes of $G$, the number of unramified primes of $K$ of norm below $x$ with Frobenius conjugacy class in $C$ is

$$\frac{|C|}{|G|}\Bigl(\mathrm{Li}(x)+O\bigl(\sqrt x(n\log x+\log|\Delta|)\bigr)\Bigr),$$

where the constant implied in the [big-O notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-O_notation) is absolute, $n$ is the degree of $L$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $\Delta$ its discriminant.

The effective form of the Chebotarev density theory becomes much weaker without the generalized Riemann hypothesis. Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G$ and degree $d$, take $\rho$ to be a nontrivial irreducible representation of $G$ of degree $n$, and take $\mathfrak{f}(\rho)$ to be the [Artin conductor](wikipedia://en/34170016) of this representation. Suppose that, for $\rho_0$ a subrepresentation of $\rho \otimes \rho$ or $\rho \otimes \bar{\rho}$, $L(\rho_0, s)$ is entire; that is, the Artin conjecture is satisfied for all $\rho_0$. Take $\chi_{\rho}$ to be the character associated to $\rho$. Then there is an absolute positive $c$ such that, for $x \ge 2$,

$$\sum_{p \,\le\, x,\, p \,\not\mid\, \mathfrak{f}(\rho)} \chi_{\rho}(\text{Fr}_p) \log p = rx + O\biggl(\frac{x^{\beta}}{\beta} + x\exp\biggl(\frac{-c(dn)^{-4} \log x }{3\log \mathfrak{f}(\rho) + \sqrt{\log x}}\biggr) (dn \log (x\mathfrak{f}(\rho))\biggr),$$

where $r$ is $1$ if $\rho$ is trivial and is otherwise $0$, and where $\beta$ is an [exceptional real zero](wikipedia://en/1967197) of $L(\rho, s)$; if there is no such zero, the $x^{\beta}/\beta$ term can be ignored. The implicit constant of this expression is absolute.[^o16]

### Infinite extensions {#Infinite_extensions}

The statement of the Chebotarev density theorem can be generalized to the case of an infinite Galois extension $L/K$ that is unramified outside a [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) $S$ of primes of $K$ (i.e. if there is a finite set $S$ of primes of $K$ such that any prime of $K$ not in $S$ is unramified in the extension $L/K$). In this case, the Galois group $G$ of $L/K$ is a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266) equipped with the Krull topology. Since $G$ is compact in this topology, there is a unique [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) $\mu$ on $G$. For every prime $v$ of $K$ not in $S$ there is an associated Frobenius conjugacy class $F_v$. The Chebotarev density theorem in this situation can be stated as follows:[^o12]

Let $X$ be a subset of $G$ that is stable under conjugation and whose boundary has Haar measure zero. Then, the set of primes $v$ of $K$ not in $S$ such that $F_v \subseteq X$ has density $\mu(X)/\mu(G).$

This reduces to the finite case when $L/K$ is finite (the Haar measure is then just the [counting measure](wikipedia://en/191786)). A consequence of this version of the theorem is that the Frobenius elements of the unramified primes of $L$ are dense in $G$.

:::

## Important consequences {#Important_consequences}

The Chebotarev density theorem reduces the problem of classifying Galois extensions of a number field to that of describing the splitting of primes in extensions. Specifically, it implies that as a Galois extension $L/K$ is uniquely determined by the set of primes of $K$ that split completely in it.[^o19] A related corollary is that if almost all prime ideals of $K$ split completely in $L$, then in fact $L = K$.[^o20]
