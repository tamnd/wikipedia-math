# Quillen–Suslin theorem

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Quillen–Suslin theorem"}

**Quillen–Suslin theorem.**

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Quillen–Suslin theorem"}

The **Quillen–Suslin theorem**, also known as **Serre's problem** or **Serre's conjecture**, is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) concerning the relationship between [free modules](wikipedia://en/326454) and [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) over [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065). In the geometric setting it is a statement about the triviality of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834).

:::

The theorem states that every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) over a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) is [free](wikipedia://en/326454).

## History {#History}

### Background {#Background}

Geometrically, finitely generated projective modules over the ring $R[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ correspond to [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) $\mathbb{A}^n_R$, where free modules correspond to trivial vector bundles. This correspondence (from modules to (algebraic) vector bundles) is given by the 'globalisation' or 'twiddlification' functor, sending $M$ to $\widetilde{M}$. [Affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) is topologically [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), so it admits no non-trivial topological or smooth vector bundles. The Oka-Grauert principle gives a bijection between isomorphism classes of topological and holomorphic vector bundles on affine space, so there are no non-trivial holomorphic vector bundles either.[^o6]

[Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), in his 1955 paper [*Faisceaux algébriques cohérents*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_important_publications_in_mathematics#Faisceaux_Algébriques_Cohérents), remarked that the corresponding question was not known for algebraic vector bundles: "It is not known whether there exist projective *A*-modules of finite type which are not free."[^o7] Here $A$ is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), that is, $A=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$.

To Serre's dismay, this problem quickly became known as Serre's conjecture. (Serre wrote, "I objected as often as I could \[to the name\]."[^o8]) The statement does not immediately follow from the proofs given in the topological or holomorphic case. These cases only guarantee that there is a continuous or holomorphic trivialization, not an algebraic trivialization.

Serre made some progress towards a solution in 1957 when he proved that every finitely generated projective module over a polynomial ring over a field was [stably free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stably_free), meaning that after forming its [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) with a finitely generated free module, it became free. The problem remained open until 1976, when [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) and [Andrei Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) independently proved the result. Quillen was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978 in part for his proof of the Serre conjecture. [Leonid Vaseršteĭn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Vaseršteĭn) later gave a simpler and much shorter proof of the theorem, which can be found in [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991)'s *Algebra*.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A generalization relating projective modules over [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring) [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129) *A* and their polynomial rings is known as the [Bass–Quillen conjecture](wikipedia://en/51717116).

Note that although $GL_n$-bundles on affine space are all trivial, this is not true for *G*-bundles where *G* is a general [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group).

:::
