# Luoshu Square

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Luoshu Square"}

The **Luoshu** ([pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin)), **Lo Shu** ([Wade-Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade-Giles)), or **Nine Halls Diagram** is an [ancient Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China) diagram and named for the [Luo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_River_%28Henan%29) near [Luoyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang), [Henan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan). The Luoshu appears in [myths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mythology) concerning the [invention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_inventions) of [writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_writing) by [Cangjie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cangjie) and other [culture heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_hero). It is a unique normal [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) of order three. It is usually paired with the [River Map or Hetu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_Map)—named in reference to the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River)—and used with the River Map in various contexts involving [Chinese geomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_geomancy), [numerology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_numerology), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_philosophy), and early [natural science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science).

:::

## Traditions {#Traditions}

The Lo Shu is part of the legacy of ancient Chinese mathematical and divination (cf. the [I Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching) 易經) traditions, and is an important emblem in *[Feng Shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_Shui)* (風水)—the art of [geomancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomancy) concerned with the placement of objects in relation to the flow of [qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi) (氣), or "natural energy".

## History {#History}

A Chinese legend concerning the pre-historic [Emperor Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_the_Great) (夏禹) tells of the Lo Shu, often in connection with the *Yellow River Map* (Hetu) and the [eight trigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua_%28concept%29). In ancient China there is a legend of a huge [deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_myth): the people offered sacrifices to the god of one of the flooding rivers, the Luo river (洛河), to try to calm his anger. A magical [turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle) emerged from the water with the curiously unnatural Lo Shu pattern on its shell: circular dots representing the integers one through nine are arranged in a three-by-three grid.[^o5]

Early records dated to 650 BCE are ambiguous, referring to a "river map", but clearly start to refer to a [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) by 80 CE, and explicitly give an example of one since 570 CE.[^o6][^o7] Recent publications have provided support that the Lo Shu Magic Square was an important model for time and space. It served as a basis for city planning, and tomb and temple design. The magic square was incidentally used to designate spaces of political and religious importance.[^o8]

## The layout {#The_layout}

The odd and even numbers alternate in the periphery of the Lo Shu pattern; the four even numbers are at the four corners, and the five odd numbers (which outnumber the even numbers by one) form a cross in the center of the square. The sums in each of the three rows, in each of the three columns, and in both diagonals, are all 15.[^o10] Since "5" is in the center cell, the sum of any two other cells that are directly through the five from each other must be 10; e.g., opposite squares and corners add up to 10, the number of the *Yellow River Map*.

## In numerology {#In_numerology}

The Lo Shu is sometimes connected numerologically with the "[Bagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua)" (八卦 or eight [trigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigrams)"), that can be arranged in the eight outer cells, reminiscent of circular trigram diagrams. Because north is placed at the bottom of maps in China, the 3x3 [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) having number 1 at the bottom and 9 at the top is used in preference to the other rotations/reflections. As seen in the ["Later Heaven"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua#King_Wen's_"Later_Heaven") arrangement, 1 and 9 correspond with ☵ Kǎn 坎 "Water 水" and ☲ Lí 離 "Fire 火" respectively. In the ["Early Heaven"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagua#Fu_Xi's_"Earlier_Heaven") arrangement, they would correspond with ☷ Kūn 坤 "Earth 地" and ☰ Qián 乾 "Heaven 天" respectively. Like the *Yellow River Map*, the Lo Shu square, in conjunction with the Bagua, is sometimes used as a [mandalic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandala) representation important in Feng Shui geomancy.
