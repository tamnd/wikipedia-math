# Complex multiplication

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex multiplication"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **complex multiplication** (**CM**) is the theory of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) *E* that have an [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) larger than the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).[^n1] Put another way, it contains the theory of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) with extra symmetries, such as are visible when the [period lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_lattice) is the [Gaussian integer](wikipedia://en/48628) [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) or [Eisenstein integer](wikipedia://en/2353035) lattice.

:::

It has an aspect belonging to the theory of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function), because such elliptic functions, or [abelian functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_function) of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), are then 'very special' functions satisfying extra identities and taking explicitly calculable special values at particular points. It has also turned out to be a central theme in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), allowing some features of the theory of [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462) to be carried over to wider areas of application. [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) is said to have remarked that the theory of complex multiplication of elliptic curves was not only the most beautiful part of mathematics but of all science.[^o3]

There is also the [higher-dimensional complex multiplication theory](wikipedia://en/1138222) of [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) *A* having *enough* endomorphisms in a certain precise sense, roughly that the action on the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) at the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of *A* is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of one-dimensional [modules](wikipedia://en/276410).

## Example of the imaginary quadratic field extension {#Example_of_the_imaginary_quadratic_field_extension}

*Image: Lattice torsion points.svg.* An elliptic curve over the complex numbers is obtained as a quotient of the complex plane by a lattice Λ, here spanned by two fundamental periods *ω*<sub>1</sub> and *ω*<sub>2</sub>. The four-torsion is also shown, corresponding to the lattice 1/4 Λ containing Λ. The example of an elliptic curve corresponding to the Gaussian integers occurs when ω<sub>2</sub> = *i* *ω*<sub>1</sub>.

Consider an imaginary quadratic field $K = \mathbb{Q}\left(\sqrt{-d}\right) , \, d \in \mathbb{Z}, d > 0$. An elliptic function $f$ is said to have **complex multiplication** if there is an algebraic relation between $f(z)$ and $f(\lambda z)$ for all $\lambda$ in $K$.

Conversely, Kronecker conjectured – in what became known as the *[Kronecker Jugendtraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_Jugendtraum)* – that every abelian extension of $K$ **could be obtained** by the (roots of the) equation of a suitable elliptic curve with complex multiplication. To this day this remains one of the few cases of [Hilbert's twelfth problem](wikipedia://en/2164726) which has actually been solved.

An example of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication is

$$\mathbb{C}/ (\theta \mathbb{Z}[i])$$

where **Z**\[*i*\] is the [Gaussian integer](wikipedia://en/48628) ring, and *θ* is any non-zero complex number. Any such complex [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) has the Gaussian integers as endomorphism ring. It is known that the corresponding curves can all be written as

$$Y^2 = 4X^3 - aX$$

for some $a \in \mathbb{C}$, which demonstrably has two conjugate order-4 [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) sending

$$Y \to \pm iY,\quad X \to -X$$

in line with the action of *i* on the [Weierstrass elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/447181).

More generally, consider the lattice Λ, an additive group in the complex plane, generated by $\omega_1,\omega_2$. Then we define the Weierstrass function of the variable $z$ in $\mathbb{C}$ as follows:

$$\wp(z;\Lambda) = \wp(z;\omega_1,\omega_2) = \frac{1}{z^2} + \sum_{(m,n)\ne (0,0)} \left\{\frac{1}{(z+m\omega_1+n\omega_2)^2} - \frac{1}{\left(m\omega_1+n\omega_2\right)^2}\right\},$$

and

$$g_2 = 60\sum_{(m,n) \neq (0,0)} (m\omega_1+n\omega_2)^{-4}$$

$$g_3 =140\sum_{(m,n) \neq (0,0)} (m\omega_1+n\omega_2)^{-6}.$$

Let $\wp'$ be the derivative of $\wp$. Then we obtain an isomorphism of complex Lie groups:

$$w\mapsto(\wp(w):\wp'(w):1) \in \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{C})$$

from the complex torus group $\mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ to the projective elliptic curve defined in homogeneous coordinates by

$$E = \left\{ (x:y:z) \in \mathbb{C}^3 \mid y^2z = 4x^3 - g_2xz^2 - g_3 z^3 \right\}$$

and where the point at infinity, the zero element of the group law of the elliptic curve, is by convention taken to be $(0:1:0)$. If the lattice defining the elliptic curve is actually preserved under multiplication by (possibly a proper subring of) the ring of integers $\mathfrak{o}_K$ of $K$, then the ring of analytic automorphisms of $E = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda$ turns out to be isomorphic to this (sub)ring.

If we rewrite $\tau = \omega_1/\omega_2$ where $\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0$ and $\Delta(\Lambda) = g_2(\Lambda)^3 - 27g_3(\Lambda)^2$, then

$$j(\tau)=j(E)=j(\Lambda)=2^63^3g_2(\Lambda)^3/\Delta(\Lambda)\ .$$

This means that the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) of $E$ is an [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) – lying in $K$ – if $E$ has complex multiplication.

## Abstract theory of endomorphisms {#Abstract_theory_of_endomorphisms}

The ring of endomorphisms of an elliptic curve can be of one of three forms: the integers **Z**; an [order](wikipedia://en/1114171) in an [imaginary quadratic number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_number_field); or an order in a definite [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890) over **Q**.[^n2]

When the field of definition is a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), there are always non-trivial endomorphisms of an elliptic curve, coming from the [Frobenius map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_map), so every such curve has *complex multiplication* (and the terminology is not often applied). But when the base field is a number field, complex multiplication is the exception. It is known that, in a general sense, the case of complex multiplication is the hardest to resolve for the [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518).

:::

## Kronecker and abelian extensions {#Kronecker_and_abelian_extensions}

[Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) first postulated that the values of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) at torsion points should be enough to generate all [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) for imaginary quadratic fields, an idea that went back to [Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482) in some cases, and even to [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125). This became known as the *[Kronecker Jugendtraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_Jugendtraum)*; and was certainly what had prompted Hilbert's remark above, since it makes explicit [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) in the way the [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) do for abelian extensions of the [rational number field](wikipedia://en/19727024), via [Shimura's reciprocity law](wikipedia://en/35152264).

Indeed, let *K* be an imaginary quadratic field with class field *H*. Let *E* be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by the integers of *K*, defined over *H*. Then the [maximal abelian extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_abelian_extension) of *K* is generated by the *x*-coordinates of the points of finite order on some Weierstrass model for *E* over *H*.[^n3]

Many generalisations have been sought of Kronecker's ideas; they do however lie somewhat obliquely to the main thrust of the [Langlands philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_philosophy), and there is no definitive statement currently known.

## Sample consequence {#Sample_consequence}

It is no accident that [Ramanujan's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan's_constant), the [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325)[^o11]

$$e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} = 262537412640768743.99999999999925007\dots\,$$

or equivalently,

$$e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} = 640320^3+743.99999999999925007\dots\,$$

is an [almost integer](wikipedia://en/14041933), in that it is [very close](wikipedia://en/740817#Containing_pi_or_e_and_number_163) to an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563).[^o12] This remarkable fact is explained by the theory of complex multiplication, together with some knowledge of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms), and the fact that

$$\mathbf{Z}\left[ \frac{1+\sqrt{-163}}{2}\right]$$

is a [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546).

Here $(1+\sqrt{-163})/2$ satisfies *α*<sup>2</sup> = *α* − 41. In general, *S*\[*α*\] denotes the set of all [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) expressions in α with coefficients in *S*, which is the smallest ring containing *α* and *S*. Because α satisfies this quadratic equation, the required polynomials can be limited to degree one.

Alternatively,

$$e^{\pi \sqrt{163}} = 12^3(231^2-1)^3+743.99999999999925007\dots\,$$

an internal structure due to certain [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458), and with similar simple expressions for the other [Heegner numbers](wikipedia://en/522147).

## Singular moduli {#Singular_moduli}

The points of the upper half-plane *τ* which correspond to the period ratios of elliptic curves over the complex numbers with complex multiplication are precisely the imaginary quadratic numbers.[^n4] The corresponding [modular invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_function) *j*(*τ*) are the **singular moduli**, coming from an older terminology in which "singular" referred to the property of having non-trivial endomorphisms rather than referring to a [singular curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_curve).[^n5]

The [modular function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_function) *j*(*τ*) is algebraic on imaginary quadratic numbers *τ*:[^n6] these are the only algebraic numbers in the upper half-plane for which *j* is algebraic.[^o18]

If Λ is a lattice with period ratio *τ* then we write *j*(Λ) for *j*(*τ*). If further Λ is an ideal **a** in the ring of integers *O<sub>K</sub>* of a quadratic imaginary field *K* then we write *j*(**a**) for the corresponding singular modulus. The values *j*(**a**) are then real algebraic integers, and generate the [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036) *H* of *K*: the [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) degree \[*H*:*K*\] = *h* is the class number of *K* and the *H*/*K* is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) isomorphic to the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) of *K*. The class group acts on the values *j*(**a**) by \[**b**\] : *j*(**a**) → *j*(**ab**).

In particular, if *K* has class number one, then *j*(**a**) = *j*(*O*) is a rational integer: for example, *j*(**Z**\[i\]) = *j*(i) = 1728.

:::
