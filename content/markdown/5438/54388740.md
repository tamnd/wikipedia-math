# Helene Stähelin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helene Stähelin"}

*Image: Rosenblatt Stahelin Tonolo Zullig Zurich1932.tif.* *Left to right:* Alfred Rosenblatt, [Helene Stähelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helene_Stähelin_%28mathematician%29) (in background), Angelo Tonolo, and J. Züllig, at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) 1932

**Helene Stähelin** (18 July 1891 – 30 December 1970) was a Swiss mathematician, teacher, and peace activist.[^o2] From 1948 to 1967, she was president of the Swiss section of the [Women's International League for Peace and Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_International_League_for_Peace_and_Freedom) and its representative in the Swiss Peace Council.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Wintersingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wintersingen), Stähelin was one of twelve children of the [parson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parson) Gustav Stähelin (1858–1934)[^o6] and his wife Luise ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née) Lieb). In 1894, the family moved from Wintersingen to [Allschwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allschwil). Stähelin attended the Töchterschule in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel), the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel), and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).

In 1922, she became teacher of mathematics and natural sciences at the Töchterinstitut in [Ftan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ftan).[^o2] In 1924, she obtained her DPhil from Basel University.[^o7] Her dissertation was advised by [Hans Mohrmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Mohrmann) and Otto Spiess.[^o2][^o8][^o9] In 1926, she became a member of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society). Between 1934 and 1956, Stähelin worked as teacher at the Protestant secondary school in [Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zug). After her pensioning she returned to Basel, where she assisted for several years in Otto Spiess' editing of the [Bernoulli family](wikipedia://en/199226) letters.[^o2][^o10]

Being a [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist), Stähelin committed herself to the [Women's International League for Peace and Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_International_League_for_Peace_and_Freedom) (IFFF) and its struggle against scientific warfare. She was president of the IFFF's Swiss section from 1947–1967, during which the main issues were the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations), [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons), and the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War).[^o2] Due to her peace activism, she was watched by Swiss authorities in the mid 1950s;[^o2] her file at the Swiss Public Prosecutor General was kept secret until 1986.[^o7] Stähelin advocated for [women's suffrage in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage_in_Switzerland), though she died before women were able to vote.[^o2] She died in Basel.
