# 31 great circles of the spherical icosahedron

*Image: Fuller 31 great circles.png.* The 31 great circles can be seen as the union of edges in 3 polyhedra. The lower right image shows 15 red circles, 10 green circles, and 6 blue circles.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="31 great circles of the spherical icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **31 great circles of the spherical icosahedron** is an arrangement of 31 [great circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_circles) in [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844).[^o2] It was first identified by [Buckminster Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller) and is used in construction of [geodesic domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_dome).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The 31 great circles can be seen in 3 sets: 15, 10, and 6, each representing edges of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) projected onto a sphere. Fifteen great circles represent the edges of a [disdyakis triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/725111), the dual of a [truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534). Six more great circles represent the edges of an [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023), and the last ten great circles come from the edges of the [uniform star](wikipedia://en/8464397) [dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157379), making pentagrams with vertices at the edge centers of the icosahedron.

There are 62 points of intersection, positioned at the 12 vertices, and center of the 30 edges, and 20 faces of a regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084).

:::
