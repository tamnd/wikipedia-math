# Vermeil's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vermeil's theorem"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Vermeil's theorem** essentially states that the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) is the only (non-trivial) absolute invariant among those of prescribed type suitable for [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)’s theory of [General Relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Relativity). The theorem was proved by the German mathematician [Hermann Vermeil](wikipedia://en/47382614) in 1917.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Standard version of the theorem"}

## Standard version of the theorem {#Standard_version_of_the_theorem}

The theorem states that the [Ricci scalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_scalar) $R$[^o4] is the only scalar invariant (or absolute invariant) linear in the second derivatives of the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $g_{\mu\nu}$.

:::
