# Aldridge Bousfield

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aldridge Bousfield"}

**Aldridge Knight Bousfield** (April 5, 1941 – October 4, 2020),[^o3] known as "Pete", was an American mathematician working in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), known for the concept of [Bousfield localization](wikipedia://en/40776006).

:::

## Work and life {#Work_and_life}

Bousfield obtained both his undergraduate degree (1963) and his doctorate (1966) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). His doctoral thesis, entitled "Higher Order Suspension Maps for Non-Additive Functors", was written under the supervision of [Daniel Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155). He was a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) and [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and moved to the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago) where he worked from 1972 to his retirement in 2000.

Bousfield married Marie Vastersavendts, a [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium) mathematician, in 1968. She worked as demographer for the city of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) and died in 2016.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Within algebraic topology, he specialised in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). The *Bousfield-Kan spectral sequence*, [Bousfield localization](wikipedia://en/40776006) of [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697) and [model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304), and the *Bousfield-Friedlander model structure*[^o8] are named after Bousfield (and [Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155) and [Friedlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Friedlander), respectively).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

He was named to the 2018 class of fellows of the American Mathematical Society "for contributions to homotopy theory and for exposition".[^o10]
