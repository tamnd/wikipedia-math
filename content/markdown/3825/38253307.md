# Bartlett's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bartlett's theorem"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), **Bartlett's theorem** gives the distribution of the number of customers in a given part of a system at a fixed time.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

Suppose that customers arrive according to a non-stationary [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with rate $A(t)$, and that subsequently they move independently around a system of nodes. Write $E$ for some particular part of the system and $p(s,t)$ the probability that a customer who arrives at time *$s$* is in $E$ at time *$t$*. Then the number of customers in $E$ at time *$t$* has a [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) with mean[^o4]

$$\mu(t) = \int_{-\infty}^t A(s) p(s,t) \, \mathrm{d}s.$$
