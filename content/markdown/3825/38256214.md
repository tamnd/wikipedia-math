# Glossary of commutative algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of commutative algebra"}

This is a **glossary of commutative algebra**.

:::

See also [list of algebraic geometry topics](wikipedia://en/351549), [glossary of classical algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/35326250), [glossary of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/35457783), [glossary of ring theory](wikipedia://en/251424) and [glossary of module theory](wikipedia://en/25334192).

In this article, all rings are assumed to be [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) with identity 1.

## A {#A}

::: {#o9 .entry cue=term name="absolute integral closure"}

**absolute integral closure**

The [absolute integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_integral_closure) is the integral closure of an integral domain in an algebraic closure of the field of fractions of the domain.

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=term name="absolutely"}

**absolutely**

The word "absolutely" usually means "not relatively"; i.e., independent of the base field in some sense. It is often synonymous with "geometrically".

An [absolutely flat ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_flat_ring) is a ring such that all modules over it are flat. (Non-commutative rings with this property are called [von Neumann regular rings](wikipedia://en/907524).)

An ideal in a polynomial ring over a field is called **absolutely prime** if its extension remains prime for every extension of the field.

An ideal in a polynomial ring over a field is called **absolutely unramified** if it is unramified for every extension of the field.

**Absolutely normal** is an alternative term for geometrically normal.

**Absolutely regular** is an alternative term for [geometrically regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrically_regular).

An **absolutely simple point** is one with a [geometrically regular local ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrically_regular_local_ring).

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=term name="acceptable ring"}

**acceptable ring**

[Acceptable rings](wikipedia://en/38573239) are generalizations of [excellent rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excellent_rings), with the conditions about regular rings in the definition replaced by conditions about Gorenstein rings.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=term name="adic"}

**adic**

The *I*-adic topology on a ring has a base of neighborhoods of 0 given by powers of the ideal *I*.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=term name="affine ring"}

**affine ring**

An affine ring *R* over another ring *S* (often a field) is a ring (or sometimes an integral domain) that is finitely generated over *S*.

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=term name="algebraic-geometrical local ring"}

**algebraic-geometrical local ring**

A local ring that is a localization of a finitely-generated domain over a field.

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=term name="almost"}

**almost**

An element *x* of a ring is called almost integral over a subring if there is a regular element *a* of the subring so that *ax*<sup>*n*</sup> is in the subring for all positive integers *n*.

An integral domain *S* is called almost finite over a subring *R* if its field of quotients is a finite extension of the field of quotients of *S*.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=term name="altitude"}

**altitude**

The **altitude** of a ring is an archaic name for its dimension.

The altitude of an ideal is another name for its height.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=term name="analytic"}

**analytic**

The analytic spread of an ideal of a local ring is the Krull dimension of the fiber at the special point of the local ring of the Rees algebra of the ideal.

The analytic deviation of an ideal is its analytic spread minus its height.

An **analytic ring** is a quotient of a ring of convergent power series in a finite number of variables over a field with a valuation.

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=term name="analytically"}

**analytically**

This often refers to properties of the completion of a local ring; cf. formally

A local ring is called **[analytically normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytically_normal)** if its completion is an integrally closed domain.

A local ring is called **[analytically unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytically_unramified)** if its completion has no nonzero nilpotent elements.

A local ring is called **[analytically irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytically_irreducible)** if its completion has no zero divisors.

Two local rings are called **analytically isomorphic** if their completions are isomorphic.

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=term name="annihilator"}

**annihilator**

The [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of a subset of a module is the ideal of elements whose product with any element of the subset is 0.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=term name="Artin"}

**Artin**

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=term name="Artinian"}

**Artinian**

[Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351)

[Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821)

An [Artinian module](wikipedia://en/542336) is a module satisfying the descending chain condition on submodules.

An [Artinian ring](wikipedia://en/489273) is a ring satisfying the descending chain condition on ideals.

The [Artin-Rees lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin-Rees_lemma) establishes a certain stability of filtration by an ideal.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=term name="ASL"}

**ASL**

Acronym for [algebra with straightening law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_with_straightening_law).

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=term name="associated"}

**associated**

An [associated prime](wikipedia://en/17286442) of a module *M* over a ring *R* is a prime ideal *p* such that *M* has a submodule isomorphic to *R*/*p*.

:::

## B {#B}

::: {#o25 .entry cue=term name="Bass number"}

**Bass number**

If *M* is a module over a local ring *R* with residue field *k*, then the *i*th [Bass number](wikipedia://en/38345367) of *M* is the *k*-dimension of Ext${}^{i}_{R}$(*k*,*M*).

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=term name="Bézout domain"}

**Bézout domain**

A [Bézout domain](wikipedia://en/3131407) is an integral domain in which the sum of two principal ideals is a principal ideal.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=term name="big"}

**big**

The word "big" when applied to a module emphasizes that the module is not necessarily finitely generated. In particular a big Cohen–Macaulay module is a module that has a system of parameters for which it is regular.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=term name="Boolean ring"}

**Boolean ring**

A [Boolean ring](wikipedia://en/54356) is a ring such that *x*<sup>2</sup>=*x* for all *x*.

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=term name="Bourbaki ideal"}

**Bourbaki ideal**

A Bourbaki ideal of a torsion-free module *M* is an ideal isomorphic (as a module) to a torsion-free quotient of *M* by a free submodule.

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=term name="Buchsbaum ring"}

**Buchsbaum ring**

A [Buchsbaum ring](wikipedia://en/34961349) is a Noetherian local ring such that every system of parameters is a weak sequence.

:::

## C {#C}

::: {#o32 .entry cue=term name="canonical"}

**canonical**

"Canonical module" is an alternative term for a [dualizing module](wikipedia://en/38513558).

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=term name="catenary"}

**catenary**

A ring is called [catenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catenary_%28ring_theory%29) if for every two prime ideals, one contained in the other, all maximal chains between them have the same length.

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=term name="center"}

**center**

The center of a valuation (or place) is the ideal of elements of positive order.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="chain"}

**chain**

A strictly increasing or decreasing sequence of prime ideals.

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=term name="characteristic"}

**characteristic**

The [characteristic of a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_ring) is a non-negative integer generating the **Z**-ideal of multiples of 1 that are zero.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="clean"}

**clean**

A finitely generated module *M* over a Noetherian ring *R* is called clean if it has a finite filtration all of whose quotients are of the form *R*/*p* for *p* an associated prime of *M*. A stronger variation of this definition says that the primes *p* must be minimal primes of the support of *M*.

An element of a ring is called clean if it is the sum of a unit and an idempotent, and is called almost clean if it is the sum of a regular element and an idempotent. A ring is called clean or almost clean if all its elements are clean or almost clean, and a module is called clean or almost clean if its endomorphism ring is clean or almost clean.

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=term name="CM"}

**CM**

Abbreviation for [Cohen–Macaulay](wikipedia://en/497407) or [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183).

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="CoCoA"}

**CoCoA**

The [CoCoA](wikipedia://en/1674062) computer algebra system for computations in commutative algebra

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=term name="codepth"}

**codepth**

The codepth of a finitely generated module over a Noetherian local ring is its dimension minus its depth.

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=term name="codimension"}

**codimension**

The codimension of a prime ideal is another name for its height.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=term name="coefficient ring"}

**coefficient ring**

A complete Noetherian local ring

A complete Noetherian local ring with finite residue field

An alternative name for a Cohen ring

:::

::: {#o43 .entry cue=term name="Cohen"}

**Cohen**

[Irvin Cohen](wikipedia://en/10051038)

A [Cohen ring](wikipedia://en/36644224) is a field or a complete discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic $(0,p)$ whose maximal ideal is generated by $p$.

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=term name="Cohen–Macaulay"}

**Cohen–Macaulay**

A local ring is called [Cohen–Macaulay](wikipedia://en/497407) if it is Noetherian and the Krull dimension is equal to the depth. A ring is called Cohen–Macaulay if it is Noetherian and all localizations at maximal ideals are Cohen–Macaulay.

A [generalized Cohen–Macaulay ring](wikipedia://en/60415782) is a Noetherian local ring such that for *i* \< the Krull dimension of the ring, the *i*-th local cohomology of the ring along the maximal ideal has finite length.

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=term name="coherent"}

**coherent**

A module is called [coherent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_module) if it is finitely generated and every homomorphism to it from a finitely generated module has a finitely generated kernel.

A [coherent ring](wikipedia://en/14777654) is a ring that is a coherent module over itself.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=term name="complete"}

**complete**

A [local complete intersection ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_complete_intersection_ring) is a Noetherian local ring whose completion is the quotient of a regular local ring by an ideal generated by a regular sequence.

A [complete local ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_local_ring) is a local ring that is complete in the topology (or rather uniformity) where the powers of the maximal ideal form a base of the neighborhoods of 0.

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=term name="completely integrally closed"}

**completely integrally closed**

A domain *R* is called [completely integrally closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_integrally_closed) if, whenever all positive powers of some element *x* of the quotient field are contained in a finitely generated *R*-module, *x* is in *R*.

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=term name="completion"}

**completion**

The [completion of a module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_of_a_module) or ring *M* at an ideal *I* is the inverse limit of the modules *M*/*I*<sup>*n*</sup>*M*.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=term name="composite"}

**composite**

A composite number is a positive integer that is neither prime nor 1.

The composite of a valuation ring *R* and a valuation ring *S* of its residue field is the inverse image of *S* in *R*.

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=term name="conductor"}

**conductor**

The [conductor](wikipedia://en/18582715) of an integral domain *R* is the annihilator of the *R*-module *T*/*R*, where *T* is the integral closure of *R* in its quotient field.

:::

::: {#o51 .entry cue=term name="congruence ideal"}

**congruence ideal**

A [congruence ideal](wikipedia://en/38301499) of a surjective homomorphism *f*: *B*→*C* of commutative rings is the image under *f* of the annihilator of the kernel of *f*.

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=term name="connected"}

**connected**

A graded algebra over a field *k* is connected if its 0th degree component is *k*.

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=term name="conormal"}

**conormal**

The conormal module of a quotient of a ring by an ideal $I$ is the module $I/I^{2}$.

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=term name="constructible"}

**constructible**

For a Noetherian ring, a [constructible subset](wikipedia://en/23234121) of the spectrum is one that is a finite union of locally closed sets. For rings that are not Noetherian the definition of a constructible subset is more complicated.

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=term name="content"}

**content**

The content of a polynomial is a greatest common divisor of its coefficients.

:::

::: {#o56 .entry cue=term name="contraction"}

**contraction**

The [contraction of an ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_of_an_ideal) is the ideal given by the inverse image of some ideal under a homomorphism of rings.

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=term name="coprimary"}

**coprimary**

A [coprimary module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprimary_module) is a module with exactly one associated prime.

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=term name="coprime"}

**coprime**

Two ideals are called coprime if their sum is the whole ring.

Two elements of a ring are called coprime if the ideal they generate is the whole ring.

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=term name="cotangent"}

**cotangent**

The [cotangent space](wikipedia://en/6620) of a local ring with maximal ideal *m* is the vector space *m*/*m*<sup>2</sup> over the residue field.

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=term name="Cox ring"}

**Cox ring**

A [Cox ring](wikipedia://en/42058042) is a sort of universal homogeneous coordinate ring for a projective variety.

:::

## D {#D}

::: {#o62 .entry cue=term name="decomposable"}

**decomposable**

A module is called [decomposable](wikipedia://en/810549) if it can be written as a direct sum of two non-zero submodules.

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=term name="decomposition group"}

**decomposition group**

A [decomposition group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition_group) is a group of automorphisms of a ring whose elements fix a given prime ideal.

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=term name="Dedekind domain"}

**Dedekind domain**

A [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) is a Noetherian integrally closed domain of dimension at most 1.

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=term name="defect"}

**defect**

:::

::: {#o66 .entry cue=term name="deficiency"}

**deficiency**

The **ramification defect** or **ramification deficiency** *d* of a valuation of a field *K* is given by \[*L*:*K*\]=*defg* where *e* is the ramification index, *f* is the inertia degree, and *g* is the number of extensions of the valuation to a larger field *L*. The number *d* is a power *p*<sup>δ</sup> of the characteristic *p*, and sometimes δ rather than *d* is called the ramification deficiency.

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=term name="depth"}

**depth**

The [I-depth](wikipedia://en/9770927) (also called **grade**) of a module *M* over a ring *R*, where *I* is an ideal, is the smallest integer *n* such that Ext${}^{n}_{R}$(*R*/*I*,*M*) is nonzero. When *I* is the maximal ideal of a local ring this is just called the depth of *M*, and if in addition *M* is the local ring *R* this is called the depth of the ring *R*.

:::

::: {#o68 .entry cue=term name="derivation"}

**derivation**

An additive homomorphism *d* from a ring to a module that satisfies Leibniz's rule *d*(*ab*)=*ad*(*b*)+*bd*(*a*).

:::

::: {#o69 .entry cue=term name="derived"}

**derived**

The **derived normal ring** of an integral domain is its integral closure in its quotient field.

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=term name="determinant module"}

**determinant module**

The [determinant module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinant_module) of a module is the top exterior power of the module.

:::

::: {#o71 .entry cue=term name="determinantal"}

**determinantal**

This often refers to properties of an ideal generated by determinants of minors of a matrix. For example, a [determinantal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinantal_ring) is generated by the entries of a matrix, with relations given by the determinants of the minors of some fixed size.

:::

::: {#o72 .entry cue=term name="deviation"}

**deviation**

A [deviation of a local ring](wikipedia://en/38030428) is an invariant that measures how far the ring is from being regular.

:::

::: {#o73 .entry cue=term name="dimension"}

**dimension**

The [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of a ring, often just called the dimension, is the maximal length of a chain of prime ideals, and the Krull dimension of a module is the maximal length of a chain of prime ideals containing its annihilator.

The [weak dimension](wikipedia://en/38263686) or [flat dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_dimension) of a module is the shortest length of a flat resolution.

The [injective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_dimension) of a module is the shortest length of an injective resolution.

The [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension) of a module is the shortest length of a projective resolution.

The [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) of a vector space over a field is the minimal number of generators; this is unrelated to most other definitions of its dimension as a module over a field.

The [homological dimension](wikipedia://en/15682063) of a module may refer to almost any of the various other dimensions, such as weak dimension, injective dimension, or projective dimension.

The [global dimension](wikipedia://en/2291739) of a ring is the supremum of the projective dimensions of its modules.

The [weak global dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_global_dimension) of a ring is the supremum of the flat dimensions of its modules.

The [embedding dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedding_dimension) of a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) is the dimension of its [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207).

The dimension of a valuation ring over a field is the transcendence degree of its residue field; this is not usually the same as the Krull dimension.

:::

::: {#o74 .entry cue=term name="discrete valuation ring"}

**discrete valuation ring**

A [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) is an integrally closed Noetherian local ring of dimension 1.

:::

::: {#o75 .entry cue=term name="divisible"}

**divisible**

A [divisible module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisible_module) is a module such that multiplication by any regular element of the ring is surjective.

:::

::: {#o76 .entry cue=term name="divisor"}

**divisor**

A divisor of an integral domain is an equivalence class of non-zero fractional ideals, where two such ideals are called equivalent if they are contained in the same principal fractional ideals.

A [Weil divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_divisor) of a ring is an element of the free abelian group generated by the codimension 1 prime ideals.

[Cartier divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor)

:::

::: {#o77 .entry cue=term name="divisorial ideal"}

**divisorial ideal**

A [divisorial ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisorial_ideal) of an integral domain is a non-zero fractional ideal that is an intersection of principal fractional ideals.

:::

::: {#o78 .entry cue=term name="domain"}

**domain**

A domain or [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is a ring with no zero-divisors and where 1≠0.

:::

::: {#o79 .entry cue=term name="dominate"}

**dominate**

A local ring *B* is said to dominate a local ring *A* if it contains *A* and the maximal ideal of *B* contains the maximal ideal of *A*.

:::

::: {#o80 .entry cue=term name="dual"}

**dual**

:::

::: {#o81 .entry cue=term name="duality"}

**duality**

:::

::: {#o82 .entry cue=term name="dualizing"}

**dualizing**

[Grothendieck local duality](wikipedia://en/38470542) is a duality for cohomology of modules over a local ring.

[Matlis duality](wikipedia://en/38077774) is a duality between Artinian and Noetherian modules over a complete local ring.

[Macaulay duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaulay_duality) is a duality between Artinian and Noetherian modules over a complete local ring that is finitely generated over a field.

A [dualizing module](wikipedia://en/38513558) (also called a canonical module) for a Noetherian ring *R* is a finitely-generated module *M* such that for any maximal ideal *m*, the *R*/*m* vector space Ext${}^{n}_{R}$(*R*/*m*,*M*) vanishes if *n*≠ height(*m*) and is 1-dimensional if *n*=height(*m*).

A [dualizing complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizing_complex) is a [complex](wikipedia://en/138404) generalizing many of the properties of a dualizing module to rings that do not have a dualizing module.

:::

::: {#o83 .entry cue=term name="DVR"}

**DVR**

Abbreviation for [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633).

:::

## E {#E}

::: {#o85 .entry cue=term name="Eakin"}

**Eakin**

The [Eakin–Nagata theorem](wikipedia://en/62674651) states: given a finite ring extension $A \subset B$, $A$ is a Noetherian ring if and only if $B$ is a Noetherian ring.

:::

::: {#o86 .entry cue=term name="Eisenstein"}

**Eisenstein**

Named after [Gotthold Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482)

The ring of [Eisenstein integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_integers) is the ring generated by a primitive cube root of 1.

An [Eisenstein polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_polynomial) is a polynomial such that its leading term is 1, all other coefficients are divisible by a prime, and the constant term is not divisible by the square of the prime.

The [Eisenstein criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_criterion) states that an Eisenstein polynomial is irreducible.

An Eisenstein extension is an extension generated by a root of an Eisenstein polynomial.

:::

[^o87]

::: {#o88 .entry cue=term name="embedded"}

**embedded**

An embedded prime of a module is a non-minimal associated prime.

:::

::: {#o89 .entry cue=term name="embedding dimension"}

**embedding dimension**

See dimension.

:::

::: {#o90 .entry cue=term name="envelope"}

**envelope**

An [injective envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_envelope) (or hull) of a module is a minimal injective module containing it.

:::

::: {#o91 .entry cue=term name="equicharacteristic"}

**equicharacteristic**

A local ring is called equicharacteristic if it has the same characteristic as its residue field.

:::

::: {#o92 .entry cue=term name="essential"}

**essential**

A submodule *M* of *N* is called an [essential submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_submodule) if it intersects every nonzero submodule of *N*.

An [essential extension](wikipedia://en/10119238) of a module *M* is a module *N* containing *M* such that every non-zero submodule intersects *M*.

:::

::: {#o93 .entry cue=term name="essentially of finite type"}

**essentially of finite type**

An algebra is said to be essentially of finite type over another algebra if it is a localization of a finitely generated algebra.

:::

::: {#o94 .entry cue=term name="étale"}

**étale**

A morphism of rings is called [étale](wikipedia://en/3022771) if it is formally etale and locally finitely presented.

An [étale algebra](wikipedia://en/24271692) over a field is a finite product of finite separable extensions.

:::

::: {#o95 .entry cue=term name="Euclidean domain"}

**Euclidean domain**

A [Euclidean domain](wikipedia://en/10376) is an integral domain with a form of [Euclid's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_algorithm).

:::

::: {#o96 .entry cue=term name="exact zero divisor"}

**exact zero divisor**

A zero divisor $x$ is said to be an **exact zero divisor** if its annihilator, $\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)=\{ r\in R\mid rx=0 \}$, is a principal ideal $yR$ whose annihilator is $xR$: $\operatorname{Ann}_R(x)=\{ r\in R\mid rx=0 \}= yR$ and $\operatorname{Ann}_R(y)=\{ r\in R\mid ry=0 \}= xR$.

:::

::: {#o97 .entry cue=term name="excellent"}

**excellent**

An [excellent ring](wikipedia://en/10016360) is a universally catenary Grothendieck ring such that for every finitely generated algebra the singular points of the spectrum form a closed subset.

:::

::: {#o98 .entry cue=term name="Ext"}

**Ext**

The [Ext functors](wikipedia://en/840758), the derived functors of the Hom functor.

:::

::: {#o99 .entry cue=term name="extension"}

**extension**

An [extension of an ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_of_an_ideal) is the ideal generated by the image under a homomorphism of rings.

An extension of a module may mean either a module containing it as a submodule or a module mapping onto it as a quotient module.

An [essential extension](wikipedia://en/10119238) of a module *M* is a module containing *M* such that every non-zero submodule intersects *M*.

**field**

A commutative ring such that every nonzero element has an inverse

A residue field is the quotient of a ring by a maximal ideal.

A quotient field may mean either a residue field of a field of fractions.

::: {#o106 .entry cue=term name="finite"}

**finite**

A finite module (or algebra) over a ring usually means one that is finitely generated as a module. It may also mean one with a finite number of elements, especially in the term [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615).

::: {#o107 .entry cue=term name="finite type"}

**finite type**

An algebra over a ring is said to be of finite type if it is finitely generated as an algebra.

:::

::: {#o108 .entry cue=term name="finitely generated"}

**finitely generated**

A module over a ring is called [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) if every element is a linear combination of a fixed finite number of elements. If the module happens to be an algebra this is much stronger than saying it is finitely generated as an algebra.

An algebra over a ring is called [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661) if it is finitely generated as an algebra, which is much weaker than saying it is finitely generated as a module.

An extension of fields is called finitely generated if elements of the larger field can all be expressed as rational functions of a finite generating set.

**Fitting ideal**

The [Fitting ideal](wikipedia://en/28034758) *I*<sub>*n*</sub>(*M*) of a module *M* generated by *g* elements is the ideal generated by the determinants of the minors of size *g*–*n* of the matrix of relations defining the module.

::: {#o110 .entry cue=term name="flat"}

**flat**

A [flat module](wikipedia://en/492616) is a module such that tensoring with it preserves exactness.

A [flat resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_resolution) is a resolution by flat modules.

For flat dimension, see dimension.

A module *M* over a ring *R* is called [normally flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normally_flat) along an ideal *I* if the *R*/*I*-module ⊕*I*<sup>*n*</sup>*M*/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup>*M* is flat.

A [flat cover](wikipedia://en/34846642) of a module *M* is a map from a flat module to *M* with superfluous kernel.

:::

::: {#o111 .entry cue=term name="formally"}

**formally**

A homomorphism *f*:*A*→*B* of rings is called [formally smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_smooth), [formally unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_unramified), or [formally etale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_etale) if for every *A*-algebra *R* with a nilpotent ideal *I*, the natural map from Hom<sub>*A*</sub>(*R*/*I*, *B*) to Hom<sub>*A*</sub>(*R*, *B*) is surjective, injective, or bijective. The algebra *B* is then called a formally smooth, formally unramified, or formally etale *A*-algebra.

A Noetherian local ring is called formally [equidimensional](wikipedia://en/21568751) (or quasi-unmixed) if its completion is equidimensional.

Formally catenary rings are rings such that every quotient by a prime ideal is formally equidimensional. For Noetherian local rings this is equivalent to the ring being [universally catenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universally_catenary).

If *K* is the ring of fractions of an integral domain *R*, then a [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of *R* is a submodule of the *R*-module *K* contained in *kR* for some *k* in *K*.

**geometrically**

The word "geometrically" usually refers to properties that continue to hold after taking finite field extensions. For example, a ring *R* over a field *k* is called geometrically normal, [geometrically regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrically_regular), or geometrically reduced if *R*⊗<sub>*k*</sub>*K* is normal, regular, or reduced for every finite extension field *K* of *k*.

::: {#o119 .entry cue=term name="going down"}

**going down**

An extension *R*⊆*S* of commutative rings is said to have the [going down property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_down_property) if whenever *p*<sub>1</sub>⊆*p*<sub>2</sub> is a chain of prime ideals in *R* and *q*<sub>2</sub> is a prime ideal of *S* with *q*<sub>2</sub>∩*R*=*p*<sub>2</sub>, there is a prime ideal *q*<sub>1</sub> of *S* with *q*<sub>1</sub>⊆*q*<sub>2</sub> and *q*<sub>1</sub>∩*R*=*p*<sub>1</sub>.

The [going down theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_down_theorem) states that an integral extension *R*⊆*S* such that *S* is a domain and *R* is integrally closed has the going down property.

:::

::: {#o120 .entry cue=term name="going up"}

**going up**

An extension *R*⊆*S* of commutative rings is said to have the [going up property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_up_property) if whenever *p*<sub>1</sub>⊆*p*<sub>2</sub> is a chain of prime ideals in *R* and *q*<sub>1</sub> is a prime ideal of *S* with *q*<sub>1</sub>∩*R*=*p*<sub>1</sub>, there is a prime ideal *q*<sub>2</sub> of *S* with *q*<sub>1</sub>⊆*q*<sub>2</sub> and *q*<sub>2</sub>∩*R*=*p*<sub>2</sub>.

The [going up theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_up_theorem) states that an integral extension *R*⊆*S* has the going up property.

::: {#o122 .entry cue=term name="grade"}

**grade**

The various uses of the term "grade" are sometimes inconsistent and incompatible with each other.

The grade grade(*I*,*M*) of an ideal *I* on a finitely-generated module *M* over a Noetherian ring is the length of a maximal *M*-regular sequence in *I*. This is also called the depth of *I* on *M*

The grade grade(*M*) of a module *M* over a ring *R* is grade(Ann *M*,*R*), which for a finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring is the smallest *n* such that Ext${}^{n}_{R}$(*M*,*R*) is non-zero.

The grade of a module *M* over a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal *I* is the grade of *m* on *I*. This is also called the depth of *M*. This is not consistent with the other definition of the grade of a module given above.

The grade grade(*I*) of an ideal is given the grade grade(*R*/*I*) of the module *R*/*I*. So the grade of the ideal *I* is usually not the same as the grade of the module *I*.

**initial ideal**

**injective**

::: {#o147 .entry cue=term name="integral"}

**integral**

The two different meanings of integral (no zero divisors, or every element being a root of a monic polynomial) are sometimes confused.

An [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) or integral ring is a nontrivial ring without zero-divisors.

An element is called integral over a subring if it is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in the subring.

An element *x* of a ring is called almost integral over a subring if there is a regular element *a* of the subring so that *ax*<sup>*n*</sup> is in the subring for all positive integers *n*.

The [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of a subring of a ring is the ring of all elements that are integral over it.

An algebra over a ring is called an integral algebra if all its elements are integral over the ring.

A ring is called locally integral if it is reduced and the localization at every prime ideal is integral.

A domain is called [integrally closed](wikipedia://en/21420209) if it is its own integral closure in the field of fractions.

:::

::: {#o148 .entry cue=term name="invertible"}

**invertible**

An invertible fractional ideal is a fractional ideal that has an inverse in the monoid of fractional ideals under multiplication.

:::

::: {#o149 .entry cue=term name="irreducible"}

**irreducible**

An element of a ring is called [irreducible](wikipedia://en/60693) if it cannot be written as a product of two non-units.

An [irreducible ring](wikipedia://en/23169189) is a ring where the zero ideal is not an intersection of two non-zero ideals, and more generally an irreducible module is a module where the zero module cannot be written as an intersection of non-zero submodules.

An ideal or submodule is called [irreducible](wikipedia://en/22260381) if it cannot be written as an intersection of two larger ideals or submodules. If the ideal or submodule is the whole ring or module this is inconsistent with the definition of an irreducible ring or module.

## N {#N}

::: {#o188 .entry cue=term name="N-1"}

**N-1**

An [N-1 ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-1_ring) is an integral domain whose integral closure in its quotient field is a finitely generated module.

:::

::: {#o189 .entry cue=term name="N-2"}

**N-2**

An [N-2 ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-2_ring) is the same as a Japanese ring, in other words an integral domain whose integral closure in any finite extension of its quotient field is a finitely generated module.

:::

::: {#o190 .entry cue=term name="Nagata ring"}

**Nagata ring**

A [Nagata ring](wikipedia://en/10092186) is a Noetherian universally Japanese ring. These are also called pseudo-geometric rings.

:::

::: {#o191 .entry cue=term name="Nakayama's lemma"}

**Nakayama's lemma**

[Nakayama's lemma](wikipedia://en/1835001) states that if a finitely generated module *M* is equal to *IM* where *I* is the Jacobson radical, then *M* is zero.

:::

::: {#o192 .entry cue=term name="neat"}

**neat**

Occasionally used to mean "unramified".

::: {#o193 .entry cue=term name="nilpotent"}

**nilpotent**

Some power is zero. Can be applied to elements of a ring or ideals of a ring. See [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235).

:::

::: {#o194 .entry cue=term name="nilradical"}

**nilradical**

The [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636) of a ring is the ideal of nilpotent elements.

:::

::: {#o195 .entry cue=term name="Noether"}

**Noether**

:::

::: {#o196 .entry cue=term name="Noetherian"}

**Noetherian**

Named after [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896)

A [Noetherian module](wikipedia://en/542431) is a module such that every submodule is finitely generated.

A [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) is a ring that is a Noetherian module over itself, in other words every ideal is finitely generated.

[Noether normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noether_normalization) represents a finitely generated algebra over a field as a finite module over a polynomial ring.

:::

::: {#o197 .entry cue=term name="normal"}

**normal**

A [normal domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_domain) is an integral domain that is integrally closed in its quotient field.

A [normal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ring) is a ring whose localizations at prime ideals are normal domains.

:::

::: {#o198 .entry cue=term name="normally flat"}

**normally flat**

A module *M* over a ring *R* is called normally flat along an ideal *I* if the *R*/*I*-module ⊕*I*<sup>*n*</sup>*M*/*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup>*M* is flat.

:::

::: {#o199 .entry cue=term name="Nullstellensatz"}

**Nullstellensatz**

German for "zero locus theorem".

Over algebraically closed field, the [weak Nullstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_Nullstellensatz) states that the points of affine space correspond to maximal ideals of its coordinate ring, and the [strong Nullstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Nullstellensatz) states that closed subsets of a variety correspond to radical ideals of its coordinate ring.

**orientation**

An orientation of a module over a ring *R* is an isomorphism from the highest non-zero exterior power of the module to *R*.

## P {#P}

::: {#o203 .entry cue=term name="parafactorial"}

**parafactorial**

A Noetherian local ring *R* is called [parafactorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parafactorial) if it has [depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_of_a_local_ring) at least 2 and the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) Pic(Spec(*R*) − *m*) of its spectrum with the [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408) *m* removed is trivial.

:::

::: {#o204 .entry cue=term name="parameter"}

**parameter**

See system of parameters.

:::

::: {#o205 .entry cue=term name="perfect"}

**perfect**

In non-commutative ring theory, [perfect ring](wikipedia://en/22658615) has an unrelated meaning.

A module is called perfect if its projective dimension is equal to its grade.

An ideal *I* of a ring *R* is called perfect if *R*/*I* is a perfect module.

A field is called perfect if all finite extension fields are separable.

:::

::: {#o206 .entry cue=term name="Pic"}

**Pic**

:::

::: {#o207 .entry cue=term name="Picard group"}

**Picard group**

The [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) Pic(*R*) of a ring *R* is the group of isomorphism classes of finite projective modules of rank 1.

:::

::: {#o208 .entry cue=term name="PID"}

**PID**

Abbreviation for [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039).

:::

::: {#o209 .entry cue=term name="place"}

**place**

A [place of a field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_of_a_field) *K* with values in a field *L* is a map from *K*∪∞ to *L*∪∞ preserving addition and multiplication and 1.

:::

::: {#o210 .entry cue=term name="presentable"}

**presentable**

A presentable ring is one that is a quotient of a regular ring.

:::

::: {#o211 .entry cue=term name="prime"}

**prime**

A [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) is a proper ideal whose complement is closed under multiplication.

A [prime element](wikipedia://en/60692) of a ring is an element that generates a prime ideal.

A **prime local ring** is a localization of the integers at a prime ideal.

"Prime sequence" is an alternative name for a regular sequence.

:::

::: {#o212 .entry cue=term name="primary"}

**primary**

A **[primary ideal](wikipedia://en/3021435)** is a proper ideal *p* of a ring *R* such that if *rm* is in *p* then either *m* is in *p* or some power of *r* is in *p*. More generally a primary submodule of a module *M* is a submodule *N* of *M* such that if *rm* is in *N* then either *m* is in *N* or some power of *r* annihilates *N*.

A **[primary decomposition](wikipedia://en/1208391)** of an ideal or submodule is an expression of it as a finite intersection of primary ideals or submodules.

:::

::: {#o213 .entry cue=term name="principal"}

**principal**

A [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) is an ideal generated by one element.

A [principal ideal ring](wikipedia://en/6961771) is a ring such that every ideal is principal.

A [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039) is an integral domain such that every ideal is principal.

:::

::: {#o214 .entry cue=term name="projective"}

**projective**

A [projective module](wikipedia://en/364488) is a module such that every epimorphism to it splits.

A [projective resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_resolution) is a resolution by projective modules.

The [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension) of a module is the smallest length of a projective resolution.

:::

::: {#o215 .entry cue=term name="Prüfer domain"}

**Prüfer domain**

A [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835) is a semiherediary integral domain.

:::

::: {#o216 .entry cue=term name="pseudo"}

**pseudo**

A finitely generated module *M* is called **pseudo-zero** if $M_{\mathfrak{p}} = 0$ for all prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ of height $\le 1$.

A morphism of modules is **pseudo-injective** if the kernel is pseudo-zero.

A morphism of modules is **pseudo-surjective** if the cokernel is pseudo-zero.

"Pseudogeometric ring" is an alternative name for a [Nagata ring](wikipedia://en/10092186).

:::

::: {#o217 .entry cue=term name="pure"}

**pure**

A [pure submodule](wikipedia://en/904490) *M* of a module *N* is a submodule such that *M*⊗*A* is a submodule of *N*⊗*A* for all modules *A*.

A pure subring *R* of a ring *R* is a subring such that *M*=*M*⊗*S* is a submodule of *M*⊗<sub>*S*</sub>*R* for all *S*-modules *M*.

A pure module *M* over a ring *R* is a module such that dim(*M*) = dim(*R*/*p*) for every associated prime *p* of *M*.

:::

::: {#o218 .entry cue=term name="purely"}

**purely**

An element *x* is [purely inseparable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purely_inseparable) over a field if either the field has characteristic zero and *x* is in the field or the field has characteristic *p* and $x^{p^r}$ is in the field for some *r*.

A field extension is purely inseparable if it consists of purely inseparable elements.

:::

## Q {#Q}

::: {#o220 .entry cue=term name="quasi"}

**quasi**

A [quasi-excellent ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-excellent_ring) is a Grothendieck ring such that for every finitely generated algebra the singular points of the spectrum form a closed subset.

A [quasi-isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3653039) is a morphism between complexes inducing an isomorphism on homology.

**Quasi-local ring** was an old term for a (possibly non-Noetherian) local ring in books that assumed local rings to be Noetherian.

**[quasi-unmixed](wikipedia://en/60330511)**; see formally equidimensional.

:::

::: {#o221 .entry cue=term name="quotient"}

**quotient**

A quotient of a ring by an ideal, or of a module by a submodule.

A [quotient field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_field) (or the field of fractions) of an integral domain is the localization at the prime ideal zero. This is sometimes confused with the first meaning.

:::

## R {#R}

::: {#o223 .entry cue=term name="R"}

**R**

The condition *R*<sub>*n*</sub> on a ring (for a non-negative integer *n*), "regular in codimension *n*", says that localization at any prime ideal of height at most *n* is regular. (cf. [Serre's criterion on normality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_criterion_on_normality))

:::

::: {#o224 .entry cue=term name="radical"}

**radical**

The [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) of a ring.

The [nilradical](wikipedia://en/289636) of a ring.

A radical of an element *x* of a ring is an element such that some positive power is *x*.

The [radical of an ideal](wikipedia://en/252009) is the ideal of radicals of its elements.

The radical of a submodule *M* of a module *N* is the ideal of elements *x* such that some power of *x* maps *N* into *M*.

A [radical extension](wikipedia://en/33085116) of a ring is an extension generated by radicals of elements.

:::

::: {#o225 .entry cue=term name="ramification group"}

**ramification group**

A [ramification group](wikipedia://en/501614) is a group of automorphisms of a ring *R* fixing some given prime ideal *p* and acting trivially on *R*/*p*<sup>*n*</sup> for some integer *n*>1. (When *n*=1 it is called the inertia group.)

:::

::: {#o226 .entry cue=term name="rank"}

**rank**

Another older name for the height of a prime ideal.

The rank or height of a valuation is the Krull dimension of the corresponding valuation ring.

The rational or real rank of a valuation or place is the rational or real rank of its valuation group, which is the dimension of the corresponding rational or real vector space constructed by tensoring the valuation group with the rational or real numbers.

The minimum number of generators of a free module.

The rank of a module *M* over an integral domain *R* is the dimension of the vector space *M*⊗*K* over the quotient field *K* of *R*.

:::

::: {#o227 .entry cue=term name="reduced"}

**reduced**

A [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749) is one with no non-zero nilpotent elements.

Over a ring of characteristic *p*>0, a polynomial in several variables is called reduced if it has degree less than *p* in each variable.

:::

::: {#o228 .entry cue=term name="reducible"}

**reducible**

See irreducible.

:::

::: {#o229 .entry cue=term name="reduction"}

**reduction**

A reduction ideal of an ideal *I* with respect to a module *M* is an ideal *J* with *JI*<sup>*n*</sup>*M*=*I*<sup>*n*+1</sup>*M* for some positive integer *n*.

:::

::: {#o230 .entry cue=term name="Rees"}

**Rees**

Named after [David Rees](wikipedia://en/15398568)

The [Rees algebra](wikipedia://en/25362831) of an ideal *I* is $\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty} t^nI^n=R[It]\subset R[t].$

A [Rees decomposition](wikipedia://en/52095463) of an algebra is a way of writing in it in terms of polynomial subalgebras.

:::

::: {#o231 .entry cue=term name="reflexive"}

**reflexive**

A module *M* is [reflexive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexive_module) if the canonical map $M \to M^{**}, m \mapsto \langle \cdot, m \rangle$ is an isomorphism.

:::

::: {#o232 .entry cue=term name="regular"}

**regular**

A [regular local ring](wikipedia://en/506959) is a Noetherian local ring whose dimension is equal to the dimension of its tangent space.

A [regular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring) is a ring whose localizations at all prime ideals are regular.

A regular element of a ring is an element that is not a zero divisor.

An *M*-regular element of a ring for some module *M* is an element of *R* that does not annihilate any non-zero element of *M*.

A [regular sequence](wikipedia://en/505119) with respect to some module *M* is a sequence of elements *a*<sub>1</sub>,*a*<sub>2</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> of *R* such that each *a*<sub>*m*+1</sub> is regular for the module *M*/(*a*<sub>1</sub>,*a*<sub>2</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*m*</sub>)*M*.

In non-commutative ring theory, a [von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524) is a ring such that for every element *x* there is an element *y* with *xyx*=*x*. This is unrelated to the notion of a regular ring in commutative ring theory. In commutative algebra, commutative rings with this property are called **absolutely flat**.

:::

::: {#o233 .entry cue=term name="regularity"}

**regularity**

[Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity](wikipedia://en/24560170) is an invariant of a graded module over a graded ring related to the vanishing of various cohomology groups.

:::

::: {#o234 .entry cue=term name="residue field"}

**residue field**

The quotient of a ring, especially a local ring, by a maximal ideal.

:::

::: {#o235 .entry cue=term name="resolution"}

**resolution**

A [resolution of a module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_of_a_module) is a chain complex whose only non-zero homology group is the module.

:::

## S {#S}

::: {#o237 .entry cue=term name="S"}

**S**

The condition *S*<sub>*n*</sub> on a ring (for a non-negative integer *n*) says that the depth of the localization at any prime ideal is the height of the prime ideal whenever the depth is less than *n*. (cf. [Serre's criterion on normality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_criterion_on_normality))

:::

::: {#o238 .entry cue=term name="saturated"}

**saturated**

A subset *X* of a ring or module is called saturated with respect to a multiplicative subset *S* if *xs* in *X* and *s* in *S* implies that *x* is in *X*.

:::

::: {#o239 .entry cue=term name="saturation"}

**saturation**

The saturation of a subset of a ring or module is the smallest saturated subset containing it.

:::

::: {#o240 .entry cue=term name="semilocal"}

**semilocal**

:::

::: {#o241 .entry cue=term name="semi-local"}

**semi-local**

A [semilocal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semilocal_ring) is a ring with only a finite number of maximal ideals.

"Semi-local ring" is an archaic term for a [Zariski ring](wikipedia://en/27901866).

:::

::: {#o242 .entry cue=term name="seminormal"}

**seminormal**

A [seminormal ring](wikipedia://en/35984591) is a commutative [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749) in which, whenever *x*, *y* satisfy $x^3 = y^2$, there is *s* with $s^2 = x$ and $s^3 = y$.

:::

::: {#o243 .entry cue=term name="separable"}

**separable**

An algebra over a field is called separable if its extension by any finite purely inseparable extension is reduced.

:::

::: {#o244 .entry cue=term name="separated"}

**separated**

An alternative term for [Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_topology), usually applied to a topology on a ring or module.

:::

::: {#o245 .entry cue=term name="simple"}

**simple**

A **simple field** is an archaic term for an algebraic number field whose ring of integers is a unique factorization domain.

:::

::: {#o246 .entry cue=term name="singular"}

**singular**

Not regular

Special in some way

The [singular computer algebra system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_%28software%29) for commutative algebra

:::

::: {#o247 .entry cue=term name="smooth"}

**smooth**

A [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) of rings is a homomorphism that is formally smooth and finitely presented. These are analogous to submersions in differential topology. An algebra over a ring is called smooth if the corresponding morphism is smooth.

:::

::: {#o248 .entry cue=term name="socle"}

**socle**

The [socle of a module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socle_of_a_module) is the sum of its simple submodules.

:::

::: {#o249 .entry cue=term name="spectrum"}

**spectrum**

The [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) of a ring, often just called the spectrum, is a locally ringed space whose underlying topological space is the set of prime ideals with the Zariski topology.

The [maximal spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_spectrum) of a ring is the set of maximal ideals with the Zariski topology.

:::

::: {#o250 .entry cue=term name="stable"}

**stable**

A decreasing filtration of a module is called stable (with respect to an ideal *I*) if *M*<sub>*n*+1</sub>=*IM*<sub>*n*</sub> for all sufficiently large *n*.

:::

::: {#o251 .entry cue=term name="stably free"}

**stably free**

A module *M* over a ring *R* is called [stably free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stably_free) if *M*⊕*R*<sup>*n*</sup> is free for some natural number *n*.

:::

::: {#o252 .entry cue=term name="Stanley"}

**Stanley**

Named after [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397)

A [Stanley–Reisner ring](wikipedia://en/24394535) is a quotient of a polynomial algebra by a square-free monomial ideal.

A [Stanley decomposition](wikipedia://en/52095250) is a way of writing a ring in terms of polynomial subrings.

:::

::: {#o253 .entry cue=term name="strictly local"}

**strictly local**

A ring is called strictly local if it is a local Henselian ring whose residue field is separably closed.

:::

::: {#o254 .entry cue=term name="superfluous"}

**superfluous**

A submodule *M* of *N* is called [superfluous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfluous_submodule) if *M*+*X*=*N* implies *X*=*N* (for submodules *X*).

:::

::: {#o255 .entry cue=term name="superheight"}

**superheight**

The superheight of an ideal is the supremum of the nonzero codimensions of the proper extensions of the ideal under ring homomorphisms.

:::

::: {#o256 .entry cue=term name="support"}

**support**

The [support of a module](wikipedia://en/16073360) *M* is the set of prime ideals *p* such that the localization of *M* at *p* is non-zero.

:::

::: {#o257 .entry cue=term name="symbolic power"}

**symbolic power**

The [symbolic power](wikipedia://en/3021435) *p*<sup>(*n*)</sup> of a prime ideal *p* is the set of elements *x* such that *xy* is in *p*<sup>*n*</sup> for some *y* not in *p*. It is the smallest *p*-primary ideal containing *p*<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

::: {#o258 .entry cue=term name="system of parameters"}

**system of parameters**

A set of dim *R* (if finite) elements of a local ring *R* with maximal ideal *m* that generates an *m*-primary ideal. It is a **regular system of parameters** if it actually generates *m*.

:::

::: {#o259 .entry cue=term name="syzygy"}

**syzygy**

An element of the kernel of one of the maps in a free resolution of a module.

:::

## T {#T}

::: {#o261 .entry cue=term name="tangent"}

**tangent**

The [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207) of a local ring is the dual of its cotangent space.

:::

::: {#o262 .entry cue=term name="tight closure"}

**tight closure**

The [tight closure](wikipedia://en/6921893) *I*\* of an ideal *I* of a ring with positive characteristic *p*>0 consists of the elements *z* such that there is some *c* not in any minimal prime ideal such that *cz*<sup>*q*</sup> is in *I*<sup>\[*q*\]</sup> for all sufficiently large powers *q* of *p*, where *I*<sup>\[*q*\]</sup> is the ideal generated by all *q*th powers of elements of *I*.

:::

::: {#o263 .entry cue=term name="Tor"}

**Tor**

The [Torsion functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_functor), the derived functors of the tensor product.

:::

::: {#o264 .entry cue=term name="torsion"}

**torsion**

A [torsion element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_element) of a module over a ring is an element annihilated by some regular element of the ring.

The torsion submodule of a module is the submodule of torsion elements.

A [torsion-free module](wikipedia://en/38400572) is a module with no torsion elements other than zero.

A torsion module is one all of whose elements are torsion elements.

The [torsion functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_functor) Tor are the derived functors of the tensor product.

A [torsionless module](wikipedia://en/27481586) is a module isomorphic to a submodule of a free module.

:::

::: {#o265 .entry cue=term name="total"}

**total**

The [total ring of fractions](wikipedia://en/4276418) or [total quotient ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_quotient_ring) of a ring is formed by forcing all non zero divisors to have inverses.

:::

::: {#o266 .entry cue=term name="trivial"}

**trivial**

A trivial ring is a ring with only one element.

:::

::: {#o267 .entry cue=term name="type"}

**type**

The type of a finitely generated module *M* of depth *d* over a Noetherian local ring *R* with residue field *k* is the dimension (over *k*) of Ext${}^{d}_{R}$(*k*,*M*).

:::

## U {#U}

::: {#o269 .entry cue=term name="UFD"}

**UFD**

Abbreviation for [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546).

:::

::: {#o270 .entry cue=term name="unibranch"}

**unibranch**

A reduced local ring is called [unibranch](wikipedia://en/26130615) if it is integral and its integral closure is a local ring. A local ring is called unibranch if the corresponding reduced local ring is unibranch.

:::

::: {#o271 .entry cue=term name="unimodular row"}

**unimodular row**

A sequence of elements $v_1, \dots, v_n$ in a ring that generate the unit ideal.

:::

::: {#o272 .entry cue=term name="unique factorization domain"}

**unique factorization domain**

Also called a factorial domain. A [unique factorization domain](wikipedia://en/60546) is an integral domain such that every element can be written as a product of primes in a way that is unique up to order and multiplication by units.

:::

::: {#o273 .entry cue=term name="universally"}

**universally**

A property is said to hold universally if it holds for various base changes. For example a ring is [universally catenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universally_catenary) if all finitely generated algebras over it are catenary.

:::

::: {#o274 .entry cue=term name="universal"}

**universal**

A universal field is an algebraically closed field with the uncountable transcendence degree over its prime field.

:::

::: {#o275 .entry cue=term name="unmixed"}

**unmixed**

An ideal *I* of a ring *R* is called unmixed if all associated primes of *R*/*I* have the same height.

:::

::: {#o276 .entry cue=term name="unramified"}

**unramified**

An [unramified morphism](wikipedia://en/58653736) of rings is a homomorphism that is formally unramified and finitely presented. These are analogous to immersions in differential topology. An algebra over a ring is called unramified if the corresponding morphism is unramified.

An ideal in a polynomial ring over a field is called unramified for some extension of the field if the corresponding extension of the ideal is an intersection of prime ideals.

:::

## V {#V}

::: {#o278 .entry cue=term name="valuation"}

**valuation**

A [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) is a homomorphism from the non-zero elements of a field to a totally ordered abelian group, with properties similar to the *p*-adic valuation of the rational numbers.

A [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) is an integral domain *R* such that if *x* is in its quotient field and if it is nonzero then either *x* or its inverse is in *R*.

A **valuation group** is a totally ordered abelian group. The valuation group of a valuation ring is the group of non-zero elements of the quotient field modulo the group of units of the valuation ring.

:::

## W {#W}

::: {#o280 .entry cue=term name="weak"}

**weak**

Weak dimension is an alternative name for flat dimension of a module.

A sequence $(a_1,\cdots,a_r)$ of elements of a maximal ideal $m$ is called a [weak sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_sequence) if $m\cdot((a_1,\cdots,a_{i-1})\colon a_i)\subset(a_1,\cdots,a_{i-1})$ for all $i$.

:::

::: {#o281 .entry cue=term name="Weierstrass ring"}

**Weierstrass ring**

A [Weierstrass ring](wikipedia://en/14623014) is local ring that is Henselian, pseudo-geometric, and such that any quotient ring by a prime ideal is a finite extension of a regular local ring.

**Zariski**

Named after [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352)

A [Zariski ring](wikipedia://en/27901866) is a complete Noetherian topological ring with a basis of neighborhoods of 0 given by the powers of an ideal in the Jacobson radical (formerly called a semi-local ring).

The [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is the topology on the [spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291) whose closed sets are the sets of prime ideals containing a given ideal.

[Zariski's lemma](wikipedia://en/37279342) says that if a field is a finitely generated algebra over another field then it is a finite dimensional vector space over the field.

[Zariski's main lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski's_main_lemma) on holomorphic functions says the *n*-th [symbolic power of a prime ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_power_of_a_prime_ideal) in a polynomial ring is the intersection of the *n*-th powers of the maximal ideals containing the prime ideal.

The [Zariski tangent space](wikipedia://en/510207) of a local ring with maximal ideal *m* is the dual of the vector space *m*/*m*<sup>2</sup>.

:::

::: {#o284 .entry cue=term name="zero divisor"}

**zero divisor**

A [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) in a ring is an element whose product with some nonzero element is 0.

:::
