# Howard Kahane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Howard Kahane"}

**Howard Kahane** (19 April 1928 – 2 May 2001) was an American professor of philosophy at [Bernard M. Baruch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_College) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). He was noted for promoting a popular, and non-mathematical, approach to logic, now known as [informal logic](wikipedia://en/14122398).[^o3] His best known publication in that area is his textbook *Logic and Contemporary Rhetoric: The Use of Reason in Everyday Life*,[^o3][^o4] now in the 12th edition, published in 2014.[^o5]

:::

Another textbook of his that saw posthumous publication is *Logic and Philosophy: A Modern Introduction* (12th edition in 2012).[^o6]

Kahane graduated with a master's degree from the [University of California at Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Los_Angeles) (1958) and received a Ph.D. from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1962. Before Baruch College, Kahane taught at [Whitman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitman_College), the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University) and the [University of Maryland at Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland_at_Baltimore).[^o3]

Nancy Cavender, who is a professor emeritus at the [College of Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Marin) and coauthored later editions of Kahane's 1971 textbook,[^o5] was also "his companion", according to *The New York Times*.[^o3] Kahane fathered one daughter.[^o3]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

According to [argumentation theory](wikipedia://en/1317383) scholar Michael A. Gilbert, before Kahane's 1971 book, North American curricula on [critical thinking](wikipedia://en/276872) and [fallacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacies) were primarily taught from textbooks (such as [Irving Copi](wikipedia://en/4352005)'s *Introduction to Logic*) in which "fallacies are presented in a brief fashion using examples that were mostly invented or taken out of context. \[...\] The 'radical change' was that Kahane's book took current examples from newspapers and periodicals dealing with issues students cared about or, at least, recognized. This meant that fallacies were more *situated* than in older books."[^o8]

Linguistics professor Louise Cummings notes that Kahane's book marked a "shift to context", that is, toward [pragmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics) criteria for evaluating [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173) arguments. She cites Van Eemeren, Grootendrost and Henkemans's (of the [pragma-dialectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragma-dialectic) school) observation that thanks to his contextual approach Kahane "added new fallacies to the traditional list, so the one will find not only *[ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226)* but also '[provincialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincialism)', not only *[ad verecundiam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_verecundiam)*, but also the '[red herring](wikipedia://en/3197900)', not only [hasty generalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasty_generalization) but also '[suppressed evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suppressed_evidence)'."[^o9]
