# Erdős–Turán inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erdős–Turán inequality"}

In mathematics, the **Erdős–Turán inequality** bounds the distance between a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/23543) on the circle and the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), in terms of [Fourier coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_coefficient). It was proved by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) in 1948.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Let *μ* be a probability measure on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) **R**/**Z**. The Erdős–Turán inequality states that, for any natural number *n*,

$$\sup_A \left| \mu(A) - \mathrm{mes}\, A \right| \leq C \left( \frac{1}{n} + \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{|\hat{\mu}(k)|}{k} \right),$$

where the supremum is over all [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) *A* ⊂ **R**/**Z** of the unit circle, *mes* stands for the Lebesgue measure,

$$\hat{\mu}(k) = \int \exp(2 \pi i k \theta) \, d\mu(\theta)$$

are the [Fourier coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_coefficients) of *μ*, and *C* > 0 is a numerical constant.

## Application to discrepancy {#Application_to_discrepancy}

Let *s*<sub>1</sub>, *s*<sub>2</sub>, *s*<sub>3</sub> ... ∈ **R** be a sequence. The Erdős–Turán inequality applied to the measure

$$\mu_m(S) = \frac{1}{m} \# \{ 1 \leq j \leq m \, | \, s_j \, \mathrm{mod} \, 1 \in S \}, \quad S \subset [0, 1),$$

yields the following bound for the [discrepancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrepancy_of_a_sequence#Discrepancy):

$$\begin{aligned} D(m) & \left( = \sup_{0 \leq a \leq b \leq 1} \Big| m^{-1} \# \{ 1 \leq j \leq m \, | \, a \leq s_j \, \mathrm{mod} \, 1 \leq b \} - (b-a) \Big| \right) \\[8pt] & \leq C \left( \frac{1}{n} + \frac{1}{m} \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{k} \left| \sum_{j=1}^m e^{2 \pi i s_j k} \right|\right). \end{aligned} \qquad (1)$$

This inequality holds for arbitrary natural numbers *m,n*, and gives a quantitative form of [Weyl's criterion](wikipedia://en/1030420#Weyl's_criterion) for [equidistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equidistribution).

A multi-dimensional variant of (1) is known as the [Erdős–Turán–Koksma inequality](wikipedia://en/478601#The_Erdős–Turán–Koksma_inequality).
