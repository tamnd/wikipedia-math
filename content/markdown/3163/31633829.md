# Czesław Olech

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Czesław Olech"}

**Czesław Olech** (22 May 1931 – 1 July 2015) was a Polish mathematician. He was a representative of the [Kraków school of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków_school_of_mathematics), especially the [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) school of [Tadeusz Ważewski](wikipedia://en/1028319).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1954 he completed his mathematical studies at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University), in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) obtained his doctorate at the Institute of Mathematical Sciences in 1958, habilitation in 1962, the title of associate professor in 1966, and the title of professor in 1973.

- 1970–1986: director of The Institute of Mathematics, [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).
- 1972–1991: director of Stefan Banach International Mathematical Center in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).
- 1979–1986: member of the Executive Committee, [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).
- 1982–1983: president of the Organizing Committee, [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw),
- 1987–1989: president of the Board of Mathematics, [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).
- 1990–2002: president of the Scientific Council, Institute of Mathematics of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences).

Czeslaw Olech, often as a visiting professor, was invited by the world's leading mathematical centers in the United States, USSR (later Russia), Canada and many European countries. He cooperated with [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), [Sergey Nikolsky](wikipedia://en/3550276), Philip Hartman and Roberto Conti, the most distinguished mathematicians involved in the theory of differential equations. Based on joint work with Hartman, he proved the [Olech theorem](wikipedia://en/55100201). Lefschetz highly valued Ważewski's school, and especially the retract method, which Olech applied by developing, among other things, [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039). He supervised nine doctoral dissertations, and reviewed a number of theses and dissertations.[^o4]

## Main fields of research interest {#Main_fields_of_research_interest}

- Contributions to [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations):
  - various applications of [Tadeusz Ważewski](wikipedia://en/1028319) topological method in studying [asymptotic behaviour](wikipedia://en/641995) of solutions;
  - exact estimates of exponential growth of solution of second-order [linear differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868) with bounded coefficients;
  - [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) concerning global [asymptotic stability](wikipedia://en/363360) of the autonomous system on the plane with stable [Jacobian matrix](wikipedia://en/195351) at each point of the plane, results establishing relation between question of global asymptotic stability of an autonomous system and that of global one-to-oneness of a differentiable map;
  - contribution to the question whether unicity condition implies convergence of successive approximation to solutions of [ordinary differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_differential_equations).
- Contribution to optimal [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039):
  - establishing a most general version of the so-called [bang-bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bang-bang_control) principle for linear control problem by detailed study of the integral of set valued map;
  - existence [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) for [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) problem with unbounded controls and multidimensional cost functions;
  - existence of solution of [differential inclusions](wikipedia://en/13725281) with nonconvex right-hand side;
  - characterization of controllability of convex processes.[^o6]
