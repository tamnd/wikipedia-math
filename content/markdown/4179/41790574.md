# Moduli stack of principal bundles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moduli stack of principal bundles"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), given a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_curve) [projective curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_curve) *X* over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbf{F}_q$ and a smooth [affine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_group_scheme) [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) *G* over it, the **moduli stack of principal bundles** over *X*, denoted by $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$, is an [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377) given by:[^o2] for any $\mathbf{F}_q$-algebra *R*,

:::

$\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)(R)=$ the category of [principal *G*-bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) over the relative curve $X \times_{\mathbf{F}_q} \operatorname{Spec}R$.

In particular, the category of $\mathbf{F}_q$-points of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$, that is, $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)(\mathbf{F}_q)$, is the category of *G*-bundles over *X*.

Similarly, $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$ can also be defined when the curve *X* is over the field of complex numbers. Roughly, in the complex case, one can define $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$ as the [quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087) of the space of holomorphic connections on *X* by the [gauge group](wikipedia://en/39240530). Replacing the quotient stack (which is not a topological space) by a [homotopy quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_quotient) (which is a topological space) gives the [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type) of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$.

In the finite field case, it is not common to define the homotopy type of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$. But one can still define a ([smooth](wikipedia://en/41891595)) [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) and [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

It is known that $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$ is a [smooth stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_stack) of dimension $(g(X) - 1) \dim G$ where $g(X)$ is the genus of *X*. It is not of finite type but locally of finite type; one thus usually uses a stratification by open substacks of finite type (cf. the [Harder–Narasimhan stratification](wikipedia://en/41799059)), also for parahoric G over curve X see [^o5] and for G only a flat group scheme of finite type over X see.[^o6]

If *G* is a split reductive group, then the set of connected components $\pi_0(\operatorname{Bun}_G(X))$ is in a natural bijection with the fundamental group $\pi_1(G)$.[^o7]

## Behrend's trace formula {#Behrend's_trace_formula}

This is a (conjectural) version of the [Lefschetz trace formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_trace_formula) for $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$ when *X* is over a finite field, introduced by [Behrend](wikipedia://en/31872403) in 1993.[^o12] It states:[^o13] if *G* is a [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) affine [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) with semisimple connected [generic fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_fiber), then

$$\# \operatorname{Bun}_G(X)(\mathbf{F}_q) = q^{\dim \operatorname{Bun}_G(X)} \operatorname{tr} (\phi^{-1}|H^*(\operatorname{Bun}_G(X); \mathbb{Z}_l))$$

where (see also [Behrend's trace formula](wikipedia://en/41791807) for the details)

- *l* is a prime number that is not *p* and the ring $\mathbb{Z}_l$ of [l-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_integers) is viewed as a subring of $\mathbb{C}$.
- $\phi$ is the [geometric Frobenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Frobenius).
- $\# \operatorname{Bun}_G(X)(\mathbf{F}_q) = \sum_P {1 \over \# \operatorname{Aut}(P)}$, the sum running over all isomorphism classes of [G-bundles](wikipedia://en/41910974) on *X* and convergent.
- $\operatorname{tr}(\phi^{-1}|V_*) = \sum_{i = 0}^\infty (-1)^i \operatorname{tr}(\phi^{-1}|V_i)$ for a [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) $V_*$, provided the [series](wikipedia://en/15287) on the right absolutely converges.

*A priori,* neither left nor right side in the formula converges. Thus, the formula states that the two sides converge to finite numbers and that those numbers coincide.

:::
