# Behrend's trace formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Behrend's trace formula"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Behrend's trace formula** is a generalization of the [Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck–Lefschetz_trace_formula) to a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_stack) [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) conjectured in 1993 [^o2] and proven in 2003 [^o3] by [Kai Behrend](wikipedia://en/31872403). Unlike the classical one, the formula counts points in the "[stacky way](wikipedia://en/13185596)"; it takes into account the presence of nontrivial automorphisms.

:::

The desire for the formula comes from the fact that it applies to the [moduli stack of principal bundles](wikipedia://en/41790574) on a curve over a finite field (in some instances indirectly, via the [Harder–Narasimhan stratification](wikipedia://en/41799059), as the moduli stack is not of finite type.[^o4][^o5]) See the [moduli stack of principal bundles](wikipedia://en/41790574) and references therein for the precise formulation in this case.

[Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) found an example[^o6] that shows the formula may be interpreted as a sort of the [Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/981694).

A proof of the formula in the context of the [six operations](wikipedia://en/39560800) formalism developed by [Yves Laszlo](wikipedia://en/2803385) and Martin Olsson[^o7] is given by Shenghao Sun.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

By definition, if *C* is a category in which each object has finitely many automorphisms, the number of points in $C$ is denoted by

$$\# C = \sum_p {1 \over \# \operatorname{Aut}(p)},$$

with the sum running over representatives *p* of all isomorphism classes in *C*. (The series may diverge in general.) The formula states: for a smooth algebraic stack *X* of finite type over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ and the ["arithmetic" Frobenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_Frobenius) $\phi^{-1}: X \to X$, i.e., the inverse of the usual geometric Frobenius $\phi$ in Grothendieck's formula,[^o11][^o12]

$$\# X (\mathbb{F}_q) = q^{\dim X} \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} (-1)^i \operatorname{tr} \left (\phi^{-1}; H^i(X, \mathbb{Q}_l) \right ).$$

Here, it is crucial that the [cohomology of a stack](wikipedia://en/61607382) is with respect to the [smooth topology](wikipedia://en/41891595) (not etale).

When *X* is a variety, the smooth cohomology is the same as etale one and, via the [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), this is equivalent to Grothendieck's trace formula. (But the proof of Behrend's trace formula relies on Grothendieck's formula, so this does not subsume Grothendieck's.)

:::

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Simple example"}

## Simple example {#Simple_example}

Consider $B\mathbb{G}_m = [\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{F}_q/\mathbb{G}_m]$, the [classifying stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classifying_stack) of the multiplicative [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) (that is, $\mathbb{G}_m(R) = R^\times$). By definition, $B \mathbb{G}_m(\mathbb{F}_q)$ is the category of [principal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) $\mathbb{G}_m$-bundles over $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{F}_q$, which has only one isomorphism class (since all such bundles are trivial by [Lang's theorem](wikipedia://en/42146937)). Its group of automorphisms is $\mathbb{G}_m$, which means that the number of $\mathbb{F}_q$-isomorphisms is $\#\mathbb{G}_m(\mathbb{F}_q) = \#\mathbb{F}_q^\times = q-1$.

On the other hand, we may compute the *l*-adic cohomology of $B\mathbb{G}_m$ directly. We remark that in the topological setting, we have $B\mathbb{C}^\times \cong \mathbb{CP}^\infty$ (where $B\mathbb{C}^\times$ now denotes the usual classifying space of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315)), whose rational cohomology ring is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) in one generator ([Borel's theorem](wikipedia://en/43948116)), but we shall not use this directly. If we wish to stay in the world of algebraic geometry, we may instead "approximate" $B\mathbb{G}_m$ by projective spaces of larger and larger dimension. Thus we consider the map $B\mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{P}^N$ induced by the $\mathbb{G}_m$-bundle corresponding to $\mathcal{O}(1).$ This map induces an isomorphism in cohomology in degrees up to *2N*. Thus the even (resp. odd) Betti numbers of $B \mathbb{G}_m$ are 1 (resp. 0), and the *l*-adic Galois representation on the *(2n)*th cohomology group is the *n*th power of the cyclotomic character. The second part is a consequence of the fact that the cohomology of $\mathbb{P}^N$ is generated by algebraic cycle classes. This shows that

$$\sum_{i \ge 0} (-1)^i \operatorname{tr} \left (\phi^{-1}; H^i(B\mathbb{G}_m, \mathbb{Q}_l) \right ) = 1 + \frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{q^2} + \cdots = \frac{q}{q-1}.$$

Note that

$$\dim B \mathbb{G}_m = \dim \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{F}_q - \dim \mathbb{G}_m = -1.$$

Multiplying by $q^{-1}$, one obtains the predicted equality.

:::
