# Erich Schönhardt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Erich Schönhardt"}

*Image: Erich Schönhardt.jpg.* Image of Erich Schönhardt

**Erich Schönhardt** (25 June 1891 – 29 November 1979)[^o2][^o3] was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his 1928 discovery of the [Schönhardt polyhedron](wikipedia://en/20346715), a non-convex polyhedron that cannot be partitioned into [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) without introducing additional [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).[^o4]

:::

Schönhardt studied at the [University of Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart),[^o3] and went on to do his graduate studies at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen), receiving his Ph.D. in 1920 for a thesis on [Schottky groups](wikipedia://en/5871034)[^o5] under the supervision of [Ludwig Maurer](wikipedia://en/17155880).[^o3] In the 1930s, he was the *Dozentenführer* ([Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) political leader of the faculty) at Tübingen,[^o3][^o6] and was responsible for denouncing fellow Tübingen mathematician [Erich Kamke](wikipedia://en/22079478) for having married a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) woman.[^o6] He moved back to the University of Stuttgart in 1936[^o3] and was [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) there from 1939 to 1942.[^o2][^o3][^o7] He was a permanent editor of the journal *[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)*.
