# Bratteli diagram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bratteli diagram"}

In mathematics, a **Bratteli diagram** is a combinatorial structure: a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) composed of vertices labelled by positive integers ("level") and unoriented edges between vertices having levels differing by one. The notion was introduced by [Ola Bratteli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ola_Bratteli)[^o2] in 1972 in the theory of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987) to describe directed sequences of finite-dimensional algebras: it played an important role in Elliott's classification of [AF-algebras](wikipedia://en/17936142) and the theory of [subfactors](wikipedia://en/2780098). Subsequently [Anatoly Vershik](wikipedia://en/9036441) associated [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) with infinite paths in such graphs.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Bratteli diagram is given by the following objects:

- A sequence of sets *V*<sub>*n*</sub> ('the vertices at level *n* ') labeled by positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) set **N**. In some literature each element v of *V*<sub>*n*</sub> is accompanied by a positive integer *b*<sub>*v*</sub> > 0.
- A sequence of sets *E*<sub>*n*</sub> ('the edges from level *n* to *n* + 1 ') labeled by **N**, endowed with maps *s*: *E*<sub>*n*</sub> → *V*<sub>*n*</sub> and *r*: *E*<sub>*n*</sub> → *V*<sub>*n*+1</sub>, such that:
  - For each *v* in *V*<sub>*n*</sub>, the number of elements *e* in *E*<sub>*n*</sub> with *s*(*e*) = *v* is finite.
  - So is the number of *e* ∈ *E*<sub>*n*−1</sub> with *r*(*e*) = *v*.
  - When the vertices have markings by positive integers *b*<sub>*v*</sub>, the number *a*<sub>*v*, *v* '</sub> of the edges with *s*(*e*) = *v* and *r*(*e*) = v' for *v* ∈ *V*<sub>*n*</sub> and v' ∈ *V*<sub>*n*+1</sub> satisfies *b*<sub>*v*</sub> *a*<sub>v, v'</sub> ≤ *b*<sub>v'</sub>.

A customary way to pictorially represent Bratteli diagrams is to align the vertices according to their levels, and put the number *b*<sub>*v*</sub> beside the vertex *v*, or use that number in place of *v*, as in

*Image: Sample Bratteli diagram.svg.*

An **ordered Bratteli diagram** is a Bratteli diagram together with a partial order on *E*<sub>*n*</sub> such that for any *v* ∈ *V*<sub>*n*</sub> the set { *e* ∈ *E*<sub>*n*−1</sub> : *r*(*e*) = *v* } is totally ordered. Edges that do not share a common range vertex are incomparable. This partial order allows us to define the set of all maximal edges *E*<sub>max</sub> and the set of all minimal edges *E*<sub>min</sub>. A Bratteli diagram with a unique infinitely long path in *E*<sub>max</sub> and *E*<sub>min</sub> is called *essentially simple*. [^o6]

:::

## Sequence of finite-dimensional algebras {#Sequence_of_finite-dimensional_algebras}

Any [semisimple algebra](wikipedia://en/3876151) over the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) **C** of finite dimension can be expressed as a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_algebras) ⊕<sub>k</sub> M<sub>n<sub>k</sub></sub>(**C**) of [matrix algebras](wikipedia://en/487541), and the **C**-algebra homomorphisms between two such algebras up to inner automorphisms on both sides are completely determined by the multiplicity number between 'matrix algebra' components. Thus, an injective homomorphism of ⊕<sub>*k*=1</sub><sup>*i*</sup> *M*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub>(**C**) into ⊕<sub>*l*=1</sub><sup>*j*</sup> *M*<sub>*m*<sub>*l*</sub></sub>(**C**) may be represented by a collection of positive numbers *a*<sub>*k*, *l*</sub> satisfying Σ *n*<sub>*k*</sub> *a*<sub>*k*, l</sub> ≤ *m*<sub>*l*</sub>. (The equality holds [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the homomorphism is unital; we can allow non-injective homomorphisms by allowing some *a*<sub>*k*,*l*</sub> to be zero.) This can be illustrated as a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) having the vertices marked by numbers (*n*<sub>*k*</sub>)<sub>*k*</sub> on one hand and the ones marked by (*m*<sub>*l*</sub>)<sub>*l*</sub> on the other hand, and having *a*<sub>*k*, *l*</sub> edges between the vertex *n*<sub>*k*</sub> and the vertex *m*<sub>*l*</sub>.

Thus, when we have a sequence of finite-dimensional semisimple algebras *A*<sub>*n*</sub> and injective homomorphisms *φ*<sub>*n*</sub> : *A*<sub>*n*</sub> → *A*<sub>*n*+1</sub>: between them, we obtain a Bratteli diagram by putting

*V*<sub>*n*</sub> = the set of simple components of *A*<sub>*n*</sub>

(each isomorphic to a matrix algebra), marked by the size of matrices.

(*E*<sub>*n*</sub>, *r*, *s*): the number of the edges between *M*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub>(**C**) ⊂ *A*<sub>*n*</sub> and *M*<sub>*m*<sub>*l*</sub></sub>(**C**) ⊂ *A*<sub>*n*+1</sub> is equal to the multiplicity of *M*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub>(**C**) into *M*<sub>*m*<sub>*l*</sub></sub>(**C**) under *φ*<sub>*n*</sub>.

## Sequence of split semisimple algebras {#Sequence_of_split_semisimple_algebras}

Any [semisimple algebra](wikipedia://en/3876151) (possibly of infinite dimension) is one whose [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) are completely reducible, i.e. they decompose into the direct sum of [simple modules](wikipedia://en/276410#Types_of_modules). Let $A_0 \subseteq A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq \cdots$ be a chain of split semisimple algebras, and let $\hat A_i$ be the indexing set for the irreducible representations of $A_i$. Denote by $A_i^\lambda$ the irreducible module indexed by $\lambda \in \hat A_i$. Because of the inclusion $A_i \subseteq A_{i+1}$, any $A_{i+1}$-module $M$ restricts to a $A_i$-module. Let $g_{\lambda, \mu}$ denote the decomposition numbers

$$A_{i+1}^\mu \downarrow^{A_{i+1}}_{A_i} = \bigoplus_{\lambda \in \hat A_i} g_{\lambda,\mu} A_i^\lambda.$$

The **Bratteli diagram** for the chain $A_0 \subseteq A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq \cdots$ is obtained by placing one vertex for every element of $\hat A_i$ on level $i$ and connecting a vertex $\lambda$ on level $i$ to a vertex $\mu$ on level $i+1$ with $g_{\lambda,\mu}$ edges.

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Brauer-Bratteli.jpg.* Bratteli diagram for Brauer and BMW algebras on i=0,1,2,3, and 4 strands.

(1) If $A_i = S_i$, the ith [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901), the corresponding Bratteli diagram is the same as [Young's lattice](wikipedia://en/14836297).[^o11]

(2) If $A_i$ is the [Brauer algebra](wikipedia://en/3875858) or the [Birman–Wenzl algebra](wikipedia://en/22180498) on *i* strands, then the resulting Bratteli diagram has partitions of *i*–2*k* (for $k=0,1,2,\ldots,\lfloor i/2 \rfloor$) with one edge between partitions on adjacent levels if one can be obtained from the other by adding or subtracting 1 from a single part.

(3) If $A_i$ is the [Temperley–Lieb algebra](wikipedia://en/2802848) on *i* strands, the resulting Bratteli has integers *i*–2*k* (for $k=0,1,2,\ldots,\lfloor i/2 \rfloor$) with one edge between integers on adjacent levels if one can be obtained from the other by adding or subtracting 1.

:::
