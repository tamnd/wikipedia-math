# Claude Lemaréchal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Claude Lemaréchal"}

**Claude Lemaréchal** is a French [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician), and former senior researcher (*directeur de recherche*) at [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA)[^o3] near [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble), France.

:::

In [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), Claude Lemaréchal is known for his work in [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/15237) for [nonlinear optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optimization), especially for problems with [nondifferentiable kinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdifferential). Lemaréchal and [Philip Wolfe](wikipedia://en/41154952) pioneered [bundle methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundle_method) of descent for [convex minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_minimization).[^o4]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1994, Claude Lemaréchal and [Roger J-B Wets](wikipedia://en/26298695) were each awarded the [George B. Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Dantzig) Prize. Recognizing "original research that has had a major impact on the field of mathematical programming", the Dantzig Prize is awarded by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) and the Mathematical Programming Society (MPS).[^o4]

## Lagrangian duality and nonconvex primal problems {#Lagrangian_duality_and_nonconvex_primal_problems}

Soon after joining [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Research_in_Computer_Science_and_Control) (then named "[IRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Research_in_Computer_Science_and_Control)"), Lemaréchal had the assignment of helping a glass-manufacturer with a problem of [scheduling its production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduling_%28production_processes%29), a problem whose first formulation required [minimizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization) a [non-convex function](wikipedia://en/245568). For this non-convex minimization problem, Lemaréchal applied the theory of [Lagrangian duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_multiplier) that was described in Lasdon's *Optimization Theory for Large Systems*.[^o7][^o8] Because the primal problem was non-convex, there was no guarantee that a solution to the dual problem would provide useful information about the primal. Nonetheless, the dual problem did furnish useful information.[^o9] Lemaréchal's success with [Lagrangian dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_multiplier) [methods](wikipedia://en/9891368) on [nonlinear programming](wikipedia://en/679709) problems with [nonconvexities](wikipedia://en/245568) interested Ivar Ekeland and Jean–Pierre Aubin, who applied the [Shapley–Folkman lemma](wikipedia://en/29237460) to explain Lemaréchal's success.[^o10][^o11] The Aubin–Ekeland analysis of duality gaps considered the *[convex](wikipedia://en/1001490#Biconjugate_convex)* [closure](wikipedia://en/1001490#Biconjugate) of a nonconvex minimization problem — that is, the problem defined by the [closed](wikipedia://en/331597) [convex](wikipedia://en/40634) [hull](wikipedia://en/483120) of the [epigraph](wikipedia://en/5744061) of the original problem. Following Ekeland and Aubin, similar applications of the [Shapley–Folkman lemma](wikipedia://en/29237460) are described in optimization monographs[^o11][^o12] and textbooks.[^o13] These developments were catalyzed by Lemaréchal's demonstration that Lagrangian-dual methods were useful on some [optimization problems](wikipedia://en/1126536) that lacked [convexity](wikipedia://en/6292).

## Bundle methods of descent {#Bundle_methods_of_descent}

Lemaréchal's research also led to his work on ([conjugate](wikipedia://en/8980593)) [subgradient methods](wikipedia://en/9891368) and on bundle methods of [descent](wikipedia://en/201489) for [convex minimization problems](wikipedia://en/1674411).
