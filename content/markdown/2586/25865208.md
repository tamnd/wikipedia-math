# Gabriel Nuchelmans

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gabriel Nuchelmans"}

**Gabriel Nuchelmans** (15 May 1922, [Oud Gastel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halderberge) – 6 August 1996, [Wassenaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassenaar)) was a Dutch [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), who focused on the [history of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_philosophy) as well as on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and the [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language) more in particular.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

After completing [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) at the Episcopal School of Roermond, Nuchelmans studied at the [Catholic University of Nijmegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Nijmegen), where he also earned his PhD in 1950. During the PhD he spent a year in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) with Olof Gigon and Joseph Maria Bocheński. In 1947/48 he attended courses by [Alfred Ayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Ayer) and [Stuart Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Hampshire), at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). He also heard, at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics), [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper) and [J. O. Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Oulton_Wisdom).

After earning his PhD, Nuchelmans taught for fourteen years Latin and Greek in [Velsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velsen). From 1964 he taught Ancient Philosophy and Analytic Philosophy and its History at the Philosophical Institute of the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden) until his retirement on 10 September 1987. In this occasion a volume of essays (Logos and Pragma) was dedicated to him to celebrate his scholarly achievements. Nuchelmans had since 1975 been a member of the [Koninklijke Nederlandse Akademie van Wetenschappen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninklijke_Nederlandse_Akademie_van_Wetenschappen).[^o3] His great work in three volumes on the history of the theories of proposition (1973, 1980, 1983) will remain for a long time the standard work on the subject.

He also wrote several more introductory books, including a work on [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume) (1965) and a survey of the history of [analytical philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_philosophy) (1969).
