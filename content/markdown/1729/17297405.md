# John Casey (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Casey"}

**John Casey** (12 May 1820, [Kilbehenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilbehenny), [County Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Limerick), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland) – 3 January 1891, [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin)) was a respected [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_people) [geometer](wikipedia://en/18973446). He is most famous for [Casey's theorem](wikipedia://en/14599476) on a circle that is tangent to four other circles, an extension of [Ptolemy's theorem](wikipedia://en/1521971). However, he contributed several novel proofs and perspectives on [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417). He and [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333) are considered to be the co-founders of the modern geometry of the circle and the triangle.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born at Kilbehenny in Limerick, Ireland and educated locally at Mitchelstown, before becoming a teacher under the Board of National Education. He later became headmaster of the Central Model Schools in [Kilkenny City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilkenny). He subsequently entered [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) in 1858, where he was [elected a Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scholars_of_Trinity_College_Dublin) in 1861 and was awarded the degree of BA in 1862. He was then Mathematics Master at Kingston School (1862–1873), Professor of Higher Mathematics and Mathematical Physics at the newly founded [Catholic University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Ireland) (1873–1881) and Lecturer in Mathematics at its successor, the University College Dublin (1881–1891).[^o5]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

In 1869, the [University of Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dublin) awarded Casey the Honorary Degree of Doctor of Laws. He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in June 1875. He was elected to the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) and in 1880 became a member of its council. In 1878 the Academy conferred upon him the much coveted Cunningham Gold Medal.[^o8] His work was also acknowledged by the Norwegian Government, among others. He was elected a member of the Societe Mathematique de France in 1884 and received the honorary degree of Doctor of Laws from the [Royal University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_University_of_Ireland) in 1885.
