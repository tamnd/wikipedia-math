# Satyendra Nath Bose

Scientists

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Satyendra Nath Bose"}

**Satyendra Nath Bose** (;[^o7] 1 January 1894 – 4 February 1974) was an Indian [theoretical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is best known for his work on [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) in the early 1920s, in developing the foundation for [Bose–Einstein statistics](wikipedia://en/188935), and the theory of the [Bose–Einstein condensate](wikipedia://en/4474). A [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society#Fellows) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), he was awarded India's second highest civilian award, the [Padma Vibhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Vibhushan), in 1954 by the [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India).[^n1][^o44][^o22]

:::

The [eponymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_phenomena_named_after_people#B) particles class described by Bose's statistics, [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson), were named by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742).[^o9][^o10]

A [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath), he had a wide range of interests in varied fields, including [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [mineralogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineralogy), [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), [arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_arts), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature), and [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music). He served on many research and development committees in [India after independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement).[^n2]

## Early life {#Early_life}

*Image: House of Satyendra Nath Bose 01.jpg.* Satyendra Nath Bose Birthplace

*Image: House of Acharya Satyendra Nath Bose.jpg.* Satyendra Nath Bose Residence (22, Ishwar Mill Lane, Kolkata) Entrance & Name Plate

Bose was born in [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta) (now Kolkata), the eldest of seven children in a [Bengali Kayastha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_Kayastha) family.[^o13] Bose's father was Surendra Nath. Bose's mother was ([Smt.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs)) Amodini Debi. Surendra was an accountant and worked in [East India Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indian_Railway_Company).[^o14] Bose was the only son, with six sisters after him. His ancestral home was in the village Bara Jagulia, in the district of [Nadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_district), in the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency). His schooling began at the age of five, near his home. When his family moved to Goabagan, he was admitted into the New Indian School. In his final year of school, he was admitted into the [Hindu School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_School). He passed his entrance examination ([matriculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation)) in 1909 and stood fifth in the order of merit. He then joined the intermediate science course at the [Presidency College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata), [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), where his teachers included [Jagadish Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagadish_Chandra_Bose), [Sarada Prasanna Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarada_Prasanna_Das), and [Prafulla Chandra Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prafulla_Chandra_Ray).

Bose received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [mixed mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_mathematics) from [Presidency College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_University,_Kolkata), standing first in 1913. Then he joined [Sir Ashutosh Mukherjee's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashutosh_Mukherjee) newly formed [Science College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajabazar_Science_College) where he again stood first in the [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (MSc) mixed mathematics exam in 1915. His marks in the MSc examination created a new record in the annals of the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta), which is yet to be surpassed.[^o15]

After completing his MSc, Bose joined the [Science College, Calcutta University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajabazar_Science_College) as a research scholar in 1916 and started his studies in the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001). It was an exciting era in the history of scientific progress. [Quantum theory](wikipedia://en/1188104) had just appeared on the horizon and significant results had started pouring in.[^o15]

His father, Surendranath Bose, worked in the Engineering Department of the [East Indian Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Indian_Railway_Company). In 1914, at age 20, Satyendra Nath Bose married Ushabati Ghosh,[^o4][^n3] the 11-year-old daughter of a prominent Calcutta physician.[^o17] They had nine children, two of whom died in early childhood. When he died in 1974, he left behind his wife, two sons, and five daughters.[^o15]

As a [polyglot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyglotism), Bose was well versed in several languages such as [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), English, French, German and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) as well as the poetry of [Lord Tennyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson), [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore) and [Kalidasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kālidāsa). In Europe, he impressed his host [Jacqueline Zadoc-Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Zadoc-Kahn_Eisenmann) with his knowledge of [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew) in literature and religion.[^n4] He could play the *[esraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esraj)*, an Indian instrument similar to a violin.[^o19] He was actively involved in running night schools that came to be known as the Working Men's Institute.[^o22][^n5]

## Research career {#Research_career}

Bose attended [Hindu School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_School) in [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta), and later [attended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kolkata_Presidencians) [Presidency College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_College,_Kolkata), also in Calcutta, earning the highest marks at each institution, while fellow student and future astrophysicist [Meghnad Saha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghnad_Saha) came second.[^o22] He came in contact with teachers such as [Jagadish Chandra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagadish_Chandra_Bose), Prafulla Chandra Ray and Naman Sharma who provided inspiration to aim high in life. From 1916 to 1921, he was a lecturer in the [physics department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_department) of the [Rajabazar Science College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajabazar_Science_College) under [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta). Along with Saha, Bose prepared the first book in English based on German and French translations of original papers on Einstein's special and general relativity in 1919.

In 1921, Satyendra Nath Bose joined as [Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in the Department of Physics of the recently founded [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka) (in present-day Bangladesh).[^o23] Bose set up whole new departments, including laboratories, to teach advanced courses for MSc and BSc honours and taught [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952) as well as [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696)'s [theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dynamical_Theory_of_the_Electromagnetic_Field) of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism).[^n6]

Bose, along with Indian Astrophysicist [Meghnad Saha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghnad_Saha), presented several papers in theoretical physics and pure mathematics from 1918 onwards. In 1924, whilst a Reader in the Physics Department of the [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka), Bose wrote a paper deriving [Planck's quantum radiation law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck's_law) without any reference to [classical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics) by using a novel way of counting states with [identical particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identical_particles). This paper was seminal in creating the important field of [quantum statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_statistics).[^o25] Though not accepted at once for publication, he sent the article directly to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) in Germany. Einstein, recognising the importance of the paper, translated it into German himself and submitted it on Bose's behalf to the *[Zeitschrift für Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_für_Physik)*. As a result of this recognition, Bose was able to work for two years in European [X-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray) and [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) laboratories, during which he worked with [Louis de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Broglie), [Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie), and Einstein.[^o22][^o26][^o27][^n7]

### Bose–Einstein statistics {#Bose–Einstein_statistics}

While presenting a lecture[^o30] at the [University of Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dhaka) on the theory of [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation) and the [ultraviolet catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_catastrophe), Bose intended to show his students that the contemporary theory was inadequate, because it predicted results not in accordance with experimental results.

In the process of describing this discrepancy, Bose for the first time took the position that the [Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution](wikipedia://en/19830) would not be true for microscopic particles, where fluctuations due to [Heisenberg's uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg's_uncertainty_principle) will be significant. Thus he stressed the probability of finding particles in the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101), each state having volume $h^{3}$, and discarding the distinct position and [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431) of the particles. Bose's key departure was to treat light quanta as particles in a one-particle [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) divided into elementary cells of volume $h^{3}$. His probability law for distributing the quanta among those cells implied a new kind of statistical dependence between light quanta, a feature often described as their indistinguishability, and yielded [Planck's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck's_law) without recourse to classical electrodynamics.[^o31]

Bose adapted this lecture into a short article called "Planck's Law and the Hypothesis of Light Quanta" and sent it to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) with the following letter:[^o32] Respected Sir, I have ventured to send you the accompanying article for your perusal and opinion. I am anxious to know what you think of it. You will see that I have tried to deduce the coefficient $8\pi \nu^{2}/c^{3}$ in Planck's Law independent of classical electrodynamics, only assuming that the ultimate elementary region in the phase-space has the content $h^{3}$. I do not know sufficient German to translate the paper. If you think the paper worth publication I shall be grateful if you arrange for its publication in *Zeitschrift für Physik*. Though a complete stranger to you, I do not feel any hesitation in making such a request. Because we are all your pupils though profiting only by your teachings through your writings. I do not know whether you still remember that somebody from Calcutta asked your permission to translate your papers on Relativity in English. You acceded to the request. The book has since been published. I was the one who translated your paper on Generalised Relativity.

Einstein agreed with him, translated Bose's papers "Planck's Law and Hypothesis of Light Quanta" into German, and had it published in *[Zeitschrift für Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_für_Physik)* under Bose's name, in 1924.[^n8]

The reason Bose's interpretation produced accurate results was that since photons are indistinguishable from each other, one cannot treat any two photons having equal energy as being two distinct identifiable photons. By analogy if, in an alternate universe, coins were to behave like photons and other [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson), the probability of producing two heads would indeed be one-third (tail-head = head-tail).

Bose's interpretation is now called [Bose–Einstein statistics](wikipedia://en/188935). This result derived by Bose laid the foundation of [quantum statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_statistics), and especially the revolutionary new philosophical conception of the indistinguishability of particles, as acknowledged by Einstein and Dirac.[^n8] When Einstein met Bose face-to-face, he asked him whether he had been aware that he had invented a new type of statistics, and he very candidly said that no, he wasn't that familiar with [Boltzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann)'s statistics and didn't realize that he was doing the calculations differently.

#### Bose–Einstein condensate {#Bose–Einstein_condensate}

Scientists

*Image: Bose Einstein condensate.png.* Velocity-distribution data of a gas of [rubidium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubidium) atoms, confirming the discovery of a new phase of matter, [the Bose–Einstein condensate](wikipedia://en/4474).[^o36] Left: just before the appearance of a Bose–Einstein condensate. Center: just after the appearance of the condensate. Right: after further evaporation, leaving a sample of nearly pure condensate.

Einstein also did not at first realize how radical Bose's departure was, and in his first paper after Bose, he was guided, like Bose, by the fact that the new method gave the right answer. But after Einstein's second paper using Bose's method in which Einstein predicted the Bose-Einstein condensate (*pictured left*), he started to realize just how radical it was, and he compared it to wave/particle duality, saying that some particles didn't behave exactly like particles. Bose had already submitted his article to the British Journal *Philosophical Magazine*, which rejected it before he sent it to Einstein. It is not known why it was rejected.[^o37]

Einstein adopted the idea and extended it to atoms. This led to the prediction of the existence of phenomena which became known as [Bose–Einstein condensate](wikipedia://en/4474), a dense collection of [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) (which are particles with integer [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829), named after Bose), which was demonstrated to exist by experiment in 1995.

### Dhaka {#Dhaka}

*Image: Satyendra-nath-bose.jpg.* Bose at Dhaka University in the 1930s

After his stay in Europe, Bose returned to [Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka) in 1926. He did not have a doctorate, and so ordinarily, under the prevailing regulations, he would not be qualified for the post of Professor he applied for, but [Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein) recommended him. He was then made [Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chair_%28official%29) of the Department of [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at [Dhaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka_University). He continued guiding and teaching at Dhaka University and was the [Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of the Faculty of Science there until 1945.

Bose designed equipment himself for an [X-ray crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_crystallography) laboratory. He set up laboratories and libraries to make the department a center of research in X-ray spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, magnetic properties of matter, optical spectroscopy, wireless, and [unified field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory). He also published an [equation of state](wikipedia://en/9908) for [real gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_gas) with [Meghnad Saha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meghnad_Saha).

### Calcutta {#Calcutta}

When the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) became imminent (1947), he returned to [Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta) (now known as Kolkata) and taught there until 1956. He insisted every student design their own equipment using local materials and local technicians. He was made [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) on his retirement.[^o26][^n9][^o22] He then became Vice-Chancellor of [Visva-Bharati University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visva-Bharati_University) in [Santiniketan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiniketan). He returned to the University of Calcutta to continue research in nuclear physics and complete earlier works in organic chemistry. In subsequent years, he worked in applied research such as extraction of [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium) in hot springs of [Bakreshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakreshwar).[^n10]

### Other fields {#Other_fields}

Apart from physics, he did research in [biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotechnology) and literature ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_literature) and English). He made studies in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), geology, [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology), [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology), engineering and other sciences. Being [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_people), he devoted significant time to promoting [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) as a teaching language, translating scientific papers into it, and promoting the development of the region.[^o27][^n11][^o44]

## Honours {#Honours}

*Image: M N Saha, J C Bose, J C Ghosh, Snehamoy Dutt, S N Bose, D M Bose, N R Sen, J N Mukherjee, N C Nag.jpg.* Bose with other scientists at the University of Calcutta

*Image: Bust of Satyendra Nath Bose at BITM 13 July 14 006.jpg.* Bust of Satyendra Nath Bose in the garden of [Birla Industrial & Technological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birla_Industrial_&_Technological_Museum)

In 1937, [Rabindranath Tagore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabindranath_Tagore) dedicated his only book on science, *Visva–Parichay*, to Satyendra Nath Bose. Bose was honoured with the title [Padma Vibhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Vibhushan) by the Indian Government in 1954. In 1959, he was appointed as the National Professor, the highest honour in the country for a scholar, a position he held for 15 years. In 1986, the [S.N. Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.N._Bose_National_Centre_for_Basic_Sciences) was established by an act of Parliament, Government of India, in Salt Lake, Calcutta.[^n12][^o47]

Bose became an adviser to the then newly formed [Council of Scientific and Industrial Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Scientific_and_Industrial_Research). He was the president of the [Indian Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Physical_Society) and the National Institute of Science. He was elected general president of the [Indian Science Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Science_Congress). He was the vice president and then the president of [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418). In 1958, he became a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society). He was nominated as [member of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament,_Rajya_Sabha) [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha).

[Partha Ghose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partha_Ghose) has stated that[^o22] Bose's work stood at the transition between the '[old quantum theory](wikipedia://en/1188104)' of Planck, Bohr and Einstein and the new quantum mechanics of [Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger), [Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg), [Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born), [Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) and others.

### Nobel Prize nomination {#Nobel_Prize_nomination}

Bose was nominated by [K. Banerjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedareswar_Banerjee) (1956), [D.S. Kothari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Singh_Kothari) (1959), S.N. Bagchi (1962), and A.K. Dutta (1962) for the Nobel Prize in Physics, for his contribution to [Bose–Einstein statistics](wikipedia://en/188935) and the [unified field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory). Banerjee, head of the Physics Department, [University of Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Allahabad), in a letter of 12 January 1956 wrote to the [Nobel Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Committee) as follows: "(1). He (Bose) made very outstanding contributions to physics by developing the statistics known after his name as Bose statistics. In recent years this statistics is found to be of profound importance in the classifications of [fundamental particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_particles) and has contributed immensely to the development of [nuclear physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_physics). (2). During the period from 1953 to date, he has made a number of highly interesting contributions of far-reaching consequences on the subject of Einstein's [Unitary Field Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_field_theory)." Bose's work was evaluated by an expert of the Nobel Committee, [Oskar Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Klein), who deemed his work not worthy of the Nobel Prize.[^o49][^o50][^o51]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

*Image: Satyendranath Bose 1994 stamp of India.jpg.* Bose on a 1994 stamp of India

[Bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosons), a class of elementary [subatomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle) particles in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) were named by Dirac after Satyendra Nath Bose to commemorate his contributions to science.[^o53][^o54]

Soviet Nobel laureate [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau) kept a list of names of physicists which he ranked on up to 5 on a logarithmic scale of productivity with lower numbers being more productive. Albert Einstein was ranked 0.5. Landau awarded a rank of 1 to Bose along with the founding fathers of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), [Niels Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Bohr), [Werner Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Heisenberg), [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742) and [Erwin Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Schrödinger), and others. Landau ranked himself as a 2.5 but later promoted to a 2.[^o55][^o56]

Although seven Nobel Prizes were awarded for research related to S N Bose's concepts of the [boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson), [Bose–Einstein statistics](wikipedia://en/188935) and [Bose–Einstein condensate](wikipedia://en/4474), Bose himself was not awarded the Nobel Prize.

In his book *The Scientific Edge*, physicist [Jayant Narlikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayant_Narlikar) observed: SN Bose's work on particle statistics (c. 1922), which clarified the behaviour of [photons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) (the [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle) of light in an enclosure) and opened the door to new ideas on statistics of Microsystems that obey the rules of quantum theory, was one of the top ten achievements of 20th century Indian science and could be considered in the Nobel Prize class.[^o57]

When Bose himself was once asked that question, he replied, "I have got all the recognition I deserve."[^o58]

One of the main academic buildings of [University of Rajshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rajshahi), the No 1 science building has been named after him.

The 4 June 2022 [Google Doodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Doodle) featured Bose, on the 98th anniversary of his sending his work to Einstein.[^o59][^o60][^o61]
