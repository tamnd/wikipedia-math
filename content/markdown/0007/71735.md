# Hugo Steinhaus

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hugo Steinhaus"}

**Hugo Dyonizy Steinhaus** ( , ; 14 January 1887 – 25 February 1972) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [educator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher). Steinhaus obtained his PhD under [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) at [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University) in 1911 and later became a professor at the [Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University#Jan_Kazimierz_University_%281919–39%29) in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) (now [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)), where he helped establish what later became known as the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328). He is credited with "discovering" mathematician [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), with whom he gave a notable contribution to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) through the [Banach–Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Steinhaus_theorem). After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) Steinhaus played an important part in the establishment of the mathematics department at [Wrocław University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University) and in the revival of Polish mathematics from the destruction of the war.

:::

Author of around 170 scientific articles and books, Steinhaus has left his legacy and contribution in many branches of mathematics, such as functional analysis, [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), and [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261). Notably he is regarded as one of the early founders of [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), which led to later development of more comprehensive approaches by other scholars.

## Early life and studies {#Early_life_and_studies}

Steinhaus was born on January 14, 1887, in [Jasło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasło),[^o16] [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) to a family with Jewish roots. His father, Bogusław, was a local industrialist, owner of a brick factory and a merchant. His mother was Ewelina, née Lipschitz. Hugo's uncle, Ignacy Steinhaus, was a lawyer and an activist in the *Koło Polskie* (Polish Circle), and a deputy to the [Galician Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Galicia), the regional [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly) of the [Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria).[^o22]

*Image: Polish students in Gottingen.jpg.* Steinhaus, standing rightmost, in 1907 in Göttingen

Hugo finished his studies at the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in Jasło in 1905. His family wanted him to become an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) but he was drawn to [abstract mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) and began to study the works of famous contemporary mathematicians on his own. In the same year he began studying philosophy and mathematics at the [University of Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University#Under_Austrian_rule).[^o22] In 1906 he transferred to [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o16] At that University he received his Ph.D. in 1911, having written his doctoral dissertation under the supervision of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). The title of his thesis was *Neue Anwendungen des Dirichlet'schen Prinzips* ("New applications to [Dirichlet's principle](wikipedia://en/1240011)").[^o23]

At the start of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) Steinhaus returned to Poland and served in [Józef Piłsudski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Piłsudski)'s [Polish Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Legions_in_World_War_I), after which he lived in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków).[^o25]

He was an atheist.[^o4]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

### Interwar Poland {#Interwar_Poland}

During the 1916-1917 period and before Poland had regained its full [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Independence_Day), which occurred in 1918, Steinhaus worked in Kraków for the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_and_Administration_of_the_Republic_of_Poland) in the ephemeral puppet state of [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_%281916–1918%29).[^o31]

In 1917 he started to work at the [University of Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University#Under_Austrian_rule) (later *[Jan Kazimierz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University#Jan_Kazimierz_University_%281919–39%29)* in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)) and acquired his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) qualification in 1920.[^o31] In 1921 he became a *profesor nadzwyczajny* ([associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor)) and in 1925 *profesor zwyczajny* (full professor) at the same university.[^o31] During this time he taught a course on the then cutting edge theory of [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration), one of the first such courses offered outside of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France).[^o23]

While in Lwów, Steinhaus co-founded the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328)[^o33] and was active in the circle of mathematicians associated with the [Scottish cafe](wikipedia://en/362651), although, according to [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam), for the circle's gatherings, Steinhaus would have generally preferred a more upscale tea shop down the street.[^o25]

### World War II {#World_War_II}

*Image: KsiegaSzkocka1.JPG.* The [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115) from the [Lwów School of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1024328), which Steinhaus contributed to and probably saved during World War II

In September 1939, after [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland) both invaded and occupied Poland, as a fulfillment of the [Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov–Ribbentrop_Pact) they had signed earlier, Lwów initially came under Soviet occupation. Steinhaus considered escaping to [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) but ultimately decided to remain in Lwów. The Soviets reorganized the university to give it a more [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians) character, but they did appoint [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) (Steinhaus's student) as the dean of the mathematics department, and Steinhaus resumed teaching there. The faculty of the department at the school were also strengthened by several Polish refugees from German-occupied Poland. According to Steinhaus, during the experience of this period, he "acquired an insurmountable physical disgust in regard to all sorts of Soviet administrators, politicians and commissars."A

During the interwar period and the time of the Soviet occupation, Steinhaus contributed ten problems to the famous [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115), including the [last one](wikipedia://en/9759038), recorded shortly before Lwów was captured by the Nazis in 1941, during [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa).[^o25]

Steinhaus, because of his Jewish background, spent the [Nazi occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_occupation_of_Poland) in hiding, first among friends in Lwów, then in the small towns of [Osiczyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiczyna), near [Zamość](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamość), and [Berdechów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdechów), near Kraków.[^o33][^o24] The [Polish anti-Nazi resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_anti-Nazi_resistance) provided him with false documents of a forest ranger who had died sometime earlier, by the name of Grzegorz Krochmalny. Under this name he taught clandestine classes (higher education was [forbidden for Poles under the German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Poland_during_World_War_II)). Worried about the possibility of imminent death if captured by Germans, Steinhaus, without access to any scholarly material, reconstructed from memory and recorded all the mathematics he knew, in addition to writing other voluminous memoirs, of which only a little part has been published.[^o24]

Also while in hiding, and cut off from reliable news on the course of the war, Steinhaus devised a statistical means of estimating for himself the German casualties at the front based on sporadic obituaries published in the local press. The method relied on the relative frequency with which the obituaries stated that the soldier who died was someone's son, someone's "second son", someone's "third son" and so on.[^o24]

According to his student and biographer, [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425), Steinhaus told him that the happiest day of his life were the twenty-four hours between the time that the Germans had left occupied Poland and the Soviets had not yet arrived ("They had left, and they had not yet come").[^o24]

### After World War II {#After_World_War_II}

*Image: Steinhaus-tablica.JPG.* Commemorative plaque, [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), Poland

In the last days of World War II Steinhaus, still in hiding, heard a rumor that University of Lwów was to be transferred to the city of [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau) ([Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław)), which Poland was to acquire as a result of the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement) (Lwów became part of Soviet Ukraine). Although initially he had doubts, he turned down offers for faculty positions in [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź) and [Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin) and made his way to the city where he began teaching at [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław#The_University_of_Wrocław).[^o33] While there, he revived the idea behind the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115) from Lwów, where prominent and aspiring mathematicians would write down problems of interest along with prizes to be awarded for their solution, by starting the *[New Scottish Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Scottish_Book)*. It was also most likely Steinhaus preserved the original *Scottish Book* from Lwów throughout the war and subsequently sent it to Stanisław Ulam, who translated it into English.[^o25]

With Steinhaus' help, Wrocław University became renowned for mathematics, much as the University of Lwów had been.[^o24]

Later, in the 1960s, Steinhaus served as a visiting professor at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) (1961–62)[^o25] and the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex) (1966).[^o32]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Steinhaus authored over 170 works.[^o25] Unlike his student, Stefan Banach, who tended to specialize narrowly in the field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), Steinhaus made contributions to a wide range of mathematical sub-disciplines, including [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), functional analysis, theory of [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/4099125) and [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) as well as [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^o23][^o25] He also wrote in the area of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and enthusiastically collaborated with [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineers), [geologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologists), [economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economists), [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians), [biologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologists) and, in Kac's words, "even [lawyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyers)".[^o24]

Probably his most notable contribution to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) was the 1927 proof of the [Banach–Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Steinhaus_theorem), given along with Stefan Banach, which is now one of the fundamental tools in this branch of mathematics.

His interest in [games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game) led him to propose an early formal definition of a [strategy](wikipedia://en/1434441), anticipating [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942)'s more complete treatment of a few years later. Consequently, he is considered an early founder of modern [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924).[^o31] As a result of his work on infinite games Steinhaus, together with another of his students, [Jan Mycielski](wikipedia://en/26510464), proposed the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300).[^o24]

Steinhaus was also an early contributor to, and co-founder of, probability theory, which at the time was in its infancy and not even considered an actual part of mathematics.[^o24] He provided the first [axiomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_%28disambiguation%29) [measure-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) description of [coin-tossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin_toss), which was to influence the full axiomatization of probability by the Russian mathematician [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) a decade later.[^o24] Steinhaus was also the first to offer precise definitions of what it means for two events to be "[independent](wikipedia://en/27593)", as well as for what it means for a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) to be "[uniformly distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29)".[^o25]

While in hiding during World War II, Steinhaus worked on the fair cake-cutting problem: how to divide a heterogeneous resource among several people with different preferences such that every person believes he received a [proportional](wikipedia://en/2260894) share. Steinhaus' work has initiated the modern research of the [fair cake-cutting](wikipedia://en/43212478) problem.B

Steinhaus was also the first person to conjecture the [ham-sandwich theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham-sandwich_theorem),[^o24][^o20] and one of the first to propose the method of [*k*-means clustering](wikipedia://en/1860407).[^o21]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

Steinhaus is said to have "discovered" the Polish mathematician Stefan Banach in 1916, after he overheard someone utter the words "[Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288)" while in a Kraków park (Steinhaus referred to Banach as his "greatest mathematical discovery").[^o18] Together with Banach and the other participant of the park discussion, [Otto Nikodym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nikodym), Steinhaus started the *Mathematical Society of Kraków*, which later evolved into the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o25] He was a member of *PAU* (the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning)) and *PAN* (the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences)), *PTM* (the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791)), the *Wrocławskie Towarzystwo Naukowe* ([Wrocław Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_Scientific_Society)) as well as many international scientific societies and science academies.[^o31]

Steinhaus also published one of the first articles in *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*, in 1921.[^o19] He also co-founded *[Studia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studia_Mathematica)* along with [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) (1929),[^o33] and *Zastosowania matematyki* (Applications of Mathematics, 1953), *Colloquium Mathematicum*, and *Monografie Matematyczne* (Mathematical Monographs).[^o16]

He received [honorary doctorate degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate#Honorary_doctorates) from [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University) (1958), [Wrocław Medical Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_Medical_Academy) (1961), [Poznań University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań_University) (1963) and Wrocław University (1965).[^o29]

Steinhaus had full command of several foreign languages and was known for his [aphorisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphorism), to the point that a booklet of his most famous ones in [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) has been published posthumously.[^o24]

In 2002, the Polish Academy of Sciences and Wrocław University sponsored "2002, The Year of Hugo Steinhaus", to celebrate his contributions to Polish and world science.[^o27]

Mathematician [Mark Kac](wikipedia://en/1062425), Steinhaus's student, wrote:

"He was one of the architects of the school of mathematics which flowered miraculously in Poland between the two wars and it was he who, perhaps more than any other individual, helped to raise Polish mathematics from the ashes to which it had been reduced by the second World War to the position of new strength and respect which it now occupies. He was a man of great culture and in the best sense of the word a product of Western civilization."[^o23]

## Chief works {#Chief_works}

- *Czym jest, a czym nie jest matematyka* (What Mathematics Is, and What It Is Not, 1923).[^o29]
- *Sur le principe de la condensation de la singularités* (with Banach, 1927)[^o23]
- *Theorie der Orthogonalreihen* (with [Stefan Kaczmarz](wikipedia://en/1062394), 1935).[^o23][^o26]
- *Kalejdoskop matematyczny* (Mathematical Snapshots, 1939).[^o23][^o29]
- *Taksonomia wrocławska* (A Wroclaw Taxonomy; with others, 1951).
- *Sur la liaison et la division des points d'un ensemble fini* (On uniting and separating the points of a finite set, with others, 1951).[^o30] One of multiple rediscoveries of [Borůvka's algorithm](wikipedia://en/197253).
- *Sto zadań* (One Hundred Problems In Elementary Mathematics, 1964).[^o25][^o17]
- *Orzeł czy reszka* (Heads or Tails, 1961).[^o34]
- *Słownik racjonalny* (A Rational Dictionary, 1980).[^o28]
