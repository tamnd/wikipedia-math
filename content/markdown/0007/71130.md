# Sum of Logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sum of Logic"}

The ***Summa Logicae*** ("Sum of Logic") is a textbook on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) by [William of Ockham](wikipedia://en/33617). It was written around 1323.

Systematically, it resembles other works of medieval logic, organised under the basic headings of the [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism) [Predicables](wikipedia://en/1180739), [Categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_being), [terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminology), [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094), and [syllogisms](wikipedia://en/48046). These headings, though often given in a different order, represent the basic arrangement of scholastic works on logic.

:::

This work is important in that it contains the main account of Ockham's [nominalism](wikipedia://en/21176), a position related to the [problem of universals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_universals).

## Book I. On Terms {#Book_I._On_Terms}

Chapters 1–17 deal with terms: what they are, and how they are divide into categorematic, abstract and concrete, absolute and connotative, *first intention*, and *second intention*. Ockham also introduces the issue of universals here.; Chapters 18–25 deal with the five predicables of [Porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_%28philosopher%29).; Chapters 26–62 deal with the [Categories](wikipedia://en/1692652) of Aristotle, known to the medieval philosophers as the *Praedicamenta* in the Latin translation of [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684). The first chapters of this section concern definition and description, the notions of subject and predicate, the meaning of terms like *whole*, *being* and so on. The later chapters deal with the ten Categories themselves, as follows: Substance (42–43), Quantity (44–49), Relation (50–54), Quality (55–56), Action (57), Passion (58), Time (59), Place (60), Position (61), Habit (62).; Chapters 63–77 onwards deal with the [theory of supposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_supposition).

## Book III. On Syllogisms {#Book_III._On_Syllogisms}

### Part I. On Syllogisms {#Part_I._On_Syllogisms}

On categorical syllogisms (1–19); On modal syllogisms (20–30); On mixed syllogisms (31–64); On syllogisms containing exponible propositions

### Part II. On Demonstration {#Part_II._On_Demonstration}

- These 41 chapters are a systematic exposition of Aristotle's [Posterior Analytics](wikipedia://en/1692809).

### Part III. On Consequences {#Part_III._On_Consequences}

- The first 37 chapters of Part II are a systematic exposition of Aristotle's [Topics](wikipedia://en/1692819). In Part III, Ockham deals with the definition and division of consequences, and provides a treatment of Aristotle's Topical rules.[^o9] According to Ockham a consequence is a [conditional proposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentence), composed of two categorical propositions by the terms 'if' and 'then'. For example, 'if a man runs, then God exists' (*Si homo currit, Deus est*).[^o10] A consequence is 'true' when the [antecedent](wikipedia://en/804755) implies the [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778). Ockham distinguishes between 'material' and 'formal' consequences, which are roughly equivalent to the modern [material implication](wikipedia://en/658808) and [logical implication](wikipedia://en/37531624) respectively. Similar accounts are given by [Jean Buridan](wikipedia://en/172466) and [Albert of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_of_Saxony_%28philosopher%29).
- Chapters 38 to 45 deal with the [Theory of obligationes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_obligationes).
- Chapter 46 deals with the [Liar Paradox](wikipedia://en/17967)

### Part IV. On Fallacies (in 18 chapters) {#Part_IV._On_Fallacies_(in_18_chapters)}

Part IV, in eighteen chapters, deals with the different species of fallacy enumerated by Aristotle in [Sophistical Refutations](wikipedia://en/1692779) (*De sophisticis elenchis*).

- Chapters 2-4 deal with the three modes of [equivocation](wikipedia://en/17690).
- Chapters 5-7 deal with the three types of [amphiboly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibology).
- Chapter 8 deals with the fallacies of [composition](wikipedia://en/523043), and [division](wikipedia://en/522936).
- Chapter 9 deals with the [fallacy of accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy_of_accent).
- Chapter 10 deals with the fallacy of 'figure of speech'.
- Chapter 11 deals with the [fallacy of accident](wikipedia://en/735233).
- Chapter 12 deals with the fallacy of [affirming the consequent](wikipedia://en/675).
- Chapter 13 deals with *[secundum quid et simpliciter](wikipedia://en/735213)*.
- Chapter 14 deals with [Ignoratio elenchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignoratio_elenchi) or irrelevant thesis.
- Chapter 15 deals with [begging the question](wikipedia://en/43582) (*petitio principii*).
- Chapter 16 deals with [false cause](wikipedia://en/235588) (*non-causam ut causam*)
- Chapter 17 deals with the [fallacy of many questions](wikipedia://en/700051) (*plures interrogationes ut unam facere*)>
- Ockham ends (chapter 18) by showing how all these fallacies err against the syllogism.
