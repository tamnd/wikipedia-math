# Hannah Cairo

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hannah Cairo"}

**Hannah Mira Cairo** (born 2007) is an American mathematician who gained recognition at age 17 for disproving the longstanding [Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture](wikipedia://en/80611405) in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Cairo was born in [Nassau, Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau,_Bahamas).[^o4] She is [transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender).[^o5] She began studying mathematics using online lessons from [Khan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Academy), and finished calculus by age 11.[^o13] During the COVID-19 pandemic, she began participating in the Berkeley [Math Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_Circle) (BMC) remotely.[^o6] After moving to the United States, she continued to attend university-level mathematics lectures at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), including courses taught by mathematician [Ruixiang Zhang](wikipedia://en/74949633).[^o4]

In fall 2025, having skipped the standard bachelor's and master's degrees, began her PhD studies at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland),[^o6] focusing on Fourier restriction theory.[^o5]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

While studying under Zhang, Cairo began working on the [Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture](wikipedia://en/80611405), which had remained unresolved since the 1980s.[^o8] Initially aiming to prove the conjecture, she instead constructed a counterexample that disproved it.[^o6] Her work involved using fractals and other tools and originally resulted in a more complex counterexample before finding a simpler example after reformulating the problem in frequency space.[^o9]

Her findings were published in the preprint titled "A Counterexample to the Mizohata–Takeuchi Conjecture" and uploaded to the arXiv preprint server on February 10, 2025.[^o10] Later that year, she presented her work at the 12th International Congress on Harmonic Analysis and Partial Differential Equations in [El Escorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Escorial,_Madrid), Spain.[^o6] Media described her as one of the youngest mathematicians to resolve a major open problem.[^o4][^o6][^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14]
