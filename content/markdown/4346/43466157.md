# Carolyn Eisele

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Carolyn Eisele"}

**Carolyn Eisele** (June 13, 1902 – January 15, 2000) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) known as an expert on the works of [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Eisele was born on June 13, 1902, in [The Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City).[^o5] She studied at [Hunter College High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College_High_School) and then [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College), graduating [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) in 1923.[^o4][^o5] She earned a master's degree in mathematics and education from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1925.[^o4][^o5] At that time, Columbia did not offer Ph.D.s in mathematics to women, but Eisele continued her graduate studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (where she studied [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)) and the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) before returning home to New York, without a doctorate, to care for her heavily injured father.[^o5] Her studies also included [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera) singing, with Jeanne Fourestier in Paris in 1931 and later with Los Angeles-based voice coach Morris Halpern, whom she married in 1943.[^o5]

Eisele taught mathematics at [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) for nearly 50 years.[^o5][^o6] She began teaching as an instructor there after her college graduation in 1923,[^o3][^o4][^o5] eventually reached the rank of full professor in 1965,[^o3][^o4] and retired in 1972.[^o5][^o4]

Eisele died on January 15, 2000[^o8] in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan), New York City.[^o5]

## Peirce studies {#Peirce_studies}

As a student at Columbia University, Eisele took a course in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) from [David Eugene Smith](wikipedia://en/3711980), but her professional contributions to the subject began in 1947, when she took a sabbatical to prepare for a course in the history of mathematics that she had been asked to teach at Hunter College.[^o5] While working in the [George Arthur Plimpton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Arthur_Plimpton) collection at the Columbia University library, she found a manuscript by [Charles Sanders Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) on [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949)'s *[Liber Abaci](wikipedia://en/167699)* and in 1951 she published a paper about her discovery in *[Scripta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/13550249)*.[^o4][^o5] Other early works of Eisele on Peirce included his correspondence with [Simon Newcomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Newcomb) and his [Peirce quincuncial projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peirce_quincuncial_projection) for maps of the world.[^o5] Her work on Peirce took a holistic view, in which his contributions to philosophy and logic were treated as part of a whole together with his contributions to mathematics and science, rather than as separate and unrelated chapters in his life.[^o3]

Eisele served as the president of the Charles S. Peirce Society from 1973 to 1975.[^o5][^o6] In 1976, Eisele began publishing a multi-volume collection of Peirce's writings that she had edited, the *New Elements of Mathematics*, and in the same year she helped organize the Peirce Bicentennial International Congress in Amsterdam.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Eisele was named a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1960.[^o12]

On her retirement from Hunter College in 1972, Eisele joined the Hunter Hall of Fame.[^o5] She was given honorary doctorates from [Texas Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tech_University) in 1980 and from [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University) in 1982.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

In 1980, Eisele became an honorary member of the crew of the [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard) ship *USC&GSS Peirce*.[^o5] In 1981, a symposium on Peirce studies was held in her honor at Hunter College, the proceedings of which were published as a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) in *[Historia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/10841658)*.[^o3][^o5] In 1985, Eisele was given the Behavioral Sciences, History and Philosophy of Sciences award from the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o13] Her other honors include a Doctor of Humanities degree awarded by [Texas Tech University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Tech_University) in 1980 and a Doctor of Science degree awarded in 1982 by [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University).[^o14]
