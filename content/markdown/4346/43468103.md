# Chamfer (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chamfer"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **chamfer** or **edge-truncation** is a topological operator that modifies one [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) into another. It separates the [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) by reducing them, and adds a new face between each two adjacent faces (moving the vertices inward). Oppositely, similar to [expansion](wikipedia://en/6341070), it moves the faces apart outward, and adds a new face between each two adjacent faces; but contrary to expansion, it maintains the original [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

:::

For a polyhedron, this operation adds a new [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) face in place of each original [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034).

In [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705), *chamfering* is represented by the letter "c". A polyhedron with $e$ edges will have a chamfered form containing $2e$ new vertices, $3e$ new edges, and $e$ new hexagonal faces.

## Platonic solids {#Platonic_solids}

Chamfers of five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) are described in detail below.

*Image: EB1911 Crystallography Figs. 30 & 31.jpg.* Historical drawings of truncated tetrahedron and slightly chamfered tetrahedron.[^n1]

- chamfered tetrahedron or alternated truncated cube: from a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521), this replaces its six edges with [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) flattened hexagons; or [alternately truncating](wikipedia://en/4349117) a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), replacing four of its eight vertices with congruent equilateral-triangle faces. This is an example of [Goldberg polyhedron](wikipedia://en/39781140) GP<sub>III</sub>(2,0) or {3+,3}<sub>2,0</sub>, containing triangular and hexagonal faces. Its dual is the alternate-triakis tetratetrahedron.[^n2]
- chamfered cube: from a cube, the resulting polyhedron has twelve hexagonal and six square [centrally symmetric faces](wikipedia://en/55312103), a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402).[^n3] This is also an example of the Goldberg polyhedron GP<sub>IV</sub>(2,0) or {4+,3}<sub>2,0</sub>. Its dual is the [tetrakis cuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrakis_cuboctahedron). A twisty puzzle of the DaYan Gem 7 is the shape of a chamfered cube.[^o6]
- chamfered octahedron or tritruncated rhombic dodecahedron: from a [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458) by chamfering,[^n4] or by [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) the eight order-3 vertices of the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), which become congruent equilateral triangles, and the original twelve [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884) faces become [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) flattened hexagons. It is a Goldberg polyhedron GP<sub>V</sub>(2,0) or {5+,3}<sub>2,0</sub>. Its dual is triakis cuboctahedron.[^n2]
- [chamfered dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4472133): by chamfering a [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), the resulting polyhedron has 80 vertices, 120 edges, and 42 faces: 12 congruent [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) pentagons and 30 [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) flattened hexagons. GP<sub>V</sub>(2,0) = {5+,3}<sub>2,0</sub>. The structure resembles C<sub>60</sub> [fullerene](wikipedia://en/10628).[^n5] Its dual is the pentakis icosidodecahedron.[^n2]
- chamfered icosahedron or tritruncated rhombic triacontahedron: by chamfering a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), or [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) the twenty order-3 vertices of the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025). The hexagonal faces of the cI can be made [equilateral](wikipedia://en/214547), but not [regular](wikipedia://en/333306), with a certain depth of truncation. Its dual is the triakis icosidodecahedron.[^n2]

## Relation to Goldberg polyhedra {#Relation_to_Goldberg_polyhedra}

The chamfer operation applied in series creates progressively larger polyhedra with new faces, hexagonal, replacing the edges of the current one. The chamfer operator transforms GP(m,n) to GP(2m,2n).

A regular polyhedron, GP(1,0), creates a [Goldberg polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldberg_polyhedra) sequence: GP(1,0), GP(2,0), GP(4,0), GP(8,0), GP(16,0)...
