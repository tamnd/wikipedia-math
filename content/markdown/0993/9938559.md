# Series–parallel graph

*Image: Series parallel composition.svg.* Series and parallel composition operations for series–parallel graphs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Series–parallel graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **series–parallel graphs** are graphs with two distinguished vertices called *terminals*, formed recursively by two simple composition operations. They can be used to model [series and parallel electric circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_and_parallel_circuits).

:::

## Definition and terminology {#Definition_and_terminology}

In this context, the term graph means [multigraph](wikipedia://en/1774970).

There are several ways to define series–parallel graphs.

### First definition {#First_definition}

The following definition basically follows the one used by [David Eppstein](wikipedia://en/4015871).[^o4]

A **two-terminal graph** (**TTG**) is a graph with two distinguished vertices, $s$ and $t$, called *source* and *sink*, respectively.

The **parallel composition** $Pc = Pc(X, Y)$ of two TTGs $X$ and $Y$ is a TTG created from the [disjoint union of graphs](wikipedia://en/9938459) $X$ and $Y$ by [merging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_merging) the sources of $X$ and $Y$ to create the source of $Pc$ and merging the sinks of $X$ and $Y$ to create the sink of $Pc$.

The **series composition** $Sc = Sc(X, Y)$ of two TTGs $X$ and $Y$ is a TTG created from the disjoint union of graphs $X$ and $Y$ by merging the sink of $X$ with the source of $Y$. The source of $X$ becomes the source of $Sc$ and the sink of $Y$ becomes the sink of $Sc$.

A **two-terminal series–parallel graph** (**TTSPG**) is a graph that may be constructed by a sequence of series and parallel compositions starting from a set of copies of a single-edge graph $K_{2}$ with assigned terminals.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition 1"}

***Definition 1***. Finally, a graph is called **series–parallel** (**SP**), if it is a TTSPG when some two of its vertices are regarded as source and sink.

In a similar way one may define series–parallel [digraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digraph_%28mathematics%29), constructed from copies of single-arc graphs, with arcs directed from the source to the sink.

:::

### Second definition {#Second_definition}

The following definition specifies the same class of graphs.[^o7]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition 2"}

***Definition 2***. A graph is an SP-graph, if it may be turned into $K_{2}$ by a sequence of the following operations:

- Replacement of a pair of parallel edges with a single edge that connects their common endpoints
- Replacement of a pair of edges incident to a vertex of degree 2 other than $s$ or $t$ with a single edge.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every series–parallel graph has [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) at most 2 and [branchwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branchwidth) at most 2.[^o11] Indeed, a graph has treewidth at most 2 if and only if it has branchwidth at most 2, if and only if every [biconnected component](wikipedia://en/4842394) is a series–parallel graph.[^o12][^o13] The [maximal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) series–parallel graphs, graphs to which no additional edges can be added without destroying their series–parallel structure, are exactly the [2-trees](wikipedia://en/31104438).

2-connected series–parallel graphs are characterised by having no subgraph [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/353021) to $K_{4}$.[^o11]

Series parallel graphs may also be characterized by their [ear decompositions](wikipedia://en/32183016).[^o4]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

SP-graphs may be recognized in linear time[^o15] and their series–parallel decomposition may be constructed in linear time as well.

Besides being a model of certain types of electric networks, these graphs are of interest in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), because a number of standard graph problems are solvable in linear time on SP-graphs,[^o16] including finding of the [maximum matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_matching), [maximum independent set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_independent_set), [minimum dominating set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_dominating_set) and [Hamiltonian completion](wikipedia://en/9944425). Some of these problems are [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) for general graphs. The solution capitalizes on the fact that if the answers for one of these problems are known for two SP-graphs, then one can quickly find the answer for their series and parallel compositions.

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

The **generalized series–parallel graphs** (**GSP-graphs**) are an extension of the SP-graphs[^o19] with the same [algorithmic efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_efficiency) for the mentioned problems. The class of GSP-graphs include the classes of SP-graphs and [outerplanar graphs](wikipedia://en/352343).

GSP graphs may be specified by *Definition 2* augmented with the third operation of [deletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_deletion) of a dangling vertex (vertex of degree 1). Alternatively, *Definition 1* may be augmented with the following operation.

The **source merge** $S = M(X, Y)$ of two TTGs $X$ and $Y$ is a TTG created from the disjoint union of graphs $X$ and $Y$ by merging the source of $X$ with the source of $Y$. The source and sink of $X$ become the source and sink of $S$ respectively.

An [SPQR tree](wikipedia://en/11220797) is a tree structure that can be defined for an arbitrary [2-vertex-connected graph](wikipedia://en/7566175). It has S-nodes, which are analogous to the series composition operations in series–parallel graphs, P-nodes, which are analogous to the parallel composition operations in series–parallel graphs, and R-nodes, which do not correspond to series–parallel composition operations. A 2-connected graph is series–parallel if and only if there are no R-nodes in its SPQR tree.

:::
