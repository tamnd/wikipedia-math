# Disjoint union of graphs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Disjoint union of graphs"}

*Image: Equivalentie.svg.* A [cluster graph](wikipedia://en/50931136), the disjoint union of [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816)

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **disjoint union of graphs** is an operation that combines two or more [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) to form a larger graph. It is analogous to the [disjoint union of sets](wikipedia://en/188946) and is constructed by making the vertex set of the result be the disjoint union of the vertex sets of the given graphs and by making the edge set of the result be the disjoint union of the edge sets of the given graphs. Any disjoint union of two or more nonempty graphs is necessarily [disconnected](wikipedia://en/1614492).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

The disjoint union is also called the **graph sum** and may be represented either by a [plus sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plus_sign) or a circled plus sign: If $G$ and $H$ are two graphs, then $G+H$ or $G\oplus H$ denotes their disjoint union.[^o4]

:::

## Related graph classes {#Related_graph_classes}

Certain special classes of graphs may be represented using disjoint union operations. In particular:

- The [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29) are the disjoint unions of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560).[^o6]
- The [cluster graphs](wikipedia://en/50931136) are the disjoint unions of [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816).[^o7]
- The [2-regular graphs](wikipedia://en/85821) are the disjoint unions of [cycle graphs](wikipedia://en/878962).[^o8]

More generally, every graph is the disjoint union of [connected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), its [connected components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29).

The [cographs](wikipedia://en/1175666) are the graphs that can be constructed from single-vertex graphs by a combination of disjoint union and [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649) operations.[^o9]
