# P-adic valuation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="P-adic valuation"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **$p$-adic valuation** or **$p$-adic order** of an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ is the [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent) of the highest power of the [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$ that [divides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divides) $n$. It is denoted $\nu_p(n)$. Equivalently, $\nu_p(n)$ is the exponent to which $p$ appears in the prime factorization of $n$.

:::

The [$p$-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) valuation is a [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) and gives rise to an analogue of the usual [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991), though unlike the latter, the $p$-adic absolute value is not [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158). Whereas the [completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of the rational numbers with respect to the usual absolute value results in the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$, the completion of the rational numbers with respect to the $p$-adic absolute value results in the [$p$-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) $\mathbb{Q}_p$.[^o2]

## Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

Let $p$ be a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666).

### Integers {#Integers}

The **$p$-adic valuation** of an integer $n$ is defined to be

$$\nu_p(n)= \begin{cases} \mathrm{max}\{k \in \mathbb{N}_0 : p^k \mid n\} & \text{if } n \neq 0\\ \infty & \text{if } n=0, \end{cases}$$

where $\mathbb{N}_0$ denotes the set of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) (including zero) and $m \mid n$ denotes [divisibility](wikipedia://en/49492) of $n$ by $m$. In particular, $\nu_p$ is a function $\nu_p \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{N}_0 \cup\{\infty\}$.[^o6]

For example, $\nu_2(-12) = 2$, $\nu_3(-12) = 1$, and $\nu_5(-12) = 0$ since $|{-12}| = 12 = 2^2 \cdot 3^1 \cdot 5^0$.

The notation $p^k \parallel n$ is sometimes used to mean $k = \nu_p(n)$.[^o7]

If $n$ is a positive integer, then $\nu_p(n) \leq \log_p n$; this follows directly from $n \geq p^{\nu_p(n)}$.

### Rational numbers {#Rational_numbers}

The $p$-adic valuation can be extended to the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) as the function

$\nu_p : \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{Z} \cup\{\infty\}$[^o9][^o10]

defined by

$$\nu_p\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)=\nu_p(r)-\nu_p(s).$$

For example, $\nu_2 \bigl(\tfrac{9}{8}\bigr) = -3$ and $\nu_3 \bigl(\tfrac{9}{8}\bigr) = 2$ since $\tfrac{9}{8} = 2^{-3}\cdot 3^2$.

Some properties are:

$$\nu_p(r\cdot s) = \nu_p(r) + \nu_p(s)$$

$$\nu_p(r+s) \geq \min\bigl\{ \nu_p(r), \nu_p(s)\bigr\}$$

Moreover, if $\nu_p(r) \neq \nu_p(s)$, then

$$\nu_p(r+s)= \min\bigl\{ \nu_p(r), \nu_p(s)\bigr\}$$

where $\min$ is the minimum (i.e. the smaller of the two).

### Formula for the $p$-adic valuation of integers {#Formula_for_the_-adic_valuation_of_integers}

[Legendre's formula](wikipedia://en/44533982) shows that $\nu_p(n!)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty{}}{\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}}$.

For any positive integer $n$, $n = \frac{n!}{(n-1)!}$ and so $\nu_p(n)=\nu_p(n!)-\nu_p((n-1)!)$.

Therefore, $\nu{}_p(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty{}}{\bigg(\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}-\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}\bigg)}$.

This infinite sum can be reduced to $\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor{\log_p{(n)}\rfloor{}}}{\bigg(\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}-\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}\bigg)}$.

This formula can be extended to negative integer values to give:

$$\nu{}_p(n) =\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor{\log_p{(|n|)}\rfloor{}}}{\bigg(\left\lfloor{\frac{|n|}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}-\left\lfloor{\frac{|n|-1}{p^i}}\right\rfloor{}\bigg)}$$

## $p$-adic absolute value {#-adic_absolute_value}

The $p$-adic [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667) (or $p$-adic norm,[^o14] though not a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) in the sense of analysis) on [$\mathbb{Q}$](wikipedia://en/19727024) is the function

$$|\cdot|_p \colon \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$$

defined by

$$|r|_p = p^{-\nu_p(r)} .$$

Thereby, $|0|_p = p^{-\infty} = 0$ for all $p$ and for example, $|{-12}|_2 = 2^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}$ and $\bigl|\tfrac{9}{8}\bigr|_2 = 2^{-(-3)} = 8 .$

The $p$-adic absolute value satisfies the following properties.

{| class="wikitable"

\|- |Non-negativity || $|r|_p \geq 0$ |- |[Positive-definiteness](wikipedia://en/649616) || $|r|_p = 0 \iff r = 0$ |- |[Multiplicativity](wikipedia://en/20059) || $|r s|_p = |r|_p|s|_p$ |- |[Non-Archimedean](wikipedia://en/333996) || $|r+s|_p \leq \max\left(|r|_p, |s|_p\right)$ |} From the [multiplicativity](wikipedia://en/20059) $|r s|_p = |r|_p|s|_p$ it follows that $|1|_p=1=|{-1}|_p$ for the [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) $1$ and $-1$ and consequently also $|{-r}|_p = |r|_p .$ The [subadditivity](wikipedia://en/745789) $|r+s|_p \leq |r|_p + |s|_p$ follows from the [non-Archimedean](wikipedia://en/333996) [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) $|r+s|_p \leq \max\left(|r|_p, |s|_p\right)$.

::: {#o16 .statement cue=named-heading name="Product formula"}

### Product formula {#Product_formula}

The choice of base $p$ in the [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) $p^{-\nu_p(r)}$ makes no difference for most of the properties, but supports the product formula:

$$\prod_{0, p} |r|_p = 1$$

where the product is taken over all primes $p$ and the usual absolute value, denoted $|r|_0$. This follows from simply taking the [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization): each prime power factor $p^k$ contributes its reciprocal to its $p$-adic absolute value, and then the usual [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158) absolute value cancels all of them.

By [Ostrowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/3360343), the usual and $p$-adic absolute values occurring in the formula are all the absolute values on the rational numbers up to equivalence. An analogous product formula can be used to axiomatically define [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358), of which the rational numbers are the simplest example.

:::

### Metric and completion {#Metric_and_completion}

A [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) can be formed on the set $\mathbb{Q}$ with a ([non-Archimedean](wikipedia://en/333996), [translation-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_invariance)) metric

$$d \colon \mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$$

defined by

$$d(r,s) = |r-s|_p .$$

The [completion](wikipedia://en/7832) of $\mathbb{Q}$ with respect to this metric leads to the set $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of $p$-adic numbers. Like the rationals, they form a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and the $p$-adic valuation and absolute value can be extended to $\mathbb{Q}_p$, making it a [complete valued field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_valued_field).
