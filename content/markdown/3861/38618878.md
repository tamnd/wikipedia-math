# Graph power

*Image: Square of a graph.svg.* The square of a graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph power"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of mathematics, the **$k$th power** $G^{k}$ of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) $G$ is another graph that has the same set of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899), but in which two vertices are adjacent when their [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) in $G$ is at most $k$. Powers of graphs are referred to using terminology similar to that of [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) of numbers: $G^{2}$ is called the *[square](wikipedia://en/213919)* of $G$, $G^{3}$ is called the *[cube](wikipedia://en/451445)* of $G$, etc.[^o2]

:::

Graph powers should be distinguished from the [products](wikipedia://en/11492935) of a graph with itself, which (unlike powers) generally have many more vertices than the original graph.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If a graph has [diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_diameter) $d$, then its $d$-th power is the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816). If a graph family has bounded [clique-width](wikipedia://en/16795502), then so do its $d$-th powers for any fixed $d$.[^o6]

### Coloring {#Coloring}

[Graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) on the square of a graph may be used to assign frequencies to the participants of wireless communication networks so that no two participants interfere with each other at any of their common neighbors,[^o9] and to find [graph drawings](wikipedia://en/325813) with high [angular resolution](wikipedia://en/33245450).[^o8]

Both the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) and the [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) of the $k$th power of a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) of maximum degree $\Delta$ are $O(\Delta^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor})$, where the degeneracy bound shows that a [greedy coloring](wikipedia://en/21051195) algorithm may be used to color the graph with this many colors.[^o9] For the special case of a square of a planar graph, Wegner conjectured in 1977 that the chromatic number of the square of a planar graph is at most $\max(\Delta + 5, \tfrac{3\Delta}{2} + 1)$, and it is known that the chromatic number is at most $\tfrac{5\Delta}{3} + O(1)$.[^o10][^o11] More generally, for any graph with degeneracy $d$ and maximum degree $\Delta$, the degeneracy of the square of the graph is $O(d\Delta)$, so many types of [sparse graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph) other than the planar graphs also have squares whose chromatic number is proportional to $\Delta$.

Although the chromatic number of the square of a nonplanar graph with maximum degree $\Delta$ may be proportional to $\Delta^{2}$ in the worst case, it is smaller for graphs of high [girth](wikipedia://en/12013), being bounded $\text{by } O(\Delta^{2} / \log \Delta)$ in this case.[^o12]

Determining the minimum number of colors needed to color the square of a graph is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard), even in the planar case.[^o13]

### Hamiltonicity {#Hamiltonicity}

The cube of every connected graph necessarily contains a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle).[^o15] It is not necessarily the case that the square of a connected graph is Hamiltonian, and it is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) to determine whether the square is Hamiltonian.[^o16] Nevertheless, by [Fleischner's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleischner's_theorem), the square of a [2-vertex-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) is always Hamiltonian.[^o17]

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The $k$th power of a graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges may be computed in time $O(mn)$ by performing a [breadth first search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadth_first_search) starting from each vertex to determine the distances to all other vertices, or slightly faster using more sophisticated algorithms.[^o19] Alternatively, If $A$ is an [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) for the graph, modified to have nonzero entries on its main diagonal, then the nonzero entries of $A^{k}$ give the adjacency matrix of the $k$th power of the graph,[^o20] from which it follows that constructing $k$th powers may be performed in an amount of time that is within a logarithmic factor of the time for [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).

The $k$th powers of trees can be recognized in time linear in the size of the input graph. [^o21]

Given a graph, deciding whether it is the square of another graph is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete). [^o22] Moreover, it is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) to determine whether a graph is a $k$th power of another graph, for a given number $k \ge 2$, or whether it is a $k$th power of a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431), for $k > 2$.[^o23]

## In directed graphs {#In_directed_graphs}

*Image: Square of digraph.svg.* The square of a [digraph](wikipedia://en/19721986)

For the square of [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986) (or digraphs), each pair of vertices connected by a directed path of length two become connected by a path in the same direction of length one. Pairs of points directed to the same vertex, then, do not cause a connection between them in the square of a directed graph.

The [second neighborhood problem](wikipedia://en/59632528) can be stated in terms of the square of a digraph, asking if there exists a vertex in every [oriented graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented_graph) whose [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) increase by at least a factor of two when the graph is squared.[^o25]

## Induced subgraphs {#Induced_subgraphs}

*Image: Demi-3-cube.svg.* $K_{4}$ as the half-square of a [cube graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_graph)

The [half-square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-square) of a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) $G$ is the subgraph of $G^{2}$ induced by one side of the bipartition of $G$. [Map graphs](wikipedia://en/50947627) are the half-squares of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314),[^o27] and [halved cube graphs](wikipedia://en/39329236) are the half-squares of [hypercube graphs](wikipedia://en/6706815).[^o28]

[Leaf powers](wikipedia://en/49639194) are the subgraphs of powers of trees induced by the leaves of the tree. A $k$-leaf power is a leaf power whose exponent is $k$.[^o29]
