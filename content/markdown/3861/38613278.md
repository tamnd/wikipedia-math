# Jules Drach

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jules Drach"}

**Jules Joseph Drach** (13 March 1871, in [Sainte-Marie-aux-Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Marie-aux-Mines) near [Colmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colmar) – 8 March 1949, in [Cavalaire-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalaire-sur-Mer), [Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-Côte_d’Azur)) was a French mathematician.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Drach came from an Alsatian family of peasants. Because of the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) the parents fled with their children to [Saint-Dié](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Dié-des-Vosges). He attended school there and in [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_France) before he studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) (ENS). In 1892 he received his [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) and in 1898 he received his PhD under [Paul Tannery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tannery) at ENS with thesis *Essai sur la théorie générale de l'intégration et sur la classification des transcendantes*. In his thesis, Drach developed a [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) for differential equations, building upon the work of [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000), [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) and [Ernest Vessiot](wikipedia://en/1142911). After that he was *Maître de Conférences* at the University (Faculté des Sciences) in [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand). Drach was successively a professor at [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), [Poitiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poitiers), and [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), before he became in 1913 Professor for Analytic Mechanics and Higher Analysis at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). During World War I he studied the mathematical theory of ballistics and published the results of his research around 1920.

In retirement he moved to Cavalaire in southern France, where he had a country estate to which he frequently withdrew because of his health. He remained mathematically active in retirement.

In addition to mathematical analysis and its applications to mechanics, Drach worked on number theory, partial differential equations, and differential geometry. In 1929 he was elected a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences).

Drach and his friend [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161) published, in 1892, their notes on lectures given by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) and, in 1895, their notes on lectures given by [Paul Tannery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tannery). Drach was also a co-editor of the collected works of Poincaré.

Drach was married to Mathilde Guitton. His son Pierre Drach was a noted biologist.
