# Kellogg's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kellogg's theorem"}

**Kellogg's theorem** is a pair of related results in the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) study of the regularity of [harmonic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_functions) on sufficiently smooth domains by [Oliver Dimon Kellogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Dimon_Kellogg).

:::

In the first version, it states that, for $k \geq 2$, if the domain's boundary is of class $C^k$ and the *k*-th derivatives of the boundary are [Dini continuous](wikipedia://en/27073288), then the harmonic functions are uniformly $C^k$ as well. The second, more common version of the theorem states that for domains which are $C^{k,\alpha}$, if the boundary data is of class $C^{k,\alpha}$, then so is the harmonic function itself.

Kellogg's method of proof analyzes the representation of harmonic functions provided by the [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583), applied to an interior tangent sphere.

In modern presentations, Kellogg's theorem is usually covered as a specific case of the boundary [Schauder estimates](wikipedia://en/23547303) for [elliptic](wikipedia://en/550137) [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).
