# Atkin–Lehner theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atkin–Lehner theory"}

In mathematics, **Atkin–Lehner theory** is part of the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) describing when they arise at a given integer *level* *N* in such a way that the theory of [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837) can be extended to higher levels.

:::

Atkin–Lehner theory is based on the concept of a **newform**, which is a [cusp form](wikipedia://en/610165) 'new' at a given *level* *N*, where the levels are the nested [congruence subgroups](wikipedia://en/382694):

$$\Gamma_0(N) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in \text{SL}(2, \mathbf{Z}): c \equiv 0 \pmod{N} \right\}$$

of the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019), with *N* ordered by [divisibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisibility). That is, if *M* divides *N*, Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of Γ<sub>0</sub>(*M*). The **oldforms** for Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) are those modular forms *f*(*τ*) of level *N* of the form *g*(*d τ*) for modular forms *g* of level *M* with *M* a proper divisor of *N*, where *d* divides *N/M*. The newforms are defined as a vector subspace of the modular forms of level *N*, complementary to the space spanned by the oldforms, i.e. the orthogonal space with respect to the [Petersson inner product](wikipedia://en/4028987).

The [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837), which act on the space of all cusp forms, preserve the subspace of newforms and are [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) and commuting operators (with respect to the Petersson inner product) when restricted to this subspace. Therefore, the algebra of operators on newforms they generate is a finite-dimensional [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) that is commutative; and by the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/123495) of such operators, there exists a basis for the space of newforms consisting of eigenforms for the full [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907189).

## Atkin–Lehner involutions {#Atkin–Lehner_involutions}

Consider a [Hall divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_divisor) *e* of *N*, which means that not only does *e* divide *N*, but also *e* and *N*/*e* are relatively prime (often denoted *e*||*N*). If *N* has *s* distinct prime divisors, there are 2<sup>*s*</sup> Hall divisors of *N*; for example, if *N* = 360 = 2<sup>3</sup>⋅3<sup>2</sup>⋅5<sup>1</sup>, the 8 Hall divisors of *N* are 1, 2<sup>3</sup>, 3<sup>2</sup>, 5<sup>1</sup>, 2<sup>3</sup>⋅3<sup>2</sup>, 2<sup>3</sup>⋅5<sup>1</sup>, 3<sup>2</sup>⋅5<sup>1</sup>, and 2<sup>3</sup>⋅3<sup>2</sup>⋅5<sup>1</sup>.

For each Hall divisor *e* of *N*, choose an integral matrix *W*<sub>*e*</sub> of the form

$$W_e = \begin{pmatrix}ae & b \\ cN & de \end{pmatrix}$$

with det *W*<sub>*e*</sub> = *e*. These matrices have the following properties:

- The elements *W*<sub>*e*</sub> *normalize* Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*): that is, if *A* is in Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*), then *W*<sub>*e*</sub>*AW*${}^{-1}_{e}$ is in Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*).
- The matrix *W*${}^{2}_{e}$, which has determinant *e*<sup>2</sup>, can be written as *eA* where *A* is in Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*). We will be interested in operators on [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165) coming from the action of *W*<sub>*e*</sub> on Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) by conjugation, under which both the scalar *e* and the matrix *A* act trivially. Therefore, the equality *W*${}^{2}_{e}$ = *eA* implies that the action of *W*<sub>*e*</sub> squares to the identity; for this reason, the resulting operator is called an **Atkin–Lehner involution**.
- If *e* and *f* are both Hall divisors of *N*, then W<sub>*e*</sub> and W<sub>*f*</sub> commute modulo Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*). Moreover, if we define *g* to be the Hall divisor *g* = *ef*/(*e*,*f*)<sup>2</sup>, their product is equal to W<sub>*g*</sub> modulo Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*).
- If we had chosen a different matrix *W* ′<sub>*e*</sub> instead of *W*<sub>*e*</sub>, it turns out that *W*<sub>*e*</sub> ≡ *W* ′<sub>*e*</sub> modulo Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*), so *W*<sub>*e*</sub> and *W* ′<sub>*e*</sub> would determine the same Atkin–Lehner involution.

We can summarize these properties as follows. Consider the subgroup of GL(2,**Q**) generated by Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) together with the matrices *W*<sub>*e*</sub>; let Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*)<sup>\+</sup> denote its quotient by positive scalar matrices. Then Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*)<sup>\+</sup> of index 2<sup>*s*</sup> (where *s* is the number of distinct prime factors of *N*); the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) is isomorphic to (**Z**/2**Z**)<sup>s</sup> and acts on the cusp forms via the Atkin–Lehner involutions.
