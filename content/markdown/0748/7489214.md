# Associative magic square

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Associative magic square"}

*Image: Magic Square Lo Shu.svg.* In the [Lo Shu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Shu_Square), pairs of opposite numbers sum to 10

*Image: Albrecht Dürer - Melencolia I (detail).jpg.* Detail from *[Melencolia I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melencolia_I)* showing a 4 × 4 associative square

An **associative magic square** is a [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) for which each pair of numbers symmetrically opposite to the center sum up to the same value. For an *n* × *n* square, filled with the numbers from 1 to *n*<sup>2</sup>, this common sum must equal *n*<sup>2</sup> + 1. These squares are also called **associated magic squares**, **regular magic squares**, **regmagic squares**, or **symmetric magic squares**.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For instance, the [Lo Shu Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo_Shu_Square) – the unique 3 × 3 magic square – is associative, because each pair of opposite points form a line of the square together with the center point, so the sum of the two opposite points equals the sum of a line minus the value of the center point regardless of which two opposite points are chosen. The 4 × 4 magic square from [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer) 1514 engraving *[Melencolia I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melencolia_I)* – also found in a 1765 letter of [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin) – is also associative, with each pair of opposite numbers summing to 17.

:::

## Existence and enumeration {#Existence_and_enumeration}

The numbers of possible associative *n* × *n* magic squares for *n* = 3,4,5,..., counting two squares as the same whenever they differ only by a rotation or reflection, are:

1, 48, 48544, 0, 1125154039419854784, ...

The number zero for *n* = 6 is an example of a more general phenomenon: associative magic squares do not exist for values of *n* that are [singly even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singly_even) (equal to 2 [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 4). Every associative magic square of [even](wikipedia://en/143135) order forms a [singular matrix](wikipedia://en/245528), but associative magic squares of [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) order can be singular or nonsingular.
