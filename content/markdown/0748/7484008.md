# Berlin procedure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Berlin procedure"}

The **Berlin procedure** (**BV**) is a mathematical procedure for [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) decomposition and [seasonal adjustment](wikipedia://en/7579959) of monthly and quarterly economic time series. The mathematical foundations of the procedure were developed in 1960's at [Berlin Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin) and the [German Institute for Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Institute_for_Economic_Research) (DIW). The most important user of the procedure is the [Federal Statistical Office of Germany](wikipedia://en/7516079).

:::

[BV4.1](wikipedia://en/7425812) is a software implementation of the latest version 4.1 of BV, available as [freeware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeware) for non-commercial purposes.

## Specific features of the procedure {#Specific_features_of_the_procedure}

The latest version 4.1 of the Berlin procedure is distinguished from other commonly used decomposition and seasonal adjustment methods (i.e. [X-12-ARIMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-12-ARIMA)) by the following characteristic features:

- The procedure works well even with time series showing strongly changing seasonal patterns.
- Trend-cycles are depicted plausibly in terms of economic points of view.
- The cost-benefit ratio is low as the user does not need special training or even expert knowledge or long-term experience with the procedure to make high-quality analyses.
- On principle the analysis results do not depend on the respective user since there is no need to determine any series-specific parameters of the procedure.
- As [linear regression models](wikipedia://en/48758386) are used, on principle there are no differences between indirect and direct analysis results of aggregate series.
