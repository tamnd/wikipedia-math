# Wiener amalgam space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wiener amalgam space"}

In mathematics, **amalgam spaces** categorize functions with regard to their local and global behavior. While the concept of [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) treating local and global behavior separately was already known earlier, **Wiener amalgams**, as the term is used today, were introduced by [Hans Georg Feichtinger](wikipedia://en/31979049) in 1980. The concept is named after [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185).

:::

Let $X$ be a normed space with norm $\|\cdot \|_X$. Then the *Wiener amalgam space*[^o2] with local component $X$ and global component $L^p_m$, a [weighted $L^p$ space](wikipedia://en/45194#Weighted_Lp_spaces) with non-negative weight $m$, is defined by

$$W(X,L^p) = \left\{ f\ :\ \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \|f(\cdot)\bar{g}(\cdot-x)\|^p_X m(x)^p \, dx\right)^{1/p} < \infty\right\},$$

where $g$ is a continuously differentiable, compactly supported function, such that $\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z^d}} g(z-x) = 1$, for all $z\in\mathbb{R}^d$. Again, the space defined is independent of $g$. As the definition suggests, Wiener amalgams are useful to describe functions showing characteristic local and global behavior.[^o3]
