# Veblen–Young theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Veblen–Young theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Veblen–Young theorem**, proved by (txt), states that a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of dimension at least 3 can be constructed as the projective space associated to a vector space over a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) (or [field](wikipedia://en/10603), by [Wedderburn's little theorem](wikipedia://en/7132002)).

:::

[Non-Desarguesian planes](wikipedia://en/21092112) give examples of 2-dimensional projective spaces that do not arise from vector spaces over division rings, showing that the restriction to dimension at least 3 is necessary.

[Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570) generalized the Veblen–Young theorem to [Tits buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tits_building), showing that those of rank at least 3 arise from [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924).

(txt) generalized the Veblen–Young theorem to [continuous geometry](wikipedia://en/32590824), showing that a [complemented modular lattice](wikipedia://en/753521) of order at least 4 is isomorphic to the [principal right ideals](wikipedia://en/6961771) of a [von Neumann regular ring](wikipedia://en/907524).

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A **projective space** *S* can be defined abstractly as a set *P* (the set of points), together with a set *L* of subsets of *P* (the set of lines), satisfying these axioms:

- Each two distinct points *p* and *q* are in exactly one line.
- Veblen's axiom: If *a*, *b*, *c*, *d* are distinct points and the lines through *ab* and *cd* meet, then so do the lines through *ac* and *bd*.
- Any line has at least 3 points on it.

The Veblen–Young theorem states that if the dimension of a projective space is at least 3 (meaning that there are two non-intersecting lines) then the projective space is isomorphic with the projective space of lines in a vector space over some [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) *K*.

:::
