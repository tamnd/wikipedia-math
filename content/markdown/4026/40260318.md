# Scattered space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scattered space"}

In mathematics, a **scattered space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* that contains no nonempty [dense-in-itself](wikipedia://en/8327127) subset.[^o2][^o3] Equivalently, every nonempty subset *A* of *X* contains a point isolated in *A*.

:::

A subset of a topological space is called a **scattered set** if it is a scattered space with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926).

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) is scattered.
- Every [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932) with the [order topology](wikipedia://en/213650) is scattered. Indeed, every nonempty subset *A* contains a minimum element, and that element is isolated in *A*.
- A space *X* with the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200), in particular the [Sierpinski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_space), is scattered. This is an example of a scattered space that is not a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721).
- The closure of a scattered set is not necessarily scattered. For example, in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) $\mathbb{R}^2$ take a [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) discrete set *A* in the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738), with the points getting denser and denser as one approaches the boundary. For example, take the union of the vertices of a series of *n*-gons centered at the origin, with radius getting closer and closer to 1. Then the closure of *A* will contain the whole circle of radius 1, which is dense-in-itself.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- In a topological space *X* the closure of a dense-in-itself subset is a [perfect set](wikipedia://en/5075551). So *X* is scattered if and only if it does not contain any nonempty perfect set.
- Every subset of a scattered space is scattered. Being scattered is a [hereditary property](wikipedia://en/13552465).
- Every scattered space *X* is a [T<sub>0</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space). (*Proof:* Given two distinct points *x*, *y* in *X*, at least one of them, say *x*, will be isolated in $\{x,y\}$. That means there is a neighborhood of *x* in *X* that does not contain *y*.)
- In a T<sub>0</sub> space the union of two scattered sets is scattered.[^o8][^o9] Note that the T<sub>0</sub> assumption is necessary here. For example, if $X=\{a,b\}$ with the [indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology), $\{a\}$ and $\{b\}$ are both scattered, but their union, $X$, is not scattered as it has no isolated point.
- Every T<sub>1</sub> scattered space is [totally disconnected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_disconnected). (*Proof:* If *C* is a nonempty connected subset of *X*, it contains a point *x* isolated in *C*. So the singleton $\{x\}$ is both open in *C* (because *x* is isolated) and closed in *C* (because of the T<sub>1</sub> property). Because *C* is connected, it must be equal to $\{x\}$. This shows that every connected component of *X* has a single point.)
- Every [second countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_countable) scattered space is [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable).[^o10]
- Every topological space *X* can be written in a unique way as the disjoint union of a [perfect set](wikipedia://en/5075551) and a scattered set.[^o11][^o12]
- Every second countable space *X* can be written in a unique way as the disjoint union of a perfect set and a countable scattered open set. (*Proof:* Use the perfect + scattered decomposition and the fact above about second countable scattered spaces, together with the fact that a subset of a second countable space is second countable.) Furthermore, every closed subset of a second countable *X* can be written uniquely as the disjoint union of a perfect subset of *X* and a countable scattered subset of *X*.[^o13] This holds in particular in any [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622), which is the contents of the [Cantor–Bendixson theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor–Bendixson_theorem).

:::
