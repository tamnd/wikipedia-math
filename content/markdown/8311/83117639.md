# Dying percolation conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dying percolation conjecture"}

The **dying percolation conjecture**[^o2] (also known as $\theta(p_c)=0$ conjecture) is a prominent conjecture in [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805).[^o3] It states that critical Bernoulli bond [percolation](wikipedia://en/299184) on $\mathbb{Z}^d$ for $d \ge 2$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) has no infinite clusters.

:::

The conjecture is proved for the cases $d=2$ by [Kesten](wikipedia://en/19816546),[^o4] for $d\ge 19$[^o5] by [Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takashi_Hara_%28mathematician%29) and [Slade](wikipedia://en/42538871) using the technique called "lace expansion". The later result was then improved to $d \ge 11$[^o6] by Fitzner and van der Hofstad.

An AI proof with claimed Lean formalization was reported in September 2026.[^o7]

The conjecture is sometimes called the "Dying percolation conjecture", a name used by mathematician [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056).[^o2][^o8][^o9][^o10] The name evokes the intuition that the cluster at origin "just barely" fails to survive at the critical threshold.

## Background {#Background}

In nearest-neighbor Bernoulli bond [percolation](wikipedia://en/187805) on $\mathbb{Z}^d$, each edge of the [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402) is independently declared *open* with probability $p\in[0,1]$ and *closed* otherwise. A **cluster** is a maximal connected subgraph of open edges. The central question of percolation theory is: for which values of $p$ does an infinite cluster exist [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908).

The percolation probability $\theta(p)$ is defined as the probability that the cluster containing $0$ is infinite. By a coupling argument, one can show that $\theta$ is a non-decreasing function of $p$. As a corollary, for any dimension $d$ there exists a critical probability (denoted by $p_c$) , such that

- For $p < p_c$, every cluster is almost surely finite.
- For $p > p_c$, an infinite cluster almost surely exists.

The conjecture asks whether $\theta(p_c) = 0$, i.e. whether $\theta$ is continuous or has a discontinuity at $p_c$.

## Equivalent formulations {#Equivalent_formulations}

The following statements are equivalent for Bernoulli percolation on $\mathbb{Z}^d$:

- Bernoulli bond percolation with critical parameter $p_c$ has no infinite cluster almost surely;
- No infinite cluster at criticality at the origin: $\theta(p_c) = 0$;
- Continuity of $\theta$ at $p_c$;
- Continuity of $\theta$.

The last formulation is equivalent to the others, since Aizenman, Kesten and Newman showed[^o13] that $\theta$ is continuous at all points of $[0, 1]$ except for possibly $p_c$.

## Significance {#Significance}

The $\theta(p_c)=0$ conjecture is among the central open problems in percolation theory.[^o25] (Benjamini Lyons Peres Schramm 1999)[^o16] open their paper by describing it as "the main long-standing open question in percolation theory". [Fields medalist](wikipedia://en/10859) [Hugo Duminil-Copin](wikipedia://en/57492975) in his [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) lecture "Sixty years of percolation" frames the development of percolation theory around this conjecture.[^o3] Louigi Addario-Berry[^o9] suggested that it's among the most important problems in the larger field of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

This conjecture needs to be true for most [percolation critical exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percolation_critical_exponents) to be [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined).[^o17] As such, it is predicted by physicists studying [phase transition theory](wikipedia://en/54423). The most relevant physically case ($d=3$) remains open.

## Known results {#Known_results}

### d=1 {#d=1}

For $d=1$, the critical probability $p_c=1$ and

$$\theta(p) = \begin{cases}0,\text{ if }p<1\\ 1,\text{ if }p=1. \end{cases}$$

So the conjecture is *false*. However, for $d \ge 2$, $p_c \in (0, 1)$..,[^o20] and the conjecture is widely believed to be true.

### d=2 {#d=2}

The dimension $d=2$ is the only dimension (besides 1) where $p_c$ is known exactly. For the other dimensions, there are only [numerical approximations](wikipedia://en/11790568). It was proven to be $\frac{1}{2}$ by [Kesten](wikipedia://en/19816546)[^o4] after years of being the problem that drove most of the efforts in the field.[^o3] This resolved the conjecture for $d=2$, since $\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=0$ was already proven by [Harris](wikipedia://en/14786020).[^o22]

For 2-dimensional lattices, continuity of the phase transition is proved only for some lattices other than square lattice. It is conjectured for them to belong to the same [universality class](wikipedia://en/353890) and so to have the same critical behavior.

### High dimensions ($d \ge 11$) {#High_dimensions_(d_\ge_11)}

For larger $d$, the conjecture is listed as Problem 5 in Section 12 of (Kesten 1982).[^o25] Since then, the partial cases and generalizations of the conjecture received much attention[^o3]

In 1990, Hara and Slade[^o5] used the lace expansion technique to show that percolation at the critical parameter $p_c$ exhibits a [mean-field](wikipedia://en/555768) behavior. Namely, they showed that quantities such as $\theta$ obey the power laws, with [exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percolation_critical_exponents) predicted by the mean-field theory. Later, their techniques were improved to lower the dimension down to 11.[^o6] However, the mean-field behavior cannot hold in dimensions 5 and less, so dimensions 3, 4 and 5 cannot be covered by an extension of this proof. For dimensions higher than 6, the conjecture is true for a "spread out" percolation, where additional edges are added to any pair of vertices on distance less than a large enough parameter $L$[^o5]

### Percolation on half-space {#Percolation_on_half-space}

It is shown in (Barsky Grimmett Newman 1991)[^o28] that for any $d \ge 2$, the critical bond percolation restricted to a half-space $\mathbb{N}\times \mathbb{Z}^{d-1}$ almost surely doesn't contain an infinite cluster. This is in contrast with the result of (Grimmett Marstrand 1990)[^o30] that for any $p > p_c$, the percolation on a slab $[0, n]^{d-2} \times \mathbb{Z}^2$ almost surely has an infinite cluster for large enough $n$.

### Nonamenable and exponential-growth graphs {#Nonamenable_and_exponential-growth_graphs}

The conjecture has been verified for a large class of transitive graphs whose [growth](wikipedia://en/498727) is faster than any polynomial.

The foundational result in this direction is due to (Benjamini Lyons Peres Schramm 1999):[^o16] critical Bernoulli percolation on any [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of a [nonamenable group](wikipedia://en/657430) has no infinite clusters. As a trivial partial case, it covers infinite $d$-regular trees.

In (Hutchcroft 2016),[^o34] this result was extended to all quasi-[transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/581175) of exponential growth.

::: {#o36 .statement cue=named-heading name="Reduction to a conjectural correlation inequality"}

### Reduction to a conjectural correlation inequality {#Reduction_to_a_conjectural_correlation_inequality}

In (Kozma Nitzan 2024),[^o38] the authors reduced the conjecture to various [correlation inequalities](wikipedia://en/20283423) decreasing in strength. They are supported by numerical evidence and apparently have been proven by AI, resolving the dying percolation conjecture.[^o7]

:::
