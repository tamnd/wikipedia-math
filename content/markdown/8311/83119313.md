# Emilija Mlakar Branc

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Emilija Mlakar Branc"}

**Emilija Mlakar Branc** (10 November 1901 – 25 June 1989) was a [Slovenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenes) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [mathematics professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor), and [author of mathematics textbooks](wikipedia://en/326471). In 1928, she became the first woman to graduate in mathematics from the [University of Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ljubljana).[^o3] She was the author and co-author of several textbooks on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960), which were popular and reprinted several times.[^o4]

:::

## Childhood {#Childhood}

She was born on 10 November 1901 in [Ptuj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptuj), where her family had moved in 1899 from [Moškanjci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moškanjci).[^o6][^o7] There, her mother Marjeta Beg had worked in an inn, while her father Štefan Mlakar worked as a stationmaster.[^o6][^o7] She had twelve brothers and sisters, eight of whom reached adulthood. Among them was the later Austro-Hungarian army officer Albin Mlakar (1890–1946).[^o7] Her parents sent all their surviving children to school, including Emilija, who graduated high school in 1924.[^o7][^o8]

## Education {#Education}

After completing primary and secondary education, she enrolled in the study of mathematics, pedagogical track, at the Faculty of Arts in Ljubljana at [University of Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ljubljana); at that time, the faculty offered no other mathematics programme.[^o10][^o3][^o11] During her studies, she lived as a lodger in an apartment in Ljubljana together with her younger brother, who was then a secondary-school student.[^o10] At the faculty, one of her classmates was Janko Branc (1902–1963) from [Kranjska Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranjska_Gora), later a lecturer in mathematics at the Faculty of Natural Sciences and Technology, whom she married on 14 July 1928.[^o6][^o12] They had no children (at least none who survived to adulthood).[^o13] In 1928 she became the first woman, and the thirteenth person overall, to graduate in mathematics from the University of Ljubljana.[^o3][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o11]

## Career {#Career}

After graduating university, she was employed in 1929 as a teacher at the State Real Gymnasium of Crown Prince Andrew in Ptuj (now [Gimnazija Ptuj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimnazija_Ptuj)).[^o18] In 1930 she became an assistant in physics at the Technical Faculty in Ljubljana in Ljubljana,[^o19] where she worked until 1933.[^o8] In 1932, she passed the professorial examination at the University of Ljubljana and earned the title of professor.[^o20]

In 1933, she was employed as a teacher of mathematics and physics at the State Real Gymnasium in Kranj (now the Gimnazija Kranj), where she remained for one year.[^o8] In 1934, she was transferred to the State Classical Gymnasium in Ljubljana in Ljubljana, where she worked as a teacher of mathematics and physics until 1937, when she was transferred to the State Women’s Real Gymnasium (now [Gimnazija Poljane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimnazija_Poljane)).[^o8][^o15][^o16] There, in addition to teaching mathematics and physics, she also served as business manager and custodian of the physics collection, the teachers' library, and the library of the support society.[^o16]

Between 1952 and 1955, as a member of the [DMFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Mathematicians,_Physicists_and_Astronomers_of_Slovenia) (Society of Mathematicians, Physicists and Astronomers of Slovenia), she also taught mathematics, alongside her full-time job, in a general educational course in mathematics and physics under the auspices of the People’s University.[^o21] Among her students there was the later chemist [Aleksandra Kornhauser Frazer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandra_Kornhauser_Frazer), who later said that Emilija Mlakar Branc had "initiated her into logical thinking for life".[^o22]

Between 1937 and 1952, Branc wrote, alone and with co-authors, four textbooks on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and two collections of exercises in [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960), which were popular in Slovenian lands and reprinted several times.[^o23][^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28][^o29][^o4]

## Later life and death {#Later_life_and_death}

In 1963, her husband died after a long illness.[^o12] After retirement, she moved to live with relatives in Ptuj. She died on 25 June 1989 in Ptuj.[^o13][^o6] She was buried at Rogoznica Cemetery in Ptuj.[^o13]
