# Seventh power

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Seventh power"}

In [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **seventh power** of a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) *n* is the result of multiplying seven instances of *n* together. So:

:::

$$n^{7} = n \times n \times n \times n \times n \times n \times n.$$

Seventh [powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_%28algebra%29) are also formed by multiplying a number by its [sixth power](wikipedia://en/54558894), the [square](wikipedia://en/213919) of a number by its [fifth power](wikipedia://en/40150949), or the [cube](wikipedia://en/451445) of a number by its [fourth power](wikipedia://en/665027).

The [sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) of seventh powers of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) is:

0, 1, 128, 2187, 16384, 78125, 279936, 823543, 2097152, 4782969, 10000000, 19487171, 35831808, 62748517, 105413504, 170859375, 268435456, 410338673, 612220032, 893871739, 1280000000, 1801088541, 2494357888, 3404825447, 4586471424, 6103515625, 8031810176, ...

In the [archaic notation](wikipedia://en/31228704) of [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247), the seventh power of a number was called the "second [sursolid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sursolid)".

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

[Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247) studied generalizations of [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569) for seventh powers, showing that every non-negative integer can be represented as a sum of at most 258 non-negative seventh powers (1<sup>7</sup> is 1, and 2<sup>7</sup> is 128). All but finitely many positive integers can be expressed more simply as the sum of at most 46 seventh powers. If powers of negative integers are allowed, only 12 powers are required.

The smallest number that can be represented in two different ways as a sum of four positive seventh powers is 2056364173794800.

The smallest seventh power that can be represented as a sum of eight distinct seventh powers is:

$$102^7=12^7+35^7+53^7+58^7+64^7+83^7+85^7+90^7.$$

The two known examples of a seventh power expressible as the sum of seven seventh powers are

$568^7 = 127^7+ 258^7 + 266^7 + 413^7 + 430^7 + 439^7 + 525^7$ (M. Dodrill, 1999);

and

$626^7 = 625^7+309^7+258^7+255^7+158^7+148^7+91^7$ (Maurice Blondot, 11/14/2000);

Any example with fewer terms in the sum would be a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](wikipedia://en/9660), which is currently only known to be false for the powers 4 and 5.
