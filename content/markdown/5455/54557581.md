# Henry Cohn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Cohn"}

**Henry Cohn** is an American mathematician. He is currently a professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4] Cohn graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2000 with a doctorate in mathematics.[^o5] Cohn was an [Erdős Lecturer](wikipedia://en/56645465) at [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) in 2008. In 2016, he became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_society_fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to discrete mathematics, including applications to computer science and physics."[^o6]

:::

In 2018, he was awarded the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) for his article “A Conceptual Breakthrough in Sphere Packing,” published in 2017[^o7] in the *[Notices of the AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notices_of_the_AMS)*.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

In 2003, with [Chris Umans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Umans), Cohn initiated a group-theoretic approach to matrix multiplication,[^o10] and is a core contributor to its continued development with various coauthors.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15]

In 2004, Cohn and [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) used linear programming methods to prove[^o16] upper bounds on sphere packings in all dimensions. Their conjecture 8.1 suggested "magic" optimizing functions existed in dimensions 2, 8, and 24.

In March 2016 [Maryna Viazovska](wikipedia://en/50004967) published[^o17] an arXiv preprint with such a magic function - a weakly holomorphic quasimodular form - proving the optimality of the *E*<sub>8</sub> lattice packing. Cohn contacted Viazovska, and within a week, Cohn, Abhinav Kumar, Stephen D. Miller, Danylo Radchenko, and Viazovska had similarly solved the [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621) problem in 24 dimensions via the [Leech lattice](wikipedia://en/344919) Λ<sub>24</sub>.[^o18][^o19]
