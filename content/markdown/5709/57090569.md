# L1-norm principal component analysis

*Image: L1-PCA.png.* L1-PCA compared with PCA. Nominal data (blue points); outlier (red point); PC (black line); L1-PC (red line); nominal maximum-variance line (dotted line).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L1-norm principal component analysis"}

**L1-norm principal component analysis (L1-PCA)** is a general method for multivariate data analysis.[^o2] L1-PCA is often preferred over standard L2-norm [principal component analysis](wikipedia://en/76340) (PCA) when the analyzed data may contain [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951) (faulty values or corruptions), as it is believed to be [robust](wikipedia://en/2885691).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

Both L1-PCA and standard PCA seek a collection of [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) directions (principal components) that define a [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) wherein data representation is maximized according to the selected criterion.[^o6][^o7][^o8] Standard PCA quantifies data representation as the aggregate of the [L2-norm](wikipedia://en/990534#Euclidean_norm) of the data point [projections](wikipedia://en/519182) into the subspace, or equivalently the aggregate [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) of the original points from their subspace-projected representations. L1-PCA uses instead the aggregate of the L1-norm of the data point projections into the subspace.[^o9] In PCA and L1-PCA, the number of principal components (PCs) is lower than the [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) of the analyzed matrix, which coincides with the dimensionality of the space defined by the original data points. Therefore, PCA or L1-PCA are commonly employed for [dimensionality reduction](wikipedia://en/579867) for the purpose of data denoising or compression. Among the advantages of standard PCA that contributed to its high popularity are [low-cost](wikipedia://en/6511) computational implementation by means of [singular-value decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular-value_decomposition) (SVD)[^o10] and statistical optimality when the data set is generated by a true [multivariate normal](wikipedia://en/50347) data source.

However, in modern big data sets, data often include corrupted, faulty points, commonly referred to as outliers.[^o11] Standard PCA is known to be sensitive to outliers, even when they appear as a small fraction of the processed data.[^o12] The reason is that the L2-norm formulation of L2-PCA places squared emphasis on the magnitude of each coordinate of each data point, ultimately overemphasizing peripheral points, such as outliers. On the other hand, following an L1-norm formulation, L1-PCA places linear emphasis on the coordinates of each data point, effectively restraining outliers.[^o13]

::: {#o15 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Consider any [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) $\mathbf X = [\mathbf x_1, \mathbf x_2, \ldots, \mathbf x_N] \in \mathbb R^{D \times N}$ consisting of $N$ $D$-dimensional data points. Define $r=rank(\mathbf X)$. For integer $K$ such that $1 \leq K < r$, L1-PCA is formulated as:[^o2]

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\begin{aligned} &\underset{\mathbf Q=[\mathbf q_1, \mathbf q_2, \ldots, \mathbf q_K] \in \mathbb R^{D \times K}}{\max}~~\| \mathbf X^\top \mathbf Q\|_1\\ &\text{subject to}~~ \mathbf Q^\top \mathbf Q=\mathbf I_K. \end{aligned} \tag{1}$$

:::

For $K=1$, ((1)) simplifies to finding the L1-norm principal component (L1-PC) of $\mathbf X$ by

::: {#o17 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\begin{aligned} &\underset{\mathbf q \in \mathbb R^{D \times 1}}{\max}~~\| \mathbf X^\top \mathbf q\|_1\\ &\text{subject to}~~ \| \mathbf q\|_2 =1. \end{aligned} \tag{2}$$

:::

In ((1))-((2)), [L1-norm](wikipedia://en/990534) $\| \cdot \|_1$ returns the sum of the absolute entries of its argument and L2-norm $\| \cdot \|_2$ returns the sum of the squared entries of its argument. If one substitutes $\| \cdot \|_1$ in ((2)) by the [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/1543735#Frobenius_norm)/L2-norm $\| \cdot \|_F$, then the problem becomes standard PCA and it is solved by the matrix $\mathbf Q$ that contains the $K$ dominant singular vectors of $\mathbf X$ (i.e., the singular vectors that correspond to the $K$ highest [singular values](wikipedia://en/709116)).

The maximization metric in ((2)) can be expanded as

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$\| \mathbf X^\top \mathbf Q\|_1=\sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{n=1}^N |\mathbf x_n^\top \mathbf q_k|. \tag{3}$$

:::

:::

## Solution {#Solution}

For any matrix $\mathbf A \in \mathbb R^{m \times n}$ with $m \geq n$, define $\Phi(\mathbf A)$ as the nearest (in the L2-norm sense) matrix to $\mathbf A$ that has orthonormal columns. That is, define

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi(\mathbf A) = & \underset{\mathbf Q \in \mathbb R^{m \times n}}{\text{argmin}}~~\| \mathbf A - \mathbf Q\|_F\\ &\text{subject to}~~ \mathbf Q^\top \mathbf Q=\mathbf I_n. \end{aligned} \tag{4}$$

:::

Procrustes Theorem[^o21][^o22] states that if $\mathbf A$ has SVD $\mathbf U_{m \times n} \boldsymbol \Sigma_{n \times n} \mathbf V_{n \times n}^\top$, then $\Phi(\mathbf A)=\mathbf U \mathbf V^\top$.

Markopoulos, Karystinos, and Pados[^o2] showed that, if $\mathbf B_{\text{BNM}}$ is the exact solution to the binary nuclear-norm maximization (BNM) problem

::: {#o23 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$\begin{aligned} \underset{\mathbf B \in \{ \pm 1\}^{N \times K}}{\text{max}}~~\| \mathbf X \mathbf B\|_*^2, \end{aligned} \tag{5}$$

:::

then

::: {#o24 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf Q_{\text{L1}} = \Phi(\mathbf X\mathbf B_{\text{BNM}}) \end{aligned} \tag{6}$$

:::

is the exact solution to L1-PCA in ((2)). The [nuclear-norm](wikipedia://en/1543735) $\| \cdot \|_*$ in ((2)) returns the summation of the singular values of its matrix argument and can be calculated by means of standard SVD. Moreover, it holds that, given the solution to L1-PCA, $\mathbf Q_{\text{L1}}$, the solution to BNM can be obtained as

::: {#o25 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf B_{\text{BNM}} = \text{sgn}(\mathbf X^\top \mathbf Q_{\text{L1}}) \end{aligned} \tag{7}$$

:::

where $\text{sgn}(\cdot)$ returns the $\{\pm 1\}$-sign matrix of its matrix argument (with no loss of generality, we can consider $\text{sgn}(0)=1$). In addition, it follows that $\| \mathbf X^\top \mathbf Q_{\text{L1}}\|_1 = \| \mathbf X \mathbf B_{\text{BNM}}\|_*$. BNM in ((5)) is a [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) problem over antipodal binary variables. Therefore, its exact solution can be found through exhaustive evaluation of all $2^{NK}$ elements of its feasibility set, with [asymptotic cost](wikipedia://en/6511) $\mathcal O(2^{NK})$. Therefore, L1-PCA can also be solved, through BNM, with cost $\mathcal O(2^{NK})$ (exponential in the product of the number of data points with the number of the sought-after components). It turns out that L1-PCA can be solved optimally (exactly) with polynomial complexity in $N$ for fixed data dimension $D$*, $\mathcal{O}(N^{rK-K+1})$*.[^o2]

For the special case of $K=1$ (single L1-PC of $\mathbf X$), BNM takes the binary-quadratic-maximization (BQM) form

::: {#o26 .equation cue=numblk name="8"}

$$\begin{aligned} & \underset{\mathbf b \in \{ \pm 1\}^{N \times 1}}{\text{max}}~~ \mathbf b^\top \mathbf X^\top \mathbf X \mathbf b. \end{aligned} \tag{8}$$

:::

The transition from ((5)) to ((8)) for $K=1$ holds true, since the unique singular value of $\mathbf X \mathbf b$ is equal to $\| \mathbf X \mathbf b\|_2 = \sqrt{\mathbf b^\top \mathbf X^\top \mathbf X \mathbf b}$, for every $\mathbf b$. Then, if $\mathbf b_{\text{BNM}}$ is the solution to BQM in ((7)), it holds that

::: {#o27 .equation cue=numblk name="9"}

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf q_{\text{L1}} = \Phi(\mathbf X \mathbf b_{\text{BNM}}) = \frac{\mathbf X \mathbf b_{\text{BNM}}}{\| \mathbf X \mathbf b_{\text{BNM}}\|_2} \end{aligned} \tag{9}$$

:::

is the exact L1-PC of $\mathbf X$, as defined in ((1)). In addition, it holds that $\mathbf b_{\text{BNM}} = \text{sgn}(\mathbf X^\top \mathbf q_{\text{L1}})$ and $\| \mathbf X^\top \mathbf q_{\text{L1}}\|_1 = \| \mathbf X \mathbf b_{\text{BNM}}\|_2$.

::: {#o29 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

### Exact solution of exponential complexity {#Exact_solution_of_exponential_complexity}

As shown above, the exact solution to L1-PCA can be obtained by the following two-step process:

\1. Solve the problem in ((5)) to obtain $\mathbf B_{\text{BNM}}$. 2. Apply SVD on $\mathbf X\mathbf B_{\text{BNM}}$ to obtain $\mathbf Q_{\text{L1}}$.

BNM in ((5)) can be solved by exhaustive search over the domain of **$\mathbf B$** with cost $\mathcal{O}(2^{NK})$.

### Exact solution of polynomial complexity {#Exact_solution_of_polynomial_complexity}

Also, L1-PCA can be solved optimally with cost $\mathcal{O}(N^{rK-K+1})$, when $r=rank(\mathbf X)$ is constant with respect to $N$ (always true for finite data dimension $D$).[^o2][^o32]

### Approximate efficient solvers {#Approximate_efficient_solvers}

In 2008, Kwak[^o13] proposed an iterative algorithm for the approximate solution of L1-PCA for $K=1$. This iterative method was later generalized for $K>1$ components.[^o34] Another approximate efficient solver was proposed by McCoy and Tropp[^o35] by means of semi-definite programming (SDP). Most recently, L1-PCA (and BNM in ((5))) were solved efficiently by means of bit-flipping iterations (L1-BF algorithm).[^o9]

#### L1-BF algorithm {#L1-BF_algorithm}

1 **function** L1BF($\mathbf X$, $K$): 2 Initialize $\mathbf B^{(0)} \in \{\pm 1\}^{N \times K}$ and $\mathcal L \leftarrow \{1,2,\ldots, NK\}$ 3 Set $t \leftarrow 0$ and $\omega \leftarrow \| \mathbf X \mathbf B^{(0)} \|_*$ 4 Until termination (or $T$ iterations) 5 $\mathbf B \leftarrow \mathbf B^{(t)}$, $t' \leftarrow t$ 6 For $x \in \mathcal L$ 7 $k \leftarrow \lceil \frac{x}{N} \rceil$, $n \leftarrow x-N(k-1)$ 8 $[\mathbf B]_{n,k} \leftarrow - [\mathbf B]_{n,k}$ *// flip bit* 9 $a(n,k) \leftarrow \| \mathbf X \mathbf B \|_*$ *// calculated by SVD or faster (see[^o9])* 10 if $a(n,k)>\omega$ 11 $\mathbf B^{(t)} \leftarrow \mathbf B$, $t' \leftarrow t+1$ 12 $\omega \leftarrow a(n,k)$ 13 end 14 if $t'=t$ *// no bit was flipped* 15 if $\mathcal L = \{1,2, \ldots, NK\}$ 16 terminate 17 else 18 $\mathcal L \leftarrow \{1,2, \ldots, NK\}$

The computational cost of L1-BF is $\mathcal O (ND min\{N,D\} + N^2K^2(K^2 + r))$.[^o9]

:::

## Complex data {#Complex_data}

L1-PCA has also been generalized to process [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) data. For complex L1-PCA, two efficient algorithms were proposed in 2018.[^o38]

## Tensor data {#Tensor_data}

L1-PCA has also been extended for the analysis of [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) data, in the form of L1-Tucker, the L1-norm robust analogous of standard [Tucker decomposition](wikipedia://en/18964319).[^o40] Two algorithms for the solution of L1-Tucker are L1-HOSVD and L1-HOOI.[^o40][^o41][^o42]
