# Judgment (mathematical logic)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Judgment"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), a **judgment** (or **judgement**) or **assertion** is a statement or enunciation in a [metalanguage](wikipedia://en/402929). For example, typical judgments in [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) would be *that a string is a [well-formed formula](wikipedia://en/404582)*, or *that a proposition is true*. Similarly, a judgment may assert the occurrence of a [free variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) in an expression of the object language, or the provability of a [proposition](wikipedia://en/81094). In general, a judgment may be any inductively definable assertion in the [metatheory](wikipedia://en/4823199).

:::

Judgments are used in formalizing [deduction systems](wikipedia://en/396102): a [logical axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_axiom) expresses a judgment, premises of a [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) are formed as a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of judgments, and their conclusion is a judgment as well (thus, hypotheses and conclusions of proofs are judgments). A characteristic feature of the variants of [Hilbert-style deduction systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-style_deduction_system) is that the *context* is not changed in any of their rules of inference, while both [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072) and [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329) contain some context-changing rules. Thus, if we are interested only in the [derivability](wikipedia://en/37531624) of [tautologies](wikipedia://en/4495335), not hypothetical judgments, then we can formalize the Hilbert-style deduction system in such a way that its rules of inference contain only judgments of a rather simple form. The same cannot be done with the other two deductions systems: as context is changed in some of their rules of inferences, they cannot be formalized so that hypothetical judgments could be avoided—not even if we want to use them just for proving derivability of tautologies.

This basic diversity among the various calculi allows such difference, that the same basic thought (e.g. [deduction theorem](wikipedia://en/480010)) must be proven as a [metatheorem](wikipedia://en/5106151) in Hilbert-style deduction system, while it can be declared explicitly as a [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) in [natural deduction](wikipedia://en/51072).

In [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282), some analogous notions are used as in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) (giving rise to connections between the two fields, e.g. [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299)). The abstraction in the notion of *judgment* in mathematical logic can be exploited also in foundation of type theory as well.
