# Covering groups of the alternating and symmetric groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Covering groups of the alternating and symmetric groups"}

In the mathematical area of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **covering groups of the alternating and symmetric groups** are groups that are used to understand the [projective representations](wikipedia://en/348860) of the [alternating](wikipedia://en/49726) and [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901). The covering groups were classified in (Schur 1911): for *n* ≥ 4, the covering groups are 2-fold covers except for the alternating groups of degree 6 and 7 where the covers are 6-fold.

:::

For example the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145) covers the [icosahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_group), an alternating group of degree 5, and the [binary tetrahedral group](wikipedia://en/10997586) covers the [tetrahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_group), an alternating group of degree 4.

## Definition and classification {#Definition_and_classification}

A group homomorphism from *D* to *G* is said to be a [Schur cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schur_cover) of the finite group *G* if:

1. the kernel is contained both in the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) and the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) of *D*, and
1. amongst all such homomorphisms, this *D* has maximal size.

The [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) of *G* is the kernel of any Schur cover and has many interpretations. When the homomorphism is understood, the group *D* is often called the Schur cover or Darstellungsgruppe.

The Schur covers of the symmetric and alternating groups were classified in (Schur 1911). The symmetric group of degree *n* ≥ 4 has Schur covers of order 2⋅*n*! There are two isomorphism classes if *n* ≠ 6 and one isomorphism class if *n* = 6. The alternating group of degree *n* has one isomorphism class of Schur cover, which has order *n*! except when *n* is 6 or 7, in which case the Schur cover has order 3⋅*n*!.

## Finite presentations {#Finite_presentations}

Schur covers can be described by means of generators and relations. The symmetric group S<sub>*n*</sub> has a [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) on *n* − 1 generators *t*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* = 1, 2, ..., n − 1 and relations

*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = 1, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 2

*t*<sub>*j*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*j*</sub>, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i* + 2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1.

These relations can be used to describe two non-isomorphic covers of the symmetric group. One covering group 2⋅S${}^{-}_{n}$ has generators *z*, *t*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *t*<sub>*n*−1</sub> and relations:

*zz* = 1

*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = *z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 2

*t*<sub>*j*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*j*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i* + 2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1.

The same group 2⋅S${}^{-}_{n}$ can be given the following presentation using the generators *z* and *s*<sub>*i*</sub> given by *t*<sub>*i*</sub> or *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*z* according as *i* is odd or even:

*zz* = 1

*s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub> = *z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub> = *s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 2

*s*<sub>*j*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub> = *s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*j*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i* + 2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1.

The other covering group 2⋅S${}^{+}_{n}$ has generators *z*, *t*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *t*<sub>*n*−1</sub> and relations:

*zz* = 1, *zt*<sub>*i*</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = 1, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 2

*t*<sub>*j*</sub>*t*<sub>*i*</sub> = *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*j*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i*+2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1.

The same group 2⋅S${}^{+}_{n}$ can be given the following presentation using the generators *z* and *s*<sub>*i*</sub> given by *t*<sub>*i*</sub> or *t*<sub>*i*</sub>*z* according as *i* is odd or even:

*zz* = 1, *zs*<sub>*i*</sub> = *s*<sub>*i*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub> = 1, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 1

*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub> = *s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*+1</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub>, for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n* − 2

*s*<sub>*j*</sub>*s*<sub>*i*</sub> = *s*<sub>*i*</sub>*s*<sub>*j*</sub>*z*, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i* + 2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1.

Sometimes all of the relations of the symmetric group are expressed as (*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*j*</sub>)<sup>*m*<sub>*ij*</sub></sup> = 1, where *m*<sub>*ij*</sub> are non-negative integers, namely *m*<sub>*ii*</sub> = 1, *m*<sub>*i*,*i*+1</sub> = 3, and *m*<sub>*ij*</sub> = 2, for 1 ≤ *i* \< *i* + 2 ≤ *j* ≤ *n* − 1. The presentation of 2⋅S${}^{-}_{n}$ becomes particularly simple in this form: (*t*<sub>*i*</sub>*t*<sub>*j*</sub>)<sup>*m*<sub>*ij*</sub></sup> = *z*, and *zz* = 1. The group 2⋅S${}^{+}_{n}$ has the nice property that its generators all have order 2.

## Projective representations {#Projective_representations}

[Covering groups](wikipedia://en/1378699) were introduced by [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) to classify [projective representations](wikipedia://en/348860) of groups. A (complex) [*linear* representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of a group *G* is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) *G* → GL(*n*, *C*) from the group *G* to a [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564), while a *projective* representation is a homomorphism *G* → PGL(*n*, *C*) from *G* to a [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667). Projective representations of *G* correspond naturally to linear representations of the covering group of *G*.

The projective representations of alternating and symmetric groups are the subject of the book (Hoffman Humphreys 1992).

## Integral homology {#Integral_homology}

Covering groups correspond to the second [group homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology) group, H<sub>2</sub>(*G*, **Z**), also known as the [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587). The Schur multipliers of the alternating groups A<sub>*n*</sub> (in the case where *n* is at least 4) are the cyclic groups of order 2, except in the case where *n* is either 6 or 7, in which case there is also a triple cover. In these cases, then, the Schur multiplier is the cyclic group of order 6, and the covering group is a 6-fold cover.

H<sub>2</sub>(A<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = 0 for *n* ≤ 3

H<sub>2</sub>(A<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = **Z**/2**Z** for *n* = 4, 5

H<sub>2</sub>(A<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = **Z**/6**Z** for *n* = 6, 7

H<sub>2</sub>(A<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = **Z**/2**Z** for *n* ≥ 8

For the symmetric group, the Schur multiplier vanishes for n ≤ 3, and is the cyclic group of order 2 for n ≥ 4:

H<sub>2</sub>(S<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = 0 for *n* ≤ 3

H<sub>2</sub>(S<sub>*n*</sub>, **Z**) = **Z**/2**Z** for *n* ≥ 4

## Construction of double covers {#Construction_of_double_covers}

*Image: An-spin-representation.svg.* The double cover of the alternating group can be constructed via the [spin representation](wikipedia://en/10160091) that covers the usual linear representation of the alternating group.

The double covers can be constructed as spin (respectively, pin) covers of faithful, irreducible, linear representations of A<sub>*n*</sub> and S<sub>*n*</sub>. These spin representations exist for all *n,* but are the covering groups only for *n* ≥ 4 (*n* ≠ 6, 7 for A<sub>*n*</sub>). For *n* ≤ 3, S<sub>*n*</sub> and A<sub>*n*</sub> are their own Schur covers.

*Image: An-Sn-covering-groups.svg.* The alternating group, symmetric group, and their double covers are related in this way, and have orthogonal representations and covering spin/pin representations in the corresponding diagram of orthogonal and spin/pin groups.

Explicitly, S<sub>*n*</sub> acts on the *n*-dimensional space **R**<sup>*n*</sup> by permuting coordinates (in matrices, as [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices)). This has a 1-dimensional [trivial subrepresentation](wikipedia://en/450573) corresponding to vectors with all coordinates equal, and the complementary (*n* − 1)-dimensional subrepresentation (of vectors whose coordinates sum to 0) is irreducible for *n* ≥ 4. Geometrically, this is the symmetries of the (*n* − 1)-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781), and algebraically, it yields maps $A_n \hookrightarrow \operatorname{SO}(n-1)$ and $S_n \hookrightarrow \operatorname{O}(n-1)$ expressing these as discrete subgroups ([point groups](wikipedia://en/1138942)). The special orthogonal group has a 2-fold cover by the [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) Spin(*n*) → SO(*n*), and restricting this cover to A<sub>*n*</sub> and taking the preimage yields a 2-fold cover 2⋅A<sub>*n*</sub> → A<sub>*n*</sub>. A similar construction with a [pin group](wikipedia://en/1794902) yields the 2-fold cover of the symmetric group: Pin<sub>±</sub>(*n*) → O(*n*). As there are two pin groups, there are two distinct 2-fold covers of the symmetric group, 2⋅S${}^{\pm}_{n}$, also called $\tilde \mathrm{S}_n$ and Ŝ<sub>*n*</sub>.

## Construction of triple cover for *n* = 6, 7 {#Construction_of_triple_cover_for_n_=_6,_7}

The triple covering of A<sub>6</sub>, denoted 3⋅A<sub>6</sub>, and the corresponding triple cover of S<sub>6</sub>, denoted 3⋅S<sub>6</sub>, can be constructed as symmetries of a certain set of vectors in a complex 6-space. While the exceptional triple covers of A<sub>6</sub> and A<sub>7</sub> extend to [extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) of S<sub>6</sub> and S<sub>7</sub>, these extensions are not [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_extension_%28mathematics%29) and so do not form Schur covers.

This construction is important in the study of the [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806), and in much of the exceptional behavior of small classical and exceptional groups, including: construction of the Mathieu group M<sub>24</sub>, the exceptional covers of the [projective unitary group](wikipedia://en/4402359) *U*<sub>4</sub>(3) and the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) $L_3(4),$ and the exceptional double cover of the [group of Lie type](wikipedia://en/1691490) G<sub>2</sub>(4).

## Exceptional isomorphisms {#Exceptional_isomorphisms}

For low dimensions there are [exceptional isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/25262819) with the map from a [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) to the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group).

For *n* = 3, the symmetric group is SL(2, 2) ≅ PSL(2, 2) and is its own Schur cover.

For *n* = 4, the Schur cover of the alternating group is given by SL(2, 3) → PSL(2, 3) ≅ A<sub>4</sub>, which can also be thought of as the [binary tetrahedral group](wikipedia://en/10997586) covering the [tetrahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_group). Similarly, GL(2, 3) → PGL(2, 3) ≅ S<sub>4</sub> is a Schur cover, but there is a second non-isomorphic Schur cover of S<sub>4</sub> contained in GL(2,9) – note that 9 = 3<sup>2</sup> so this is [extension of scalars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_of_scalars) of GL(2, 3). In terms of the above presentations, GL(2, 3) ≅ Ŝ<sub>4</sub>.

For *n* = 5, the Schur cover of the alternating group is given by SL(2, 5) → PSL(2, 5) ≅ A<sub>5</sub>, which can also be thought of as the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145) covering the [icosahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_group). Though PGL(2, 5) ≅ S<sub>5</sub>, GL(2, 5) → PGL(2, 5) is not a Schur cover as the kernel is not contained in the [derived subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_subgroup) of GL(2 ,5). The Schur cover of PGL(2, 5) is contained in GL(2, 25) – as before, 25 = 5<sup>2</sup>, so this extends the scalars.

For *n* = 6, the double cover of the alternating group is given by SL(2, 9) → PSL(2, 9) ≅ A<sub>6</sub>. While PGL(2, 9) is contained in the automorphism group [PΓL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PΓL)(2, 9) of PSL(2, 9) ≅ A<sub>6</sub>, PGL(2, 9) is not isomorphic to S<sub>6</sub>, and its Schur covers (which are double covers) are not contained in nor a quotient of GL(2, 9). Note that in almost all cases, $S_n \cong \operatorname{Aut}(A_n),$ with the unique exception of A<sub>6</sub>, due to [the exceptional outer automorphism of A<sub>6</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_exceptional_outer_automorphism_of_S6). Another subgroup of the automorphism group of A<sub>6</sub> is M<sub>10</sub>, the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) of degree 10, whose Schur cover is a triple cover. The Schur covers of the symmetric group S<sub>6</sub> itself have no faithful representations as a subgroup of GL(*d*, 9) for *d* ≤ 3. The four Schur covers of the automorphism group PΓL(2, 9) of A<sub>6</sub> are double covers.

For *n* = 8, the alternating group A<sub>8</sub> is isomorphic to SL(4, 2) = PSL(4, 2), and so SL(4, 2) → PSL(4, 2), which is 1-to-1, not 2-to-1, is not a Schur cover.

## Properties {#Properties}

Schur covers of finite [perfect groups](wikipedia://en/585271) are [superperfect](wikipedia://en/9915961), that is both their first and second integral homology vanish. In particular, the double covers of A<sub>*n*</sub> for *n* ≥ 4 are superperfect, except for *n* = 6, 7, and the six-fold covers of A<sub>*n*</sub> are superperfect for *n* = 6, 7.

As stem extensions of a simple group, the covering groups of A<sub>*n*</sub> are [quasisimple groups](wikipedia://en/1642729) for *n* ≥ 5.
