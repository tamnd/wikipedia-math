# Simple polytope

*Image: Associahedron K5.svg.* Three-dimensional [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446). Each vertex has three neighboring edges and faces, so this is a simple polyhedron.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simple polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a $d$-dimensional **simple polytope** is a $d$-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) each of whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are adjacent to exactly $d$ [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) (also $d$ [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647)). The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of a simple $d$-polytope is a $(d - 1)$-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781).

:::

Simple polytopes are topologically [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) to [simplicial polytopes](wikipedia://en/8853373). The family of polytopes which are both simple and simplicial are [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) or two-dimensional [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621). A *simple polyhedron* is a three-dimensional [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) whose vertices are adjacent to three edges and three faces. The dual to a simple polyhedron is a *simplicial polyhedron*, in which all faces are triangles.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Three-dimensional simple polyhedra include the [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) (including the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)), the regular [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) and [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), and, among the [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), the [truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/279651), [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529), [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), [truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879), [truncated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/376538), [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282), and [truncated icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/286534). They also include the [Goldberg polyhedra](wikipedia://en/39781140) and [fullerenes](wikipedia://en/10628), including the [chamfered tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamfered_tetrahedron), [chamfered cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamfered_cube), and [chamfered dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4472133). In general, any polyhedron can be made into a simple one by [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) its vertices of valence four or higher. For instance, [truncated trapezohedrons](wikipedia://en/3521844) are formed by truncating only the high-degree vertices of a trapezohedron; they are also simple.

Four-dimensional simple polytopes include the regular [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) and [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112). Simple [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) include the [truncated 5-cell](wikipedia://en/4046891), [truncated tesseract](wikipedia://en/4023176), [truncated 24-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_24-cell), [truncated 120-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_120-cell), and [duoprisms](wikipedia://en/2114155). All bitruncated, cantitruncated, or omnitruncated four-polytopes are simple.

Simple polytopes in higher dimensions include the *d*-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781), [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783), [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446), [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947), and all [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) polytopes.

:::

## Unique reconstruction {#Unique_reconstruction}

[Micha Perles](wikipedia://en/35294296) conjectured that a simple polytope is completely determined by its 1-skeleton; his conjecture was proven in 1987 by [Roswitha Blind](wikipedia://en/68449302) and Peter Mani-Levitska. [Gil Kalai](wikipedia://en/19709056) shortly after provided a simpler proof of this result based on the theory of [unique sink orientations](wikipedia://en/18476346).
