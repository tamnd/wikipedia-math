# John Morgan (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Morgan"}

**John Willard Morgan** (born March 21, 1946) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). He is a Professor Emeritus at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and a member of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Morgan received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1968 and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1969, both from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University).[^o4][^o6] His Ph.D. thesis, entitled *Stable tangential homotopy equivalences*, was written under the supervision of [Morton L. Curtis](wikipedia://en/32407013).[^o4][^o5] He was an instructor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1969 to 1972, and an assistant professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) from 1972 to 1974.[^o4][^o6][^o7] He has been on the faculty at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) since 1974, serving as the Chair of the Department of Mathematics from 1989 to 1991 and becoming Professor Emeritus in 2010.[^o4][^o6][^o7] Morgan is a member of the [Simons Center for Geometry and Physics](wikipedia://en/25568388) at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) and served as its founding director from 2009 to 2016.[^o6][^o7]

From 1974 to 1976, Morgan was a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship).[^o4] In 2008, he was awarded a [Gauss Lectureship](wikipedia://en/48440954) by the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973). In 2009 he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o7] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8] Morgan is a Member of the [European Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Morgan's best-known work deals with the topology of complex manifolds and algebraic varieties. In the 1970s, [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) developed the notion of a minimal model of a [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863).[^o10] One of the simplest examples of a differential graded algebra is the space of smooth differential forms on a smooth manifold, so that Sullivan was able to apply his theory to understand the topology of smooth manifolds. In the setting of [Kähler geometry](wikipedia://en/390538), due to the corresponding version of the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/404181#Poincaré_lemma), this differential graded algebra has a decomposition into holomorphic and anti-holomorphic parts. In collaboration with [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414), and Sullivan, Morgan used this decomposition to apply Sullivan's theory to study the topology of compact Kähler manifolds. Their primary result is that the real homotopy type of such a space is determined by its [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723). Morgan later extended this analysis to the setting of smooth complex algebraic varieties, using Deligne's formulation of [mixed Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/10297305) to extend the Kähler decomposition of smooth differential forms and of the exterior derivative.[^o11]

In 2002 and 2003, [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) posted three papers to the [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) which used [Richard Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911)'s theory of [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291) to apparently resolve the [geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642) in three-dimensional topology, of which the renowned [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798) is a special case.[^o12] Perelman's first two papers claimed to prove the geometrization conjecture; the third paper gives an argument which would obviate the technical work in the second half of the second paper in order to give a shortcut to prove the Poincaré conjecture.

Starting in 2003, and culminating in a 2008 publication, [Bruce Kleiner](wikipedia://en/8488020) and [John Lott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lott_%28mathematician%29) posted detailed annotations of Perelman's first two papers to their websites, covering his work on the proof of the geometrization conjecture.[^o13] In 2006, [Huai-Dong Cao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai-Dong_Cao) and [Xi-Ping Zhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi-Ping_Zhu) published an exposition of Hamilton and Perelman's works, also covering Perelman's first two articles.[^o14] In 2007, Morgan and [Gang Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_Tian) published a book on Perelman's first paper, the first half of his second paper, and his third paper. As such, they covered the proof of the Poincaré conjecture. In 2014, they published a book covering the remaining details for the geometrization conjecture. In 2006, Morgan gave a [plenary lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid), saying that Perelman's work had "now been thoroughly checked. He has proved the Poincaré conjecture."[^o15]
