# Infinitely near point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinitely near point"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), an **infinitely near point** of an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) *S* is a point on a surface obtained from *S* by repeatedly blowing up points. Infinitely near points of algebraic surfaces were introduced by (txt).[^o3]

:::

There are some other meanings of "infinitely near point". Infinitely near points can also be defined for higher-dimensional varieties: there are several inequivalent ways to do this, depending on what one is allowed to blow up. [Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) gave a definition of infinitely near points of [smooth varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_variety),[^o4] though these are not the same as infinitely near points in algebraic geometry. In the line of [hyperreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51429), an extension of the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) line, two points are called infinitely near if their difference is [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990).

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

When [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) is applied to a point *P* on a surface *S*, the new surface *S*\* contains a whole curve *C* where *P* used to be. The points of *C* have the geometric interpretation as the tangent directions at *P* to *S*. They can be called infinitely near to *P* as way of visualizing them on *S*, rather than *S*\*. More generally this construction can be iterated by blowing up a point on the new curve *C*, and so on.

An **infinitely near point** (of order *n*) *P*<sub>*n*</sub> on a surface *S*<sub>0</sub> is given by a sequence of points *P*<sub>0</sub>, *P*<sub>1</sub>,...,*P*<sub>*n*</sub> on surfaces *S*<sub>0</sub>, *S*<sub>1</sub>,...,*S*<sub>*n*</sub> such that *S*<sub>*i*</sub> is given by blowing up *S*<sub>*i*–1</sub> at the point *P*<sub>*i*–1</sub> and *P*<sub>*i*</sub> is a point of the surface *S*<sub>*i*</sub> with image *P*<sub>*i*–1</sub>.

In particular the points of the surface *S* are the infinitely near points on *S* of order 0.

Infinitely near points correspond to 1-dimensional [valuations](wikipedia://en/347049) of the function field of *S* with 0-dimensional center, and in particular correspond to some of the points of the [Zariski–Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski–Riemann_surface). (The 1-dimensional valuations with 1-dimensional center correspond to irreducible curves of *S*.) It is also possible to iterate the construction infinitely often, producing an infinite sequence *P*<sub>0</sub>, *P*<sub>1</sub>,... of infinitely near points. These infinite sequences correspond to the 0-dimensional valuations of the function field of the surface, which correspond to the "0-dimensional" points of the [Zariski–Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski–Riemann_surface).

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

If *C* and *D* are distinct irreducible curves on a smooth surface *S* intersecting at a point *p*, then the [multiplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_multiplicity) of their intersection at *p* is given by

$$\sum_{x \text{ infinitely near }p} m_x(C)m_x(D)$$

where *m*<sub>*x*</sub>(*C*) is the multiplicity of *C* at *x*. In general this is larger than *m*<sub>*p*</sub>(*C*)*m*<sub>*p*</sub>(*D*) if *C* and *D* have a common tangent line at *x* so that they also intersect at infinitely near points of order greater than 0, for example if *C* is the line *y* = 0 and *D* is the parabola *y* = *x*<sup>2</sup> and *p* = (0,0).

The [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_a_curve) of *C* is given by

$$g(C)=g(N)+\sum_{\text{infinitely near points }x}m_x(m_x-1)/2$$

where *N* is the [normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_of_an_algebraic_variety) of *C* and *m*<sub>*x*</sub> is the multiplicity of the infinitely near point *x* on *C*.
