# Von Neumann neighborhood

*Image: Von Neumann neighborhood.svg.* Manhattan distance r = 1

*Image: Manhattan\_distance\_r=2.svg.* Manhattan distance r = 2

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Neumann neighborhood"}

In [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata), the **von Neumann neighborhood** (or **4-neighborhood**) is classically defined on a two-dimensional [square lattice](wikipedia://en/2874853) and is composed of a central cell and its four adjacent cells.[^o2] The neighborhood is named after [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), who used it to define the [von Neumann cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/2234009) and the [von Neumann universal constructor](wikipedia://en/8597086) within it.[^o3] It is one of the two most commonly used neighborhood types for two-dimensional cellular automata, the other one being the [Moore neighborhood](wikipedia://en/4323874).

:::

This neighbourhood can be used to define the notion of [4-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-connected_neighborhood) [pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel) in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics).[^o4]

The von Neumann neighbourhood of a cell is the cell itself and the cells at a [Manhattan distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_distance) of 1.

The concept can be extended to higher dimensions, for example forming a 6-cell [octahedral](wikipedia://en/80177635) neighborhood for a cubic cellular automaton in three dimensions.[^o6]

## Von Neumann neighborhood of range *r* {#Von_Neumann_neighborhood_of_range_r}

An extension of the simple von Neumann neighborhood described above is to take the set of points at a [Manhattan distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_distance) of *r* > 1. This results in a diamond-shaped region (shown for *r* = 2 in the illustration). These are called von Neumann neighborhoods of range or extent *r*. The number of cells in a 2-dimensional von Neumann neighborhood of range *r* can be expressed as $r^2 + (r+1)^2$. The number of cells in a *d*-dimensional von Neumann neighborhood of range *r* is the [Delannoy number](wikipedia://en/14736250) *D*(*d*,*r*).[^o6] The number of cells on a surface of a *d*-dimensional von Neumann neighborhood of range *r* is the Zaitsev number .
