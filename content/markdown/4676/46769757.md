# Graphical lasso

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graphical lasso"}

In statistics, the **graphical lasso**[^o2] is a penalized likelihood [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043) for the [precision matrix](wikipedia://en/24467258) (also called the concentration matrix or inverse [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752)) of a multivariate [elliptical distribution](wikipedia://en/27014075). Through the use of an $L_1$ penalty, it performs [regularization](wikipedia://en/2009061) to give a sparse estimate for the precision matrix. In the case of [multivariate Gaussian distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Gaussian_distribution), sparsity in the precision matrix corresponds to [conditional independence](wikipedia://en/801135) between the variables therefore implying a Gaussian [graphical model](wikipedia://en/447298).

:::

The graphical lasso was originally formulated to solve Dempster's covariance selection problem[^o3][^o4] for the [multivariate Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Gaussian_distribution) when observations were limited. Subsequently, the optimization algorithms to solve this problem were improved[^o5] and extended[^o6] to other types of estimators and distributions.

## Setting {#Setting}

Let $S$ be the [sample covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/10005756#Definition_of_sample_covariance) of an [independent identically distributed](wikipedia://en/30876902) sample from a multivariate [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution) $X \sim N(\mu, \Sigma)$. We are interested in estimating the [precision matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_matrix) $\Sigma^{-1} = \Theta = (\Theta_{ij})$.

The graphical lasso estimator $\hat{\Theta}$ is the maximiser of the $L_1$ penalised [log-likelihood](wikipedia://en/44968#Log-likelihood):

$$\hat{\Theta} = \operatorname{argmax}_{\Theta \succ 0} \left(\log \det(\Theta) - \operatorname{tr}(S \Theta) - \lambda \sum_{i,j} |\Theta_{ij}| \right)$$

where $\lambda$ is a penalty parameter,[^o5] $\operatorname{tr}$ is the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) function and $\Theta \succ 0$ refers to the set of [positive definite matrices](wikipedia://en/40326).

A popular alternative form of the graphical lasso removes the penalty on the diagonal, only penalising the off-diagonal entries:[^o8]

$$\hat{\Theta} = \operatorname{argmax}_{\Theta \succ 0} \left(\log \det(\Theta) - \operatorname{tr}(S \Theta) - \lambda \sum_{i \neq j} |\Theta_{ij}| \right)$$

Because the graphical lasso estimate is not invariant to scalar multiplication of the variables,[^o9] it is important to [normalize](wikipedia://en/2978513) the data before applying the graphical lasso.

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

To obtain the estimator in programs, users could use the R package [glasso](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/glasso/glasso.pdf),[^o12] [GraphicalLasso() class](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.covariance.GraphicalLasso.html) in the [scikit-learn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scikit-learn) [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) library,[^o13] or the [skggm](https://github.com/skggm/skggm) Python package[^o14] (similar to scikit-learn).
