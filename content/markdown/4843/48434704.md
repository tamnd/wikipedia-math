# Roger Dingledine

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Dingledine"}

**Roger Dingledine** is an American computer scientist who co-founded [the Tor Project](wikipedia://en/43283728). An [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) graduate in mathematics, computer science, and electrical engineering, Dingledine is also known by the pseudonym **arma.**[^o3] he is Project Leader, Director, and Research Director at the Tor Project.[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Dingledine graduated from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) with [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degrees in Mathematics and Computer Science and Engineering in 2000. He later obtained a [Master of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Engineering) in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

### Tor Project {#Tor_Project}

Tor was developed by Dingledine[^o9]—with [Nick Mathewson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mathewson) and [Paul Syverson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Syverson)[^o4]—under a contract from the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory).[^o10] the software they developed was being distributed using proceeds from the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation), by the [Tor Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_Project).[^o10] As described at the end of 2015,

The Tor Project develops and maintains ... The Tor Browser system, also known as The Onion Router ... a free, open source and sophisticated privacy tool that provides anonymity for web surfing and communication as well as developing and maintaining other software tools and applications.[^o11][^o12]

## Publications and presentations {#Publications_and_presentations}

Dingledine has written several highly cited papers, including the Tor design paper titled **Tor: The Second-Generation Onion Router**,[^o14] which won the Usenix Security "Test of Time" award.[^o15] Other highly cited papers include [Mixminion](wikipedia://en/2070675)’s protocols for anonymous email,[^o16] the [Free Haven Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Haven_Project) distributed anonymous storage service,[^o17] various attacks and vulnerabilities related to anonymity technologies,[^o18][^o19] and the [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [network effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_effects) of technologies for anonymity.[^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23]

As an advocate for strong privacy, Dingledine is frequently invited to speak about security and privacy, including at academic conferences,[^o6] the [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (2014),[^o24] the [NSA](wikipedia://en/21939) (2007),[^o25] and periodic interviews.[^o26][^o27]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Dingledine was named as one of the 2006 thirty-five [Innovators Under 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovators_Under_35) by *[MIT Technology Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Technology_Review)*, for his work on internet [anonymization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymizer) technologies through the Tor Project.[^o29] The *Review* described the importance of the work in this way:

A dissident in China uses Web-based e-mail to contact a journalist in Canada. An intelligence agency wants to surveil a foreign website. Like every operation on the Internet, these activities leave tracks. Online anonymity measures provide a way around this problem; one of the most advanced is [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29), or the [Onion Router](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_Router). / Computer scientist Roger Dingledine developed Tor ...[^o29]

In 2012, Dingledine and the other two initial developers of Tor, [Nick Mathewson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Mathewson) and [Paul Syverson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Syverson), were recognized by [Foreign Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Policy) magazine as #78 in their top 100 global thinkers.[^o30]

## Media attention {#Media_attention}

Dingledine has drawn attention after the [leak of NSA documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leak_of_NSA_documents) by [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden), and public disclosure of the rules guiding the operation of [XKeyscore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XKeyscore), the NSA's collection system, given XKeyscore's targeting of Tor Project onion servers, including the one Dingledine runs at MIT, which serves a directory authority for the system, as well as being the base of operation of the [Mixminion](wikipedia://en/2070675) mail service, and host to various gaming and other websites (from which the [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) might be collecting IP addresses).[^o32]
