# Peter Landrock

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Landrock"}

**Peter Landrock** (born August 20, 1948 in [Horsens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsens)) is a Danish [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and mathematician. He is known for his contributions to data [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) methods and codes.[^o19] Landrock has been active since the 1970s as research scientist and faculty member for [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) and the [University of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aarhus) and others, and was active for [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) and [Cryptomathic](wikipedia://en/171385).[^o3][^o4] He has been visiting professor at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), [Leuven University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven)[^o5] and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o6]

:::

## Background and career {#Background_and_career}

Landrock obtained a diploma in mathematics and physics in 1972 from the [University of Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aarhus). He received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1974[^o8] for his research on elementary [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) and [dihedral](wikipedia://en/100267) defect groups, under [George Isaac Glauberman](wikipedia://en/9744285) and [Richard Dagobert Brauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dagobert_Brauer).[^o9] In 1975, Landrock became associate Professor in the Department of Mathematics at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University), then full Professor. From 1982 until 1983, Landrock was visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey.[^o6]

In 1986 he founded the company [Cryptomathic](wikipedia://en/171385) together with [Ivan Damgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Damgård). It was his research work on [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) at the [Isaac Newton Institute](wikipedia://en/736837), which inspired him to shift the focus of his work to corporate research at Cryptomathic,[^o4][^o8] where he joined forces with researchers such as [Vincent Rijmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Rijmen) and [Whitfield Diffie](wikipedia://en/427776).[^o10]

By 1996 he had joined the Isaac Newton Institute, Cambridge University as Research Program Organizer, and since 1997, Landrock has been senior member of the [Wolfson College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfson_College,_Cambridge) University.[^o3][^o4] Landrock has been member of the Danish IT Security Council as adviser to the Danish Government[^o8] from 1999 to 2007. From 1997 until 2010, Landrock was as a Member of Microsoft's Technical Advisory Board in Cambridge and has also served as a member of the board of the [Villum Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villum_Foundation) in Copenhagen since 2008. In 2014 Landrock became Member of the Technical Advisory Board of the Turing Gateway of Mathematics at Cambridge University.[^o3] In 2021, he was elected a By-Fellow of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge).[^o11]

## Cryptography {#Cryptography}

Landrock was President of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043) from 1992 to 1995[^o3] and General Chair at the [Eurocrypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocrypt) conference for cryptography research in 1990.[^o13] In 1996 he was one of the organizers of a research programme in Cryptography at the Newton Institute at University of Cambridge.[^o3] The term "What You See Is What You Sign" ([WYSIWYS](wikipedia://en/6187863)) was coined in 1998 by Landrock and Torben P. Pedersen of Cryptomathic during their work on delivering secure and legally binding digital signatures for Pan-European projects.[^o14]

Landrock contributed to more than twenty entries to the *[Encyclopedia of Cryptography and Security](wikipedia://en/19553737)* including articles on [PKCS](wikipedia://en/616418), [SSH](wikipedia://en/28814), [public key infrastructure](wikipedia://en/194112) and [certificate authorities](wikipedia://en/296370).[^o15] His research focus since the late 1980s included subject areas as [Key management](wikipedia://en/631063) systems,[^o16] [EMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMV) and Card Payment Solutions[^o17] and [Authentication](wikipedia://en/47967).[^o18]

He has lectured on cryptography at more than 150 Universities. The [European Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Office) recognized that Landrock's “inventions have helped secure electronic voting systems and electronic passport solutions”.[^o19]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

In 1991 Landrock was awarded the Danish Data Security Prize, and in 2004, Landrock received the BIT Price for engineering entrepreneurship from the Danish Engineers.[^o21]

His achievements with Cryptomathic were recognised by the [World Economic Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Economic_Forum) in 2003 and he received the [VISA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Inc.) Smart Start Award for the work on Chip and Pin.[^o4] In 2010, Landrock was named a finalist for European Inventor 2010 in the "Lifetime Achievement" category by the [European Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Office) stating that many of today’s established data encryption methods and codes “bear the mark of ... Peter Landrock”[^o19]

In July 2019, Landrock was awarded the degree of Doctor of Science honoris causa for his lifetime achievement in cryptographic technology.[^o22]
