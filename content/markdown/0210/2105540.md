# Peter Swinnerton-Dyer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Swinnerton-Dyer"}

**Sir Henry Peter Francis Swinnerton-Dyer, 16th Baronet**, (2 August 1927 – 26 December 2018) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). As a mathematician he was best known for his part in the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) relating algebraic properties of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) to special values of [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283), which was developed with [Bryan Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Birch) during the first half of the 1960s with the help of machine computation, and for his work on the [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_%281963_computer%29) operating system.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Swinnerton-Dyer was the son of Sir Leonard Schroeder Swinnerton Dyer, 15th [Baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baronet), and his wife Barbara, daughter of Hereward Brackenbury. He was educated at the [Dragon School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_School) in Oxford, [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he was supervised by [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood), and at some time in the late 1940s achieved the position of [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) on the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at the latter.[^o6] He also spent a year abroad as a [Commonwealth Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Fund) Fellow at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o7]

Swinnerton-Dyer was later made a fellow of Trinity, and was master of [St Catharine's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catharine's_College,_Cambridge) from 1973 to 1983 and [vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28education%29) of the University of Cambridge from 1979 to 1981. In 1983 he was made an [honorary fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Honorary_Fellows_of_St_Catharine's_College,_Cambridge) of St Catharine's.

In that same year, he became chairman of the [University Grants Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Grants_Committee_%28UK%29) and then from 1989, chief executive of its successor, the [Universities Funding Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_Funding_Council).

He was elected a [fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1967 and was made a [KBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) in 1987.[^o7] In 1981, he was awarded an honorary degree (Doctor of Science) by the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath).[^o8] In 1991, he received an honorary DSc degree from [Ulster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_University).[^o9] In 2006, he was awarded the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal),[^o10] and also the [Pólya Prize (LMS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29).[^o11]

Swinnerton-Dyer was, in his younger days, an international [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_bridge) player, representing the British team twice in the European Open teams championship. In 1953 at [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) he was partnered by Dimmie Fleming: the team came second out of fifteen teams. In 1962 he was partnered by Ken Barbour; the team came fourth out of twelve teams at [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut).[^o12]

He married [Harriet Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Crawford) in 1983.[^o13][^o14]

### Research {#Research}

[Atkin](wikipedia://en/3721430) and Swinnerton-Dyer verified and extended[^o16] several conjectures of [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397)[^o17] on congruences of the partition function p(n). Extensions of Dyson's musings on congruences of the partition function continue to be an active area of research.

In the late 1950s Swinnerton-Dyer began a collaboration[^o18][^o19] with [Bryan Birch](wikipedia://en/4949339), exploring ideas of [Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) and [Tamagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuneo_Tamagawa) in the context of elliptic curves over the rationals. They did extensive computations of elliptic curves on the Cambridge EDSAC-2, which led them to their famous [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).

Swinnerton-Dyer[^o20] was the first to construct a cubic surface over $\mathbb{Q}$ for which the [Hasse principle](wikipedia://en/489707) provably fails. [Ernst S. Selmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_S._Selmer) had previously shown by example that the [Hasse–Minkowski theorem](wikipedia://en/1109126) cannot be extended to forms of degree 3.[^o21]

Atkin and Swinnerton-Dyer initiated the study of the arithmetic properties of modular forms for noncongruence subgroups in the late 1960s, and observed very interesting congruence relations for such forms. They conjectured that[^o22] if a modular form $f(\tau)$ is not modular for some [congruence subgroup](wikipedia://en/382694) of the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019), then the Fourier coefficients of $f(\tau)$ have unbounded denominators, which became known as the Atkin-Swinnerton-Dyer unbounded denominators conjecture. The conjecture was proved by [Frank Calegari](wikipedia://en/63299027), Vesselin Dimitrov and [Yunqing Tang](wikipedia://en/72206056) in 2021. [^o23]

### Death {#Death}

Swinnerton-Dyer died on 26 December 2018 at the age of 91.[^o25]
