# Differentiable measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differentiable measure"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), a **differentiable measure** is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) that has a notion of a [derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28mathematics%29). The theory of differentiable measure was introduced by Russian mathematician [Sergei Fomin](wikipedia://en/3017297) and proposed at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1966 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) as an infinite-dimensional analog of the theory of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29).[^o2] Besides the notion of a derivative of a measure by Sergei Fomin there exists also one by [Anatoliy Skorokhod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Skorokhod),[^o3] one by [Sergio Albeverio](wikipedia://en/28869024) and [Raphael Høegh-Krohn](wikipedia://en/28711790), and one by [Oleg Smolyanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Smolyanov) and Heinrich von Weizsäcker.[^o4]

:::

## Differentiable measure {#Differentiable_measure}

Let

- $X$ be a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370),
- $\mathcal{A}$ be σ-algebra that is invariant under translation by vectors $h\in X$, i.e. $A +th\in \mathcal{A}$ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}$.

This setting is rather general on purpose since for most definitions only linearity and measurability is needed. But usually one chooses $X$ to be a real [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [locally convex space](wikipedia://en/518844) with the Borel or cylindrical σ-algebra $\mathcal{A}$.

For a measure $\mu$ let $\mu_h(A):=\mu(A+h)$ denote the shifted measure by $h\in X$.

### Fomin differentiability {#Fomin_differentiability}

A measure $\mu$ on $(X,\mathcal{A})$ is **Fomin differentiable** along $h\in X$ if for every set $A\in\mathcal{A}$ the limit

$$d_{h}\mu(A):=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{\mu(A+th)-\mu(A)}{t}$$

exists. We call $d_{h}\mu$ the *Fomin derivative* of $\mu$.

Equivalently, for all sets $A\in\mathcal{A}$ is $f_{\mu}^{A,h}:t\mapsto \mu(A+th)$ differentiable in $0$.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

- The Fomin derivative is again another measure and [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) with respect to $\mu$.
- Fomin differentiability can be directly extend to [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811).
- Higher and mixed derivatives will be defined inductively $d^n_{h}=d_{h}(d^{n-1}_{h})$.

:::

### Skorokhod differentiability {#Skorokhod_differentiability}

Let $\mu$ be a [Baire measure](wikipedia://en/13520509) and let $C_b(X)$ be the space of bounded and continuous functions on $X$.

$\mu$ is **Skorokhod differentiable** (or *S-differentiable*) along $h\in X$ if a Baire measure $\nu$ exists such that for all $f\in C_b(X)$ the limit

$$\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\int_X\frac{ f(x-th)-f(x)}{t}\mu(dx)=\int_X f(x)\nu(dx)$$

exists.

In shift notation

$$\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\int_X\frac{ f(x-th)-f(x)}{t}\mu(dx)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\int_Xf\; d\left(\frac{\mu_{th}-\mu}{t}\right).$$

The measure $\nu$ is called the *Skorokhod derivative* (or *S-derivative* or *weak derivative*) of $\mu$ along $h\in X$ and is unique.[^o7][^o11]

### Albeverio-Høegh-Krohn Differentiability {#Albeverio-Høegh-Krohn_Differentiability}

A measure $\mu$ is **Albeverio-Høegh-Krohn differentiable** (or *AHK differentiable*) along $h\in X$ if a measure $\lambda\geq 0$ exists such that

1. $\mu_{th}$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\lambda$ such that $\lambda_{th}=f_t\cdot \lambda$,
1. the map $g:\mathbb{R}\to L^2(\lambda),\; t\mapsto f_{t}^{1/2}$ is differentiable.[^o7]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

- The AHK differentiability can also be extended to signed measures.

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let $\mu$ be a measure with a continuously differentiable [Radon-Nikodým density](wikipedia://en/338746) $g$, then the Fomin derivative is

$$d_{h}\mu(A)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{\mu(A+th)-\mu(A)}{t}=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\int_A\frac{g(x+th)-g(x)}{t}\mathrm{d}x=\int_A g'(x)\mathrm{d}x.$$

:::
