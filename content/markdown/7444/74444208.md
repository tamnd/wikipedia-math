# Paul Couderc

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Couderc"}

**Paul Couderc** (15 July 1899 – 5 February 1981) was a French academic who held [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) [professorships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship) at lycées in [Chartres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartres) (1926–1929) and [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) (1930–1944).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Couderc completed his education at lycées in [Nevers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevers) and [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon), followed by a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in mathematical sciences from the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure) in Paris.[^o5] In 1926, he married Blanch Jurus.[^o5]

Throughout his career, Couderc authored approximately fifteen works in the field of [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy).[^o5] He provided an interpretation for the phenomena of light echoes around Nova Persei (1901), specifically their perceived superluminal expansion.[^o5] This geometrical explanation later found application in the study of [supernovae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernovae), [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasar), and [γ-ray bursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-ray_burst).[^o5]
