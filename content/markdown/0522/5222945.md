# Polynomial lemniscate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polynomial lemniscate"}

*Image: Cyc7.png.* $|z^6+z^5+z^4+z^3+$ $z^2+z+1|=1$

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **polynomial lemniscate** or *polynomial level curve* is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of degree 2n, constructed from a polynomial *p* with complex coefficients of degree *n*.

:::

For any such polynomial *p* and positive real number *c*, we may define a set of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) by $|p(z)| = c.$ This set of numbers may be equated to points in the real Cartesian plane, leading to an algebraic curve *ƒ*(*x*, *y*) = *c*<sup>2</sup> of degree 2*n*, which results from expanding out $p(z) \bar p(\bar z)$ in terms of *z* = *x* + *iy*.

When *p* is a polynomial of degree 1 then the resulting curve is simply a circle whose center is the zero of *p*. When *p* is a polynomial of degree 2 then the curve is a [Cassini oval](wikipedia://en/2040906).

## Erdős lemniscate {#Erdős_lemniscate}

*Image: Erdos5.png.* Erdős lemniscate of degree ten and genus six

A conjecture of [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) which has attracted considerable interest concerns the maximum [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of a polynomial lemniscate *ƒ*(*x*, *y*) = 1 of degree 2*n* when *p* is [monic](wikipedia://en/314730), which Erdős conjectured was attained when *p*(*z*) = z<sup>*n*</sup> − 1. This is still not proved but Fryntov and [Nazarov](wikipedia://en/21079027) proved that *p* gives a local maximum.[^o3] In the case when *n* = 2, the Erdős lemniscate is the [Lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908)

$$(x^2+y^2)^2=2(x^2-y^2)\,$$

and it has been proven that this is indeed the maximal length in degree four. The Erdős lemniscate has three ordinary *n*-fold points, one of which is at the origin, and a [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) of (*n* − 1)(*n* − 2)/2. By [inverting](wikipedia://en/295844) the Erdős lemniscate in the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071), one obtains a nonsingular curve of degree *n*.

## Generic polynomial lemniscate {#Generic_polynomial_lemniscate}

In general, a polynomial lemniscate will not touch at the origin, and will have only two ordinary *n*-fold singularities, and hence a genus of (*n* − 1)<sup>2</sup>. As a real curve, it can have a number of disconnected components. Hence, it will not look like a [lemniscate](wikipedia://en/5210590), making the name something of a misnomer.

An interesting example of such polynomial lemniscates are the Mandelbrot curves. If we set *p*<sub>0</sub> = *z*, and *p*<sub>*n*</sub> = *p*<sub>*n*−1</sub><sup>2</sup> + *z*, then the corresponding polynomial lemniscates M<sub>n</sub> defined by |*p*<sub>*n*</sub>(*z*)| = 2 converge to the boundary of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562).[^o5] The Mandelbrot curves are of degree 2<sup>n+1</sup>.[^o6]
