# Claude LeBrun

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Claude LeBrun"}

**Claude R. LeBrun** (born 1956) is an American mathematician who holds the position of Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University). Much of his research concerns the [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/490861) of 4-manifolds, or related topics in complex and differential geometry.

:::

After enrolling as an undergraduate at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) at age 16, LeBrun received his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in mathematics from [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) in 1977. He then went on to earn his [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (Oxford equivalent of a Ph.D.) from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1980, at the age of 23, for a thesis on complex differential geometry written under the supervision of [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193). That same year, he then accepted his first faculty position at Stony Brook.[^o4] Although he would eventually go on to spend most of his career at Stony Brook, he has also held positions at a number of other institutions, including the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o5]

He is the namesake of the [LeBrun manifolds](wikipedia://en/35068126), a family of self-dual manifolds that he discovered in 1989 and that was named after him by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894).[^o6] LeBrun is also known for his work on [Einstein manifolds](wikipedia://en/644814) and the [Yamabe invariant](wikipedia://en/12783357). In particular, he produced examples showing that the [converse](wikipedia://en/155941#Converse_of_a_theorem) of the [Hitchin–Thorpe inequality](wikipedia://en/22283794) does not hold: there exist infinitely many four-dimensional compact smooth simply connected [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) that obey the inequality but do not admit Einstein metrics.

LeBrun was an invited speaker at the 1994 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o4] In 2012, he became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7] In 2016, a conference in his honor was held at the Centre de Recherches Mathématiques in Montreal.[^o8] In 2018, he became a Simons Foundation Fellow in Mathematics.[^o9] In 2020, he was appointed as Distinguished Professor at the State University of New York.[^o10] In 2025, he became a Simons Foundation Fellow in Mathematics for the second time,[^o11] while also becoming a laureate of the Chaires d'Excellence program of the Fondation Sciences Mathématiques de Paris.[^o12] In 2026, a conference in his honor [^o13] was held at the International Center for Theoretical Physics in Trieste, Italy.
