# Capsule (geometry)

*Image: Capsule\_geometry.svg.* A two-dimensional [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/313741#Orthographic_projection) at the left with a three-dimensional one at the right depicting a capsule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Capsule"}

A **capsule** (la), or **[stadium](wikipedia://en/38383047) of revolution**, is a basic [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3054853) [geometric shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_shape) consisting of a [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) with [hemispherical](wikipedia://en/27859#Hemisphere) ends.[^o2] Another name for this shape is **spherocylinder**.[^o3][^o8][^o4][^o5]

:::

It can also be referred to as an [oval](wikipedia://en/2027249) although the sides (either vertical or horizontal) are straight [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497).

## Usages {#Usages}

The shape is used for some objects like [containers for pressurised gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_vessel#Cylindrical_vessel_with_hemispherical_ends), [building domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome), and [pharmaceutical capsules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsule_%28pharmacy%29).

In [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), this shape is used as a basic model for non-spherical particles. It appears, in particular as a model for the molecules in [liquid crystals](wikipedia://en/17973)[^o7][^o8][^o4] or for the particles in [granular matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material).[^o5][^o13][^o14]

::: {#o10 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

The volume $V$ of a capsule is calculated by adding the volume of a ball of radius $r$ (that accounts for the two hemispheres) to the volume of the cylindrical part. Hence, if the cylinder has height $h$,

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 + (\pi r^2h)= \pi r^2 \left (\frac{4}{3}r + h \right ).$$

The surface area of a capsule of radius $r$ whose cylinder part has height $h$ is $2 \pi r (2r + h)$.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A capsule can be equivalently described as the [Minkowski sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_sum) of a ball of radius $r$ with a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) of length $a$.[^o5] By this description, capsules can be straightforwardly generalized as Minkowski sums of a ball with a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). The resulting shape is called a spheropolyhedron.[^o13][^o14]

:::

## Related shapes {#Related_shapes}

A capsule is the three-dimensional shape obtained by revolving the two-dimensional [stadium](wikipedia://en/38383047) around the [line of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_symmetry) that [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831).
