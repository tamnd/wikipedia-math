# Stadium (geometry)

*Image: Stadium\_geometry.svg.* Parameters of a stadium

*Image: BunimovichStadium.svg.* The [Bunimovich stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunimovich_stadium), a [chaotic](wikipedia://en/6295) [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) based on the stadium shape

*Image: Plastic laundry basket.jpg.* The bottom of this plastic [basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket) is stadium-shaped.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stadium"}

A **stadium** is a two-dimensional [geometric shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_shape) constructed of a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) with [semicircles](wikipedia://en/617831) at a pair of opposite sides. The same shape is known also as a **pill shape**,[^o3] **discorectangle**,[^o4] **obround**,[^o5][^o6] **racetrack** or **sausage body**.[^o7]

:::

The shape is based on a [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium), a place used for [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_track) and [horse racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_racing) tracks.

A stadium may be constructed as the [Minkowski sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_sum) of a [disk](wikipedia://en/329542) and a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860).[^o7] Alternatively, it is the [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) of points within a given distance from a line segment. A stadium is a type of [oval](wikipedia://en/2027249). However, unlike some other ovals such as the [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277), it is not an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) because different parts of its boundary are defined by different equations.

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of a stadium is calculated by the formula $P = 2 (\pi r+a)$ where *a* is the length of the straight sides and *r* is the radius of the semicircles. With the same parameters, the [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_%28geometry%29) of the stadium is $A = \pi r^2 + 2ra = r(\pi r + 2a)$.[^o10]

:::

## Bunimovich stadium {#Bunimovich_stadium}

When this shape is used in the study of [dynamical billiards](wikipedia://en/2160429), it is called the [Bunimovich stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunimovich_stadium). [Leonid Bunimovich](wikipedia://en/2652937) used this shape to show that it is possible for billiard tracks to exhibit [chaotic behavior](wikipedia://en/6295) (positive [Lyapunov exponent](wikipedia://en/166441) and exponential divergence of paths) even within a convex billiard table.[^o12]

## Related shapes {#Related_shapes}

A *[capsule](wikipedia://en/38383335)* is produced by revolving a stadium around the [line of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_symmetry) that [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the semicircles.
