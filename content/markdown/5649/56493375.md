# Yvonne Dold-Samplonius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yvonne Dold-Samplonius"}

*Image: Frei Milnor Dold-Samplonius Dold.jpg.* *From left:* [Günther Frei](wikipedia://en/64441855), [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), Yvonne Dold-Samplonius, [Albrecht Dold](wikipedia://en/31337135), Zürich 2007

**Yvonne Dold-Samplonius** (20 May 1937 – 16 June 2014) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian](wikipedia://en/14220) who specialised in the history of Islamic mathematics during the Middle Ages. She was particularly interested in the mathematical methods used by Islamic architects and builders of the Middle Ages for measurements of volumes and measurements of religious buildings or in the design of [muqarnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqarnas).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born on 20 May 1937 in [Haarlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem), Yvonne Samplonius obtained her degree in mathematics and Arabic from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) (Doktoratsexamen) in 1966.[^o3] In 1965 Yvonne Dold-Samplonius married the German mathematician [Albrecht Dold](wikipedia://en/31337135). During 1966 and 1967, she studied at Harvard University under the direction of Professor [John E. Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_E._Murdoch).[^o3] In 1977 she was awarded a PhD for her analysis of the treatise *Kitāb al-mafrādāt li Aqāţun* (Book of Assumptions of Aq*ā*tun) under the supervision of Prof. Evert Marie Bruins and Prof. Juan Vernet.[^o4][^o5]

She came into contact with the work of the Persian mathematician, physicist and astronomer [Abū Sahl al-Qūhī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_Sahl_al-Qūhī), who worked in Baghdad in the 10th century and worked on the geometrical forms of buildings.[^o6] Through his work, she became interested in the geometrical calculations that helped with the building of many of the domes of palaces and mosques, called [muqarnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqarnas), in the Arab world and Persia.[^o7][^o8][^o11] She wrote articles on the Islamic mathematicians [Jamshīd al-Kāshī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshīd_al-Kāshī) and [Abu-Abdullah Muhammad ibn Īsa Māhānī](wikipedia://en/1782729) in the Dictionary of the Middle Ages and in the Dictionary of Scientific Biography.[^o9][^o10]

In her later years her interest shifted to the use of mathematics in Islamic architecture from an historic point of view.[^o11][^o12] From 1995, she was an associate member of the [Interdisciplinary Centre for Scientific Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary_Center_for_Scientific_Computing) (IWR) of the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), with whom she has published several videos on Islamic geometrical art.[^o13][^o14] In 1985, she was a visiting professor at the [University of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Siena). In 2000, she organised with [Joseph Dauben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Dauben) the conference "2000 Years of Transmission of Mathematical Ideas".[^o15] In 2002, she became a Corresponding Member of the [International Academy of the History of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_the_History_of_Science) and was elected an effective member in 2007. She was made honorary citizen of [Kashan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashan) in Iran in 2000.[^o13]
