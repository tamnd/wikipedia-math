# Enneahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Enneahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **enneahedron** (or **nonahedron**) is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with nine [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). There are 2606 types of [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) enneahedra, each having a different pattern of vertex, edge, and face connections.[^o2] None of them are [regular](wikipedia://en/333299).

## Space-filling enneahedra {#Space-filling_enneahedra}

*Image: Apsis Onze Lieve Vrouwkerk Maastricht.jpg.* The [Basilica of Our Lady (Maastricht)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_%28Maastricht%29), whose enneahedral tower tops form a space-filling polyhedron.

Slicing a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) in half through the long diagonals of four of its faces results in a self-dual enneahedron, the square [diminished trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/40097376), with one large square face, four rhombus faces, and four isosceles triangle faces. Like the rhombic dodecahedron itself, this shape can be used to [tessellate](wikipedia://en/321671) three-dimensional space.[^o6] An elongated form of this shape that still tiles space can be seen atop the rear side towers of the 12th-century Romanesque [Basilica of Our Lady (Maastricht)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_%28Maastricht%29). The towers themselves, with their four pentagonal sides, four roof facets, and square base, form another space-filling enneahedron.

More generally, (Goldberg 1982) found at least 40 topologically distinct space-filling enneahedra.

## Topologically distinct enneahedra {#Topologically_distinct_enneahedra}

There are 2606 topologically distinct *convex* enneahedra, excluding mirror images. These can be divided into subsets of 8, 74, 296, 633, 768, 558, 219, 50, with 7 to 14 vertices, respectively.[^o9] A table of these numbers, together with a detailed description of the nine-vertex enneahedra, was first published in the 1870s by [Thomas Kirkman](wikipedia://en/7803789).
