# Ernst Christian Julius Schering

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Christian Julius Schering"}

**Ernst Christian Julius Schering** (13 July 1833 – 2 November 1897) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Born in 1833 near [Bleckede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleckede) at the [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) as the son of a forester, he attended [Realschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realschule) ("Johanneum") in [Lüneburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lüneburg) from 1845 to 1850, where he already showed a certain talent for mathematics. With the intention to engage in architectural engineering, he attended the [Hannover Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hanover) from 1850 to 1852.

## At the University of Göttingen {#At_the_University_of_Göttingen}

Following a fondness of mathematics and physics, in 1852, at the age of 19, he moved on to study in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), attending classes by [Gauß](wikipedia://en/6125), [Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Eduard_Weber), [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002), [Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Abraham_Stern) and [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980). In 1857, he received a [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree) for his award-winning manuscript "Zur mathematischen Theorie electrischer Ströme" ("On the mathematical theory of electric currents"). In 1858, he [habilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) based on his work "Über die conforme Abbildung des Ellipsoids auf der Ebene" ("On the conformal mapping of the ellipsoid on the plane").

After turning down a call to [Gießen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gießen) in 1860, he was appointed associate professor, and he became a member of the [Königliche Akademie der Wissenschaften zu Göttingen (Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) in 1862. From 1860 onwards he was also appointed by the Göttingen Academy to edit the works of [Gauß](wikipedia://en/6125), of which he eventually completed six volumes.

During the 1860s he was appointed by the hanoverian government as a member of a commission for the European [arc measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_measurement). In 1868, management of the [Göttingen Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Observatory) was divided into two divisions, one for practical astronomy and one for theoretical astronomy, [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608) and mathematical physics, of which Schering took over the former division from [Wilhelm Klinkerfues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Klinkerfues) and was appointed full professor. Among other observations, he was particularly busy with magnetic observations in connection with the polar expeditions in 1882/1883.

## Family {#Family}

His younger brother Karl Schering also studied physics and mathematics in Göttingen. He later became professor of mathematics at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) and subsequently professor of physics at the [Technical University of Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Darmstadt). His wife, Maria Heliodora, whom he married in 1876, was the daughter of the Swedish mathematician [Carl Johan Malmsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Johan_Malmsten). They had one daughter and two sons, one of which, physicist [Harald Schering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Schering), would later become professor of electrical engineering at the [Technische Hochschule Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Hannover).
