# Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities"}

The **Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities** (**ARIC**) Study is a multi-site, prospective, biracial [cohort study](wikipedia://en/397271) funded by the [National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Heart,_Lung,_and_Blood_Institute) of the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health).[^o2][^o3][^o4] The ARIC Study was designed to investigate the etiology and clinical outcomes of [atherosclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherosclerosis). A total of 15,792 middle-aged (45–64 years) men and women were enrolled from four U.S. communities:

:::

- [Forsyth County, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forsyth_County,_North_Carolina)
- [Jackson, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi)
- Suburbs of [Minneapolis, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis,_Minnesota)
- [Washington County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Maryland)

Participating organizations include four field centers ([Wake Forest Baptist Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_Baptist_Medical_Center), [University of Mississippi Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mississippi_Medical_Center), [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University)), data coordinating center ([University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill)), and many collaborating centers and laboratories.

The initial study visit took place in 1987-1989, the second in 1990-1992, the third in 1993-1995, the fourth in 1996-1998, and the fifth in 2011-2013. During these extensive examinations, information is obtained on demographics, medical history, medication use, and health behaviors.

To date, the ARIC study has published over 2,300 peer-reviewed journal articles in diverse areas of clinical and population research. Data from the ARIC study have become an important resource for the study of heart disease, [kidney disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_kidney_disease), [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus), and [cognitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive) decline. These data have also contributed to clinical practice guidelines and policy statements. [^o5] ARIC is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov registry under trial identifier NCT00005131.[^o6]
