# Carnot's theorem (inradius, circumradius)

*Image: Carnot theorem2.svg.* $|DG| + |DH| - |DF| = R + r$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carnot's theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **Carnot's theorem** ( , ) states that the sum of the [signed distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_distance) from the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) $D$ to the sides of an arbitrary triangle $ABC$ is

$$DF + DG + DH = R + r,\$$

:::

where $r$ is the [inradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inradius) and $R$ is the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) of the triangle. Here the sign of the [distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distances) is taken to be negative if and only if the open [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) $DX$ (for $X$ equal to $F$, $G$, or $H$) lies completely outside the triangle. For example, in the diagram, $DF$ is negative and both $DG$ and $DH$ are positive. The theorem can be viewed as saying that the sum of the actual-distance [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of the circumcenter is $R+r$.

The theorem is named after [Lazare Carnot](wikipedia://en/26532764) (1753–1823). It is used in a proof of the [Japanese theorem for concyclic polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_theorem_for_concyclic_polygons).
