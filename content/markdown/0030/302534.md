# Claude Gaspar Bachet de Méziriac

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Claude Gaspar Bachet de Méziriac"}

**Claude Gaspar Bachet Sieur de Méziriac[^o11]** (9 October 1581 – 26 February 1638) was a French mathematician and poet born in [Bourg-en-Bresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourg-en-Bresse), at that time belonging to [Duchy of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy).[^o7] He wrote Problèmes plaisans et délectables qui se font par les nombres,[^o3] Les éléments arithmétiques,[^o4] and a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) translation of the *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)* of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) (the very translation where Fermat wrote a margin note about [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953)). He also discovered means of solving [indeterminate equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equation) using [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199), a method of constructing [magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202), and a proof of [Bézout's identity](wikipedia://en/4947).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Diophantus-cover.png.* Title page of the 1621 edition of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110)' *Arithmetica*, translated into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) by Claude Gaspard Bachet de Méziriac.

Claude Gaspar Bachet de Méziriac was born in [Bourg-en-Bresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourg-en-Bresse) on 9 October 1581. By the time he reached the age of six, both his mother (Marie de Chavanes) and his father (Jean Bachet) had died. He was then looked after by the Jesuit Order. For a year in 1601, Bachet was a member of the [Jesuit Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_Order) (he left due to an illness).[^o7]

Bachet lived a comfortable life in Bourg-en-Bresse. He married Philiberte de Chabeu in 1620 and had seven children.[^o7]

Bachet was a pupil of the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit_Order) mathematician [Jacques de Billy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_de_Billy) at the Jesuit College in [Rheims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheims). They became close friends.[^o6]

Bachet wrote the *Problèmes plaisans et délectables qui se font par les nombres*[^o3] of which the first edition was issued in 1612, a second and enlarged edition was brought out in 1624; this contains an interesting collection of arithmetical tricks and questions, many of which are quoted in [W. W. Rouse Ball](wikipedia://en/308290)'s *Mathematical Recreations and Essays*.[^o7][^o8]

He also wrote *Les éléments arithmétiques*,[^o4] which exists in manuscript; and a translation, from Greek to Latin, of the *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)* of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) (1621). It was this very translation in which [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat) wrote his famous margin note claiming that he had a proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953). The same text renders Diophantus' term παρισὀτης as *adaequalitat*, which became Fermat's technique of [adequality](wikipedia://en/30609033), a pioneering method of [infinitesimal calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus).[^o9]

Bachet was the earliest writer who discussed the solution of [indeterminate equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equations) by means of [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199). He also did work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and found a method of constructing [magic squares](wikipedia://en/52202).[^o8] In the second edition of his *Problèmes plaisants* (1624) he gives a proof of [Bézout's identity](wikipedia://en/4947) (as proposition XVIII) 142 years before it got published by Bézout.[^o10][^o11]

He was elected member of the [Académie française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_française) in 1635.[^o7]
