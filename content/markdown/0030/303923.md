# James Mercer (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Mercer"}

**James Mercer** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o2] (15 January 1883 – 21 February 1932) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), born in [Bootle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootle), close to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool), England.

:::

He was educated at [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), and then [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He became a Fellow, saw active service at the [Battle of Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jutland) in World War I and, after decades of ill health, died in London.

He proved [Mercer's theorem](wikipedia://en/303990), which states that [positive-definite kernels](wikipedia://en/10273855) can be expressed as a [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) in a high-dimensional [space](wikipedia://en/27667#Mathematics). This theorem is the basis of the [kernel trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_trick) ([applied by Aizerman](wikipedia://en/41485209)), which allows linear algorithms to be easily converted into non-linear algorithms.
