# W. W. Rouse Ball

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="W. W. Rouse Ball"}

**Walter William Rouse Ball** (14 August 1850 – 4 April 1925), known as **W. W. Rouse Ball**, was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), lawyer, and fellow at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), from 1878 to 1905. He was also a keen amateur magician, and the founding president of the Cambridge [Pentacle Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentacle_Club) in 1919, one of the world's oldest magic societies.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born 14 August 1850 in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), Ball was the son and heir of Walter Frederick Ball, of 3, St John's Park Villas, South Hampstead, London. Educated at [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School), he entered [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), in 1870, became a scholar and first [Smith's Prizeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize), and gained his BA in 1874 as second [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29). He became a Fellow of Trinity in 1875, and remained one for the rest of his life.

He died on 4 April 1925 in Elmside, [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge),[^o4] and is buried at the [Parish of the Ascension Burial Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension_Parish_Burial_Ground,_Cambridge) in Cambridge.[^o7]

He is commemorated in the naming of the small pavilion, now used as changing rooms and toilets, on [Jesus Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Green) in Cambridge.
