# De Sitter space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Sitter space"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), *n*-dimensional **de Sitter space** (often denoted dS<sub>*n*</sub>) is a maximally symmetric [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold) with constant positive [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622). It is analogue of an [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782), with a [Lorentzian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_metric) in place of the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) of the latter.

:::

The main application of de Sitter space is its use in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), where it serves as one of the simplest mathematical models of the universe consistent with the observed [accelerating expansion of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accelerating_expansion_of_the_universe). More specifically, de Sitter space is the maximally symmetric [vacuum solution](wikipedia://en/1441464) of [Einstein's field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein's_field_equations) in which the [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992) $\Lambda$ is positive (corresponding to a positive vacuum energy density and negative pressure).

De Sitter space and [anti-de&nbsp;Sitter space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-de&nbsp;Sitter_space) are named after [Willem de&nbsp;Sitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de&nbsp;Sitter) (1872–1934),[^o2][^o3] professor of astronomy at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) and director of the [Leiden Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_Observatory). Willem de Sitter and [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) worked closely together in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden) in the 1920s on the spacetime structure of the universe. De Sitter space was also discovered, independently, and about the same time, by [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A de Sitter space can be defined as a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a generalized [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) of one higher [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398), including the induced metric. Take Minkowski space **R**<sup>1,*n*</sup> with the standard [metric](wikipedia://en/195795):

$$ds^2 = -dx_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n dx_i^2.$$

The *n*-dimensional de Sitter space is the submanifold described by the [hyperboloid](wikipedia://en/145844) of one sheet

$$-x_0^2 + \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 = \alpha^2,$$

where $\alpha$ is some nonzero constant with its dimension being that of length. The induced [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) on the de Sitter space is induced from the ambient Lorentzian metric. It is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate) and has Lorentzian signature. (If one replaces $\alpha^2$ with $-\alpha^2$ in the above definition, one obtains a [hyperboloid](wikipedia://en/145844) of two sheets. The induced metric in this case is [positive-definite](wikipedia://en/20437320), and each sheet is a copy of [hyperbolic *n*-space](wikipedia://en/318742). See *Minkowski space#Geometry*.)

The de Sitter space can also be defined as the [quotient](wikipedia://en/363325) O(1, *n*) / O(1, *n* − 1) of two [indefinite orthogonal groups](wikipedia://en/293609), which shows that it is a non-Riemannian [symmetric space](wikipedia://en/1642877).

[Topologically](wikipedia://en/29954), dS<sub>*n*</sub> is **R** × *S*<sup>*n*−1</sup>, which is [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) if *n* ≥ 3.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) of de Sitter space is the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489) O(1, *n*). The metric therefore then has *n*(*n* + 1)/2 independent [Killing vector fields](wikipedia://en/1025455) and is maximally symmetric. Every maximally symmetric space has constant curvature. The [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) of de Sitter space is given by[^o9]

$$R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = {1 \over \alpha^2}\left(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\right)$$

(using the sign convention $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_{\mu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\sigma}$ for the Riemann curvature tensor). De Sitter space is an [Einstein manifold](wikipedia://en/644814) since the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) is proportional to the metric:

$$R_{\mu\nu} = R^\lambda{}_{\mu\lambda\nu} = \frac{n - 1}{\alpha^2}g_{\mu\nu}$$

This means de Sitter space is a vacuum solution of Einstein's equation with cosmological constant given by

$$\Lambda = \frac{(n - 1)(n - 2)}{2\alpha^2}.$$

The [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) of de Sitter space is given by[^o9]

$$R = \frac{n(n - 1)}{\alpha^2} = \frac{2n}{n - 2}\Lambda.$$

For the case *n* = 4, we have Λ = 3/*α*<sup>2</sup> and *R* = 4Λ = 12/*α*<sup>2</sup>.

:::

## Coordinates {#Coordinates}

### Static coordinates {#Static_coordinates}

We can introduce [static coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Static_spacetime) $(t, r, \ldots)$ for de Sitter as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \sqrt{\alpha^2 - r^2}\sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \\ x_1 &= \sqrt{\alpha^2 - r^2}\cosh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \\ x_i &= r z_i \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad 2 \le i \le n, \end{aligned}$$

where $z_i$ gives the standard embedding the (*n* − 2)-sphere in **R**<sup>*n*−1</sup>. In these coordinates the de Sitter metric takes the form:

$$ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{\alpha^2}\right)dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{r^2}{\alpha^2}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega_{n-2}^2.$$

Note that there is a [cosmological horizon](wikipedia://en/2848730) at $r = \alpha$.

### Flat slicing {#Flat_slicing}

Let

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \alpha \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) + \frac{1}{2\alpha}r^2 e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}t}, \\ x_1 &= \alpha \cosh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) - \frac{1}{2\alpha}r^2 e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}t}, \\ x_i &= e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}t}y_i, \qquad 2 \leq i \leq n \end{aligned}$$

where $r^2 = \sum_i y_i^2$. Then in the $\left(t, y_i\right)$ coordinates metric reads:

$$ds^{2} = -dt^{2} + e^{2\frac{1}{\alpha}t} dy^{2}$$

where $dy^2 = \sum_i dy_i^2$ is the flat metric on $y_i$'s.

Setting $\zeta = \zeta_{\infty} - \alpha e^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}t}$, we obtain the conformally flat metric:

$$ds^2 = \frac{\alpha^2}{(\zeta_\infty - \zeta)^2}\left(dy^2 - d\zeta^2\right)$$

### Open slicing {#Open_slicing}

Let

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \alpha \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \cosh\xi, \\ x_1 &= \alpha \cosh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right), \\ x_i &= \alpha z_i \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \sinh\xi, \qquad 2 \leq i \leq n \end{aligned}$$

where $\sum_i z_i^2 = 1$ forming a $S^{n-2}$ with the standard metric $\sum_i dz_i^2 = d\Omega_{n-2}^2$. Then the metric of the de Sitter space reads

$$ds^2 = -dt^2 + \alpha^2 \sinh^2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) dH_{n-1}^2,$$

where

$$dH_{n-1}^2 = d\xi^2 + \sinh^2(\xi) d\Omega_{n-2}^2$$

is the standard hyperbolic metric.

### Closed slicing {#Closed_slicing}

Let

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \alpha \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right), \\ x_i &= \alpha \cosh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) z_i, \qquad 1 \leq i \leq n \end{aligned}$$

where $z_i$s describe a $S^{n-1}$. Then the metric reads:

$$ds^2 = -dt^2 + \alpha^2 \cosh^2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) d\Omega_{n-1}^2.$$

Changing the time variable to the conformal time via $\tan\left(\frac{1}{2}\eta\right) = \tanh\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}t\right)$ we obtain a metric conformally equivalent to Einstein static universe:

$$ds^2 = \frac{\alpha^2}{\cos^2\eta}\left(-d\eta^2 + d\Omega_{n-1}^2\right).$$

These coordinates, also known as "global coordinates" cover the maximal extension of de Sitter space, and can therefore be used to find its [Penrose diagram](wikipedia://en/681666).[^o15]

### dS slicing {#dS_slicing}

Let

$$\begin{aligned} x_0 &= \alpha \sin\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\chi\right) \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \cosh\xi, \\ x_1 &= \alpha \cos\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\chi\right), \\ x_2 &= \alpha \sin\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\chi\right) \cosh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right), \\ x_i &= \alpha z_i \sin\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\chi\right) \sinh\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) \sinh\xi, \qquad 3 \leq i \leq n \end{aligned}$$

where $z_i$s describe a $S^{n-3}$. Then the metric reads:

$$ds^2 = d\chi^2 + \sin^2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\chi\right) ds_{dS,\alpha,n-1}^2,$$

where

$$ds_{dS,\alpha,n-1}^2 = -dt^2 + \alpha^2 \sinh^2\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}t\right) dH_{n-2}^2$$

is the metric of an $n - 1$ dimensional de Sitter space with radius of curvature $\alpha$ in open slicing coordinates. The hyperbolic metric is given by:

$$dH_{n-2}^2 = d\xi^2 + \sinh^2(\xi) d\Omega_{n-3}^2.$$

This is the analytic continuation of the open slicing coordinates under $\left(t, \xi, \theta, \phi_1, \phi_2, \ldots, \phi_{n-3}\right) \to \left(i\chi, \xi, it, \theta, \phi_1, \ldots, \phi_{n-4}\right)$ and also switching $x_0$ and $x_2$ because they change their timelike/spacelike nature.
