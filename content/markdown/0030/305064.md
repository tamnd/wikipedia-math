# Jan Łukasiewicz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Łukasiewicz"}

**Jan Leopold Łukasiewicz** (; 21 December 1878 – 13 February 1956) was a Polish [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) who is best known for [Polish notation](wikipedia://en/25056) and [Łukasiewicz logic](wikipedia://en/11001950).[^o94] His work centred on [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [history of logic](wikipedia://en/59945).[^o95] He thought innovatively about traditional [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154), the principle of [non-contradiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_contradiction) and the [law of excluded middle](wikipedia://en/17637), offering one of the earliest systems of [many-valued logic](wikipedia://en/38024). Contemporary research on [Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic) also builds on innovative works by Łukasiewicz, which applied methods from modern [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) to the formalization of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s [syllogistic](wikipedia://en/321652).[^n1]

:::

The Łukasiewicz approach was reinvigorated in the early 1970s in a series of papers by [John Corcoran](wikipedia://en/23686234) and [Timothy Smiley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Smiley) that inform modern translations of *[Prior Analytics](wikipedia://en/491442)* by Robin Smith in 1989 and [Gisela Striker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisela_Striker) in 2009.[^o96] Łukasiewicz is regarded as one of the most important historians of logic.

## Life {#Life}

He was born as Jan Leopold Łukasiewicz[^o6] on 21 December 1878 in [Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemberg), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (now [Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)) and was the only child of Paweł Łukasiewicz, a captain in the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Armed_Forces), and Leopoldina, *née* Holtzer, the daughter of a civil servant. His family was [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic).[^o5]

He finished his *[gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29)* studies in philology and in 1897 went on to [Lemberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University), where he studied philosophy and mathematics. He was a pupil of the philosopher [Kazimierz Twardowski](wikipedia://en/1022980).[^o97]

In 1902, he received a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree under the patronage of Emperor [Franz Joseph I of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria), who gave him a special doctoral ring with diamonds.[^o98] His thesis entitled *Indukcja jako inwersja dedukcji* (On Induction as the Inverse of Deduction) was written under the supervision of Twardowski.[^o6][^o7]

He spent three years as a private teacher, and in 1905, he received a scholarship to complete his philosophy studies at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) and the [University of Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_University_of_Louvain) in Belgium.[^o98]

Łukasiewicz continued studying for his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) qualification and in 1906 submitted his thesis to the University of Lemberg. That year, he was appointed a lecturer at the University of Lemberg, where he was eventually appointed Extraordinary Professor by Emperor Franz Joseph I. He taught there until the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^o98]

In 1915, he was invited to lecture as a full professor at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), which the [German occupation authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Poland_during_World_War_I) had reopened after it had been closed down by the [Tsarist government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in the 19th century.[^o98]

In 1919, Łukasiewicz left the university to serve as Polish Minister of Religious Denominations and Public Education in [Paderewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Jan_Paderewski)'s government until 1920. Łukasiewicz led the development of a Polish curriculum replacing the Russian, German and Austrian curricula that had been used in partitioned Poland. The Łukasiewicz curriculum emphasized the early acquisition of logical and mathematical concepts.

In 1928, he married Regina Barwińska.[^o98]

He remained a professor at the University of Warsaw from 1920 until 1939, when the family house was destroyed by German bombs, and the university was closed by the German occupation. He had been a rector of the university twice during which Łukasiewicz and [Stanisław Leśniewski](wikipedia://en/422271) had founded the [Lwów–Warsaw school of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_school_of_logic), which was later made famous internationally by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), who had been a student of Leśniewski.

During the start of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), he worked at the Warsaw [Underground University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_University). After the German occupation authorities had closed the university, he earned a meager living in the Warsaw city archive. His friendship with [Heinrich Scholz](wikipedia://en/41316662) (German professor of mathematical logic) helped him, too, and it was Scholz who arranged for the Łukasiewicz family's passage to Germany in 1944 (Łukasiewicz was fearful of the Red Army advance). As it became increasingly clear that Germany would lose the war, Łukasiewicz and his wife tried to move to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), but were unable to get permission from the German authorities. They thus spent the last months of the war in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster), Germany. After the end of the war, unwilling to return to a Soviet-controlled Poland, they moved first to Belgium, where Łukasiewicz taught logic at a provisional Polish Scientific Institute.[^o98]

In February 1946, at the invitation of Irish political leader [Éamon de Valera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éamon_de_Valera) (himself a mathematician by profession), Łukasiewicz and his wife relocated to Dublin, where they remained until his death there a decade later. In Ireland, he briefly served as Professor of Mathematical Logic at the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) (a position created for him). His duties involved giving frequent public lectures.[^o98]

During this period, his book *Elements of Mathematical Logic* was published in English by Macmillan (1963, translated from Polish by Olgierd Wojtasiewicz).[^n2]

Jan Łukasiewicz died on 13 February 1956. He was buried in [Mount Jerome Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Jerome_Cemetery_and_Crematorium), in Dublin. At the urging of the Armenian community in Poland, his remains were repatriated to Poland 66 years later. He was reburied on 22 November 2022 in Warsaw's Old [Powązki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powązki_Cemetery).[^o9]

From October to December 2022, the Royal Irish Academy in Dublin hosted an exhibition on his life and work.[^o10]

Łukasiewicz's [papers](https://www.library.manchester.ac.uk/rylands/special-collections/a-to-z/detail/?mms_id=992983876595601631) (post-1945) are held by the [University of Manchester Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester_Library).

## Work {#Work}

A number of axiomatizations of [classical](wikipedia://en/55868) [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154) are due to Łukasiewicz. A particularly elegant axiomatization features a mere three [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms) and is still invoked to the present day. He was a pioneer investigator of [multi-valued logics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-valued_logic); his [three-valued propositional calculus](wikipedia://en/249904), introduced in 1917, was the first explicitly axiomatized [non-classical](wikipedia://en/55868) [logical calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). He wrote on the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), and his approach to the making of scientific theories was similar to the thinking of [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper).

Łukasiewicz invented the [Polish notation](wikipedia://en/25056) (named after his nationality) for the [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) around 1920. A quotation from a paper by Jan Łukasiewicz in 1931[^n3][^n4] states how the notation was invented:

The reference cited by Łukasiewicz, i.e., Łukasiewicz (1),[^n5] is apparently a lithographed report in [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language). The referring paper[^n6] by Łukasiewicz was reviewed by [Henry A. Pogorzelski](wikipedia://en/789040) in the *Journal of Symbolic Logic* in 1965.[^o99]

In Łukasiewicz's 1951 book, *Aristotle's Syllogistic from the Standpoint of Modern Formal Logic*, he mentions that the principle of his notation was to write the functors before the arguments to avoid brackets (i.e., parentheses) and that he had employed his notation in his logical papers since 1929.[^n7] He then goes on to cite, as an example, a 1930 paper he wrote with [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) on the [sentential calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus).

This notation is the root of the idea of the *[recursive stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_stack)*, a last-in, first-out computer memory store proposed by several researchers including [Turing](wikipedia://en/1208), [Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_L._Bauer) and [Hamblin](wikipedia://en/19501790), and first implemented in 1957. In 1960, Łukasiewicz's notation concepts and stacks were used as the basis of the [Burroughs B5000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burroughs_large_systems) computer designed by [Robert S. Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_S._Barton) and his team at [Burroughs Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burroughs_Corporation) in [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California). The concepts also led to the design of the English Electric multi-programmed [KDF9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_KDF9) computer system of 1963, which had two such hardware register stacks. A similar concept underlies the [reverse Polish notation](wikipedia://en/26513) (*RPN*, a postfix notation) of the [Friden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friden,_Inc.) EC-130 calculator and its successors, many [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) calculators, the [Lisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_%28programming_language%29) and [Forth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forth_%28programming_language%29) programming languages, and the [PostScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PostScript) page description language.

## Recognition and honours {#Recognition_and_honours}

In 1923, Łukasiewicz became the recipient of the [Commander's Cross with Star of the Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta).[^o19][^o20]

In 1935, Łukasiewicz was awarded the [Hungarian Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Order_of_Merit).[^o20]

In 1955, Łukasiewicz received an [honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_doctorate) from the [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) in acknowledgement of his academic achievements.[^o21] A commemorative plaque has been unveiled on the house where Łukasiewicz lived in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin) at 57 Fitzwilliam Square.[^o22]

In 1998, a [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet) discovered by [Paul G. Comba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_G._Comba) was named [27114 Lukasiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27114_Lukasiewicz) in honour of the Polish philosopher and logician.

In 2008, the [Polish Information Processing Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Information_Processing_Society) established the Jan Łukasiewicz Award, to be presented to the most innovative Polish IT companies.[^o100]

From 1999 to 2004, the Department of Computer Science building at the [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) was called the Łukasiewicz Building, until all campus buildings were renamed after the disciplines they housed.[^o21]

His model of [three-valued logic](wikipedia://en/249904) allowed for formulating [Kleene's](wikipedia://en/28805) ternary logic and a meta-model of empiricism, mathematics and logic, i.e. senary logic.[^o101]

## Chronology {#Chronology}

*Image: 20070206 uw buw hall glowny biblioteki.jpg.* [Warsaw University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_Library) – at entrance (seen from rear) are pillared statues of [Lwów-Warsaw School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv-Warsaw_School_of_Logic) philosophers (*right to left*) [Kazimierz Twardowski](wikipedia://en/1022980), Jan Łukasiewicz, [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), [Stanisław Leśniewski](wikipedia://en/422271).

- 1878 born in [Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemberg) (now Lviv)
- 1890–1902 studies with [Kazimierz Twardowski](wikipedia://en/1022980) in Lemberg (Lwów, L'viv)
- 1902 doctorate (mathematics and philosophy), [University of Lemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv) with the highest distinction possible
- 1906 [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) thesis completed, University of Lemberg
- 1906 becomes a lecturer
- 1910 essays on the principle of non-contradiction and the excluded middle
- 1911 extraordinary professor at Lemberg
- 1915 invited to the newly reopened University of Warsaw
- 1916 new Kingdom of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) declared
- 1917 develops three-valued propositional calculus
- 1919 Polish [Minister of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Education_of_Poland)
- 1920–1939 professor at [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University) founds with [Stanisław Leśniewski](wikipedia://en/422271) the [Lwów–Warsaw school of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_school_of_logic) (see also [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), [Hugo Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735), [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471), [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203))
- 1928 marries Regina Barwińska
- 1944 flees to Germany and settles in [Hembsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brakel,_Germany#Hembsen), in the [Nethegau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nethegau), where he was brought for his own safety.
- 1946 exile in [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium)
- 1946 held a chair at the [Royal Irish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Irish_Academy) in Dublin.
- 1953 writes autobiography
- 1956 dies in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin)
