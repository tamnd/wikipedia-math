# Totally disconnected space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Totally disconnected space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and related branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **totally disconnected space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that has only [singletons](wikipedia://en/381798) (and the empty set) as [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631). In every topological space, the singletons (and, when it is considered connected, the empty set) are connected; in a totally disconnected space, these are the *only* connected subsets.

:::

An important example of a totally disconnected space is the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172), which is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the set of [*p*-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423#p-adic_integers). Another example, playing a key role in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), is the field $\mathbf{Q}_{p}$ of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A topological space $X$ is **totally disconnected** if the [connected components](wikipedia://en/6233) in $X$ are the one-point sets.[^n1][^n2] Analogously, a topological space $X$ is **totally path-disconnected** if all [path-components](wikipedia://en/6233#Path_connectedness) in $X$ are the one-point sets.

Another closely related notion is that of a [totally separated space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_separated_space), i.e. a space where [quasicomponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicomponents) are singletons. That is, a topological space $X$ is **totally separated** if for every $x\in X$, the [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) of all [clopen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clopen) [neighborhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) of $x$ is the singleton $\{x\}$. Equivalently, for each pair of distinct points $x, y\in X$, there is a pair of disjoint open neighborhoods $U, V$ of $x, y$ such that $X= U\sqcup V$.

Every totally separated space is evidently totally disconnected but the converse is false even for [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018). For instance, take $X$ to be the [Cantor's teepee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_teepee), which is the [Knaster–Kuratowski fan](wikipedia://en/14988066) with the apex removed. Then $X$ is totally disconnected but its quasicomponents are not singletons. For [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) the two notions (totally disconnected and totally separated) are equivalent.

Confusingly, in the literature[^o6] totally disconnected spaces are sometimes called **hereditarily disconnected**,[^n3] while the terminology **totally disconnected** is used for totally separated spaces.[^n3]

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following are examples of totally disconnected spaces:

- [Discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061)
- The [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024)
- The [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689)
- The *p*-adic numbers; more generally, all [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266) are totally disconnected.
- The [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172) and the [Cantor space](wikipedia://en/352376)
- The [Baire space](wikipedia://en/2078245)
- The [Sorgenfrey line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorgenfrey_line)
- Every Hausdorff space of [small inductive dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_inductive_dimension) 0 is totally disconnected
- The [Erdős space](wikipedia://en/30749895) ℓ<sup>*2*</sup>$\, \cap \, \mathbb{Q}^{\omega}$ is a totally disconnected Hausdorff space that does not have small inductive dimension 0.
- [Extremally disconnected](wikipedia://en/5783949) Hausdorff spaces
- [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219)
- The [Knaster–Kuratowski fan](wikipedia://en/14988066) provides an example of a connected space, such that the removal of a single point produces a totally disconnected space.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- [Subspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29), [products](wikipedia://en/24910), and [coproducts](wikipedia://en/1564380) of totally disconnected spaces are totally disconnected.
- Totally disconnected spaces are [T<sub>1</sub> spaces](wikipedia://en/148721), since connected components are closed.
- Continuous images of totally disconnected spaces are not necessarily totally disconnected, in fact, every [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is a continuous image of the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172).
- A [locally compact Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_Hausdorff_space) has [small inductive dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_inductive_dimension) 0 if and only if it is totally disconnected.
- Every totally disconnected compact metric space is homeomorphic to a subset of a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) product of [discrete spaces](wikipedia://en/56061).
- It is in general not true that every open set in a totally disconnected space is also closed.
- It is in general not true that the closure of every open set in a totally disconnected space is open, i.e. not every totally disconnected Hausdorff space is [extremally disconnected](wikipedia://en/5783949).

:::

## Constructing a totally disconnected quotient space of any given space {#Constructing_a_totally_disconnected_quotient_space_of_any_given_space}

Let $X$ be an arbitrary topological space. Let $x\sim y$ if and only if $y\in \mathrm{conn}(x)$ (where $\mathrm{conn}(x)$ denotes the largest connected subset containing $x$). This is obviously an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) whose equivalence classes are the connected components of $X$. Endow $X/{\sim}$ with the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology), i.e. the [finest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finest_topology) making the map $m:x\mapsto \mathrm{conn}(x)$ continuous. With a little bit of effort we can see that $X/{\sim}$ is totally disconnected.

In fact this space is not only *some* totally disconnected quotient but in a certain sense the *biggest*: The following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) holds: For any totally disconnected space $Y$ and any continuous map $f : X\rightarrow Y$, there exists a *unique* continuous map $\breve{f}:(X/\sim)\rightarrow Y$ with $f=\breve{f}\circ m$.
