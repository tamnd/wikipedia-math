# James H. Wilkinson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James H. Wilkinson"}

**James Hardy Wilkinson** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o4] (27 September 1919 – 5 October 1986) was a prominent figure in the field of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), a field at the boundary of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) particularly useful to [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and engineering. In 1970 he won the ACM [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Born in [Strood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strood), England, he won a Foundation Scholarship to [Sir Joseph Williamson's Mathematical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Joseph_Williamson's_Mathematical_School) in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Kent).[^o9] He studied the [Cambridge Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated as [Senior Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29).[^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Taking up war work in 1940, he began working on ballistics but transferred to the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory,_UK)[^o3] in 1946, where he worked with [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) on the [ACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_ACE)[^o12] computer project. Later, Wilkinson's interests took him into the numerical analysis field, where he discovered many significant [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775).

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Wilkinson received the [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) in 1970 "for his research in numerical analysis to facilitate the use of the high-speed digital computer, having received special recognition for his work in computations in linear algebra and 'backward' [error analysis](wikipedia://en/44281046)." In the same year, he also gave the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM)](wikipedia://en/1883376) [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) Lecture.

Wilkinson also received an [Honorary Doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University) in 1973.[^o14]

He was elected as a Distinguished Fellow of the [British Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Computer_Society) in 1974 for his pioneering work in computer science.

The [James H. Wilkinson Prize in Numerical Analysis and Scientific Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_H._Wilkinson_Prize_in_Numerical_Analysis_and_Scientific_Computing), established in 1982 by SIAM, and [J. H. Wilkinson Prize for Numerical Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._Wilkinson_Prize_for_Numerical_Software), established in 1991, are named in his honour.

In 1987, Wilkinson won the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), for his paper "The Perfidious Polynomial".[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Wilkinson married Heather Ware in 1945. He died at home of a heart attack on 5 October 1986. His wife and their son survived him, a daughter having predeceased him.
