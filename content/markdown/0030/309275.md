# Respondent

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Respondent"}

A **respondent** is a person who is called upon to issue a response to a communication made by another. The term is used in legal contexts, in [survey methodology](wikipedia://en/272134), and in psychological conditioning.

:::

## Legal usage {#Legal_usage}

In [legal usage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law), this term specifically refers to the [defendant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defendant) in a [legal proceeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_process) commenced by a petitioner, and also to an [appellee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellee), or the opposing party, in an [appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal#Who_can_appeal) from a decision by an initial fact-finder or tribunal. For example in a Court of Appeal case, the respondents are the party facing the appellant, who is challenging a lower court decision or some aspect of it. The respondent may have been the "claimant" or the "defendant" in the lower court.[^o3]

In the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate), the two sides in an [impeachment trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_trial) are called the management and the respondent.

## Psychology usage {#Psychology_usage}

In [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), respondent conditioning is a synonym for [classical conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_conditioning) or [Pavlovian conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlovian_conditioning). Respondent behavior specifically refers to the behavior consistently elicited by a reflexive or classically conditioned stimulus.

## Survey usage {#Survey_usage}

In [population survey](wikipedia://en/13437523) and [questionnaire pretesting](wikipedia://en/269927#Pretesting), a respondent is a [research participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_participant) replying with answers or feedback to a survey.[^o6][^o7] Depending on the survey questions and context, respondent answers may represent themselves as individuals, a household or organization of which they are a part, or as a proxy to another individual.

## Other usages {#Other_usages}

In non-legal or informal usage, the term refers to one who refutes or responds to a thesis or an argument. In [cross-cultural communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-cultural_communication), the second person responding to the meaning or message from an original source which has been [contextualised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contextualization_%28sociolinguistics%29) or decoded for the understanding of respondents as *recipients or hearers* of the message occurring from a different cultural context.
