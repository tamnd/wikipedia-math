# Concept and object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Concept and object"}

In the [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), the distinction between **concept and object** is attributable to the German philosopher [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) in 1892 (in his paper "**Concept and Object**"; German: "Über Begriff und Gegenstand").

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

According to Frege, any sentence that expresses a singular thought consists of an expression (a [proper name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_names) or a general term plus the definite article) that signifies an object together with a predicate (the [copula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_%28linguistics%29) "is", plus a general term accompanied by the indefinite article or an adjective) that signifies a concept. Thus "Socrates is a philosopher" consists of "Socrates", which signifies the object *Socrates*, and "is a philosopher", which signifies the concept of *being a philosopher*.

The distinction was of fundamental importance to the development of logic and mathematics. Frege's distinction helped to clarify the notions of a [set](wikipedia://en/27553), of the membership relation between element and set, and of [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) and [infinite sets](wikipedia://en/142679). However, Frege's conception of a class (in his terminology an extension of a concept) differs from the current iterative conception of a set.

Frege's distinction leads to the famous difficulty or "awkwardness of language" that some expressions which purport to signify a concept — Frege's example is "the concept *horse*" — are grammatically expressions that by his criterion signify an object. Thus "the concept *horse* is not a concept, whereas the city of Berlin is a city."

[Anthony Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Kenny) sought to justify the distinction, other philosophers such as Hartley Slater and [Crispin Wright](wikipedia://en/302883) have argued that the distinguished category of *entity* cannot be associated with predication in the way that individual objects are associated with the use of [singular terms](wikipedia://en/246918).
