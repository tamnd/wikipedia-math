# Christian Kramp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christian Kramp"}

**Christian Kramp** (8 July 1760 – 13 May 1826) was a French mathematician, who worked primarily with [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606).

*Image: Kramp, Christian – Analyse des réfractions astronomiques et terrestres, 1799 – BEIC 745581.jpg.* *Analyse des réfractions astronomiques et terrestres*, 1799

:::

Christian Kramp's father was his teacher at grammar school in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg). Kramp studied medicine and graduated; however, his interests certainly ranged outside medicine, for in addition to a number of medical publications he published a work on [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography) in 1793. In 1795, France annexed the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland) area in which Kramp was carrying out his work and after this he became a teacher at [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) (this city was French from 1794 to 1815), teaching mathematics, [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). Kramp could read and write in German and French.[^o3]

Kramp was appointed professor of mathematics at [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), the town of his birth, in 1809. He was elected to the geometry section of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1817. As [Bessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bessel), [Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695) and [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) did, Kramp worked on the generalised factorial function which applied to non-[integers](wikipedia://en/14563). His work on factorials is independent of that of [James Stirling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stirling_%28mathematician%29) and [Vandermonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandermonde). He was the first to use the notation [*n*!](wikipedia://en/10606) in *Elements d'arithmétique universelle*, 1808.[^o4] In fact, the more general concept of factorial was found at the same time by [Arbogast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Arbogast).

I use the very simple notation *n*! to designate the product of numbers decreasing from *n* to unity, i.e. *n*(*n* − 1)(*n* − 2) ... 3 . 2 . 1. The constant use in combinatorial analysis, in most of my proofs, that I make of this idea, has made this notation necessary. \[...\] I have given it the name 'faculty'. Arbogast has substituted the name 'factorial' which is clearer and more French. In adopting his idea I congratulate myself on paying homage to the memory of my friend.[^o3][^o5]

*Kramp's function*, a scaled [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [error function](wikipedia://en/177111), is today better known as the [Faddeeva function](wikipedia://en/16381196).
