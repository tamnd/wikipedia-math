# Riccati equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riccati equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Riccati equation** in the narrowest sense is any first-order [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753) that is [quadratic](wikipedia://en/187240) in the unknown function. In other words, it is an equation of the form

$$y'(x) = q_0(x) + q_1(x) \, y(x) + q_2(x) \, y^2(x)$$

where $q_0(x) \neq 0$ and $q_2(x) \neq 0$. If $q_0(x) = 0$ the equation reduces to a [Bernoulli equation](wikipedia://en/1911217), while if $q_2(x) = 0$ the equation becomes a first order [linear ordinary differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_ordinary_differential_equation).

:::

The equation is named after [Jacopo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Riccati) (1676–1754)[^o2] though John Bernoulli was the first to publish an equation of this form (1694) and James Bernoulli was the first to discover a solution (1703).[^o3]

The term **Riccati equation** is now used very generally to refer to [matrix equations](wikipedia://en/27567102#Nonlinear_matrix_difference_equations:_Riccati_equations) with an analogous quadratic term, which occur in both [continuous-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-time) and [discrete-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete-time) [linear-quadratic-Gaussian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear-quadratic-Gaussian_control). The steady-state (non-dynamic) version of these is referred to as the [algebraic Riccati equation](wikipedia://en/16684054).

## Conversion to a second order linear equation {#Conversion_to_a_second_order_linear_equation}

The quadratic **non-linear** ordinary differential equation (ODE) known as the Riccati equation can be converted to a second order [linear ordinary differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_ordinary_differential_equation).[^o5]

First, we show that there is a standard form where the Riccati equation's quadratic coefficient is unity. For a given $y' = q_0(x) + q_1(x)y + q_2(x)y^2$, wherever $q_2$ is non-zero and differentiable, we may substitute $v = yq_2$, and find

$$\begin{aligned} v' &= (yq_2)' \\[4pt] &= y'q_2 +yq_2' \\ &= \left(q_0+q_1 y + q_2 y^2\right) q_2 + v \frac{q_2'}{q_2} \\ &= q_0q_2 + \left(q_1+\frac{q_2'}{q_2}\right) v + v^2 \\ &=S(x) + R(x) v + v^2 \end{aligned}$$

with $S = q_0q_2$ and $R = q_1 + {q_2'}/{q_2}$.

Given this standard form, let us suppose there exists an unknown function $u(x)$ such that $u' = -uv$. Then

$$\begin{aligned} u''&= -u'v - v' u \\ &= v^2 u - v' u \\ &= v^2u - (S(x) + R(x) v + v^2) u \\ &= - u S(x) - R(x) u v \\ &= - u S(x) + R(x) u' . \end{aligned}$$

Thus, the solution of $y(x)$ of a Riccati equation can be derived from the solution of the system

$$\begin{aligned} y &= v/q_2 \\ 0 &= u' + u v \\ 0 &= u'' - R(x) u' + S(x) u. \end{aligned}$$

If the general solution $u(x)$ of the linear equation is known, then $y = -\frac{u'}{q_2u} = -q_2^{-1} \left[\log(u) \right]'$ suffices to have global knowledge of the general solution of the Riccati equation by the formula:[^o6]

$$y = -q_2^{-1} \left[\log(c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2) \right]'.$$

Conversely, second order linear homogeneous ODEs link the [Bessel equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_equation) can be transformed to a system equations based on a Riccati equation.

## Complex analysis {#Complex_analysis}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), the Riccati equation occurs as the first-order nonlinear ODE in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) of the form[^o8]

$$\frac{d w}{dz} = F(w,z) = \frac{P(w,z)}{Q(w,z)},$$

where $P$ and $Q$ are [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) in $w$ and locally [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) of $z \in \mathbb{C}$, i.e., $F$ is a [complex rational function](wikipedia://en/361210#Complex_rational_functions). The only equation of this form that is of [Painlevé type](wikipedia://en/2033003), is the Riccati equation

$$\frac{dw(z)}{dz} = A_0 (z) + A_1 (z) w + A_2(z) w^2,$$

where $A_i (z)$ are (possibly matrix) functions of $z$.

### Application to the Schwarzian equation {#Application_to_the_Schwarzian_equation}

An important application of the Riccati equation is to the 3rd order [Schwarzian differential equation](wikipedia://en/555745)

$$S(w) := \left(\frac{w''}{w'}\right)' - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{w''}{w'}\right)^2 = f$$

which occurs in the theory of [conformal mapping](wikipedia://en/50627) and [univalent functions](wikipedia://en/1534382). In this case the ODEs are in the complex domain and differentiation is with respect to a complex variable. (The [Schwarzian derivative](wikipedia://en/555745) $S(w)$ has the remarkable property that it is invariant under Möbius transformations, i.e. $S\bigl(\tfrac{aw+b}{cw+d}\bigr) = S(w)$ whenever $ad-bc$ is non-zero.) The function $y = \tfrac{w''}{w'}$ satisfies the Riccati equation

$$y' = \frac{1}{2}y^2 + f.$$

By the above $y = -2 \tfrac{u'}{u}$ where $u$ is a solution of the linear ODE

$$u'' + \frac{1}{2}fu = 0.$$

Since $\tfrac{w''}{w'} = -2\tfrac{u'}{u},$ integration gives $w' = \tfrac{C}{u^2}$ for some constant $C$. On the other hand any other independent solution $U$ of the linear ODE has constant non-zero [Wronskian](wikipedia://en/302846) $U'u - Uu'$ which can be taken to be $C$ after scaling. Thus

$$w' = \frac{U'u-Uu'}{u^2} = \left(\frac{U}{u}\right)'$$

so that the Schwarzian equation has solution $w = \tfrac{U}{u}.$

## Obtaining solutions by quadrature {#Obtaining_solutions_by_quadrature}

The correspondence between Riccati equations and second-order linear ODEs has other consequences. For example, if one solution of a 2nd order ODE is known, then it is known that another solution can be obtained by quadrature, i.e., a simple integration. The same holds true for the Riccati equation. In fact, if one particular solution $y_{1}$ can be found, the general solution is obtained as

$$y = y_1 + u$$

Substituting

$$y_1 + u$$

in the Riccati equation yields

$$y_1' + u' = q_0 + q_1 \cdot (y_1 + u) + q_2 \cdot (y_1 + u)^2,$$

and since

$$y_1' = q_0 + q_1 \, y_1 + q_2 \, y_1^2,$$

it follows that

$$u' = q_1 \, u + 2 \, q_2 \, y_1 \, u + q_2 \, u^2$$

or

$$u' - (q_1 + 2 \, q_2 \, y_1) \, u = q_2 \, u^2,$$

which is a [Bernoulli equation](wikipedia://en/1911217). The substitution that is needed to solve this Bernoulli equation is

$$z =\frac{1}{u}$$

Substituting

$$y = y_1 + \frac{1}{z}$$

directly into the Riccati equation yields the linear equation

$$z' + (q_1 + 2 \, q_2 \, y_1) \, z = -q_2$$

A set of solutions to the Riccati equation is then given by

$$y = y_1 + \frac{1}{z}$$

where $z$ is the general solution to the aforementioned [linear equation](wikipedia://en/17570).
