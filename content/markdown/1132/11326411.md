# Positive current

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Positive current"}

In mathematics, more particularly in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a **positive current** is a [positive](wikipedia://en/10592733) (*n-p*,*n-p*)-form over an *n*-dimensional [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), taking values in distributions.

:::

For a formal definition, consider a manifold *M*. [Currents](wikipedia://en/3021207) on *M* are (by definition) differential forms with coefficients in [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29); integrating over *M*, we may consider currents as "currents of integration", that is, functionals

$$\eta \mapsto \int_M \eta\wedge \rho$$

on smooth forms with compact support. This way, currents are considered as elements in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) to the space $\Lambda_c^*(M)$ of forms with compact support.

Now, let *M* be a complex manifold. The [Hodge decomposition](wikipedia://en/221530#Hodge_decomposition) $\Lambda^i(M)=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\Lambda^{p,q}(M)$ is defined on currents, in a natural way, the *(p,q)*-currents being functionals on $\Lambda_c^{p, q}(M)$.

A **positive current** is defined as a real [current](wikipedia://en/3021207) of Hodge type *(p,p)*, taking non-negative values on all [positive](wikipedia://en/10592733) *(p,p)*-forms.

## Characterization of [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) {#Characterization_of_Kähler_manifolds}

Using the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860), Harvey and [Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Blaine_Lawson) proved the following criterion of existence of [Kähler metrics](wikipedia://en/390538).[^o3]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let *M* be a compact complex manifold. Then *M* does not admit a [Kähler structure](wikipedia://en/390538) if and only if *M* admits a non-zero positive (1,1)-current $\Theta$ which is a (1,1)-part of an exact 2-current.

Note that the [de Rham differential](wikipedia://en/221530) maps 3-currents to 2-currents, hence $\Theta$ is a differential of a 3-current; if $\Theta$ is a current of integration of a [complex curve](wikipedia://en/173181), this means that this curve is a (1,1)-part of a boundary.

When *M* admits a surjective map $\pi:\; M \mapsto X$ to a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) with 1-dimensional fibers, this theorem leads to the following result of complex algebraic geometry.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=inline-head name="Corollary"}

**Corollary:** In this situation, *M* is non-[Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the [homology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class) of a generic fiber of $\pi$ is a (1,1)-part of a boundary.

:::
