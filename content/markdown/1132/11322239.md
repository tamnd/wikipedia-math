# Gårding domain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gårding domain"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Gårding domain** is a concept in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315). The concept is named after the [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Lars Gårding](wikipedia://en/8000370).

:::

Let *G* be a topological group and let *U* be a [strongly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_continuous) [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of *G* in a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*. Denote by ***g*** the family of all [one-parameter subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-parameter_subgroup) of *G*. For each *δ* = { *δ*(*t*) | *t* ∈ **R** } ∈ ***g***, let *U*(*δ*) denote the [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) [generator](wikipedia://en/3171371) of the unitary one-parameter subgroup { *U*(*δ*(*t*)) | *t* ∈ **R** }. A **Gårding domain** for *U* is a [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of *H* that is *U*(*g*)- and *U*(*δ*)-[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) for all *g* ∈ *G* and *δ* ∈ ***g*** and is also a domain of [essential self-adjointness](wikipedia://en/187408) for *U*

Gårding showed in 1947 that, if *G* is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), then a Gårding domain for *U* consisting of infinitely differentiable vectors exists for each continuous unitary representation of *G*. In 1961, Kats extended this result to arbitrary [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) topological groups. However, these results do not extend easily to the non-locally compact case because of the lack of a [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on the group. In 1996, Danilenko proved the following result for groups *G* that can be written as the [inductive limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) of an increasing sequence *G*<sub>1</sub> ⊆ *G*<sub>2</sub> ⊆ ... of locally compact [second countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_countable) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011):

Let *U* be a strongly continuous unitary representation of *G* in a separable Hilbert space *H*. Then there exist a separable [nuclear](wikipedia://en/2702957) [Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319) *F* and a continuous, [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942), [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) *J* : *F* → *H* such that

- the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of *F*, denoted by *F*<sup>∗</sup>, has the structure of a separable [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801) with respect to the strong topology on the dual pairing (*F*<sup>∗</sup>, *F*);
- the image of *J*, im(*J*), is [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in *H*;
- for all *g* ∈ *G*, *U*(*g*)(im(*J*)) = im(*J*);
- for all *δ* ∈ ***g***, *U*(*δ*)(im(*J*)) ⊆ im(*J*) and im(*J*) is a domain of essential self-adjointness for *U*(*δ*);
- for all *g* ∈ *G*, *J*<sup>−1</sup>*U*(*g*)*J* is a continuous linear map from *F* to itself;
- moreover, the map *G* → Lin(*F*; *F*) taking *g* to *J*<sup>−1</sup>*U*(*g*)*J* is continuous with respect to the topology on *G* and the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) on Lin(*F*; *F*).

The space *F* is known as a **strong Gårding space** for *U* and im(*J*) is called a **strong Gårding domain** for *U*. Under the above assumptions on *G* there is a natural [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) structure on *G*, so it makes sense to call ***g*** the Lie algebra of *G*.
