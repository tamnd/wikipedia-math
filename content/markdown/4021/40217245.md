# Heinrich Bruns

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heinrich Bruns"}

**Ernst Heinrich Bruns** (4 September 1848 – 23 September 1919) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), who also contributed to the development of the field of theoretical [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Heinrich Bruns was born on 4 September 1848 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) to Christian Gerhard Bruns, a [landscape painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_painter), and his wife, Caroline Henriette Hasse.

## Education and professional appointments {#Education_and_professional_appointments}

Bruns studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) during 1866–1871 under [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117) and [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) and earned a doctoral degree with a dissertation titled *De proprietate quadam functionis potentialis corporum homogeneorum* ("On the properties of a certain potential function of homogeneous bodies"). From 1872 to 1873 he was employed at the [Pulkowa Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulkowa_Observatory) in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) as a calculator. There he met and married Marie Wilhelmine Schleussner, who also worked as a calculator at the observatory. In 1873 he became an observer at the [Observatory of Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu_Observatory) (now [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu)) in [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia), where he remained until 1876. During this time he also worked as a lecturer at the [University of Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tartu).

In 1876, Bruns was appointed an associate professor of mathematics at the University of Berlin. He also worked at the [Prussian Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Military_Academy) and the [Geodetic Institute of Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodetic_Institute_of_Potsdam). In 1882 he went to [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Saxony) as a full professor of astronomy at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) and director of the [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) Observatory. That same year, he was elected a member of the academy of science *[Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina)*.

## Work {#Work}

Heinrich Bruns was mainly engaged in developing the theoretical side of "the [shape of the Earth](wikipedia://en/233668)" (the title of one of his major works). The fields of [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) and the study of [equilibrium shapes](wikipedia://en/51776#Planetary_geology) owe many key results to him, including the *[Bruns formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruns_formula)*.

For the study of [astronomical refraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_refraction) he developed an unusual method of calculating the [vertical gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature_gradient) of [air temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_temperature) together with his [assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_fellow) [Felix Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989). However, due to a lack of sufficiently accurate measurement methods this method has not been used in practice.

The 20th century's higher geodesy (a sub-field of geodesy concerned with measuring the earth on a global scale) as practiced by [Karl Ledersteger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Ledersteger) was based on theories developed by Bruns, including "Bruns' polyhedron". This construct was envisioned as a world-spanning net. [Satellite geodesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_geodesy) turned this thought experiment into a reality with the development of the [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS).

## Publications {#Publications}

- *Über die Perioden der elliptischen Integrale erster und zweiter Ordnung* (On the periods of [elliptic integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_integrals) of first and second order). (Dorpat 1875)
- *Die Figur der Erde* (The shape of the Earth). (Berlin 1878)
- *Über eine Aufgabe der Ausgleichsrechnung* (On a problem of [curve fitting](wikipedia://en/555425)). (Leipzig 1886)
- *Über die Integrale des Vielkörperproblems* (On the integrals of the [many-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420)). (Leipzig 1887)
- *Das Eikonal* (The [Eikonal](wikipedia://en/4557698)). (Leipzig 1895)
