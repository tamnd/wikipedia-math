# Eleanor Krawitz Kolchin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eleanor Krawitz Kolchin"}

**Eleanor Krawitz Kolchin** (1927 - January 25, 2019) was an American mathematician, computer programmer, author, and teacher.[^o3] She worked at [Watson Scientific Computing Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Scientific_Computing_Laboratory) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) to calculate the orbit of planets, phases of the moon, and trajectories of asteroids using [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) [tabulating machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabulating_machine).[^o3] Her calculations were used in the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program).[^o5]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Eleanor attended [Samuel J. Tilden High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_J._Tilden_High_School)[^o3] where she took an interest in [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) and graduated in 1943. She earned her B.A. in mathematics at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) in January 1947. While there, she served as treasurer for [Pi Mu Epsilon](wikipedia://en/1565972), the national mathematics honor society.[^o5][^o6] After graduating, she applied and was accepted to Columbia University, where she took classes towards her master's degree in mathematics.[^o6]

## Professional work {#Professional_work}

After high school, Eleanor took a job as a substitute teacher at the high school level at [Midwood High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwood_High_School) and later at her alma mater, Tilden High.[^o3]

In 1947, Kolchin was hired to work at IBM’s Thomas J. Watson Computing Laboratory at Columbia University.[^o6] She was hired along with one woman and three men to take on a project at the Watson Laboratory operating tabulating machines for astrophysics. She simultaneously pursued a master's degree in Mathematics at Columbia while working in the laboratory.[^o6]

Kolchin was tabulating supervisor in the laboratory and taught in Columbia's astronomy department.[^o6]

She was the first woman to contribute to *Columbia Engineering Quarterly.*[^o8]

In 1949, she published an article describing the Watson Lab’s work. It has since been translated into over 20 languages.[^o6]
