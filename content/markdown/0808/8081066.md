# Fundamental plane (spherical coordinates)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental plane"}

The **fundamental plane** in a [spherical coordinate system](wikipedia://en/29181) is a [plane of reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_of_reference) that divides the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) into two [hemispheres](wikipedia://en/27859). The [geocentric latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentric_latitude) of a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) is then the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) between the fundamental plane and the line joining the point to the centre of the sphere.[^o2]

:::

For a [geographic coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_coordinate_system) of the Earth, the fundamental plane is the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator).

[Astronomical coordinate systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_coordinate_system) have varying fundamental planes:[^o3]

- The [horizontal coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48386) uses the observer's [horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon).
- The [Besselian coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besselian_coordinate_system) uses Earth's [terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_%28solar%29) (day/night boundary).[^o4] This is a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) (*x*, *y*, *z*).
- The [equatorial coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48384) uses the [celestial equator](wikipedia://en/153681).
- The [ecliptic coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48387) uses the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264).
- The [galactic coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48389) uses the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way)'s galactic equator.
