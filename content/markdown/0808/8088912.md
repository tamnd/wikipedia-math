# Imaginary line (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Imaginary line"}

In [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), an **imaginary line** is a [straight line](wikipedia://en/946975) that only contains one [real point](wikipedia://en/8088939). It can be proven that this point is the intersection point with the [conjugated line](wikipedia://en/8088986).[^o2]

:::

It is a special case of an [imaginary curve](wikipedia://en/12070637).

An imaginary line is found in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) P<sup>2</sup>(C) where points are represented by three [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) $(x_1,\ x_2,\ x_3),\quad x_i \in C .$

[Boyd Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyd_Patterson) described the lines in this plane:[^o3]

The locus of points whose coordinates satisfy a homogeneous linear equation with complex coefficients

$$a_1\ x_1 +\ a_2\ x_2 \ + a_3\ x_3 \ =\ 0$$

is a straight line and the line is *real* or *imaginary* according as the coefficients of its equation are or are not proportional to three [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).

[Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) described imaginary geometrical structures: "We will characterize a geometric structure as imaginary if its coordinates are not all real.:[^o4]

According to Hatton:[^o5]

The locus of the [double points](wikipedia://en/449738) (imaginary) of the overlapping [involutions](wikipedia://en/293450) in which an overlapping involution pencil (real) is cut by real transversals is a pair of imaginary straight lines.

Hatton continues,

Hence it follows that an imaginary straight line is determined by an imaginary point, which is a double point of an involution, and a real point, the vertex of the involution pencil.
