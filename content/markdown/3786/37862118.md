# Rice's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rice's formula"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Rice's formula** counts the average number of times an [ergodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_process) [stationary process](wikipedia://en/329898) *X*(*t*) per unit time crosses a fixed level *u*.[^o2] Adler and Taylor describe the result as "one of the most important results in the applications of smooth stochastic processes."[^o3] The formula is often used in engineering.[^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

The formula was published by [Stephen O. Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_O._Rice) in 1944,[^o6] having previously been discussed in his 1936 note entitled "Singing Transmission Lines."[^o7][^o8]

::: {#o10 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

Write *D*<sub>*u*</sub> for the number of times the ergodic stationary stochastic process *x*(*t*) takes the value *u* in a unit of time (i.e. *t* ∈ \[0,1\]). Then Rice's formula states that

$$\mathbb E(D_u) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty |x'|p(u,x') \, \mathrm{d}x'$$

where *p*(*x*,*x*') is the joint probability density of the *x*(*t*) and its mean-square derivative *x**(*****t*).[^o11]***

If the process *x*(*t*) is a [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944) and *u* = 0 then the formula simplifies significantly to give[^o11][^o12]

$$\mathbb E(D_0) = \frac{1}{\pi} \sqrt{-\rho''(0)}$$

where *ρ*″ is the second derivative of the normalised [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) of *x*(*t*) at 0.

:::

::: {#o14 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

Rice's formula can be used to approximate an [excursion probability](wikipedia://en/37881516)[^o15]

$$\mathbb P \left\{ \sup_{t\in[0,1]} X(t) \geq u \right\}$$

as for large values of *u* the probability that there is a level crossing is approximately the probability of reaching that level.

:::
