# Knaster's condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knaster's condition"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* is said to have **Knaster's condition upwards** (sometimes **property (K)**) if any [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) subset *A* of *P* has an [upwards-linked](wikipedia://en/32926234) uncountable subset. An analogous definition applies to **Knaster's condition downwards**.

:::

The property is named after [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Bronisław Knaster](wikipedia://en/1003125).

Knaster's condition implies the [countable chain condition](wikipedia://en/1550771) (ccc), and it is sometimes used in conjunction with a weaker form of [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783), where the ccc requirement is replaced with Knaster's condition. Not unlike ccc, Knaster's condition is also sometimes used as a property of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), in which case it means that the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) (as in, the family of all open sets) with [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29) satisfies the condition.

Furthermore, assuming [MA](wikipedia://en/1550783)($\omega_1$), ccc implies Knaster's condition, making the two equivalent.
