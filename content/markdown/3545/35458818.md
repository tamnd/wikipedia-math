# Jacobson's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobson's conjecture"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), **Jacobson's conjecture** is an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335) in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) concerning the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of powers of the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) of a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404).

:::

It has only been [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) for special types of Noetherian rings, so far. Examples exist to show that the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) can fail when the ring is not Noetherian on a side, so it is absolutely necessary for the ring to be two-sided Noetherian.

The conjecture is named for the algebraist [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) who posed the first version of the conjecture.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

For a ring *R* with Jacobson radical *J*, the nonnegative powers $J^n$ are defined by using the [product of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_of_ideals).

*Jacobson's conjecture:* In a right-and-left [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129), $\bigcap_{n\in \mathbb{N}}J^n=\{0\}.$

In other words: "The only element of a Noetherian ring in all powers of *J* is 0."

The original conjecture posed by Jacobson in 1956[^o4] asked about [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/24062516) one-sided Noetherian rings, however [Israel Nathan Herstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Nathan_Herstein) produced a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) in 1965,[^n1] and soon afterwards, Arun Vinayak Jategaonkar produced a different example which was a left [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039).[^n2] From that point on, the conjecture was reformulated to require two-sided Noetherian rings.

:::

## Partial results {#Partial_results}

Jacobson's conjecture has been verified for particular types of Noetherian rings:

- [Commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) Noetherian rings all satisfy Jacobson's conjecture. This is a consequence of the [Krull intersection theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krull_intersection_theorem).
- [Fully bounded Noetherian rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fully_bounded_Noetherian_ring)[^n3][^n4]
- Noetherian rings with [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) 1[^n5]
- Noetherian rings satisfying the [second layer condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_layer_condition)[^n6]
