# Sayre equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sayre equation"}

In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), the **Sayre equation**, named after [David Sayre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sayre) who introduced it in 1952, is a mathematical relationship that allows one to calculate probable values for the [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_problem) of some diffracted beams. It is used when employing [direct methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_methods_%28crystallography%29) to solve a structure. Its formulation is the following:

:::

$$F_{hkl} = \sum_{h'k'l'} F_{h'k'l'}F_{h-h',k-k',l-l'}$$

which states how the [structure factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_factor) for a beam can be calculated as the sum of the products of pairs of structure factors whose indices sum to the desired values of $h,k,l$. Since weak diffracted beams will contribute a little to the sum, this method can be a powerful way of finding the phase of related beams, if some of the initial phases are already known by other methods.

In particular, for three such related beams in a [centrosymmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrosymmetric) structure, the phases can only be 0 or $\pi$ and the Sayre equation reduces to the triplet relationship:

$$S_{h} \approx S_{h'} S_{h-h'}$$

where the $S$ indicates the sign of the structure factor (positive if the phase is 0 and negative if it is $\pi$) and the $\approx$ sign indicates that there is a certain degree of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that the relationship is true, which becomes higher the stronger the beams are.
