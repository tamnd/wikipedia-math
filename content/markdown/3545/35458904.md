# Data science

*Image: PIA23792-1600x1200(1).jpg.* The existence of [Comet NEOWISE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet_NEOWISE) (here depicted as a series of red dots) was discovered by analyzing [astronomical survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_survey) data acquired by a [space telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_telescope), the [Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-field_Infrared_Survey_Explorer).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Data science"}

**Data science** is an [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) academic field[^o2] that uses [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing), [scientific methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), processing, [scientific visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_visualization), [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775), coding (like Python, SQL, and R), and systems to extract or extrapolate [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge) from potentially noisy, [structured](wikipedia://en/82871), or [unstructured data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstructured_data).[^o3] A **data scientist** is a professional who creates programming code and combines it with statistical knowledge to summarize data.[^o4]

:::

## Scope of data science {#Scope_of_data_science}

Data science plays a critical role in modern [decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making) by enabling organizations to extract actionable insights from large and complex [datasets.](wikipedia://en/65740838)[^o6] Data science also integrates domain knowledge from the underlying application domain (e.g., natural sciences, information technology, and medicine).[^o7] Data science is multifaceted and can be described as a science, a research paradigm, a research method, a discipline, a workflow, and a profession.[^o8]

Data science is "a concept to unify [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), [informatics](wikipedia://en/23997153), and their related [methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method)" to "understand and analyze actual [phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomena)" with [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data).[^o9] It uses techniques and theories drawn from many fields within the context of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science), and [domain knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_knowledge).[^o10] However, data science is distinct from [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and information science. [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) winner [Jim Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gray_%28computer_scientist%29) imagined data science as a "fourth paradigm" of science ([empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_research), [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_research), [computational](wikipedia://en/1181008), and now data-driven) and asserted that "everything about science is changing because of the impact of [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology)" and the [data deluge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_explosion).[^o11][^o12]

Data science is often described as a multidisciplinary and rapidly evolving field because it draws on techniques from diverse areas, such as computer science, statistics, information science, and other subject-specific disciplines. Some researchers argue that the combination of the different fields resembles how information science was decades ago.[^o13] These similarities help clarify how data science gradually became its own field of study.[^o13]

## Foundations {#Foundations}

Data science is an [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinarity) [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline)[^o15] focused on [extracting knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_extraction) from typically [large](wikipedia://en/27051151) [data sets](wikipedia://en/8495) and applying the knowledge from that data to solve problems in other application domains. The field encompasses preparing data for analysis, formulating data science problems, [analyzing](wikipedia://en/1134) data, and summarizing these findings. As such, it incorporates skills from computer science, mathematics, [data visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualization), [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design), [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication), and [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business).[^o16]

[Vasant Dhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasant_Dhar) writes that statistics emphasizes quantitative data and description. In contrast, data science deals with quantitative and qualitative data (e.g., from images, text, sensors, transactions, customer information, etc.) and emphasizes prediction and action.[^o17] [Andrew Gelman](wikipedia://en/25745941) of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) has described statistics as a non-essential part of data science.[^o18] Stanford professor [David Donoho](wikipedia://en/26137723) writes that data science is not distinguished from statistics by the size of datasets or use of computing and that many graduate programs misleadingly advertise their analytics and statistics training as the essence of a data-science program. He describes data science as an applied field growing out of traditional statistics.[^o21]

## Etymology {#Etymology}

### Early usage {#Early_usage}

In 1962, [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974) described a field he called "[data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954)", which resembles modern data science.[^o21] In 1985, in a lecture given to the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Beijing, [C.&nbsp;F. Jeff Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.&nbsp;F._Jeff_Wu) used the term "data science" for the first time as an alternative name for statistics.[^o22] Later, attendees at a 1992 statistics symposium at the [University of Montpellier II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_2_University) acknowledged the emergence of a new discipline focused on data of various origins and forms, combining established concepts and principles of statistics and data analysis with computing.[^o23][^o24]

The term "data science" has been traced back to 1974, when [Peter Naur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Naur) proposed it as an alternative name to computer science. In his 1974 book Concise Survey of Computer Methods, Peter Naur proposed using the term ‘data science’ rather than ‘computer science’ to reflect the growing emphasis on data-driven methods[^o25][^o10] In 1996, the International Federation of Classification Societies became the first conference to specifically feature data science as a topic.[^o10] However, the definition was still in flux. After the 1985 lecture at the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Beijing, in 1997 [C.&nbsp;F. Jeff Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.&nbsp;F._Jeff_Wu) again suggested that statistics should be renamed data science. He reasoned that a new name would help statistics shed inaccurate stereotypes, such as being synonymous with accounting or limited to describing data.[^o26] In 1998, Hayashi Chikio argued for data science as a new, interdisciplinary concept, with three aspects: data design, collection, and analysis.[^o24]

### Modern usage {#Modern_usage}

In 2012, technologists [Thomas H. Davenport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_H._Davenport) and [DJ Patil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Patil) declared "Data Scientist: The Sexiest Job of the 21st Century",[^o28] a catchphrase that was picked up even by major-city newspapers like the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)*[^o29] and the *[Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Globe)*.[^o30] A decade later, they reaffirmed it, stating that "the job is more in demand than ever with employers".[^o31]

The modern conception of data science as an independent discipline is sometimes attributed to [William S. Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Cleveland). In 2014, the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988)'s Section on [Statistical Learning](wikipedia://en/1053303) and [Data Mining](wikipedia://en/42253) changed its name to the Section on Statistical Learning and Data Science, reflecting the ascendant popularity of data science.[^o33]

The professional title of "data scientist" has been attributed to [DJ Patil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Patil) and [Jeff Hammerbacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hammerbacher) in 2008.[^o34] Though it was used by the [National Science Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Board) in their 2005 report "Long-Lived Digital Data Collections: Enabling Research and Education in the 21st Century", it referred broadly to any key role in managing a digital [data collection](wikipedia://en/11501746).[^o35]

## Data science and data analysis {#Data_science_and_data_analysis}

*Image: EDA example - Always plot your data.jpg.* Example of [exploratory data analysis](wikipedia://en/416589) using the [Datasaurus dozen](wikipedia://en/75971902) data set

Data analysis typically involves working with structured datasets to answer specific questions or solve specific problems. This can involve tasks such as [data cleaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_cleaning) and [data visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualization) to summarize data and develop hypotheses about relationships between [variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_%28research%29). [Data analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_analyst) typically use statistical methods to test these hypotheses and draw conclusions from the data.[^o37]

Data science involves working with larger datasets that often require advanced computational and statistical methods to analyze. Data scientists often work with [unstructured data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstructured_data) such as text or images and use [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) algorithms to build predictive models. Data science often uses [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis), [data preprocessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_preprocessing), and [supervised learning](wikipedia://en/20926).[^o38][^o39]

## Cloud computing for data science {#Cloud_computing_for_data_science}

*Image: Cloud computing in enabling data science at scale.jpg.* A cloud-based architecture for enabling big data analytics. Data flows from various sources, such as [personal computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer), [laptops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laptop), and [smart phones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone), through cloud services for processing and analysis, finally leading to various [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151) applications.

[Cloud computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing) can offer access to large amounts of computational power and [storage](wikipedia://en/28174).[^o41] In [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151), where volumes of information are continually generated and processed, these platforms can be used to handle complex and resource-intensive analytical tasks.[^o42]

Some distributed computing frameworks are designed to handle big data workloads. These frameworks can enable data scientists to process and analyze large datasets in parallel, which can reduce processing times.[^o43]

## Ethical consideration in data science {#Ethical_consideration_in_data_science}

Data science involves collecting, processing, and analyzing data which often includes personal and sensitive information. Ethical concerns include potential privacy violations, bias perpetuation, and negative societal impacts.[^o45][^o46]

Machine learning models can amplify existing biases present in training data, leading to discriminatory or unfair outcomes.[^o47][^o48]
