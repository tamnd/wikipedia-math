::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="D7 polytope"}

In 7-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), there are 95 [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821719) with D<sub>7</sub> symmetry; 32 are unique, and 63 are shared with the B<sub>7</sub> symmetry. There are two regular forms, the [7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-orthoplex), and [7-demicube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-demicube) with 14 and 64 vertices respectively.

:::

They can be visualized as symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) in [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) of the D<sub>6</sub> Coxeter group, and other subgroups.

## Graphs {#Graphs}

Symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) of these 32 polytopes can be made in the D<sub>7</sub>, D<sub>6</sub>, D<sub>5</sub>, D<sub>4</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, A<sub>5</sub>, A<sub>3</sub>, [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). A<sub>k</sub> has *\[k+1\]* symmetry, D<sub>k</sub> has *\[2(k-1)\]* symmetry. B<sub>7</sub> is also included although only half of its \[14\] symmetry exists in these polytopes.

These 32 polytopes are each shown in these 8 symmetry planes, with vertices and edges drawn, and vertices colored by the number of overlapping vertices in each projective position in progressive order: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, magenta, red-violet.
