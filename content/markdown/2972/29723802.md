::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="D6 polytope"}

In 6-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), there are 47 [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821728) with D<sub>6</sub> symmetry, of which 16 are unique and 31 are shared with the B<sub>6</sub> symmetry. There are two regular forms, the [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518), and [6-demicube](wikipedia://en/10275945) with 12 and 32 vertices respectively.

:::

They can be visualized as symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) in [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) of the D<sub>6</sub> Coxeter group, and other subgroups.

## Graphs {#Graphs}

Symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) of these 16 polytopes can be made in the D<sub>6</sub>, D<sub>5</sub>, D<sub>4</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, A<sub>5</sub>, A<sub>3</sub>, [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). A<sub>k</sub> has *\[k+1\]* symmetry, D<sub>k</sub> has *\[2(k-1)\]* symmetry. B<sub>6</sub> is also included although only half of its \[12\] symmetry exists in these polytopes.

Each of these 16 polytopes is shown as a projection in the 7 symmetry planes, with vertices and edges drawn, and vertices colored by the number of overlapping vertices in each projective position in progressive order: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, magenta, red-violet.
