# Takashi Ono (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Takashi Ono"}

**Takashi Ono** was a Japanese-born American mathematician, specializing in number theory and algebraic groups.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Ono was born in [Nishinomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishinomiya), Japan, on 18 December 1928. He received his Ph.D. in 1958 at [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University).

## Career {#Career}

Ono immigrated to the United States after receiving an invitation from [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer) to work at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) with a fellowship for the two academic years from 1959 to 1961[^o5] and then went to the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) to work as an assistant professor of mathematics[^o6] from 1961 to 1964. From 1964 to 1969 Ono was a tenured professor at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). From 1969 to his retirement in 2011, he was a professor at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). In 1966 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow).[^o5] In 2012 he was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Ono's youngest son, [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono), is also a mathematician[^o9] and professor at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) as well as a former triathlete.[^o10] His middle son, [Santa J. Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_J._Ono), served as the 15th [President of the University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_University_of_Michigan) (previously the President and Vice-Chancellor of the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia)) and is a biomedical researcher. His eldest son, Momoro Ono, is a music professor at [Creighton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_University).[^o11] Ono died on 11 January 2026, at the age of 97.[^o12]
