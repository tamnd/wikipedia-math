# Ramp function

*Image: Ramp function.svg.* [Graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of the ramp function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ramp function"}

The **ramp function** is a [unary](wikipedia://en/1514907) [real function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_function), whose [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) is shaped like a [ramp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramp). It can be expressed by numerous definitions, for example "0 for negative inputs, output equals input for non-negative inputs". The term "ramp" can also be used for other functions obtained by [scaling and shifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaling_and_shifting), and the function in this article is the *unit* ramp function (slope 1, starting at 0).

:::

In mathematics, the ramp function is also known as the [positive part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_part).

In [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), it is commonly known as a [ReLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier_%28neural_networks%29) [activation function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation_function)[^o2][^o3] or a [rectifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier_%28neural_networks%29) in analogy to [half-wave rectification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-wave_rectification) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) (when used as a [likelihood function](wikipedia://en/44968)) it is known as a [tobit model](wikipedia://en/3722064).

This function has numerous applications in mathematics and engineering, and goes by various names, depending on the context. There are [differentiable variants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier_%28neural_networks%29#Other_non-linear_variants) of the ramp function.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The ramp function ($R(x) : \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R}_{0}^{+}$) may be defined analytically in several ways. Possible definitions are:

- A [piecewise function](wikipedia://en/404130): $$R(x) := \begin{cases} x, & x \ge 0; \\ 0, & x<0 \end{cases}$$
- Using the [Iverson bracket](wikipedia://en/1721092) notation: $$R(x) := x \cdot [x \geq 0]$$ or $$R(x) := x \cdot [x > 0]$$
- The [max function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima): $$R(x) := \max(x,0)$$
- The [mean](wikipedia://en/612) of an [independent variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_variable) and its [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) (a straight line with unity gradient and its modulus): $$R(x) := \frac{x+|x|}{2}$$ this can be derived by noting the following definition of $\max(a, b)$, $$\max(a,b) = \frac{a + b + |a - b|}{2}$$ for which $a = x$ and $b = 0$
- The [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299) multiplied by a straight line with unity gradient: $$R\left( x \right) := x H(x)$$
- The [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) of the Heaviside step function with itself: $$R\left( x \right) := H(x) * H(x)$$
- The [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) of the Heaviside step function: $$R(x) := \int_{-\infty}^{x} H(\xi)\,d\xi$$
- [Macaulay brackets](wikipedia://en/11940501): $$R(x) := \langle x\rangle$$
- The [positive part](wikipedia://en/3344921) of the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069): $$R := \operatorname{id}^+$$
- As a limit function: $$R\left( x \right) := \lim_{a\to \infty} \begin{cases} \frac{1}{a} ,\quad x=0 \\ \dfrac{x}{1-e^{-ax}},\quad x\neq 0\end{cases}$$

It could approximated as close as desired by choosing an increasing positive value $a>0$.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The ramp function has numerous applications in engineering, such as in the theory of [digital signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing).

*Image: Long call option.svg.* Payoff and profits from buying a [call option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_option)

In [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), the payoff of a [call option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_option) is a ramp (shifted by *strike price*). Horizontally flipping a ramp yields a [put option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_option), while vertically flipping (taking the negative) corresponds to *selling* or being "short" an option. In finance, the shape is widely called a "[hockey stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_stick)", due to the shape being similar to an [ice hockey stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_stick).

*Image: Friedmans mars hinge functions.png.* A mirrored pair of [hinge functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_adaptive_regression_splines#Hinge_functions) with a knot at x=3.1

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [hinge functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_adaptive_regression_splines#Hinge_functions) of [multivariate adaptive regression splines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_adaptive_regression_splines) (MARS) are ramps, and are used to build [regression models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_model).

:::

## Analytic properties {#Analytic_properties}

### Non-negativity {#Non-negativity}

In the whole [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) the function is non-negative, so its [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) is itself, i.e.

$$\forall x \in \mathbb{R}: R(x) \geq 0$$

and

$$\left| R (x) \right| = R(x)$$

::: {#o11 .proof cue=math-proof}

by the mean of definition 2, it is non-negative in the first quarter, and zero in the second; so everywhere it is non-negative.

:::

### Derivative {#Derivative}

Its derivative is the [Heaviside step function](wikipedia://en/87299):

$$R'(x) = H(x)\quad \mbox{for } x \ne 0.$$

### Second derivative {#Second_derivative}

The ramp function satisfies the differential equation:

$$\frac{d^2}{dx^2} R(x - x_0) = \delta(x - x_0),$$

where $\delta(x)$ is the [Dirac delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_delta). This means that $R(x)$ is a [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) for the second derivative operator. Thus, any function, $f(x)$, with an integrable second derivative, $f''(x)$, will satisfy the equation:

$$f(x) = f(a) + (x-a) f'(a) + \int_{a}^b R(x - s) f''(s) \,ds \quad \mbox{for }a < x < b .$$

### Antiderivative {#Antiderivative}

Ramp function has the following antiderivative:

$$\int {R(x)dx} = \frac{x^2 + x\vert x \vert}{4} + C = \frac{x}{2}R(x) +C\,,$$

where $C$ is the [integration constant](wikipedia://en/147939).

### [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) {#Fourier_transform}

$$\mathcal{F}\big\{ R(x) \big\}(f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} R(x) e^{-2\pi ifx} \, dx = \frac{i\delta '(f)}{4\pi}-\frac{1}{4 \pi^2 f^2},$$

where $\delta(x)$ is the [Dirac delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_delta) (in this formula, its [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) appears).

### [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) {#Laplace_transform}

The single-sided [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of $R(x)$ is given as follows,[^o17]

$$\mathcal{L}\big\{R(x)\big\} (s) = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-sx}R(x)dx = \frac{1}{s^2}.$$

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

### Iteration invariance {#Iteration_invariance}

Every [iterated function](wikipedia://en/1448702) of the ramp mapping is itself, as

$$R \big( R(x) \big) = R(x) .$$

::: {#o21 .proof cue=math-proof}

$$R \big( R(x) \big) := \frac{R(x)+|R(x)|}{2} = \frac{R(x)+R(x)}{2} = R(x) .$$

This applies the non-negative property.

:::
