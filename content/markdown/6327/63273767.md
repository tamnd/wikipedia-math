# Geodesic bicombing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geodesic bicombing"}

In [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), a **geodesic bicombing** distinguishes a class of [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). The study of metric spaces with distinguished geodesics traces back to the work of the mathematician [Herbert Busemann](wikipedia://en/25491802).[^o2] The convention to call a collection of paths of a metric space bicombing is due to [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190).[^o3] By imposing a weak global [non-positive curvature](wikipedia://en/12989803) condition on a geodesic bicombing several results from the theory of [CAT(0) spaces](wikipedia://en/12941912) and [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) theory may be recovered in a more general setting.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(X,d)$ be a metric space. A map $\sigma\colon X\times X\times [0,1]\to X$ is a *geodesic bicombing* if for all points $x,y\in X$ the map $\sigma_{xy}(\cdot):=\sigma(x,y,\cdot)$ is a unit speed metric geodesic from $x$ to $y$, that is, $\sigma_{xy}(0)=x$, $\sigma_{xy}(1)=y$ and $d(\sigma_{xy}(s), \sigma_{xy}(t))=\vert s-t\vert d(x,y)$ for all real numbers $s,t\in [0,1]$.[^o6]

:::

## Different classes of geodesic bicombings {#Different_classes_of_geodesic_bicombings}

A geodesic bicombing $\sigma\colon X\times X\times [0,1]\to X$ is:

- *reversible* if $$\sigma_{xy}(t)=\sigma_{yx}(1-t)$$ for all $x,y\in X$ and $t\in [0,1]$.
- *consistent* if $$\sigma_{xy}((1-\lambda)s+\lambda t)=\sigma_{pq}(\lambda)$$ whenever $x,y\in X, 0\leq s\leq t\leq 1, p:=\sigma_{xy}(s), q:=\sigma_{xy}(t),$and $\lambda\in [0,1]$.
- *conical* if $$d(\sigma_{xy}(t), \sigma_{x^\prime y^\prime}(t))\leq (1-t)d(x,x^\prime)+t d(y,y^\prime)$$ for all $x,x^\prime, y, y^\prime\in X$ and $t\in [0,1]$.
- *convex* if $$t\mapsto d(\sigma_{xy}(t), \sigma_{x^\prime y^\prime}(t))$$ is a convex function on $[0,1]$ for all $x,x^\prime, y, y^\prime\in X$.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of metric spaces with a conical geodesic bicombing include:

- Banach spaces.
- CAT(0) spaces.
- [injective metric spaces](wikipedia://en/7840768).
- the spaces $(P_1(X),W_1),$ where $W_1$ is the first [Wasserstein distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserstein_distance).
- any [ultralimit](wikipedia://en/18609360) or 1-Lipschitz [retraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retraction_%28category_theory%29) of the above.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every consistent conical geodesic bicombing is convex.
- Every convex geodesic bicombing is conical, but the reverse implication does not hold in general.
- Every [proper metric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space) with a conical geodesic bicombing admits a convex geodesic bicombing.[^o6]
- Every [complete metric space](wikipedia://en/7832) with a conical geodesic bicombing admits a reversible conical geodesic bicombing.[^o12]

:::
