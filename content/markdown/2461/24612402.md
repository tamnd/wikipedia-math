# List of isotoxal polyhedra and tilings

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of isotoxal polyhedra and tilings"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra) and [tilings](wikipedia://en/321671) are defined by the property that they have symmetries taking any edge to any other edge.[^o2] Polyhedra with this property can also be called "edge-transitive", but they should be distinguished from [edge-transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/584406), where the symmetries are combinatorial rather than geometric.

:::

[Regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra) are isohedral (face-transitive), isogonal (vertex-transitive), and isotoxal (edge-transitive).

[Quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651) polyhedra are isogonal and isotoxal, but not isohedral; their duals are isohedral and isotoxal, but not isogonal.

The dual of an isotoxal polyhedron is also an isotoxal polyhedron. (See the [Dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) article.)

## Convex isotoxal polyhedra {#Convex_isotoxal_polyhedra}

The dual of a convex polyhedron is also a convex polyhedron.[^o4]

There are nine [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) isotoxal polyhedra based on the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905): the five (regular) Platonic solids, the two ([quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651)) common cores of dual Platonic solids, and their two duals.

The [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the quasiregular forms are (squares or) rectangles; the vertex figures of the duals of the quasiregular forms are (equilateral triangles and equilateral triangles, or) equilateral triangles and squares, or equilateral triangles and regular pentagons.
