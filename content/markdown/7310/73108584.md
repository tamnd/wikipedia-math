# Richard Maunder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Maunder"}

**Charles Richard Francis Maunder** (23 November 1937 – 5 June 2018) was a British mathematician and musicologist.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Maunder was educated at the [Royal Grammar School, High Wycombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Grammar_School,_High_Wycombe), and [Jesus College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge), before going on to complete a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) at [Christ’s College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ’s_College,_Cambridge), in 1962. After teaching at [Southampton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_University) he became a fellow of Christ’s in 1964.[^o4]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Maunder's field of work was [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He used [Postnikov systems](wikipedia://en/15708202) to give an alternative construction of the [Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/6795342). With this construction, the differentials can be better described.[^o6][^o7] The family of higher [cohomology operations](wikipedia://en/1226719) on mod-2 cohomology that he constructed has been discussed by several authors.[^o8][^o9][^o10] In 1981 he gave a short proof of the [Kan-Thurston theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan-Thurston_theorem),[^o11] according to which for every [path-connected](wikipedia://en/6233#Path_connectedness) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) X there is a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) π such that there is a [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) K(π,1) after X. His textbook *Algebraic Topology* (1970) continues to circulate in the 1996 Dover edition.

## Musicology {#Musicology}

Maunder created a new version of [Mozart's Requiem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_%28Mozart%29). Following on from other musicologists such as [Ernst Hess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Hess_%28composer%29), [Franz Beyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beyer_%28musicologist%29) and [Robert D. Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Levin_%28musicologist%29), he presented a fundamental revision of Mozart's last work, in which, like his predecessors, he wanted to remove [Süssmayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Xaver_Süssmayr)'s additions as far as possible and replace them with Mozart's own ideas. This new version was recorded by [Christopher Hogwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hogwood) with the [Academy of Ancient Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Ancient_Music) in 1983 and the score was published in 1988.[^o13] In 1992 it was recorded by Rupert Gottfried Frieberger.[^o14]

In doing so, Maunder rejected Süssmayr's *Sanctus* and *Benedictus* completely and removed them from the work; he considered only the *Agnus Dei* to be authentic because of its comparisons with other church music works by Mozart. Maunder also composed an *Amen* fugue for the conclusion of the *Lacrimosa*, for which he took Mozart's sketch sheet and a fugue for organ roll by Mozart (K. 608) as a starting point. He also fundamentally revised Süssmayr's instrumentation throughout the Requiem.[^o15]

This version was performed several times in German-speaking countries, including a dance version *Requiem!* by Birgit Scherzer.

Maunder's edition of Mozart's [C minor Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mass_in_C_minor,_K._427) was published in 1990[^o16] and was first recorded by Hogwood in the same year. Maunder edited also pieces by Francesco Geminiani, Tomaso Albinoni, Henry Purcell, members of the Bach Family, Giuseppe Sammartini and others. https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Maunder,\_Richard
