# Dimensionless numbers in fluid mechanics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dimensionless numbers in fluid mechanics"}

*[Dimensionless numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_numbers)* (or *characteristic numbers*) have an important role in analyzing the behavior of [fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) and their flow as well as in other [transport phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_phenomena).[^o2] They include the [Reynolds](wikipedia://en/23868856) and the [Mach numbers](wikipedia://en/20051), which describe as ratios the relative magnitude of fluid and physical system characteristics, such as [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density), [viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754), [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853), and [flow speed](wikipedia://en/20491903). To compare a real situation (e.g. an [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft)) with a small-scale model it is necessary to keep the important characteristic numbers the same. Names and formulation of these numbers were standardized in [ISO 31-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_31-12) and in [ISO 80000-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_80000-11).

:::

As a general example of how dimensionless numbers arise in fluid mechanics, the classical numbers in [transport phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_phenomena) of [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_mass), [momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_momentum), and [energy](wikipedia://en/67088) are principally analyzed by the ratio of effective [diffusivities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusivity) in each transport mechanism. The six [dimensionless numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) give the relative strengths of the different phenomena of [inertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia), [viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754), [conductive heat transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_conduction), and diffusive [mass transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transport). (In the table, the diagonals give common symbols for the quantities, and the given dimensionless number is the ratio of the left column quantity over top row quantity; e.g. [Re](wikipedia://en/23868856) = inertial force/viscous force = *vd*/[*ν*](wikipedia://en/18963754).) These same quantities may alternatively be expressed as ratios of characteristic time, length, or energy scales. Such forms are less commonly used in practice, but can provide insight into particular applications.

Droplet formation mostly depends on momentum, viscosity and surface tension.[^o7] In [inkjet printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkjet_printing) for example, an ink with a too high [Ohnesorge number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohnesorge_number) would not jet properly, and an ink with a too low Ohnesorge number would be jetted with many satellite drops.[^o8] These dimensionless ratios can be obtained by relating each term in a consistent form, such as energy per volume or pressure. For example, the ratios of characteristic pressures (or energies per volume) for [inertial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_pressure), viscous, gravity, and [surface tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace_pressure) effects gives dimensionless ratios of each pair. More fundamentally, these and other dimensionless numbers are derived from [dimensional analysis](wikipedia://en/8267) and/or [non-dimensionalization of the Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-dimensionalization_and_scaling_of_the_Navier–Stokes_equations). Not all of the quantity ratios are explicitly named, though each of the unnamed ratios could be expressed as a product of two other named dimensionless numbers.
