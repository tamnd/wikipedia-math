# Mahesh Kakde

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mahesh Kakde"}

**Mahesh Ramesh Kakde**[^o3] (born 1983) is a mathematician working in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mahesh Kakde was born on 1983 in [Akola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akola), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India).[^o5] He obtained a [Bachelor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Mathematics) degree at the [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418) in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) in 2004, and a [Certificate of Advanced Study in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 2005.[^o5] He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) under the supervision of [John Coates](wikipedia://en/230705) at the University of Cambridge in 2008.[^o3][^o5] He subsequently worked at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), and [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London), before becoming a professor at the [Indian Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Science) in 2019.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Kakde proved the [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332) in the [totally real](wikipedia://en/312853) [$\mu = 0$ case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwasawa's_μ-invariant).[^o7] Together with [Samit Dasgupta](wikipedia://en/64749132) and Kevin Ventullo, he proved the [Gross–Stark conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross–Stark_conjecture).[^o8] In a joint project with Samit Dasgupta, they proved the [Brumer–Stark conjecture](wikipedia://en/20510092) away from 2 in 2020,[^o9] and later over $\mathbb Z$ in 2023.[^o10] Generalising these methods, they also gave a solution to [Hilbert's 12th problem](wikipedia://en/2164726) for totally real fields.[^o11][^o12] Their methods were subsequently used by Johnston and Nickel to prove the equivariant Iwasawa main conjecture for [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) without the $\mu = 0$ hypothesis.[^o13]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2019, Kakde was awarded a [Swarnajayanti Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarnajayanti_Fellowship).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18]

Together with Samit Dasgupta, Kakde was one of the invited speakers at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) 2022, where they gave a joint talk on their work on the Brumer–Stark conjecture.[^o19][^o20]

In 2022, Kakde received the [Infosys Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys_Prize) for his contributions to algebraic number theory.[^o21] In his congratulatory message, Jury Chair [Chandrashekhar Khare](wikipedia://en/11350853) noted that "\[Kakde’s\] work on the main conjecture of non-commutative Iwasawa theory, on the Gross-Stark conjecture and on the Brumer-Stark conjecture has had a big impact on the field of algebraic number theory. His work makes important progress towards a p-adic analytic analog of Hilbert’s 12th problem on construction of abelian extensions of number fields."[^o21]

In 2023, Kakde became a laureate of the [Asian Scientist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist_100) by the *[Asian Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist)*.[^o22]
