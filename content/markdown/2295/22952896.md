# Zonal spherical harmonics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zonal spherical harmonics"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) study of [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096), the **zonal spherical harmonics** are special [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056) that are invariant under the rotation through a particular fixed axis. The [zonal spherical functions](wikipedia://en/17708915) are a broad extension of the notion of zonal spherical harmonics to allow for a more general [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186).

:::

On the two-dimensional sphere, the unique zonal spherical harmonic of degree ℓ invariant under rotations fixing the north pole is represented in [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates) by

$$Z^{(\ell)}(\theta,\phi) = \frac{2\ell + 1}{4 \pi} P_\ell(\cos\theta)$$

where $P_{\ell}$ is the normalized [Legendre polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendre_polynomial) of degree $\ell$, $P_\ell(1) = 1$. The generic zonal spherical harmonic of degree ℓ is denoted by $Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})$, where **x** is a point on the sphere representing the fixed axis, and **y** is the variable of the function. This can be obtained by rotation of the basic zonal harmonic $Z^{(\ell)}(\theta,\phi).$

In *n*-dimensional Euclidean space, zonal spherical harmonics are defined as follows. Let **x** be a point on the (*n*−1)-sphere. Define $Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}$ to be the [dual representation](wikipedia://en/26452) of the linear functional

$$P\mapsto P(\mathbf{x})$$

in the finite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal H_\ell$ of spherical harmonics of degree $\ell$ with respect to the [uniform measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on the sphere $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$. In other words, we have a [reproducing kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproducing_kernel):

$$Y(\mathbf{x}) = \int_{S^{n-1}} Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})Y(\mathbf{y})\,d\Omega(y), \quad \forall Y \in \mathcal H_\ell$$

where $\Omega$ is the uniform measure on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$.

## Relationship with harmonic potentials {#Relationship_with_harmonic_potentials}

The zonal harmonics appear naturally as coefficients of the [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583) for the unit ball in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>: for **x** and **y** unit vectors,

$$\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{1-r^2}{|\mathbf{x}-r\mathbf{y}|^n} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty r^k Z^{(k)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}),$$

where $\omega_{n-1}$ is the surface area of the (n-1)-dimensional sphere. They are also related to the [Newton kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_kernel) via

$$\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{n-2}} = \sum_{k=0}^\infty c_{n,k} \frac{|\mathbf{x}|^k}{|\mathbf{y}|^{n+k-2}}Z_{\mathbf{x}/|\mathbf{x}|}^{(k)}(\mathbf{y}/|\mathbf{y}|)$$

where $\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y} \in \mathbf{R}^{n}$ and the constants $c_{n,k}$ are given by

$$c_{n,k} = \frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\frac{2k+n-2}{(n-2)}.$$

The coefficients of the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) of the Newton kernel (with suitable normalization) are precisely the [ultraspherical polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraspherical_polynomials). Thus, the zonal spherical harmonics can be expressed as follows. If $\alpha = (n-2)/2$, then

$$Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}) = \frac{n+2\ell-2}{n-2}C_\ell^{(\alpha)}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y})$$

where $c_{n, \ell}$ are the constants above and $C_\ell^{(\alpha)}$ is the ultraspherical polynomial of degree $\ell$. The 2-dimensional case

$$Z^{(\ell)}(\theta,\phi) = \frac{2\ell + 1}{4 \pi} P_\ell(\cos\theta)$$

is a special case of that, since the Legendre polynomials are the special case of the ultraspherical polynomial when $\alpha = 1/2$.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The zonal spherical harmonics are rotationally invariant, meaning that $$Z^{(\ell)}_{R\mathbf{x}}(R\mathbf{y}) = Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y})$$ for every orthogonal transformation *R*. Conversely, any function $f(x,y)$ on $S^{n-1}\times S^{n-1}$ that is a spherical harmonic in *y* for each fixed *x*, and that satisfies this invariance property, is a constant multiple of the degree $\ell$ zonal harmonic.
- If *Y*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *Y*<sub>*d*</sub> is an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of $\mathbf{H}_{\ell}$, then $$Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y}) = \sum_{k=1}^d Y_k(\mathbf{x})\overline{Y_k(\mathbf{y})}.$$
- Evaluating at $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{y}$ gives $$Z^{(\ell)}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}) = \omega_{n-1}^{-1} \dim \mathbf{H}_\ell.$$

:::
