# Uniform honeycombs in hyperbolic space

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Find the complete set of hyperbolic uniform honeycombs.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform honeycombs in hyperbolic space"}

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), a **uniform honeycomb in hyperbolic space** is a uniform [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of [uniform polyhedral](wikipedia://en/2537223) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29). In 3-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) there are nine [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) families of compact [convex uniform honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961), generated as [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777), and represented by [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of [rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram#Application_with_uniform_polytopes) of the [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) for each family.

:::

## Hyperbolic uniform honeycomb families {#Hyperbolic_uniform_honeycomb_families}

Honeycombs are divided between compact and paracompact forms defined by [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004), the first category only including finite cells and vertex figures (finite subgroups), and the second includes affine subgroups.

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Compact uniform honeycomb"}

### Compact uniform honeycomb families {#Compact_uniform_honeycomb_families}

The nine compact [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) are listed here with their [Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram),[^o9] in order of the relative volumes of their [fundamental simplex domains](wikipedia://en/469365).[^o10]

These 9 families generate a total of 76 unique uniform honeycombs. The full list of hyperbolic uniform honeycombs has not been proven and an unknown number of non-Wythoffian forms exist. Two known examples are cited with the {3,5,3} family below. Only two families are related as a mirror-removal halving: \[5,3<sup>1,1</sup>\] ↔ \[5,3,4,1<sup>\+</sup>\].

There are just two radical subgroups with non-simplicial domains that can be generated by removing a set of two or more mirrors separated by all other mirrors by even-order branches. One is \[(4,3,4,3<sup>\*</sup>)\], represented by Coxeter diagrams an index 6 subgroup with a [trigonal trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/3504388) [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) ↔ , which can be extended by restoring one mirror as . The other is \[4,(3,5)<sup>\*</sup>\], index 120 with a [dodecahedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecahedral) fundamental domain.

:::

### Paracompact hyperbolic uniform honeycombs {#Paracompact_hyperbolic_uniform_honeycombs}

There are also 23 [paracompact Coxeter groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram#Paracompact_%28Koszul_simplex_groups%29) of rank 4 that produce paracompact uniform honeycombs with infinite or unbounded [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) or [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719), including [ideal vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_vertex) at infinity.

### Other non-Wythoffians {#Other_non-Wythoffians}

There are infinitely many known non-Wythoffian uniform compact hyperbolic honeycombs, and there may be more undiscovered ones. Two have been listed above as diminishings of the icosahedral honeycomb {3,5,3}.[^o47]

In 1997 Wendy Krieger discovered an infinite series of uniform hyperbolic honeycombs with [pseudoicosahedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoicosahedron) vertex figures, made from 8 cubes and 12 *p*-gonal prisms at a vertex for any integer *p*. In the case *p* = 4 (a regular icosahedron), all cells are cubes and the result is the order-5 cubic honeycomb. The case *p* = 2 (with the pseudoicosahedron degenerating to a regular octahedron) degenerates to the Euclidean [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318).[^o47]

Another four known ones are related to *noncompact* families. The tessellation consists of [truncated cubes](wikipedia://en/280529) and infinite [order-8 triangular tilings](wikipedia://en/38323559) . However the latter intersect the sphere at infinity orthogonally, having exactly the same curvature as the hyperbolic space, and can be replaced by mirror images of the remainder of the tessellation, resulting in a *compact* uniform honeycomb consisting only of the truncated cubes. (So they are analogous to the hemi-faces of spherical [hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemipolyhedra).)[^o47][^o48] Something similar can be done with the tessellation consisting of [small rhombicuboctahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_rhombicuboctahedron) , infinite [order-8 triangular tilings](wikipedia://en/38323559) , and infinite [order-8 square tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-8_square_tiling) . The order-8 square tilings already intersect the sphere at infinity orthogonally, and if the order-8 triangular tilings are augmented with a set of [triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/2859584), the surface passing through their centre points also intersects the sphere at infinity orthogonally. After replacing with mirror images, the result is a compact honeycomb containing the small rhombicuboctahedra and the triangular prisms.[^o49]

Two more such constructions were discovered in 2023 by Daniel Sebald. The first one arises from the fact that and have the same circumradius; the former has [truncated octahedra](wikipedia://en/279654) and [order-6 square tilings](wikipedia://en/38249159) , while the latter has [cuboctahedra](wikipedia://en/6280) and order-6 square tilings . A compact uniform honeycomb is taken by discarding the order-6 square tilings they have in common, using only the truncated octahedra and cuboctahedra. The second one arises from a similar construction involving (which has [small rhombicosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/285486) , [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635) , and [order-4 pentagonal tilings](wikipedia://en/9421111) ) and (which is the prism of the order-4 pentagonal tiling, having [pentagonal prisms](wikipedia://en/2859659) and order-4 pentagonal tilings ). These two likewise have the same circumradius, and a compact uniform honeycomb is obtained by taking only the finite cells of both, discarding the order-4 pentagonal tilings they have in common.[^o50] In 2025, Sebald discovered a third such construction, taking the tessellation that consists of [small rhombicuboctahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_rhombicuboctahedron) , [octagonal prisms](wikipedia://en/3026569) , and tetraoctagonal tilings , and noting that the tetraoctagonal tilings have the same curvature as the whole honeycomb and so can be replaced by mirror images of the remainder of the tessellation. This yields a compact uniform honeycomb consisting only of the small rhombicuboctahedra and the octagonal prisms.[^o47]

Another non-Wythoffian was discovered in 2021. It has as vertex figure a snub cube with 8 vertices removed and contains two [octahedra](wikipedia://en/80177635) and eight [snub cubes](wikipedia://en/180249) at each vertex.[^o47] Subsequently Krieger found a non-Wythoffian with a snub cube as the vertex figure, containing 32 tetrahedra and 6 octahedra at each vertex, and that the truncated and rectified versions of this honeycomb are still uniform. In 2022, Richard Klitzing generalised this construction to use any snub as vertex figure: the result is compact for p=4 or 5 (with a snub cube or [snub dodecahedral](wikipedia://en/287137) vertex figure respectively), paracompact for p=6 (with a [snub trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864718) as the vertex figure), and hypercompact for p>6. Again, the truncated and rectified versions of these honeycombs are still uniform. The cases p=3 and p=2 are spherical (they are the regular [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) and [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422), respectively).[^o47]

There are also other forms based on [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) domains. Two known forms generalise the [cubic-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/42747363), having distorted small rhombicuboctahedral vertex figures. One form has small rhombicuboctahedra, cuboctahedra, and cubes; another has small rhombicosidodecahedra, [icosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/15023), and cubes. (The version with tetrahedral-symmetry polyhedra is the cubic-octahedral honeycomb, using cuboctahedra, octahedra, and cubes).[^o50]

In 2025 another non-Wythoffian was discovered, whose vertex figure is the central section of the [rectified 600-cell](wikipedia://en/2720121) (oriented to have an icosahedral cell on the top). Its cells are tetrahedra, icosidodecahedra, and pentagonal prisms.[^o47]

### Nonconvex cases {#Nonconvex_cases}

There are no *regular* nonconvex compact uniform honeycombs in hyperbolic space: all forms with a regular star polyhedron as cell, vertex figure or both end up being spherical. But this does not exclude uniform nonconvex compact uniform honeycombs. One known example arises from taking the icosahedral honeycomb and replacing each icosahedron by an inscribed [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441), whose pentagonal faces are connected by dodecahedra. This honeycomb has as its vertex figure a uniform [small ditrigonal icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157350). Having a uniform vertex figure, it can furthermore be [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429), producing a uniform honeycomb with [truncated dodecahedra](wikipedia://en/376538), [truncated great dodecahedra](wikipedia://en/4157430), and small ditrigonal icosidodecahedra; or [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366), producing a uniform honeycomb with [icosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/15023), [dodecadodecahedra](wikipedia://en/4157379), and small ditrigonal icosidodecahedra.[^o47]

It is also possible to start with the truncated icosahedral honeycomb, which has dodecahedra and truncated icosahedra, and replace the dodecahedra with small ditrigonal icosidodecahedra having the same vertices and the truncated icosahedra with [small snub icosicosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/4157567) with the same vertices. The cells can then be connected by tetrahedra, and the result slightly distorted to return to uniformity. Something similar can be done with the cantellated order-5 dodecahedral honeycomb, which has small rhombicosidodecahedra, pentagonal prisms, and icosidodecahedra: the small rhombicosidodecahedra are replaced by [small icosicosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/4157496), the gaps filled by truncated icosahedra, and uniformity recovered by reverting to all unit edge lengths.[^o47]
