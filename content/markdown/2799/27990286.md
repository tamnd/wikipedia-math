# Octagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **octagonal tiling** is a [regular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hyperbolic_tiling) of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It is represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of *{8,3}*, having three regular [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575) around each vertex. It also has a construction as a truncated order-8 square tiling, t{4,8}.

:::

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

Like the [hexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2863719#Uniform_colorings) of the Euclidean plane, there are 3 uniform colorings of this hyperbolic tiling. The dual tiling V8.8.8 represents the [fundamental domains](wikipedia://en/469365) of \[(4,4,4)\] symmetry.

## Regular maps {#Regular_maps}

The [regular map](wikipedia://en/21533914) {8,3}<sub>2,0</sub> can be seen as a 6-coloring of the {8,3} hyperbolic tiling. Within the regular map, octagons of the same color are considered the same face shown in multiple locations. The 2,0 subscripts show the same color will repeat by moving 2 steps in a straight direction following opposite edges. This regular map also has a representation as a double covering of a cube, represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {8/2,3}, with 6 octagonal faces, double wrapped {8/2}, with 24 edges, and 16 vertices. It was described by [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) in his 2003 paper *Are Your Polyhedra the Same as My Polyhedra?*[^o5]

*Image: Double-cube-regular-map.png.*

## Related polyhedra and tilings {#Related_polyhedra_and_tilings}

This tiling is topologically part of sequence of regular polyhedra and tilings with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {n,3}.

And also is topologically part of sequence of regular tilings with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {8,n}.

From a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) there are ten hyperbolic [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/18640974) that can be based from the regular octagonal tiling.

Drawing the tiles colored as red on the original faces, yellow at the original vertices, and blue along the original edges, there are 10 forms.
