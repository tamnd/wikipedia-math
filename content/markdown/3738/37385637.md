# Margaret Meyer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Margaret Meyer"}

**Margaret Theodora Meyer** (September 1862 – 27 January 1924), also known as **Maud Meyer**[^o4] was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). She was one of the first directors of studies in mathematics, and one of the earliest members of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o5] In 1916, she was one of the first women to be elected a fellow of the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society).[^o7]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Meyer was born in [Strabane, Tyrone, Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabane), to a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian) minister, Theodore Jonas Meyer, and his wife Jane Ann. She had an older brother, Sir [William Stevenson Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Stevenson_Meyer), who served as [first high commissioner for India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_High_Commissioners_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_India).[^o7] Meyer spent much of her childhood in Italy. She attended the [North London Collegiate School for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_London_Collegiate_School_for_Girls), then enrolled at [Girton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge) in 1879, graduating 15th [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in mathematics 1882. In 1907, she was awarded an [ad eundem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_eundem_degree) MA by [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin).[^o8]

She taught at [Notting Hill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notting_Hill_and_Ealing_High_School), in London, from 1882 to 1888, and then became a resident lecturer in mathematics at Girton College, where she remained for 30 years. During World War I, Meyer undertook calculational work for the British [War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Office) in her spare time. In 1918 she resigned from work at the college and worked for the British [Air Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Ministry), which related to aircraft design and construction.[^o7]

Meyer had an interest in astronomy, as part of her degree concerned [mathematical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_astronomy). She carried out much unpublished work on the subject. In 1916, she was one of the first women to be elected to the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society) along with [A. Grace Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Grace_Cook), [Fiammetta Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiammetta_Wilson), Ella Church, [Mary Blagg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Adela_Blagg) and Irene Elizabeth Toye Warner.[^o7]

## Other activities {#Other_activities}

Meyer carved, and supervised students in the carving, of the oak paneling around the chancel of the [college chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge#Chapel) at Girton College.[^o8] She also had a passion for mountain climbing, and was a member and later president of the [Ladies' Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladies'_Alpine_Club) (1916-1919).[^o10][^o11] She regularly visited the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Alps) to pursue her mountaineering interests; her final visit was during the 1923, only a few months before her death.[^o12]

## Death {#Death}

Meyer died, aged 61, in a collision with a bus, in [Paddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddington),[^o14] while cycling in 1924.[^o3] In her will, she bequeathed [£](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/£sd)2000 to Girton College for the benefit of women mathematics students,[^o15] an additional £1000, and a collection of mathematics books.[^o8]
