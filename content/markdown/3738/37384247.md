# Nanny Wermuth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nanny Wermuth"}

**Nanny Wermuth** (born 4 December 1943) is the Professor [emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of Statistics, [Chalmers University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalmers_University_of_Technology)/[University of Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gothenburg).[^o3] Her research interests are Multivariate statistical models and their properties, especially graphical Markov models, as well as their applications in the life sciences and in the natural sciences.

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

### Education {#Education}

- 1967 First degree in Economics (Diplom-Volkswirtin), [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich)
- 1972 Degree in Statistics (Doctor of Philosophy), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University)
- 1977 Degree in Medical Statistics (Professor), [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz)

### Professional positions {#Professional_positions}

- 1972–1978 Research Assistant in Statistics; [University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dortmund), University of Mainz
- since 1978 Professor of Statistics and of Methods in Psychology, University of Mainz
- 1997–2000 Head of Research and Development, Center of Survey Research, Mannheim
- since 2003 Professor of Statistics, Department of Mathematical Sciences at Chalmers University of Technology and University of Gothenburg

### Selected services to the profession {#Selected_services_to_the_profession}

- 1993–2001 Coordinating member of the [European Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Science_Foundation) network HSSS
- 1993–2003 Editorial Advisor for the Springer Series of Statistics
- 1995–1996 President, German Region of the International Biometric Society
- 2000–2001 President, [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society)
- 2001–2004 Chair of the Life Science Committee of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute)
- 2008–2009 President, [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics)
- 2007–2010 Associate Editor of Bernoulli

### Selected recognitions {#Selected_recognitions}

- 1968–1972 Stipends of the Fulbright Commission, of the International Peace Scholarship Fund and of Harvard University
- 1984–1985 Fulbright Scholar, Department of Statistics, [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University)
- 1992 Max Planck-Research Prize, jointly with [Sir David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771), [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford)
- 2001 Short term Research Fellowship, [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University)
- 2001–2002 Invited Research Fellow at Harvard’s [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Study)
- 2011–2012 Senior Scientist Research Award, [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer)

### Professional affiliations {#Professional_affiliations}

She was elected as member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) (1982) and of the German Academy of Sciences (2002); as fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (1989) and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) (2001).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

She has four sons with Dr. Dieter Wermuth - [Jochen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochen_Wermuth) (1969) Martin (1974), Peter (1976) and Ulli (1981)
