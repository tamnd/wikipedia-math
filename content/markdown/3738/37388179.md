# Frances Hardcastle

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Frances Hardcastle"}

**Frances Hardcastle** (13 August 1866 – 26 December 1941) was an English mathematician, in 1894 one of the founding members of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4] Her work included contributions to the theory of [point groups](wikipedia://en/1138942).[^o9]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Writtle, just outside [Chelmsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelmsford), [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex),[^o9][^o6] Hardcastle was a daughter of Henry Hardcastle, a barrister,[^o7] by his marriage in 1865 to Maria Sophia Herschel,[^o8] daughter of the astronomer, mathematician, and chemist [Sir John Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Herschel).[^o9]

She was educated at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge) (Tripos Part I 1891 & Part II 1892), and obtained a Certificate in Mathematics.[^o9][^o10]

In 1892, she went to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) for a year as an honorary fellow, then spent another year at [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) studying under [Charlotte Scott](wikipedia://en/8610998). While at Bryn Mawr she was president of the Graduate Club and translated [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887)'s book *On Riemann's Theory of Algebraic functions and Integrals*. In 1895, she recommenced postgraduate studies at Cambridge, and within a few years had published several papers on point-groups. She earned a BA degree from the University of London in 1903.[^o9] [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) awarded her an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin%29) ([ad eundem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_eundem_degree)) in 1905.

Hardcastle was one of 156 British women who publicly supported the aims of the [International Congress of Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Congress_of_Women#The_Hague.2C_April._E2.80.93May_1915), held in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague) in April 1915. These aims were, "1. To demand that international disputes shall in future be settled by some other means than war," and "2. To claim that women shall have a voice in the affairs of nations."[^o11] Until 1909, she was an Honorary Secretary of the [National Union of Women's Suffrage Societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Women's_Suffrage_Societies) (NUWSS).[^o12]

Hardcastle was the lifelong companion of [Dr Ethel Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethel_Williams_%28physician%29), a physician, [Justice of the Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace), feminist, and social reformer.[^o13] She died at the Royal Hotel, Cambridge, on 26 December 1941, when her home address was stated as Low Bridges, [Stocksfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stocksfield), [Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland), and left a substantial estate valued at £4,400. Probate was granted to Ethel Williams and Mabel Annie Burnip.[^o14]
