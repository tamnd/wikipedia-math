# Radical symbol

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Radical symbol"}

In mathematics, the **radical symbol**, **radical sign**, **root symbol**, or **surd** is a [symbol](wikipedia://en/277184) for the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) or [higher-order root](wikipedia://en/235029) of a number. The square root of a number $x$ is written as

:::

$$\sqrt{x},$$

while the $n$th root of $x$ is written as

$$\sqrt[n]{x}.$$

It is also used for other meanings in more advanced mathematics, such as the [radical of an ideal](wikipedia://en/252009).

In [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), the symbol is used to denote a [root word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_word).

## Principal square root {#Principal_square_root}

Each positive real number has two square roots, one positive and the other negative. The radical symbol refers to the [principal value](wikipedia://en/408225) of the square root function called the principal square root, which is the positive one. The two square roots of a negative number are both [imaginary numbers](wikipedia://en/14554), and the square root symbol refers to the principal square root, the one with a positive imaginary part. For the definition of the principal square root of other [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), see Square root.

## Origin {#Origin}

The origin of the root symbol √ is largely speculative. Some sources imply that the symbol was first used by Arab mathematicians. One of those mathematicians was [Abū al-Hasan ibn Alī al-Qalasādī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_al-Hasan_ibn_Alī_al-Qalasādī) (1421–1486). Legend has it that it was taken from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) letter "ج" (*[ǧīm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimel#Arabic_ǧīm)*), which is the first letter in the Arabic word "جذر" (*jadhir*, meaning "root").[^o5] However, [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902)[^o6] believed it originated from the letter "r", the first letter of the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word "[radix](wikipedia://en/701207)" (meaning "root"), referring to the same [mathematical operation](wikipedia://en/4140245).

The symbol was first seen in print without the [vinculum](wikipedia://en/673457) (the horizontal "bar" over the numbers inside the radical symbol) in the year 1525 in *Die Coss* by [Christoff Rudolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoff_Rudolff), a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) mathematician. In 1637 [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renee_Descartes) was the first to unite the German radical sign √ with the vinculum to create the radical symbol in common use today.[^o7]

## Encoding {#Encoding}

The Unicode and HTML character codes for the radical symbols are:

- 221A
- 221B
- 221C
- 23B7

However, these characters differ in appearance from most mathematical typesetting by omitting the [overline](wikipedia://en/4780165) connected to the radical symbol, which surrounds the argument of the square root function. The [OpenType](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenType) math table allows adding this overline following the radical symbol.

The Symbol font displays the character without any vinculum whatsoever; the overline may be a separate character at 0x60.[^o9] The JIS,[^o10] Wansung[^o11] and CNS 11643[^o25][^o12] code charts include a short overline attached to the radical symbol, whereas the GB 2312[^o13] and [GB 18030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GB_18030) charts do not.[^o14]

Additionally a "Radical Symbol Bottom" (U+23B7, ⎷) is available in the [Miscellaneous Technical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscellaneous_Technical) block.[^o15] This was used in contexts where [box-drawing characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-drawing_character) are used, such as in the [technical character set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEC_Technical_Character_Set) of [DEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Equipment_Corporation) terminals, to join up with box drawing characters on the line above to create the vinculum.[^o16]

In [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) the square root symbol may be generated by the `\sqrt` macro,[^o17] and the square root symbol without the overline may be generated by the `\surd` macro.[^o18]

### Legacy encodings {#Legacy_encodings}

Legacy encodings of the square root character U+221A include:

- 0xC3 in [Mac OS Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS_Roman) and [Mac OS Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS_Cyrillic_encoding)
- 0xFB (Alt+251) in [Code page 437](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_page_437) and [Code page 866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_page_866) (but not [Code page 850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_page_850)) on DOS and the Windows console
- 0xD6 in the [Symbol font encoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol_%28typeface%29)[^o20]
- 02-69 (7-bit 0x2265, [SJIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_JIS) 0x81E3, [EUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUC-JP) 0xA2E5) in Japanese [JIS X 0208](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JIS_X_0208)[^o21]
- 01-78 ([EUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUC-KR)/[UHC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Hangul_Code) 0xA1EE) in Korean [Wansung code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KS_X_1001)[^o22]
- 01-44 ([EUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUC-CN) 0xA1CC) in Mainland Chinese [GB 2312](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GB_2312) or [GBK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBK_%28character_encoding%29)[^o23]
- Traditional Chinese: 0xA1D4 in [Big5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big5)[^o24][^o25] or 1-2235 (kuten 01-02-21, [EUC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUC-TW) 0xA2B5 or 0x8EA1A2B5) in [CNS 11643](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNS_11643)[^o25][^o26]
