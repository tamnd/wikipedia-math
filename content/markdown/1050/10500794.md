# Plain folks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plain folks"}

**"Plain folks"** is a form of [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda) and a [logical fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_fallacy).[^o2] A plain folks argument is one in which the speaker presents themselves as an [average Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Average_Joe) — a [common person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commoner) who can understand and empathize with a listener's concerns.[^o3][^o4]

:::

It is like the propaganda, bandwagon. The most important part of this appeal is the speaker's portrayal of themselves as someone who has had a similar experience to the listener and knows why they may be skeptical or cautious about accepting the speaker's point of view. In this way, the speaker gives the audience a sense of [trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_%28social_sciences%29) and comfort, believing that the speaker and the audience share common goals and that they thus should agree with the speaker.
