# Inconsistent triad

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inconsistent triad"}

An **inconsistent triad** is a set of three propositions that cannot all be true together. For example, 'She was an orphan; Tim outlived her; Tim was her father'.[^o2]

:::

All inconsistent triads lead to [trilemmas](wikipedia://en/488382):

- If A and B are true, C must be false.
- If A and C are true, B must be false.
- If B and C are true, A must be false.[^o3]

## Epistemology {#Epistemology}

1. Knowledge is justified true belief.[^o5]
1. Humans cannot provide justification for their beliefs.[^o6]
1. Humans possess knowledge.[^o7]

## Political philosophy {#Political_philosophy}

1. A just society maximizes individual liberty.[^o9]
1. A just society maximizes material equality.[^o10]
1. A just society cannot maximize both liberty and equality.[^o11]

## Ethics {#Ethics}

1. Actions that maximize overall well-being are morally right.[^o13]
1. Lying can sometimes maximize overall well-being.[^o14]
1. Lying is always morally wrong.[^o15]
