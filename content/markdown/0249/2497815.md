# Complex differential form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex differential form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **complex differential form** is a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) (usually a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742)) which is permitted to have [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) coefficients.

:::

Complex forms have broad applications in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). On complex manifolds, they are fundamental and serve as the basis for much of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [Kähler geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric), and [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). Over non-complex manifolds, they also play a role in the study of [almost complex structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure), the theory of [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276), and [CR structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_structure).

Typically, complex forms are considered because of some desirable decomposition that the forms admit. On a complex manifold, for instance, any complex *k*-form can be decomposed uniquely into a sum of so-called **(*p*, *q*)-forms**: roughly, wedges of *p* [differentials](wikipedia://en/220782) of the holomorphic coordinates with *q* differentials of their complex conjugates. The ensemble of (*p*, *q*)-forms becomes the primitive object of study, and determines a finer geometrical structure on the manifold than the *k*-forms. Even finer structures exist, for example, in cases where [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) applies.

## Differential forms on a complex manifold {#Differential_forms_on_a_complex_manifold}

Suppose that *M* is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) of complex dimension *n*. Then there is a local [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) consisting of *n* complex-valued functions *z*<sup>1</sup>, ..., z<sup>*n*</sup> such that the coordinate transitions from one patch to another are [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) of these variables. The space of complex forms carries a rich structure, depending fundamentally on the fact that these transition functions are holomorphic, rather than just [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold).

### One-forms {#One-forms}

We begin with the case of one-forms. First decompose the complex coordinates into their real and imaginary parts: *z*<sup>*j*</sup> = *x*<sup>*j*</sup> + *iy*<sup>*j*</sup> for each *j*. Letting

$$dz^j=dx^j+idy^j,\quad d\bar{z}^j=dx^j-idy^j,$$

one sees that any differential form with complex coefficients can be written uniquely as a sum

$$\sum_{j=1}^n\left(f_jdz^j+g_jd\bar{z}^j\right).$$

Let Ω<sup>1,0</sup> be the space of complex differential forms containing only $dz$'s and Ω<sup>0,1</sup> be the space of forms containing only $d\bar{z}$'s. One can show, by the [Cauchy–Riemann equations](wikipedia://en/7583), that the spaces Ω<sup>1,0</sup> and Ω<sup>0,1</sup> are stable under holomorphic coordinate changes. In other words, if one makes a different choice *w*<sub>i</sub> of holomorphic coordinate system, then elements of Ω<sup>1,0</sup> transform [tensorially](wikipedia://en/29965), as do elements of Ω<sup>0,1</sup>. Thus the spaces Ω<sup>0,1</sup> and Ω<sup>1,0</sup> determine complex [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on the complex manifold.

### Higher-degree forms {#Higher-degree_forms}

The [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) of complex differential forms is defined in the same way as with real forms. Let *p* and *q* be a pair of non-negative integers ≤ *n*. The space Ω<sup>p,q</sup> of (*p*, *q*)-forms is defined by taking linear combinations of the wedge products of *p* elements from Ω<sup>1,0</sup> and *q* elements from Ω<sup>0,1</sup>. Symbolically,

$$\Omega^{p,q}=\underbrace{\Omega^{1,0}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\Omega^{1,0}}_{p \text{ times}}\wedge\underbrace{\Omega^{0,1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge\Omega^{0,1}}_{q \text{ times}}$$

where there are *p* factors of Ω<sup>1,0</sup> and *q* factors of Ω<sup>0,1</sup>. Just as with the two spaces of 1-forms, these are stable under holomorphic changes of coordinates, and so determine vector bundles.

If *E*<sup>*k*</sup> is the space of all complex differential forms of total degree *k*, then each element of *E*<sup>*k*</sup> can be expressed in a unique way as a linear combination of elements from among the spaces Ω<sup>p,q</sup> with *p* + *q* = *k*. More succinctly, there is a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_bundles) decomposition

$$E^k=\Omega^{k,0}\oplus\Omega^{k-1,1}\oplus\dotsb\oplus\Omega^{1,k-1}\oplus\Omega^{0,k}=\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\Omega^{p,q}.$$

Because this direct sum decomposition is stable under holomorphic coordinate changes, it also determines a vector bundle decomposition.

In particular, for each *k* and each *p* and *q* with *p* + *q* = *k*, there is a canonical projection of vector bundles

$$\pi^{p,q}:E^k\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q}.$$

### The Dolbeault operators {#The_Dolbeault_operators}

The usual [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) defines a mapping of sections $d: \Omega^{r} \to \Omega^{r+1}$ via

$$d(\Omega^{p,q}) \subseteq \bigoplus_{r + s = p + q + 1} \Omega^{r,s}$$

The exterior derivative does not in itself reflect the more rigid complex structure of the manifold.

Using *d* and the projections defined in the previous subsection, it is possible to define the **Dolbeault operators**:

$$\partial=\pi^{p+1,q}\circ d:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{p+1,q},\quad \bar{\partial}=\pi^{p,q+1}\circ d:\Omega^{p,q}\rightarrow\Omega^{p,q+1}$$

To describe these operators in local coordinates, let

$$\alpha=\sum_{|I|=p,|J|=q}\ f_{IJ}\,dz^I\wedge d\bar{z}^J\in\Omega^{p,q}$$

where *I* and *J* are [multi-indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-index). Then

$$\partial\alpha=\sum_{I,J}\sum_\ell \frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial z^\ell}\,dz^\ell\wedge dz^I\wedge d\bar{z}^J$$

$$\bar{\partial}\alpha=\sum_{I,J}\sum_\ell \frac{\partial f_{IJ}}{\partial \bar{z}^\ell}d\bar{z}^\ell\wedge dz^I\wedge d\bar{z}^J.$$

The following properties are seen to hold:

$$d=\partial+\bar{\partial}$$

$$\partial^2=\bar{\partial}^2=\partial\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}\partial=0.$$

These operators and their properties form the basis for [Dolbeault cohomology](wikipedia://en/3189601) and many aspects of [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590).

On a [star-shaped domain](wikipedia://en/5983414) of a complex manifold the Dolbeault operators have dual homotopy operators [^o7] that result from splitting of the [homotopy operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincare_lemma) for $d$.[^o7] This is a content of the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030) on a complex manifold.

The Poincaré lemma for $\bar \partial$ and $\partial$ can be improved further to the [local $\partial \bar \partial$-lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ddbar_lemma), which shows that every $d$-exact complex differential form is actually $\partial \bar \partial$-exact. On compact [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) a global form of the local $\partial \bar \partial$-lemma holds, known as the [$\partial \bar \partial$-lemma](wikipedia://en/72112048). It is a consequence of [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), and states that a complex differential form which is globally $d$-exact (in other words, whose class in [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) is zero) is globally $\partial \bar \partial$-exact.

:::

### Holomorphic forms {#Holomorphic_forms}

For each *p*, a **holomorphic *p*-form** is a holomorphic section of the bundle Ω<sup>*p*,0</sup>. In local coordinates, then, a holomorphic *p*-form can be written in the form

$$\alpha=\sum_{|I|=p}f_I\,dz^I$$

where the $f_I$ are holomorphic functions. Equivalently, and due to the [independence of the complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/7583#Independence_of_the_complex_conjugate), the (*p*, 0)-form *α* is holomorphic if and only if

$$\bar{\partial}\alpha=0.$$

The [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of holomorphic *p*-forms is often written Ω<sup>*p*</sup>, although this can sometimes lead to confusion so many authors tend to adopt an alternative notation.
