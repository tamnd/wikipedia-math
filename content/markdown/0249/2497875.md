# Alternating algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alternating algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **alternating algebra** is a $\mathbf{Z}$-[graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) for which $xy = (-1)^{\deg(x)\deg(y)}yx$ for all nonzero [homogeneous elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_element) $x$ and $y$ (i.e. it is an [anticommutative algebra](wikipedia://en/52405825)) and has the further property that $x^{2} = 0$ ([nilpotence](wikipedia://en/252235)) for every homogeneous element $x$ of odd degree.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms#Operations) on a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) form an alternating algebra.
- The [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) is an alternating algebra.
- The [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is an alternating algebra.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The algebra formed as the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of the homogeneous subspaces of even degree of an anticommutative algebra $A$ is a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) contained in the [centre](wikipedia://en/53796787) of $A$, and is thus [commutative](wikipedia://en/2112#Definition).
- An anticommutative algebra $A$ over a (commutative) base [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$ in which 2 is not a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) is alternating.[^o2]
