# P-stable group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="P-stable group"}

In [finite group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_group_theory), a ***p*-stable group** for an [odd prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_prime) *p* is a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) satisfying a technical condition introduced by (txt) in order to extend Thompson's uniqueness results in the [odd order theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_order_theorem) to groups with dihedral Sylow 2-subgroups.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

There are several equivalent definitions of a *p*-stable group.

### Glauberman {#Glauberman}

We give definition of a *p*-stable group in two parts. The definition used here comes from (Glauberman 1968, 1104).

**1**. Let *p* be an odd prime and *G* be a finite group with a nontrivial *p*-core $O_p(G)$. Then *G* is *p*-stable if it satisfies the following condition: Let *P* be an arbitrary *p*-subgroup of *G* such that $O_{p'\!}(G)$ is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*. Suppose that $x \in N_G(P)$ and $\bar x$ is the [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) of $C_G(P)$ containing *x*. If $[P,x,x]=1$, then $\overline{x}\in O_n(N_G(P)/C_G(P))$.

Now, define $\mathcal{M}_p(G)$ as the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all *p*-subgroups of *G* maximal with respect to the property that $O_p(M)\not= 1$.

**2**. Let *G* be a finite group and *p* an odd prime. Then *G* is called *p*-stable if every element of $\mathcal{M}_p(G)$ is *p*-stable by definition **1**.

### Alternative {#Alternative}

Let *p* be an odd prime and *H* a finite group. Then *H* is *p*-stable if $F^*(H)=O_p(H)$ and, whenever *P* is a normal *p*-subgroup of *H* and $g \in H$ with $[P,g,g]=1$, then $gC_H(P)\in O_p(H/C_H(P))$.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If *p* is an odd prime and *G* is a finite group such that SL<sub>2</sub>(*p*) is not involved in *G*, then *G* is *p*-stable. If furthermore *G* contains a normal *p*-subgroup *P* such that $C_G(P)\leqslant P$, then $Z(J_0(S))$ is a [characteristic subgroup](wikipedia://en/7198) of *G*, where $J_0(S)$ is the subgroup introduced by [John Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189) in (Thompson 1969, 149–151).

:::
