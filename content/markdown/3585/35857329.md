# Steiner point (triangle)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steiner point"}

In [triangle geometry](wikipedia://en/30654), the **Steiner point** is a particular point associated with a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654).[^o2] It is a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488)[^o3] and it is designated as the center X(99) in [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593). [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) (1796–1863), Swiss mathematician, described this point in 1826. The point was given Steiner's name by [Joseph Neuberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Neuberg) in 1886.[^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Steiner point construction 01 .svg.* **Construction of the Steiner point.** solid #B8860B 2px solid #8B0000 2px solid #00BFFF 2px solid magenta 2px *Lines concurring at the Steiner point:* solid green 2px solid blue 2px solid red 2px

The Steiner point is defined as follows. (This is not the way in which Steiner defined it.[^o3])

Let $ABC$ be any given triangle. Let $O$ be the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and $K$ be the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) of triangle $ABC$. The [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) with $OK$ as diameter is the [Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012) of triangle $ABC$. The line through $O$ perpendicular to the line $BC$ intersects the Brocard circle at another point $A'$. The line through $O$ perpendicular to the line $CA$ intersects the Brocard circle at another point $B'$. The line through $O$ perpendicular to the line $AB$ intersects the Brocard circle at another point $C'$. (The triangle $A'B'C'$ is the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335) of triangle $ABC$.) Let $L_{A}$ be the line through $A$ parallel to the line $B'C'$, $L_{B}$ be the line through $B$ parallel to the line $C'A'$ and $L_{C}$ be the line through $C$ parallel to the line $A'B'$. Then the three lines $L_{A}$, $L_{B}$ and $L_{C}$ are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157). The point of concurrency is the *Steiner point* of triangle $ABC$.

In the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) the Steiner point is defined as follows:

*Image: Steiner point Construction 02.svg.* Alternative construction of the Steiner point

Let $ABC$ be any given triangle. Let $O$ be the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and $K$ be the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) of triangle $ABC$. Let $l_{A}$ be the reflection of the line $OK$ in the line $BC$, $l_{B}$ be the reflection of the line $OK$ in the line $CA$ and $l_{C}$ be the reflection of the line $OK$ in the line $AB$. Let the lines $l_{B}$ and $l_{C}$ intersect at $A''$, the lines $l_{C}$ and $l_{A}$ intersect at $B''$ and the lines $l_{A}$ and $l_{B}$ intersect at $C''$. Then the lines $AA''$, $BB''$ and $CC''$ are concurrent. The point of [concurrency](wikipedia://en/2206157) is the Steiner point of triangle $ABC$.

:::

## Trilinear coordinates {#Trilinear_coordinates}

The [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of the Steiner point are given below.

$$bc / (b^2 - c^2) : ca / (c^2 - a^2) : ab / (a^2 - b^2)$$

$$= b^2 c^2 \csc(b - C) : c^2 a^2 \csc(c - a) : a^2 b^2 \csc(a - b)$$

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

1. The [Steiner circumellipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_circumellipse) of triangle $ABC$, also called the Steiner ellipse, is the ellipse of least area that passes through the vertices $A$, $B$ and $C$. The Steiner point of triangle $ABC$ lies on the Steiner circumellipse of triangle $ABC$.
1. The [Simson line](wikipedia://en/3307757) of the Steiner point of triangle $ABC$ is parallel to the [Brocard axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_axis), the line $OK$ where $O$ is the circumcenter and $K$ is the symmmedian point of triangle $ABC$.
1. The Steiner point of the Brocard triangle is the symmedian point of $ABC$. The Tarry point (see below) of the Brocard triangle is the circumcenter of $ABC$.[^o10]
1. The Steiner point of triangle $ABC$ is the [Brianchon point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianchon_point) of the [Kiepert parabola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_parabola) with respect to triangle $ABC$.[^o11]
1. The Steiner point of the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743) of $ABC$ is the center of the [Kiepert hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_hyperbola) of triangle $ABC$.[^o12]

### Misconception {#Misconception}

Canadian mathematician [Ross Honsberger](wikipedia://en/37256856) stated the following as a property of Steiner point: *The Steiner point of a triangle is the [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) of the system obtained by suspending at each vertex a mass equal to the magnitude of the exterior angle at that vertex.*[^o10] The center of mass of such a system is in fact not the Steiner point, but the **Steiner curvature centroid**, which has the trilinear coordinates $\left(\frac{\pi - A}{a} : \frac{\pi - B}{b} : \frac{\pi - C}{c}\right)$.[^o14] It is the triangle center designated as X(1115) in [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593).

:::

## Tarry point {#Tarry_point}

*Image: Tarry point Construction.svg.* The line through $A$ perpendicular to $B'C'$, the line through $B$ perpendicular to $C'A'$, and the line through $C$ perpendicular to $A'B'$ concur at the Tarry point.

The Tarry point of a triangle is closely related to the Steiner point of the triangle. Let $ABC$ be any given triangle. The point on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of triangle $ABC$ diametrically opposite to the Steiner point of triangle $ABC$ is called the [Tarry point](wikipedia://en/14662924) of triangle $ABC$. The Tarry point is a triangle center and it is designated as the center X(98) in [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593). The trilinear coordinates of the Tarry point are given below:

$$\sec(A + \omega) : \sec(B + \omega) : \sec(C + \omega) = f(a,b,c) : f(b,c,a) : f(c,a,b)$$

where $\omega$ is the [Brocard angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocard_angle) of triangle $ABC$

and $f(a,b,c) = \frac{bc}{b^4 + c^4 - a^2 b^2 - a^2 c^2}$

Similar to the definition of the Steiner point, the Tarry point can be defined as follows:

Let $ABC$ be any given triangle. Let $A'B'C'$ be the Brocard triangle of triangle $ABC$. Let $L_{A}$ be the line through $A$ perpendicular to the line $B'C'$, $L_{B}$ be the line through $B$ perpendicular to the line $C'A'$ and $L_{C}$ be the line through $C$ perpendicular to the line $A'B'$. Then the three lines $L_{A}$, $L_{B}$ and $L_{C}$ are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157). The point of concurrency is the *Tarry point* of triangle $ABC$.
