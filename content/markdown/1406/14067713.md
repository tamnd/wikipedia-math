# Frances Cave-Browne-Cave

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frances Cave-Browne-Cave"}

**Frances Evelyn Cave-Browne-Cave** [FRAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Astronomical_Society) (1876–1965) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and educator.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Frances Cave-Browne-Cave was the daughter of Sir Thomas Cave-Browne-Cave and Blanche Matilda Mary Ann Milton. She was educated at home in [Streatham Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streatham_Common) with her sisters and entered [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), with her elder sister [Beatrice Mabel Cave-Browne-Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Mabel_Cave-Browne-Cave) in 1895. She obtained a first-class degree and she would have been Fifth [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1898 if she had been a man. She took Part II of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) in 1899.[^o4]

## Career and continued studies {#Career_and_continued_studies}

Like her sister, she was usually known by the single surname Cave professionally.[^o4] Along with Beatrice, she worked with [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London).[^o6] Her work was funded by the first research grant offered at Girton: an Old Students' Research Studentship from Girton, provided by [Florence Margaret Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Margaret_Durham).[^o7] She also received a stipend as a result of a grant to Pearson's laboratory from the [Worshipful Company of Drapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Drapers).[^o8] Her research in the field of [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) produced two publications in the *[Proceedings of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_Royal_Society)* which discussed [barometric measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barometer), and was read to the British Association at Cambridge in 1904.[^o7][^o9]

In 1903, Cave returned to Girton as a fellow. She prioritised teaching over research, and focused on developing the weakest students because she felt that was where the biggest difference could be made.[^o4] She became the director of studies in 1918.[^o7] She was on the executive council of the college and was largely responsible for drafting the charter of incorporation granted in 1924.[^o9] On the 11 November 1921 she was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society).[^o10] Cave was made honorary fellow of Girton in 1942.[^o7]

Cave received an MA from [Trinity College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Dublin) in 1907 as a [steamboat lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat_ladies)[^o11] since the rules of Cambridge University did not then permit women to take degrees, and from Cambridge in 1926.[^o7]

## Later life and death {#Later_life_and_death}

Cave retired to Southampton in 1936. She died in [Shedfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shedfield) in a nursing home on 30 March 1965.[^o4]
