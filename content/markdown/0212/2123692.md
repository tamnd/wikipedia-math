# Borel equivalence relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel equivalence relation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Borel equivalence relation** on a [Polish space](wikipedia://en/396622) *X* is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on *X* that is a [Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) subset of *X* × *X* (in the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910)).

:::

Given Borel equivalence relations *E* and *F* on Polish spaces *X* and *Y* respectively, one says that *E* is *Borel reducible* to *F*, in symbols *E* ≤<sub>B</sub> *F*, if and only if there is a [Borel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_function)

Θ : *X* → *Y*

such that for all *x*,*x*' ∈ *X*, one has

*x* *E* *x*' ⇔ Θ(*x*) *F* Θ(*x*').

Conceptually, if *E* is Borel reducible to *F*, then *E* is "not more complicated" than *F*, and the quotient space *X*/*E* has a lesser or equal "Borel cardinality" than *Y*/*F*, where "Borel cardinality" is like [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) except for a definability restriction on the witnessing mapping.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Kuratowski's theorem"}

## Kuratowski's theorem {#Kuratowski's_theorem}

A [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) *X* is called a **[standard Borel space](wikipedia://en/29819325)** if it is Borel-isomorphic to a Borel subset of a Polish space. Kuratowski's theorem then states that two standard Borel spaces *X* and *Y* are Borel-isomorphic [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) |*X*| = |*Y*|.

:::
