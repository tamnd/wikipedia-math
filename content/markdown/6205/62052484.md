# Stahl's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stahl's theorem"}

In [matrix analysis](wikipedia://en/40956141) **Stahl's theorem** is a theorem proved in 2011 by [Herbert Stahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Robert_Stahl) concerning Laplace transforms for special matrix functions.[^o2] It originated in 1975 as the Bessis-Moussa-Villani (BMV) conjecture by Daniel Bessis, Pierre Moussa, and Marcel Villani.[^o3] In 2004 [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) and [Robert Seiringer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Seiringer) gave two important reformulations of the BMV conjecture.[^o4] In 2015, [Alexandre Eremenko](wikipedia://en/34534867) gave a simplified proof of Stahl's theorem.[^o5]

:::

In 2023, [Otte Heinävaara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otte_Heinävaara) proved a structure theorem for [Hermitian matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_matrices) introducing [tracial joint spectral measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracial_joint_spectral_measures) that implies Stahl's theorem as a corollary.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\operatorname{tr}$ denote the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859). If $A$ and $B$ are $n\times n$ [Hermitian matrices](wikipedia://en/189682) and $B$ is [positive semidefinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_semidefinite_matrix), define $\mathbf{f}(t) = \operatorname{tr}(\exp(A-tB))$, for all real $t\geq 0$. Then $\mathbf{f}$ can be represented as the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of a non-negative [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) $\mu$ on $[0,\infty)$. In other words, for all real $t\geq 0$,

$\mathbf{f}$($t$) = $\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{-ts}\, d\mu(s)$,

for some non-negative measure $\mu$ depending upon $A$ and $B$.[^o9]

:::
