# Simplicial manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial manifold"}

Symplectic manifold

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the term **simplicial manifold** commonly refers to one of several loosely defined objects, commonly appearing in the study of [Regge calculus](wikipedia://en/1012545). These objects combine attributes of a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) with those of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). There is no standard usage of this term in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and so the concept can refer to a [triangulation in topology](wikipedia://en/1440976), or a [piecewise linear manifold](wikipedia://en/2174184), or one of several different [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) from either the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) or the category of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784) to the category of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

:::

## A manifold made out of simplices {#A_manifold_made_out_of_simplices}

A simplicial manifold is a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) for which the [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/723105) is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193). This is essentially the concept of a [triangulation in topology](wikipedia://en/1440976). This can mean simply that a [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) of each vertex (i.e. the set of [simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplices) that contain that point as a vertex) is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a *n*-dimensional [ball](wikipedia://en/160556).

## A simplicial object built from manifolds {#A_simplicial_object_built_from_manifolds}

A simplicial manifold is also a [simplicial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_object) in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). This is a special case of a [simplicial space](wikipedia://en/41419857) in which, for each *n*, the space of *n*-simplices is a manifold.

For example, if *G* is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), then the [simplicial nerve](wikipedia://en/5127933) of *G* has the manifold $G^n$ as its space of *n*-simplices. More generally, *G* can be a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923).
