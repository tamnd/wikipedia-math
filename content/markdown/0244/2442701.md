# Anania Shirakatsi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anania Shirakatsi"}

**Anania Shirakatsi** (Անանիա Շիրակացի, xcl, [anglicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicized): **Ananias of Shirak**) was a 7th-century [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians) [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) and [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy), author of extant works covering mathematics, astronomy, geography, chronology, and other fields. Little is known for certain of his life outside of his own writings, but he is considered the father of the [exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_sciences) and [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) in Armenia—the first Armenian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer),[^n1][^n2] and [cosmographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmographer).[^n3]

:::

A part of the Armenian [Hellenizing School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenizing_School) and one of the few secular scholars in medieval Armenia, Anania was educated primarily by Tychicus, in [Trebizond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabzon). He composed science textbooks and the first known geographic work in [classical Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Armenian) (*[Ashkharhatsuyts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkharhatsuyts)*),[^n4] which provides detailed information about [Greater Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_%28antiquity%29), Persia and the Caucasus ([Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28country%29) and [Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Albania)).

In mathematics, his accomplishments include the earliest known table of results of the four basic [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245),[^n2][^n5] the earliest known collection of [recreational math](wikipedia://en/26446) puzzles and problems,[^n6] and the earliest book of math problems in Armenian.[^n7] He also devised a system of [mathematical notation](wikipedia://en/277184) based on the [Armenian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet), although he was the only writer known to have used it.

## Name {#Name}

His name is usually [anglicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation) as Ananias of Shirak (xcl).[^o13] Anania is the Armenian variant of the biblical name Ananias, itself the Greek version of the Hebrew [Hananiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hananiah).[^n8] The second part of his name denotes his place of origin, the region of Shirak,[^n1] though it may have become a sort of surname.[^n9] In some manuscripts, he is called *Shirakuni* (Շիրակունի) and *Shirakavants’i* (Շիրակաւանցի).[^o17]

## Life {#Life}

*Image: Անանիա Շիրակացի, քանդակագործ\` Ա. Պապոյան.jpg.* A statue of Anania at the Alphabet Park near [Artashavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artashavan) by Artush Papoyan (2005).[^o19]

### Background {#Background}

Anania Shirakatsi lived in the 7th century.[^o21] The dates of his birth and death have not been definitively established. [Robert H. Hewsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Hewsen) noted in 1968 that Anania is widely believed to have been born between 595 and 600;[^n10] a quarter-century later he settled on c. 610 as a birthdate and 685 as the year he died.[^n11] [Agop Jack Hacikyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agop_Jack_Hacikyan) et al. place his birth in early 600s but agree on 685.[^n12] [Sen Arevshatyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sen_Arevshatyan), [James R. Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Russell), Edward G. Mathews, and Theo van Lint also concur with 610–685,[^n13][^n14][^n3][^n2] while Greenwood suggests c. 600–670.[^n7] Vardanyan places his death in the early 690s.[^n15]

Anania is the only classical Armenian scholar to have written an [autobiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobiography).[^n10] It is a brief text, characterized as "somewhat self-congratulatory"[^n16] and "more a statement of academic pedigree" than autobiography.[^n17] It was probably written as the preface to one of his scholarly works, possibly the *K’nnikon*.[^n3] He was the son of [Yovhannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovhannes) and was born in the village of Anania/Aneank’ (Անեանք) or in the town of [Shirakavan (Yerazgavors)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirakavan_%28ancient_city%29),[^o35] in the canton of Shirak, in the central Armenian province of [Ayrarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrarat).[^o36] Aneank' may be connected to the later city of [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani), the [Bagratid Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia) capital.[^o37]

Anania probably came from a noble family.[^n3] Since his name is sometimes spelled as Shirakuni (Շիրակունի), Hewsen argued that he may have belonged to the house of the [Kamsarakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamsarakan) or Arsharuni princes of Shirak and [Arsharunik’](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsharunik), respectively.[^n10] Greenwood suggests that it is more likely that Anania came from the lesser nobility in Shirak, who served the house of Kamsarakan.[^n18] Broutian describes his father as a "minor Armenian nobleman."[^n19] Vardanyan believes he either came from the Kamsarakan family or that they were his [patrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage).[^n20]

Anania is traditionally thought to have been buried in the village of Anavank’; however, the tradition probably originated from the name of the village.[^n2]

### Education {#Education}

Anania received his early education at the local Armenian schools, possibly at Dprevank’ monastery,[^n21] where he studied sacred texts and earlier Armenian authors.[^n10][^n22] Due to the lack of teachers and books in Armenia, he decided to travel to the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) to study mathematics.[^n7][^n22] After first traveling to [Theodosiopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzurum), then to the Byzantine-controlled province of [Fourth Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Armenia) (probably [Martyropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvan,_Diyarbakır)),[^n23] where he studied under the mathematician Christosatur for six months.[^n23] He then left to find a better teacher and learned about Tychicus, who was based at the monastery (or martyrium) of [Saint Eugenios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenios_of_Trebizond) in [Trebizond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabzon).[^n10][^n7] Redgate dated this to the 620s.[^n23]

*Image: YSU Shirakatsi statue.jpg.* A statue of Anania at [Yerevan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_University)

Anania devoted a significant part of his autobiography to Tychicus (born c. 560), with whom he spent eight years in the 620s or 630s.[^o55] Tychicus had studied the [Armenian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) and [its literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_literature) while serving in the [Byzantine army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_army) in Armenia.[^n16][^n24] Wounded by the [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine–Sasanian_War_of_602–628), he retired from the military and later studied in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), and [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople).[^n16][^n24] Tychicus later returned to his native Trebizond, where he established a school c. 615.[^n24] Tychicus taught many students from Constantinople (including from the imperial court) and was renowned among Byzantine kings.[^n25][^n7] He provided Anania special attention and taught him what Anania called a "perfect knowledge of mathematics".[^n12] In Tychicus's vast library, Anania found "everything, [exoteric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoteric) and esoteric",[^n14] including sacred and secular Greek authors, including works on the sciences, medicine, chronology, and history.[^o65] Russell believed his library may have included [Pythagorean](wikipedia://en/291170) and [alchemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemical) books.[^n14] Anania considered Tychicus to have been "predestined by God for the introduction of science into Armenia."[^n24]

### Educator and scientist {#Educator_and_scientist}

*Image: Anania Shirakatsi, 7th century, Phases of the Moon, Bolorakner.png.* List of [Moon Phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Phases) by Anania Shirakatsi

Anania himself established a school in Armenia upon his return.[^n12] That school, the first in Armenia to teach [quadrivium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrivium), is presumed to have been located in his native Shirak.[^n2][^n24] He was disappointed with the laziness of his students and their departure after learning the basics.[^n24] Anania complained about Armenians' lack of interest in mathematics,[^n16] writing that they "love neither learning, nor knowledge."[^n26] [Nicholas Adontz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Adontz) considered it an exaggeration, "if not an absolute slander, to deny the Armenian innate love of investigation."[^n27] The 12th-century chronicler [Samuel of Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Anetsi) listed five of Shirakatsi's students,[^n2] who are otherwise unknown.[^n28] Anania financed his research in several fields with the money he earned teaching.[^n24]

## Relationship with the church {#Relationship_with_the_church}

Thomson wrote that as a lay scholar, Anania was a "rarity in early Armenia."[^n16] Hewsen termed him the only lay classical Armenian author besides [Grigor Magistros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Magistros),[^n10] adding that he had a close relationship with the Armenian Church.[^n29] Hacikyan et al. describe Anania as a "devout Christian and well versed in the Bible" who "made some attempts to reconcile science and Scripture."[^n30] In his later years, Anania may have been a monk in the Armenian Church.[^n10] This is based on his religious discourses and attempts to date the feasts of the church.[^n31] [John A. C. Greppin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_A._C._Greppin) doubts that Anania was ever in any religious order.[^n32] Several scholars consider him a church ideologist akin to [Cosmas Indicopleustes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_Indicopleustes), whom he actually criticized.[^n33]

Hewsen noted that some of Anania's "more revolutionary ideas" were suppressed by the Armenian Church after his death.[^n34] Greppin suggested that Anania, a largely secular author, had fallen into a "bad clerical odor."[^n32] [S. Peter Cowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._Peter_Cowe) disagreed with [Ashot G. Abrahamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashot_G._Abrahamian)'s hypothesis that his name was "censored in the Middle Ages because of ecclesiastical disapproval" and argued that it is "more applicable to Soviet practice than that of the relatively tolerant Armenian and other eastern churches."[^n35] Soviet historians represented him as a founder of [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligious) and [anti-clerical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clerical) thought in Armenia, who pioneered [double-truth theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_truth).[^n36] Vazgen Chaloyan called him a "progressive representative of the feudal period of Armenian science."[^n37] Gevorg Khrlopian went as far as to argue that Anania was an enemy of the [Armenian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) and fought against its [obscurantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obscurantism).[^n29] Hewsen opposed this view, suggesting that, instead, he was an "independent thinker of sorts."[^n10]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

*Image: Buste d'Anania Shirakatsi (Anania de Shirak) au Matenadaran.JPG.* A bust at the [Matenadaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matenadaran)

Anania is considered by modern scholars to be a representative of the [Hellenizing School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenizing_School) since many of his works were based on classical Greek sources.[^n38][^n39] He was the first Armenian scholar to have "imported a set of scientific notions, and examples of their applications, from the Greek-speaking schools" into Armenia.[^n40] He was well versed in Greek literature,[^n41] and the influence of [Greek syntax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language#Syntax) is evident in his works.[^n42] Anania was also knowledgeable about native Armenian and Iranian cultural traditions;[^n7] several of his works provide important information on late [Sassanian Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanian_Iran).[^n7]

[James R. Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Russell) describes him as an [alchemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemist) and a [Pythagorean](wikipedia://en/291170) who "does not usually rely on mythology to explain natural phenomena."[^n43] Anania accepted the importance of experience, observation, rational practice and theory, and was influenced by the ideas of the 5th-century [Neoplatonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonist) philosopher [Davit Anhaght](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_the_Invincible) (the Invincible), and Greek philosophers [Thales of Miletus](wikipedia://en/30072), [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates), [Democritus](wikipedia://en/8211), [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), [Zeno of Citium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_of_Citium), [Epicurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicurus), [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979), [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300), and [Cosmas Indicopleustes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_Indicopleustes).[^n44] Aristotle's *[On the Heavens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Heavens)* had a significant influence on Anania's thought.[^n26] According to Gevorg Khrlopian, Anania was heavily influenced by [Yeghishe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeghishe)'s *An Interpretation of Creation*, the anonymous *Interpretation of the Categories of Aristotle*, and the works of Davit Anhaght,[^n45] who had established Neoplatonism in Armenian thought.[^n44] Anania was also the first Armenian scholar to quote [Philo of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philo) by name.[^n46]

Anania was the last known lay scholar in Christian Armenia until [Grigor Magistros Pahlavuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Magistros) in the 11th century.[^n10][^n16] He advocated [rationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism) in studying nature and attacked [superstitious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstitious) beliefs and [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology) as the "babblings of the foolish."[^n47][^n48] He adopted the [classical theory of four elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element), which considered all matter to be composed of four elements: fire, air, water, and earth. He believed that while God directly created these elements, He did not interfere with the "natural course of the development of things." He asserted that the creation, existence, and decay of natural bodies and phenomena occurred through the union of these elements—without the interference of God.[^n49] Both living and non-living matter came into existence from a synthesis of the four elements.[^n49]

Anania accepted that the [Earth is round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_Earth), describing it as "like an egg with a spherical yolk (the globe) surrounded by a layer of white (the atmosphere) and covered with a hard shell (the sky)."[^n29] He accurately explained [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse) and [lunar eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_eclipse), the [phases of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phases_of_the_Moon), and the structure of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way),[^n47] describing the latter as a "mass of dense but faintly luminous stars."[^n29] Anania also correctly attributed [tides](wikipedia://en/30718) to the influence of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon).[^n34][^n48] He described the topmost sphere as the [aether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aether_%28classical_element%29) (xcl), the source of light and heat (through the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun)).[^n50]

## Works {#Works}

Anania was a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) and [natural philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy).[^o122] About 40 works in various disciplines have been attributed to Anania, but only half are extant. They include studies and translations in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography), [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology), and [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904).[^n2] Many of his works are believed to have been part of the *K’nnikon* (Քննիկոն, from "canon", Greek: *Kanonikon*), completed circa 666,[^n2][^n51] and used as the standard science textbook in medieval Armenia.[^n3][^n52] Artashes Matevosyan termed it the first secular Armenian science textbook,[^o128] while Valentina Calzolari described it as a "monumental encyclopedic work."[^o129] Greenwood argued that the *K’nnikon* was a "fluid compilation, whose contents fluctuated over time, reflecting the interests and resources of different teachers and practitioners."[^n53]

Modern scholars have praised Anania's writing as concise, simple, and to the point, retaining the reader's attention and citing examples to illustrate his point.[^n47][^n44]

### Mathematical {#Mathematical}

*Image: Shirakatsi manuscript.JPG.* An arithmetic book by Shirakatsi

Anania was primarily devoted to mathematics,[^n3] which he considered the "mother of all knowledge."[^n2][^n10] His mathematical books were used as textbooks in Armenia.[^n16]

Of Anania's several mathematical works, the most important is the book of [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) (*Hamaroghut’iun*, Համարողութիւն; or *T’vabanut’iun*, Թւաբանութիւն),[^n54] a comprehensive collection of tables on the four basic [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245).[^n47] It is the earliest extant known work of its kind.[^n2][^n5] The operations reach up to a total of 80 million, which is the highest number.[^n5] A possible theoretical part is believed lost.[^n47]

*Problems and Solutions* (alternatively translated as *On Questions and Answers*), a collection of 24 arithmetical problems and their solutions, is based on the application of fractions;[^n47][^n6] it is the earliest such work in Armenian. Many of its problems allude to real-world situations: six connect to the princely house of Shirak, the [Kamsarakans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamsarakan),[^n5] and at least three to Iran.[^n7] Greenwood calls the problems "a rich source for seventh-century history whose value has not been sufficiently recognized."[^n55]

The third work, probably an appendix of the book of arithmetic, is titled xcl (Խրախճանականք), literally "things for festive occasions". It has been translated into English as *Mathematical Pastimes*,[^n54] *Fun with Arithmetic* or *Problems for Amusement*. It also contains 24 problems "intended for mathematical entertainment in social gatherings."[^n47] According to Mathews this may be the oldest extant text of its kind.[^n6]

#### Numerical notation {#Numerical_notation}

For his mathematical works, Anania developed a unique numerical notation based on 12 letters of the [Armenian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet). For the units, he used the first nine letters of the Armenian script (Ա, Բ, Գ, Դ, Ե, Զ, Է, Ը, Թ), similar to the standard traditional [Armenian numerical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_numerals). The letters used for 10, 100, and 1000 were also identical to the traditional Armenian system (Ժ, Ճ, Ռ), but all other numbers up to 10,000 were written using these 12 letters. For instance, 50 would be written ԵԺ (5×10) and not Ծ as in the standard system. Thus, the notation is multiplicative-additive as opposed to the ciphered-additive standard system and requires knowing 12 letters, instead of 36, to write numbers less than 10,000. Numbers greater than that could be written using multiplicative combinations of just 2 or 3 signs, but using all 36 letters.[^n56]

Stephen Chrisomalis believes this system was created by Anania since it only occurs in his works and is not found in [Greek](wikipedia://en/166697), Syriac, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_numerals), or any other [alphabetic numeral system](wikipedia://en/61315332).[^n57] Allen Shaw has argued it was just a variant of the Armenian numerals designed specifically for the representation of large numbers.[^n58] No other writer used it.[^n57]

### Astronomical {#Astronomical}

*Image: Shirakatsi, 1283.jpg.* A manuscript of Anania's *Cosmology*.

One of Anania's most significant works is the *Cosmology* (Տիեզերագիտութիւն, *Tiezeragitut’iun*).[^n59] Abrahamian's version is composed of ten chapters, with an introduction titled "In the Fulfillment of a Promise", implying a patron.[^n60] It covers the sun, the moon, celestial spheres, [constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellations), the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way), and meteorological changes.[^o160]

Works used for the parts of the *Cosmology* include the Bible (mostly the [Pentateuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentateuch) and [Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms)) and works by the [Church Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Fathers). Anania cites the work of [Basil of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea), [Gregory the Illuminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator), and Amphilochius (perhaps, [of Iconium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphilochius_of_Iconium)).[^n61] Some chapters of the work, such as "On Clouds" (also called "On the Sky" or "Concerning the Skies"), are largely based on Basil's *[Hexameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexameron)*.[^o162] Anania also repeats the classical Greek notions in the fields of astronomy, physics or meteorology.[^n62] Pambakian wrote about the significance of the *Cosmology*: In conclusion, when viewed as a Byzantine text, the *Cosmology*'s originality may be sought in the way in which pagan and Christian traditions are combined (and often intrinsically intertwined), and much may be learned about the ‘making of science’ in the context of the seventh-century. Within the specific context of Armenian literature, this text deals with many aspects of natural philosophy in unprecedented depth.[^n63] Another of Anania's astronomical works, *Tables of the Motions of the Moon* (Խորանք ընթացիք լուսոյ, xcl),[^n54] is based on the works of [Meton of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meton_of_Athens) and his own observations.[^n64]

#### Perpetual calendar {#Perpetual_calendar}

In 667 Anania was invited by [Catholicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians) Anastas I of Akori (r. 661/2–667) to the Armenian Church's central seat at [Dvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvin_%28ancient_city%29) to establish a fixed calendar of the movable and immovable feasts of the Armenian Church.[^n16][^n65] The result was a [perpetual calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_calendar) based on a 532-year cycle (ՇԼԲ բոլորակ),[^n3] combining the [solar cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cycle) and the [lunar cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_cycle) since they coincide every 532 years. It was first proposed by [Victorius of Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorius_of_Aquitaine) in 457 and adopted by the [Church of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Alexandria).[^n29] Anania's calendar was never implemented by the Armenian Church;[^n3][^n16] [Hovhannes Draskhanakerttsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovhannes_Draskhanakerttsi) believes that Anastas's death prevented a church council from ratifying it.[^n66] Krikor H. Maksoudian described the endeavor a "serious attempt", which was not adopted due to circumstances.[^o175]

### Geographical {#Geographical}

*Image: Armenia According to the Ashkharhatsuyts.jpg.* Armenia according to Anania's Geography (*Ashkharhatsuyts*) as reconstructed by [Suren Eremian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suren_Yeremian)

The *[Ashkharhatsuyts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkharhatsuyts)*[^n16] (Classical Armenian: Աշխարհացոյց, xcl, lit. "showing the world") is an anonymously published [world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_map), believed to have been written sometime between 610 and 636,[^n67] "probably shortly before AD 636", i.e. the [Muslim Arab invasions and conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Armenia).[^n68][^n11] It lacks a drawn map, but contains "earliest surviving detailed description of Armenian lands."[^o181] Its authorship has been disputed in the modern period; formerly believed to have been the work of [Movses Khorenatsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi), most scholars now attribute it to Anania.[^o182] Hewsen calls it "one of the most valuable works to come down to us from Armenian antiquity."[^n69]

The *Armenian Geography*—as it is alternatively known—has been especially important for research into the history and geography of Greater Armenia, the Caucasus ([Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_%28country%29) and [Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Albania)) and the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire),[^n7] which are all described in detail.[^n30][^n69] The information on Armenia is not found elsewhere in historical sources,[^n6] as it is the only known Armenian geographical work prior to the 13th century.[^n4] Cowe described it as "the only one of its kind in Armenia until the introduction of modern [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) in the seventeenth century."[^n70] [Christina Maranci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Maranci) described it as the "earliest surviving description of the entire known world in the Armenian language."[^o190]

The *Ashkharhatsuyts* has survived in long and short [recensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recension).[^n69] According to the scholarly consensus, the long recension was the original.[^n71] For the description of Europe, North Africa and Asia (all the [known world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_world_maps) from Spain to China),[^n6] it largely uses Greek sources, namely the now lost geography of [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) (4th century), which in turn, is based on the *[Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_%28Ptolemy%29)* of [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979) (2nd century).[^n3][^n7][^n69] According to Hewsen, it is the "last work based on ancient geographical knowledge written before the Renaissance."[^n29] Edmond Schütz called it an "outstanding work of medieval sciences, a rich post-[Ptolemaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom) heredity."[^o198]

It was one of the earliest secular Armenian works to be published (in 1668 by [Voskan Yerevantsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskan_Yerevantsi)).[^n72] It has been translated into four languages: English, Latin (both 1736), French (1819), and Russian (1877).[^n73] In 1877, [Kerovbe Patkanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerovbe_Patkanian) first attributed it to Anania as the most probable author.[^n74]

Another geographical work of Anania, *The Itinerary* (Մղոնաչափք, *Mghonach’ap’k’*), may have been a part of the *Ashkharhatsuyts*. It presents six routes from [Dvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvin_%28ancient_city%29), Armenia's capital at that time, to the major settlements in different directions, with distances in miles (մղոն, *mghon*), referring to the [Arabic mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_mile) of 1,917.6 m, according to [Hakob Manandian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakob_Manandian).[^n75]

### Chronology {#Chronology}

Anania's major chronological work, the *Chronicle*, listed important events in order of their occurrence.[^n16] Written between 686 and 690, it is composed of two parts: a universal chronicle, utilizing the lost works of [Annianus of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annianus_of_Alexandria) and the lost Roman imperial sequence from [Eusebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius)'s *[Chronographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicon_%28Eusebius%29)*, and an ecclesiastical history from a [miaphysite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miaphysite) perspective, which records the six [ecumenical councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_council).[^n76]

Another chronological work, known as the *Calendar* (*Tomar*), included texts and tables about the calendars of 15 peoples: Armenians, Hebrews, Arabs, Macedonians, Romans, Syrians, Greeks, Egyptians, Ethiopians, Athenians, Bythanians, Cappadocians, Georgians, Caucasian Albanians, and Persians.[^n77][^n78] The calendars of the Armenians, Romans, Hebrews, Syrians, Greeks, and Egyptians contain texts, while those of other peoples only have the names of months and their length.[^n79]

### Other {#Other}

Anania wrote several books on weights and measures. He extensively used the [work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Weights_and_Measures) of [Epiphanius of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis) to present the system of weights used by the Greeks, Jews, and Syrians, and his own knowledge as well as other sources for those of the Armenians and Persians.[^n80][^n6]

Anania wrote several works on [precious stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gems),[^n31] music, and the known languages of the world.[^n6]

Anania's discourses on Christmas/[Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_%28holiday%29) and [Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter) are discussions on the dates of the two feasts. In the first, he uses a lost work he ascribes to [Polycarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycarp) of Smyrna and insists that the Armenian custom of celebrating Christmas and the Epiphany on the same date is truer to the holidays' intent than celebrating them separately as is common elsewhere in the Christian world.[^n31][^n54]

## Traditions and legends {#Traditions_and_legends}

Anania also wrote on [herbal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine), though none of his medical writings have survived. He is traditionally credited with the discovery of the miraculous flower called xcl or *hamasp’iur* (համասփիւռ).[^n14] One 16th century manuscript mentions that he dealt with its therapeutic properties. It has been identified by modern scholars as *[Silene latifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silene_latifolia)* (white campion). He is credited with discovering the plant in Dzoghakert (near modern [Taşburun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taşburun,_Karakoyunlu), [Iğdır](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iğdır_Province), Turkey)[^o218] and using it medically.[^o219][^o220][^o221]

The authorship of the "Book of the Six Thousand" (*Vec‘ hazareak*), the "most important Armenian magical text of the Middle Ages", has traditionally been attributed to Anania.[^n14][^o223] According to a later legend, Anania taught [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy) to the king of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice).[^n14]

## Influence in the Middle Ages {#Influence_in_the_Middle_Ages}

*Image: «Անանիա Շիրակացի» հուշարձան, բազալտ, 2009, Գյումրու Անանիա Շիրակացի փողոց.jpg.* 2009 statue of Anania in [Gyumri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyumri)

Anania laid the foundations of the [exact sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_sciences) in Armenia and greatly influenced many Armenian scholars who came after him.[^n30] [Robert H. Hewsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Hewsen) argued that Anania failed to create a true intellectual school with disciples who continued his legacy and his isolated genius exposes the dire state of medieval Armenian science.[^o255] [Hovhannes Imastaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovhannes_Imastaser) (Sarkavag) and other medieval scholars extensively cited and incorporated Anania's works.[^n44] In a 1037 letter, [Grigor Magistros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigor_Magistros), a scholar from the [Pahlavuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavuni) noble family, asked Catholicos [Petros Getadardz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Armenia) for Anania's manuscripts of his *K’nnikon*, which were locked up at the [catholicosate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicosate) for centuries.[^n81][^n82] Grigor used these as a textbook at his school at [Sanahin Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanahin_Monastery).[^n83] Anania may have also influenced Byzantine scholars of Armenian origin, namely [Leo the Mathematician](wikipedia://en/20205548) in the 9th century[^n84] and [Nicholas Artabasdos Rhabdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Artabasdos_Rhabdas) in the 14th century.[^n85]

## Reemergence in the modern period {#Reemergence_in_the_modern_period}

In the printed age, passing references to Anania were made as early as 1742 by [Paghtasar Dpir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paghtasar_Dpir), but it was not until the latter half of the 19th century that Anania and his work became a subject of scholarly study.[^n86] In 1877 [Kerovbe Patkanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerovbe_Patkanian) published a collection of Anania's works in the original [classical Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Armenian) at [St Petersburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Petersburg_University).[^n87] Titled *Sundry Studies* (Մնացորդք բանից, *Mnats’ordk’ banits’*),[^n88] it is the first-ever print publication of his works.[^n30] Galust Ter-Mkrtchian published a number of Anania's works in 1896.[^n87] The [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) published [Joseph Orbeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Orbeli)'s Russian translation of Anania's *Problems and Solutions* in 1918.[^n87][^o240]

Systematic study and publication of his works began in the Soviet period.[^n87] [Ashot G. Abrahamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashot_G._Abrahamian), who began his research at the [Matenadaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matenadaran) in the 1930s, first published one of Anania's arithmetical texts in 1939,[^n87] followed by a complete compilation of Anania's work in 1944.[^n89] One critic objected to his compilation for attributing disputed works to Anania.[^n90] Abrahamian and Garegin Petrosian published an updated edition in 1979.[^n91] Some criticism persisted: Varag Arakelian noted a number of errors in translations from classical Armenian and concluded that a new translation of his works was needed.[^n92] Another Soviet scholar, [Suren T. Eremian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suren_Yeremian), studied the *Geography*. He insisted on Anania's authorship and published his research in 1963.[^n93]

The first translation of Anania's work into a European language was done by the British Orientalist [Frederick Cornwallis Conybeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Cornwallis_Conybeare), who translated into English Anania's *On Christmas*, in 1896, and *On Easter* and Anania's autobiography, in 1897.[^n94][^n87] Lemerle noted that Conybeare translated Anania's autobiography from a Russian translation, and it contains numerous serious errors.[^n95] Renewed interest in Anania's work emerged in the West since the 1950s, with a series of English articles in the *[Armenian Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Review)*.[^o251] A French translation of his autobiography appeared in 1964 by Haïg Berbérian.[^n96] [Robert H. Hewsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Hewsen) authored an introductory article on Anania's life and scholarship in 1968.[^n97] Hewsen [dedicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedication_%28publishing%29) his monumental atlas of Armenia (2001) to Anania, whom he called "Armenia's First Scientist."[^n98][^o255]

## Modern assessment {#Modern_assessment}

Hailed as a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath),[^n99][^o257][^n7] Anania is considered by modern scholars as the "father of the exact sciences in Armenia."[^o259] Modern historians consider him as the greatest scientist of medieval Armenia[^o260] and, possibly, all Armenian history, up to the 20th century astrophysicist [Viktor Ambartsumian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Ambartsumian).[^o261][^n100] He is widely regarded as the founder of the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) in the country.[^n36][^n13] He was the first classical Armenian scholar to study mathematics and several scientific subjects, such as cosmography and chronology.[^n101][^n95] [Nicholas Adontz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Adontz) argued that Anania "occupied the same position in Armenian education as [Leo \[the Mathematician](wikipedia://en/20205548)\] did in Byzantine education. He was the first to sow the seeds of science among the Armenians."[^n102] [Robert H. Hewsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Hewsen) called him "one of the most original" and "one of the most curious figures in Armenian cultural history."[^o255] [Hacikyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agop_Jack_Hacikyan) et al. wrote in *The Heritage of Armenian Literature*: "Shirakatsi was an educator and an organizer of ideas and materials rather than an original thinker. He was often in the forefront of scientific thinking, but at other times he repeated the accepted theories of his time."[^n30]

[Suren Yeremian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suren_Yeremian) named Anania, along with historian [Movses Khorenatsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi) and philosopher [David the Invincible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_the_Invincible), one of the prominent thinkers of the "great cultural flourishing" in Armenia of the fifth to seventh centuries, when Hellenistic traditions were still strong and continued to bear the influence of the secular thinking of the pre-Christian times.[^o269] [Sen Arevshatyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sen_Arevshatyan) argued that the "Christian spirit" is more prominent in Anania than in earlier figures of the Hellenizing School as he aims to bridge Hellenistic science and church doctrine. "Nevertheless, despite his inconsistencies, he emerges as the greatest representative of natural knowledge and natural philosophy in early feudal Armenia."[^n13]

In Soviet [reference books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_book), Anania was often mentioned as the earliest astronomer among [its peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Soviet_Union#Ethnic_groups).[^o271][^o272] Greenwood argues that studying Anania and his works "resonated with twentieth-century political beliefs and offered a suitable subject for academic research in ways that works on medieval theology or Biblical exegesis did not. Anania came to be projected as a national hero from the distant Armenian past, linking and affirming past and present identities."[^n103]

## Tributes {#Tributes}

- Statuettes of Anania were created by Nikolai Nikoghosyan (c. 1945)[^o275] and [Yervand Kochar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yervand_Kochar) ([gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), 1952).[^o276] [Ara Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ara_Sargsyan) crafted a [plaquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaquette) of him in 1957.[^o277]
- Anania was one of six medieval scholars whose statue was erected in front of the [Matenadaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matenadaran), the museum-institute of Armenian manuscripts in Yerevan, in the 1960s.[^n104] Sculpted by Grigor Badalyan in [basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt), it was erected in 1963.[^o279] A bust of Anania by Badalyan stands inside the Matenadaran.
- A statue, sculpted by Aram Gharibyan, was erected in the front yard of [Yerevan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_University) in 1999.[^o280] Another basalt statue of Anania by Samvel Petrosyan was erected in Gyumri in 2009.[^o281]
- A [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirakatsi_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) was named Shirakatsi in 1979.[^o282]
- The Anania Shirakatsy Lyceum, an [International Baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate) school in Yerevan, was established in 1990.[^o283][^o284]
- In 1993 the Medal of Anania Shirakatsi, a [state award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders,_decorations,_and_medals_of_Armenia), was established. It is awarded for "significant activities, inventions, and discoveries in the spheres of economy, engineering, architecture, science, and technology."[^o285]
- In 2005 the [Central Bank of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Armenia) issued a [commemorative coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commemorative_coin), while [HayPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HayPost) issued a stamp dedicated to him.[^o286][^o287]
- A short 2016 documentary by [Armenian Public TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Public_TV).[^o288]
