# Inoue surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inoue surface"}

In [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), an **Inoue surface** is any of several [complex surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_surface) of [Kodaira class VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_class_VII). They are named after [Masahisa Inoue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masahisa_Inoue), who gave the first non-trivial examples of Kodaira class VII surfaces in 1974.[^o2]

:::

The Inoue surfaces are not [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538).

## Inoue surfaces with *b*<sub>2</sub> = 0 {#Inoue_surfaces_with_b2_=_0}

Inoue introduced three families of surfaces, *S*<sup>0</sup>, *S*<sup>\+</sup> and *S*<sup>−</sup>, which are compact quotients of $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}$ (a product of a [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) by a half-plane). These Inoue surfaces are [solvmanifolds](wikipedia://en/4292247). They are obtained as quotients of $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}$ by a solvable [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) which acts holomorphically on $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}.$

The solvmanifold surfaces constructed by Inoue all have second [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) $b_2=0$. These surfaces are of [Kodaira class VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_class_VII), which means that they have $b_1=1$ and [Kodaira dimension](wikipedia://en/1866873) $-\infty$. It was proven by [Bogomolov](wikipedia://en/8823369),[^o4] Li–[Yau](wikipedia://en/303137)[^o5] and Teleman[^o6] that any [surface of class VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfaces_of_class_VII) with $b_2=0$ is a [Hopf surface](wikipedia://en/7016769) or an Inoue-type solvmanifold.

These surfaces have no meromorphic functions and no curves.

K. Hasegawa [^o7] gives a list of all complex 2-dimensional solvmanifolds; these are [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042), [hyperelliptic surface](wikipedia://en/8358276), [Kodaira surface](wikipedia://en/21256202) and Inoue surfaces *S*<sup>0</sup>, *S*<sup>\+</sup> and *S*<sup>−</sup>.

The Inoue surfaces are constructed explicitly as follows.[^o7]

### Of type *S*<sup>0</sup> {#Of_type_S0}

Let *φ* be an integer 3 × 3 matrix, with two complex eigenvalues $\alpha, \overline{\alpha}$ and a real eigenvalue *c* > 1, with $|\alpha|^2c=1$. Then *φ* is invertible over integers, and defines an action of the group of integers, $\mathbb{Z},$ on $\mathbb{Z}^3$. Let $\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}^3\rtimes\mathbb{Z}.$ This group is a lattice in [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945)

$$\mathbb{R}^3\rtimes\mathbb{R} = (\mathbb{C} \times\mathbb{R} ) \rtimes\mathbb{R},$$

acting on $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R},$ with the $(\mathbb{C} \times\mathbb{R} )$-part acting by translations and the $\rtimes\mathbb{R}$-part as $(z,r) \mapsto (\alpha^tz, c^tr).$

We extend this action to $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H} = \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^{>0}$ by setting $v \mapsto e^{\log ct} v$, where *t* is the parameter of the $\rtimes\mathbb{R}$-part of $\mathbb{R}^3\rtimes\mathbb{R},$ and acting trivially with the $\mathbb{R}^3$ factor on $\mathbb{R}^{>0}$. This action is clearly holomorphic, and the quotient $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}/\Gamma$ is called **Inoue surface of type** $S^0.$

The Inoue surface of type *S*<sup>0</sup> is determined by the choice of an integer matrix *φ*, constrained as above. There is a countable number of such surfaces.

### Of type *S*<sup>\+</sup> {#Of_type_S+}

Let *n* be a positive integer, and $\Lambda_n$ be the group of upper triangular matrices

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & x & z/n \\ 0 & 1 & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad x,y,z \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

The quotient of $\Lambda_n$ by its center *C* is $\mathbb{Z}^2$. Let *φ* be an automorphism of $\Lambda_n$, we assume that *φ* acts on $\Lambda_n/C=\mathbb{Z}^2$ as a matrix with two positive real eigenvalues *a, b*, and *ab* = 1. Consider the solvable group $\Gamma_n := \Lambda_n\rtimes \mathbb{Z},$ with $\mathbb{Z}$ acting on $\Lambda_n$ as *φ*. Identifying the group of upper triangular matrices with $\mathbb{R}^3,$ we obtain an action of $\Gamma_n$ on $\mathbb{R}^3= \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R}.$ Define an action of $\Gamma_n$ on $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}= \mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^{>0}$ with $\Lambda_n$ acting trivially on the $\mathbb{R}^{>0}$-part and the $\mathbb{Z}$ acting as $v \mapsto e^{t \log b}v.$ The same argument as for Inoue surfaces of type $S^0$ shows that this action is holomorphic. The quotient $\mathbb{C} \times \mathbb{H}/\Gamma_n$ is called **Inoue surface of type** $S^+.$

### Of type *S*<sup>−</sup> {#Of_type_S−}

**Inoue surfaces of type** $S^-$ are defined in the same way as for *S*<sup>\+</sup>, but two eigenvalues *a, b* of *φ* acting on $\mathbb{Z}^2$ have opposite sign and satisfy *ab* = −1. Since a square of such an endomorphism defines an Inoue surface of type *S*<sup>\+</sup>, an Inoue surface of type *S*<sup>−</sup> has an unramified double cover of type *S*<sup>\+</sup>.

## Parabolic and hyperbolic Inoue surfaces {#Parabolic_and_hyperbolic_Inoue_surfaces}

Parabolic and hyperbolic Inoue surfaces are Kodaira class VII surfaces defined by [Iku Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iku_Nakamura) in 1984.[^o13] They are not solvmanifolds. These surfaces have positive second Betti number. They have [spherical shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_shell_conjecture), and can be deformed into a blown-up [Hopf surface](wikipedia://en/7016769).

Parabolic Inoue surfaces contain a cycle of rational curves with 0 self-intersection and an elliptic curve. They are a particular case of Enoki surfaces which have a cycle of rational curves with zero self-intersection but without elliptic curve. Half-Inoue surfaces contain a cycle *C* of rational curves and are a quotient of a hyperbolic Inoue surface with two cycles of rational curves.

Hyperbolic Inoue surfaces are class VII<sub>0</sub> surfaces with two cycles of rational curves.[^o14] Parabolic and hyperbolic surfaces are particular cases of minimal surfaces with global spherical shells (GSS) also called Kato surfaces. All these surfaces may be constructed by non invertible contractions.[^o15]
