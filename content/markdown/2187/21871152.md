# Dyson's transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dyson's transform"}

**Dyson's transform** is a fundamental technique in [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333) for transforming a pair of [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421) into another pair of integer sequences.[^o2] It was developed by [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397) as part of his proof of [Mann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mann's_theorem) on the [Schnirelmann density](wikipedia://en/343156) of [sumsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset),[^o3] is used to prove such fundamental results of additive number theory as the [Cauchy–Davenport theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Davenport_theorem) on the size of [restricted sumsets](wikipedia://en/7960510),[^o2] and was used by [Olivier Ramaré](wikipedia://en/2916107) in his work (related to the [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture)) that proved that every even integer is the sum of at most 6 [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).[^o4] The term *Dyson's transform* for this technique is used by Ramaré.[^o4] Halberstam and Roth call it the τ-transformation.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

This formulation of the transform is from Ramaré.[^o4] Let $A$ be a sequence of natural numbers, and $x$ be any [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). Write $A(x)$ for the number of elements of $A$ which lie in $[1,x]$. Suppose $A= \{a_1<a_2< \cdots\}$ and $B= \{0=b_1<b_2<\cdots\}$ are two sequences of natural numbers. We write $A+B$ for the [sumset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset), that is, the set of all elements $a+b$ where $a$ is in $A$ and $b$ is in $B$; and similarly $A-B$ for the set of differences $a-b$. For any element $e$ in $A$, Dyson's transform consists in forming the sequences $A'= A \cup (B + \{e\})$ and $\,B'= B \cap (A - \{e\})$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The transformed sequences have the properties:

- $$A' + B' \subset A + B$$
- $$\{e\} + B' \subset A'$$
- $$0 \in B'$$
- $$A'(m)+ B'(m-e) = A(m) + B(m-e)$$

:::

## Related transforms {#Related_transforms}

Other closely related transforms are sometimes referred to as Dyson transforms. This includes the transform defined by $A_1 = A \cap (A + \{e\})$, $A_2 = A \cup (A + \{e\})$, $B_1 = B \cap (-\{e\} + B)$, $B_2 = B \cup (-\{e\} + B)$ for $A, B$ sets in a (not necessarily abelian) group. This transformation has the property that

- $$A_1 + B_1 \subset A + B, A_2 + B_2 \subset A + B$$
- $|A_1| + |A_2| = 2|A|$, $|B_1| + |B_2| = 2|B|$

It can be used to prove a generalisation of the [Cauchy-Davenport theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy-Davenport_theorem).[^o10]
