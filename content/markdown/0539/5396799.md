# Riemann xi function

*Image: Riemann Xi cplot.svg.* Riemann xi function $\xi(s)$ in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). The color of a point $s$ encodes the value of the function. Darker colors denote values closer to zero and hue encodes the value's [argument](wikipedia://en/5826).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemann xi function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Riemann xi function** is a variant of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809), and is defined so as to have a particularly simple [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377). The function is named in honour of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Riemann's original lower-case "xi"-function, $\xi$ was renamed with a $\Xi$ ([Greek uppercase letter "xi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_%28letter%29)) by [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230). Landau's $\xi$ (lower-case "xi") is defined as[^o4]

$$\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2} s(s-1) \pi^{-s/2} \Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right) \zeta(s)$$

for $s \in \mathbb{C}$. Here $\zeta(s)$ denotes the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) and $\Gamma(s)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

The functional equation (or [reflection formula](wikipedia://en/4025531)) for Landau's $\xi$ is

$$\xi(1-s) = \xi(s) .$$

Riemann's original function, renamed as the upper-case $\Xi$ by Landau,[^o4] satisfies

$$\Xi(z) = \xi \left(\tfrac{1}{2} + z i \right) ,$$

and obeys the functional equation

$$\Xi(-z) = \Xi(z) .$$

Both functions are [entire](wikipedia://en/10372) and purely real for real arguments.

:::

## Values {#Values}

The general form for positive even integers is

$$\xi(2n) = (-1)^{n+1}\frac{n!}{(2n)!}B_{2n}2^{2n-1}\pi^{n}(2n-1)$$

where $B_n$ denotes the $n$th [Bernoulli number](wikipedia://en/4964). For example:

$$\xi(2) = {\frac{\pi}{6}}$$

## Series representations {#Series_representations}

The $\xi$ function has the series expansion

$$\frac{d}{dz} \ln \xi \left(\frac{-z}{1-z}\right) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \lambda_{n+1} z^n,$$

where

$$\lambda_n = \frac{1}{(n-1)!} \left. \frac{d^n}{ds^n} \left[s^{n-1} \log \xi(s) \right] \right|_{s=1} = \sum_{\rho} \left[ 1- \left(1-\frac{1}{\rho}\right)^n \right],$$

where the sum extends over $\rho$, the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function, in order of $\vert\Im(\rho)\vert$.

This expansion plays a particularly important role in [Li's criterion](wikipedia://en/4049772), which states that the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) is equivalent to having $\lambda_n > 0$ for all positive $n$.

## Hadamard product {#Hadamard_product}

A simple [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) expansion is

$$\xi(s) = \frac12 \prod_\rho \left(1 - \frac{s}{\rho} \right),$$

where $\rho$ ranges over the roots of $\xi$.

To ensure convergence in the expansion, the product should be taken over "matching pairs" of zeroes, i.e., the factors for a pair of zeroes of the form $\rho$ and $\bar\rho$ should be grouped together.
