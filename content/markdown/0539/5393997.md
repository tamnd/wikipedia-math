# Elementary amenable group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elementary amenable group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is called **elementary amenable** if it can be built up from [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) and [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) by a sequence of simple operations that result in [amenable groups](wikipedia://en/657430) when applied to amenable groups. Since finite groups and abelian groups are amenable, every elementary amenable group is amenable - however, the converse is not true.

:::

Formally, the class of elementary amenable groups is the smallest subclass of the class of all groups that satisfies the following conditions:

- it contains all finite and all abelian groups
- if *G* is in the subclass and *H* is isomorphic to *G*, then *H* is in the subclass
- it is closed under the operations of taking [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011), forming [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526), and forming [extensions](wikipedia://en/345807)
- it is closed under [directed unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_union).

The [Tits alternative](wikipedia://en/12704641) implies that any amenable linear group is locally virtually solvable; hence, for linear groups, amenability and elementary amenability coincide.
