# Transfer entropy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transfer entropy"}

**Transfer entropy** is a [non-parametric statistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics) measuring the amount of directed (time-asymmetric) transfer of [information](wikipedia://en/18985062) between two [random processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_process).[^o2][^o3][^o4] Transfer entropy from a process *X* to another process *Y* is the amount of uncertainty reduced in future values of *Y* by knowing the past values of *X* given past values of *Y*. More specifically, if $X_t$ and $Y_t$ for $t\in \mathbb{N}$ denote two random processes and the amount of information is measured using [Shannon's entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon's_entropy), the transfer entropy can be written as:

:::

$$T_{X\rightarrow Y} = H\left( Y_t \mid Y_{t-1:t-L}\right) - H\left( Y_t \mid Y_{t-1:t-L}, X_{t-1:t-L}\right),$$

where *H*(*X*) is Shannon's entropy of *X*. The above definition of transfer entropy has been extended by other types of [entropy](wikipedia://en/15445) measures such as [Rényi entropy](wikipedia://en/1731689).[^o4][^o5]

Transfer entropy is [conditional mutual information](wikipedia://en/18867510),[^o6][^o7] with the history of the influenced variable $Y_{t-1:t-L}$ in the condition:

$$T_{X\rightarrow Y} = I(Y_t ; X_{t-1:t-L} \mid Y_{t-1:t-L}).$$

Transfer entropy reduces to [Granger causality](wikipedia://en/1648224) for [vector auto-regressive processes](wikipedia://en/1434444).[^o8] Hence, it is advantageous when the model assumption of Granger causality doesn't hold, for example, analysis of [non-linear signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_regression).[^o16][^o9] However, it usually requires more samples for accurate estimation.[^o10] The probabilities in the entropy formula can be estimated using different approaches (binning, nearest neighbors) or, in order to reduce complexity, using a non-uniform embedding.[^o11] While it was originally defined for [bivariate analysis](wikipedia://en/30408417), transfer entropy has been extended to [multivariate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis) forms, either conditioning on other potential source variables[^o12] or considering transfer from a collection of sources,[^o13] although these forms require more samples again.

Transfer entropy has been used for estimation of [functional connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_connectivity) of [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons),[^o13][^o14][^o15] [social influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_influence) in [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_networks)[^o16] and [statistical causality](wikipedia://en/37103476) between armed conflict events.[^o17] Transfer entropy is a finite version of the [directed information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_information) which was defined in 1990 by [James Massey](wikipedia://en/9543907)[^o18] as $I(X^n\to Y^n) =\sum_{i=1}^n I(X^i;Y_i|Y^{i-1})$, where $X^n$ denotes the vector $X_1,X_2,...,X_n$ and $Y^n$ denotes $Y_1,Y_2,...,Y_n$. The [directed information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_information) places an important role in characterizing the fundamental limits ([channel capacity](wikipedia://en/231204)) of communication channels with or without feedback[^o19][^o20] and [gambling](wikipedia://en/11921) with causal side information.[^o21]
