# Muthu Alagappan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Muthu Alagappan"}

**Muthu Alagappan** (born c. 1990[^o12][^o9]) is a former medical resident known for his [professional basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association) analytics. He was born in England and raised in Texas. During college at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), he began an internship at [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151) [startup company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) Ayasdi, where he used their software on [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball) statistics to determine 13 distinct [positions of play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_positions). After he spoke at the 2012 [MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference](wikipedia://en/33296481), several professional teams began to use the company's software. He was given the top prize at the conference, and *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ)* called his work both "a new frontier for the NBA" and "Muthuball"—an allusion to [Moneyball](wikipedia://en/28692) [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball) [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis) known for revolutionizing the sport.[^o12] *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* included him in their 2012 and 2013 "[30 Under 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_30_Under_30)" list of influential people in the sports industry. His work has received mention in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*, *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_%28magazine%29)*, and *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_%28magazine%29)*.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Alagappan was born in [Crewe, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewe,_England), and raised in [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston), US.[^o9][^o14] He is of [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils) heritage.[^o11] He graduated from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 2012 with a degree in biomechanical engineering[^o11][^o15] and the [Stanford Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_Medical_School) in 2016.[^o4] , he was an internal medicine resident at [Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Israel_Deaconess_Medical_Center).[^o5] Alagappan is a longtime [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets) fan.[^o9]

## Basketball analytics {#Basketball_analytics}

Alagappan was hired as an [intern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intern) with Ayasdi, a [big data](wikipedia://en/27051151) [startup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) in [Palo Alto, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California), through Stanford's Mayfield Fellows Program, where he was hired to do sales.[^o11] He tested the company's software on basketball stats and called the output "the true positions of the [NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association)".[^o12] The [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009) mapped similarities between NBA players and identified 13 distinct [positions of play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_positions), as opposed to the traditional five.[^o13] Those traditional positions, such as [power forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_forward_%28basketball%29) and [shooting guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_guard), have become ambiguous and outmoded as a result of the increased variety of player styles categorized by them.[^o13] Alagappan's method groups professional basketball players by statistical strengths and places them into the new positional model.[^o9]

He started with a collection of seven basic statistics ([assists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_%28basketball%29), [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_%28basketball%29), [fouls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_%28basketball%29), [points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_%28basketball%29), [rebounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebound_%28basketball%29), [steals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_%28basketball%29), and [turnovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnover_%28basketball%29)) from [Yahoo! Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!_Sports) on every NBA player from the previous season and adjusted the stats for time in play.[^o11] The software grouped the players into color-coded nodes and connected them with lines that represented statistical similarities.[^o11] Alagappan had expected the software to create five groups of nodes by statistical advantages, which would each represent a position of play, but the software instead returned 13 node groups representing 13 mathematically described positions.[^o11] The assignments compare player statistics and not individual style.[^o11] Originally unconfident in his results, he explored the data on his own for a week before sharing his findings with his bosses and basketball statisticians including [Ken Pomeroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Pomeroy), who replied in encouragement.[^o11] He then applied and was accepted to speak at the 2012 [MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference](wikipedia://en/33296481).[^o11] By May 2012, at least four NBA teams and one [MLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball) team had contacted Ayasdi about using their technology.[^o11]

The method is intended to change existing basketball team composition paradigms to help NBA teams win championships.[^o11] Alagappan used [Devin Ebanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devin_Ebanks) of the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) as an example, whom Alagappan considered promising for his classification as a "scoring rebounder" alongside [Carmelo Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelo_Anthony) and [Dirk Nowitzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Nowitzki) despite a subpar season record.[^o11] *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ)* reported that Ebanks would come to fulfill the model's forecast in following games for six weeks when two other players sat out and Ebanks [started](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_lineup#Basketball) games.[^o11] While the model couldn't have predicted [Linsanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linsanity), when given college basketball stats, it was able to determine three players most similar to Lin in college, of which two were already drafted in the NBA.[^o16] The forecasting is expected to improve with the growth of the data set.[^o11]

His data-driven analysis has been well received. He won the top prize in the "Evolution of Sports" category at the 2012 [MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference](wikipedia://en/33296481).[^o12][^o11] *GQ* called his work "a new frontier for the NBA" and "Muthuball", an allusion to [Billy Beane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Beane)'s "[Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball:_The_Art_of_Winning_an_Unfair_Game)" [sabermetrics](wikipedia://en/28692) ([baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball) [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis) known for revolutionizing the sport).[^o12] *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* included him in their 2012 and 2013 "30 Under 30" lists of influential people in the sports industry.[^o12][^o9][^o10] His work has received mention in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*, *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_%28magazine%29)*, and *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_%28magazine%29)*.[^o12][^o11]

Rob Mahoney of *The New York Times* called the model "a novel execution of a productive thought", but cited the "one-of-a-kind" catch-all position and the substantial number of players categorized within it as weak points.[^o13] ESPN [TrueHoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrueHoop)'s Jim Calvan criticized Alagappan's Sloan conference presentation for its lack of data showing how team reconfiguration to cover the 13 positions results in more wins.[^o8]
