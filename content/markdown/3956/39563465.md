## Computer algebra systems {#Computer_algebra_systems}

A [computer algebra system](wikipedia://en/54427) (CAS) is a software product designed for manipulation of [mathematical formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_formulae). The principal objective of a computer algebra system is to systematize monotonous and sometimes problematic [algebraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_data_type) manipulation tasks. The primary difference between a computer algebra system and a traditional [calculator](wikipedia://en/7593) is the ability to deal with equations [symbolically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_computation) rather than numerically. The precise uses and capabilities of these systems differ greatly from one system to another, yet their purpose remains the same: manipulation of [symbolic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computing). Computer algebra systems often include facilities for [graphing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infographic) equations and provide a [programming language](wikipedia://en/23015) for the users' own [procedures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subroutine).

### [Axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_%28computer_algebra_system%29) {#Axiom}

Axiom is a general-purpose CAS. It has been in development since 1971 by [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), and was originally named *scratchpad*. Richard Jenks originally headed it but over the years Barry Trager who then shaped the direction of the scratchpad project took over the project. It was eventually sold to the [Numerical Algorithms Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_Algorithms_Group) (NAG) and was renamed Axiom. After a failure to launch as a product, NAG decided to release it as a [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) in 2001 with more than 300 man-years worth of research involved. Axiom is licensed under a Modified [BSD license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSD_license).

### [Cadabra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadabra_%28computer_program%29) {#Cadabra}

A CAS designed for the solution of problems in [physical field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) theory. An unpublished computational program written in [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28programming_language%29) called Abra inspired this [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software). *Abra* was originally designed for [physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicists) to compute problems present in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). Kespers Peeters then decided to write a similar program in [C computing language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) rather than Pascal, which he renamed [Cadabra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadabra_%28computer_program%29). However, Cadabra has been expanded for a wider range of uses, it is no longer restricted to physicists.

### [CoCoA](wikipedia://en/1674062) {#CoCoA}

CoCoA (COmputations in COmmutative Algebra) is [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) used for computing [multivariate polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials) and initiated in 1987. Originally written in [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_%28programming_language%29), [CoCoA](wikipedia://en/1674062) was later translated into [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29).

### [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28software%29) {#GAP}

GAP was initiated by [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University) in 1986. This was the case until in 1997 when they decided to co-develop GAP further with CIRCA (Centre for Research in Computational Algebra). Unlike MAXIMA and Axiom, GAP is a system for computational [discrete algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) with particular emphasis on [computational group theory](wikipedia://en/618584). In March 2005 the GAP Council and the GAP developers have agreed that status and responsibilities of "GAP Headquarters" should be passed to an equal collaboration of a number of "GAP Centres", where there is permanent staff involvement and an element of collective or organizational commitment, while fully recognizing the vital contributions of many individuals outside those centers.

### [Mathomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathomatic) {#Mathomatic}

[Mathomatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathomatic) is [portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porting) [general-purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-purpose_computing_on_graphics_processing_units) [computer algebra system](wikipedia://en/54427) capable of performing symbolic [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), algebraic simplification, and equation solving.

### Mathics {#Mathics}

Mathics is an open-source GPL3 license version of the [Wolfram Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Language).

### [Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_%28software%29) {#Maxima}

This [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) had an earlier incarnation, [Macsyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macsyma). Developed by [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in the 1960s, it was maintained by [William Schelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Schelter) from 1982 to 2001. In 1998, Schelter obtained permission to release Maxima as [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) under the [GNU General Public license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Public_License) and the [source code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_code) was released later that year. Since his death in 2001, a group of Maxima enthusiasts have continued to provide technical support.

### [PARI/GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARI/GP) {#PARI/GP}

PARI/GP is a computer algebra system that facilitates number-theory computation. Besides support of factoring, algebraic number theory, and analysis of elliptic curves, it works with mathematical objects like matrices, polynomials, [power series](wikipedia://en/59958), algebraic numbers, and [transcendental functions](wikipedia://en/297021).[^o27] Originally developed by Henri Cohen et al at Université Bordeaux I, France, it now is [GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPL) software. The gp interactive shell allows GP-language scripting; the gp2c compiler compiles GP scripts into C; and the PARI C library allows C programs to use PARI/GP functions.

### [Sympy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympy) {#Sympy}

Sympy is a computer algebra system written in [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29).

### [Xcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xcas) {#Xcas}

Xcas/Giac is an open-source project developed at the [Joseph Fourier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fourier_University) of [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble) since 2000. Written in [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++), maintained by Bernard Parisse et al. and available for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS), [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) and many others platforms. It has a compatibility mode with [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29), [Derive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derive_%28computer_algebra_system%29) and [MuPAD](wikipedia://en/30874575) software and [TI-89](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-89), [TI-92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-92) and [Voyage 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyage_200) calculators. The system was chosen by [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) as the CAS for their [HP Prime](wikipedia://en/40988986) calculator, which utilizes the Giac/Xcas 1.1.2 engine under a dual-license scheme.

## Numerical analysis {#Numerical_analysis}

[Numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) is an area of mathematics that creates and analyzes [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms) for obtaining [numerical approximations](wikipedia://en/21506) to problems involving [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109). When an [arbitrary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrary) [function](wikipedia://en/185427) does not have a closed form as its solution, there would not be any analytical tools present to evaluate the desired solutions, hence an approximation method is employed instead.

### [Calcpad](wikipedia://en/82056772) {#Calcpad}

Calcpad is an open-source [numerical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_software) designed primarily for engineering calculations. It supports numerical differentiation and integration, root and extrema finding, vector and matrix calculations, combined with extensive Html reporting.

### [Euler Mathematical Toolbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Mathematical_Toolbox) {#Euler_Mathematical_Toolbox}

[Euler Mathematical Toolbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Mathematical_Toolbox) is an open-source [numerical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_software) system combining matrix language, symbolic algebra (via [Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_%28software%29)), and plotting capabilities.

### [FreeMat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeMat) {#FreeMat}

FreeMat is an alternative to [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412).

### [GNU Scientific Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Scientific_Library) {#GNU_Scientific_Library}

The GNU Scientific Library (or GSL) is a software library for numerical computations in applied mathematics and science. The GSL is written in C and wrappers are available for other programming languages. The GSL is part of the GNU Project and is distributed under the GNU General Public License.

### [Octave](wikipedia://en/48707) {#Octave}

Octave (aka GNU Octave) is an alternative to [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412). Originally conceived in 1988 by John W. Eaton as a companion software for an undergraduate textbook, Eaton later opted to modify it into a more flexible tool. Development began in 1992 and the [alpha version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_version) was released in 1993. Subsequently, version 1.0 was released a year after that in 1994. Octave is a high level [language](wikipedia://en/23015) with the primary intention in [numerical computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_computation).

### [Modelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modelica) {#Modelica}

Modelica is an object-oriented, declarative, multi-domain modeling language for component-oriented modeling of complex systems including algebraic and differential equations. OpenModelica and Jmodelica are some of the opensource implementations of the language.

### [Scilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scilab) {#Scilab}

Inspired by [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412), Scilab was initiated in the mid-1980s at the [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA) (French national Institute for computer science and control). François Delebecque and Serge Steer developed it and it was released by [INRIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INRIA) in 1994 as an open-source software. Since 2008, [Scilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scilab) has been distributed under the [CeCILL license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CeCILL), which is [GPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPL) compatible. In 2010, Scilab Enterprise was founded to provide even more support to the software.

### [SciPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SciPy) {#SciPy}

SciPy is a [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) programming language library to take advantage of Python's ability to handle large data sets.[^o139]

## Statistics {#Statistics}

[Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) is the study of how to collate and interpret numerical information from data. It is the science of learning from data and communicating uncertainty. There are two branches in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685): ‘[Descriptive statistics](wikipedia://en/8187)’’ and ‘’ [Inferential statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferential_statistics)

[Descriptive statistics](wikipedia://en/8187) involves methods of organizing, picturing and summarizing information from data. [Inferential statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferential_statistics) involves methods of using information from a [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) to draw conclusions about the Population.

### Alternatives to [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088) {#Alternatives_to_SPSS}

[R Statistics Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_%28language%29), [SOFA Statistics](wikipedia://en/23699874), [PSPP](wikipedia://en/16653967) and [JASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASP) are open source software competitors to [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088), widely used for statistical analysis of sampled data. PSPP is maintained by the GNU project. [SOFA Statistics](wikipedia://en/23699874) is addressing beginners with basic features, while [GNU-R Statistics Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_%28language%29) has a large community that maintains [R packages](wikipedia://en/65740838) also beyond statistical analysis.

### [R](wikipedia://en/376707) {#R}

[R](wikipedia://en/376707) is both a [language](wikipedia://en/23015) and [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) used for [statistical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_computing) and [graphing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infographic). [R](wikipedia://en/376707) was originally developed by [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) (Currently known as [Lucent Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent)) by [John Chambers](wikipedia://en/16745085). Since [R](wikipedia://en/376707) is largely written in [C language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_language), users can use [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28language%29) or [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) commands to manipulate R-objects directly. Also, [R](wikipedia://en/376707) runs on most [UNIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNIX) platforms. [R](wikipedia://en/376707) is currently part of the *[Free Software Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Software_Foundation)* [GNU project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_project).

### [Demetra+](wikipedia://en/30867211) {#Demetra+}

Demetra is a program for [seasonal adjustments](wikipedia://en/7579959) that was developed and published by [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) – [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission) under the [EUPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUPL) license.

## Multipurpose mathematics software {#Multipurpose_mathematics_software}

Such [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) were created with the original intent of providing a [math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math) platform that can be compared to [proprietary software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software) such as [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412) and [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica). They contain multiple other [free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software) and hence have more features than the rest of the software mentioned.

### [SageMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SageMath) {#SageMath}

SageMath is designed partially as a free alternative to the general-purpose mathematics products [Maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_%28software%29) and [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412). It can be downloaded or used through a web site. SageMath comprises a variety of other free packages, with a common interface and language. SageMath is developed in Python.

SageMath was initiated by [William Stein](wikipedia://en/7920662), of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2005 for his personal project in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). It was originally known as "HECKE and Manin". After a short while it was renamed SAGE, which stands for ‘’Software of Algebra and Geometry Experimentation’’. Sage 0.1 was released in 2005 and almost a year later Sage 1.0 was released. It already consisted of [Pari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARI/GP), [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_%28computer_algebra_system%29), [Singular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_%28software%29) and [Maxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_%28software%29) with an [interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interface_%28computing%29) that rivals that of [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica).
