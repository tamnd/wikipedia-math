# Antonio Ambrosetti

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Antonio Ambrosetti"}

**Antonio Ambrosetti** (25 November 1944 – 20 November 2020) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked in the fields of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).

:::

## Scientific activity {#Scientific_activity}

Ambrosetti studied at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) and was professor of mathematics at the [International School for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_for_Advanced_Studies). He is known for his basic work on topological methods in the calculus of variations. These provide tools aimed at establishing the existence of solutions to variational problems when classical [direct methods of the calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/24499402) cannot be applied. In particular, the so-called [mountain pass theorem](wikipedia://en/4408952) he established with [Paul Rabinowitz](wikipedia://en/22335165) is nowadays a classical tool in the context of nonlinear analysis problems.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Ambrosetti has been awarded the [Caccioppoli prize](wikipedia://en/23538352) in 1982, and the [Amerio Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerio_Prize) by the Istituto Lombardo Accademia di Scienze e Lettere in 2008. Jointly with [Andrea Malchiodi](wikipedia://en/39307807), Ambrosetti has been awarded the 2005 edition of the [Ferran Sunyer i Balaguer](wikipedia://en/36136840) prize.[^o8] In 1983 he has been [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) and he was fellow of the [Accedemia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei).
