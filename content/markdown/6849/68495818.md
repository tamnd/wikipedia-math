# Patrick Doreian

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick Doreian"}

**Patrick Doreian** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [social scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science), whose specialty is [network analysis](wikipedia://en/325726). His specific research interests include [blockmodeling](wikipedia://en/68458864), [social structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_structure) and [network processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_process).[^o3]

:::

Doreian, [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) from the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) in [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), was during his research career focused on social network research, especially regarding [temporal networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_network), [scientific collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_collaboration), [partitioning networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_network), [signed networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_network), [network autocorrelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_autocorrelation) and the [US Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Supreme_Court). He was also an (co)editor of *[The Journal of Mathematical Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Mathematical_Sociology)* (1982–2005) and *[Social Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Networks_%28journal%29)* (2006–2015).[^o4]

He was also a [Centennial professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_professor) at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) (2002) and a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at the [University of California-Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California-Irvine) and the [University of Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ljubljana).[^o5]

## Work {#Work}

With [Thomas J. Fararo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Fararo) in 1984, he introduced [tripartite structural analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_structural_analysis).[^o7]

With [Norman P. Hummon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_P._Hummon) in 1989, he proposed a [main path analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_path_analysis), a mathematical tool,[^o8] to identify the major paths in a [citation network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_network), which is one form of a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002) (DAG).

In 1994, with [Vladimir Batagelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Batagelj) and [Anuška Ferligoj](wikipedia://en/29730180), he introduced the [generalized blockmodeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_blockmodeling).[^o9]

## Awards {#Awards}

His co-authored book *Generalized blockmodeling* (with [Vladimir Batagelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Batagelj) and [Anuška Ferligoj](wikipedia://en/29730180)), was in 2007 awarded the Harrison White Outstanding Book Award by the Mathematical Sociology Section of [American Sociological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sociological_Association).[^o11]
