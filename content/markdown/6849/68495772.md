# Mabuchi functional

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mabuchi functional"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and especially [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), the **Mabuchi functional** or **K-energy functional** is a [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) on the space of [Kähler potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_potential) of a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) whose [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) are [constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics](wikipedia://en/37860980). The Mabuchi functional was introduced by [Toshiki Mabuchi](wikipedia://en/64788574) in 1985 as a functional which integrates the [Futaki invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futaki_invariant), which is an obstruction to the existence of a [Kähler–Einstein metric](wikipedia://en/7335270) on a Fano manifold.[^o2]

:::

The Mabuchi functional is an analogy of the log-norm functional of the [moment map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_map) in [geometric invariant theory](wikipedia://en/1639003) and [symplectic reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_reduction).[^o3] The Mabuchi functional appears in the theory of [K-stability](wikipedia://en/62719320) as an analytical functional which characterises the existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics. The slope at infinity of the Mabuchi functional along any [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) ray in the space of Kähler potentials is given by the [Donaldson–Futaki invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Futaki_invariant) of a corresponding [test configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_configuration).

Due to the variational techniques of Berman–Boucksom–Jonsson[^o4] in the study of Kähler–Einstein metrics on Fano varieties, the Mabuchi functional and various generalisations of it have become critically important in the study of [K-stability of Fano varieties](wikipedia://en/70175623), particularly in settings with singularities.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Mabuchi functional is defined on the space of Kähler potentials inside a fixed Kähler [cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_class) on a compact [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742).[^o7] Let $(M,\omega)$ be a compact Kähler manifold with a fixed Kähler metric $\omega$. Then by the [$\partial \bar \partial$-lemma](wikipedia://en/72112048), any other Kähler metric in the class $[\omega]\in H^2_{\text{dR}}(M)$ in [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) may be related to $\omega$ by a smooth function $\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)$, the Kähler potential:

$$\omega_\varphi = \omega + i \partial \bar \partial \varphi.$$

In order to ensure this new two-form is a Kähler metric, it must be a [positive form](wikipedia://en/10592733):

$$\omega_\varphi > 0.$$

These two conditions define the space of **Kähler potentials**

$$\mathcal{K} = \{ \varphi: M \to \mathbb{R} \mid \varphi\in C^{\infty}(X),\quad \omega + i \partial \bar \partial \varphi > 0\}.$$

Since any two Kähler potentials which differ by a constant function define the same Kähler metric, the space of Kähler metrics in the class $[\omega]$ can be identified with $\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{R}$, the Kähler potentials modulo the constant functions. One can instead restrict to those Kähler potentials which normalise so that their integral over $M$ vanishes.

The tangent space to $\mathcal{K}$ can be identified with the space of smooth real-valued functions on $M$. Let $S_\varphi$ denote the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) of the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) corresponding to $\omega_\varphi$, and let $\hat S$ denote the average of this scalar curvature over $M$, which does not depend on the choice of $\varphi$ by [Stokes theorem](wikipedia://en/29040). Define a differential [one-form](wikipedia://en/314692) on the space of Kähler potentials by

$$\alpha_\varphi (\psi) = \int_M \psi (\hat S - S_\varphi) \omega_\varphi^n.$$

This one-form is closed.[^o7] Since $\mathcal{K}$ is a [contractible space](wikipedia://en/1752414), this one-form is [exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_differential_form), and there exists a functional $\mathcal{M}: \mathcal{K} \to \mathbb{R}$ normalised so that $\mathcal{M}(0)=0$ such that $d\mathcal{M} = \alpha$, the **Mabuchi functional** or **K-energy**.

The Mabuchi functional has an explicit description given by integrating the one-form $\alpha$ along a path. Let $\varphi_0$ be a fixed Kähler potential, which may be taken as $\varphi_0=0$, and let $\varphi_1=\varphi$, and $\varphi_t$ be a path in $\mathcal{K}$ from $\varphi_0$ to $\varphi_1$. Then

$$\mathcal{M}(\varphi) = \int_0^1 \int_M \dot \varphi_t (\hat S - S_{\varphi_t}) \omega_{\varphi_t}^n dt.$$

This integral can be shown to be independent of the choice of path $\varphi_t$.

:::

## Constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics {#Constant_scalar_curvature_Kähler_metrics}

From the definition of the Mabuchi functional in terms of the one-form $\alpha$, it can be seen that for a Kähler potential $\varphi\in \mathcal{K}$, the variation

$$\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0} \mathcal{M}(\varphi + t \psi) = \int_M \psi (\hat S - S_\varphi) \omega_\varphi^n$$

vanishes for all tangent vectors $\psi \in C^{\infty}(M)$ if and only if $\hat S = S_\varphi$. That is, the critical points of the Mabuchi functional are precisely the Kähler potentials which have constant scalar curvature.[^o7]
