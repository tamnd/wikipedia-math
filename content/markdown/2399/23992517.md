# Difference algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Difference algebra"}

**Difference algebra** is a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) concerned with the study of [difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_equation) (or [functional](wikipedia://en/364377)) equations from the [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) point of view. Difference algebra is analogous to [differential algebra](wikipedia://en/1816587) but concerned with difference equations rather than [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). As an independent subject it was initiated by [Joseph Ritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Ritt) and his student Richard Cohn.

:::

## Difference rings, difference fields and difference algebras {#Difference_rings,_difference_fields_and_difference_algebras}

A *difference ring* is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$ together with a [ring endomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_endomorphism) $\sigma\colon R\to R$. Often it is assumed that $\sigma$ is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective). When $R$ is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) one speaks of a *difference field*. A classical example of a difference field is the field $K=\mathbb{C}(x)$ of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) with the difference operator $\sigma$ given by $\sigma(f(x))=f(x+1)$. The role of difference rings in difference algebra is similar to the role of commutative rings in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). A morphism of difference rings is a morphism of rings that commutes with $\sigma$. A *difference algebra* over a difference field $K$ is a difference ring $R$ with a [$K$-algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) structure such that $K\to R$ is a morphism of difference rings, i.e. $\sigma\colon R\to R$ extends $\sigma\colon K\to K$. A difference algebra that is a field is called a *difference field extension*.

## Algebraic difference equations {#Algebraic_difference_equations}

The difference polynomial ring $K\{y\}=K\{y_1,\ldots,y_n\}$ over a difference field $K$ in the (difference) variables $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ is the polynomial ring over $K$ in the infinitely many variables $\sigma^i(y_j),\ (i\in\mathbb{N}, 1\leq j\leq n)$. It becomes a difference algebra over $K$ by extending $\sigma$ from $K$ to $K\{y\}$ as suggested by the naming of the variables.

By a *system of algebraic difference* equations over $K$ one means any subset $F$ of $K\{y\}$. If $R$ is a difference algebra over $K$ the solutions of $F$ in $R$ are

$$\mathbb{V}_R(F)=\{a\in R^n|\ f(a)=0 \text{ for all } f\in F\}.$$

Classically one is mainly interested in solutions in difference field extensions of $K$. For example, if $K=\mathbb{C}(x)$ and $R$ is the field of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) on $\mathbb{C}$ with difference operator $\sigma$ given by $\sigma(f(x)) = f(x+1)$, then the fact that the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) $\Gamma$ satisfies the functional equation $\Gamma(x+1) = x\Gamma(x)$ can be restated abstractly as $\Gamma\in\mathbb{V}_R(\sigma(y_1)-xy_1)$.

## Difference varieties {#Difference_varieties}

Intuitively, a *difference variety* over a difference field $K$ is the set of solutions of a system of algebraic difference equations over $K$. This definition has to be made more precise by specifying where one is looking for the solutions. Usually one is looking for solutions in the so-called universal family of difference field extensions of $K$.[^o5][^o6] Alternatively, one may define a difference variety as a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of difference field extensions of $K$ to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), which is of the form $R\rightsquigarrow \mathbb{V}_R(F)$ for some $F\subseteq K\{y\}$.

There is a [one-to-one correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence) between the difference varieties defined by algebraic difference equations in the variables $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ and certain [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) in $K\{y\}$, namely the perfect difference ideals of $K\{y\}$.[^o7] One of the basic theorems in difference algebra asserts that every ascending chain of perfect difference ideals in $K\{y\}$ is finite. This result can be seen as a difference analog of [Hilbert's basis theorem](wikipedia://en/13733).

## Applications {#Applications}

Difference algebra is related to many other mathematical areas, such as discrete [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), or [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). While some real life problems, such as [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466), can be modeled by algebraic difference equations, difference algebra also has applications in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210). For example, there is a proof of the [Manin–Mumford conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manin–Mumford_conjecture) using methods of difference algebra.[^o10] The model theory of difference fields has been studied.
