# Exponential-logarithmic distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exponential-logarithmic distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Exponential-Logarithmic (EL)** distribution is a family of lifetime [distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) with decreasing [failure rate](wikipedia://en/1336960), defined on the interval \[0, ∞). This distribution is [parameterized](wikipedia://en/19653337) by two parameters $p\in(0,1)$ and $\beta >0$.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

The study of lengths of the lives of organisms, devices, materials, etc., is of major importance in the [biological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) sciences. In general, the lifetime of a device is expected to exhibit decreasing failure rate (DFR) when its behavior over time is characterized by 'work-hardening' (in engineering terms) or 'immunity' (in biological terms).

The exponential-logarithmic model, together with its various properties, are studied by Tahmasbi and Rezaei (2008).[^o4] This model is obtained under the concept of population heterogeneity (through the process of compounding).

## Properties of the distribution {#Properties_of_the_distribution}

### Distribution {#Distribution}

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf) of the EL distribution is given by Tahmasbi and Rezaei (2008)[^o4]

$$f(x; p, \beta) := \left( \frac{1}{-\ln p}\right) \frac{\beta(1-p)e^{-\beta x}}{1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x}}$$

where $p\in (0,1)$ and $\beta >0$. This function is strictly decreasing in $x$ and tends to zero as $x\rightarrow \infty$. The EL distribution has its [modal value](wikipedia://en/1432127) of the density at x=0, given by

$$\frac{\beta (1-p)}{-p \ln p}$$

The EL reduces to the [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) with rate parameter $\beta$, as $p\rightarrow 1$.

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is given by

$$F(x;p,\beta)=1-\frac{\ln(1-(1-p) e^{-\beta x})}{\ln p},$$

and hence, the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) is given by

$$x_\text{median}=\frac{\ln(1+\sqrt{p})}{\beta}.$$

### Moments {#Moments}

The [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) of $X$ can be determined from the pdf by direct integration and is given by

$$M_X(t) = E(e^{tX}) = -\frac{\beta(1-p)}{\ln p (\beta-t)} F_{2,1}\left(\left[1,\frac{\beta-t}{\beta}\right],\left[\frac{2\beta-t}{\beta}\right],1-p\right),$$

where $F_{2,1}$ is a [hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/2055223). This function is also known as *Barnes's extended hypergeometric function*. The definition of $F_{N,D}({n,d},z)$ is

$$F_{N,D}(n,d,z):=\sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{ z^k \prod_{i=1}^p\Gamma(n_i+k)\Gamma^{-1}(n_i)}{\Gamma(k+1)\prod_{i=1}^q\Gamma(d_i+k)\Gamma^{-1}(d_i)}$$

where $n=[n_1, n_2,\dots , n_N]$ and ${d}=[d_1, d_2, \dots , d_D]$.

The moments of $X$ can be derived from $M_X(t)$. For $r\in\mathbb{N}$, the raw moments are given by

$$E(X^r;p,\beta)=-r!\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{r+1}(1-p) }{\beta^r\ln p},$$

where $\operatorname{Li}_a(z)$ is the [polylogarithm](wikipedia://en/482471) function which is defined as follows:[^o8]

$$\operatorname{Li}_a(z) =\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{k^a}.$$

Hence the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of the EL distribution are given, respectively, by

$$E(X)=-\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(1-p)}{\beta\ln p},$$

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=-\frac{2 \operatorname{Li}_3(1-p)}{\beta^2\ln p}-\left(\frac{ \operatorname{Li}_2(1-p)}{\beta\ln p}\right)^2.$$

### The survival, hazard and mean residual life functions {#The_survival,_hazard_and_mean_residual_life_functions}

*Image: Hazard EL.png.* Hazard function

The [survival function](wikipedia://en/30876757) (also known as the reliability function) and [hazard function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_function) (also known as the failure rate function) of the EL distribution are given, respectively, by

$$s(x)=\frac{\ln(1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x})}{\ln p},$$

$$h(x)=\frac{-\beta(1-p)e^{-\beta x}}{(1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x})\ln(1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x})}.$$

The mean residual lifetime of the EL distribution is given by

$$m(x_0;p,\beta)=E(X-x_0|X\geq x_0;\beta,p)=-\frac{\operatorname{Li}_2(1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x_0})}{\beta \ln(1-(1-p)e^{-\beta x_0})}$$

where $\operatorname{Li}_2$ is the [dilogarithm](wikipedia://en/2398437) function

### Random number generation {#Random_number_generation}

Let *U* be a [random variate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variate) from the standard [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29). Then the following transformation of *U* has the EL distribution with parameters *p* and *β*:

$$X = \frac{1}{\beta}\ln \left(\frac{1-p}{1-p^U}\right).$$

## Estimation of the parameters {#Estimation_of_the_parameters}

To estimate the parameters, the [EM algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectation-maximization_algorithm) is used. This method is discussed by Tahmasbi and Rezaei (2008).[^o4] The EM iteration is given by

$$\beta^{(h+1)} = n \left( \sum_{i=1}^n\frac{x_i}{1-(1-p^{(h)})e^{-\beta^{(h)}x_i}} \right)^{-1},$$

$$p^{(h+1)}=\frac{-n(1-p^{(h+1)})} { \ln( p^{(h+1)}) \sum_{i=1}^n \{1-(1-p^{(h)})e^{-\beta^{(h)} x_i}\}^{-1}}.$$

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

The EL distribution has been generalized to form the Weibull-logarithmic distribution.[^o13]

If *X* is defined to be the [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) which is the minimum of *N* independent realisations from an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) with rate parameter *β*, and if *N* is a realisation from a [logarithmic distribution](wikipedia://en/1119231) (where the parameter *p* in the usual parameterisation is replaced by (1 − *p*)), then *X* has the exponential-logarithmic distribution in the parameterisation used above.
