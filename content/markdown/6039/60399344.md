# Eduardo Sáenz de Cabezón

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eduardo Sáenz de Cabezón"}

**Eduardo Sáenz de Cabezón Irigaray** (born 24 June 1972, [Logroño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logroño)) is a Spanish mathematician, and professor of [computer languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_language) and systems at the [University of La Rioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_La_Rioja) since 2001. He is a recognized specialist in scientific [monologues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monologue). He develops his research in the area of [computational algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), to which he has contributed 25 research publications and collaborations with Spanish and European mathematicians such as [Henry P. Wynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wynn).[^o3][^o4]

:::

Cabezón is also known for disseminating mathematics through conferences, shows and talks for people of all ages around the world. He was the winner of competition for scientific monologues at [Famelab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famelab) Spain (2013) and at the Aquae Foundation (2014), also becoming a finalist at the [Cheltenham Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham_Science_Festival) (United Kingdom).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Eduardo Sáenz de Cabezón studied during his youth at the Sagasta Institute in [Logroño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logroño). After high school, he decided to study mathematics and computer science, inspired by a teacher passionate about mathematics.[^o7] He was also licensed in theology at the [Pontifical University of Comillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comillas_Pontifical_University), and holds a PhD in mathematics from the [University of La Rioja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_La_Rioja).[^o8]

### Doctoral thesis {#Doctoral_thesis}

Cabezón obtained his PhD with the thesis "Combinatorial Koszul homology: computations and applications" for which he obtained the grade of outstanding [cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors) unanimously of the court.[^o10] His thesis is framed within the area of [computational algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805). In it, the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of [Koszul](wikipedia://en/5147910) for monomial ideals is studied. In the thesis, Cabezón described the structure of this type of ideals based on his Koszul homology, described [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for the calculation of this homology, and implemented algorithms that show to be effective.[^o11]

## Popular science {#Popular_science}

*Image: Eduardo\_Fame\_Lab.jpg.* Eduardo Sáenz de Cabezón at Famelab 2013.

Cabezón is known for his career in [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) and mathematics. He has been telling stories in bars and cafes for 22 years, but has branched out to host and participate in a variety of mathematics communication events:

### Events and conferences {#Events_and_conferences}

- He is a member and founder of a group of active [scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist) and researchers called *Big Van Science* since 2013, dedicated to scientific dissemination with the aim of bringing [scientific communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_communication) to all types of audiences. He is also the author of the mathematical show *"El baúl de Pitágoras"*, which was shown in theaters and bars in several cities in Spain since 2012.
- He has participated with the [TED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_%28conference%29) organization doing conferences and lectures on the mathematical world.[^o14]

### TV {#TV}

- He is presenter of the Spanish late night show of science and humor *[Órbita Laika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Órbita_Laika)*.[^o16]

### Internet {#Internet}

- He currently has the YouTube channel called *Derivando*, in which he teaches and explains curiosities about the mathematical world, with more than 1.3 million subscribers.[^o18]

### Books {#Books}

- He is the author of the book *Inteligencia matemática* which attempts to popularize mathematical concepts by comparing them to examples in everyday life. Eduardo argues that they are an opportunity to enjoy reality because: "*whether we want it or not, we all have a mathematician in our interior, who maybe got scared at school and remains hidden in a corner"*.[^o20]
- He is also co-author of the book *Gardner para aficionados: Juegos de matemática recreativa*.
