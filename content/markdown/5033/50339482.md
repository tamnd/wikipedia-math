# Alan J. Goldman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alan J. Goldman"}

**Alan J. Goldman** (1932–2010) was an American expert in [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Goldman was born in 1932 and grew up in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), where his parents both worked for the public school system. In 1949, he was a winner of the [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search). He studied mathematics and physics at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College), graduating in 1952.[^o2][^o3] He went on to graduate study in mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), completing his doctorate in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) in 1957 under the supervision of [Ralph Fox](wikipedia://en/1343918). Goldman worked at the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards) from 1956 until 1979, when he became a professor of mathematical sciences at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). He retired in 1999.[^o2][^o3]

While at Princeton, Goldman came under the influence of [Albert W. Tucker](wikipedia://en/935841), with whom he published three "seminal papers" in *Annals of Mathematics Studies* on [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) and [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885). His work at the National Bureau of Standards included work on [facility location](wikipedia://en/10469986) for the [US Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Postal_Service) and on [transportation planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_planning); he was also a mentor there to [Jack Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459) and [George Nemhauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Nemhauser).[^o2] After moving to Johns Hopkins, his doctoral students included combinatorialist [Arthur T. Benjamin](wikipedia://en/12428320).[^o5]

In 1976, Goldman won the Gold Medal for Excellence in Service of the [US Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Commerce). He was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 1989.[^o2][^o3]
