# Suzuki groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Suzuki groups"}

In the area of modern algebra known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Suzuki groups**, denoted by Sz(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), <sup>2</sup>*B*<sub>2</sub>(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), Suz(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), or *G*(2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>), form an infinite family of [groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_Lie_type) found by (txt), that are simple for *n* ≥ 1. These simple groups are the only finite non-abelian ones with orders not divisible by 3.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

### Suzuki {#Suzuki}

(Suzuki 1960) originally constructed the Suzuki groups as subgroups of SL<sub>4</sub>(**F**<sub>2<sup>2*n*+1</sup></sub>) generated by certain explicit matrices.

### Ree {#Ree}

Ree observed that the Suzuki groups were the fixed points of exceptional automorphisms of some [symplectic groups](wikipedia://en/173977) of dimension 4, and used this to construct two further families of simple groups, called the [Ree groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ree_groups). In the lowest case the symplectic group *B*<sub>2</sub>(2)≈*S*<sub>6</sub>; its [exceptional automorphism](wikipedia://en/28901#Automorphism_group) fixes the subgroup Sz(2) or <sup>2</sup>*B*<sub>2</sub>(2), of order 20. (txt) gave a detailed exposition of Ree's observation.

### Tits {#Tits}

(txt) constructed the Suzuki groups as the symmetries of a certain ovoid in 3-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) over a field of characteristic 2.

### [Wilson](wikipedia://en/4582220) {#Wilson}

(txt) constructed the Suzuki groups as the subgroup of the symplectic group in 4 dimensions preserving a certain product on pairs of orthogonal vectors.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let *q* = 2<sup>2*n*+1</sup> and *r* = 2<sup>*n*</sup>, where *n* is a non-negative integer.

The Suzuki groups Sz(*q*) or <sup>2</sup>*B*<sub>2</sub>(*q*) are simple for *n*≥1. The group Sz(2) is solvable and is the [Frobenius group](wikipedia://en/1716123) of order 20.

The Suzuki groups Sz(*q*) have orders *q*<sup>2</sup>(*q*<sup>2</sup>+1)(*q*−1). These groups have orders divisible by 5, but not by 3.

The [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) is trivial for *n*>1, [Klein 4-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_4-group) for *n*=1, i. e. Sz(8).

The [outer automorphism group](wikipedia://en/628466) is cyclic of order 2*n*+1, given by automorphisms of the field of order *q*.

Suzuki group are [Zassenhaus groups](wikipedia://en/1724820) acting on sets of size (2<sup>2*n*+1</sup>)<sup>2</sup>+1, and have 4-dimensional representations over the field with 2<sup>2*n*+1</sup> elements.

Suzuki groups are [CN-groups](wikipedia://en/5745210): the centralizer of every non-trivial element is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235).

### Subgroups {#Subgroups}

When *n* is a positive integer, Sz(*q*) has at least 4 types of maximal subgroups.

The [diagonal subgroup](wikipedia://en/1314854) is cyclic, of order *q* – 1.

- The lower triangular (Borel) subgroup and its conjugates, of order *q*<sup>2</sup>·(*q*-1). They are one-point stabilizers in a doubly transitive permutation representation of Sz(*q*).
- The dihedral group *D*<sub>*q*–1</sub>, normalizer of the diagonal subgroup, and conjugates.
- *C*<sub>*q*+2*r*+1</sub>:4
- *C*<sub>*q*–2*r*+1</sub>:4
- Smaller Suzuki groups, when 2*n*+1 is composite.

Either *q*+2*r*+1 or *q*–2*r*+1 is divisible by 5, so that Sz(*q*) contains the Frobenius group *C*<sub>5</sub>:4.

### Conjugacy classes {#Conjugacy_classes}

(txt) showed that the Suzuki group has *q*+3 conjugacy classes. Of these, *q*+1 are strongly real, and the other two are classes of elements of order 4.

- *q*<sup>2</sup>+1 Sylow 2-subgroups of order *q*<sup>2</sup>, of index *q*–1 in their normalizers. 1 class of elements of order 2, 2 classes of elements of order 4.
- *q*<sup>2</sup>(*q*<sup>2</sup>+1)/2 cyclic subgroups of order *q*–1, of index 2 in their normalizers. These account for (*q*–2)/2 conjugacy classes of non-trivial elements.
- Cyclic subgroups of order *q*+2*r*+1, of index 4 in their normalizers. These account for (*q*+2*r*)/4 conjugacy classes of non-trivial elements.
- Cyclic subgroups of order *q*–2*r*+1, of index 4 in their normalizers. These account for (*q*–2*r*)/4 conjugacy classes of non-trivial elements.

The normalizers of all these subgroups are Frobenius groups.

### Characters {#Characters}

(Suzuki 1960) showed that the Suzuki group has *q*+3 irreducible representations over the complex numbers, 2 of which are complex and the rest of which are real. They are given as follows:

- The trivial character of degree 1.
- The [Steinberg representation](wikipedia://en/5862365) of degree *q*<sup>2</sup>, coming from the doubly transitive permutation representation.
- (*q*–2)/2 characters of degree *q*<sup>2</sup>+1
- Two complex characters of degree *r*(*q*–1) where *r*=2<sup>*n*</sup>
- (*q*+2*r*)/4 characters of degree (*q*–2*r*+1)(*q*–1)
- (*q*–2*r*)/4 characters of degree (*q*+2*r*+1)(*q*–1).
