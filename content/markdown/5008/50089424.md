# Ideographic approach

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ideographic approach"}

The **ideographic approach** refers to the idea in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) proposed by [Marc Brysbaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brysbaert) that the conclusions of a study stay limited to the phenomenon under study.[^o2] It also focuses on the importance of individual traits when determining [behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior) instead of group norms.[^o3] It reinforces the position that the study of the individual is the most effective method of understanding behavior.[^o4]

:::

## Background {#Background}

A major tradition in behavioral psychology is concerned with the variables and how one's characteristics are abstracted from other people's.[^o6] The ideographic approach emerged from the position among pioneers in personality psychology that focuses on the study of individuality.[^o6]

## Methodologies {#Methodologies}

The ideographic approach often use qualitative methodologies in its study of small number of individuals rather than make generalizations out of information collected from a large quantitative database.[^o8] The methods operate according to the idea that each person is like no other person.[^o9]

The psychological literature distinguishes ideographic approach from the [nomothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomothetic) laws, which are concerned with the general perspective.[^o10]
