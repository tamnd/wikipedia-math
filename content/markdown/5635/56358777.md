# Hardware-based encryption

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hardware-based encryption"}

**Hardware-based encryption** is the use of [computer hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hardware) to assist software, or sometimes replace software, in the process of data [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294). Typically, this is implemented as part of the [processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU)'s [instruction set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instruction_set_architecture). For example, the [AES](wikipedia://en/1260) encryption algorithm (a modern [cipher](wikipedia://en/5244)) can be implemented using the [AES instruction set](wikipedia://en/23095336) on the ubiquitous [x86 architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86_architecture).[^o16] Such instructions also exist on the [ARM architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture).[^o17] However, more unusual systems exist where the cryptography module is separate from the central processor, instead being implemented as a [coprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprocessor), in particular a [secure cryptoprocessor](wikipedia://en/59865) or [cryptographic accelerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_accelerator), of which an example is the [IBM 4758](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4758), or its successor, the [IBM 4764](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4764).[^o18] Hardware implementations can be faster and less prone to exploitation than traditional software implementations, and furthermore can be protected against tampering.[^o19]

:::

## History {#History}

Prior to the use of computer hardware, cryptography could be performed through various mechanical or [electro-mechanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-mechanical) means. An early example is the [Scytale](wikipedia://en/148487) used by the [Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartan).[^o4] The [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256) was an electro-mechanical system cipher machine notably used by the Germans in [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), purely electronic systems were developed. In 1987 the ABYSS (A Basic Yorktown Security System) project was initiated.[^o20][^o21] The aim of this project was to protect against [software piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_piracy). However, the application of computers to cryptography in general dates back to the 1940s and [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), where the [Colossus computer](wikipedia://en/6229) was used to break the encryption used by German High Command during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). The use of computers to *encrypt*, however, came later. In particular, until the development of the [integrated circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit), of which the first was produced in 1960, computers were impractical for encryption, since, in comparison to the portable [form factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_factor_%28design%29) of the [Enigma machine](wikipedia://en/9256),[^o22] [computers of the era](wikipedia://en/740918) took the space of an entire building. It was only with the development of the [microcomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcomputer) that computer encryption became feasible, outside of niche applications. The development of the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web) lead to the need for consumers to have access to encryption, as [online shopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_shopping) became prevalent.[^o23] The key concerns for consumers were security and speed.[^o23] This led to the eventual inclusion of the key algorithms into processors as a way of both increasing speed and security.[^o19]

## Implementations {#Implementations}

### In the instruction set {#In_the_instruction_set}

#### x86 {#x86}

The [X86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X86) [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_architecture), as a [CISC (Complex Instruction Set Computer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_instruction_set_computer) Architecture, typically implements complex [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms) in hardware.[^o24] Cryptographic algorithms are no exception. The x86 architecture implements significant components of the [AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)](wikipedia://en/1260) algorithm,[^o16] which can be used by the [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) for [Top Secret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Secret) information.[^o25] The architecture also includes support for the [SHA](wikipedia://en/897855) Hashing Algorithms through the [Intel SHA extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_SHA_extensions).[^o16] Whereas AES is a cipher, which is useful for encrypting documents, [hashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hash_function) is used for verification, such as of passwords (see [PBKDF2](wikipedia://en/1568244)).

#### ARM {#ARM}

[ARM processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_processor) can optionally support Security Extensions. Although ARM is a [RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RISC) architecture, there are several optional extensions specified by [ARM Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_Holdings).[^o17][^o27]

### As a coprocessor {#As_a_coprocessor}

- [IBM 4758](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4758) – The predecessor to the [IBM 4764](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4764).[^o28] This includes its own specialised processor, [memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random-access_memory) and a [Random Number Generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_Number_Generator).[^o26]
- [IBM 4764](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4764) and [IBM 4765](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4765), identical except for the connection used.[^o28] The former uses [PCI-X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCI-X), while the latter uses [PCI-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCI-e).[^o18] Both are [peripheral devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_devices) that plug into the [motherboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motherboard).

### Proliferation {#Proliferation}

[Advanced Micro Devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Micro_Devices) (AMD) processors are also x86 devices, and have supported the [AES instructions](wikipedia://en/23095336) since the 2011 [Bulldozer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulldozer_%28microarchitecture%29) processor iteration.[^o29] Due to the existence of encryption instructions on modern processors provided by both [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) and AMD, the instructions are present on most modern computers.[^o30] They also exist on many tablets and smartphones due to their implementation in [ARM processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture).[^o30]

:::

## Advantages {#Advantages}

Implementing cryptography in hardware means that part of the processor is dedicated to the task. This can lead to a large increase in speed.[^o19] In particular, modern processor architectures that support [pipelining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipelining_%28computing%29) can often perform other instructions concurrently with the execution of the encryption instruction. Furthermore, hardware can have methods of protecting data from software. Consequently, even if the [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) is compromised, the data may still be secure (see [Software Guard Extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Guard_Extensions)).[^o31]

## Disadvantages {#Disadvantages}

If, however, the hardware implementation is compromised, major issues arise. Malicious software can retrieve the data from the (supposedly) secure hardware – a large class of methods used is the [timing attack](wikipedia://en/481813).[^o32] This is far more problematic to solve than a software bug, even within the [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system). [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft) regularly deals with security issues through [Windows Update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Update). Similarly, regular security updates are released for [Mac OS X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS_X) and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux), as well as mobile operating systems like [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS), [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29), and [Windows Phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Phone). However, hardware is a different issue. Sometimes, the issue will be fixable through updates to the processor's [microcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcode) (a low level type of software). However, other issues may only be resolvable through replacing the hardware, or a workaround in the operating system which mitigates the performance benefit of the hardware implementation, such as in the [Spectre exploit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectre_%28security_vulnerability%29).[^o33]
