# Tutte graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tutte graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Tutte graph** is a 3-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with 46 vertices and 69 edges named after [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021). It has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 3, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, girth 4 and diameter 8.

:::

The Tutte graph is a [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270), but is non-[hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph). Therefore, it is a counterexample to [Tait's conjecture](wikipedia://en/52435) that every 3-regular polyhedron has a Hamiltonian cycle.[^o4]

Published by Tutte in 1946, it is the first counterexample constructed for this conjecture.[^o5] Other counterexamples were found later, in many cases based on [Grinberg's theorem](wikipedia://en/22816079).

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Tutte fragment.svg.* The Tutte fragment.

From a small planar graph called the Tutte fragment, [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) constructed a non-Hamiltonian polyhedron, by putting together three such fragments. The "compulsory" edges of the fragments, that must be part of any Hamiltonian path through the fragment, are connected at the central vertex; because any cycle can use only two of these three edges, there can be no Hamiltonian cycle.

The resulting graph is [3-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_connectivity) and [planar](wikipedia://en/24314), so by [Steinitz' theorem](wikipedia://en/24656270) it is the graph of a polyhedron. It has 25 faces.

It can be realized geometrically from a tetrahedron (the faces of which correspond to the four large nine-sided faces in the drawing, three of which are between pairs of fragments and the fourth of which forms the exterior) by multiply truncating three of its vertices.

:::

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The automorphism group of the Tutte graph is **Z**/3**Z**, the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 3.

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Tutte graph is :

$$(x-3)(x^{15}-22x^{13}+x^{12}+184x^{11}-26x^{10}-731x^9+199x^8+1383x^7-576x^6-1061x^5+561x^4+233x^3-151x^2+4x+4)^2$$

$$(x^{15}+3x^{14}-16x^{13}-50x^{12}+94x^{11}+310x^{10}-257x^9-893x^8+366x^7+1218x^6-347x^5-717x^4+236x^3+128x^2-56x+4).$$

## Related graphs {#Related_graphs}

Although the Tutte graph is the first 3-regular non-Hamiltonian polyhedral graph to be discovered, it is not the smallest such graph.

In 1965 Lederberg found the [Barnette–Bosák–Lederberg graph](wikipedia://en/61147574) on 38 vertices.[^o11] In 1968, Grinberg constructed additional small counterexamples to the Tait's conjecture – the Grinberg graphs on 42, 44 and 46 vertices.[^o13] In 1974 Faulkner and Younger published two more graphs – the Faulkner–Younger graphs on 42 and 44 vertices.[^o14]

Finally Holton and McKay showed there are exactly six 38-vertex non-Hamiltonian polyhedra that have nontrivial three-edge cuts. They are formed by replacing two of the vertices of a [pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859659) by the same fragment used in Tutte's example.[^o15]
