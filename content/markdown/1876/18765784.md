# Wealth and religion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wealth and religion"}

*Image: Religion economy.png.* [The average annual income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) of countries correlates negatively [with national levels of religiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Importance_of_religion_by_country).[^o2]

The correlation between **wealth and religion** has been subject to academic research. [Wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) is the status of being the beneficiary or proprietor of a large accumulation of [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_%28economics%29) and [economic power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_power). [Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) is a [socio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_system)-[cultural system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_system) that often involves belief in [supernatural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernatural) forces and may intend to provide a moral system or a meaning to life.

:::

## Statistics {#Statistics}

### Global {#Global}

According to a study from 2015, [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) hold the largest amount of wealth (55% of the total world wealth), followed by [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims) (5.8%), [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) (3.3%), and [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) (1.1%). According to the same study it was found that adherents under the classification "[Irreligion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion)", or other religions, hold about 34.8% of the total global wealth.[^o5][^o6]

### United States {#United_States}

A study in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) (based on data from 1985 to 1998), conducted by the sociologist Lisa A. Keister and published in the *[Social Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Forces)* journal, found that adherents of [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) and [Episcopalianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_%28United_States%29)[^n1] accumulated the most wealth, believers in [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) and mainline [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants) were in the middle, while conservative Protestants accumulated the least; in general, people who attend religious services accumulated more wealth than those who do not (taking into account variations of education and other factors).[^o9] Keister suggested that [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family) processes shape wealth accumulation.[^n2] According to the study, the median [net worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_worth) of people believing in [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) is calculated at US\$150,890, while the median net worth of conservative [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants) (including [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists), [Seventh-day Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist_Church), [Christian Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Scientists)) was US\$26,200. The overall median in the dataset was US\$48,200.

Some of the [wealthiest and most affluent American families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_money) such as the [Vanderbilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_family), [Astors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astor_family), [Rockefellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_family),[^o11] [Du Ponts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Pont_family), [Roosevelts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_family), [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_family), [Fords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_family),[^o11] [Mellons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_family),[^o11] [Whitneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_family), [Morgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_family), and Harrimans are white primarily [mainline Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) families.[^o12]

Another study in the United States, from 2012, stated that 48% of [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) had a [household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income) of \$100,000 or more, and 70% make at least \$75,000, which is the highest among all religions in United States.[^o13]

*Image: US household wealth by education.png.* Pew Research states that wealth differences between religions "largely mirror the differences in educational attainment."[^o14]

[Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) reported in 2015 that Hindus, Jews, [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheists), and [agnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnostics) were relatively wealthy compared to other religious demographics. The report stated that these differences "across religious traditions largely mirror the differences in educational attainment."[^o14]

According to a 2014 study by the Pew Research Center, Jewish again ranked as the most financially successful religious group in the United States, with 44% of Jews living in households with incomes of at least \$100,000, followed by [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) (36%), [Episcopalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_%28United_States%29) (35%), and [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_%28USA%29) (32%).[^o15] In a 2016 report about their study, Pew Research stated "members of some religious groups – not to mention atheists and agnostics – on average have a higher household income than others and those in the richest religious groups also tend, on average, to be better educated than most Americans."[^o15] Amongst Jews, in 2016, [Modern Orthodox Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Orthodox_Jews) had a median household income of \$158,000, while [Open Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Orthodoxy) Jews had a median household income of \$185,000 (compared to the American median household income of \$59,000 in 2016).[^o16] According to the same study, there is a correlation between education and income; about 77% of American Hindus have an [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) degree, followed by Jews (59%), Episcopalians (56%), and Presbyterians (47%).[^o17]

## Explanations {#Explanations}

A study published in the *[American Journal of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Sociology)* by Lisa Keister found that "wealth affects religion indirectly through educational attainment, fertility, and female labor force participation", but also found some evidence of direct effects of religion on wealth attainment.[^n3] Keister notes that certain religious beliefs ("one should have many children", "women should not work") lower wealth accumulation, both on the micro- and macro-scales.[^n3][^o21]
