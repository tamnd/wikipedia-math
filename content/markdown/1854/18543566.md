# John Griffiths (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Griffiths"}

**John Griffiths** (1837 – May 1916) was a [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales) mathematician and academic associated with [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford), for nearly 60 years.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Griffiths was born in [Llangyndeyrn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llangyndeyrn) near [Kidwelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidwelly) in [Carmarthenshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthenshire), Wales, and educated at [Cowbridge Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowbridge_Grammar_School). He [matriculated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matriculation) at Jesus College in 1857 with a scholarship, and obtained a first-class degree in mathematics, winning the Junior and Senior Mathematical Scholarships. In 1863, he was elected as [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbridge_Fellow) and Tutor in Mathematics at Jesus College. Griffiths held his fellowship until his death in May 1916, by which time he had become the longest-serving fellow. He also served as the college's bursar for a time.[^o3]

Griffiths was particularly interested in [analytical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_geometry), publishing numerous papers in mathematical journals and two tracts on theorems connected with the geometry of the triangle. He was described in his obituary in [The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times) as "a man of a very sociable and affectionate nature \[but\] excessively shy". He died in May 1916 in his native village in Carmarthenshire.[^o3]

[Griffiths' theorem](wikipedia://en/75330134) describing a property of [pedal circles](wikipedia://en/75329877) in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is named after him.
