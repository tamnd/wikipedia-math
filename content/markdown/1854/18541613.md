# Hans-Egon Richert

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hans-Egon Richert"}

**Hans-Egon Richert** (June 2, 1924 – November 25, 1993) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked primarily in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). He is the author (with [Heini Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam)) of a definitive book[^o3] on [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Hans-Egon Richert was born in 1924 in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). He attended the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg) and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) under [Max Deuring](wikipedia://en/5638258) in 1950. He held a temporary chair at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and then a newly created chair at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg). In 1972 he moved to the [University of Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulm), where he remained until his retirement in 1991. He died on November 25, 1993, in [Blaustein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaustein), near [Ulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).[^o5]

## Work {#Work}

Richert worked primarily in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), and beginning around 1965 started a collaboration with [Heini Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam) and shifted his focus to [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915). For many years he was a chairman of the Analytic Number Theory meetings at the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach).[^o5]

### Analytic number theory {#Analytic_number_theory}

Richert made contributions to [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333), [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258), [Riesz summability](wikipedia://en/5811855), the multiplicative analog of the [Erdős–Fuchs theorem](wikipedia://en/18543655), estimates of the number of non-isomorphic [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), and bounds for [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937). He proved the exponent 15/46 for the [Dirichlet divisor problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_divisor_problem), a record that stood for many years.[^o5]

### Sieve methods {#Sieve_methods}

One of Richert's notable results was the [Jurkat–Richert theorem](wikipedia://en/21987832), joint work with Wolfgang B. Jurkat that improved the [Selberg sieve](wikipedia://en/18739942) and is used in the proof of [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840).[^o9] Richert also produced a "readable form"[^o5] of Chen's theorem (it is covered in the last chapter of *Sieve Methods*[^o3]).

[Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam) & Richert's book *Sieve Methods*[^o3] was the first exhaustive account of the subject. [^o10] In reviewing the book in 1976, [Hugh Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Montgomery_%28mathematician%29) wrote "In the past, researchers have generally derived the sieve bounds required for an application, but now workers will find that usually an appeal to an appropriate theorem of *Sieve methods* will suffice," and "For years to come, *Sieve methods* will be vital to those seeking to work in the subject, and also to those seeking to make applications."[^o10]
