# Table of congruences

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Table of congruences"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a [congruence](wikipedia://en/48167) is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). The following sections list important or interesting prime-related congruences.

:::

## Other prime-related congruences {#Other_prime-related_congruences}

There are other prime-related congruences that provide necessary and sufficient conditions on the primality of certain subsequences of the natural numbers. Many of these alternate statements characterizing primality are related to [Wilson's theorem](wikipedia://en/227323), or are restatements of this classical result given in terms of other special variants of [generalized factorial functions](wikipedia://en/507208#Generalizations). For instance, new variants of [Wilson's theorem](wikipedia://en/227323) stated in terms of the [hyperfactorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperfactorials), [subfactorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfactorials), and [superfactorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superfactorials) are given in.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Variants of Wilson's theorem"}

### Variants of Wilson's theorem {#Variants_of_Wilson's_theorem}

For integers $k \geq 1$, we have the following form of Wilson's theorem:

$$(k-1)! (p-k)! \equiv (-1)^k \pmod{p} \iff p \text{ prime. }$$

If $p$ is odd, we have that

$$\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)!^2 \equiv (-1)^{(p+1)/2} \pmod{p} \iff p \text{ an odd prime. }$$

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Clement's theorem concerning the twin primes"}

### Clement's theorem concerning the twin primes {#Clement's_theorem_concerning_the_twin_primes}

Clement's congruence-based theorem characterizes the [twin primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_primes) pairs of the form $(p, p+2)$ through the following conditions:

$$4[(p-1)!+1] \equiv -p \pmod{p(p+2)} \iff p,p+2 \text{ are both prime. }$$

P. A. Clement's original 1949 paper [^o10] provides a proof of this interesting elementary number theoretic criteria for twin primality based on Wilson's theorem. Another characterization given in Lin and Zhipeng's article provides that

$$2 \left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)!^2 + (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}} (5p+2) \equiv 0 \iff p,p+2 \text{ are both prime. }$$

:::

### Characterizations of prime tuples and clusters {#Characterizations_of_prime_tuples_and_clusters}

The prime pairs of the form $(p, p+2k)$ for some $k \geq 1$ include the special cases of the [cousin primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin_primes) (when $k=2$) and the [sexy primes](wikipedia://en/343116) (when $k=3$). We have elementary congruence-based characterizations of the primality of such pairs, proved for instance in the article.[^o12] Examples of congruences characterizing these prime pairs include

$$2k (2k)![(p-1)!+1] \equiv [1-(2k)!]p \pmod{p(p+2k)} \iff p,p+2k \text{ are both prime, }$$

and the alternate characterization when $p$ is odd such that $p \not{\mid} (2k-1)!!^2$ given by

$$2k(2k-1)!!^2 \left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)!^2 + (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left[(2k-1)!!^2(p+2k)-(-4)^k \cdot p\right] \equiv 0 \iff p,p+2k \text{ are both prime. }$$

Still other congruence-based characterizations of the primality of triples, and more general [prime clusters](wikipedia://en/11421646) (or [prime tuples](wikipedia://en/11421646)) exist and are typically proved starting from Wilson's theorem.[^o13]).
