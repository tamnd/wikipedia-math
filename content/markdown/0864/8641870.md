# Weinstein–Aronszajn identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weinstein–Aronszajn identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Weinstein–Aronszajn identity** states that if $A$ and $B$ are [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of size $m \times n$ and $n \times m$ respectively (either or both of which may be infinite) then, provided $AB$ (and hence, also $BA$) is of [trace class](wikipedia://en/192266),

:::

$$\det(I_m + AB) = \det(I_n + BA),$$

where $I_k$ is the $k \times k$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718).

It is closely related to the [matrix determinant lemma](wikipedia://en/11269780) and its generalization. It is the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) analogue of the [Woodbury matrix identity](wikipedia://en/430493) for [matrix inverses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inverse).

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The identity may be proved as follows.[^o4] Let $M$ be a matrix consisting of the four [blocks](wikipedia://en/457464) $I_m$, $A$, $B$ and $I_n$:

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} I_m & A \\ B & I_n \end{pmatrix}.$$

Because $I_{m}$ is [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122), the formula for the [determinant of a block matrix](wikipedia://en/8468#Block_matrices) gives

$$\det\!\begin{pmatrix} I_m & A \\ B & I_n \end{pmatrix} = \det(I_m) \det(I_n - B I_m^{-1} A) = \det(I_n - BA).$$

Because $I_{n}$ is invertible, the formula for the determinant of a block matrix gives

$$\det\!\begin{pmatrix} I_m & A\\ B & I_n \end{pmatrix} = \det(I_n) \det(I_m - A I_n^{-1} B) = \det(I_m - AB).$$

Thus

$$\det(I_n - B A) = \det(I_m - A B).$$

Substituting $-A$ for $A$ then gives the Weinstein–Aronszajn identity.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Let $\lambda \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. The identity can be used to show the somewhat more general statement that

$$\det(AB - \lambda I_m) = (-\lambda)^{m - n} \det(BA - \lambda I_n).$$

It follows that the non-zero [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of $AB$ and $BA$ are the same.

This identity is useful in developing a [Bayes estimator](wikipedia://en/6978815) for [multivariate Gaussian distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Gaussian_distribution).

The identity also finds applications in [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory) by relating determinants of large matrices to determinants of smaller ones.[^o7]

:::
