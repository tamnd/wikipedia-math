# Mathematical chemistry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematical chemistry"}

**Mathematical chemistry**[^o2] is the area of research engaged in novel applications of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) to [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry); it concerns itself principally with the [mathematical modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_modeling) of chemical phenomena.[^o3] Mathematical chemistry has also sometimes been called **computer chemistry**, but should not be confused with [computational chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_chemistry).

:::

Major areas of research in mathematical chemistry include [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708), which deals with [topology](wikipedia://en/30275938) such as the mathematical study of [isomerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomerism) and the development of [topological descriptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_descriptor) or indices which find application in [quantitative structure-property relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_structure-property_relationship); and chemical aspects of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), which finds applications in [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry) and [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry). Another important area is molecular knot theory and [circuit topology](wikipedia://en/43717327) that describe the topology of folded linear molecules such as proteins and nucleic acids.

The history of the approach may be traced back to the 19th century. [Georg Helm](wikipedia://en/21342143) published a treatise titled "The Principles of Mathematical Chemistry: The Energetics of Chemical Phenomena" in 1894.[^o4] Some of the more contemporary periodical publications specializing in the field are MATCH Communications in Mathematical and in Computer Chemistry, first published in 1975, and the Journal of Mathematical Chemistry, first published in 1987. In 1986 a series of annual conferences MATH/CHEM/COMP taking place in [Dubrovnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubrovnik) was initiated by the late [Ante Graovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ante_Graovac).

The basic [models](wikipedia://en/20590) for mathematical chemistry are [molecular graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_graph) and [topological index](wikipedia://en/14221614).

In 2005 the [International Academy of Mathematical Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_Mathematical_Chemistry) (IAMC) was founded in Dubrovnik (Croatia) by [Milan Randić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Randić). The Academy has 82 members (2009) from all over the world, including six scientists awarded with a Nobel Prize.
