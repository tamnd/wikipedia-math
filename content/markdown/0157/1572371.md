# Allan J. C. Cunningham

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Allan J. C. Cunningham"}

**Allan Joseph Champneys Cunningham** (1842–1928) was a British-Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi), Cunningham was the son of [Sir Alexander Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Alexander_Cunningham), archaeologist and the founder of the [Archaeological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India).[^o5] He started a military career with the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company)'s [Bengal Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Engineer_Group) at a young age. From 1871 to 1881, he was instructor in mathematics at Thomason College of Civil Engineering which later became the [Indian Institute Of Technology Roorkee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_Of_Technology_Roorkee) (IIT Roorkee). Upon returning to the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) in 1881, he continued teaching at military institutes in [Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham,_Kent), [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin) and [Shorncliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorncliffe_Army_Camp). He left the army in 1891. He spent the rest of his life studying [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He applied his expertise to finding [factors](wikipedia://en/49492) of large numbers of the form *a<sup>n</sup>* ± *b<sup>n</sup>*, such as [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_numbers) ($2^p-1$) and [Fermat numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_numbers) ($2^{2^n}+1$) which have *b* = 1. His work is continued in the [Cunningham project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunningham_project).
