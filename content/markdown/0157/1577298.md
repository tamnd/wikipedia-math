# Decile

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decile"}

In [descriptive statistics](wikipedia://en/8187), a **decile** is any of the nine values that divide the sorted data into ten equal parts, so that each part represents 1/10 of the sample or population.[^o2] A decile is one possible form of a [quantile](wikipedia://en/25184); others include the [quartile](wikipedia://en/25170) and [percentile](wikipedia://en/354907).[^o3] A decile rank arranges the data in order from lowest to highest and is done on a scale of one to ten where each successive number corresponds to an increase of 10 percentage points.

:::

## Decile mean {#Decile_mean}

A moderately [robust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robust_%28statistics%29) measure of [central tendency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency), known as the **decile mean**, can be computed by making use of a sample's deciles $D_{1}$ to $D_{9}$ ($D_{1}$ = 10th percentile, $D_{2}$ = 20th percentile and so on). It is calculated as follows:[^o5]

$$DM = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^9 D_{i}} {9}$$

Apart from serving as an alternative for the [mean](wikipedia://en/10005756) and the [truncated mean](wikipedia://en/430588), it also forms the basis for robust measures of [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) and [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848), and even a [normality test](wikipedia://en/8617378).[^o6]
