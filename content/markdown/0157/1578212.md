# Hille–Yosida theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hille–Yosida theorem"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) in mathematics, the **Hille–Yosida theorem** characterizes the generators of [strongly continuous one-parameter semigroups](wikipedia://en/1644938) of [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) on [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). It is sometimes stated for the special case of [contraction semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup), with the general case being called the **Feller–Miyadera–Phillips theorem** (after [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644), Isao Miyadera, and [Ralph Phillips](wikipedia://en/27858707)). The contraction semigroup case is widely used in the theory of [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process). In other scenarios, the closely related [Lumer–Phillips theorem](wikipedia://en/12281541) is often more useful in determining whether a given operator generates a [strongly continuous contraction semigroup](wikipedia://en/1644938). The Hille–Yosida theorem is named after [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [Einar Hille](wikipedia://en/1643924) and [Kōsaku Yosida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kōsaku_Yosida), who independently proved it around 1948.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definitions"}

## Formal definitions {#Formal_definitions}

If *X* is a Banach space, a [one-parameter semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-parameter_semigroup) of operators on *X* is a family of operators indexed on the non-negative real numbers {*T*(*t*)}<sub>*t* ∈ \[0, ∞)</sub> such that

- $$T(0)= I \quad$$
- $$T(s+t)= T(s) \circ T(t), \quad \forall t,s \geq 0.$$

The semigroup is said to be **strongly continuous**, also called a (*C*<sub>0</sub>) semigroup, if and only if the mapping

$$t \mapsto T(t) x$$

is continuous for all *x* ∈ *X*, where \[0, ∞) has the usual topology and *X* has the norm topology.

The infinitesimal generator of a one-parameter semigroup *T* is an operator *A* defined on a possibly proper subspace of *X* as follows:

- The domain of *A* is the set of *x* ∈ *X* such that

$$h^{-1}\bigg(T(h) x - x\bigg)$$

has a limit as *h* approaches 0 from the right.

- The value of *Ax* is the value of the above limit. In other words, *Ax* is the right-derivative at 0 of the function

$$t \mapsto T(t)x.$$

The infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous one-parameter semigroup is a [closed linear operator](wikipedia://en/64713671) defined on a [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of *X*.

The Hille–Yosida theorem provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a [closed linear operator](wikipedia://en/64713671) *A* on a Banach space to be the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous one-parameter semigroup.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *A* be a linear operator defined on a linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the Banach space *X*, *ω* a real number, and *M* > 0. Then *A* generates a [strongly continuous semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C0_semigroup) *T* that satisfies $\|T(t)\|\leq M{\rm e}^{\omega t}$ if and only if[^o6]

1. *A* is [closed](wikipedia://en/1422584#Closed_linear_operators) and *D*(*A*) is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) in *X*,
1. every real *λ* > *ω* belongs to the [resolvent set](wikipedia://en/8721698) of *A* and for such λ and for all positive [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers) *n*,

$$\|(\lambda I-A)^{-n}\|\leq\frac{M}{(\lambda-\omega)^n}.$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Hille-Yosida theorem for contraction semigroups"}

## Hille-Yosida theorem for contraction semigroups {#Hille-Yosida_theorem_for_contraction_semigroups}

In the general case the Hille–Yosida theorem is mainly of theoretical importance since the estimates on the powers of the [resolvent operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolvent_operator) that appear in the statement of the theorem can usually not be checked in concrete examples. In the special case of [contraction semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) (*M* = 1 and *ω* = 0 in the above theorem) only the case *n* = 1 has to be checked and the theorem also becomes of some practical importance. The explicit statement of the Hille–Yosida theorem for contraction semigroups is:

Let *A* be a linear operator defined on a linear subspace *D*(*A*) of the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X*. Then *A* generates a [contraction semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_semigroup) if and only if[^o9]

1. *A* is [closed](wikipedia://en/1422584#Closed_linear_operators) and *D*(*A*) is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29) in *X*,
1. every real *λ* > 0 belongs to the resolvent set of *A* and for such *λ*,

$$\|(\lambda I-A)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.$$

:::
