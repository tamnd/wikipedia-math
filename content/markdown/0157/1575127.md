# Mimesis (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mimesis"}

In mathematics, **mimesis** is the quality of a numerical method which imitates some properties of the continuum problem. The goal of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) is to approximate the continuum, so instead of solving a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) one aims to solve a discrete version of the continuum problem. Properties of the continuum problem commonly imitated by numerical methods are [conservation laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29), [solution symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_in_physics), and fundamental identities and theorems of vector and tensor calculus like the [divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864).[^o2] Both [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147) or [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581) can be mimetic; it depends on the properties that the method has.

:::

For example, a mixed finite element method applied to [Darcy flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy's_law) strictly [conserves the mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_mass) of the flowing fluid.

The term *[geometric integration](wikipedia://en/2208188)* denotes the same philosophy.
