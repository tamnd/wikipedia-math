# Cunningham Project

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cunningham Project"}

The **Cunningham Project** is a collaborative effort started in 1925 to [factor](wikipedia://en/15491) numbers of the form *b*<sup>*n*</sup> ± 1 for *b* = 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12 and large *n*. The project is named after [Allan Joseph Champneys Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allan_Joseph_Champneys_Cunningham), who published the first version of the table together with [Herbert J. Woodall](wikipedia://en/1561620).[^o2] There are three printed versions of the table, the most recent published in 2002,[^o3] as well as an online version by [Samuel Wagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_S._Wagstaff_Jr.).[^o4]

:::

The current limits of the exponents are:

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| Base | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Limit | 1500 | 900 | 600 | 550 | 500 | 450 | 400 | 400 |
| [Aurifeuillean](wikipedia://en/23683488) (LM) limit | 3000 | 1800 | 1200 | 1100 | 1000 | 900 | 800 | 800 |

:::

## Factors of Cunningham number {#Factors_of_Cunningham_number}

Two types of factors can be derived from a Cunningham number without having to use a [factorization algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorization_algorithm): [algebraic factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_factor) of [binomial numbers](wikipedia://en/4861681) (e.g. [difference of two squares](wikipedia://en/1102297) and [sum of two cubes](wikipedia://en/72077175)), which depend on the exponent, and [aurifeuillean factors](wikipedia://en/23683488), which depend on both the base and the exponent.

### Algebraic factors {#Algebraic_factors}

From elementary algebra,

$$(b^{kn}-1) = (b^n-1) \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} b^{rn}$$

for all *k*, and

$$(b^{kn}+1) = (b^n+1) \sum_{r=0}^{k-1} (-1)^r \cdot b^{rn}$$

for [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) *k*. In addition, $b^{2n} - 1 = (b^{n} - 1)(b^{n} + 1)$. Thus, when *m* [divides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divides) *n*, $b^{m} - 1$ and $b^{m} + 1$ are factors of $b^{n} - 1$ if the quotient of *n* over *m* is [even](wikipedia://en/143135); only the first number is a factor if the quotient is odd. $b^{m} + 1$ is a factor of $b^{n} - 1$, if *m* divides *n* and the quotient is odd.

In fact,

$$b^n-1 = \prod_{d \mid n} \Phi_d(b)$$

and

$$b^n+1 = \prod_{d \mid 2n,\, d \nmid n} \Phi_d(b)$$

See [this page](https://stdkmd.net/nrr/repunit/repunitnote.htm#repunit_factorization) for more information.

When the number is of a particular form (the exact expression varies with the base), aurifeuillean factorization may be used, which gives a product of two or three numbers. The following equations give aurifeuillean factors for the Cunningham project bases as a product of *F*, *L* and *M*:[^o10]

Let *b* = *s*<sup>2</sup>×*k* with [squarefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree) *k*, if one of the conditions holds, then $\Phi_n(b)$ have aurifeuillean factorization.

(i) $k\equiv 1 \pmod 4$ and $n\equiv k \pmod{2k};$

(ii) $k\equiv 2, 3 \pmod 4$ and $n\equiv 2k \pmod{4k}.$

### Other factors {#Other_factors}

Once the algebraic and aurifeuillean factors are removed, the other factors of $b^{n} \pm 1$ are always of the form $2kn + 1$, since the factors of $b^{n} - 1$ are all factors of $\Phi_n(b)$, and the factors of $b^{n} + 1$ are all factors of $\Phi_{2n}(b)$. When *n* is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), both algebraic and aurifeuillean factors are not possible, except the trivial factors ($b - 1$ for $b^{n} - 1$ and $b + 1$ for $b^{n} + 1$). For [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_numbers), the trivial factors are not possible for prime *n*, so all factors are of the form $2kn + 1$. In general, all factors of $(b^{n} - 1)\,/(b - 1)$ are of the form $2kn + 1,$ where $b \ge 2$ and *n* is prime, except when *n* divides $b - 1$, in which case $(b^{n} - 1)\,/(b - 1)$ is divisible by *n* itself.

Cunningham numbers of the form $b^{n} - 1$ can only be prime if *b* = 2 and *n* is prime, assuming that *n* ≥ 2; these are the Mersenne numbers. Numbers of the form $b^{n} + 1$ can only be prime if *b* is even and *n* is a [power of 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_2), again assuming $n \ge 2;$ these are the generalized Fermat numbers, which are [Fermat numbers](wikipedia://en/91127) when *b* = 2. Any factor of a Fermat number $2^{2^{n}} + 1$ is of the form $k\cdot2^{n+2} + 1$.

## Notation {#Notation}

*b*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1 is denoted as *b*,*n*−. Similarly, *b*<sup>*n*</sup> + 1 is denoted as *b*,*n*+. When dealing with numbers of the form required for aurifeuillean factorization, *b*,*n*L and *b*,*n*M are used to denote L and M in [the products above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunningham_project#Aurifeuillean_factors).[^o15] References to *b*,*n*− and *b*,*n*+ are to the number with all algebraic and aurifeuillean factors removed. For example, Mersenne numbers are of the form 2,*n*− and Fermat numbers are of the form 2,2<sup>*n*</sup>+; the number [Aurifeuille](wikipedia://en/44178951) factored in 1871 was the product of 2,58L and 2,58M.
