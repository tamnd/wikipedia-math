# Series expansion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Series expansion"}

*Image: Taylor cos.gif.* An animation showing the [cosine function](wikipedia://en/30367) being approximated by successive truncations of its [Maclaurin series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclaurin_series).

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **series expansion** is a technique that expresses a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) as an infinite sum, or [series](wikipedia://en/15287), of simpler functions. It is a method for calculating a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that cannot be expressed by just elementary operators (addition, subtraction, multiplication and division).[^o2]

:::

The resulting so-called *[series](wikipedia://en/15287)* often can be limited to a finite number of terms, thus yielding an [approximation](wikipedia://en/336271) of the function. The fewer terms of the sequence are used, the simpler this approximation will be. Often, the resulting inaccuracy (i.e., the [partial sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sum) of the omitted terms) can be described by an equation involving [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578) (see also [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090)). The series expansion on an [open interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_interval) will also be an approximation for non-[analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions).[^o3]

## Types of series expansions {#Types_of_series_expansions}

There are several kinds of series expansions, listed below.

### Taylor series {#Taylor_series}

A *[Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448)* is a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) based on a function's [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) at a single point.[^o6] More specifically, if a function $f: U\to\mathbb{R}$ is infinitely differentiable around a point $x_0$, then the Taylor series of *f* around this point is given by

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{f^{(n)}(x_0)}{n!}(x - x_0)^n$$

under the convention $0^0 := 1$.[^o6][^o7] The *[Maclaurin series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclaurin_series)* of a function *f* is a special case of its Taylor series about $x_0 = 0$.[^o8][^o7]

### Laurent series {#Laurent_series}

A *[Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213)* is a generalization of the Taylor series, allowing terms with negative exponents; it takes the form $\sum_{k = -\infty}^{\infty} c_k (z - a)^k$ and converges in an [annulus](wikipedia://en/356158).[^o10] In particular, a Laurent series can be used to examine the behavior of a complex function near a singularity by considering the series expansion on an annulus centered at the singularity.

### Dirichlet series {#Dirichlet_series}

*Image: ZetaSpiral.gif.* Convergence and divergence of partial sums of the Dirichlet series defining the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). Here, the yellow line represents the first fifty successive partial sums $\sum_{n = 1}^k n^{-s},$ the magenta dotted line represents $\tfrac{n^{-s+1}}{-s+1} + \zeta(s),$ and the green dot represents $\zeta(s)$ as *s* is varied from -0.5 to 1.5.

A *general [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258)* is a series of the form $\sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} a_ne^{-\lambda_n s}.$ One important special case of this is the *ordinary Dirichlet series* $\sum_{n = 1}^{\infty}\frac{a_n}{n^s}.$[^o12] Used in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

### Fourier series {#Fourier_series}

A *[Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038)* is an expansion of periodic functions as a sum of many [sine](wikipedia://en/30367#Sine,_cosine,_and_tangent) and [cosine](wikipedia://en/30367#Sine,_cosine,_and_tangent) functions.[^o14] More specifically, the Fourier series of a function $f(x)$ of period $2L$ is given by the expression

$$a_0 + \sum_{n = 1}^{\infty} \left[a_n\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) + b_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\right]$$

where the coefficients are given by the formulae[^o14][^o15]

$$\begin{aligned} a_n &:= \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^L f(x)\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx, \\[5pt] b_n &:= \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^L f(x)\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx. \end{aligned}$$

### Other series {#Other_series}

- In [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics), e.g., the [fundamental tone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_frequency) and the [overtones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtone) together form an example of a Fourier series.
- [Newtonian series](wikipedia://en/3868748)
- [Legendre polynomials](wikipedia://en/100349): Used in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) to describe an arbitrary electrical field as a [superposition](wikipedia://en/1201321) of a [dipole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipole) field, a [quadrupole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrupole) field, an [octupole](wikipedia://en/1425534) field, etc.
- [Zernike polynomials](wikipedia://en/2025291): Used in [optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optics) to calculate [aberrations](wikipedia://en/2704) of optical systems. Each term in the series describes a particular type of aberration.

*Image: Stirling series relative error.svg.* The relative error in a truncated Stirling series vs. $n$, for 0 to 5 terms. The kinks in the curves represent points where the truncated series coincides with $\Gamma(n + 1).$

- The *[Stirling series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_series)* $$\operatorname{Ln}\Gamma(z)\sim\left(z-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\ln z-z+\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)+\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{B_{2k}}{2k(2k-1)z^{2k-1}}$$ is an approximation of the [log-gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316#The_log-gamma_function).[^o17]

## Examples {#Examples}

The following is the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) of the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) $e^x$:

$$e^x=\sum^\infty_{n=0}\frac{x^n}{n!}= 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2} + \frac{x^3}{6}+\cdots$$

[^o20][^o21]

The Dirichlet series of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) is

$$\zeta(s) := \sum_{n = 1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^s} = \frac{1}{1^s} + \frac{1}{2^s} + \cdots$$

[^o12]

:::
