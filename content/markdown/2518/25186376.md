# Generalized symmetric group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized symmetric group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **generalized symmetric group** is the [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) $S(m,n) := C_m \wr S_n$ of the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) *m* and the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) of order *n*.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- For $m=1,$ the generalized symmetric group is exactly the ordinary symmetric group: $S(1,n) = S_n.$
- For $m=2,$ one can consider the cyclic group of order 2 as positives and negatives ($C_2 \cong \{\pm 1\}$) and identify the generalized symmetric group $S(2,n)$ with the [signed symmetric group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_symmetric_group).

:::

## Representation theory {#Representation_theory}

There is a natural [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of elements of $S(m,n)$ as [generalized permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_permutation_matrices), where the nonzero entries are *m*-th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity): $C_m \cong \mu_m.$

The representation theory has been studied since (Osima 1954); see references in (Can 1996). As with the symmetric group, the representations can be constructed in terms of [Specht modules](wikipedia://en/5122533); see (Can 1996).

## Homology {#Homology}

The first [group homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology) group – concretely, the [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) – is $C_m \times C_2$ (for *m* [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) this is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to $C_{2m}$): the $C_m$ factors (which are all [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176), hence must map identically in an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974), since conjugation is trivial in an abelian group) can be mapped to $C_m$ (concretely, by taking the product of all the $C_m$ values), while the sign map on the symmetric group yields the $C_2.$ These are independent, and generate the group, hence are the abelianization.

The second homology group – in classical terms, the [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) – is given by (Davies Morris 1974):

$$H_2(S(2k+1,n)) = \begin{cases} 1 & n < 4\\ \mathbf{Z}/2 & n \geq 4.\end{cases}$$

$$H_2(S(2k+2,n)) = \begin{cases} 1 & n = 0, 1\\ \mathbf{Z}/2 & n = 2\\ (\mathbf{Z}/2)^2 & n = 3\\ (\mathbf{Z}/2)^3 & n \geq 4. \end{cases}$$

Note that it depends on *n* and the [parity](wikipedia://en/143135) of *m:* $H_2(S(2k+1,n)) \approx H_2(S(1,n))$ and $H_2(S(2k+2,n)) \approx H_2(S(2,n)),$ which are the Schur multipliers of the symmetric group and signed symmetric group.
