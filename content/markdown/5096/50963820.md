# Rebecca Goldin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rebecca Goldin"}

**Rebecca Freja Goldin** is an American mathematician who works as a professor of mathematical sciences at [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University)[^o3] and director of the [Statistical Assessment Service](wikipedia://en/10707391), a nonprofit organization associated with GMU that aims to improve the use of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in journalism.[^o4] Her mathematical research concerns [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), including work on [Hamiltonian actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_action) and [symplectic quotients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_map#Symplectic_quotients).[^o10]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After graduating with honors in mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o6] Goldin studied in France for a year with [Bernard Teissier](wikipedia://en/28779508) at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure),[^o7] pursuing research on [toric varieties](wikipedia://en/737087).[^o10] She completed her Ph.D. in 1999 at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) under the supervision of [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636).

After postdoctoral research at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland), she joined the GMU faculty in 2001.[^o6][^o7]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She was the inaugural winner of the [Ruth I. Michler Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/62170910) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM), in 2007.[^o10][^o7] She was also the 2008 [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer), speaking on "The Use and Abuse of Statistics in the Media".[^o11]

She was included in the 2019 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to differential geometry and service to the mathematical community, particularly in support of promoting mathematical and statistical thinking to a wide audience".[^o12]
