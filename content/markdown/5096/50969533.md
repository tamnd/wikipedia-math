# David Gibb (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Gibb"}

**David Gibb** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (31 October 1883 – 28 March 1946) was a Scottish mathematician and astronomer. He was the first person to use the term *[numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089)*.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Gibb, eldest son of Robert Gibb, a salt manufacturer, and his wife Joanna, was born in [Methil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methil) near [Leven, Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leven,_Fife) on 31 October 1883. He attended Leven Public School and then [George Watsons College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watsons_College) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) (1896–99). He studied mathematics and sciences at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), graduating with a MA/BSc in 1906 . While being a student, he lodged with Mr Flockhart at 3 West Preston Street, Edinburgh. In 1909 he began lecturing in mathematics at the university.[^o4]

In 1910 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) for his contributions to mathematics and astronomy. His proposers were [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803), Sir [Frank Watson Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Watson_Dyson), [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott) and [Ellice Horsburgh](wikipedia://en/52249022).[^o5]

During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) he worked on the Ballistic Department Ordnance Committee at the [Royal Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Arsenal) in [Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich), remotely calculating complex gun angles to fire on hidden or obscured targets, such as at the Gallipoli peninsula.[^o6]

He returned to the University of Edinburgh after the war. From 1920 he had been President of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940).[^o7]

In 1934 he was promoted to Reader in Mathematics and remained in this role until his death in 1946. He died in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 28 March 1946.[^o8]
