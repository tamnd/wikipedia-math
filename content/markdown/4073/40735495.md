# Serial subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Serial subgroup"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of a given [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* is a **serial subgroup** of *G* if there is a chain *C* of subgroups of *G* extending from *H* to *G* such that for consecutive subgroups *X* and *Y* in *C*, *X* is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *Y*.[^o2] The relation is written *H ser G* or *H is serial in G*.[^o3]

:::

If the chain is finite between *H* and *G*, then *H* is a [subnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/2774415) of *G*. Then every subnormal subgroup of *G* is serial. If the chain *C* is well-ordered and ascending, then *H* is an [ascendant subgroup](wikipedia://en/4966647) of *G*; if descending, then *H* is a [descendant subgroup](wikipedia://en/19175980) of *G*. If *G* is a [locally finite group](wikipedia://en/4939270), then the set of all serial subgroups of *G* form a [complete sublattice](wikipedia://en/44777) in the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of all normal subgroups of *G*.[^o3]
