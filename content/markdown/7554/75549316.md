# Alexander Mihailovich Zamorzaev

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Mihailovich Zamorzaev"}

**Alexander Mihailovich Zamorzaev** (Александр Михайлович Заморзаев; 27 January 1927 – 1 November 1997) was a Soviet mathematician and crystallographer. In 1953 Zamorzaev was the first to derive the complete list of [magnetic space groups](wikipedia://en/60491326) ([Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429) groups).[^o3][^o4][^o5] In 1957 Zamorzaev founded the field of generalised [antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718) by introducing the concept of more than one kind of two-valued antisymmetry operation ([multiple antisymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_antisymmetry)).[^o6]

:::

## Life {#Life}

### Career {#Career}

Zamorzaev was born on 23 January 1927 in Leningrad. In 1953 at the University of Leningrad, under the supervision of [A.D. Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Aleksandrov_%28mathematician%29), he gained the M.A. degree with the dissertation *Generalization of Fedorov groups*, in which he developed the general theory of [antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718). In this work he derived for the first time the 1651 antisymmetry space groups, and named them "Shubnikov groups", after [A.V. Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429) the pioneer of antisymmetry.[^o9]

In 1953 he became a mathematics lecturer at the newly opened [University of Kishinev (Chișinău)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_State_University). Besides teaching the regular mathematics curriculum, and supervising graduate students, Zamorzaev devised and taught new courses in the areas of [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), theoretical [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), and [antisymmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718) and its generalisations.

In 1971 he gained his doctoral degree with a thesis entitled *Theory of Antisymmetry and its Different Generalizations*. The thesis was based on his new theories of geometry and mathematical crystallography, 1) multiple antisymmetry;[^o10] 2) similarity and conformal symmetry;[^o11] and 3) P-symmetry,[^o12] including generalisations of A. V. Shubnikov's antisymmetry and [N. V. Belov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Belov_%28geochemist%29)'s [color symmetry](wikipedia://en/73619197).[^o13]

In 1973 a department of higher geometry was established within the university and Zamorzaev was appointed as professor and head of the department.[^o14] A history of the personnel and achievements of Zamorzaev's school of geometry is available online.[^o15]

### Works {#Works}

The majority of Zamorzaev's works were published in Russian. Books published by Zamorzaev:

- Theory of simple and multiple antisymmetry (1976)[^o10]
- Theory of discrete symmetry groups (1977)[^o17]
- Color symmetry, its generalizations and applications (1978)[^o18]
- P-symmetry and its further development (1986)[^o19]

Zamorzaev published 110 papers. Selected papers available in English:

- Similarity symmetric and antisymmetric groups (1964).[^o11]
- Quasisymmetry (p-symmetry) groups (1968)[^o12]
- Color-symmetry space groups (1969)[^o20]
- Antisymmetry, its generalizations and geometrical applications (1980)[^o21]
- Generalized antisymmetry (1988).[^o22]

### Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

- [E. S. Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._S._Fedorov) Prize of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) for his contributions to the theory of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) (1973)
- State Prize of the [Moldovan SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in Science and Technology for his contributions in the field of [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468) (1974)
- Honored Worker of Science of the Moldovan SSR for his achievements in science and education (1977)
- Elected Corresponding Member of the [Academy of Sciences of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Moldova) (1989)
