# Van Houtum distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van Houtum distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Van Houtum distribution** is a [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) named after prof. Geert-Jan van Houtum.[^o2] It can be characterized by saying that all values of a finite set of possible values are equally probable, except for the smallest and largest element of this set. Since the Van Houtum distribution is a generalization of the [discrete uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699214), i.e. it is uniform except possibly at its boundaries, it is sometimes also referred to as **quasi-uniform**.

:::

It is regularly the case that the only available information concerning some discrete random variable are its first two moments. The Van Houtum distribution can be used to fit a distribution with finite support on these moments.

A simple example of the Van Houtum distribution arises when throwing a [loaded dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loaded_dice) which has been tampered with to land on a 6 twice as often as on a 1. The possible values of the sample space are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Each time the die is thrown, the probability of throwing a 2, 3, 4 or 5 is 1/6; the probability of a 1 is 1/9 and the probability of throwing a 6 is 2/9.

## Probability mass function {#Probability_mass_function}

A [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) *U* has a Van Houtum (*a*, *b*, *p*<sub>*a*</sub>, *p*<sub>*b*</sub>) distribution if its [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) is

$$\Pr(U=u) = \begin{cases} p_a & \text{if } u=a; \\[8pt] p_b & \text{if } u=b \\[8pt] \dfrac{1-p_a-p_b}{b-a-1} & \text{if } a<u<b \\[8pt] 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

## Fitting procedure {#Fitting_procedure}

Suppose a random variable $X$ has mean $\mu$ and squared [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) $c^2$. Let $U$ be a Van Houtum distributed random variable. Then the first two moments of $U$ match the first two moments of $X$ if $a$, $b$, $p_a$ and $p_b$ are chosen such that:[^o5]

$$\begin{aligned} a &= \left\lceil \mu - \frac{1}{2} \left\lceil \sqrt{1+12c^2\mu^2} \right\rceil \right\rceil \\[8pt] b &= \left\lfloor \mu + \frac{1}{2} \left\lceil \sqrt{1+12c^2\mu^2} \right\rceil \right\rfloor \\[8pt] p_b &= \frac{(c^2+1)\mu^2-A-(a^2-A)(2\mu-a-b)/(a-b)}{a^2+b^2-2A} \\[8pt] p_a &= \frac{2\mu-a-b}{a-b}+p_b \\[12pt] \text{where } A & = \frac{2a^2+a+2ab-b+2b^2}{6}. \end{aligned}$$

There does not exist a Van Houtum distribution for every combination of $\mu$ and $c^2$. By using the fact that for any real mean $\mu$ the discrete distribution on the integers that has minimal variance is concentrated on the integers $\lfloor \mu \rfloor$ and $\lceil \mu \rceil$, it is easy to verify that a Van Houtum distribution (or indeed any discrete distribution on the integers) can only be fitted on the first two moments if [^o6]

$$c^2\mu^2 \geq (\mu-\lfloor \mu \rfloor)(1+\mu-\lceil \mu \rceil)^2+(\mu-\lfloor \mu \rfloor)^2(1+\mu-\lceil \mu \rceil).$$
