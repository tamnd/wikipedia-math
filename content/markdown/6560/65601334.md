# Overcategory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Overcategory"}

In mathematics, an **overcategory** (also called a **slice category**) is a construction from [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) used in multiple contexts, such as with [covering spaces (espace étalé)](wikipedia://en/245466). They were introduced as a mechanism for keeping track of data surrounding a fixed object $X$ in some [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathcal{C}$. The [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) notion is that of an **undercategory** (also called a **coslice category**).

:::

Both can be expressed in terms of the more general construction of a [comma category](wikipedia://en/840950).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category and $X$ a fixed object of $\mathcal{C}$[^o4]<sup>pg 59</sup>. The **overcategory** (also called a **slice category**) $\mathcal{C}/X$ is an associated category whose objects are pairs $(A, \pi)$ where $\pi:A \to X$ is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) in $\mathcal{C}$. Then, a morphism between objects $f:(A, \pi) \to (A', \pi')$ is given by a morphism $f:A \to A'$ in the category $\mathcal{C}$ such that the following diagram [commutes](wikipedia://en/138484)

> $$\begin{matrix} A & \xrightarrow{f} & A' \\ \pi\downarrow \text{ } & \text{ } &\text{ } \downarrow \pi' \\ X & = & X \end{matrix}$$

There is a dual notion called the **undercategory** (also called a **coslice category**) $X/\mathcal{C}$ whose objects are pairs $(B, \psi)$ where $\psi:X\to B$ is a morphism in $\mathcal{C}$. Then, morphisms in $X/\mathcal{C}$ are given by morphisms $g: B \to B'$ in $\mathcal{C}$ such that the following diagram commutes

> $$\begin{matrix} X & = & X \\ \psi\downarrow \text{ } & \text{ } &\text{ } \downarrow \psi' \\ B & \xrightarrow{g} & B' \end{matrix}$$

These two notions have generalizations in [2-category theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_category)[^o5] and [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013)[^o6]<sup>pg 43</sup>, with definitions either analogous or essentially the same.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Many categorical properties of $\mathcal{C}$ are inherited by the associated over and undercategories for an object $X$. For example, if $\mathcal{C}$ has finite [products](wikipedia://en/237868) and [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889), it is immediate that the category $\mathcal{C}/X$ has finite coproducts and the category $X/\mathcal{C}$ has finite products, since the product and coproduct can be constructed in $\mathcal{C}$, and through [universal properties](wikipedia://en/32245), there exists a unique morphism either to $X$ or from $X$. In addition, this applies to [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimits) as well.

By construction, $(X,\operatorname{id})$ is a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) of $\mathcal{C}/X$ and an initial object of $X/\mathcal{C}$.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Overcategories on a site {#Overcategories_on_a_site}

Recall that a [site](wikipedia://en/12910) $\mathcal{C}$ is a categorical generalization of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) first introduced by [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). One of the canonical examples comes directly from topology, where the category $\text{Open}(X)$ whose objects are open subsets $U$ of some topological space $X$, and the morphisms are given by inclusion maps. Then, for a fixed open subset $U$, the overcategory $\text{Open}(X)/U$ is canonically equivalent to the category $\text{Open}(U)$ for the induced topology on $U \subseteq X$. This is because every object in $\text{Open}(X)/U$ is an open subset $V$ contained in $U$.

### Category of algebras as an undercategory {#Category_of_algebras_as_an_undercategory}

The category of commutative $A$-[algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) is equivalent to the undercategory $A/\text{CRing}$ for the category of commutative rings. This is because the structure of an $A$-algebra on a commutative ring $B$ is directly encoded by a [ring morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_morphism) $A \to B$. If we consider the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399), it is an overcategory of [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme), $\text{Aff}/\text{Spec}(A)$, or just $\text{Aff}_A$.

### Overcategories of spaces {#Overcategories_of_spaces}

Another common overcategory considered in the literature are overcategories of spaces, such as [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold), or topological spaces. These categories encode objects relative to a fixed object, such as the category of schemes over $S$, $\text{Sch}/S$. [Fiber products](wikipedia://en/56444110) in these categories can be considered intersections (e.g. the [scheme-theoretic intersection](wikipedia://en/52580781)), given the objects are [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) of the fixed object.
