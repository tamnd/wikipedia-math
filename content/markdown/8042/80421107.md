# Hideki Omori

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hideki Omori"}

**Hideki Omori** (大森英樹, born December 3, 1938[^o3]) is a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specialized in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and worked at [Tokyo University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Science).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Hideki Omori was born in [Nishinomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishinomiya), [Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyōgo_Prefecture), on December 3, 1938. He completed both his undergraduate and graduate studies at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo). In 1966, he was awarded his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) degree for his [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) on the study of [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group) on [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). After completing his doctoral studies, Omori began his professional academic career at [Tokyo Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_University), where he held his first research position. In 1967, early in his career, he was invited to the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). This was followed by a position at the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) in 1970, and later at [Bonn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_University) in 1972. Together with Akira Yoshioka, he wrote an introductory [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) textbook: 直観世界からの微・積分入門.[^o3]

During his time at Warwick, he developed a strong interest in the work of [K. David Elworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._David_Elworthy) on [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis). Omori is credited with being the first person to introduce Elworthy's work on stochastic analysis to the Japanese mathematical community. In 1980, Omori moved to [Okayama University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okayama_University), where he continued his research and teaching. Two years later, in 1982, he joined the faculty of [Tokyo University of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_University_of_Science) as a professor. In 1996, he was awarded the [Geometry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_Prize) of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786). Omori continued his active research well beyond typical retirement age, focusing particularly on problems of [deformation quantization](wikipedia://en/4278245) beginning in 1999. His retirement from Tokyo University of Science was celebrated in April 2004 with an conference held at the Morito Hall of Tokyo University of Science.[^o3]
