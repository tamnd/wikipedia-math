# Random closed set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random closed set"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [stochastic geometry](wikipedia://en/22037813), a **random closed set** is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) whose values are [closed subsets](wikipedia://en/47279) of a given [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), typically [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$. Random closed sets generalize the concept of random variables and random processes by allowing entire [sets](wikipedia://en/26691), rather than individual points or [vectors](wikipedia://en/23218956), to be treated as random elements. They are widely used in areas such as [spatial statistics](wikipedia://en/9467349), [image analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_analysis), [materials science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science), and [mathematical morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_morphology).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A random closed set in $\mathbb{R}^d$ is a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) from a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(\Omega,\mathcal{A}, P)$ into $(\mathcal{F}, \Sigma)$. Here $\mathcal{F}$ is the collection of all closed subsets of $\mathbb{R}^d$ and $\Sigma$ is the [sigma-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) generated over $\mathcal{F}$ by the sets

$$\mathcal{F}_K=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:F\cap K=\emptyset\}$$

for all [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subsets $K\subset\mathbb{R}^d$.

:::

## History {#History}

Mentions of random sets have appeared for almost a century beginning with [A.N. Kolmogorov's](wikipedia://en/91161) book, *Foundations of the Theory of Probability*, which provided the axiomatic foundation for probability theory. In this book, Kolmogorov defined what is now referred to as a random set.[^o5] Up until the 1960s, mentions of random sets could be found scattered throughout publications before [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193) formalized the concept of a random set. French mathematician [Georges Matheron](wikipedia://en/4415402) is recognized as the first person to concentrate on random sets with *closed* values and formulate a definition.[^o6]
