# Tisserand's parameter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tisserand's parameter"}

**Tisserand's parameter** (or **Tisserand's invariant**) is a number calculated from several [orbital elements](wikipedia://en/98663) ([semi-major axis](wikipedia://en/23409699), [orbital eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity), and [inclination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_inclination)) of a relatively small object and a larger "[perturbing body](wikipedia://en/1490148)". It is used to distinguish different kinds of orbits. The term is named after French astronomer [Félix Tisserand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Félix_Tisserand) who derived it[^o2] and applies to [restricted three-body problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restricted_three-body_problem) in which the three objects all differ greatly in mass.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a small body with semi-major axis $a\,\!$, orbital eccentricity $e\,\!$, and orbital inclination $i\,\!$, relative to the orbit of a perturbing larger body with [semimajor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semimajor_axes) axis $a_P$, the parameter is defined as follows:[^o5][^o6]

$$T_P\ = \frac{a_P}{a} + 2\cos i\sqrt{\frac{a}{a_P} (1-e^2)}$$

:::

## Tisserand invariant conservation {#Tisserand_invariant_conservation}

In the three-body problem, the quasi-conservation of Tisserand's invariant is derived as the limit of the [Jacobi integral](wikipedia://en/1713939) away from the main two bodies (usually the star and planet).[^o5] Numerical simulations show that the Tisserand invariant of orbit-crossing bodies is conserved in the three-body problem on Gigayear timescales.[^o8][^o9]

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Tisserand parameter's conservation was originally used by Tisserand to determine whether or not an observed orbiting body is the same as one previously observed. This is usually known as the [Tisserand's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisserand's_criterion).

### Orbit classification {#Orbit_classification}

The value of the Tisserand parameter with respect to the planet that most perturbs a small body in the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System) can be used to delineate groups of objects that may have similar origins.

- T<sub>J</sub>, Tisserand's parameter with respect to [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter) as perturbing body, is frequently used to distinguish [asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) (typically $T_J > 3$) from [Jupiter-family comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet#Short_period) (typically $2< T_J < 3$).[^o13]
- The minor planet group of [damocloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damocloid) are defined by a Jupiter Tisserand's parameter of 2 or less ($T_{J} \le 2$).[^o19]
- T<sub>N</sub>, Tisserand's parameter with respect to [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune), has been suggested to distinguish near-[scattered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattered_disc) (affected by Neptune) from extended-scattered [trans-Neptunian objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Neptunian_object) (not affected by Neptune; e.g. [90377 Sedna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90377_Sedna)).
- T<sub>N</sub>, Tisserand's parameter with respect to [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune) may also be used to distinguish Neptune-crossing trans-Neptunian objects that may be injected onto retrograde and polar Centaur orbits ($-1 \le T_{N} \le 2$) and those that may be injected onto prograde Centaur orbits ($2 \le T_{N} \le 2.82$).[^o8][^o9]

### Other uses {#Other_uses}

- The quasi-conservation of Tisserand's parameter constrains the orbits attainable using [gravity assist](wikipedia://en/155758) for [outer Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System#Outer_Solar_System) exploration.
- Tisserand's parameter could be used to infer the presence of an [intermediate-mass black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate-mass_black_hole) at the center of the [Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way) using the motions of orbiting stars.[^o15]

:::

## Related notions {#Related_notions}

The parameter is derived from one of the so-called [Delaunay](wikipedia://en/523533) standard variables, used to study the perturbed [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/198319#Overview) in a [three-body system](wikipedia://en/1208420). Ignoring higher-order perturbation terms, the following value is [conserved](wikipedia://en/2458485):

$$\sqrt{a (1-e^2)} \cos i$$

Consequently, perturbations may lead to the [resonance](wikipedia://en/41660) between the orbital inclination and eccentricity, known as [Kozai resonance](wikipedia://en/3275457). Near-circular, highly inclined orbits can thus become very eccentric in exchange for lower inclination. For example, such a mechanism can produce [sungrazing comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungrazing_comet), because a large eccentricity with a constant semimajor axis results in a small [perihelion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perihelion_and_aphelion).
