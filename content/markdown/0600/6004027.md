# Walther Mayer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walther Mayer"}

Walter Mayer (disambiguation)|Walter Mayer

*Image: Walther Mayer (cropped).jpg.* Mayer in 1931

**Walther Mayer** (11 March 1887 – 10 September 1948) was an Austrian mathematician, born in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary).[^o3] With [Leopold Vietoris](wikipedia://en/105043) he is the namesake of the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](wikipedia://en/378677) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).[^o2] He served as an assistant to [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) and subsequently worked with him several years as a close collaborator,[^o3] leading to the nickname "Einstein's calculator".[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mayer studied at the [Federal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) before receiving his doctorate in 1912 from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna);[^o3] his thesis concerned the [Fredholm integral equation](wikipedia://en/363377).[^o7][^o8] He served in the military between 1914 and 1919, during which he found time to complete a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o7] Because he was Jewish, he had little opportunity for an academic career in Austria, and left the country; however, in 1926, with help from Einstein, he returned to a position at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) as Privatdozent (lecturer).[^o9] He made a name for himself in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) with the [Mayer–Vietoris sequence](wikipedia://en/378677),[^o2] and with an axiomatic treatment of [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) predating the [Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms](wikipedia://en/3122600).[^o10] He also published a book on [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) in 1930, the second volume of a textbook on differential geometry that had been started by [Adalbert Duschek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalbert_Duschek) with a volume on curves and surfaces.[^o7]

In 1929, on the recommendation of [Richard von Mises](wikipedia://en/821517), he became Albert Einstein's assistant with the explicit understanding that he work with him on [distant parallelism](wikipedia://en/903686), and from 1931 to 1936, he collaborated with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) on the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001).[^o3] In 1933, after [Hitler's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) assumption of power, he followed Einstein to the United States and became an associate in mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o3] He continued working on mathematics at the Institute, and died in Princeton in 1948.[^o3]
