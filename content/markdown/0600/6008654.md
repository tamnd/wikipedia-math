# Honeycomb (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Honeycomb"}

*Image: cubic honeycomb.png.* [Cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **honeycomb** is a *space filling* or *[close packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_packing)* of [polyhedral](wikipedia://en/23470) or higher-dimensional *cells*, so that there are no gaps. It is an example of the more general mathematical *tiling* or *[tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671)* in any number of dimensions. Its dimension can be clarified as *n*-honeycomb for a honeycomb of *n*-dimensional space.

:::

Honeycombs are usually constructed in ordinary [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417) ("flat") space. They may also be constructed in [non-Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/58610), such as hyperbolic honeycombs. Any finite [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) can be projected to its [circumsphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumsphere) to form a uniform honeycomb in spherical space.

*Image: Wallpaper group-cmm-1.jpg.* It is possible to fill the plane with [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621) which do not meet at their corners, for example using [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048), as in a [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick) wall pattern: this is not a proper tiling because corners lie part way along the edge of a neighbouring polygon. Similarly, in a proper honeycomb, there must be no edges or vertices lying part way along the face of a neighbouring cell. Interpreting each brick face as a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) having two interior angles of 180 degrees allows the pattern to be considered as a proper tiling. However, not all geometers accept such hexagons.

## Classification {#Classification}

There are infinitely many honeycombs, which have only been partially classified. The more regular ones have attracted the most interest, while a rich and varied assortment of others continue to be discovered.

The simplest honeycombs to build are formed from stacked layers or *slabs* of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) based on some [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane. In particular, for every [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975), copies can fill space, with the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) being special because it is the only *regular* honeycomb in ordinary (Euclidean) space. Another family is the [Hill tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/20700599) and their generalizations, which can also tile the space.

### Uniform 3-honeycombs {#Uniform_3-honeycombs}

A 3-dimensional **uniform honeycomb** is a honeycomb in [3-space](wikipedia://en/3054853) composed of [uniform polyhedral](wikipedia://en/2537223) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29), and having all vertices the same (i.e., the group of \[isometries of 3-space that preserve the tiling\] is *[transitive on vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)*). There are 28 [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) examples in Euclidean 3-space,[^o4] also called the **[Archimedean honeycombs](wikipedia://en/2961)**.

A honeycomb is called **regular** if the group of isometries preserving the tiling acts transitively on flags, where a **flag** is a vertex lying on an edge lying on a face lying on a cell. Every regular honeycomb is automatically uniform. However, there is just one regular honeycomb in Euclidean 3-space, the [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318). Two are *quasiregular* (made from two types of regular cells):

The [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260) and [gyrated tetrahedral-octahedral honeycombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_tetrahedral-octahedral_honeycomb) are generated by 3 or 2 positions of slab layer of cells, each alternating tetrahedra and octahedra. An infinite number of unique honeycombs can be created by higher order of patterns of repeating these slab layers.

### Space-filling polyhedra {#Space-filling_polyhedra}

A honeycomb having all cells identical within its symmetries is said to be **[cell-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-transitive)** or **isochoric**. In the 3-dimensional euclidean space, a cell of such a honeycomb is said to be a *[space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583)*. A [necessary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) for a polyhedron to be a space-filling polyhedron is that its [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051) must be zero,[^o9][^o10] ruling out any of the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) other than the cube.

Five space-filling convex polyhedra can tessellate 3-dimensional euclidean space using translations only. They are called [parallelohedra](wikipedia://en/25207334):

1. [Cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) (or variations: [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802), rhombic [hexahedron](wikipedia://en/237209) or [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975))
1. [Hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb)[^o11]
1. [Rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb)
1. [Elongated dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongated_dodecahedral_honeycomb)[^o12]
1. [Bitruncated cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3736299) or [truncated octahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_octahedra)[^o13]

Other known examples of space-filling polyhedra include:

- The [triangular prismatic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/8802963)
- The [gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb)
- The [triakis truncated tetrahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/37070120). The Voronoi cells of the carbon atoms in diamond are this shape.[^o15]
- The [trapezo-rhombic dodecahedral honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezo-rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb)[^o16]

### Other honeycombs with two or more polyhedra {#Other_honeycombs_with_two_or_more_polyhedra}

Sometimes, two[^o19] or more different polyhedra, not necessarily having different nets[^o20], may be combined to fill space. Besides many of the uniform honeycombs, another well known example is the [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211), adopted from the structure of clathrate hydrate crystals.[^o21]

### Non-convex 3-honeycombs {#Non-convex_3-honeycombs}

Documented examples are rare. Two classes can be distinguished:

- Non-convex cells which pack without overlapping, analogous to tilings of concave polygons. These include [a packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellated_rhombic_dodecahedral_honeycomb) of the small [stellated rhombic dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellated_rhombic_dodecahedron), as in the [Yoshimoto Cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshimoto_Cube).
- Overlapping of cells whose positive and negative densities 'cancel out' to form a uniformly dense continuum, analogous to overlapping tilings of the plane.

::: {#o24 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Regular hyperbolic honeycomb"}

### Hyperbolic honeycombs {#Hyperbolic_honeycombs}

In 3-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) of a polyhedron depends on its size. The regular hyperbolic honeycombs thus include two with four or five [dodecahedra](wikipedia://en/8407) meeting at each edge; their dihedral angles thus are π/2 and 2π/5, both of which are less than that of a Euclidean dodecahedron. Apart from this effect, the hyperbolic honeycombs obey the same topological constraints as Euclidean honeycombs and polychora.

The 4 compact and 11 paracompact regular hyperbolic honeycombs and many [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_uniform_honeycombs_in_hyperbolic_space) and [paracompact](wikipedia://en/39591384) uniform hyperbolic honeycombs have been enumerated.

:::

## Duality of 3-honeycombs {#Duality_of_3-honeycombs}

For every honeycomb there is a dual honeycomb, which may be obtained by exchanging:

cells for vertices.

faces for edges.

These are just the rules for dualising four-dimensional [4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/24979), except that the usual finite method of reciprocation about a concentric hypersphere can run into problems.

The more regular honeycombs dualise neatly:

- The cubic honeycomb is self-dual.
- That of octahedra and tetrahedra is dual to that of rhombic dodecahedra.
- The slab honeycombs derived from uniform plane tilings are dual to each other in the same way that the tilings are.
- The duals of the remaining Archimedean honeycombs are all cell-transitive and have been described by Inchbald.[^o26]

## Self-dual honeycombs {#Self-dual_honeycombs}

Honeycombs can also be [self-dual](wikipedia://en/609737). All *n*-dimensional [hypercubic honeycombs](wikipedia://en/12237982) with [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) {4,3<sup>*n*−2</sup>,4}, are self-dual.
