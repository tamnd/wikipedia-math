# Joseph P. LaSalle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph P. LaSalle"}

**Joseph Pierre LaSalle** (born 28 May 1916 in [State College, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_College,_Pennsylvania); died 7 July 1983 in [Little Compton, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Compton,_Rhode_Island)) was an American mathematician specialising in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and responsible for important contributions to [stability theory](wikipedia://en/5493795), such as [LaSalle's invariance principle](wikipedia://en/4021739) which bears his name.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Joseph LaSalle defended his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) thesis on ″*Pseudo-Normed Linear Sets over Valued Rings*″ at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1941.[^o4] In 1946 he joined the Mathematics Department at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) and remained there until 1958, becoming a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) in 1956.[^o5] During a visit to [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1947–1948, LaSalle developed a deep interest in [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) through his interaction with [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) and [Richard Bellman](wikipedia://en/730173), with whom he developed a close friendship.[^o5] From 1958 until 1964 LaSalle was based at the [Research Institute for Advanced Studies](wikipedia://en/25718963) (RIAS) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland), where he worked closely with [Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) and in 1960 published his extension of Lyapunov stability theory,[^o6] known today as [LaSalle's invariance principle](wikipedia://en/4021739).[^o5]

In 1962-1963 he was President of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM)[^o7] and was a member of its board of trustees in 1964–1967.[^o5] In 1964 LaSalle founded the [Journal of Differential Equations](wikipedia://en/84067337)[^o8] and served as its Editor-in-Chief until 1980.[^o5] In 1964 he became the first director of the Center for Dynamical Systems at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), where he was also the chairman of the Division of Applied Mathematics in 1968–1973.[^o9]

Together with [J. K. Hale](wikipedia://en/47758144), LaSalle was the recipient of the 1965 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for their article, ″*Differential Equations: Linearity vs. Nonlinearity*″,[^o10] published in the [SIAM Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Review).[^o11] In 1975 he was awarded the [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).[^o12]
