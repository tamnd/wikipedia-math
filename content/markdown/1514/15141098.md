**Jia Xian** (; ca. 1010–1070) was a [Chinese mathematician](wikipedia://en/1524543) from [Kaifeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaifeng) of the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty). He described [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) during the 11th century.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

According to the history of the Song dynasty, Jia was a palace [eunuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunuch) of the Left Duty Group. He studied under the mathematician Chu Yan, and was well versed in mathematics, writing many books on the subject. Jia Xian described the [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) (Jia Xian triangle) around the middle of the 11th century, about six centuries before [Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068). Jia used it as a tool for extracting [square](wikipedia://en/29208) and [cubic roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_root). The original book by Jia entitled *Shi Suo Suan Shu* was lost; however, Jia's method was expounded in detail by [Yang Hui](wikipedia://en/1716898), who explicitly acknowledged his source: "My method of finding square and cubic roots was based on the Jia Xian method in *Shi Suo Suan Shu*."[^o3] A page from the *[Yongle Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongle_Encyclopedia)* preserved this historic fact.

Jia Xian's additive-multiplicative method implemented the "Horner" rule.[^o4]
