# Peter Dodds (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Dodds"}

**Peter Sheridan Dodds** is an Australian [applied Mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician). He is the director of the [Vermont Complex Systems Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermont_Complex_Systems_Institute) and Professor at the [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont)'s Department of Mathematics and Statistics. He has collaborated in several researches related to big data problems in areas as [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language), [stories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative), [sociotechnical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociotechnical_systems), [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system), [Earth science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_science), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), and [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology). With [Chris Danforth](wikipedia://en/65718865), he co-runs the [Computational Story Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_Story_Lab), the [MassMutual Center of Excellence in Complex Systems and Data Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MassMutual_Center_of_Excellence_in_Complex_Systems_and_Data_Science), and together, they developed the [hedonometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedonometer).[^o3][^o4]

:::

Recent research directed by Dodds has been commented on in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, *[Smithsonian Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Magazine)*, and other media. In October 2020, the Hedonometor tool created by Dodds and his Danforth analyzed the sentiment of people through their tweets to identify May 31 as the saddest day recorded.[^o5][^o6]

In Dodds's early career, from 2002 to 2007, he was a frequent collaborator of [Duncan J. Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_J._Watts).

In 2016, Dodds collaborated with Danforth and Mark Ibrahim on writing about [the phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia_philosophy_phenomenon) where [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia) articles are connected by "[Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy)".[^o7]

In 2023, as part of a group of researchers in the Vermont Complex Systems Institute, he helped win a National Science Foundation award to study the science of stories. This 5-year grant was called The Science of Online Corpora, Knowledge, and Stories (SOCKS).[^o8]
