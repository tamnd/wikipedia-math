# Harry Rauch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Rauch"}

**Harry Ernest Rauch** (November 9, 1925 – June 18, 1979) was an American mathematician, who worked on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He was born in [Trenton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey), and died in [White Plains, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Plains,_New_York).

:::

Rauch earned his PhD in 1948 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847) with thesis *Generalizations of Some Classic Theorems to the Case of Functions of Several Variables*. From 1949 to 1951 he was a visiting member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He was in the 1960s a professor at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University) and from the mid-1970s a professor at the [Graduate School of the City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_School_of_the_City_University_of_New_York). His research was on differential geometry (especially [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on *n*-dimensional manifolds), [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces), and [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745).

In the early 1950s Rauch made fundamental progress on the *quarter-pinched sphere conjecture* in differential geometry.[^o4] In the case of positive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) and simply connected differential manifolds, Rauch proved that, under the condition that the sectional curvature *K* does not deviate too much from *K* = 1, the manifold must be homeomorphic to the sphere (*i.e.* the case where there is constant sectional curvature *K* = 1). Rauch's result created a new paradigm in differential geometry, that of a "pinching theorem;" in Rauch's case, the assumption was that the curvature was pinched between 0.76 and 1. This was later relaxed to pinching between 0.55 and 1 by [Wilhelm Klingenberg](wikipedia://en/33387331), and finally replaced with the sharp result of pinching between 0.25 and 1 by [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) and Klingenberg in the early 1960s. This optimal result is known as the [sphere theorem for Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/4685719).

The [Rauch comparison theorem](wikipedia://en/16221737) is also named after Harry Rauch. He proved it in 1951.
