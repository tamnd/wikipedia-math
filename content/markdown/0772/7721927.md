# Siegel modular form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegel modular form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Siegel modular forms** are a type of [automorphic form](wikipedia://en/782146) that generalize conventional elliptic [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), which are closely related to [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). The [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) constructed in the theory of Siegel modular forms are [Siegel modular varieties](wikipedia://en/61144542), which are basic models for what a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) for abelian varieties (with some extra [level structure](wikipedia://en/47016783)) should be and are constructed as quotients of the [Siegel upper half-space](wikipedia://en/1141106), rather than the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) by [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536).

:::

Siegel modular forms are [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on the set of [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) $n\times n$ matrices with [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix) imaginary part; the forms must satisfy an automorphy condition. Siegel modular forms can be thought of as multivariable modular forms, i.e. as [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).

Siegel modular forms are named after [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602), who first investigated them for the purpose of studying [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) analytically.[^n1] These primarily arise in various branches of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), such as [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [elliptic cohomology](wikipedia://en/10295395). Siegel modular forms have also been used in some areas of [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), such as [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774) and [black hole thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/339350) in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

## Definition {#Definition}

### Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

Let $g, N \in \mathbb{N}$ and define the [Siegel upper half-space](wikipedia://en/1141106)

$$\mathcal{H}_g=\left\{\tau \in M_{g \times g}(\mathbb{C}) \ \big| \ \tau^{\mathrm{T}}=\tau,\; \textrm{Im}(\tau) \text{ positive definite} \right\}.$$

Define the [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) of level $N$, denoted by $\Gamma_g(N),$ as

$$\Gamma_g(N)=\left\{ \gamma \in GL_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}) \ \big| \ \gamma^{\mathrm{T}} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_g \\ -I_g & 0 \end{pmatrix} \gamma= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & I_g \\ -I_g & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \ \gamma \equiv I_{2g}\!\!\!\mod N\right\},$$

where $I_g$ is the $g \times g$ [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718). Finally, let

$$\rho:\textrm{GL}_g(\mathbb{C}) \rightarrow \textrm{GL}(V)$$

be a [rational representation](wikipedia://en/5784135), where $V$ is a finite-dimensional complex [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370).

### Siegel modular form {#Siegel_modular_form}

Given a matrix

$$\gamma=\begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ C & D \end{pmatrix}$$

and $\gamma \in \Gamma_g(N)$, define

$$(f\,\vert\,\gamma)(\tau)=(\rho(C\tau+D))^{-1}f(\gamma\tau).$$

Then a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) $f:\mathcal{H}_g \rightarrow V$ is called a *Siegel modular form* of degree (or genus) $g$, weight $\rho$, and level $N$ if

$$(f\,\vert\,\gamma)=f$$

for all $\gamma \in \Gamma_g(N)$. In the case that $g=1$, we further require that $f$ be holomorphic "at infinity". This assumption is not necessary for $g>1$ due to the Koecher principle, explained below.

The space of weight $\rho$, degree $g$, and level $N$ Siegel modular forms is denoted by $M_{\rho}(\Gamma_g(N))$.

## Examples {#Examples}

Some methods for constructing Siegel modular forms include:

- [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458)
- [Theta functions of lattices](wikipedia://en/24016906) and [Siegel theta series](wikipedia://en/44513304)
- [Saito–Kurokawa lift](wikipedia://en/44536593) for degree 2
- [Ikeda lift](wikipedia://en/44483753)
- [Miyawaki lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyawaki_lift)
- Products of Siegel modular forms.

### Level 1, small degree {#Level_1,_small_degree}

For degree 1, the level 1 Siegel modular forms are the same as level 1 modular forms. The ring of such forms is a polynomial ring **C**\[*E*<sub>4</sub>,*E*<sub>6</sub>\] in the (degree 1) Eisenstein series *E*<sub>4</sub> and *E*<sub>6</sub>.

For degree 2, [Jun-ichi Igusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun-ichi_Igusa) showed that the ring of level 1 Siegel modular forms is generated by the (degree 2) Eisenstein series *E*<sub>4</sub> and *E*<sub>6</sub> and 3 more forms of weights 10, 12, and 35.[^n2] The ideal of relations between them is generated by the square of the weight 35 form minus a certain polynomial in the others.

For degree 3, Tsuyumine described the ring of level 1 Siegel modular forms, giving a set of 34 generators.[^n3]

For degree 4, the level 1 Siegel modular forms of small weights have been found. There are no cusp forms of weights 2, 4, or 6. The space of cusp forms of weight 8 is 1-dimensional, spanned by the [Schottky form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottky_form). The space of cusp forms of weight 10 has dimension 1, the space of cusp forms of weight 12 has dimension 2, the space of cusp forms of weight 14 has dimension 3, and the space of cusp forms of weight 16 has dimension 7 (Poor Yuen 2007).

For degree 5, the space of cusp forms has dimension 0 for weight 10, dimension 2 for weight 12. The space of forms of weight 12 has dimension 5.

For degree 6, there are no cusp forms of weights 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. The space of Siegel modular forms of weight 2 has dimension 0, and those of weights 4 or 6 both have dimension 1.

### Level 1, small weight {#Level_1,_small_weight}

For small weights and level 1, we have the following results (for any positive degree):[^n4]

- Weight 0: The space of forms is 1-dimensional, spanned by 1.
- Weight 1: The only Siegel modular form is 0.
- Weight 2: The only Siegel modular form is 0.
- Weight 3: The only Siegel modular form is 0.
- Weight 4: For any degree, the space of forms of weight 4 is 1-dimensional, spanned by the theta function of the E<sub>8</sub> lattice (of appropriate degree). The only cusp form is 0.
- Weight 5: The only Siegel modular form is 0.
- Weight 6: The space of forms of weight 6 has dimension 1 if the degree is at most 8, and dimension 0 if the degree is at least 9. The only cusp form is 0.
- Weight 7: The space of cusp forms vanishes if the degree is 4 or 7.
- Weight 8:In genus 4, the space of cusp forms is 1-dimensional, spanned by the [Schottky form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottky_form) and the space of forms is 2-dimensional. There are no cusp forms if the genus is 8.
- There are no cusp forms if the genus is greater than twice the weight.

## Koecher principle {#Koecher_principle}

The theorem known as the *Koecher principle* states that if $f$ is a Siegel modular form of weight $\rho$, level 1, and degree $g>1$, then $f$ is bounded on subsets of $\mathcal{H}_g$ of the form

$$\left\{\tau \in \mathcal{H}_g \ | \textrm{Im}(\tau) > \epsilon I_g \right\},$$

where $\epsilon>0$. Corollary to this theorem is the fact that Siegel modular forms of degree $g>1$ have [Fourier expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_expansion) and are thus holomorphic at infinity.[^o22]

:::

## Applications to physics {#Applications_to_physics}

In the D1D5P system of [supersymmetric black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_black_hole) in string theory, the function that naturally captures the microstates of black hole entropy is a Siegel modular form.[^o24] In general, Siegel modular forms have been described as having the potential to describe black holes or other gravitational systems.[^o24]

Siegel modular forms also have uses as generating functions for families of CFT2 with increasing central charge in [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774), particularly the hypothetical [AdS/CFT correspondence](wikipedia://en/644443).[^o25]
