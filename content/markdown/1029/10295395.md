# Elliptic cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elliptic cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **elliptic cohomology** is a [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) in the sense of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). It is related to [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) and [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms).

:::

## History and motivation {#History_and_motivation}

Historically, elliptic cohomology arose from the study of [elliptic genera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_genus). It was known by [Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) that if $S^1$ acts smoothly and non-trivially on a [spin manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold), then the index of the [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) vanishes. In 1983, [Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) conjectured that in this situation the equivariant index of a certain twisted Dirac operator is at least constant. This led to certain other problems concerning $S^1$-actions on manifolds, which could be solved by Ochanine by the introduction of elliptic genera. In turn, Witten related these to (conjectural) index theory on [free loop](wikipedia://en/18742821) spaces. Elliptic cohomology, invented in its original form by [Landweber](wikipedia://en/41079632), [Stong](wikipedia://en/34712394) and [Ravenel](wikipedia://en/27877012) in the late 1980s, was introduced to clarify certain issues with elliptic genera and provide a context for (conjectural) index theory of families of differential operators on free loop spaces. In some sense it can be seen as an approximation to the [K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) of the free loop space.

## Definitions and constructions {#Definitions_and_constructions}

Call a cohomology theory $A^*$ even periodic if $A^i = 0$ for $i$ odd and there is an invertible element $u\in A^2$. These theories possess a [complex orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_orientation), which gives a [formal group law](wikipedia://en/480335). A particularly rich source for formal group laws are [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). A cohomology theory $A$ with

$$A^0 = R$$

is called *elliptic* if it is even periodic and its formal group law is isomorphic to a formal group law of an elliptic curve $E$ over $R$. The usual construction of such elliptic cohomology theories uses the [Landweber exact functor theorem](wikipedia://en/28686098). If the formal group law of $E$ is Landweber exact, one can define an elliptic cohomology theory (on finite complexes) by

$$A^*(X) = MU^*(X)\otimes_{MU^*}R[u,u^{-1}]. \,$$

Franke has identified the condition needed to fulfill Landweber exactness:

1. $R$ needs to be flat over $\mathbb{Z}$
1. There is no irreducible component $X$ of $\text{Spec }R/pR$, where the fiber $E_x$ is [supersingular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersingular) for every $x\in X$

These conditions can be checked in many cases related to elliptic genera. Moreover, the conditions are fulfilled in the universal case in the sense that the map from the [moduli stack of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/49905706) to the moduli stack of [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group)

$$\mathcal{M}_{1,1}\to\mathcal{M}_{fg}$$

is [flat](wikipedia://en/542520). This gives then a [presheaf](wikipedia://en/4840944) of [cohomology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_algebraic_geometry)

> $$\mathcal{O}_{e\ell\ell}^{pre}: \text{Aff}/(\mathcal{M}_{1,1})_{flat} \to \textbf{Spectra}$$

over the [site](wikipedia://en/12910) of affine [schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_%28algebraic_geometry%29) flat over the moduli stack of elliptic curves. The desire to get a universal elliptic cohomology theory by taking global sections has led to the construction of the [topological modular forms](wikipedia://en/10274436)[^o4]<sup>pg 20</sup>

> $$\mathbf{Tmf} = \underset{X \to \mathcal{M}_{1,1}}{\textbf{Holim}}\text{ } \mathcal{O}_{e\ell\ell}^{pre}(X)$$

as the [homotopy limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_limit) of this presheaf over the previous site.
