# Conjugacy problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conjugacy problem"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **conjugacy problem** for a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* with a given [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) is the [decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336) of determining, given two words *x* and *y* in *G*, whether or not they represent [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) elements of *G*. That is, the problem is to determine whether there exists an element *z* of *G* such that

:::

$$y = zxz^{-1}.\,\!$$

The conjugacy problem is also known as the **transformation problem**.

The conjugacy problem was identified by [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602) in 1911 as one of the fundamental decision problems in group theory; the other two being the [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) and the [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026). The conjugacy problem contains the word problem as a special case: if *x* and *y* are words, deciding if they are the same word is equivalent to deciding if $xy^{-1}$ is the identity, which is the same as deciding if it's conjugate to the identity. In 1912 Dehn gave an algorithm that solves both the word and conjugacy problem for the [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of closed orientable two-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) of genus greater than or equal to 2 (the genus 0 and genus 1 cases being trivial).

It is known that the conjugacy problem is [undecidable](wikipedia://en/15631055) for many classes of groups. Classes of group presentations for which it is known to be solvable include:

- free groups (no defining relators)
- one-relator groups with torsion
- [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138)
- [knot groups](wikipedia://en/1164126)
- finitely presented conjugacy separable groups
- finitely generated abelian groups (relators include all commutators)
- [Gromov-hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817)
- [biautomatic groups](wikipedia://en/4224977#Biautomatic_groups)
- [CAT(0) groups](wikipedia://en/12942017)
- [Fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of geometrizable 3-manifolds
