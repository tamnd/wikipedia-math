# Bryan John Birch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bryan John Birch"}

**Bryan John Birch** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 25 September 1931) is a British mathematician. His name has been given to the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bryan John Birch was born in [Burton-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton-on-Trent), the son of Arthur Jack and Mary Edith Birch. He was educated at [Shrewsbury School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_School) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He married Gina Margaret Christ in 1961. They have three children.

As a doctoral student at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), he was officially working under [J. W. S. Cassels](wikipedia://en/1709206). More influenced by [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111), he proved [Birch's theorem](wikipedia://en/5147723), one of the results to come out of the [Hardy–Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method).

Birch then worked with [Peter Swinnerton-Dyer](wikipedia://en/2105540) on computations relating to the [Hasse–Weil L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Weil_L-function) of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). Their subsequently formulated conjecture relating the [rank of an elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/51244926) to the order of zero of an L-function has been an influence on the development of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) from the mid-1960s onwards.

Birch introduced [modular symbols](wikipedia://en/37642125) in about 1971.

In later work, Birch contributed to [algebraic *K*-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) ([Birch–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/7774402)). He then formulated ideas on the role of [Heegner points](wikipedia://en/5828654) (he was one of those reconsidering [Kurt Heegner](wikipedia://en/621799)'s original work on the [class number one problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_one_problem), which had not initially gained acceptance). Birch put together the context in which the [Gross–Zagier theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross–Zagier_theorem) was proved; the correspondence is now published.[^o4]

Birch was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the fall of 1983.[^o5] He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1972; was awarded the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) in 1993 and the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) in 2007 both of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6] In 2020 he was awarded the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) by the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o7]
