# Mary Clem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Clem"}

**Mary A. Clem** (née **Mary A. McLaughlin**; 19051979)[^o5][^o3] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and a [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28job_description%29). She was a staff member at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University), and was recognized for inventing the “zero check” technique for detecting errors.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Clem was born on October 19, 1905[^o3] in the small town of [Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada,_Iowa), in [Story County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_County,_Iowa), central [Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa). She completed her high school degree and found employment for several years with the [Iowa State Highway Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Department_of_Transportation) and [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) as a computing clerk, auditing clerk, and bookkeeper.[^o5]

In 1931, she joined the Mathematics Statistical Service of the Mathematics Department of Iowa State College to work as a human computer under the supervision of [George Snedecor](wikipedia://en/1134409).[^o6] Although she complained that mathematics was her poorest subject in high school, she was fascinated with figures and data. Most of her work was done via [punch cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punched_card), both creating formulas and cards, and running accuracy checks on them. She invented the “zero check” while working in Snedecor’s lab. The “zero check” is a sum that should equal zero if all other numbers had been correctly calculated.[^o6] These sums helped check for errors in computing algorithms.[^o7] Clem expressed that her lack of training as a mathematician is what made her notice these sums, as they had often been overlooked by others.[^o6] In 1940, Clem was advanced to be technician and chief statistical clerk in charge of the Computing Service of the Statistical Laboratory.[^o5][^o8] In 1962, she transferred to the new Computation Center at Iowa State University.[^o5]

Clem went on the 2nd Allied Mission to Greece in 1946 as a junior statistician, and there she observed the elections.[^o5][^o9] In 1952, she was a statistical consultant to the [Atomic Bomb Casualty Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) in [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima), Japan.[^o5]
