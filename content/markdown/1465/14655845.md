# Complex-base system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex-base system"}

In [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), a **complex-base system** is a [positional numeral system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positional_numeral_system) whose [radix](wikipedia://en/701207) is an [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) (proposed by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) in 1955[^o2][^o3]) or [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) (proposed by S. Khmelnik in 1964[^o4] and Walter F. Penney in 1965[^o5][^o6][^o7]).

:::

## In general {#In_general}

Let $D$ be an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) $\subset \mathbb{C}$, and $|\cdot|$ the [(Archimedean) absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667#Types_of_absolute_value) on it.

A number $X\in D$ in a positional number system is represented as an expansion

$$X = \pm \sum_{\nu}^{ } x_\nu \rho^\nu,$$

where

{| class="table left"

\|- | $\rho \in D$ || || is the **radix** (or **base**) with $|\rho| > 1$, |- | $\nu \in \mathbb{Z}$ || || is the exponent (position or place), |- | $x_\nu$ || || are digits from the *finite* set of digits $Z \subset D$, usually with $|x_\nu| < |\rho|.$ |} The [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) $R:=|Z|$ is called the *level of decomposition*.

A positional number system or **coding system** is a pair

$$\left\langle \rho, Z \right\rangle$$

with radix $\rho$ and set of digits $Z$, and we write the standard set of digits with $R$ digits as

$$Z_R := \{0, 1, 2,\dotsc, {R-1}\}.$$

Desirable are coding systems with the features:

- Every number in $D$, e. g. the integers $\mathbb{Z}$, the [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628) $\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm i]$ or the integers $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{-1+\mathrm i\sqrt7}2]$, is *uniquely* representable as a *finite* code, possibly with a [sign](wikipedia://en/7951270) ±.
- Every number in the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) $K:=\operatorname{Quot}(D)$, which possibly is [completed](wikipedia://en/7832) for the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) given by $|\cdot|$ yielding $K:=\mathbb{R}$ or $K:=\mathbb{C}$, is representable as an infinite series $X$ which converges under $|\cdot|$ for $\nu \to -\infty$, and the [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) of the set of numbers with more than one representation is 0. The latter requires that the set $Z$ be minimal, i.e. $R=|\rho|$ for [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and $R=|\rho|^2$ for complex numbers.

## In the real numbers {#In_the_real_numbers}

In this notation our standard decimal coding scheme is denoted by

$$\left\langle 10, Z_{10} \right\rangle,$$

the standard binary system is

$$\left\langle 2, Z_2 \right\rangle,$$

the [negabinary](wikipedia://en/10784136) system is

$$\left\langle -2, Z_2 \right\rangle,$$

and the balanced ternary system[^o3] is

$$\left\langle 3, \{-1,0,1\} \right\rangle.$$

All these coding systems have the mentioned features for $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{R}$, and the last two do not require a sign.

## In the complex numbers {#In_the_complex_numbers}

Well-known positional number systems for the complex numbers include the following ($\mathrm i$ being the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358)):

- $\left\langle\sqrt{R},Z_R\right\rangle$, e.g. $\left\langle\pm \mathrm i \sqrt{2},Z_2\right\rangle$ [^o2] and

$\left\langle\pm 2\mathrm i,Z_4\right\rangle$,[^o3] the [quater-imaginary base](wikipedia://en/239699), proposed by [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) in 1955.

- $\left\langle\sqrt{2}e^{\pm \tfrac{\pi}2 \mathrm i}=\pm \mathrm i\sqrt{2},Z_2\right\rangle$ and

$\left\langle\sqrt{2}e^{\pm \tfrac{3 \pi}4 \mathrm i}=-1\pm\mathrm i,Z_2\right\rangle$[^o4][^o6] (see also the section Base −1 ± **i** below).

- $\left\langle\sqrt{R}e^{\mathrm i\varphi},Z_R\right\rangle$, where $\varphi=\pm \arccos{(-\beta/(2\sqrt{R}))}$, $\beta<\min(R, 2\sqrt{R})$ and $\beta_{ }^{ }$ is a positive integer that can take multiple values at a given $R$.[^o11] For $\beta=1$ and $R=2$ this is the system

$$\left\langle\tfrac{-1+\mathrm i\sqrt7}2,Z_2\right\rangle.$$

- $\left\langle 2e^{\tfrac{\pi}3 \mathrm i},A_4:=\left\{0,1,e^{\tfrac{2 \pi}3 \mathrm i},e^{-\tfrac{2 \pi}3 \mathrm i}\right\}\right\rangle$.[^o12]
- $\left\langle-R,A_R^2\right\rangle$, where the set $A_R^2$ consists of complex numbers $r_\nu=\alpha_\nu^1+\alpha_\nu^2\mathrm i$, and numbers $\alpha_\nu^{ } \in Z_R$, e.g.

$\left\langle -2, \{0,1,\mathrm i,1+\mathrm i\}\right\rangle.$[^o12]

- $\left\langle\rho=\rho_2,Z_2\right\rangle$, where $\rho_2=\begin{cases} (-2)^{\tfrac{\nu}2} & \text{if } \nu \text{ even,}\\ (-2)^{\tfrac{\nu-1}2}\mathrm i & \text{if } \nu \text{ odd.} \end{cases}$ [^o13]

## Base $-1 \pm i$ {#Base}

*Image: ComplexTwindragon.svg.* The complex numbers with integer part all zeroes in the base $\mathbf{i} - 1$ system

Of particular interest are the [quater-imaginary base](wikipedia://en/239699) (base $2\mathbf{i}$) and the base $-1 \pm \mathbf{i}$ systems discussed below, both of which can be used to finitely represent the [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628) without sign.

Base $-1 \pm \mathbf{i}$, using digits $0$ and $1$, was proposed by S. Khmelnik in 1964[^o4] and Walter F. Penney in 1965.[^o5][^o7]

### Connection to the twindragon {#Connection_to_the_twindragon}

The rounding region of an integer – i.e., a set $S$ of complex (non-integer) numbers that share the integer part of their representation in this system – has in the complex plane a fractal shape: the [twindragon](wikipedia://en/367064#Twindragon) (see figure). This set $S$ is, by definition, all points that can be written as $\textstyle \sum_{k\geq 1}x_k (\mathrm i-1)^{-k}$ with $x_k\in Z_2$. $S$ can be decomposed into 16 pieces congruent to $\tfrac14 S$. Notice that if $S$ is rotated counterclockwise by 135°, we obtain two adjacent sets congruent to $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}S$, because $(\mathrm i-1)S=S\cup(S+1)$. The rectangle $R\subset S$ in the center intersects the coordinate axes counterclockwise at the following points: $\tfrac2{15}\gets 0.\overline{00001100}$, $\tfrac1{15} \mathrm i\gets 0.\overline{00000011}$, and $-\tfrac8{15}\gets 0.\overline{11000000}$, and $-\tfrac4{15} \mathrm i\gets 0.\overline{00110000}$. Thus, $S$ contains all complex numbers with absolute value ≤$\tfrac{1}{15}$.[^o20]

As a consequence, there is an [injection](wikipedia://en/45196) of the complex rectangle

$$[-\tfrac8{15},\tfrac2{15}]\times[-\tfrac4{15},\tfrac1{15}]\mathrm i$$

into the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $[0,1)$ of real numbers by mapping

$$\textstyle \sum_{k\geq 1}x_k (\mathrm i-1)^{-k} \mapsto \sum_{k\geq 1}x_k b^{-k}$$

with $b > 2$.[^o21]

Furthermore, there are the two mappings

$$\begin{array}{lll} Z_2^\mathbb{N} & \to & S \\ \left(x_k\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} & \mapsto & \sum_{k\geq 1}x_k (\mathrm i-1)^{-k} \end{array}$$

and

$$\begin{array}{lll} Z_2^\mathbb{N} & \to & [0,1) \\ \left(x_k\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}} & \mapsto & \sum_{k\geq 1}x_k 2^{-k} \end{array}$$

both [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective), which give rise to a surjective (thus space-filling) mapping

$$[0,1) \qquad \to \qquad S$$

which, however, is not [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) and thus *not* a [space-filling *curve*](wikipedia://en/352398). But a very close relative, the [Davis-Knuth dragon](wikipedia://en/367064#Twindragon), is continuous and a space-filling curve.
