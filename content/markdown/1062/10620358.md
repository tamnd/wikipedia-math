# Principal series representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principal series representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **principal series representations** of certain kinds of [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) *G* occur in the case where *G* is not a [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326). There, by analogy with [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713), one expects that the [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011) of *G* will decompose according to some kind of [continuous spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137#Continuous_spectrum), of representations involving a continuous parameter, as well as a [discrete spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137#Discrete_spectrum). The *principal series* representations are some [induced representations](wikipedia://en/357452) constructed in a uniform way, in order to fill out the continuous part of the spectrum.

:::

In more detail, the [unitary dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_dual) is the space of all representations relevant to decomposing the regular representation. The [discrete series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_series) consists of 'atoms' of the unitary dual (points carrying a [Plancherel measure](wikipedia://en/31244986) > 0). In the earliest examples studied, the rest (or most) of the unitary dual could be parametrised by starting with a subgroup *H* of *G*, simpler but not compact, and building up induced representations using representations of *H* which were accessible, in the sense of being easy to write down, and involving a parameter. (Such an induction process may produce representations that are not unitary.)

For the case of a [semisimple Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) *G*, the subgroup *H* is constructed starting from the [Iwasawa decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139148)

*G* = *KAN*

with *K* a [maximal compact subgroup](wikipedia://en/1496726). Then *H* is chosen to contain *AN* (which is a non-compact [solvable Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvable_Lie_group)), being taken as

H := *MAN*

with *M* the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) in *K* of *A*. Representations ρ of *H* are considered that are irreducible, and unitary, and are the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573) on the subgroup *N*. (Assuming the case *M* a trivial group, such ρ are analogues of the representations of the group of [diagonal matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_matrices) inside the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823).) The induced representations of such ρ make up the principal series. The **spherical principal series** consists of representations induced from 1-dimensional representations of *MAN* obtained by extending characters of *A* using the homomorphism of *MAN* onto *A*.

There may be other continuous series of representations relevant to the unitary dual: as their name implies, the principal series are the 'main' contribution.

This type of construction has been found to have application to groups *G* that are not [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) (for example, [finite groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_groups_of_Lie_type), groups over [p-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field)).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For examples, see the [representation theory of SL<sub>2</sub>(R)](wikipedia://en/11103925). For the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL<sub>2</sub> over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), the dimension of the [Jacquet module](wikipedia://en/32162058) of a principal series representation is two.[^o4]
