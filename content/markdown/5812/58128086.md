# Supergolden ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supergolden ratio"}

In mathematics, the **supergolden ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](wikipedia://en/1021), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation $x3 = x2 + 1$. Its decimal expansion begins with $1.465571231876768$ .

:::

The name *supergolden ratio* is by analogy with the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), the positive solution of the equation $x2 = x + 1.$

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Supergolden cuboid.svg.* ψ = $\tfrac{a}{b}$ = $\tfrac{a+c}{a}$ = $\tfrac{b}{c}$. For b = 1 the boxes have volumes $\psi3 = \psi2 (\operatorname{red}) + 1 (\operatorname{green}).$

Three quantities $a > b > c > 0$ are in the supergolden ratio if

$$\frac{a+c}{a}=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{b}{c}$$

This common ratio is commonly denoted $\psi.$

Substituting $b=\psi c$ and $a=\psi b =\psi^2 c$ in the first fraction,

$$\psi =\frac{c(\psi^2 +1)}{\psi^2 c}.$$

It follows that the supergolden ratio is the unique real solution of the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) $\psi^3 -\psi^2 -1 =0$.

The [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) for the reciprocal root is the [depressed cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depressed_cubic) $x^3 +x -1$, thus the simplest solution with [Cardano's formula](wikipedia://en/180787#Cardano's_formula),

$$\begin{aligned} w_{1,2} &=\left( 1 \pm \frac{1}{3} \sqrt{ \frac{31}{3}} \right) /2 \\ 1 /\psi &=\sqrt[3]{w_1} +\sqrt[3]{w_2} \end{aligned}$$

or, using the [hyperbolic sine](wikipedia://en/180787#Trigonometric_and_hyperbolic_solutions),

$$1 /\psi =\frac{2}{ \sqrt{3}} \sinh \left( \frac{1}{3} \operatorname{arsinh} \left( \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2} \right) \right).$$

*Image: Supergolden triangle.png.* A triangle with side lengths $\psi, 1,$ and $1 \text{∕} \psi$ has an angle of exactly 120 degrees.

$1 /\psi$ is the superstable [fixed point](wikipedia://en/7344320) of the [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) iteration $x \gets (2x^3 +1) /(3x^2 +1)$.

Dividing the defining trinomial $x^3 -x^2 -1$ by $x -\psi$ one obtains $x^2 +x /\psi^2 +1 /\psi$, and the [conjugate elements](wikipedia://en/458467) of $\psi$ are

$$x_{1,2} = \left( -1 \pm i \sqrt{4 \psi^2 + 3} \right) /2 \psi^2 ,$$

with $x_1 +x_2 = 1 -\psi$ and $x_1x_2 =1 /\psi$.

The iteration $x \gets \sqrt[3]{\phantom{|} 1 +x^2}$ results in the [continued radical](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals)

$$\psi^3 -1 =\psi^2 =\sqrt[3/2]{1 +\sqrt[3/2]{1 +\sqrt[3/2]{\phantom{|} 1 +\cdots}}}$$

Better convergence is attained by using relation $y^3 =y(1-2y) +3 \,$ with real zero $\psi^2 -1$. Divide both sides by $y$, and substitute $\psi^{-3} =1/(y+2) \,\text{ for }\, 3/y -2y.$ This gives the iteration $y \gets \sqrt{1 +\tfrac{1}{2+y}},$ and the continued reciprocal square root

$$\psi^2 -1=\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{2+\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{2+\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{2 +\ddots}}}}}}$$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: SuperGoldenSquare\_6.png.* Rectangles in aspect ratios $\psi, \psi^{2}$ and $\psi^{3}$ (from left to right) tile the square.

The supergolden ratio can be expressed in terms of itself in many different ways, for example as fractions

$$2\psi -1 =\frac{\psi^2 +2}{\psi^2} =\frac{\psi^3 +1}{\psi^3 -1} =\frac{2\psi^7 -1}{\psi^7},$$

along with

$$\begin{aligned} 1 +\psi^{-1} &=\frac{\psi^2 +2}{\psi +1} =\frac{2\psi^3 -1}{\psi^3} \\ 1 +\psi^{-3} &=\frac{\psi^2 +2}{\psi^2 +1} \\ 1 +\psi^{-5} &=\frac{\psi +1}{\psi^2} \\ =\psi^2 -1 &=\frac{\psi^7 +1}{\psi^7 -1} \\ \psi^4 -1 &=\frac{\psi^2 +2}{\psi^2 -1} \\ \psi^6 -1 &=\frac{\psi^2 +2}{\psi -1}.\end{aligned}$$

Similarly as infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} \psi^3 &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-n} \\ \frac{\psi^2}{\psi^2 -1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-2n} \\ \psi &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-3n} \\ \frac{\psi}{\psi^2 -1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-4n} \\ \frac{1}{3 -\psi^2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-5n} \\ \frac{1}{\psi^2 -1} +\frac{1}{\psi^2 +2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-6n} \\ \frac{\psi^2}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \psi^{-7n}.\end{aligned}$$

Additionally, $\sum_{n=0}^{7} \psi^{-n} = 3.$

For every integer $n$ one has

$$\begin{aligned} \psi^n &=\psi^{n-1} +\psi^{n-3} \\ &=\psi^{n-2} +\psi^{n-3} +\psi^{n-4} \\ &=\psi^{n-2} +2\psi^{n-4} +\psi^{n-6} \end{aligned}$$

from this an infinite number of further relations can be found.

Argument $\theta =\operatorname{arcsec}(2\psi^{4})$ satisfies the identity $\tan(\theta) -4\sin(\theta) =3\sqrt{{(}}3{{)}}$.[^o10]

[Continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) pattern of a few low powers

$$\begin{aligned} \psi^{-1} &=[0;1,2,6,1,3,5,4,22,...] \approx 0.6823 \;(\tfrac{13}{19}) \\ \psi^0 &=[1] \\ \psi^1 &=[1;2,6,1,3,5,4,22,1,...] \approx 1.4656 \;(\tfrac{22}{15}) \\ \psi^2 &=[2;6,1,3,5,4,22,1,1,...] \approx 2.1479 \;(\tfrac{15}{7}) \\ \psi^3 &=[3;6,1,3,5,4,22,1,1,...] \approx 3.1479 \;(\tfrac{22}{7}) \\ \psi^4 &=[4;1,1,1,1,2,2,1,2,2,...] \approx 4.6135 \;(\tfrac{60}{13}) \\ \psi^5 &=[6;1,3,5,4,22,1,1,4,...] \approx 6.7614 \;(\tfrac{115}{17}) \end{aligned}$$

Notably, the continued fraction of $\psi^2$ begins as [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of the first six natural numbers; the next term is equal to their sum + 1.

As derived from its continued fraction expansion, the simplest rational approximations of $\psi$ are:

$$\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{19}{13},\tfrac{22}{15},\tfrac{85}{58},\tfrac{277}{189},\tfrac{447}{305},\tfrac{1873}{1278},\tfrac{41653}{28421},\tfrac{43526}{29699},\tfrac{85179}{58120}, \ldots$$

*Image: Supergolden Newton map.svg.* [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) for $p(z) = z3 - z2 - 1:$ $\psi$ (right) and its complex conjugates at the nuclei of their [basins of attraction](wikipedia://en/191094#Basins_of_attraction). [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) of the Newton map in orange, with unit circle and real curve for reference.

The supergolden ratio is the fourth smallest [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number).[^o11] By definition of these numbers, the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Complex_numbers) $1 /\sqrt{\psi}$ of the algebraic conjugates is smaller than 1, thus powers of $\psi$ generate [almost integers](wikipedia://en/14041933). For example: $\psi^{11} =67.000222765...$ $\approx 67 +1/4489$. After eleven rotation steps the [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) of the inward spiraling conjugate pair – initially close to $\pm 13 \pi/22$ – nearly align with the imaginary axis.

The [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of the supergolden ratio $m(x) =x^3 -x^2 -1$ has [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $\Delta=-31 .$ The [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036) of imaginary [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $K = \mathbb{Q}( \sqrt{\Delta})$ can be formed by adjoining $\psi.$ With argument $\tau=(1 +\sqrt{\Delta})/2\,$ a generator for the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$, one has the special value of [Dedekind eta](wikipedia://en/447020) quotient

$$\psi =\frac{ e^{\pi i/24}\,\eta(\tau)}{ \sqrt{2}\,\eta(2\tau)}.$$

Expressed in terms of the [Weber-Ramanujan class invariant G<sub>n</sub>](wikipedia://en/33559472)

$$\psi =\frac{ \mathfrak{f} ( \sqrt{ \Delta} )}{ \sqrt{2} } =\frac{ G_{31} }{ \sqrt[4]{2} }.$$

Properties of the related [Klein j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988#The_q-expansion_and_moonshine) $j(\tau)$ result in near identity $e^{\pi \sqrt{- \Delta}} \approx \left( \sqrt{2}\,\psi \right)^{24} - 24 .$ The difference is $< 1/143092$.

The [elliptic integral singular value](wikipedia://en/27903678#Lambda-star) $k_{r} =\lambda^{*}(r)$ for $r=31$ has closed form expression

$$\lambda^{*}(31) =\sin ( \arcsin \left( ( \sqrt[4]{2}\,\psi)^{-12} \right) /2)$$

(which is less than 1/10 the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472#Ellipses) of the orbit of Venus).

## Narayana sequence {#Narayana_sequence}

Narayana's cows is a [recurrence sequence](wikipedia://en/49700795) originating from a problem proposed by the 14th century Indian mathematician [Narayana Pandita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayana_Pandita_%28mathematician%29). He asked for the number of cows and calves in a herd after 20 years, beginning with one cow in the first year, where each cow gives birth to one calf each year from the age of three onwards.

The Narayana sequence has a close connection to the [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/10918) and [Padovan sequences](wikipedia://en/2161709) and plays an important role in data coding, cryptography and combinatorics. The number of [compositions](wikipedia://en/550741) of n into parts 1 and 3 is counted by the *n*th Narayana number.

The Narayana sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$N_n =N_{n-1} +N_{n-3} \text{ for } n >2,$$

with initial values

$$N_0 =N_1 =N_2 =1.$$

The first few terms are 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 28, 41, 60, 88,... . The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the supergolden ratio:$\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty} N_{n+1}/N_n =\psi.$

The first 11 indices n for which $N_n$ is prime are n = 3, 4, 8, 9, 11, 16, 21, 25, 81, 6241, 25747 . The last number has 4274 decimal digits.

The sequence is extended to negative indices using

$$N_n =N_{n+3} -N_{n+2},$$

obtaining (1), 0, 0, 1, 0,−1, 1, 1,−2, 0, 3,−2,−3, 5,...

Powers of the supergolden ratio can be written with Narayana numbers as quadratic coefficients

$$\psi^n =\psi^2 N_{n-2} +\psi N_{n-4} +N_{n-3},$$

which is proved by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $n.$ This relation also holds for $n < 0.$ The order of the coefficients corresponds to the bottom row of matrix $Q$ below.

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the Narayana sequence for non-negative $n$ is given by

$$\frac{1}{1 -x -x^3} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} N_n x^n \text{ for } x <\tfrac{1}{\psi}$$

The Narayana numbers are related to sums of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by

$$N_n =\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n / 3 \rfloor}{n-2k \choose k}$$

The [characteristic equation](wikipedia://en/31050469) of the recurrence is $x^3 -x^2 -1 =0 .$ If the three solutions are real root $\psi$ and conjugate pair $\beta$ and $\gamma$, the Narayana numbers are given by the [Binet formula](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula) [^o16]

$$N_{n-1} =a \psi^n +b \beta^n +c \gamma^n ,$$

with real $a$ and conjugates $b$ and $c$ the roots of $31x^3 -3x -1 =0.$

Since $\left\vert b \beta^n +c \gamma^n \right\vert < \psi^{-n/2}$, the number $N_n$ is the [nearest integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function) to $\,a\,\psi^{n+1}$, with $n \ge 0$ and coefficient $a =\psi^2 /(\psi^3 +2) =$ 0.41723

Coefficients $a =b =c =1$ result in the Binet formula for the related sequence $A_n =N_n +2N_{n-3} .$

The first few terms are 3, 1, 1, 4, 5, 6, 10, 15, 21, 31, 46, 67, 98, 144,... .

This anonymous sequence has the [Fermat property](wikipedia://en/53453): if p is prime, $A_{p} \equiv A_1 \bmod p .$ The converse does not hold, but the small number of odd [pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/28787420) $\,n \mid (A_n -1)$ makes the sequence special.[^o17] The 8 odd composite numbers below $10^{8}$ to pass the test are n = 1155, 552599, 2722611, 4822081, 10479787, 10620331, 16910355, 66342673.

*Image: Supergolden\_Rauzy\_ab.png.* A supergolden Rauzy fractal of type a ↦ ab, with areas as above. The fractal boundary has [box-counting](wikipedia://en/601025) [dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) 1.50

The Narayana numbers are obtained as integral powers $n > 3$ of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with real [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) $\psi$ [^o15]

$$Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,$$

$$Q^n =\begin{pmatrix} N_n & N_{n-2} & N_{n-1} \\ N_{n-1} & N_{n-3} & N_{n-2} \\ N_{n-2} & N_{n-4} & N_{n-3} \end{pmatrix}$$

The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of $Q^n$ gives the above $A_n$.

Alternatively, $Q$ can be interpreted as [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) for a [D0L](wikipedia://en/37432570#D0L_system) [Lindenmayer system](wikipedia://en/46565) on the alphabet $\{a,b,c\}$ with corresponding [substitution rule](wikipedia://en/2452154)

$$\begin{cases} a \;\mapsto \;ab \\ b \;\mapsto \;c \\ c \;\mapsto \;a \end{cases}$$

and initiator $w_0=b$. The series of words $w_n$ produced by iterating the substitution have lengths $l(w_n) =N_n$. The number of $c, b\text{ and } a's$ in each word is equal to successive Narayana numbers. Associated to this string rewriting process is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) set composed of [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) tiles called the [Rauzy fractal](wikipedia://en/29344658), that visualizes the [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/20913490) information contained in a multiple-generation three-letter sequence.[^o18]

The ratio between the number of digits in successive strings for the binary version of the [look-and-say sequence](wikipedia://en/563980) converges to $\psi$.[^o20]

## Geometry {#Geometry}

### Psi-quad substitution tiling {#Psi-quad_substitution_tiling}

In the complex plane, if $\psi$ is a non-real root of $\psi^3 -\psi^2 -1 =0$, then the vertices of a self-similar quadrilateral tiling (the *psi-quad* [substitution tiling](wikipedia://en/5558956)) can be taken as the six points $\psi^0, \psi^1, \psi^2, \psi^3, \psi^4, -\psi^2$. The iterated boundary is the supergolden fractal triangle, which has [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294) $d_{\partial} =\frac{2 \log \zeta}{\log \psi}$, where $\zeta$ is the real root of $\zeta^7 -2\zeta^2 -1 =0$, so numerically $d_{\partial} \approx 1.0295240599$.

### Supergolden rectangle {#Supergolden_rectangle}

*Image: Supergolden\_ratio.svg.* Nested supergolden rectangles with perpendicular diagonals and side lengths in powers of $\psi$.

A supergolden rectangle is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) whose side lengths are in a $\psi:1$ ratio. Compared to the [golden rectangle](wikipedia://en/219374), the supergolden rectangle has one more degree of [self-similarity](wikipedia://en/28782).

Given a rectangle of height $1$, length $\psi$ and diagonal length $\sqrt{\psi^3}$ (according to $1 +\psi^2 =\psi^3$). The triangles on the diagonal have [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) $1 /\sqrt{\psi}\,;$ each perpendicular foot divides the diagonal in ratio $\psi^2$.

On the left-hand side, cut off a square of side length $1$ and mark the intersection with the falling diagonal. The remaining rectangle now has [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) $\psi^2 :1$ (according to $\psi -1 =\psi^{-2}$). Divide the original rectangle into four parts by a second, horizontal cut passing through the intersection point.[^o25][^o26]

The rectangle below the diagonal has aspect ratio $\psi^3$, the other three are all supergolden rectangles, with a fourth one between the feet of the altitudes. The parent rectangle and the four scaled copies have linear sizes in the ratios $\psi^3 :\psi^2 :\psi :\psi^2 -1 :1.$ It follows from the [theorem of the gnomon](wikipedia://en/59808021) that the areas of the two rectangles opposite the diagonal are equal.

In the supergolden rectangle above the diagonal, the process is repeated at a scale of $1:\psi^2$.

:::

### Supergolden spiral {#Supergolden_spiral}

*Image: Supergolden\_spiral.svg.* Supergolden spirals with different initial radii on a $\psi$− rectangle.

A supergolden spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) that gets wider by a factor of $\psi$ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) $r( \theta) =a \exp(k \theta),$ with initial radius $a$ and parameter $k =\frac{2}{\pi} \ln( \psi).$ If drawn on a supergolden rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio $\psi$ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor $\psi^{-1}.$
