# Louis Kollros

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis Kollros"}

*Image: Picone Franel Dandliker Kraitchik Kollros Wolff Straszewicz Zurich1932.tif.* Louis Kollros (3rd from right) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) 1932 in Zürich

**Louis Kollros** (7 May 1878 – 19 June 1959) was a Swiss mathematician. From 1909 to 1948 he was a professor ordinarius of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kollros was born on 7 May 1878 in [La Chaux-de-Fonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Chaux-de-Fonds). Kollros, the son of a baker, was from 1896 as a student of mathematics and physics at the [Zurich Polytechnikum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), where he was a fellow student of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) and [Marcel Grossmann](wikipedia://en/1247037).[^o4] After graduating in 1900, Kollros taught mathematics from 1900 to 1909 in secondary school in his hometown of La Chaux-de-Fonds. In 1903–1904 to 1909 he studied in Göttingen with [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). From 1904 to 1909 Kollross was a *privat-docent* (lecturer) at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel). He received his doctorate in 1905 from the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) with thesis advisor Hermann Minkowski and thesis *Un algorithme pour l'approximation simultaneé de deux grandeurs*. At ETH Zurich, where Marcel Grossmann taught until 1927 in the same field, Kollross held from 1909 to 1948 the francophone chair of *géométrie descriptive et de géométrie euclidienne*.[^o2]

In 1940–1941 he was president of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society) and from 1958 an honorary member of the Society.[^o6] He was president of the [Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445)-[Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) committee (tasked with the publication of their works). In this role, he was co-editor of Schläfli's collected works (3 vols.,1950–1956).[^o2]

He wrote biographies of [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) (1949, 24 p.) and Jakob Steiner (1947, 24 p.), which appeared in the supplements to the *[Elemente der Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elemente_der_Mathematik)* ([Birkhäuser Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhäuser_Verlag)).[^o2]

His doctoral students include [Ferdinand Gonseth](wikipedia://en/54012910).

He died on 19 June 1959 in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich).
