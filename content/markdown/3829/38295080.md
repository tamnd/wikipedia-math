# Vittorio Francesco Stancari

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vittorio Francesco Stancari"}

*Image: Stancari, Vittorio Francesco – Schedae mathematicae post ejus obitum collectae ejusdem Observationes astronomicae, 1713 – BEIC 4713493.jpg.* *Schedae mathematicae post ejus obitum collectae ejusdem Observationes astronomicae*, 1713

**Vittorio Francesco Stancari** (29 July 1678 – 28 March 1709) was a professor of mathematics at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) who undertook research into the measurement of sounds, and into optics and hydrostatics.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Vittorio Francesco Stancari was born in Bologna in 1678. In 1698 he became a professor of mathematics at the University of Bologna.[^o9] Stancari was one of a group of young men at the University who became interested in the techniques of [Cartesian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_geometry) and [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416), and who engaged in experiments and astronomical observation. Others were [Eustachio Manfredi](wikipedia://en/38290281), his brother [Gabriele Manfredi](wikipedia://en/38298712) and [Giuseppe Sentenziola Verzaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Sentenziola_Verzaglia). Of these, Gabriele Manfredi developed the most advanced understanding of mathematics.[^n1] Stancari was awarded the chair of infinitesimal calculus in Bologna in 1708.[^n2] He died in Bologna in 1709, aged about 31.[^o9]

## Work {#Work}

Stancari's dissertations and manuscripts show that he applied [Leibnizian](wikipedia://en/12281) [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) to problems of physics, hydrodynamics, meteorology and mechanics.[^n2] He was also aware of Sir [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s *Principia Mathematica*, and discussed Newton in lectures before the *[Accademia degli Inquieti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_degli_Inquieti)* in Bologna.[^n3]

Stancari developed a method of measuring the pitch of sound in 1706, using foil that was excited into vibration by rotating toothed wheels.[^o9] Working in the observatory founded by [Count Marsigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Ferdinando_Marsili),[^o10] Stancari and Eustachio Manfredi discovered the comet [C/1707 W1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C/1707_W1) in the evening of 25 November 1707. They described it as visible to the naked eye, white, irregular and with a short, faint tail. It had the same apparent size as Jupiter.[^n4]

Stancari experimented with [Guillaume Amontons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Amontons)' air thermometer, where air in the bulb pushes up a column of mercury as it expands due to rising temperature. He discovered that the humidity of the air in the bulb had a significant effect on the readings.[^n5]
