# Ailana Fraser

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ailana Fraser"}

**Ailana Margaret Fraser** is a Canadian mathematician and professor of mathematics at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia).[^o3] She is known for her work in [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) and the theory of [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734).[^o4] Her research is particularly focused on extremal [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) problems and sharp eigenvalue estimates for surfaces, min-max minimal surface theory, free boundary minimal surfaces, and positive isotropic curvature.[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Fraser was born in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario).[^o4] She received her Ph.D. from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1998 under the supervision of [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen).[^o4][^o7] After postdoctoral studies at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), she taught at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) before moving to UBC.[^o4]

## Major work {#Major_work}

Fraser is well-known for her 2011 work with Schoen on the first "Steklov eigenvalue" of a compact Riemannian manifold-with-boundary. This is defined as the minimal nonzero eigenvalue of the "Dirichlet to Neumann" operator which sends a function on the boundary to the normal derivative of its harmonic extension into the interior. In the two-dimensional case, Fraser and Schoen were able to adapt [Paul Yang](wikipedia://en/45633093) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137)'s use of the Hersch trick in order to approximate the product of the first Steklov eigenvalue with the length of the boundary from above, by topological data.[^o9][^o10]

Under an [ansatz](wikipedia://en/1359360) of rotational symmetry, Fraser and Schoen carefully analyzed the case of an annulus, showing that the metric optimizing the above eigenvalue-length product is obtained as the intrinsic geometry of a geometrically meaningful part of the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329). By use of the [uniformization theorem](wikipedia://en/288270) for surfaces with boundary, they were able to remove the condition of rotational symmetry, replacing it by certain weaker conditions; however, they conjectured that their result should be unconditional.

In general dimensions, Fraser and Schoen developed a "boundary" version of [Peter Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Li_%28mathematician%29) and Yau's "conformal volume."[^o11] By building upon some of Li and Yau's arguments, they gave lower bounds for the first Steklov eigenvalue in terms of conformal volumes, in addition to [isoperimetric inequalities](wikipedia://en/326182) for certain minimal surfaces of the unit ball.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Fraser won the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) in 2012[^o4] and became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2013.[^o13] In 2018 the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) listed her in their inaugural class of fellows[^o14] and in 2021 awarded her, along with [Marco Gualtieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Gualtieri), the [Cathleen Synge Morawetz](wikipedia://en/1254108) Prize.[^o15] In 2022 she was awarded a [Simons Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Foundation).[^o16]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Fraser is the daughter of statistician [Donald A. S. Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_A._S._Fraser).[^o18]
