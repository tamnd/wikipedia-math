# Michael Hutchings (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Hutchings"}

**Michael Lounsbery Hutchings** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o3] He is known for proving the [double bubble conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bubble_conjecture) on the shape of two-chambered [soap bubbles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_bubble),[^o4] and for his work on [circle-valued Morse theory](wikipedia://en/3313648) and on [embedded contact homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_contact_homology), which he defined.

:::

## Career {#Career}

As an undergraduate student at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), Hutchings did an [REU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Experiences_for_Undergraduates) project with [Frank Morgan](wikipedia://en/20185599) at [Williams College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_College) that began his interest in the mathematics of soap bubbles.[^o6] He finished his undergraduate studies in 1993, and stayed at Harvard for graduate school, earning his Ph.D. in 1998 under the supervision of [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920). After postdoctoral and visiting positions at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in [Bonn, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn,_Germany), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), he joined the UC Berkeley faculty in 2001.

His work on circle-valued Morse theory (partly in collaboration with Yi-Jen Lee) studies torsion invariants that arise from circle-valued Morse theory and, more generally, [closed 1-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms), and relates them to the three-dimensional [Seiberg–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/14407762) and the Meng–Taubes theorem, in analogy with Taubes' [Gromov](wikipedia://en/3008788)–Seiberg–Witten theorem in four dimensions.

The main body of his work involves [embedded contact homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_contact_homology), or ECH. ECH is a holomorphic curve model for the [Seiberg–Witten Floer homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_Floer_homology) of a three-manifold, and is thus a version of Taubes's Gromov invariant for certain four-manifolds with boundary. Ideas connected to ECH were important in Taubes's proof of the [Weinstein conjecture](wikipedia://en/4120135) for three-manifolds. Embedded contact homology has now been proven to be isomorphic to both [monopole Floer homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopole_Floer_homology) (Kutluhan–Lee–Taubes) and [Heegaard Floer homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heegaard_Floer_homology) (Colin–Ghiggini–Honda). Hutchings has also introduced a sequence of [symplectic capacities](wikipedia://en/23825035#Symplectic_capacaties) known as ECH capacities, which have applications to embedding problems for [Liouville domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville_domain).

He won a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 2003.[^o8] He gave an [invited talk](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2010, entitled "Embedded contact homology and its applications". In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]
