# Walk-regular graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walk-regular graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **walk-regular graph** is a [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) where the number of closed walks of any length $\ell$ from a vertex to itself does only depend on $\ell$ but not depend on the choice of vertex.[^o2] Walk-regular graphs can be thought of as a [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914) analogue of [vertex-transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/581175). While a walk-regular graph is not necessarily very [symmetric](wikipedia://en/15094186), all its vertices still behave identically with respect to the graph's spectral properties.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definitions"}

## Equivalent definitions {#Equivalent_definitions}

Suppose that $G$ is a simple graph. Let $A$ denote the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of $G$, $V(G)$ denote the set of vertices of $G$, and $\Phi_{G - v}(x)$ denote the characteristic polynomial of the vertex-deleted subgraph $G - v$ for all $v \in V(G).$Then the following are equivalent:

- $G$ is walk-regular.
- $A^k$ is a constant-diagonal matrix for all $k \geq 0.$
- $\Phi_{G - u}(x) = \Phi_{G - v}(x)$ for all $u, v \in V(G).$

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [vertex-transitive graphs](wikipedia://en/581175) are walk-regular.[^o7]
- The [distance-regular graphs](wikipedia://en/7768943) are walk-regular.[^o2] More generally, any simple graph in a homogeneous [coherent algebra](wikipedia://en/55268440) is walk-regular.
- A connected [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) is walk-regular if it has at most four distinct [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A walk-regular graph is necessarily a regular graph, since the number of closed walks of length two starting at any vertex is twice the vertex's degree.[^o2]
- [Complements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_complement) of walk-regular graphs are walk-regular. [^o10]
- [Cartesian products](wikipedia://en/2856852) of walk-regular graphs are walk-regular.[^o10]
- [Categorical products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_graphs) of walk-regular graphs are walk-regular.[^o10]
- [Strong products](wikipedia://en/11494014) of walk-regular graphs are walk-regular. [^o10]
- In general, the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of a walk-regular graph is not walk-regular.

:::

## *k*-walk-regular graphs {#k-walk-regular_graphs}

A graph is **$k$-walk-regular** if for any two vertices $v$ and $w$ of [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) at most $k,$ the number of walks of length $\ell$ from $v$ to $w$ depends only on $k$ and $\ell$.[^o2][^o12]

The class of $0$-walk-regular graphs is exactly the class of walk-regular graphs

In analogy to walk-regular graphs generalizing vertex-transitive graphs, 1-walk-regular graphs can be thought of as generalizing [symmetric graphs](wikipedia://en/1577896), that is, graphs that are both vertex- and edge-transitive. For example, the [Hoffman graph](wikipedia://en/24211555) is 1-walk-regular but not symmetric.

If $k$ is at least the [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) of the graph, then the $k$-walk-regular graphs coincide with the [distance-regular graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance-regular_graphs). In fact, if $k\ge 2$ and the graph has an eigenvalue of multiplicity at most $k$ (except for eigenvalues $d$ and $-d$, where $d$ is the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of the graph), then the graph is already distance-regular.[^o13]
