# Riemann–Roch-type theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemann–Roch-type theorem"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), there are various generalizations of the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261); among the most famous is the [Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/1532606), which is further generalized by the formulation due to [Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149) et al.

:::

## Formulation due to Baum, Fulton and MacPherson {#Formulation_due_to_Baum,_Fulton_and_MacPherson}

Let $G_*$ and $A_*$ be [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) on the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *C* of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_scheme) and [locally of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_of_finite_type) over the base field *k* with [proper morphisms](wikipedia://en/603782) such that

- $G_*(X)$ is the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of [coherent sheaves](wikipedia://en/603780) on *X*,
- $A_*(X)$ is the rational [Chow group](wikipedia://en/4282051) of *X*,
- for each proper morphism *f*, $G_*(f), A_*(f)$ are the [direct images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_image_sheaf) (or push-forwards) along *f*.

Also, if $f: X \to Y$ is a (global) [local complete intersection morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_complete_intersection_morphism); i.e., it factors as a closed regular embedding $X \hookrightarrow P$ into a [smooth scheme](wikipedia://en/35033819) *P* followed by a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041) $P \to Y$, then let

$$T_f = [T_{P/Y}|_X] - [N_{X/P}]$$

be the class in the Grothendieck group of vector bundles on *X*; it is independent of the factorization and is called the [virtual tangent bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_tangent_bundle) of *f*.

Then the Riemann–Roch theorem then amounts to the construction of a unique [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022):[^o3]

$$\tau: G_* \to A_*$$

between the two functors such that for each scheme *X* in *C*, the [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $\tau_X : G(X) \to A(X)$ satisfies: for a local complete intersection morphism $f: X \to Y$, when there are closed embeddings $X \subset M, Y \subset P$ into smooth schemes,

$$\tau_X f^* = \operatorname{td}(T_f) \cdot f^* \tau_Y$$

where $\operatorname{td}$ refers to the [Todd class](wikipedia://en/1568489).

Moreover, it has the properties:

- $\tau_X(\beta \otimes \alpha) = \operatorname{ch}(\beta) \tau(\alpha)$ for each $\alpha \in G_*(X)$ and the [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) $\operatorname{ch}(\beta)$ (or the action of it) of the $\beta$ in the Grothendieck group of vector bundles on *X*.
- it *X* is a closed [subscheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscheme) of a smooth scheme *M*, then the theorem is (roughly) the restriction of the theorem in the smooth case and can be written down in terms of a [localized Chern class](wikipedia://en/61622110).

## The equivariant Riemann–Roch theorem {#The_equivariant_Riemann–Roch_theorem}

Over the complex numbers, the theorem is (or can be interpreted as) a special case of the [equivariant index theorem](wikipedia://en/35112066).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Riemann–Roch theorem for Deligne–Mumford stacks"}

## The Riemann–Roch theorem for Deligne–Mumford stacks {#The_Riemann–Roch_theorem_for_Deligne–Mumford_stacks}

Aside from [algebraic spaces](wikipedia://en/2394027), no straightforward generalization is possible for [stacks](wikipedia://en/13185596). The complication already appears in the [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) case ([Kawasaki's Riemann–Roch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki's_Riemann–Roch)).

The equivariant Riemann–Roch theorem for finite groups is equivalent in many situations to the Riemann–Roch theorem for [quotient stacks](wikipedia://en/40579087) by finite groups.

One of the significant applications of the theorem is that it allows one to define a [virtual fundamental class](wikipedia://en/50529391) in terms of the *K*-theoretic virtual fundamental class.

:::
