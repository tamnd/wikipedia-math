# Jonathan Pila

::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Jonathan Pila"}

**Jonathan Solomon Pila** (born 1962)[^o2] is an Australian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). He was awarded a BSc from the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne) in 1984, and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1988, the latter for research supervised by [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak).[^o3] His dissertation was entitled "Frobenius Maps of Abelian Varieties and Finding Roots of Unity in Finite Fields". In 2010, he received an MA from Oxford.[^o6]

:::

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Research interests lie in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). A focus has been applying the [theory of o-minimality](wikipedia://en/30865852) to [Diophantine problems](wikipedia://en/9109). This work began with an early paper with [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475), and developed through collaborations with [Alex Wilkie](wikipedia://en/10343119) and [Umberto Zannier](wikipedia://en/58056511). The techniques obtained have led to advances in Diophantine problems, including Pila's unconditional proof of the [André–Oort conjecture](wikipedia://en/32262980) for powers of the modular curve.[^o4] Work by Pila and [Jacob Tsimerman](wikipedia://en/48497888), demonstrated the André–Oort conjecture in the case of the [Siegel modular variety](wikipedia://en/7721927).[^o8]

Pila has held posts at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) and (as a visiting member) the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). Pila also took a substantial break from professional mathematics to work in his family's manufacturing business.[^o4]

Pila has been the Editor of Proceedings of the *[Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940)*, and of *[Algebra and Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_and_Number_Theory)*.[^o6]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Pila was awarded a Clay Research Award for his work on the Andre-Oort conjecture in 2011[^o10]. In June 2011, he was awarded the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o11] This prize is "awarded in recognition of work in and influence on and service to mathematics; or lecturing gifts."[^o11] Specifically, the citation recognized "his startling recent work on the Andre-Oort and [Manin-Mumford conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manin-Mumford_conjecture). The approach he and his collaborators have developed, which combines analytic ideas with [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), is entirely new and shows great promise for further applications."[^o11]

In addition to the Clay and London Mathematical Society awards, Pila delivered the [Arf Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahit_Arf#Arf_Lectures) in 2011, was awarded the Leverhulme Trust Research Fellowship 2008–2010.[^o6] and received the [Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize) in 2013.[^o12] Pila was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2015).[^o4] In 2022, he received the [Rolf Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Schock_Prizes) in the category of "Mathematics".[^o13]
