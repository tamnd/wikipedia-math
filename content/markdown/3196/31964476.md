# Peter Henrici (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Henrici"}

**Peter Karl Henrici** (13 September 1923 – 13 March 1987) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his contributions to the field of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Henrici was born in [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel) and studied law for two years at [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel). After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he transferred to [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) where he received a [diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) (1948) and a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science#Germany,_Austria,_Switzerland) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) with [Eduard Stiefel](wikipedia://en/12223876) as his advisor (1952).

### Moving to the USA {#Moving_to_the_USA}

In 1951 he moved to the United States and worked on a joint contract with [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University) and the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards). Then, from 1956 to 1962, he taught at [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) where he became a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor). In 1962 he returned to ETH Zürich as a professor, a position he kept for the rest of his life, though he also held a part-time appointment as [William R. Kenan, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_R._Kenan,_Jr.) Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) from 1985.[^o5]

### Numerical analyst {#Numerical_analyst}

Henrici was an internationally recognized numerical analyst who published 11 books and more than 80 research papers within his field. Henrici was also a gifted pianist and a highly regarded teacher.[^o5] He was an editor of a number of scientific journals, including *[Numerische Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerische_Mathematik)* and *[Zeitschrift für Angewandte Mathematik und Physik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_für_Angewandte_Mathematik_und_Physik)*. In 1962, he was a speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), and in 1978 he gave the [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) John von Neumann Lecture.[^o6]

## Peter Henrici Prize {#Peter_Henrici_Prize}

Every four years since 1999, the [Peter Henrici Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Henrici_Prize) is awarded by ETH Zürich and SIAM for "original contributions to applied analysis and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and/or for exposition appropriate for applied mathematics and scientific computing".[^o9]
