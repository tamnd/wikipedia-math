# Real rank (C\*-algebras)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real rank"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **real rank** of a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) is a [noncommutative](wikipedia://en/662624) analogue of [Lebesgue covering dimension](wikipedia://en/398886). The notion was first introduced by [Lawrence G. Brown](wikipedia://en/48948022) and [Gert K. Pedersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gert_K._Pedersen).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The real rank of a unital C\*-algebra *A* is the smallest non-negative integer *n*, denoted RR(*A*), such that for every (*n* + 1)-tuple (*x*<sub>0</sub>, *x*<sub>1</sub>, ... ,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>) of [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) elements of *A* and every *ε* > 0, there exists an (*n* + 1)-tuple (*y*<sub>0</sub>, *y*<sub>1</sub>, ... ,*y*<sub>*n*</sub>) of self-adjoint elements of *A* such that $\sum_{i=0}^n y_i^2$ is [invertible](wikipedia://en/45199) and $\lVert \sum_{i=0}^n (x_i - y_i)^2 \rVert < \varepsilon$. If no such integer exists, then the real rank of *A* is infinite. The real rank of a non-unital C\*-algebra is defined to be the real rank of its [unitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitalization).

:::

## Comparisons with dimension {#Comparisons_with_dimension}

If *X* is a [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637), then RR(*C*<sub>0</sub>(*X*)) = dim(*X*), where dim is the Lebesgue covering dimension of *X*. As a result, real rank is considered a noncommutative generalization of dimension, but real rank can be rather different when compared to dimension. For example, most [noncommutative tori](wikipedia://en/37990843) have real rank zero, despite being a noncommutative version of the two-dimensional [torus](wikipedia://en/74800). For locally compact Hausdorff spaces, being [zero-dimensional](wikipedia://en/517682) is equivalent to being [totally disconnected](wikipedia://en/303356). The analogous relationship fails for C\*-algebras; while [AF-algebras](wikipedia://en/17936142) have real rank zero, the converse is false. Formulas that hold for dimension may not generalize for real rank. For example, Brown and Pedersen conjectured that RR(*A* ⊗ *B*) ≤ RR(*A*) + RR(*B*), since it is true that dim(*X* × *Y*) ≤ dim(*X*) + dim(*Y*). They proved a special case that if *A* is AF and *B* has real rank zero, then *A* ⊗ *B* has real rank zero. But in general their conjecture is false, there are C\*-algebras *A* and *B* with real rank zero such that *A* ⊗ *B* has real rank greater than zero.[^o6]

## Real rank zero {#Real_rank_zero}

C\*-algebras with real rank zero are of particular interest. By definition, a unital C\*-algebra has real rank zero if and only if the invertible self-adjoint elements of *A* are [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in the self-adjoint elements of *A*. This condition is equivalent to the previously studied conditions:

- (FS) The self-adjoint elements of *A* with finite [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) are dense in the self-adjoint elements of *A*.
- (HP) Every [hereditary C\*-subalgebra](wikipedia://en/13437009) of *A* has an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628) consisting of [projections](wikipedia://en/519182#Orthogonal_projections).

This equivalence can be used to give many examples of C\*-algebras with real rank zero including [AW\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/39435930), [Bunce–Deddens algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunce–Deddens_algebra),[^o8] and [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185). More broadly, [simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_algebra) unital [purely infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purely_infinite) C\*-algebras have real rank zero including the [Cuntz algebras](wikipedia://en/4463325) and [Cuntz–Krieger algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuntz–Krieger_algebra). Since simple [graph C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/46697691) are either AF or purely infinite, every simple graph C\*-algebra has real rank zero.

Having real rank zero is a property closed under taking [direct limits](wikipedia://en/211566), hereditary C\*-subalgebras, and [strong Morita equivalence](wikipedia://en/3296134#Further_directions). In particular, if *A* has real rank zero, then *M*<sub>*n*</sub>(*A*), the algebra of *n* × *n* matrices over *A*, has real rank zero for any integer *n* ≥ 1.
