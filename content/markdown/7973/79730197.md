# Randomised non-comparative trial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Randomised non-comparative trial"}

A **randomised non-comparative trial**, RNCT (or also non-comparative randomised trial[^o2]), is a type of [clinical trial](wikipedia://en/241717) where participants are randomised to different conditions (arms), but where the primary analysis involves comparing each arm separately to a historical control, predefined benchmark or something else, with no formal comparison between the two arms.[^o3]

:::

The study design appears to have arisen in [oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology), where single-arm studies are not unusual. It promises reduced [sample size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_size) requirements.[^o4] An RNCT acts like multiple single-arm designs run concurrently.[^o3] A review found RNCTs dating back to 2002, and having been used in high-profile oncology studies and also beyond oncology.[^o4]

The design has been criticised by statisticians. It is unclear what benefit randomisation adds compared to running separate single-arm studies.[^o4] Pavlos Msaouel described the use of the word "randomisation" as merely being "talismanic" in a 2025 article, it leading readers to think the study has the benefits of a [randomised controlled trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomised_controlled_trial) (RCT).[^o3] While the point of an RNCT is not to have a comparison between arms, about half of reported RNCTs still reported some sort of comparison.[^o4]
