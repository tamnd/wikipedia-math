# Gheorghe Vrănceanu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gheorghe Vrănceanu"}

**Gheorghe Vrănceanu** (June 30, 1900 – April 27, 1979) was a Romanian mathematician, best known for his work in [differential geometry and topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry_and_topology).[^o3] He was [titular member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) and vice-president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in 1900 in [Valea Hogei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipova,_Bacău), then a village in [Vaslui County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaslui_County#Historical_county), now a component of [Lipova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipova,_Bacău) commune, in [Bacău County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacău_County). He was the eldest of five children in his family.[^o8] After attending primary school in his village and high school in [Vaslui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaslui), he went to study mathematics at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University) in 1919. There, he took courses with Alexandru Myller, [Vera Myller](wikipedia://en/59097885), Simion Sanielevici, [Victor Vâlcovici](wikipedia://en/45710371), and [Simion Stoilow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Stoilow). After graduating in 1922, he went in 1923 to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he studied under [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). Thereafter, he went to the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), where he studied under [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), obtaining his doctorate on November 5, 1924, with thesis *Sopra una teorema di Weierstrass e le sue applicazioni alla stabilità*. The thesis defense committee was composed of 11 faculty, and was headed by [Vito Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Volterra).[^o5]

Vrănceanu returned to [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași), where he was appointed a lecturer at the university. In 1927–1928, he was awarded a [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) scholarship to study in France and the United States, where he was in a contact with [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) and [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen).[^o7] In 1929, he returned to Romania, and was appointed professor at the [University of Cernăuți](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chernivtsi). In 1939, he moved to the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), where he was appointed Head of the Geometry and Topology department in 1948, a position he held until his retirement in 1970. His doctoral students include [Henri Moscovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Moscovici) and Kostake Teleman.[^o6]

Vrănceanu was elected to the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) as a corresponding member in 1946, then as a full member in 1955. From 1964 he was president of the Mathematics Section of the Romanian Academy. Also from 1964, he was an editor of the journal *[Revue Roumaine de mathématiques pures et appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revue_Roumaine_de_mathématiques_pures_et_appliquées)*, founded that year. At the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) held in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), Canada in 1974, he was elected vice-president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), a position he held from 1975 to 1978. He died in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest) in 1979 of an [intestinal obstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowel_obstruction) and was buried at the city's [Bellu Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellu_Cemetery).[^o8]

A high school in [Bacău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacău) (Colegiul Național "Gheorghe Vrânceanu") is named after him, and so is a school in [Lipova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipova,_Bacău).[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

During his career, Vrănceanu published over 300 articles in journals throughout the world. His work covers a whole range of modern [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), from the classical theory of surfaces, to the notion of non-[holonomic](wikipedia://en/9777067) spaces, which he discovered.

In 1928 he gave an [invited talk](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna), titled *Parallelisme et courbure dans une variété non holonome*. In it, he introduced the notion of "non-holonomic manifolds," which are [smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) provided with a [smooth distribution](wikipedia://en/2976052) that is generally not [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213).
