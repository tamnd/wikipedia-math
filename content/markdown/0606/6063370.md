# Torsten Carleman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Torsten Carleman"}

**Torsten Carleman** (8 July 1892, Visseltofta, [Osby Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osby_Municipality) – 11 January 1949, [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm)), born **Tage Gillis Torsten Carleman**, was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his results in [classical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_analysis) and its applications. As the director of the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) for more than two decades, Carleman was the most influential mathematician in Sweden.

:::

## Work {#Work}

The dissertation of Carleman under [Erik Albert Holmgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Albert_Holmgren), as well as his work in the early 1920s, was devoted to [singular integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_equations). He developed the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [integral operators](wikipedia://en/2649421) with *Carleman kernels*, that is, [kernels](wikipedia://en/430912) *K*(*x*, *y*) such that *K*(*y*, *x*) = *K*(*x*, *y*) for [almost every](wikipedia://en/44688) (*x*, *y*), and

$$\int | K(x, y) |^2 dy < \infty$$

for almost every *x*.[^o4][^o5]

In the mid-1920s, Carleman developed the theory of [quasi-analytic functions](wikipedia://en/22385007). He proved the necessary and sufficient condition for quasi-analyticity, now called the [Denjoy–Carleman theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denjoy–Carleman_theorem).[^o6] As a corollary, he obtained a [sufficient condition](wikipedia://en/9442947) for the determinacy of the [moment problem](wikipedia://en/1244992).[^o7] As one of the steps in the proof of the Denjoy–Carleman theorem in (Carleman 1926), he introduced the [Carleman inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleman_inequality)

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left(a_1 a_2 \cdots a_n\right)^{1/n} \le e \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n,$$

valid for any sequence of non-negative real numbers *a*<sub>*k*</sub>.[^o9]

At about the same time, he established the *[Carleman formulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleman_formulae)* in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), which reconstruct an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) in a domain from its values on a subset of the boundary. He also proved a generalisation of [Jensen's formula](wikipedia://en/6054639), now called the [Jensen–Carleman formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jensen–Carleman_formula).[^o10]

In the 1930s, independently of [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), he discovered the [mean ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_ergodic_theorem).[^o11] Later, he worked in the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), where he introduced the *[Carleman estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleman_estimates)*,[^o12] and found a way to study the spectral asymptotics of [Schrödinger operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator).[^o13]

In 1932, following the work of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), [Erik Ivar Fredholm](wikipedia://en/669963), and [Bernard Koopman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Koopman), he devised the *[Carleman embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleman_embedding)* (also called *Carleman linearization*), a way to embed a finite-dimensional system of nonlinear differential equations $\tfrac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt}$ = **P**(**u**) for **u**: **R**<sup>*k*</sup> → **R**, where the components of **P** are polynomials in **u**, into an infinite-dimensional system of linear differential equations.[^o14][^o15]

In 1933 Carleman published a short proof of what is now called the [Denjoy–Carleman–Ahlfors theorem](wikipedia://en/41750415).[^o16] This theorem states that the number of asymptotic values attained by an [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) of order ρ along curves in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) going outwards toward infinite absolute value is less than or equal to 2ρ.

In 1935, Torsten Carleman introduced a generalisation of [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247), which foreshadowed the work of [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336) on [hyperfunctions](wikipedia://en/1678626);[^o17] his notes were published in (Carleman 1944). He considered the functions *f* of at most polynomial growth, and showed that every such function can be decomposed as *f* = *f*<sub>\+</sub> + *f*<sub>−</sub>, where *f*<sub>\+</sub> and *f*<sub>−</sub> are analytic in the upper and lower half planes, respectively, and that this representation is essentially unique. Then he defined the Fourier transform of (*f*<sub>\+</sub>, *f*<sub>−</sub>) as another such pair (*g*<sub>\+</sub>, *g*<sub>−</sub>). Though conceptually different, the definition coincides with the one given later by [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) for [tempered distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29#Tempered_distributions_and_Fourier_transform).[^o17] Carleman's definition gave rise to numerous extensions.[^o17][^o19]

Returning to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) in the 1930s, Carleman gave the first proof of global existence for [Boltzmann's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann's_equation) in the [kinetic theory](wikipedia://en/64204) of gases (his result applies to the space-homogeneous case).[^o20] The results were published posthumously in (Carleman 1957).

Carleman supervised the Ph.D. theses of Ulf Hellsten, Karl Persson (Dagerholm), [Åke Pleijel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Åke_Pleijel) and (jointly with [Fritz Carlson](wikipedia://en/5094208)) of [Hans Rådström](wikipedia://en/34034780).

## Life {#Life}

Carleman was born in [Visseltofta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visseltofta) to Alma Linnéa Jungbeck and Karl Johan Carleman, a school teacher.[^o10] He studied at [Växjö Cathedral School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katedralskolan,_Växjö), graduating in 1910.

He continued his studies at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University), being one of the active members of the Uppsala Mathematical Society. Kjellberg recalls:

> He was a genius! My older friends in Uppsala used to tell me about the wonderful years they had had when Carleman was there. He was the most active speaker in the Uppsala Mathematical Society and a well-trained gymnast. When people left the seminar crossing the [Fyris River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyris_River), he walked on his hands on the railing of the bridge.[^o23]

From 1917 he was docent at Uppsala University, and from 1923 — a full professor at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University). In 1924 he was appointed professor at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University). He was elected a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1926 and of the [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters) in 1934.[^o24] From 1927, he was director of the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) and editor of [Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708).[^o10]

From 1929 to 1946 Carleman was married to Anna-Lisa Lemming (1885–1954),[^o25] the half-sister[^o26] of the athlete [Eric Lemming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lemming) who won four golden medals and three bronze at the Olympic Games.[^o27] During this period he was also known as a recognized fascist, anti-semite and xenophobe. His interaction with [William Feller](wikipedia://en/206644) before the former departure to the United States was not particularly pleasant, at some point being reported due to his opinion that "Jews and foreigners should be [executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution)".[^o28]

Carlson remembers Carleman as: "secluded and taciturn, who looked at life and people with a bitter humour. In his heart, he was inclined to kindliness towards those around him, and strove to assist them swiftly."[^o10] Towards the end of his life, he remarked to his students that "professors ought to be shot at the age of fifty."[^o29]

During the last decades of his life, Carleman abused alcohol, according to [Norbert&nbsp;Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert&nbsp;Wiener)[^o30][^o32] and [William&nbsp;Feller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William&nbsp;Feller).[^o31] His final years were plagued by [neuralgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuralgia). At the end of 1948, he developed the liver disease [jaundice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaundice); he died from complications of the disease.[^o10][^o32]
