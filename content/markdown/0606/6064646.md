# Index case

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Index case"}

Patient zero

The **index case** or **patient zero** is the first documented [patient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient) in a [disease epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic) within a population,[^o2] or the first documented patient included in an [epidemiological](wikipedia://en/66997) study.[^o3] It can also refer to the first case of a condition or syndrome (not necessarily contagious) to be described in the medical literature, whether or not the patient is thought to be the first person affected.[^o7] An index case can achieve the status of a "classic" case study in the literature, as did [Phineas Gage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phineas_Gage), the first known person to exhibit a definitive personality change as a result of [traumatic brain injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traumatic_brain_injury).[^o4]

:::

## Term {#Term}

The index case may or may not indicate the source of the [disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease), the possible spread, or which reservoir holds the disease in between [outbreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outbreak), but may bring awareness of an emerging outbreak.[^o6][^o7] Earlier cases may or may not be found and are labeled primary or coprimary, secondary, tertiary, etc.[^o6] The term primary case can only apply to infectious diseases that spread from human to human, and refers to the person who first brings a disease into a group of people.[^o7] In epidemiology, the term is often used by both scientists and journalists alike to refer to the individual known or believed to have been the first infected or source of the resulting outbreak in a population as the index case, but such would technically refer to the primary case.[^o7]

### Origin of patient zero {#Origin_of_patient_zero}

"Patient zero" was used to refer to the supposed source of [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV) outbreak in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_the_United_States), flight attendant [Gaëtan Dugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaëtan_Dugas) in the popular press, but the term's use was based on a misunderstanding (and Dugas was not the index case).[^o10] In the 1984 study of [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) (CDC), one of the earliest recorded HIV-patients was code-named "patient O", which stands for "patient **o**ut of California". The letter *O*, however, was interpreted by some readers of the report as the numeral *0*. The designation *patient zero* (for Gaëtan Dugas) was subsequently propagated by the *[San Francisco Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Chronicle)* journalist [Randy Shilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Shilts) in his book *[And the Band Played On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_the_Band_Played_On)* in 1987. William Darrow, a CDC behavioral scientist responsible for figuring out why gay men in Los Angeles were dying of a strange illness, said: "That's correct. I never labeled him Patient Zero".[^o11]

The term has been expanded into general usage to refer to an individual identified as the first carrier of a communicable disease in a population (the primary case) or [pandemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic), or the first incident in the onset of a catastrophic trend.[^o12][^o13] In some cases, a known or suspected patient zero may be informally referred to as an index case for the purpose of a scientific study, such as the two-year-old boy in a remote village in [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea) who was thought to be the source of the [largest Ebola virus outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Ebola_virus_epidemic) in history,[^o3][^o14] or an unknown one, such as the [mysterious patient zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigations_into_the_origin_of_COVID-19) of [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19).[^o15][^o16]

In genetics, the index case is the case of the original patient (i.e. *propositus* or *[proband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proband)*) that stimulates investigation of other members of the family to discover a possible genetic factor.[^o17]

The term can also be used in non-medical fields to describe the first individual affected by something negative that since propagated to others, such as the first user on a network infected by [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware).[^o47]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Gaëtan Dugas {#Gaëtan_Dugas}

*Image: AIDS index case graph.svg.* A 1984 paper[^o21] linked 40 AIDS patients by sexual contact. Of those patients, Dugas was supposedly the first to experience an onset of symptoms of AIDS. In the above graph, Dugas is represented by the circle labeled 0, highlighted in red.

In the early years of the [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS) [epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic), a *patient zero* transmission scenario was compiled by [William Darrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Darrow) and colleagues at the United States [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) (CDC).[^o24] This epidemiological study showed how patient zero had infected multiple partners with [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV), and they, in turn, transmitted it to others causing rapid spread of the [virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus) to locations all over the world (Auerbach et al., 1984). The CDC identified [Gaëtan Dugas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaëtan_Dugas) as a carrier of the virus from Europe to the United States, who spread it to other men he had sexual contact with at [gay bathhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_bathhouse).[^o22]

Journalist [Randy Shilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Shilts) subsequently wrote about patient zero, based on Darrow's findings,[^o24] in his 1987 book *[And the Band Played On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_the_Band_Played_On)*, which identified patient zero as being Gaëtan Dugas.[^o23] Dugas was a flight attendant who was sexually promiscuous in several North American cities, according to Shilts' book. He was vilified for several years as a "mass spreader" of HIV, and was seen as the original source of the HIV epidemic among [homosexual men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_men). Four years later, Darrow repudiated the study's methodology and how Shilts had represented its conclusions.[^o24]

A 2007 study by Michael Worobey and Arthur Pitchenik published in the *[Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_United_States_of_America)* claimed that, based on the results of [genetic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_analysis), current North American strains of HIV probably moved from [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa) to [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti) before entering the United States around 1969,[^o25] probably through a single [immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant). However, a teenager named [Robert Rayford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rayford) died in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri), possibly of complications from AIDS in 1969, having most likely become infected with the virus *before* 1966. This would imply that there were prior carriers of HIV-strains in North America.[^o26][^o27]

The phrase patient zero is now used in the media to refer to the primary case for infectious disease outbreaks, as well as for computer virus outbreaks, and more broadly, as the source of ideas or actions that have far-reaching consequences.[^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31][^o32]

[David Heymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_L._Heymann), professor of Infectious Disease Epidemiology at the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine, and formerly with the [World Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Health_Organization) (*WHO*),[^o33] has questioned the importance of finding patient zero, stating, "Finding patient zero may be important in some instances, but only if they are still alive and spreading the disease; and more often than not, especially in large disease outbreaks, they're not."[^o34]

### Others {#Others}

- [Mary Mallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Mallon) ("Typhoid Mary") was an index case of a [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid) outbreak in the early 1900s. An apparently healthy carrier, she infected at least 47 people while working as a [cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_%28profession%29). She eventually was isolated to prevent her from spreading the disease to others.[^o36]
- The first recorded victim of [Ebola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola) was a 44-year-old schoolteacher named Mabalo Lokela, who died on 8 September 1976, 14 days after symptom onset.[^o37]
- 64-year-old [Liu Jianlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Jianlun), a [Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong) doctor, transmitted [SARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome) internationally by infecting other [super-spreaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-spreader) during a stay in the [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) Metropole Hotel in 2003.[^o38][^o39]
- A baby in the Lewis House at 40 Broad Street, named Frances Lewis, is considered the index patient in the [1854 cholera outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1854_Broad_Street_cholera_outbreak) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), England.[^o40]
- Édgar Enrique Hernández may be patient zero of the [2009 flu pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_flu_pandemic).[^o28] He recovered, and a bronze statue has been erected in his honor.[^o41] Maria Adela Gutierrez, who contracted the virus about the same time as Hernández, became the first officially confirmed fatality.
- One-year-old [Emile Ouamouno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Ouamouno) is believed to be patient zero in the 2014 Ebola epidemic in [Guinea and West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_epidemic_in_West_Africa).[^o42]
- 51-year-old Jesus Lujan was the index case of the [1924 Los Angeles pneumonic plague outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Los_Angeles_pneumonic_plague_outbreak) which killed 33.[^o43]
- There are many known "patient zeros" across the world for the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), known for different symptoms and stories. Out of Los Angeles, patient zero Gregg Garfield spent 64 days in the hospital, including 30 days of coma-state after contracting the virus on a ski trip. Doctors said he had a 1% chance to live. He survived, but had fingers and toes amputated.[^o44]
  - Another patient zero of the COVID-19 pandemic includes an elderly man who was diagnosed on 1 December 2019, someone who had no contact with the [Huanan Seafood Wholesale Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanan_Seafood_Wholesale_Market). Three other people experienced symptoms in the following days who also did not have contact with the Market.[^o45]

:::

## Non-medical usage {#Non-medical_usage}

The term is used to identify the first computer or user to be infected with [malware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware) on a network, which then infected other systems.[^o47][^o48]

[Monica Lewinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Lewinsky) has described herself as the "patient zero" of online harassment, meaning that she was the first person to receive widespread public harassment via the internet.[^o49]
