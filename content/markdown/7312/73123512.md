# Degree-Rips bifiltration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Degree-Rips bifiltration"}

The **degree-Rips bifiltration** is a [simplicial](wikipedia://en/252686) [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) used in [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009) for analyzing the shape of [point cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_cloud) data. It is a multiparameter extension of the [Vietoris–Rips](wikipedia://en/13056778) filtration that possesses greater stability to data [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951) than single-parameter filtrations, and which is more amenable to practical computation than other multiparameter constructions. Introduced in 2015 by Lesnick and Wright, the degree-Rips bifiltration is a parameter-free and density-sensitive vehicle for performing persistent [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) computations on point cloud data.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

It is standard practice in [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009) (TDA) to associate a sequence of nested [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686) to a finite data set in order to detect the persistence of [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) features over a range of scale parameters. One way to do this is by considering the sequence of [Vietoris–Rips complexes](wikipedia://en/13056778) of a finite set in a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) indexed over all scale parameters.

If $X$ is a finite set in a metric space, then this construction is known as the Vietoris–Rips (or simply "Rips") filtration on $X$, commonly denoted $\text{Rips}(X)$ or $\mathcal R (X)$.[^o5][^o6][^o7] The Rips filtration can be expressed as a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $\text{Rips}(X): \mathbb R \to \mathbf{Simp}$ from the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) (viewed as a [poset](wikipedia://en/23572) [category](wikipedia://en/53759)) to the category of [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686) and [simplicial maps](wikipedia://en/25738537), a [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) of the category $\mathbf{Top}$ of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) and [continuous maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_maps) via the [geometric realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_realization_functor) functor.[^o8]

The Rips filtration is indexed over a single parameter, but we can capture more information (e.g., density) about the underlying data set by considering multiparameter filtrations. A filtration indexed by the product of two [totally-ordered sets](wikipedia://en/30330) is known as a bifiltration, first introduced by [Gunnar Carlsson](wikipedia://en/35054518) and Afra Zomorodian in 2009.[^o9]

The degree-Rips bifiltration filters each simplicial complex in the Rips filtration by the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of each [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) in the [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the [1-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) at each index. More formally, let $(a,b)$ be an element of $\mathbb R^2$ and define $G_{a,b}$ to be the subgraph of the 1-skeleton of $\text{Rips}(X)_b$ containing all vertices whose degree is at least $a$. Subsequently building the maximal simplicial complex possible on this 1-skeleton, we obtain a complex $\text{D-Rips}(X)_{a,b}$. By doing this for all possible vertex degrees, and across all scale parameters in the Rips filtration, we extend the Rips construction to a bifiltration $\{ \text{D-Rips}(X)_{a,b}\}_{(a,b) \in \mathbb R^2}$.[^o2]

Note that since the size of each complex will decrease as $a$ increases, we should identify the indexing set $\mathbb R^2$ with $\mathbb R^{\text{op}}\times \mathbb R$, where $\mathbb R^{\text{op}}$ is the [opposite poset category](wikipedia://en/372399) of $\mathbb R$. Therefore the degree-Rips bifiltration can be viewed as a functor $\text{D-Rips}(X): \mathbb R^{\operatorname{op}}\times \mathbb R \to \mathbf{Simp}$.[^o10]

The idea behind the degree-Rips bifiltration is that vertices of higher degree will correspond to higher density regions of the underlying data set. However, since degree-Rips does not depend on an arbitrary choice of a parameter (such as a pre-selected density parameter, which is *a priori* difficult to determine), it is a convenient tool for analyzing data.[^o11]

:::

## Applications to data analysis {#Applications_to_data_analysis}

The degree-Rips bifiltration possesses several properties that make it a useful tool in data analysis. For example, each of its skeleta has [polynomial size](wikipedia://en/641995); the k-dimensional skeleton of $\text{D-Rips}(X)$ has $O(|X|^{k+2})$ simplices, where $O$ denotes an [asymptotic upper bound](wikipedia://en/44578).[^o10] Moreover, it has been shown that the degree-Rips bifiltration possesses reasonably strong stability properties with respect to perturbations of the underlying data set.[^o10] Further work has also been done examining the stable components and homotopy types of degree-Rips complexes.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

The software [RIVET](https://rivet.readthedocs.io/) was created in order to visualize several multiparameter invariants (i.e., data structures that attempt to capture underlying geometric information of the data) of 2-parameter persistence modules, including the persistent homology modules of the degree-Rips bifiltration. These invariants include the [Hilbert function](wikipedia://en/6612596), [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) invariant, and fibered barcode.[^o2]

As a follow-up to the introduction of degree-Rips in their original 2015 paper, Lesnick and Wright showed in 2022 that a primary component of persistent homology computations (namely, computing [minimal presentations](wikipedia://en/99494) and [bigraded](wikipedia://en/103094) [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262)) can be achieved efficiently in a way that outperforms other persistent homology software.[^o16] Methods of improving algorithmic efficiency of multiparameter persistent homology have also been explored that suggest the possibility of substantial speed increases for data analysis tools such as RIVET.[^o17]

The degree-Rips bifiltration has been used for analyzing [data clusters](wikipedia://en/669675) with respect to variations in density.[^o18][^o19][^o20] There has been some preliminary experimental analysis of the performance of degree-Rips with respect to outliers in particular, but this is an ongoing area of research as of February 2023.[^o21]
