# François Jaeger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="François Jaeger"}

**François Philippe Louis Jaeger** (14 December 1947 - 18 August 1997) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [matroid theory](wikipedia://en/244321), and [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Jaeger was born in [Boulogne-Billancourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulogne-Billancourt) to a father who was a doctor and a mother who was a pharmacist but did not practice in order to be able to take care of the education of her three children. According to his father, he was particularly interested in mathematics from an early age.[^o6]

In 1967, he was admitted to the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in Paris. In 1970, after graduating, he started a thesis in [Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenoble) with [Jean Kuntzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Kuntzmann) at the IMAG laboratory in the Algebra, Logic and Combinatorics team. This team later became the laboratory Logique, Structures Discrètes et Didactique (LSD2, Grenoble 1982-1995) and was subsequently integrated into the Leibniz laboratory (1995-2005). Jaeger also met frequently with mathematicians from the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) and from the Institut Fourier (Grenoble), in particular [Yves Colin de Verdière](wikipedia://en/5302248). Jaeger was hired by the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) as a permanent researcher in Grenoble in 1971, at the same time as his close colleague, collaborator and friend Charles Payan, and he spent his entire career in this organisation. He defended his PhD thesis "Study of some invariants and existence problems in graph theory"[^o7] on June 8, 1976 (supervised by [Jean Kuntzmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Kuntzmann) and Charles Payan).

Jaeger died on August 18, 1997 in [La Tronche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Tronche) from a [lung cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_cancer), at the age of 49.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Initially, most of his research themes had as their main subject [graphs](wikipedia://en/12401), [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535) and [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321), and were linked to famous and difficult problems in [Graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) and [nowhere-zero flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere-zero_flows), such as the [Four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949), the [Strong perfect graph theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_perfect_graph_conjecture), and [Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021)'s 3-flow,[^o10] 4-flow,[^o11] and 5-flow[^o12] conjectures. Gradually the research themes of Jaeger became more and more linked to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), to [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), with also an interest in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics) and [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511).

Jaeger is known for a number of conjectures in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) (see for instance the [Petersen coloring conjecture](wikipedia://en/194926#Petersen_coloring_conjecture)) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).[^o13] In addition to his research work, he wrote several surveys on various topics, including the [Cycle double cover conjecture](wikipedia://en/21486302),[^o14] [link invariants](wikipedia://en/1161784),[^o15] and [nowhere-zero flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere-zero_flows).[^o16]

## Honors {#Honors}

A year after his death, his colleagues organized a symposium[^o18] in his memory in Grenoble, attended by international experts in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555), [matroid theory](wikipedia://en/244321), and [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), including [Claude Berge](wikipedia://en/5893760), [Jack Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459), [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628), [Alexander Schrijver](wikipedia://en/35280549), [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708), [William Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021), and [Dominic Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Welsh). A 1998 volume of the Banach Center Publications ([Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences)) was dedicated to his memory[^o19] and a 1999 special volume of the [Annales de l'Institut Fourier](wikipedia://en/1424210) was also published in his honor.[^o20]
