# Masatake Kuranishi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Masatake Kuranishi"}

**Masatake Kuranishi** (倉西 正武 *Kuranishi Masatake*; July 19, 1924 – June 22, 2021)[^o2] was a Japanese mathematician who worked on [several complex variables](wikipedia://en/360243), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Kuranishi received in 1952 his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Nagoya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_University). He became a lecturer there in 1951, an associate professor in 1952, and a full professor in 1958.[^o4] From 1955 to 1956 he was a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).[^o5] From 1956 to 1961 he was a visiting professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He became a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in the summer of 1961.[^o4]

Kuranishi was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1962 at [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) with the talk *On deformations of compact complex structures*[^o6] and in 1970 at [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) with the talk *Convexity conditions related to 1/2 estimate on elliptic complexes*. He was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for the academic year 1975–1976.[^o7] In 2000 he received the [Stefan Bergman](wikipedia://en/9395687) Prize.[^o4] In 2014 he received the [Geometry Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_prize) of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786).

## Research {#Research}

Kuranishi and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) established the eponymous [Cartan–Kuranishi Theorem](wikipedia://en/5710861) on the continuation of exterior differential forms.[^o9] In 1962, based upon the work of [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326) and [Donald Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer), Kuranishi constructed locally complete deformations of compact complex manifolds.[^o10]

In 1982 he made important progress in the embedding problem for [CR manifolds](wikipedia://en/3131794) (Cauchy–Riemann structures).

In a series of deep papers published in 1982 \[Kur I,[^o11] II,[^o12] III[^o13]\], Kuranishi developed the theory of harmonic integrals on strongly pseudoconvex CR structures over small balls along the line developed by [D. C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer), [C. B. Morrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_B._Morrey_Jr.), [J. J. Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_J._Kohn) and [Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725). He considered a strongly pseudoconvex CR structure on a manifold of real dimension $2 n - 1$. In \[Kur I\], he established the [a priori estimate](wikipedia://en/30669047) for the [Neumann boundary problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumann_boundary_problem) on the complex associated with the structure, in the case the structure is induced by an embedding in $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ and restricted to a small ball of special type, provided $1 \le q \le n - 3$, where *q* is the degree of differential forms. In \[Kur II\], he developed the regularity theorem of solutions of the Neumann boundary problem based on the a priori estimate of \[Kur I\]. As a significant application of his deep theory, he proved in \[Kur III\] that, when $n \ge 5$, the structure is realized on a neighborhood of a reference point by an embedding in $\mathbb{C}^{n}$.[^o14]

Thus, by Kuranishi's work, in real dimension 9 and higher, local embedding of abstract CR structures is true and is also true in real dimension 7 by the work of Akahori.[^o15] A simplified presentation of Kuranishi's proof is due to Sidney Webster.[^o16] For $n = 2$ (*i.e.*, real dimension 3), Nirenberg published a counterexample. The local embedding problem remains open in real dimension 5.
