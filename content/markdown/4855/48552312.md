# Marguerite Lehr

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marguerite Lehr"}

**Marguerite Lehr** (October 22, 1898 – December 14, 1987) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who studied [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born on October 22, 1898, to Margaret Kreuter and George Lehr in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), Marguerite Lehr attended [Goucher College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goucher_College) for her undergraduate education and graduated with a bachelor's degree in 1919. After her undergraduate education, Lehr moved to Rome to study at the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) for the 1923–1924 academic year, funded by the [American Association of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Women) and the M. Carey Thomas University Fellowship. In 1925, Lehr earned her Ph.D. at [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College).[^o3][^o4]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After earning her doctorate, Lehr stayed at her alma mater, Bryn Mawr, as an instructor; she was promoted to associate lecturer in 1929, assistant professor in 1935, and associate professor in 1937. In 1955, she was made a full professor. While at Bryn Mawr, she had temporary or honorary appointments at several other universities. At [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), she was an honorary fellow from 1931 to 1932; she was also a visiting fellow at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1956 to 1957. In 1958 she was given a traveling lectureship by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) and the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), and was funded to give lectures across the country. She also worked with the MAA and [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) to produce educational films about mathematics, and conducted a televised lecture course on mathematics in 1953 and 1954.[^o3][^o4] While at Princeton, and for the following decade, she was a member of the [Woodrow Wilson Fellowship Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_Fellowship_Award) committee. She was also a member of the [International Federation of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_University_Women) awards committee. Lehr lectured at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) in the summer of 1944, and researched at the [Institut Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/10513595) in Paris in 1950.[^o3][^o4]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Lehr was honored by Goucher College for her work with a "distinguished citation" in 1954. She was a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society), the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), and the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o3][^o4]
