# Calogero–Moser–Sutherland model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calogero–Moser–Sutherland model"}

**Calogero–Moser–Sutherland models** (short **CMS models**) are a type of [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) one-dimensional [many-body systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_systems), which can be studied with exact results both from the [classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics) and [quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum) perspective. Such models describe pointlike particles on a line or a circle with quadratic or inverse quadratic interactions between them. CMS models are named after [Francesco Calogero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Calogero), [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316) and [Bill Sutherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Bill_Sutherland). Calogero, in 1971, first considered the quantum model on a line with both a quadratic and inverse quadratic interaction. He computed the [energy spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_spectrum) and described [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) [scattering](wikipedia://en/164483) without the quadratic term. Sutherland, also in 1971, further considered the quantum model on the circle and modified the inverse quadratic interaction to include the sine. He also computed the energy spectrum and developed an algorithm to obtain the corresponding eigenfunctions. Moser, later in 1975, proved the integrability of both systems using Lax pairs and solved the scattering problem. A [relativistic](wikipedia://en/30001) generalization of CMS models was later developed with [Ruijsenaars–Schneider models](wikipedia://en/80269892) (short RS models).[^o2]

:::

## Description {#Description}

For $N$ particles on the real line $\mathbb{R}$, the Hamiltonian of the CMS model is given by:[^o4]

$$H =\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^Np_i^2 +\frac{g^2}{m}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}V(x_i-x_j).$$

Different [potentials](wikipedia://en/23703) lead to different CMS models, which the four types most often considered being:[^o5]

- Type I/rational:

$$V(x) =\frac{1}{x^2}.$$

- Type II/hyperbolic:

$$V(x) =\frac{a^2}{4\sinh(ax/2)^2}, a>0.$$

- Type III/trigonometric:

$$V(x) =\frac{a^2}{4\sin(ax/2)^2}.$$

- Type IV/elliptic:

$$V(x) =\wp(x;\omega_1,\omega_2).$$
