# Kuratowski Prize

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kuratowski Prize"}

The **Kuratowski Prize** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Nagroda im. Kazimierza Kuratowskiego*) is a Polish annual [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) award conferred jointly by the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (PAN) and the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) (PTM) for contributions in the field of mathematics granted to individuals under the age of 30.[^o3] It is named in honour of Polish mathematician and logician [Kazimierz Kuratowski](wikipedia://en/46075) (1896–1980).

:::

## Description and history {#Description_and_history}

The prize was established in 1981 on the initiative of physician and politician [Zofia Kuratowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zofia_Kuratowska), who was personally the daughter of Kazimierz Kuratowski. It is presented annually by the [Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences](wikipedia://en/67042302) and the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791) (*Polskie Towarzystwo Matematyczne*). The Kuratowski Prize ceremony takes place during the scientific session of the Polish Mathematical Society and the laureate of the prize is invited to give a speech on a chosen subject. It is considered the most prestigious award for young mathematicians in Poland.[^o5]

In 2015, [Joanna Kułaga-Przymus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Kułaga-Przymus) became the first woman to be awarded the prize.[^o6] As of 2026, there have been two women mathematicians to receive this recognition, the other being [Agnieszka Hejna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agnieszka_Hejna) (2023).

Notable laureates of the prize have included two [Prize of the Foundation for Polish Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prize_of_the_Foundation_for_Polish_Science) winners: [Mariusz Lemańczyk](wikipedia://en/54594784) (1987) and [Tomasz Łuczak](wikipedia://en/32126054) (1997); [Erdős Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_Prize) winner [Wojciech Samotij](wikipedia://en/47871930) (2013); and [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791#Stefan_Banach_Prize) winner [Jerzy Weyman](wikipedia://en/75604598) (1984).
