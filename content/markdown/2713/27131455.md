# Multitree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multitree"}

*Image: Butterfly multitree.svg.* The [butterfly network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_network), a multitree used in distributed computation, showing in red the undirected tree induced by the subgraph reachable from one of its vertices.

In [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), a **multitree** may describe either of two equivalent structures: a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002) (DAG) in which there is at most one directed path between any two [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899), or equivalently in which the [subgraph](wikipedia://en/325802#subgraph) reachable from any vertex induces an [undirected tree](wikipedia://en/48560), or a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) (poset) that does not have four items $a$, $b$, $c$, and $d$ forming a diamond suborder with $a \le b \le d$ and $a \le c \le d$ but with $b$ and $c$ incomparable to each other (also called a **diamond-free poset**[^o2]).

:::

In [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), multitrees have also been called **strongly unambiguous graphs** or **mangroves**; they can be used to model [nondeterministic algorithms](wikipedia://en/665957) in which there is at most one computational path connecting any two states.[^o3]

Multitrees may be used to represent multiple overlapping [taxonomies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy_%28general%29) over the same ground set.[^o4] If a [family tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_tree) may contain multiple marriages from one family to another, but does not contain marriages between any two blood relatives, then it forms a multitree.[^o5]

## Equivalence between DAG and poset definitions {#Equivalence_between_DAG_and_poset_definitions}

In a directed acyclic graph, if there is at most one directed path between any two vertices, or equivalently if the subgraph reachable from any vertex induces an undirected tree, then its [reachability](wikipedia://en/2833097) relation is a diamond-free partial order. Conversely, in a diamond-free partial order, the [transitive reduction](wikipedia://en/3757117) identifies a directed acyclic graph in which the subgraph reachable from any vertex induces an undirected tree.

## Diamond-free families {#Diamond-free_families}

A diamond-free [family of sets](wikipedia://en/723043) is a family *F* of sets whose inclusion ordering forms a diamond-free poset. If *D*(*n*) denotes the largest possible diamond-free family of subsets of an *n*-element set, then it is known that

$$2\le \lim_{n\to\infty} D(n) \Big/ \binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\le 2\frac{3}{11},$$

and it is conjectured that the limit is 2.[^o2]

## Related structures {#Related_structures}

A [polytree](wikipedia://en/1731488), a directed acyclic graph formed by [orienting](wikipedia://en/36619818) the edges of an undirected tree, is a special case of a multitree.

The subgraph reachable from any vertex in a multitree is an [arborescence](wikipedia://en/13251077) rooted in the vertex, that is a polytree in which all edges are oriented away from the root.

The word "multitree" has also been used to refer to a [series–parallel partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series–parallel_partial_order),[^o9] or to other structures formed by combining multiple trees.
