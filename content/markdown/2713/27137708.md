# Mumford–Tate group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mumford–Tate group"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Mumford–Tate group** (or **Hodge group**) *MT*(*F*) constructed from a [Hodge structure](wikipedia://en/10297305) *F* is a certain [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G*. When *F* is given by a [rational representation](wikipedia://en/5784135) of an [algebraic torus](wikipedia://en/504697), the definition of *G* is as the [Zariski closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_closure) of the image in the representation of the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177), over the [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers). (txt) introduced Mumford–Tate groups over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) under the name of Hodge groups. (Serre 1967) introduced the *p*-adic analogue of Mumford's construction for [Hodge–Tate modules](wikipedia://en/34197327), using the work of (txt) on [p-divisible groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-divisible_group), and named them Mumford–Tate groups.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

The algebraic torus *T* used to describe Hodge structures has a concrete matrix representation, as the 2×2 invertible matrices of the shape that is given by the action of *a*+*bi* on the basis {1,*i*} of the complex numbers **C** over **R**:

$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ -b & a \end{bmatrix}.$$

The circle group inside this group of matrices is the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) *U*(1).

Hodge structures arising in geometry, for example on the [cohomology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_group) of [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538), have a [lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_%28group_theory%29) consisting of the integral cohomology classes. Not quite so much is needed for the definition of the Mumford–Tate group, but it does assume that the vector space *V* underlying the Hodge structure has a given rational structure, i.e. is given over the rational numbers *Q*. For the purposes of the theory the complex vector space *V*<sub>*C*</sub>, obtained by extending the scalars of *V* from *Q* to *C*, is used.

The weight *k* of the Hodge structure describes the action of the diagonal matrices of *T*, and *V* is supposed therefore to be homogeneous of weight *k*, under that action. Under the action of the full group *V*<sub>*C*</sub> breaks up into subspaces *V*<sup>*pq*</sup>, complex conjugate in pairs under switching *p* and *q*. Thinking of the matrix in terms of the complex number λ it represents, *V*<sup>*pq*</sup> has the action of λ by the *p*th power and of the complex conjugate of λ by the *q*th power. Here necessarily

*p* + *q* = *k*.

In more abstract terms, the torus *T* underlying the matrix group is the [Weil restriction](wikipedia://en/361168) of the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742) *GL*(1), from the complex field to the real field, an algebraic torus whose character group consists of the two homomorphisms to *GL*(1), interchanged by complex conjugation.

Once formulated in this fashion, the rational representation ρ of *T* on *V* setting up the Hodge structure *F* determines the image ρ(*U*(1)) in *GL*(*V*<sub>*C*</sub>); and *MT*(*F*) is by definition the smallest algebraic group defined over *Q* containing this image.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Mumford–Tate conjecture"}

## Mumford–Tate conjecture {#Mumford–Tate_conjecture}

The original context for the formulation of the group in question was the question of the [Galois representation](wikipedia://en/486929) on the [Tate module](wikipedia://en/3094921) of an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *A*. Conjecturally, the image of such a Galois representation, which is an [l-adic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic) Lie group for a given prime number *l*, is determined by the corresponding Mumford–Tate group *G* (coming from the Hodge structure on *H*<sup>1</sup>(*A*)), to the extent that knowledge of *G* determines the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the Galois image. This conjecture is known only in particular cases.[^o10] Through generalisations of this conjecture, the Mumford–Tate group has been connected to the [motivic Galois group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_Galois_group), and, for example, the general issue of extending the [Sato&ndash;Tate conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sato&ndash;Tate_conjecture) (now a theorem).

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Period conjecture"}

## Period conjecture {#Period_conjecture}

A related conjecture on abelian varieties states that the [period matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_matrix) of *A* over number field has [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree), in the sense of the field generated by its entries, predicted by the dimension of its Mumford–Tate group, as in the previous section. Work of [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) has shown that the dimension bounds the transcendence degree; so that the Mumford–Tate group catches sufficiently many algebraic relations between the periods. This is a special case of the full Grothendieck period conjecture.[^o13][^o14]

:::
