# Positive linear functional

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Positive linear functional"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **positive linear functional** on an [ordered vector space](wikipedia://en/8099221) $(V, \leq)$ is a [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $f$ on $V$ so that for all [positive elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_element_%28ordered_group%29) $v \in V,$ that is $v \geq 0,$ it holds that

$$f(v) \geq 0.$$

:::

In other words, a positive linear functional is guaranteed to take nonnegative values for positive elements. The significance of positive linear functionals lies in results such as [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504).

When $V$ is a [complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) vector space, it is assumed that for all $v\ge0,$ $f(v)$ is real. As in the case when $V$ is a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) with its partially ordered subspace of self-adjoint elements, sometimes a partial order is placed on only a subspace $W\subseteq V,$ and the partial order does not extend to all of $V,$ in which case the positive elements of $V$ are the positive elements of $W,$ by abuse of notation. This implies that for a C\*-algebra, a positive linear functional sends any $x \in V$ equal to $s^{\ast}s$ for some $s \in V$ to a real number, which is equal to its complex conjugate, and therefore all positive linear functionals preserve the self-adjointness of such $x.$ This property is exploited in the [GNS construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNS_construction) to relate positive linear functionals on a C\*-algebra to [inner products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product).

## Sufficient conditions for continuity of all positive linear functionals {#Sufficient_conditions_for_continuity_of_all_positive_linear_functionals}

There is a comparatively large class of [ordered topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/63988132) on which every positive linear form is necessarily continuous.[^n1] This includes all [topological vector lattices](wikipedia://en/63989962) that are [sequentially complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequentially_complete_topological_vector_space).[^n1]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** Let $X$ be an [Ordered topological vector space](wikipedia://en/63988132) with [positive cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_cone_of_an_ordered_vector_space) $C \subseteq X$ and let $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ denote the family of all bounded subsets of $X.$ Then each of the following conditions is sufficient to guarantee that every positive linear functional on $X$ is continuous:

1. $C$ has non-empty topological interior (in $X$).[^n1]
1. $X$ is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) and [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable) and $X = C - C.$[^n1]
1. $X$ is [bornological](wikipedia://en/1804457) and $C$ is a [semi-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-complete) [strict $\mathcal{B}$-cone](wikipedia://en/63990912) in $X.$[^n1]
1. $X$ is the [inductive limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) of a family $\left(X_{\alpha} \right)_{\alpha \in A}$ of ordered [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801) with respect to a family of positive linear maps where $X_{\alpha} = C_{\alpha} - C_{\alpha}$ for all $\alpha \in A,$ where $C_{\alpha}$ is the positive cone of $X_{\alpha}.$[^n1]

:::

## Continuous positive extensions {#Continuous_positive_extensions}

The following theorem is due to H. Bauer and independently, to Namioka.[^n1]

**Theorem**:[^n1] Let $X$ be an [ordered topological vector space](wikipedia://en/63988132) (TVS) with positive cone $C,$ let $M$ be a vector subspace of $E,$ and let $f$ be a linear form on $M.$ Then $f$ has an extension to a continuous positive linear form on $X$ if and only if there exists some convex neighborhood $U$ of $0$ in $X$ such that $\operatorname{Re} f$ is bounded above on $M \cap (U - C).$

**Corollary**:[^n1] Let $X$ be an [ordered topological vector space](wikipedia://en/63988132) with positive cone $C,$ let $M$ be a vector subspace of $E.$ If $C \cap M$ contains an interior point of $C$ then every continuous positive linear form on $M$ has an extension to a continuous positive linear form on $X.$

**Corollary**:[^n1] Let $X$ be an [ordered vector space](wikipedia://en/8099221) with positive cone $C,$ let $M$ be a vector subspace of $E,$ and let $f$ be a linear form on $M.$ Then $f$ has an extension to a positive linear form on $X$ if and only if there exists some convex [absorbing subset](wikipedia://en/1728179) $W$ in $X$ containing the origin of $X$ such that $\operatorname{Re} f$ is bounded above on $M \cap (W - C).$

Proof: It suffices to endow $X$ with the finest locally convex topology making $W$ into a neighborhood of $0 \in X.$

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Consider, as an example of $V,$ the C\*-algebra of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [square matrices](wikipedia://en/166022) with the positive elements being the [positive-definite matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix). The [trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_of_a_matrix) function defined on this C\*-algebra is a positive functional, as the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of any positive-definite matrix are positive, and so its trace is positive.

Consider the [Riesz space](wikipedia://en/8099018) $\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)$ of all [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) complex-valued functions of [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [support](wikipedia://en/381013) on a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) $X.$ Consider a [Borel regular measure](wikipedia://en/542326) $\mu$ on $X,$ and a functional $\psi$ defined by

$$\psi(f) = \int_X f(x) d \mu(x) \quad \text{ for all } f \in \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{c}}(X).$$

Then, this functional is positive (the integral of any positive function is a positive number). Moreover, any positive functional on this space has this form, as follows from the [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504).

:::

## Positive linear functionals (C\*-algebras) {#Positive_linear_functionals_(C*-algebras)}

Let $M$ be a C\*-algebra (more generally, an [operator system](wikipedia://en/10698414) in a C\*-algebra $A$) with identity $1.$ Let $M^+$ denote the set of positive elements in $M.$

A linear functional $\rho$ on $M$ is said to be positive if $\rho(a) \geq 0,$ for all $a \in M^+.$

**Theorem.** A linear functional $\rho$ on $M$ is positive if and only if $\rho$ is bounded and $\|\rho\| = \rho(1).$[^o18]

::: {#o20 .statement cue=named-heading name="Cauchy–Schwarz inequality"}

### Cauchy–Schwarz inequality {#Cauchy–Schwarz_inequality}

If $\rho$ is a positive linear functional on a C\*-algebra $A,$ then one may define a semidefinite [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) on $A$ by $\langle a,b\rangle = \rho(b^{\ast}a).$ Thus from the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128) we have

$$\left|\rho(b^{\ast}a)\right|^2 \leq \rho(a^{\ast}a) \cdot \rho(b^{\ast}b).$$

:::

## Applications to economics {#Applications_to_economics}

Given a space $C$, a price system can be viewed as a continuous, positive, linear functional on $C$.
