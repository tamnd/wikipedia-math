# Herman Wold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herman Wold"}

**Herman Ole Andreas Wold** (25 December 1908 – 16 February 1992) was a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_people)-born [econometrician](wikipedia://en/10390) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who had a long career in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). Wold was known for his work in [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255), in [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) analysis, and in [econometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econometric) statistics.

:::

In [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), Wold contributed the [Cramér–Wold theorem](wikipedia://en/2951323) characterizing the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) and developed the [Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition) in [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis). In [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics), Wold advanced [utility theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_theory) and the theory of [consumer demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_demand). In [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384), Wold contributed the methods of [partial least squares (**PLS**)](wikipedia://en/1046736) and [graphical models](wikipedia://en/447298). Wold's work on [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) [inference](wikipedia://en/27577) from [observational studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observational_studies) was decades ahead of its time, according to [Judea Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_Pearl).[^o3]

## Early life {#Early_life}

Herman Wold was born in [Skien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skien_%28town%29), southern [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway). He was the youngest in a family of six brothers and sisters. In 1912 the family moved to [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) and became Swedish citizens. Herman's father had a small fur and hide business.

## Scientific achievements {#Scientific_achievements}

Herman Wold had a long and productive career, spanning six decades.

### Studying with Harald Cramér {#Studying_with_Harald_Cramér}

In 1927 Wold enrolled at the [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) to study [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). It was an opportune time, for [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129) had been appointed Professor of Actuarial Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics. Wold would later write, "To belong to Cramér's first group of students was good luck, an advantage that simply cannot be exaggerated."

After graduating in 1930 Wold worked for an insurance company; he also did work on mortality data with Cramér and later designed a tariff for the insurance companies. He started work on a PhD on stochastic processes with Cramér as supervisor. Away from the thesis Wold and Cramér did some joint work, their best known result being the [Cramér–Wold theorem](wikipedia://en/2951323) (1936).

### Time series and the Wold decomposition {#Time_series_and_the_Wold_decomposition}

Wold's thesis, *A Study in the analysis of stationary time series,* was an important contribution. The main result was the "[Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition)" by which a [stationary series](wikipedia://en/329898) is expressed as a sum of a deterministic component and a stochastic component which can itself be expressed as an infinite [moving average](wikipedia://en/990809). Beyond this, the work brought together for the first time the work on individual processes by English statisticians, principally [Udny Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udny_Yule), and the theory of stationary stochastic processes created by Russian mathematicians, principally [A. Ya. Khinchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Yakovlevich_Khinchin). Wold's results on univariate time series were generalized to multivariate time series by his student [Peter Whittle](wikipedia://en/2600214).

The [Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition) and the related [Wold's theorem](wikipedia://en/4415141) inspired [Beurling](wikipedia://en/286530)'s [factorization theorem](wikipedia://en/314780#Factorization) in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147#Abstract_harmonic_analysis) and related work on [invariant subspaces](wikipedia://en/585826) of [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator).

### Theory of consumer demand {#Theory_of_consumer_demand}

In 1938 a government committee appointed Wold to do an econometric study of consumer demand in Sweden. The results were published in 1940. In parallel, he worked on the [theory of demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand). His book *Demand Analysis* (1952) brought together his work on the theory of demand, the theory of stochastic processes, the [theory of regression](wikipedia://en/826997) and his work on Swedish data.[^o9]

### Systems of simultaneous equations and causal inference {#Systems_of_simultaneous_equations_and_causal_inference}

In 1943 and 1944, [Trygve Haavelmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Haavelmo) put forward his ideas on the [simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equation_methods_%28econometrics%29) model, arguing that [systems of simultaneous equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equation_methods_%28econometrics%29) should be central in econometric research. Wold noted some limitation of the maximum-likelihood approach favoured by Haavelmo and the [Cowles Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Commission_for_Research_in_Economics); Wold cautioned that the literature contained some exaggerated claims for the superiority of maximum-likelihood estimation.

In 1945 to 1965, Wold proposed and elaborated on his "recursive causal chain" model, which was more appropriate for many applications, according to Wold: For such "recursive causal chain" models, the [least squares](wikipedia://en/82359) method was computationally efficient and enjoyed superior [theoretical](wikipedia://en/1565926) properties, which it lacked for general time-series models. Wold's writings on causality and recursive-chain models have been recognized as scientific inventions by recent work on [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) and [graphical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_models) in statistics, especially by [Judea Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_Pearl) and Nanny Wermuth.

### Multivariate analysis and partial least squares {#Multivariate_analysis_and_partial_least_squares}

At the end of his career, Wold turned away from econometric modelling and developed multivariate techniques for what he called "soft" modelling. Some of these methods were developed through interactions with his student [K. G. Jöreskog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Jöreskog), although the latter's focus was primarily on maximum likelihood methods. His son [Svante Wold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svante_Wold) applied these techniques in chemistry and developed the field of chemometrics.

## Appointments {#Appointments}

The story of Wold's academic appointments is briefly told. In 1942 he became professor of statistics at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) and he stayed there until 1970. He then moved to [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg), retiring from there in 1975.

In 1960 Wold became a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences). He was a member of the Prize Committee for the [Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Riksbank_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences_in_Memory_of_Alfred_Nobel) from 1968 to 1980.
