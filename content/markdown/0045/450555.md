# Field norm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Field norm"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **(field) norm** is a particular mapping defined in [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), which maps elements of a larger [field](wikipedia://en/10603) into a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *K* be a field and *L* a [finite](wikipedia://en/4382697) [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) (and hence an [algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125)) of *K*.

The field *L* is then a [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over *K*.

Multiplication by *α*, an element of *L*,

$$m_\alpha\colon L\to L$$

$$m_\alpha (x) = \alpha x,$$

is a *K*-[linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of this vector space into itself.

The **norm**, **N**<sub>*L*/*K*</sub>(*α*), is defined as the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of this linear transformation.[^o4]

If *L*/*K* is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064), one may compute the norm of *α* ∈ *L* as the product of all the [Galois conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_conjugate) of *α*:

$$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha) = \! \prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)} \!\! \sigma(\alpha),$$

where Gal(*L*/*K*) denotes the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of *L*/*K*.[^o5] (Note that there may be a repetition in the terms of the product.)

For a general field extension *L*/*K*, and nonzero *α* in *L*, let *σ*${}_{1}$(*α*), ..., σ${}_{n}$(*α*) be the [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) of the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of *α* over *K* (roots listed with multiplicity and lying in some extension field of *L*); then

$$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha)=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^n\sigma_j(\alpha) \biggr)^{[L:K(\alpha)]}.$$

If *L*/*K* is [separable](wikipedia://en/363340), then each root appears only once in the product (though the exponent, the [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) \[*L*:*K*(*α*)\], may still be greater than 1).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### Quadratic field extensions {#Quadratic_field_extensions}

One of the basic examples of norms comes from [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) extensions $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{Q}$ where $a$ is a [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525).

Then, the multiplication map by $\sqrt{a}$ on an element $x + y \cdot \sqrt{a}$ is

$$\sqrt{a}\cdot (x + y\cdot\sqrt{a}) = y \cdot a + x \cdot \sqrt{a}.$$

The element $x + y \cdot \sqrt{a}$ can be represented by the vector

$$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix},$$

since there is a direct sum decomposition $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a}) = \mathbb{Q}\oplus \mathbb{Q}\cdot\sqrt{a}$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space.

The [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of $m_\sqrt{a}$ is then

$$m_{\sqrt{a}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & a \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

and the norm is $N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a})/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{a}) = -a$, since it is the determinant of this matrix.

#### Norm of Q(√2) {#Norm_of_Q(√2)}

Consider the [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

The Galois group of $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ has order $d = 2$ and is generated by the element which sends $\sqrt{2}$ to $-\sqrt{2}$. So the norm of $1+\sqrt{2}$ is:

$$(1+\sqrt{2})(1-\sqrt{2}) = -1.$$

The field norm can also be obtained without the Galois group.

Fix a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, say:

$$\{1,\sqrt{2}\}.$$

Then multiplication by the number $1+\sqrt{2}$ sends

$1$ to $1+\sqrt{2}$ and

$\sqrt{2}$ to $2+\sqrt{2}$.

So the determinant of "multiplying by $1+\sqrt{2}$" is the determinant of the matrix which sends the vector

$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$ (corresponding to the first basis element, i.e., 1) to $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$,

$\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ (corresponding to the second basis element, i.e., $\sqrt{2}$) to $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$,

viz.:

$$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}.$$

The determinant of this matrix is −1.

### *p*-th root field extensions {#p-th_root_field_extensions}

Another easy class of examples comes from field extensions of the form $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[p]{a})/\mathbb{Q}$ where the prime factorization of $a \in \mathbb{Q}$ contains no $p$-th powers, for $p$ a fixed odd prime.

The multiplication map by $\sqrt[p]{a}$ of an element is

> $$\begin{aligned} m_{\sqrt[p]{a}}(x) &= \sqrt[p]{a} \cdot (a_0 + a_1\sqrt[p]{a} + a_2\sqrt[p]{a^2} + \cdots + a_{p-1}\sqrt[p]{a^{p-1}} )\\ &= a_0\sqrt[p]{a} + a_1\sqrt[p]{a^2} + a_2\sqrt[p]{a^3} + \cdots + a_{p-1}a \end{aligned}$$

giving the matrix

> $$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & a \\ 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

The determinant gives the norm

$$N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[p]{a})/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{a}) = (-1)^{p-1} a = a.$$

### Complex numbers over the reals {#Complex_numbers_over_the_reals}

The field norm from the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) to the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) sends

*x* + *iy*

to

*x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup>,

because the Galois group of $\mathbb{C}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ has two elements,

- the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) and
- complex conjugation,

and taking the product yields (*x* + *iy*)(*x* − *iy*) = *x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup>.

### Finite fields {#Finite_fields}

Let *L* = GF(*q*<sup>*n*</sup>) be a finite extension of a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) *K* = GF(*q*).

Since *L*/*K* is a Galois extension, if *α* is in *L*, then the norm of *α* is the product of all the Galois conjugates of *α*, i.e.[^o13]

$$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha)=\alpha \cdot \alpha^q \cdot \alpha^{q^2} \cdots \alpha^{q^{n-1}} = \alpha^{(q^n - 1)/(q-1)}.$$

In this setting we have the additional properties,[^o14]

- $$\forall \alpha \in L, \quad \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha^q) = \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha)$$
- $$\forall a \in K, \quad \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(a) = a^n.$$

:::

## Properties of the norm {#Properties_of_the_norm}

Several properties of the norm function hold for any finite extension.[^o16][^o20]

### Group homomorphism {#Group_homomorphism}

The norm **N**${}_{L/K}$ : *L*\* → *K*\* is a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from the multiplicative group of *L* to the multiplicative group of *K*, that is

$$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha \beta) = \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha) \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\beta) \text{ for all }\alpha, \beta \in L^*.$$

Furthermore, if $a \in K$:

$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(a \alpha) = a^{[L:K]} \operatorname{N}_{L/K}(\alpha) \text{ for all }\alpha \in L,$ and

$$\operatorname{N}_{L/K}(a) = a^{[L:K]}.$$

### Composition with field extensions {#Composition_with_field_extensions}

Additionally, the norm behaves well in [towers of fields](wikipedia://en/28194169):

if *M* is a finite extension of *L*, then the norm from *M* to *K* is just the composition of the norm from *M* to *L* with the norm from *L* to *K*, i.e.

$$\operatorname{N}_{M/K}=\operatorname{N}_{L/K}\circ\operatorname{N}_{M/L}.$$

### Reduction of the norm {#Reduction_of_the_norm}

The norm of an element in an arbitrary field extension can be reduced to an easier computation if the degree of the field extension is already known. This is

> $N_{L/K}(\alpha) = N_{K(\alpha)/K}(\alpha)^{[L:K(\alpha)]}$[^o20]

For example, for $\alpha = \sqrt{2}$ in the field extension $L = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\zeta_3), K =\mathbb{Q}$, the norm of $\alpha$ is

> $$\begin{aligned} N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2}) &= N_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{2})^{[\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\zeta_3):\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})]}\\ &= (-2)^{2}\\ &= 4 \end{aligned}$$

since the degree of the field extension $L/K(\alpha)$ is $2$.

### Detection of units {#Detection_of_units}

For $\mathcal{O}_K$ the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $K$, an element $\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K$ is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) if and only if $N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha) = \pm 1$.

For instance

$$N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)/\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_3) = 1$$

where

$$\zeta_3^3 = 1.$$

Thus, any number field $K$ whose ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ contains $\zeta_3$ has it as a unit.

## Further properties {#Further_properties}

The norm of an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) is again an integer, because it is equal (up to sign) to the [constant term](wikipedia://en/79861) of the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268).

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) one defines also norms for [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977). This is done in such a way that if *I* is a nonzero ideal of *O*<sub>*K*</sub>, the ring of integers of the number field *K*, **N**(*I*) is the number of residue classes in $O_K / I$ – i.e. the cardinality of this [finite ring](wikipedia://en/23706953). Hence this [ideal norm](wikipedia://en/460672) is always a positive integer.

When *I* is a [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) *αO<sub>K</sub>* then **N**(*I*) is equal to the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of the norm to *Q* of *α*, for *α* an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981).
