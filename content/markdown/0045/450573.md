# Trivial representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trivial representation"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), a **trivial representation** is a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) $(V,\,\varphi)$ of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* on which all elements of *G* act as the [identity mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_mapping) of *V*. A trivial [representation](wikipedia://en/6355948) of an [associative](wikipedia://en/2112) or [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is an ([Lie](wikipedia://en/318712)) [algebra representation](wikipedia://en/854978) for which all elements of the algebra act as the zero [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) ([endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569)) which sends every element of *V* to the [zero vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_vector).

:::

For any group or Lie algebra, an [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) trivial representation always exists over any [field](wikipedia://en/10603), and is one-dimensional, hence unique up to isomorphism. The same is true for associative algebras unless one restricts attention to [unital algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) and unital representations.

Although the trivial representation is constructed in such a way as to make its properties seem tautologous, it is a fundamental object of the theory. A subrepresentation is equivalent to a trivial representation, for example, if it consists of invariant vectors; so that searching for such subrepresentations is the whole topic of [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762).

The **trivial character** is the [character](wikipedia://en/293418) that takes the value of one for all group elements.
