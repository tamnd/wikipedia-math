# Mitchell's embedding theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mitchell's embedding theorem"}

**Mitchell's embedding theorem**, also known as the **Freyd–Mitchell theorem** or the **full embedding theorem**, is a result about small [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063); it states that these categories, while abstractly defined, can be represented as [concrete categories](wikipedia://en/167001) whose objects are [modules](wikipedia://en/276410). In particular, the result allows one to use element-wise [diagram chasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram_chasing) proofs in abelian categories. The theorem is named after [Barry Mitchell](wikipedia://en/80837588) and [Peter Freyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Freyd).

:::

## Details {#Details}

The precise statement is as follows: if **A** is a small abelian category, then there exists a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* (with 1, not necessarily commutative) and a [full](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_functor), [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor) and [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945) *F*: **A** → *R*-Mod (where the latter denotes the category of all [left *R*-modules](wikipedia://en/276410)).

The functor *F* yields an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between **A** and a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) of *R*-Mod in such a way that [kernels](wikipedia://en/142616) and [cokernels](wikipedia://en/279715) computed in **A** correspond to the ordinary kernels and cokernels computed in *R*-Mod. Such an equivalence is necessarily [additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_functor). The theorem thus essentially says that the objects of **A** can be thought of as *R*-modules, and the morphisms as *R*-linear maps, with kernels, cokernels, [exact sequences](wikipedia://en/138215) and sums of morphisms being determined as in the case of modules. However, [projective](wikipedia://en/3017382) and [injective](wikipedia://en/473779) objects in **A** do not necessarily correspond to [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) and [injective](wikipedia://en/473775) *R*-modules.

## Sketch of the proof {#Sketch_of_the_proof}

Let $\mathcal{L} \subset \operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal{A}, Ab)$ be the category of [left exact functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_exact_functor) from the abelian category $\mathcal{A}$ to the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) $Ab$. First we construct a [contravariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariance_and_contravariance_of_functors) embedding $H:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}$ by $H(A) = h^A$ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}$, where $h^A$ is the covariant hom-functor, $h^A(X)=\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{A}(A,X)$. The [Yoneda Lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_Lemma) states that $H$ is fully faithful and we also get the left exactness of $H$ very easily because $h^A$ is already left exact. The proof of the right exactness of $H$ is harder and can be read in Swan, *Lecture Notes in Mathematics 76*.

After that we prove that $\mathcal{L}$ is an abelian category by using localization theory (also Swan). This is the hard part of the proof.

It is easy to check that the abelian category $\mathcal{L}$ is an [AB5 category](wikipedia://en/30051646) with a [generator](wikipedia://en/9550415) $\bigoplus_{A\in\mathcal{A}} h^A$. In other words it is a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538) and therefore has an injective cogenerator $I$.

The [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) $R := \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}} (I,I)$ is the ring we need for the category of *R*-modules.

By $G(B) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{L}} (B,I)$ we get another contravariant, exact and fully faithful embedding $G:\mathcal{L}\to R\operatorname{-Mod}.$ The composition $GH:\mathcal{A}\to R\operatorname{-Mod}$ is the desired covariant exact and fully faithful embedding.

Note that the proof of the [Gabriel–Quillen embedding theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel–Quillen_embedding_theorem) for [exact categories](wikipedia://en/10987985) is almost identical.
