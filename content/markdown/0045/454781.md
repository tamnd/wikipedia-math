# Quadratic field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadratic field"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), a **quadratic field** is an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) of [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) two over $\mathbf{Q}$, the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).

:::

Every such quadratic field is some $\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ where $d$ is a (uniquely defined) [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525) different from $0$ and $1$. If $d>0$, the corresponding quadratic field is called a **real quadratic field**, and, if $d<0$, it is called an **imaginary quadratic field** or a **complex quadratic field**, corresponding to whether or not it is a [subfield](wikipedia://en/11634) of the field of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).

Quadratic fields have been studied in great depth, initially as part of the theory of [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129). There remain some unsolved problems. The [class number problem](wikipedia://en/596282) is particularly important.

## Ring of integers {#Ring_of_integers}

## Discriminant {#Discriminant}

For a nonzero square free integer $d$, the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of the quadratic field $K = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ is $d$ if $d$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, and otherwise $4d$. For example, if $d$ is $-1$, then $K$ is the field of [Gaussian rationals](wikipedia://en/318052) and the discriminant is $-4$. The reason for such a distinction is that the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$ is generated by $(1+\sqrt{d})/2$ in the first case and by $\sqrt{d}$ in the second case.

The set of discriminants of quadratic fields is exactly the set of [fundamental discriminants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_discriminant) (apart from $1$, which is a fundamental discriminant but not the discriminant of a quadratic field).

## Prime factorization into ideals {#Prime_factorization_into_ideals}

Any prime number $p$ gives rise to an ideal $p\mathcal{O}_K$ in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) $\mathcal{O}_K$ of a quadratic field $K$. In line with general theory of [splitting of prime ideals in Galois extensions](wikipedia://en/1190816), this may be[^o5]

**$p$ is [inert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inert_prime)**

$(p)$ is a prime ideal.

The quotient ring is the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with $p^2$ elements: $\mathcal{O}_K / p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbf{F}_{p^2}$.

**$p$ [splits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_prime)**

$(p)$ is a product of two distinct prime ideals of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

The quotient ring is the product $\mathcal{O}_K/p\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbf{F}_p\times\mathbf{F}_p$.

**$p$ is [ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramified_prime)**

$(p)$ is the square of a prime ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$.

The quotient ring contains non-zero [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) elements.

The third case happens if and only if $p$ divides the discriminant $D$. The first and second cases occur when the [Kronecker symbol](wikipedia://en/706301) $(D/p)$ equals $-1$ and $+1$, respectively. For example, if $p$ is an odd prime not dividing $D$, then $p$ splits if and only if $D$ is congruent to a square modulo $p$. The first two cases are, in a certain sense, equally likely to occur as $p$ runs through the primes—see [Chebotarev density theorem](wikipedia://en/535349).[^o6]

The law of [quadratic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/25272) implies that the splitting behaviour of a prime $p$ in a quadratic field depends only on $p$ modulo $D$, where $D$ is the field discriminant.

## Class group {#Class_group}

Determining the class group of a quadratic field extension can be accomplished using [Minkowski's bound](wikipedia://en/8491791) and the [Kronecker symbol](wikipedia://en/706301) because of the finiteness of the [class group](wikipedia://en/150907).[^o8] A quadratic field $K = \mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{d})$ has [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963)

$$\Delta_K = \begin{cases} d & d \equiv 1 \pmod 4 \\ 4d & d \equiv 2,3 \pmod 4; \end{cases}$$

so the Minkowski bound is[^o9]

$$M_K = \begin{cases} 2\sqrt{|\Delta|}/\pi & d < 0 \\ \sqrt{|\Delta|}/2 & d > 0 . \end{cases}$$

Then, the ideal class group is generated by the prime ideals whose norm is less than $M_K$. This can be done by looking at the decomposition of the ideals $(p)$ for $p \in \mathbf{Z}$ prime where $|p| < M_k.$[^o5] <sup>page 72</sup> These decompositions can be found using the [Dedekind–Kummer theorem](wikipedia://en/69521086).

## Quadratic subfields of cyclotomic fields {#Quadratic_subfields_of_cyclotomic_fields}

### The quadratic subfield of the prime cyclotomic field {#The_quadratic_subfield_of_the_prime_cyclotomic_field}

A classical example of the construction of a quadratic field is to take the unique quadratic field inside the [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) generated by a primitive $p$th root of unity, with $p$ an odd prime number. The uniqueness is a consequence of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), there being a unique subgroup of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) $2$ in the Galois group over $\mathbf{Q}$. As explained at [Gaussian period](wikipedia://en/456085), the discriminant of the quadratic field is $p$ for $p=4n+1$ and $-p$ for $p=4n+3$. This can also be predicted from enough [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) theory. In fact, $p$ is the only prime that ramifies in the cyclotomic field, so $p$ is the only prime that can divide the quadratic field discriminant. That rules out the 'other' discriminants $-4p$ and $4p$ in the respective cases.

### Other cyclotomic fields {#Other_cyclotomic_fields}

If one takes the other cyclotomic fields, they have Galois groups with extra $2$-torsion, so contain at least three quadratic fields. In general a quadratic field of field discriminant $D$ can be obtained as a subfield of a cyclotomic field of $D$-th roots of unity. This expresses the fact that the [conductor](wikipedia://en/26355302) of a quadratic field is the absolute value of its discriminant, a special case of the [conductor-discriminant formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conductor-discriminant_formula).

## Orders of quadratic number fields of small discriminant {#Orders_of_quadratic_number_fields_of_small_discriminant}

The following table shows some [orders](wikipedia://en/1114171) of small discriminant of quadratic fields. The *maximal order* of an algebraic number field is its [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613), and the discriminant of the maximal order is the discriminant of the field. The discriminant of a non-maximal order is the product of the discriminant of the corresponding maximal order by the square of the determinant of the matrix that expresses a basis of the non-maximal order over a basis of the maximal order. All these discriminants may be defined by the formula of Discriminant of an algebraic number field.

For real quadratic integer rings, the [ideal class number](wikipedia://en/150907#Properties), which measures the failure of unique factorization, is given in [OEIS A003649](https://oeis.org/A003649); for the imaginary case, they are given in [OEIS A000924](https://oeis.org/A000924).
