# Category of abelian groups

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of abelian groups"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [category](wikipedia://en/5869) $\mathbf{Ab}$ has the [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396) as [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). This is the prototype of an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063):[^o2] indeed, every [small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category) [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) can be embedded in $\mathbf{Ab}$.[^o3]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) of $\mathbf{Ab}$ is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) $\{0\}$ which consists only of its [neutral element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_element).

The [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) in $\mathbf{Ab}$ are the [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) group homomorphisms, the [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539) are the [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) group homomorphisms, and the [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) are the [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) group homomorphisms.

$\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) of $\mathbf{Grp}$, the [category of *all* groups](wikipedia://en/800092). The main difference between $\mathbf{Ab}$ and $\mathbf{Grp}$ is that the sum of two homomorphisms $f$ and $g$ between abelian groups is again a group homomorphism:

$$(f+g)(x+y)=f(x+y)+g(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)+g(x)+g(y)$$

$$=f(x)+g(x)+f(y)+g(y)=(f+g)(x)+(f+g)(y)$$

The third equality requires the group to be abelian. This addition of morphism turns $\mathbf{Ab}$ into a [preadditive category](wikipedia://en/59521), and because the [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_abelian_groups) of finitely many abelian groups yields a [biproduct](wikipedia://en/59574), we indeed have an [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854).

In $\mathbf{Ab}$, the notion of [kernel in the category theory sense](wikipedia://en/142616) coincides with [kernel in the algebraic sense](wikipedia://en/45240), i.e. the categorical kernel of the morphism $f:A\to B$ is the subgroup $K$ of $A$ defined by $K=\{x\in A:f(x)=0\}$, together with the inclusion homomorphism $i:K\to A$. The same is true for [cokernels](wikipedia://en/279715); the cokernel of *f* is the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $C=B/f(A)$ together with the natural projection $p:B\to C$. (Note a further crucial difference between $\mathbf{Ab}$ and $\mathbf{Grp}$: in $\mathbf{Grp}$ it can happen that $f(A)$ is not a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $B$, and that therefore the quotient group $B/f(A)$ cannot be formed.) With these concrete descriptions of kernels and cokernels, it is quite easy to check that $\mathbf{Ab}$ is indeed an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).

The forgetful functor from $\mathbb{Z}\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ to $\mathbf{Ab}$ that sends a $\mathbb{Z}$-module $(M,+,\cdot)$ to its underlying abelian group $(M,+)$ and the functor from $\mathbf{Ab}$ to $\mathbb{Z}$ that sends an abelian group $(G,+)$ to the $\mathbb{Z}$-module $(G,+,\cdot)$ obtained by setting $k \cdot g := g^{k}$ define an [isomorphism of categories](wikipedia://en/489935).

The [product](wikipedia://en/237868) in $\mathbf{Ab}$ is given by the [product of groups](wikipedia://en/3706015), formed by taking the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of the underlying sets and performing the group operation componentwise. Because $\mathbf{Ab}$ has kernels, one can then show that $\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [complete category](wikipedia://en/62781). The [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) in $\mathbf{Ab}$ is given by the direct sum; since $\mathbf{Ab}$ has cokernels, it follows that $\mathbf{Ab}$ is also [cocomplete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocomplete).

We have a [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) $\mathbf{Ab}\to\mathbf{Set}$ which assigns to each abelian group the underlying [set](wikipedia://en/26691), and to each group homomorphism the underlying [function](wikipedia://en/185427). This functor is [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor), and therefore $\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001). The forgetful functor has a [left adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991) (which associates to a given set the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) with that set as basis) but does not have a right adjoint.

Taking [direct limits](wikipedia://en/211566) in $\mathbf{Ab}$ is an [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945). Since the group of integers $\mathbb{Z}$ serves as a [generator](wikipedia://en/9550415), the category $\mathbf{Ab}$ is therefore a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538); indeed it is the prototypical example of a Grothendieck category.

An object in $\mathbf{Ab}$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/473775) if and only if it is a [divisible group](wikipedia://en/472147); it is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) if and only if it is a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296). The category has a projective generator ($\mathbb{Z}$) and an [injective cogenerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_cogenerator) ($\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$).

Given two abelian groups $A$ and $B$, their [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) $A\otimes B$ is defined; it is again an abelian group. With this notion of product, $\mathbf{Ab}$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/1396924) [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604).

$\mathbf{Ab}$ is not a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464) since e.g. it has a zero object.
