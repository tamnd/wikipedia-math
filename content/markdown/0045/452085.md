# Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems"}

**"Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems"** is a paper published in 1949 by [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) discussing [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) from the viewpoint of [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773).[^o3] It is one of the foundational treatments (arguably *the* foundational treatment) of modern cryptography.[^o4] His work has been described as a "turning point, and marked the closure of classical cryptography and the beginning of modern cryptography."[^o5] It has also been described as turning cryptography from an "art to a science".[^o6] It is also a proof that all theoretically unbreakable ciphers must have the same requirements as the [one-time pad](wikipedia://en/22210).

:::

The paper serves as the foundation of [secret-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/53042), including the work of [Horst Feistel](wikipedia://en/530509), the [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) (DES), [Advanced Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/1260) (AES), and more.[^o7] In the paper, Shannon defined [unicity distance](wikipedia://en/71630), and the principles of [confusion and diffusion](wikipedia://en/537555), which are key to a secure cipher.[^o8]

Shannon published an earlier version of this research in the formerly classified report *A Mathematical Theory of Cryptography,* Memorandum MM 45-110-02, Sept. 1, 1945, Bell Laboratories.[^o9][^o10] This report also precedes the publication of his "[A Mathematical Theory of Communication](wikipedia://en/828061)", which appeared in 1948.
