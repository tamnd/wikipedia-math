# State (functional analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="State"}

In the mathematical field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **state** of an [operator system](wikipedia://en/10698414) is a [positive linear functional](wikipedia://en/454930) of [norm](wikipedia://en/246722) 1. States in functional analysis [generalize](wikipedia://en/12746) the notion of [density matrices](wikipedia://en/62844) in quantum mechanics, which represent [quantum states](wikipedia://en/30876419), both [mixed states](wikipedia://en/30876419#Mixed_states) and [pure states](wikipedia://en/30876419#Pure_states_as_rays_in_a_complex_Hilbert_space). Density matrices in turn generalize [state vectors](wikipedia://en/30876419), which only represent pure states. For *M* an operator system in a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *A* with identity, the set of all states ofM, sometimes denoted by S(*M*), is convex, weak-\* closed in the Banach dual space *M*<sup>\*</sup>. Thus the set of all states of *M* with the weak-\* topology forms a compact Hausdorff space, known as the **state space of *M***. In the C\*-algebraic formulation of quantum mechanics, states in this previous sense correspond to physical states, i.e. mappings from physical observables (self-adjoint elements of the C\*-algebra) to their expected measurement outcome (real number).

:::

## Jordan decomposition {#Jordan_decomposition}

States can be viewed as noncommutative generalizations of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437). By [Gelfand representation](wikipedia://en/364372), every commutative C\*-algebra *A* is of the form *C*<sub>0</sub>(*X*) for some locally compact Hausdorff *X*. In this case, *S*(*A*) consists of positive [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) on *X*, and the pure states are the evaluation functionals on *X*.

More generally, the [GNS construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNS_construction) shows that every state is, after choosing a suitable representation, a [vector state](wikipedia://en/454959#Vector_states).

A bounded linear functional on a C\*-algebra *A* is said to be **self-adjoint** if it is real-valued on the self-adjoint elements of *A*. Self-adjoint functionals are noncommutative analogues of [signed measures](wikipedia://en/2985811).

The [Jordan decomposition](wikipedia://en/2985858) in measure theory says that every signed measure can be expressed as the difference of two positive measures supported on disjoint sets. This can be extended to the noncommutative setting.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Every self-adjoint $f$ in $A^{\ast}$ can be written as $f = f_{+} - f_{-}$ where $f_{+}$ and $f_{-}$ are positive functionals and $\Vert f \Vert = \Vert f_{+}\Vert + \Vert f_{-}\Vert$.

:::

::: {#o4 .proof cue=math-proof}

A proof can be sketched as follows: Let $\Omega$ be the weak\*-compact set of positive linear functionals on $A$ with norm ≤ 1, and $C(\Omega)$ be the continuous functions on $\Omega$.

$A$ can be viewed as a closed linear subspace of $C(\Omega)$ (this is *[Kadison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_V._Kadison)'s function representation*). By Hahn–Banach, $f$ extends to a $g$ in $C(\Omega)^{\ast}$ with $\Vert g \Vert = \Vert f \Vert$.

Using results from measure theory quoted above, one has:

$$g(\cdot) = \int \cdot \; d \mu$$

where, by the self-adjointness of $f$, $\mu$ can be taken to be a signed measure. Write:

$$\mu = \mu_+ - \mu_-, \;$$

a difference of positive measures. The restrictions of the functionals $\textstyle\int\cdot\;d\mu_{+}$ and $\textstyle\int\cdot\;d\mu_{-}$ to $A$ has the required properties of $f_{+}$ and $f_{-}$. This proves the theorem.

:::

It follows from the above decomposition that *A\** is the linear span of states.

## Some important classes of states {#Some_important_classes_of_states}

### Pure states {#Pure_states}

By the [Krein-Milman theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krein-Milman_theorem), the state space of *M* has [extreme points](wikipedia://en/454968). The extreme points of the state space are termed **pure states** and other states are known as **mixed states**.

### Vector states {#Vector_states}

For a Hilbert space *H* and a vector *x* in *H*, the formula ω<sub>*x*</sub>(*T*) := ⟨*Tx*,*x*⟩ (for *T* in *B(H)*) defines a positive linear functional on *B(H)*. Since ω<sub>*x*</sub>(*1*)=||*x*||<sup>2</sup>, ω<sub>*x*</sub> is a state if ||*x*||=1. If *A* is a C\*-subalgebra of *B(H)* and *M* an [operator system](wikipedia://en/10698414) in *A*, then the restriction of ω<sub>*x*</sub> to *M* defines a positive linear functional on *M*. The states of *M* that arise in this manner, from unit vectors in *H*, are termed **vector states** of *M*.

### Faithful states {#Faithful_states}

A state $\tau$ is **faithful**, if $\tau(a^* a) = 0$ implies $a = 0$.

### Normal states {#Normal_states}

A state $\tau$ is called **normal**, iff for every monotone, increasing [net](wikipedia://en/22170) $H_\alpha$ of operators with least upper bound $H$, $\tau(H_\alpha)\;$ converges to $\tau(H)\;$.

### Tracial states {#Tracial_states}

A **tracial state** is a state $\tau$ such that

$$\tau(AB)=\tau (BA)\;.$$

For any separable C\*-algebra, the set of tracial states is a [Choquet simplex](wikipedia://en/3655598).

### Factorial states {#Factorial_states}

A **factorial state** of a C\*-algebra *A* is a state such that the commutant of the corresponding GNS representation of *A* is a [factor](wikipedia://en/294185#Factors).
