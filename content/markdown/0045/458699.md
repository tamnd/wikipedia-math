# Strong operator topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strong operator topology"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **strong operator topology**, often abbreviated **SOT**, is the [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) on the set of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H* induced by the [seminorms](wikipedia://en/292067) of the form $T\mapsto\|Tx\|$, as *x* varies in *H*.

:::

Equivalently, it is the [coarsest topology](wikipedia://en/1197123) such that, for each fixed *x* in *H*, the evaluation map $T\mapsto Tx$ (taking values in *H*) is continuous in T. The equivalence of these two definitions can be seen by observing that a [subbase](wikipedia://en/395395) for both topologies is given by the sets $U(T_0,x,\epsilon) = \{T : \|Tx-T_0x\| < \epsilon\}$ (where *T<sub>0</sub>* is any bounded operator on *H*, *x* is any vector and ε is any positive real number).

In concrete terms, this means that $T_i\to T$ in the strong operator topology if and only if $\|T_ix-Tx\|\to 0$ for each *x* in *H*.

The SOT is [stronger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finer_topology) than the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) and weaker than the [norm topology](wikipedia://en/246722).

The SOT lacks some of the nicer properties that the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) has, but being stronger, things are sometimes easier to prove in this topology. It can be viewed as more natural, too, since it is simply the topology of [pointwise convergence](wikipedia://en/340198).

The SOT topology also provides the framework for the [measurable functional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_functional_calculus), just as the norm topology does for the [continuous functional calculus](wikipedia://en/784808).

The [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on the set of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that are continuous in the SOT are precisely those continuous in the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) (WOT). Because of this, the closure of a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) of operators in the WOT is the same as the closure of that set in the SOT.

This language translates into convergence properties of Hilbert space operators. For a complex Hilbert space, it is easy to verify by the [polarization identity](wikipedia://en/2047965), that Strong Operator convergence implies Weak Operator convergence.
