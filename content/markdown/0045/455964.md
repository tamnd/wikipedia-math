# Bicommutant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bicommutant"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **bicommutant** of a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) *S* of a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) (such as an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) or a [group](wikipedia://en/19447)) is the [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant) of the commutant of that subset. It is also known as the double commutant or second commutant and is written $S^{\prime \prime}$.

:::

The bicommutant is particularly useful in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), due to the [von Neumann double commutant theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_double_commutant_theorem), which relates the algebraic and analytic structures of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987). Specifically, it shows that if *M* is a unital, self-adjoint operator algebra in the [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *B(H)*, for some [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H*, then the [weak closure](wikipedia://en/458698), [strong closure](wikipedia://en/458699) and bicommutant of *M* are equal. This tells us that a unital [C\*-subalgebra](wikipedia://en/7184) *M* of *B(H)* is a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) if, and only if, $M = M^{\prime \prime}$, and that if not, the von Neumann algebra it generates is $M^{\prime \prime}$.

The bicommutant of *S* always contains *S*. So $S^{\prime \prime \prime} = \left(S^{\prime \prime}\right)^{\prime} \subseteq S^{\prime}$. On the other hand, $S^{\prime} \subseteq \left(S^{\prime}\right)^{\prime \prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime}$. So $S^{\prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime}$, i.e. the commutant of the bicommutant of *S* is equal to the commutant of *S*. By induction, we have:

$$S^{\prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime \prime \prime} = \cdots = S^{2n-1} = \cdots$$

and

$$S \subseteq S^{\prime \prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime \prime} = S^{\prime \prime \prime \prime \prime \prime} = \cdots = S^{2n} = \cdots$$

for *n* > 1.

It is clear that, if *S*<sub>1</sub> and *S*<sub>2</sub> are subsets of a semigroup,

$$\left( S_1 \cup S_2 \right)' = S_1 ' \cap S_2 ' .$$

If it is assumed that $S_1 = S_1'' \,$ and $S_2 = S_2''\,$ (this is the case, for instance, for [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185)), then the above equality gives

$$\left(S_1' \cup S_2'\right)'' = \left(S_1 '' \cap S_2 ''\right)' = \left(S_1 \cap S_2\right)' .$$
