# Limit cycle

*Image: Limit cycle Poincare map.svg.* Stable limit cycle (shown in bold) and two other trajectories spiraling into it

*Image: VanDerPolPhaseSpace.png.* Stable limit cycle (shown in bold) for the [Van der Pol oscillator](wikipedia://en/1961786)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Limit cycle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the study of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) with two-dimensional [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101), a **limit cycle** is a closed [trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) in phase space having the property that at least one other trajectory spirals into it either as time approaches infinity or as time approaches negative infinity. Such behavior is exhibited in some [nonlinear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_systems). Limit cycles have been used to model the behavior of many real-world oscillatory systems. The study of limit cycles was initiated by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) (1854–1912).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

We consider a two-dimensional dynamical system of the form

$$x'(t)=V(x(t))$$

where

$$V : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$$

is a smooth function. A *trajectory* of this system is some smooth function $x(t)$ with values in $\mathbb{R}^2$ which satisfies this differential equation. Such a trajectory is called *closed* (or *periodic*) if it is not constant but returns to its starting point, i.e. if there exists some $t_0>0$ such that $x(t + t_0) = x(t)$ for all $t \in \mathbb{R}$. An [orbit](wikipedia://en/252250) is the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a trajectory, a subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$. A *closed orbit*, or *cycle*, is the image of a closed trajectory. A *limit cycle* is a cycle which is the [limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198) of some other trajectory.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

By the [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444), every closed trajectory divides the plane into two regions, the interior and the exterior of the curve.

Given a limit cycle and a trajectory in its interior that approaches the limit cycle for time approaching $+ \infty$, then there is a neighborhood around the limit cycle such that *all* trajectories in the interior that start in the neighborhood approach the limit cycle for time approaching $+ \infty$. The corresponding statement holds for a trajectory in the interior that approaches the limit cycle for time approaching $- \infty$, and also for trajectories in the exterior approaching the limit cycle.

:::

## Stable, unstable and semi-stable limit cycles {#Stable,_unstable_and_semi-stable_limit_cycles}

In the case where all the neighboring trajectories approach the limit cycle as time approaches infinity, it is called a *[stable](wikipedia://en/5466649)* or *attractive* limit cycle (ω-limit cycle). If instead, all neighboring trajectories approach it as time approaches negative infinity, then it is an *unstable* limit cycle (α-limit cycle). If there is a neighboring trajectory which spirals into the limit cycle as time approaches infinity, and another one which spirals into it as time approaches negative infinity, then it is a *semi-stable* limit cycle. There are also limit cycles that are neither stable, unstable nor semi-stable: for instance, a neighboring trajectory may approach the limit cycle from the outside, but the inside of the limit cycle is approached by a family of other cycles (which would not be limit cycles).

Stable limit cycles are examples of [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094). They imply self-sustained [oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillations): the closed trajectory describes the perfect periodic behavior of the system, and any small perturbation from this closed trajectory causes the system to return to it, making the system stick to the limit cycle.

## Finding limit cycles {#Finding_limit_cycles}

Every closed trajectory contains within its interior a [stationary point](wikipedia://en/885651) of the system, i.e. a point $p$ where $V'(p) = 0$. The [Bendixson–Dulac theorem](wikipedia://en/634543) and the [Poincaré–Bendixson theorem](wikipedia://en/2784317) predict the absence or existence, respectively, of limit cycles of two-dimensional nonlinear dynamical systems.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Open problems"}

## Open problems {#Open_problems}

Finding limit cycles, in general, is a very difficult problem. The number of limit cycles of a polynomial differential equation in the plane is the main object of the second part of [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750). It is unknown, for instance, whether there is any system $x' = V(x)$ in the plane where both components of $V$ are quadratic polynomials of the two variables, such that the system has more than 4 limit cycles.

:::

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: Hopfbifurcation.png.* Examples of limit cycles branching from fixed points near [Hopf bifurcation](wikipedia://en/3626981). Trajectories in red, stable structures in dark blue, unstable structures in light blue. The parameter choice determines the occurrence and stability of limit cycles.

Limit cycles are important in many scientific applications where systems with self-sustained oscillations are modelled. Some examples include:

- Aerodynamic limit-cycle oscillations[^o12]
- The [Hodgkin–Huxley model](wikipedia://en/2991090) for [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential) in [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron).
- The Sel'kov model of [glycolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycolysis).[^o13]
- The daily oscillations in gene expression, hormone levels and body temperature of animals, which are part of the [circadian rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circadian_rhythm),[^o14][^o15] although this is contradicted by more recent evidence.[^o16]
- The [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_migration) of [cancer cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_cell) in confining micro-environments follows limit cycle oscillations.[^o17]
- Some non-linear [electrical circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Circuit) exhibit limit cycle oscillations,[^o18] which inspired the original [Van der Pol model](wikipedia://en/1961786).
- The control of respiration and hematopoiesis, as appearing in the [Mackey-Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackey-Glass_equations) equations.[^o19]

:::
