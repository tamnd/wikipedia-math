# Galois extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Galois extension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Galois extension** is an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/2125) [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *E*/*F* that is [normal](wikipedia://en/929925) and [separable](wikipedia://en/363340);[^n1] or equivalently, *E*/*F* is algebraic, and the [field fixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_field) by the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) Aut(*E*/*F*) is precisely the base [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F*. The significance of being a Galois extension is that the extension has a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) and obeys the [fundamental theorem of Galois theory](wikipedia://en/1947635).

:::

A result of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) allows one to construct Galois extensions as follows: If *E* is a given field, and *G* is a finite group of automorphisms of *E* with fixed field *F*, then *E*/*F* is a Galois extension.[^n2]

The property of an extension being Galois behaves well with respect to [field composition and intersection](wikipedia://en/8533497).[^n3]

## Characterization of Galois extensions {#Characterization_of_Galois_extensions}

An important theorem of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) states that for a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) $E/F$, each of the following statements is equivalent to the statement that $E/F$ is Galois:

- $E/F$ is a [normal extension](wikipedia://en/929925) and a [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340).
- $E$ is a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) of a set of [separable polynomials](wikipedia://en/362400) with coefficients in $F$.
- $|\!\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)| = [E:F]$, that is, the number of automorphisms equals the [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_%28field_theory%29) of the extension.

Other equivalent statements are:

- Every irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ with at least one root in $E$ splits over $E$ and is separable.
- $|\!\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)| \geq [E:F]$, that is, the number of automorphisms is at least the degree of the extension.
- $F$ is the fixed field of a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(E)$.
- $F$ is the fixed field of $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$.
- There is a one-to-one [correspondence](wikipedia://en/1947635#Explicit_description_of_the_correspondence) between subfields of $E/F$ and subgroups of $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)$.

An infinite field extension $E/F$ is Galois if and only if $E$ is the union of finite Galois subextensions $E_i/F$ indexed by an (infinite) index set $I$, i.e. $E=\bigcup_{i\in I}E_i$ and the Galois group is an [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) $\operatorname{Aut}(E/F)=\varprojlim_{i\in I}{\operatorname{Aut}(E_i/F)}$ where the inverse system is ordered by field inclusion $E_i\subset E_j$.[^n4]

## Examples {#Examples}

There are two basic ways to construct examples of Galois extensions.

- Take any field $E$, any finite subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(E)$, and let $F$ be the fixed field.
- Take any field $F$, any separable polynomial in $F[x]$, and let $E$ be its [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440).

[Adjoining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28field_theory%29) to the [rational number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number_field) the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) gives a Galois extension, while adjoining the cubic root of 2 gives a non-Galois extension. Both these extensions are separable, because they have [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero). The first of them is the splitting field of $x^2 -2$; the second has [normal closure](wikipedia://en/929925) that includes the complex [cubic roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), and so is not a splitting field. In fact, it has no automorphism other than the identity, because it is contained in the real numbers and $x^3 -2$ has just one real root. For more detailed examples, see the page on the [fundamental theorem of Galois theory](wikipedia://en/1947635).

An [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) $\bar K$ of an arbitrary field $K$ is Galois over $K$ if and only if $K$ is a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088).
