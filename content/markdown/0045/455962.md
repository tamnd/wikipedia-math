# Von Neumann bicommutant theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Von Neumann bicommutant theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **von Neumann bicommutant theorem** relates the [closure](wikipedia://en/293802) of a set of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) in certain [topologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_topology) to the [bicommutant](wikipedia://en/455964) of that set. In essence, it is a connection between the [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) and topological sides of [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675).

:::

The formal statement of the theorem is as follows:

**Von Neumann bicommutant theorem.** Let $\mathbf{M}$ be an [algebra](wikipedia://en/455987) consisting of bounded operators on a Hilbert space $H$, containing the identity operator, and closed under taking [adjoints](wikipedia://en/667175). Then the [closures](wikipedia://en/42309) of $\mathbf{M}$ in the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) and the [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699) are equal, and are in turn equal to the [bicommutant](wikipedia://en/455964) $\mathbf{M}''$ of $\mathbf{M}$.

This algebra is called the [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) generated by $\mathbf{M}$.

There are several other topologies on the space of bounded operators, and one can ask what are the \*-algebras closed in these topologies. If $\mathbf{M}$ is closed in the [norm topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_topology) then it is a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184), but not necessarily a von Neumann algebra. One such example is the C\*-algebra of [compact operators](wikipedia://en/6690773) (on an infinite dimensional Hilbert space). For most other common topologies the closed \*-algebras containing 1 are von Neumann algebras; this applies in particular to the weak operator, strong operator, \*-strong operator, [ultraweak](wikipedia://en/30876937), [ultrastrong](wikipedia://en/2707163), and \*-ultrastrong topologies.

It is related to the [Jacobson density theorem](wikipedia://en/488391).

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Let $H$ be a Hilbert space and $L(H)$ the bounded operators on $H$. Consider a self-adjoint unital [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) $\mathbf{M}$ of $L(H)$ (this means that $\mathbf{M}$ contains the adjoints of its members, and the identity operator on $H$).

The theorem is equivalent to the combination of the following three statements:

(i) $\operatorname{cl}_{W}(\mathbf{M}) \subseteq \mathbf{M}''$

(ii) $\operatorname{cl}_{S}(\mathbf{M}) \subseteq \operatorname{cl}_{W}(\mathbf{M})$

(iii) $\mathbf{M}'' \subseteq \operatorname{cl}_{S}(\mathbf{M})$

where the $W$ and $S$ subscripts stand for [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) in the [weak](wikipedia://en/458698) and [strong](wikipedia://en/458699) operator topologies, respectively.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of (i)"}

### Proof of (i) {#Proof_of_(i)}

For any $x$ and $y$ in $H$, the map *T* → \<*Tx*, *y*> is continuous in the weak operator topology, by its definition. Therefore, for any fixed operator $O$, so is the map

$$T \to \langle (OT - TO)x, y\rangle = \langle Tx, O^*y\rangle - \langle TOx, y\rangle$$

Let *S* be any subset of $L(H)$, and *S*′ its [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant). For any operator $T$ in *S*′, this function is zero for all *O* in *S*. For any $T$ not in *S*′, it must be nonzero for some *O* in *S* and some *x* and *y* in $H$. By its continuity there is an open neighborhood of $T$ for the weak operator topology on which it is nonzero, and which therefore is also not in *S*′. Hence any commutant *S*′ is [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) in the weak operator topology. In particular, so is $\mathbf{M}''$; since it contains $\mathbf{M}$, it also contains its weak operator closure.

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of (ii)"}

### Proof of (ii) {#Proof_of_(ii)}

This follows directly from the weak operator topology being coarser than the strong operator topology: for every point $x$ in $\operatorname{cl}_{S}(\mathbf{M})$, every open neighborhood of $x$ in the weak operator topology is also open in the strong operator topology and therefore contains a member of $\mathbf{M}$; therefore $x$ is also a member of $\operatorname{cl}_{W}(\mathbf{M})$.

:::

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of (iii)"}

### Proof of (iii) {#Proof_of_(iii)}

Fix $X \in \mathbf{M}''$. We must show that $X \in \operatorname{cl}_{S}(\mathbf{M})$, i.e. for each *h* ∈ *H* and any $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists *T* in $\mathbf{M}$ with $||Xh - Th|| < \varepsilon$.

Fix *h* in $H$. The [cyclic subspace](wikipedia://en/41392084) $\mathbf{M}h = \{Mh : M \in \mathbf{M}$} is invariant under the action of any *T* in $\mathbf{M}$. Its [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) $\operatorname{cl}(\mathbf{M}h)$ in the norm of *H* is a closed linear subspace, with corresponding [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) $P$ : *H* → $\operatorname{cl}(\mathbf{M}h)$ in *L*(*H*). In fact, this *P* is in $\mathbf{M}'$, as we now show.

**Lemma.** $P \in \mathbf{M}'$.

**Proof.** Fix $x \in H$. As $Px \in \operatorname{cl}(\mathbf{M}h)$, it is the limit of a sequence $O_{n}h$ with $O_{n}$ in $\mathbf{M}$. For any $T \in \mathbf{M}$, $TO_{n}h$ is also in $\mathbf{M}h$, and by the continuity of $T$, this sequence converges to $TPx$. So $TPx \in \operatorname{cl}(\mathbf{M}h)$, and hence *PTPx* = *TPx*. Since *x* was arbitrary, we have *PTP* = *TP* for all $T$ in $\mathbf{M}$.

Since $\mathbf{M}$ is closed under the adjoint operation and *P* is [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408), for any $x, y \in H$ we have

$$\langle x,TPy\rangle = \langle x,PTPy\rangle = \langle (PTP)^*x,y\rangle = \langle PT^*Px,y\rangle = \langle T^*Px,y\rangle = \langle Px,Ty\rangle = \langle x,PTy\rangle$$

So *TP* = *PT* for all $T \in \mathbf{M}$, meaning *P* lies in $\mathbf{M}'$.

By definition of the [bicommutant](wikipedia://en/455964), we must have *XP* = *PX*. Since $\mathbf{M}$ is unital, $h \in \mathbf{M}h$, and so $h = Ph$. Hence $Xh = XPh = PXh \in \operatorname{cl}(\mathbf{M}h)$. So for each $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists *T* in $\mathbf{M}$ with $||Xh - Th|| < \varepsilon$, i.e. $X$ is in the strong operator closure of $\mathbf{M}$.

:::

### Non-unital case {#Non-unital_case}

A C\*-algebra $\mathbf{M}$ acting on **H** is said to act *non-degenerately* if for *h* in $H$, $\mathbf{M}h = \{0\}$ implies $h = 0$. In this case, it can be shown using an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628) in $\mathbf{M}$ that the identity operator *I* lies in the strong closure of $\mathbf{M}$. Therefore, the conclusion of the bicommutant theorem holds for $\mathbf{M}$.

:::
