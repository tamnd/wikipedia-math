# Marshall Hall (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Marshall Hall"}

**Marshall Hall Jr.** (17 September 1910 – 4 July 1990) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hall studied mathematics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), graduating in 1932. He studied for a year at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) under a Henry Fellowship working with [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599).[^o5] He returned to Yale to take his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1936 under the supervision of [Øystein Ore](wikipedia://en/1643733).[^o7]

He worked in [Naval Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Naval_Intelligence) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), including six months in 1944 at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), the center of British wartime code breaking. In 1946 he took a position at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University). In 1959 he moved to the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) where, in 1973, he was named the first IBM Professor at Caltech, the first named chair in mathematics. After retiring from Caltech in 1981, he accepted a post at [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) in 1985.

Hall died in 1990 in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) on his way to a conference to mark his 80th birthday.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He wrote a number of papers of fundamental importance in group theory, including his solution of [Burnside's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside's_problem) for groups of exponent 6, showing that a finitely generated group in which the order of every element divides 6 must be finite.

His work in combinatorics includes an important paper of 1943 on [projective planes](wikipedia://en/24350), which for many years was one of the most cited mathematics research papers.[^o9] In this paper he constructed a family of [non-Desarguesian planes](wikipedia://en/21092112) which are known today as [Hall planes](wikipedia://en/35791745). He also worked on [block designs](wikipedia://en/1188184) and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869).

His classic book on group theory was well received when it came out and is still useful today. His book *Combinatorial Theory* came out in a second edition in 1986, published by [John Wiley & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wiley_&_Sons).

He proposed [Hall's conjecture](wikipedia://en/7119900) on the differences between [perfect squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_squares) and [perfect cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_cubes), which remains an open problem as of 2015.

Hall's work[^o10] on continued fractions showed that the [Lagrange spectrum](wikipedia://en/19043396) includes all numbers greater than 6. This interval is known as Hall's Ray. The lower limit of Hall's ray was established by [Freiman](wikipedia://en/34986640) in 1975.
