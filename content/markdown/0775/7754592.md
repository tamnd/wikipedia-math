# Andrews–Curtis conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrews–Curtis conjecture"}

In mathematics, the **Andrews–Curtis conjecture** states that every balanced [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) (i.e. a presentation with the same number of generators and relations) of the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059) can be transformed into a trivial presentation by a sequence of [Nielsen transformations](wikipedia://en/18400182) on the relators together with conjugations of relators. It is named after [James J. Andrews](wikipedia://en/32406830) and [Morton L. Curtis](wikipedia://en/32407013) who proposed it in 1965. It is difficult to verify whether the conjecture holds for a given balanced presentation or not.

:::

It is widely believed that the Andrews–Curtis conjecture is false. While there are no counterexamples known, there are numerous potential counterexamples.[^o2] It is known that the [Zeeman conjecture](wikipedia://en/38271866) on [collapsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapsibility) implies the Andrews–Curtis conjecture.
