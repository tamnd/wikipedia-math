# Sequence number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sequence number"}

A **sequence number** is a consecutive number in a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of numbers, usually of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) ([natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474)). Sequence numbers have many practical applications. They can be used, among other things, as part of [serial numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_number) on manufactured parts, in case management,[^o2] or in [databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database) as a [surrogate key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrogate_key) for registering and identifying unique entries in a [table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_%28database%29)[^o3][^o4] (in which case it is used as a [primary key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_key)).[^o5][^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Historically, the [Norwegian Mapping Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Mapping_Authority) have used sequence numbers for [land registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_registration) as a [placeholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placeholder_name) in cases where an organization number or [national identity number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identity_number_%28Norway%29) have not been known.[^o9]

In [elections in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Norway), sequence numbers are used in the duplicate check to prevent votes being counted twice or to detect duplicate [ballots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot).[^o10]

An example of a sequence number being used as a surrogate key is the *snr* number used by [Statistics Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Norway) since 1970,[^o11] which uniquely identifies a person even if their social security number changes. The snr number will then be linked to both social security numbers, and act as a link that ensures that each person can be identified by a unique key at all times.[^o11]

:::

## Comparison with serial number {#Comparison_with_serial_number}

A distinction is sometimes made between a sequence number and a serial number. For example, a Swiss [locomotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive) may have the designation "[Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RegioExpress) 465 003-2",[^o13] where:

- 465 is the serial number, and stands for 4 [drive shafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive_shaft), 6th version, and 5 for the company [BLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BLS_AG)
- 003 is the sequence number, and means that it is the third locomotive in this series
- \-2 is a [check digit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Check_digit)
