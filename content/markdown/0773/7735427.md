# E-function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E-function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **E-functions** are a type of [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) that satisfy particular arithmetic conditions on the coefficients. They are of interest in [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), and are closely related to [G-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-function_%28power_series%29).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A power series with coefficients in the field of algebraic numbers

$$f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n \frac{x^n}{n!} \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}[\![x]\!]$$

is called an **$E$-function**[^o4] if it satisfies the following three conditions:

- It is a solution of a non-zero [linear differential equation](wikipedia://en/379868) with polynomial coefficients (this implies that all the coefficients $c_{n}$ belong to the same [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822), $K$, which has [finite degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) over the rational numbers);
- For all $\varepsilon>0$, $\overline{\left|c_n\right|}=O\left(n^{n\varepsilon}\right),$

where the left hand side represents the maximum of the absolute values of all the [algebraic conjugates](wikipedia://en/458467) of $c_{n}$;

- For all $\varepsilon>0$ there is a sequence of natural numbers $q_{0}, q_{1}, q_{2},...$ such that $q_{n}c_{k}$ is an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) in $K$ for $k = 0, 1, 2,..., n$, and $n = 0, 1, 2,...$ and for which $q_n=O\left(n^{n\varepsilon}\right).$

The second condition implies that $f$ is an [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) of $x$.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

$E$-functions were first studied by [Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) in 1929.[^o7] He found a method to show that the values taken by certain $E$-functions were [algebraically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_independent). This was a result which established the algebraic independence of classes of numbers rather than just [linear independence](wikipedia://en/101863).[^o8] Since then these functions have proved somewhat useful in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and in particular they have application in [transcendence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_numbers) proofs and [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Siegel–Shidlovsky theorem"}

## The Siegel–Shidlovsky theorem {#The_Siegel–Shidlovsky_theorem}

Perhaps the main result connected to $E$-functions is the Siegel–Shidlovsky theorem (also known as the Siegel and Shidlovsky theorem), named after [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) and Andrei Borisovich Shidlovsky.

Suppose that we are given $n$ $E$-functions, $E_{1}(x),...,E_{n}(x)$, that satisfy a system of homogeneous linear differential equations

$$y^\prime_i=\sum_{j=1}^n f_{ij}(x)y_j\quad(1\leq i\leq n)$$

where the $f_{ij}$ are [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) of $x$, and the coefficients of each $E$ and $f$ are elements of an algebraic number field $K$. Then the theorem states that if $E_{1}(x),...,E_{n}(x)$ are algebraically independent over $K(x)$, then for any non-zero algebraic number $\alpha$ that is not a pole of any of the $f_{ij}$ the numbers $E_{1}(\alpha),...,E_{n}(\alpha)$ are algebraically independent.

:::

::: {#o13 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

1. Any polynomial with algebraic coefficients is a simple example of an $E$-function.
1. The [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) is an $E$-function, in its case $c_{n} = 1$ for all of the $n$.
1. If $\lambda$ is an algebraic number then the [Bessel function](wikipedia://en/4700) $J_{\lambda}$ is an $E$-function.
1. The sum or product of two $E$-functions is an $E$-function. In particular $E$-functions form a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404).
1. If $a$ is an algebraic number and $f(x)$ is an $E$-function then $f(ax)$ will be an $E$-function.
1. If $f(x)$ is an $E$-function then the derivative and integral of $f$ are also $E$-functions.

:::
