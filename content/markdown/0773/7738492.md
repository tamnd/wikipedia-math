# Goldie's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goldie's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Goldie's theorem** is a basic structural result in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), proved by [Alfred Goldie](wikipedia://en/20962143) during the 1950s. What is now termed a right **Goldie ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* that has finite [uniform dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_dimension) (="finite rank") as a right module over itself, and satisfies the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) on right [annihilators](wikipedia://en/328684) of subsets of *R*.

:::

Goldie's theorem states that the [semiprime](wikipedia://en/7739701) right Goldie rings are precisely those that have a [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) [Artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) right [classical ring of quotients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_ring_of_quotients). The structure of this ring of quotients is then completely determined by the [Artin–Wedderburn theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin–Wedderburn_theorem).

In particular, Goldie's theorem applies to semiprime right [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129), since by definition right Noetherian rings have the ascending chain condition on *all* right ideals. This is sufficient to guarantee that a right-Noetherian ring is right Goldie. The converse does not hold: every right [Ore domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore_domain) is a right Goldie domain, and hence so is every commutative [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462).

A consequence of Goldie's theorem, again due to Goldie, is that every semiprime [principal right ideal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_right_ideal_ring) is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of [prime](wikipedia://en/487540) principal right ideal rings. Every prime principal right ideal ring is isomorphic to a [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over a right [Ore domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore_domain).

## Sketch of the proof {#Sketch_of_the_proof}

This is a sketch of the characterization mentioned in the introduction. It may be found in (Lam 1999, p.324).

- If *R* is a semiprime right Goldie ring, then it is a right order in a semisimple ring:
  - [Essential right ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_submodule) of *R* are exactly those containing a [regular element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_element_%28ring_theory%29).
  - There are no non-zero [nil ideals](wikipedia://en/3100140) in *R*.
  - *R* is a right [nonsingular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsingular_ring).[^o4]
  - From the previous observations, *R* is a right [Ore ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore_ring), and so its right classical ring of quotients *Q*<sup>*r*</sup> exists. Also from the previous observations, *Q*<sup>*r*</sup> is a [semisimple ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring). Thus *R* is a right order in *Q*<sup>*r*</sup>.
- If *R* is a right order in a semisimple ring *Q*, then it is semiprime right Goldie:
  - Any right order in a Noetherian ring (such as *Q*) is right Goldie.
  - Any right order in a Noetherian semiprime ring (such as *Q*) is itself semiprime.
  - Thus, *R* is semiprime right Goldie.
