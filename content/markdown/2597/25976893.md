# Multiplicity-one theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplicity-one theorem"}

In the mathematical theory of [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation), a **multiplicity-one theorem** is a result about the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of an [adelic](wikipedia://en/1168608) [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group). The multiplicity in question is the number of times a given abstract [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695) is realised in a certain space, of [square-integrable functions](wikipedia://en/28680558), given in a concrete way.

:::

A multiplicity one theorem may also refer to a result about the [restriction](wikipedia://en/880754) of a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* to a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H*. In that context, the pair (*G*, *H*) is called a strong [Gelfand pair](wikipedia://en/3018619).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a reductive algebraic group over a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *K* and let **A** denote the [adeles](wikipedia://en/247978) of *K*. Let *Z* denote the [centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_group) of *G* and let $\omega$ be a [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28mathematics%29) [unitary character](wikipedia://en/293418) from *Z*(*K*)\\Z(**A**)<sup>×</sup> to **C**<sup>×</sup>. Let *L*<sup>2</sup><sub>0</sub>(*G*(*K*)/*G*(**A**), $\omega$) denote the [space of cusp forms with central character ω](wikipedia://en/6043458) on *G*(**A**). This space decomposes into a [direct sum of Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_Hilbert_spaces)

$$L^2_0(G(K)\backslash G(\mathbf{A}),\omega)=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{(\pi,V_\pi)}m_\pi V_\pi$$

where the sum is over [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) [subrepresentations](wikipedia://en/55943952) and *m*<sub>$\pi$</sub> are non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

The group of adelic points of *G*, *G*(**A**), is said to satisfy the **multiplicity-one property** if any [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_representation) irreducible [admissible representation](wikipedia://en/11055227) of *G*(**A**) occurs with multiplicity at most one in the space of [cusp forms](wikipedia://en/610165) of central character $\omega$, i.e. *m*<sub>$\pi$</sub> is 0 or 1 for all such $\pi$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

The fact that the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564), *GL*(*n*), has the multiplicity-one property was proved by (Jacquet Langlands 1970) for *n* = 2 and independently by (Piatetski-Shapiro 1979) and (txt) for *n* > 2 using the uniqueness of the [Whittaker model](wikipedia://en/20679744). Multiplicity-one also holds for [*SL*(2)](wikipedia://en/144823), but not for *SL*(*n*) for *n* > 2 (Blasius 1994).

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Strong multiplicity one theorem"}

## Strong multiplicity one theorem {#Strong_multiplicity_one_theorem}

The strong multiplicity one theorem of (Piatetski-Shapiro 1979) and (txt) states that two cuspidal automorphic representations of the general linear group are isomorphic if their local components are isomorphic for all but a finite number of places.

:::
