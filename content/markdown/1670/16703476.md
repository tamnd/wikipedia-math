# Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equation"}

*Image: BBM equation - overtaking solitary waves animation.gif.* An animation of the overtaking of two [solitary waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_wave_%28water_waves%29) according to the Benjamin–Bona–Mahony (BBM) equation. The [wave heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_height) of the solitary waves are 1.2 and 0.6, respectively, and their [celerities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_speed) are 1.4 and 1.2. The upper graph is for a [frame of reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference) moving with the average celerity of the solitary waves. The [envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envelope_%28waves%29) of the overtaking waves is shown in grey: note that the maximum wave height reduces during the interaction. The lower graph (with a different vertical scale and in a stationary frame of reference) shows the [oscillatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation) tail produced by the interaction.[^o2] Thus, the solitary wave solutions of the BBM equation are not [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654).

The **Benjamin–Bona–Mahony equation** (**BBM equation**, also **regularized long-wave equation**; **RLWE**) is the [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564)

:::

$$u_t+u_x+uu_x-u_{xxt}=0.\,$$

This equation was studied in () as an improvement of the [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) (KdV equation) for modeling long [surface gravity waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_gravity_wave) of small amplitude – [propagating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_propagation) uni-directionally in 1+1 dimensions. They show the stability and uniqueness of solutions to the BBM equation. This contrasts with the KdV equation, which is unstable in its high [wavenumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavenumber) components. Further, while the KdV equation has an infinite number of [integrals of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_of_motion), the BBM equation only has three.[^o4][^o5]

Before, in 1966, this equation was introduced by [Peregrine](wikipedia://en/19388360), in the study of [undular bores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undular_bore).[^o6]

A generalized *n*-dimensional version is given by[^o7][^o8]

$$u_t-\nabla^2u_t+\operatorname{div}\,\varphi(u)=0.\,$$

where $\varphi$ is a sufficiently smooth function from $\mathbb R$ to $\mathbb R^n$. (Avrin Goldstein 1985) proved global existence of a solution in all dimensions.

## Solitary wave solution {#Solitary_wave_solution}

The BBM equation possesses [solitary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_wave_%28water_waves%29) solutions of the form:[^o5]

$$u = 3 \frac{c^2}{1-c^2} \operatorname{sech}^2 \frac12 \left( cx - \frac{ct}{1-c^2} + \delta \right),$$

where sech is the [hyperbolic secant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_secant) function and $\delta$ is a phase shift (by an initial horizontal displacement). For $|c|<1$, the solitary waves have a positive [crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_%28physics%29) elevation and travel in the positive $x$-direction with velocity $1/(1-c^2).$ These solitary waves are not [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654), i.e. after interaction with other solitary waves, an oscillatory tail is generated and the solitary waves have changed.[^o2][^o5]

## Hamiltonian structure {#Hamiltonian_structure}

The BBM equation has a [Hamiltonian structure](wikipedia://en/1197531), as it can be written as:[^o12]

$u_t = - \mathcal{D} \frac{\delta H}{\delta u},\,$ with Hamiltonian $H = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \left( \tfrac12 u^2 + \tfrac16 u^3 \right)\, \text{d}x\,$ and operator $\mathcal{D} = \left( 1 - \partial_x^2 \right)^{-1}\, \partial_x.$

Here $\delta H/\delta u$ is the [variation](wikipedia://en/171882) of the Hamiltonian $H(u)$ with respect to $u(x),$ and $\partial_x$ denotes the partial differential operator with respect to $x.$

## Conservation laws {#Conservation_laws}

The BBM equation possesses exactly three independent and non-trivial [conservation laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29).[^o5] First $u$ is replaced by $u=-v-1$ in the BBM equation, leading to the equivalent equation:

$$v_t - v_{xxt} = v\, v_x.$$

The three conservation laws then are:[^o5]

$$\begin{aligned} v_t - \left( v_{xt} + \tfrac12 v^2 \right)_x & = 0, \\ \left( \tfrac12 v^2 + \tfrac12 v_x^2 \right)_t - \left( v\, v_{xt} + \tfrac13 v^3 \right)_x & = 0, \\ \left( \tfrac13 v^3 \right)_t + \left( v_t^2 - v_{xt}^2 - v^2\, v_{xt} - \tfrac14 v^4 \right)_x & = 0. \end{aligned}$$

Which can easily expressed in terms of $u$ by using $v=-u-1.$

:::

## Linear dispersion {#Linear_dispersion}

The linearized version of the BBM equation is:

$$u_t + u_x - u_{xxt}=0.$$

Periodic progressive wave solutions are of the form:

$$u = a\, \mathrm{e}^{i (k x-\omega t)},$$

with $k$ the [wavenumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavenumber) and $\omega$ the [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency). The [dispersion relation](wikipedia://en/853778) of the linearized BBM equation is[^o4]

$$\omega_\mathrm{BBM} = \frac{k}{1+k^2}.$$

Similarly, for the linearized KdV equation $u_t + u_x + u_{xxx} = 0$ the dispersion relation is:[^o4]

$$\omega_\mathrm{KdV} = k - k^3.$$

This becomes unbounded and negative for $k\to\infty,$ and the same applies to the [phase velocity](wikipedia://en/25098) $\omega_\mathrm{KdV}/k$ and [group velocity](wikipedia://en/12778) $\mathrm{d}\omega_\mathrm{KdV}/\mathrm{d}k.$ Consequently, the KdV equation gives waves travelling in the negative $x$-direction for high wavenumbers (short [wavelengths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelengths)). This is in contrast with its purpose as an approximation for uni-directional waves propagating in the positive $x$-direction.[^o4]

The strong growth of frequency $\omega_\mathrm{KdV}$ and phase speed with wavenumber $k$ posed problems in the numerical solution of the KdV equation, while the BBM equation does not have these shortcomings.[^o4]
