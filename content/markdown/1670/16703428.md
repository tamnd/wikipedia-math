# Euler–Poisson–Darboux equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler–Poisson–Darboux equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Euler–Poisson–Darboux (EPD)**[^o2][^o3] equation is the [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564)

:::

$$u_{x,y}+\frac{N(u_x+u_y)}{x+y}=0.$$

This equation is named for [Siméon Poisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siméon_Poisson), [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), and [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux). It plays an important role in solving the classical [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691).

This equation is related to

$$u_{rr}+\frac{m}{r}u_r-u_{tt}=0,$$

by $x=r+t$, $y=r-t$, where $N=\frac{m}{2}$[^o3] and some sources quote this equation when referring to the Euler–Poisson–Darboux equation.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

The EPD equation is the simplest linear hyperbolic equation in two independent variables whose coefficients exhibit singularities, therefore it has an interest as a paradigm to relativity theory.[^o8]

[Compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support) [self-similar solution](wikipedia://en/58495269) of the EPD equation for [thermal conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_conduction) was derived starting from the modified Fourier-Cattaneo law.[^o9]

It is also possible to solve the non-linear EPD equations with the method of generalized [separation of variables](wikipedia://en/387750).[^o10]
