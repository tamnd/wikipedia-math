# Jacquet–Langlands correspondence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacquet–Langlands correspondence"}

In mathematics, the **Jacquet–Langlands correspondence** is a correspondence between [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) on [GL<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/113564) and its twisted forms, proved by (txt, section 16) in their book *[Automorphic Forms on GL(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_Forms_on_GL%282%29)* using the [Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/981694). It was one of the first examples of the [Langlands philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_philosophy) that maps between [L-groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) should induce maps between [automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representations). There are generalized versions of the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence relating automorphic representations of GL<sub>*r*</sub>(*D*) and GL<sub>*dr*</sub>(*F*), where *D* is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) of degree *d*<sup>2</sup> over the [local](wikipedia://en/145375) or [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) *F*.

:::

Suppose that *G* is an inner twist of the algebraic group GL<sub>2</sub>, in other words the [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742) of a [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890). The Jacquet–Langlands correspondence is [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between

- [Automorphic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) of *G* of dimension greater than 1
- Cuspidal automorphic representations of GL<sub>2</sub> that are [square integrable](wikipedia://en/28680558) (modulo the center) at each [ramified](wikipedia://en/384644) place of *G*.

Corresponding representations have the same local components at all unramified places of *G*.

(Rogawski 1983) and (Deligne Kazhdan Vignéras 1984) extended the Jacquet–Langlands correspondence to division algebras of higher dimension.
