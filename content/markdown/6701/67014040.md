# Question under discussion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Question under discussion"}

In [semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_%28linguistics%29), [pragmatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics), and [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), a **question under discussion (QUD)** is a [question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question) which the [interlocutors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlocutor_%28linguistics%29) in a [discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse) are attempting to answer. In many [formal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_linguistics) and [computational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) theories of discourse, the QUD (or an ordered set of QUD's) is among the elements of a [tuple](wikipedia://en/132729) called the [conversational scoreboard](wikipedia://en/68477468) which represents the current state of the conversation. [Craige Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craige_Roberts) introduced the concept of a QUD in 1996 in order to formalize [conversational relevance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_principle#Maxim_of_relation_%28relevance%29) and explain its consequences for [information structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_structure) and [focus marking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_%28linguistics%29). It has subsequently become a staple of work in semantics and pragmatics, playing a role in analyses of disparate phenomena including [donkey anaphora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_anaphora) and [presupposition](wikipedia://en/1965727) projection.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::
