# Colm Mulcahy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Colm Mulcahy"}

**Colm Mulcahy** (born September 1958) is an Irish mathematician, academic, columnist, book author, public outreach speaker, amateur magician and Professor Emeritus at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College). In addition to algebra, number theory, and geometry, his interests include [mathemagical card magic](wikipedia://en/2164767) and the culture of mathematics–particularly the contributions of Irish mathematicians and also the works of iconic mathematics writer [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435).

:::

In 2024 he received the [Maths Week Ireland](wikipedia://en/65912318) Award.[^o3]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mulcahy got his BSc and MSc in mathematical science at [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) in 1978 and 1979, and a PhD from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1985 where his advisor was [Alex F. T. W. Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/47675768).[^o31] From 1988 to 2020, he taught mathematics at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College), in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta), Georgia, and is now Professor Emeritus.[^o6] He served as chair of the department there from 2003 to 2006 and recently created the Archive of Spelman Mathematicians.[^o5] In 2014 he was one of the organizers of [Mathematics Awareness Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_Awareness_Month).[^o6]

He has blogged for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), [The Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huffington_Post), [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American), and (aperiodically) for The Aperiodical.[^o7] His puzzles have been featured in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* and *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*.\<ref=numberland>[Algebra-cadabra! Here's a classic magic trick, and the mathematical secret behind it](https://www.theguardian.com/science/alexs-adventures-in-numberland/2014/feb/04/mathematics) by Alex Bellos, The Guardian, Tue 4 Feb 2014 \</ref>[^o8][^o9] Mulcahy serves on the Advisory Council of the [Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Mathematics) in New York City.[^o10] From January 2021 to December 2024, he was the Chair of [Gathering 4 Gardner, Inc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner,_Inc).[^o11] He is the creator and curator of the *Annals of Irish Mathematics and Mathematicians*.[^o12]

Mulcahy has an [Erdös number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 2, as a result of a collaboration with [Neil Calkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Calkin).[^o13]

## Legacy of Martin Gardner {#Legacy_of_Martin_Gardner}

Mulcahy was a friend of longtime Scientific American columnist [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) during the last decade of Gardner's life. Mulcahy is a mainstay of [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) (Vice-President 2016-2020, Chair 2021–2024), an organisation formed to honour the wide-ranging contributions made by the celebrated mathematician, skeptic, magician, philosopher, and writer. Usually shortened to G4G, it first met in 1993 and since 1996 meets every two years.[^o15] In 2013 he created the official Martin Gardner site, and in 2013–2014 he chaired the Martin Gardner Centennial Committee.[^o16] Mulcahy has been particularly active in promoting an associated series of meetings known as [Celebration of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner#Celebration_of_Mind_events), also inspired by the works of Gardner.[^o17] These days there are over 100 of these latter events every year, all around the world.[^o18][^o19]

## Irish mathematicians and mathematics {#Irish_mathematicians_and_mathematics}

Mulcahy frequently writes about the culture and history of mathematics in Ireland.[^o21] He is active in both [Maths Week Ireland](wikipedia://en/65912318), the world's largest mathematics outreach program, and the [Irish Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Mathematical_Society).[^o22] He is the creator and curator of the *Annals of Irish Mathematics and Mathematicians*,[^o23] which chronicles four centuries of mathematical activity in Ireland, and has hosted monthly blogs since September 2016.[^o24] This is now sponsored by Maths Week Ireland, which has produced annual Irish Mathematics Calendars since 2016.[^o25]

## Card magic {#Card_magic}

Mulcahy is recognised as an authority on the mathematical principles and effects underlying cards tricks.[^o17] From 2004 to 2014 he authored *Card Colm*, a column about mathematics and magic–especially card magic–for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o27] Much of this work is collected in his book *Mathematical Card Magic: Fifty-Two New Effects* [^o28] He has appeared in Brady Haran's [Numberphile](wikipedia://en/61716514) web series.[^o29]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1997 Mulcahy received the MAA's [Allendoerfer Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allendoerfer_Award) for excellence in expository writing for a paper on the basics of [wavelet image compression](wikipedia://en/3352292#Wavelet_compression).[^o31] An article he co-authored, on the centennial of Martin Gardner, was featured in the book, *The Best Writing on Mathematics 2015*.[^o32]

In 2024 he received the [Maths Week Ireland Award](wikipedia://en/65912318#Maths_Week_Ireland_Award) for outstanding work in raising public awareness of mathematics. In his acceptance remarks, Mulcahy said,[^o33]

Maths is a language that explains so much of the world around us: the sun, moon, planets and stars, hence the calendar and tides here on earth. That’s how it started for us humans; that and commerce. It’s about observation, curiosity and patterns, explaining symmetries and coincidences, navigating on earth and in space, and making reliable predictions.

## Personal {#Personal}

Mulcahy was married to [Vicki Powers](wikipedia://en/61655205),[^o35] an algebraic geometer and election theorist. They had the same doctoral advisor at Cornell, Alex F. T. W. Rosenberg.[^o36]
