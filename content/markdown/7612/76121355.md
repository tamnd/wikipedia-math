# Filon quadrature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Filon quadrature"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **Filon quadrature** or **Filon's method** is a technique for [numerical integration](wikipedia://en/170089) of [oscillatory](wikipedia://en/475449) integrals. It is named after English mathematician [Louis Napoleon George Filon](wikipedia://en/36568151), who first described the method in 1934.[^o14]

:::

## Description {#Description}

The method is applied to oscillatory definite integrals in the form:

$$\int_a^b f(x) g(x) dx$$

where $f(x)$ is a relatively slowly-varying function and $g(x)$ is either [sine or cosine](wikipedia://en/5010838) or a complex exponential that causes the rapid oscillation of the integrand, particularly for high frequencies. In Filon quadrature, the $f(x)$ is divided into $2N$ subintervals of length $h$, which are then [interpolated](wikipedia://en/14569) by [parabolas](wikipedia://en/23231). Since each subinterval is now converted into a [Fourier integral](wikipedia://en/52247) of [quadratic polynomials](wikipedia://en/187240), these can be evaluated in closed-form by [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252). For the case of $g(x)=\cos(kx)$, the integration formula is given as:[^o14][^o10]

$$\int_a^b f(x) \cos(kx) dx \approx h ( \alpha \left[ f(b) \sin(kb)-f(a) \sin(ka)\right] + \beta C_{2n} + \gamma C_{2n-1} )$$

where

$$\alpha=\left(\theta^2 + \theta \sin(\theta)\cos(\theta)-2 \sin^2(\theta)\right)/\theta^3$$

$$\beta=2\left[\theta (1+\cos^2(\theta)) - 2\sin(\theta)\cos(\theta) \right]/\theta^3$$

$$\gamma=4(\sin(\theta)-\theta \cos(\theta))/\theta^3$$

$$C_{2n}=\frac{1}{2}f(a)\cos(ka) + f(a+2h)\cos(k(a+2h)) + f(a+4h)\cos(k(a+4h)) + \ldots + \frac{1}{2}f(b)\cos(kb)$$

$$C_{2n-1}=f(a+h)\cos(k(a+h)) + f(a+3h)\cos(k(a+3h)) + \ldots + f(b-h)\cos(k(b-h))$$

$$\theta=kh$$

Explicit Filon integration formulas for sine and complex exponential functions can be derived similarly.[^o10] The formulas above fail for small $\theta$ values due to [catastrophic cancellation](wikipedia://en/19985542);[^o7] [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) approximations must be in such cases to mitigate numerical errors, with $\theta=1/6$ being recommended as a possible switchover point for 44-bit [mantissa](wikipedia://en/419250).[^o10]

Modifications, extensions and generalizations of Filon quadrature have been reported in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) literature; these are known as Filon-type integration methods.[^o16][^o19] These include Filon-[trapezoidal](wikipedia://en/573452)[^o10] and Filon–[Clenshaw–Curtis](wikipedia://en/7020660) methods.[^o12]

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Filon quadrature is widely used in physics and engineering for robust computation of Fourier-type integrals. Applications include evaluation of oscillatory [Sommerfeld integrals](wikipedia://en/3644058) for [electromagnetic](wikipedia://en/3849994) and [seismic](wikipedia://en/28935) problems in layered media[^o8][^o17][^o9] and numerical solution to steady [incompressible flow](wikipedia://en/461477) problems in [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988),[^o11] as well as various different problems in [neutron scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_scattering),[^o15] [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202)[^o13] and [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy).[^o18]
