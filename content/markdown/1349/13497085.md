# David X. Li

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David X. Li"}

[Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_%28surname%29)

**David X. Li** ([^o6] born [Nanjing, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing,_China) in the 1960s) is a Chinese-born Canadian [quantitative analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analyst) and [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405) who pioneered the use of [Gaussian copula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_copula) models for the pricing of [collateralized debt obligations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateralized_debt_obligation) (CDOs) in the early 2000s.[^o3][^o4][^o5] The *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)* has called him "the world's most influential actuary",[^o6] while after the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), to which Li's model has been partly credited to blame,[^o6][^o3] his model has been called a "recipe for disaster" in the hands of those who did not fully understand his research and misapplied it.[^o3] Widespread application of simplified Gaussian copula models to [financial products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_products) such as [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities) may have contributed to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^o3][^o7] David Li is currently an adjunct professor at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in the Statistics and Actuarial Sciences department.[^o12]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Li was born as Li Xianglin and raised in a rural part of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) during the 1960s;[^o3] his family had been relocated during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) to a rural village in southern China for "re-education".[^o6] Li received a [master's degree in economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Economics) from [Nankai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nankai_University).[^o6] After leaving China in 1987 at the behest of the Chinese government to study [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) in [Western society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world),[^o6] he earned an [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) from [Laval University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laval_University) in [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec), a [MMath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMath) in [Actuarial Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_Science) and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in statistics from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in [Waterloo, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo,_Ontario)[^o3] in 1995 with a thesis titled "*An estimating function approach to credibility theory"* under the supervision of [Harry Panjer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Panjer).[^o9][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Li began his career in finance in 1997 at the [Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Imperial_Bank_of_Commerce) in the World Markets division.[^o3] He moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in 2000 where he became a partner in [J.P. Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.P._Morgan)'s RiskMetrics unit.[^o5] By 2003 he was director and global head of credit [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_%28finance%29) research at [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup).[^o6] In 2004 he moved to [Barclays Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Capital) and lead the credit [quantitative analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analyst) team.[^o3] In 2008 Li moved to [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing) where he worked for [China International Capital Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_International_Capital_Corporation) as the head of the [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management) department.[^o3]

David Li is currently an adjunct professor at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) in the Statistics and Actuarial Sciences department.[^o12] He is also a professor at the [Shanghai Advanced Institute of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Advanced_Institute_of_Finance) (SAIF).[^o12]

## CDOs and Gaussian copula {#CDOs_and_Gaussian_copula}

Li's paper "On Default Correlation: A Copula Function Approach"[^o4] was the first appearance of the [Gaussian copula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_copula) applied to CDOs published in 2000, which quickly became a tool for financial institutions to correlate associations between multiple financial [securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_%28finance%29).[^o3] This allowed for CDOs to be supposedly accurately priced for a wide range of investments that were previously too complex to price, such as [mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgages).

However, after the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), the model has been seen as a "recipe for disaster".[^o3] According to [Nassim Nicholas Taleb](wikipedia://en/1093496), "People got very excited about the Gaussian copula because of its mathematical elegance, but the thing never worked. Co-association between securities is not measurable using [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057)"; in other words, "anything that relies on correlation is charlatanism."[^o3]

Li himself apparently understood the fallacy of his model, in 2005 saying "Very few people understand the essence of the model."[^o16] Li also wrote that "The current copula framework gains its popularity owing to its simplicity....However, there is little theoretical justification of the current framework from financial economics....We essentially have a credit portfolio model without solid credit portfolio theory."[^o14] Kai Gilkes of [CreditSights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CreditSights) says "Li can't be blamed"; although he invented the model, it was the bankers who misinterpreted and misused it.[^o3]

### Li's paper {#Li's_paper}

Li's paper is called "On Default Correlation: A Copula Function Approach" (2000), published in *[Journal of Fixed Income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Fixed_Income)*, Vol. 9, Issue 4, pages 43–54.[^o16][^o4] In section 1 through 5.3, Li describes actuarial math that sets the stage for his theory. The mathematics are from established statistical theory, actuarial models, and probability theory. In section 5.4, he uses [Gaussian copula](wikipedia://en/1793003#Gaussian_copula) to measure event relationships, or mathematically, *correlations*, between random economic events, expressed as:

$$C_\rho(u,v) = \Phi \left(\Phi^{-1}(u), \Phi^{-1}(v); \rho \right)$$

In layman's terms, he proposes to quantify the relationship between two events "House A" defaulting and "House B" defaulting by looking at the dependence between their time-unit-default (or survival time; see [survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259)). While under some scenarios (such as real estate) this correlation appeared to work most of the time, the underlying problem is that the single numerical data of correlation is a poor way to summarize history, and hence is not enough to predict the future. From section 6.0 onward, the paper presents experimental results using the Gaussian copula.
