# Jean Prestet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Prestet"}

**Jean Prestet** (1648–1690) was a French [Oratorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus) [priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_%28Catholic_Church%29) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who contributed to the fields of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170)[^o4][^o5] and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

Prestet grew up poor. As a teenager, he worked as a servant of the [Oratory of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). He was promoted to secretary for [Nicolas Malebranche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Malebranche), who taught him mathematics.

Under the guidance of Malebranche, Prestet began work in 1670 on the textbook *Elémens des Mathématiques*.[^o6] Unusually for the time, the textbook focused exclusively on algebra but did not cover geometry at all.[^o7] Prestet believed that algebra was the most fundamental field of mathematics, and geometry merely applied algebra.[^o8] Gert Schubring writes that "\[t\]he self-confidence of Prestet in claiming superiority for the 'moderns' over the 'ancients' … proved to be a bold and modernizing approach, disseminating [Cartesian](wikipedia://en/25525) conceptions and preparing the way for [rationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalism) in France."[^o8]

The book contained a proof of [Descartes' rule of signs](wikipedia://en/2641493) that Prestet later acknowledged to be incomplete.[^o9] It also included a generalization of [Euclid's lemma](wikipedia://en/826617) to non-prime divisors.[^o10]

*Elémens* was published in 1675 by the Oratorian order for use in the curriculum of the many Oratorian colleges. Starting in the early 17th century, the order had founded colleges in smaller cities and towns to challenge the influence of the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus). *Elémens* was one of several textbooks published around that time by notable Oratorian mathematics instructors including [Bernard Lamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lamy).[^o8] [Abraham de Moivre](wikipedia://en/57327) used *Elémens* in an abortive early attempt to teach himself mathematics.[^o11]

With the publication of *Elémens*, Prestet's reputation as a mathematics instructor grew. He was appointed the mathematical chair at the [University of Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Angers) in 1681. A revised and expanded edition, titled *Nouveaux Elémens des mathématiques*, was published in 1689.[^o12] This edition included some early work on the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556).[^o3]
