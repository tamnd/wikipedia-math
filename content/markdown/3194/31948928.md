# Circle packing in an equilateral triangle

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the smallest possible [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) which an amount $n$ of [unit circles](wikipedia://en/27072071) can be packed into?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Circle packing in an equilateral triangle"}

**Circle packing in an equilateral triangle** is a [packing problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packing_problem) in [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) where the objective is to pack $n$ [unit circles](wikipedia://en/27072071) into the smallest possible [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285). Optimal solutions have been proved for $n \le 15$, and for any [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) of circles, and conjectures are available for $n \le 34$.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

A conjecture of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and Norman Oler states that, if $n$ is a triangular number, then the optimal packings of $n - 1$ and of $n$ circles have the same side length: that is, according to the conjecture, an optimal packing for $n - 1$ circles can be found by removing any single circle from the optimal hexagonal packing of $n$ circles.[^o7] This conjecture is now known to be true for $n \le 15$.[^o8] In a paper by Graham and Lubachevsky concerning solutions for $22 \le n \le 34$ they also conjectured seven infinite families of optimal solutions in addition to the one by Erdős and Oler. These families give conjectured solutions for many more numbers, including $n = 37, 40, 42, 43, 46, 49$.[^o5]
