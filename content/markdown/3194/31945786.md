# N-ellipse

*Image: N-ellipse.svg.* Examples of 3-ellipses for three given foci. The progression of the distances is not linear.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="N-ellipse"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **$n$-ellipse** is a [generalization](wikipedia://en/12746) of the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) allowing more than two [foci](wikipedia://en/1032607).[^o8] $n$-ellipses go by numerous other names, including **multifocal ellipse**,[^o2] **polyellipse**,[^o6] **egglipse**,[^o7] **$k$-ellipse**,[^o3] and **Tschirnhaus'sche Eikurve** (after [Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus](wikipedia://en/26456397)). They were first investigated by [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696) in 1846.[^o9]

:::

Given $n$ focal points $(u_{i},\,v_{i})$ in a plane, an $n$-ellipse is the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of points of the plane whose sum of distances to the $n$ foci is a constant $d$. In formulas, this is the set

$$\left\{(x, y) \in \mathbf{R}^2: \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt{(x-u_i)^2 + (y-v_i)^2} = d\right\}.$$

The 1-ellipse is the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), and the 2-ellipse is the classic ellipse. Both are [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) of degree 2.

For any number $n$ of foci, the $n$-ellipse is a [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_curve), [convex curve](wikipedia://en/29747989).[^o2] The curve is [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) unless it goes through a focus.[^o3]

The *n*-ellipse is in general a subset of the points satisfying a particular [algebraic equation](wikipedia://en/404001).[^o3] If *n* is [odd](wikipedia://en/143135), the algebraic degree of the curve is $2^n$, while if *n* is [even](wikipedia://en/143135) the degree is $2^n - \binom{n}{n/2}.$[^o3]

*n*-ellipses are special cases of [spectrahedra](wikipedia://en/28733878).
