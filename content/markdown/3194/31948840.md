# Square packing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Square packing"}

**Square packing** is a [packing problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packing_problem) where the objective is to determine how many [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) can be packed into some larger shape, often a square or circle.

:::

## In a square {#In_a_square}

**Square packing in a square** is the problem of determining the maximum number of [unit squares](wikipedia://en/285301) (squares of side length one) that can be packed inside a larger square of side length $a$. If $a$ is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), the answer is $a^2,$ but the precise – or even [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) – amount of unfilled space for an arbitrary non-integer $a$ is an open question.[^o3]

The smallest value of $a$ that allows the packing of $n$ unit squares is known when $n$ is a perfect square (in which case it is $\sqrt{n}$), as well as for $n={}$2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 13, 14, 15, 24, 34, 35, 46, 47, and 48. For most of these numbers (with the exceptions only of 5 and 10), the packing is the natural one with axis-aligned squares, and $a$ is $\lceil\sqrt{n}\,\rceil$, where $\lceil\,\ \rceil$ is the [ceiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceiling_function) (round up) function.[^o10][^o4] The figure shows the optimal packings for 5 and 10 squares, the two smallest numbers of squares for which the optimal packing involves tilted squares.[^o5][^o6]

The smallest unresolved case is $n=11$. It is known that 11 unit squares cannot be packed in a square of side length less than $\textstyle 2+\frac{4}{\sqrt{5}} \approx 3.789$. By contrast, the tightest known packing of 11 squares is inside a square of side length approximately 3.877084 found by [Walter Trump](wikipedia://en/2885379).[^o5][^o7]

The smallest case where the best known packing involves squares at three different angles is $n=17$. It was discovered in 1998 by John Bidwell, an undergraduate student at the [University of Hawaiʻi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hawaiʻi), and has side length $a\approx 4.6756$.[^o5]

*Image: Mutilated chessboard vectorized.svg.* A [mutilated chessboard](wikipedia://en/9308202), an optimal packing for $n^{2} - 2$ squares

Below are the minimum solutions for values up to $n = 16$; the case $n=11$ remains unresolved.

::: {#o9 .table cue=table}

| Number of unit squares $n$ | Minimal side length $a$ of big square |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2, 3, 4 | 2 |
| 5 | $2+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 2.707\ldots$ |
| 6, 7, 8, 9 | 3 |
| 10 | $3+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 3.707\ldots$ |
| 11 | \3.877... ? |
| 12, ..., 16 | 4 |

:::

Some numbers of unit squares are never the optimal number in a packing. In particular, if a square of size $a\times a$ allows the packing of $n^2-2$ unit squares, then it must be the case that $a\ge n$ and that a packing of $n^2$ unit squares is also possible.[^o10]

### Asymptotic results {#Asymptotic_results}

::: {#o12 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the asymptotic growth rate of wasted space for square packing in a half-integer square?

:::

For larger values of the side length $a$, the exact number of unit squares that can pack an $a\times a$ square remains unknown. It is always possible to pack a $\lfloor a\rfloor \!\times\! \lfloor a\rfloor$ grid of axis-aligned unit squares, but this may leave a large area, approximately $2a(a-\lfloor a\rfloor)$, uncovered and wasted.[^o5] Instead, [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) showed that for a different packing by tilted unit squares, the wasted space could be significantly reduced to $O(a^{7/11}) \subseteq o(a^{0.637})$ (the latter written in [little o notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_o_notation)).[^o13] Later, Graham and [Fan Chung](wikipedia://en/246562) further reduced the wasted space to $O(a^{0.631})$,[^o14] and subsequent work reduced the wasted space to $O(a^{0.625})$,[^o15] and then $O(a^{0.6})$.[^o16] However, as [Klaus Roth](wikipedia://en/787827) and [Bob Vaughan](wikipedia://en/4871951) [proved](wikipedia://en/82285), all solutions must waste space at least $\Omega\bigl((a \cdot |a-\operatorname{round} a|)^{1/2}\bigr)$. In particular, when $a$ is a [half-integer](wikipedia://en/1568608), the wasted space is at least proportional to its [square root](wikipedia://en/29208).[^o17] The precise [asymptotic growth rate](wikipedia://en/641995) of the wasted space, even for half-integer side lengths, remains an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335).[^o3]

## In a circle {#In_a_circle}

**Square packing in a circle** is a related problem of packing *n* unit squares into a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) with radius as small as possible. For this problem, good solutions are known for *n* up to 35. Here are the minimum known solutions for up to $n = 12$ (although only the cases $n = 1, 2$ and $n = 4$ are known to be optimal):[^o20]

::: {#o21 .table cue=table}

| Number of squares | Circle radius |
|---|---|
| 1 | $\sqrt{2}/2 \approx 0.707\ldots$ |
| 2 | $\sqrt{5}/2 \approx 1.118\ldots$ |
| 3 | $5\sqrt{17}/16 \approx 1.288\ldots$ |
| 4 | $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\ldots$ |
| 5 | $\sqrt{10}/2 \approx 1.581\ldots$ |
| 6 | \1.688... |
| 7 | $\sqrt{13}/2 \approx 1.802\ldots$ |
| 8 | \1.978... |
| 9 | $\sqrt{1105}/16 \approx 2.077\ldots$ |
| 10 | $3\sqrt{2}/2 \approx 2.121\ldots$ |
| 11 | \2.214... |
| 12 | $\sqrt{5} \approx 2.236\ldots$ |

:::

## In other shapes {#In_other_shapes}

Packing squares into other shapes can have high [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511): testing whether a given number of axis-parallel unit squares can fit into a given [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete). It remains NP-complete even for a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530) (with no holes) that is [orthogonally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_convexity), with [axis-parallel sides](wikipedia://en/8779182), and with [half-integer](wikipedia://en/1568608) vertex coordinates.[^o23]
