# Kate Okikiolu

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Kate Okikiolu"}

**Kate Adebola Okikiolu** (born 1965) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4] She is known for her work with [elliptic differential operators](wikipedia://en/550137) as well as her work with inner-city children.[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Okikiolu was born in 1965 in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England). Her father was George Olatokunbo Okikiolu, a renowned [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria) mathematician[^o7] and the most published black mathematician on record.[^o8] Her [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) mother was a high school mathematics teacher. Okikiolu received a B.A. in mathematics from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1987. In 1991 she earned her Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of California at Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Los_Angeles),[^o9] for her thesis *The Analogue of the Strong Szego Limit Theorem on the Torus and the 3-Sphere*.[^o8][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Based on her PhD work, Okikiolu resolved a conjecture of [Peter Wilcox Jones](wikipedia://en/1604461) concerning a continuous version of the [travelling salesman problem](wikipedia://en/31248)[^o13] in her paper *Characterization of subsets of rectifiable curves in* $\mathbf{R}^{n}.$[^o14] Okikiolu was an instructor and later assistant professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1993 to 1995. She then worked as a visiting assistant professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and joined the faculty at the [University of California at San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_San_Diego) in 1995.[^o10] In 2011 she joined the Mathematics Department at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University).[^o15]

She was an invited speaker at the 1996 meeting of the [Association of Women in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Women_in_Mathematics).[^o16] She also delivered the Claytor-Woodard lecture at the 2002 meeting of the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356), an organization for African-American mathematicians.[^o10]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1997, Okikiolu won a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship),[^o18] becoming the first black recipient of this fellowship. In 1997 she also was awarded a [Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers)[^o19] for both her mathematical research and her development of mathematics curricula for inner-city school children. This award is given to only 60 scientists and engineers each year and has a prize of \$500,000.[^o10] In 2009, she presented the [Etta Z. Falconer Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecture) on "The Sum of Squares of Wavelengths of a Closed Surface"[^o20] an award given by the [AWM](wikipedia://en/9858698) for women with distinguished careers in mathematics or mathematics education.
