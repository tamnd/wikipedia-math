# Schulz–Zimm distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schulz–Zimm distribution"}

The **Schulz–Zimm distribution** is a special case of the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079). It is widely used to model the [polydispersity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polydispersity) of [polymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer). In this context it has been introduced in 1939 by [Günter Victor Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Günter_Victor_Schulz)[^o3] and in 1948 by [Bruno H. Zimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_H._Zimm).[^o4]

:::

This distribution has only a shape parameter *k*, the scale being fixed at *θ*=1/*k*. Accordingly, the probability density function is

$$f(x)=\frac{k^k x^{k - 1} e^{-kx}}{\Gamma(k)},$$

where *Γ(x)* is the [Gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).

When applied to polymers, the variable *x* is the relative mass or chain length $x=M/M_n$. Accordingly, the mass distribution $f(M)$ is just a gamma distribution with scale parameter $\theta=M_n/k$. This explains why the Schulz–Zimm distribution is unheard of outside its conventional application domain.

The distribution has mean 1 and variance 1/*k*. The polymer dispersity is $\langle x^2\rangle / \langle x\rangle = 1+1/k$.

For large *k* the Schulz–Zimm distribution approaches a [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution). In algorithms where one needs to draw samples $x\ge 0$, the Schulz–Zimm distribution is to be preferred over a Gaussian because the latter requires an arbitrary cut-off to prevent negative *x*.
