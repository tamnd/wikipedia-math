# Glaisher–Kinkelin constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glaisher–Kinkelin constant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Glaisher–Kinkelin constant** or **Glaisher's constant**, typically denoted $A$, is a [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551), related to [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) like the [$K$-function](wikipedia://en/2060130) and the [Barnes $G$-function](wikipedia://en/2060183). The constant also appears in a number of [sums](wikipedia://en/246160) and [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532), especially those involving the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809). It is named after [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [James Whitbread Lee Glaisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whitbread_Lee_Glaisher) and [Hermann Kinkelin](wikipedia://en/38058761).

:::

Its approximate value is:

$A$ = \1.28242712910062263687... .

Glaisher's constant plays a role both in mathematics and in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). It appears when giving a closed form expression for [Porter's constant](wikipedia://en/53629370), when estimating the efficiency of the [Euclidean algorithm](wikipedia://en/10377). It also is connected to solutions of [Painlevé differential equations](wikipedia://en/2033003) and the [Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317).[^o7]

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Glaisher–Kinkelin constant $A$ can be defined via the following [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602):[^o4]

$$A=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \frac{H(n)}{n^{\tfrac{n^2}{2}+\tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac{1}{12}}\,e^{-\tfrac{n^2}{4}}}$$

where $H(n)$ is the [hyperfactorial](wikipedia://en/507209):

$$H(n)= \prod_{i=1}^{n} i^i = 1^1\cdot 2^2\cdot 3^3 \cdot {...}\cdot n^n$$

An analogous limit, presenting a similarity between $A$ and $\sqrt{2\pi}$, is given by [Stirling's formula](wikipedia://en/151783) as:

$$\sqrt{2\pi}=\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n!}{n^{n+\frac12}\,e^{-n}}$$

with

$$n!= \prod_{i=1}^{n} i = 1 \cdot 2\cdot 3\cdot {...} \cdot n$$

which shows that just as *π* is obtained from approximation of the [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606), *A* is obtained from the approximation of the hyperfactorials.

:::

## Relation to special functions {#Relation_to_special_functions}

Just as the factorials can be extended to the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) by the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) such that $\Gamma(n)=(n-1)!$ for positive integers *n*, the hyperfactorials can be extended by the [K-function](wikipedia://en/2060130)[^o6] with $K(n)= H(n-1)$ also for positive integers *n*, where:

$$K(z)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{z-1}2} \exp\left[\binom{z}{2}+\int_0^{z-1} \ln \Gamma(t + 1)\,dt\right]$$

This gives:[^o7]

$$A=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \frac{K(n+1)}{n^{\tfrac{n^2}{2}+\tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac{1}{12}}\,e^{-\tfrac{n^2}{4}}}.$$

A related function is the [Barnes $G$-function](wikipedia://en/2060183) which is given by

$$G(n)=\frac{(\Gamma(n))^{n-1}}{K(n)}$$

and for which a similar limit exists:[^o4]

$$\frac1A=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \frac{G(n+1)}{\left(2\pi\right)^\frac{n}{2} n^{\frac{n^2}{2}-\frac{1}{12}} e^{-\frac{3n^2}{4}+\frac{1}{12}}}.$$

The Glaisher-Kinkelin constant also appears in the evaluation of the K-function and Barnes-G function at half and quarter [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) values such as:[^o7][^o8]

$$K(1/2) = \frac{A^{3/2}}{2^{1/24}e^{1/8}}$$

$$K(1/4) = A^{9/8}\exp\left(\frac{G}{4\pi}-\frac{3}{32}\right)$$

$$G(1/2) = \frac{2^{1/24}e^{1/8}}{A^{3/2}\pi^{1/4}}$$

$$G(1/4) = \frac{1}{2^{9/16}A^{9/8}\pi^{3/16}\varpi^{3/8}}\exp\left(\frac{3}{32}-\frac{G}{4\pi}\right)$$

with $G$ being [Catalan's constant](wikipedia://en/46860) and $\varpi=\frac{\Gamma(1/4)^2}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}$ being the [lemniscate constant](wikipedia://en/2079065).

Similar to the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/4239318#Products), there exists a multiplication formula for the K-function. It involves Glaisher's constant:[^o10]

$$\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}K\left(\frac jn \right) = A^{\frac{n^2-1}{n}}n^{-\frac{1}{12n}}e^{\frac{1-n^2}{12n}}$$

The [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of *G*(*z* + 1) has the following [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090), as established by Barnes:[^o9]

$$\ln G(z+1) = \frac{z^2}{2} \ln z - \frac{3z^2}{4} + \frac{z}{2}\ln 2\pi -\frac{1}{12} \ln z + \left(\frac{1}{12}-\ln A \right)+\sum_{k=1}^N \frac{B_{2k + 2}}{4k\left(k + 1\right)z^{2k}}+O\left(\frac{1}{z^{2N + 2}}\right)$$

The Glaisher-Kinkelin constant is related to the derivatives of the [Euler-constant function](wikipedia://en/148594#Euler-constant_function):[^o10][^o11]

$$\gamma'(-1)= \frac{11}{6}\ln2 + 6\ln A - \frac32 \ln\pi - 1$$

$$\gamma''(-1) = \frac{10}{3}\ln2 + 24\ln A - 4 \ln\pi - \frac{7\zeta(3)}{2\pi^2}- \frac{13}{4}$$

$A$ also is related to the [Lerch transcendent](wikipedia://en/1342978):[^o12]

$$\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial s}(-1,-1,1)=3\ln A - \frac13\ln2 - \frac 14$$

Glaisher's constant may be used to give values of the derivative of the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) as closed form expressions, such as:[^o4][^o13]

$$\zeta'(-1)=\frac{1}{12}-\ln A$$

$$\zeta'(2)=\frac{\pi^2} 6 \left( \gamma+\ln 2\pi - 12 \ln A \right)$$

where $\gamma$ is the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant).

## Series expressions {#Series_expressions}

The above formula for $\zeta'(2)$ gives the following series:[^o4]

$$\sum_{k=2}^\infty \frac{\ln k}{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2} 6 \left( 12 \ln A - \gamma-\ln 2\pi \right)$$

which directly leads to the following product found by [Glaisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whitbread_Lee_Glaisher):

$$\prod_{k=1}^\infty k^\frac{1}{k^2} = \left(\frac{A^{12}}{2\pi e^\gamma} \right)^\frac{\pi^2}{6}$$

Similarly it is

$$\sum_{k\ge 3}^{k\text{ odd}} \frac{\ln k}{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{24} \left( 36 \ln A - 3 \gamma - \ln 16\pi^3 \right)$$

which gives:

$$\prod_{k\ge 3}^{k\text{ odd}} k^\frac{1}{k^2} = \left(\frac{A^{36}}{16\pi^3 e^{3\gamma}} \right)^\frac{\pi^2}{24}$$

An alternative product formula, defined over the [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers), reads:[^o15]

$$\prod_{p \text{ prime}} p^\frac{1}{p^2-1} = \frac{A^{12}}{2\pi e^\gamma},$$

Another product is given by:[^o10]

$$\prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(\frac{en^n}{(n+1)^n}\right)^{(-1)^{n-1}} = \frac{2^{1/6} e\sqrt\pi}{A^6}$$

A series involving the [cosine integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine_integral) is:[^o16]

$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\text{Ci}(2k\pi)}{k^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}(4\ln A -1)$$

[Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) gave another series representation for the logarithm of Glaisher's constant, following from a series for the Riemann zeta function:[^o17]

$$\ln A=\frac 1 8 - \frac 1 2 \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac 1 {n+1} \sum_{k=0}^n (-1)^k \binom n k (k+1)^2 \ln(k+1)$$

## Integrals {#Integrals}

The following are some definite integrals involving Glaisher's constant:[^o7]

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{x \ln x}{e^{2 \pi x}-1} \, dx = \frac 1 {24}-\frac 1 2 \ln A$$

$$\int_0^\frac12 \ln\Gamma(x) \, dx = \frac 3 2 \ln A+\frac 5 {24} \ln 2+\frac 1 4 \ln \pi$$

the latter being a special case of:[^o19]

$$\int_0^z \ln \Gamma(x)\,dx=\frac{z(1-z)}{2}+\frac{z}{2}\ln 2\pi +z\ln\Gamma(z) -\ln G(1+z)$$

A double integral is given by:[^o17]

$$\int_0^1 \int_0^1 \frac{-x}{(1+xy)^2\ln xy}dxdy= 6\ln A - \frac16 \ln 2 - \frac12 \ln\pi - \frac12$$

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Glaisher-Kinkelin constant can be viewed as the first constant in a sequence of infinitely many so-called *generalized Glaisher constants* or *Bendersky constants*.[^o7] They emerge from studying the following product:

$$\prod_{m=1}^{n} m^{m^k} = 1^{1^k} \cdot 2^{2^k}\cdot 3^{3^k}\cdot {...} \cdot n^{n^k}$$

Setting $k = 0$ gives the factorial $n!$, while choosing $k = 1$ gives the hyperfactorial $H(n)$.

Defining the following function

$$P_k(n) = \left(\frac{n^{k+1}}{k+1}+\frac{n^k}{2}+\frac{B_{k+1}}{k+1}\right)\ln n - \frac{n^{k+1}}{(k+1)^2}+k!\sum_{j=1}^{k-1} \frac{B_{j+1}}{(j+1)!}\frac{n^{k-j}}{(k-j)!}\left(\ln n + \sum_{i=1}^j \frac{1}{k-i+1}\right)$$

with the [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964) $B_k$ (and using $B_1=0$), one may approximate the above products asymptotically via $\exp({P_k(n)})$.

For $k = 0$ we get Stirling's approximation without the factor $\sqrt{2\pi}$ as $\exp({P_0(n)})=n^{n+\frac12} e^{-n}$.

For $k = 1$ we obtain $\exp({P_1(n)})=n^{\tfrac{n^2}{2}+\tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac{1}{12}}\,e^{-\tfrac{n^2}{4}}$, similar as in the limit definition of $A$.

This leads to the following definition of the generalized Glaisher constants:

$$A_k:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \left( e^{-P_k(n)} \prod_{m=1}^{n} m^{m^k}\right)$$

which may also be written as:

$$\ln A_k:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \left(-P_k(n)+\sum_{m=1}^{n} {m^k}\ln m\right)$$

This gives $A_0=\sqrt{2\pi}$ and $A_1=A$ and in general:[^o7][^o22][^o23]

$$A_k=\exp\left(\frac{B_{k+1}}{k+1}H_k-\zeta'(-k)\right)$$

with the [harmonic numbers](wikipedia://en/214729) $H_k$ and $H_0=0$.

Because of the formula

$$\zeta'(-2m)=(-1)^m \frac{(2m)!}{2(2\pi)^{2m}}\zeta(2m+1)$$

for $m > 0$, there exist closed form expressions for $A_{k}$ with even $k=2m$ in terms of the [values of the Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/2334098) such as:[^o7]

$$A_2=\exp\left(\frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^2}\right)$$

$$A_4=\exp\left(-\frac{3\zeta(5)}{4\pi^4}\right)$$

For odd $k=2m-1$ one can express the constants $A_{k}$ in terms of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function such as:

$$A_1=\exp\left(-\frac{\zeta'(2)}{2\pi^2}+\frac{\gamma+\ln2\pi}{12}\right)$$

$$A_3=\exp\left(\frac{3\zeta'(4)}{4\pi^4}-\frac{\gamma+\ln2\pi}{120}\right)$$
