# Heun function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heun function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **local Heun function** $H \ell (a,q;\alpha ,\beta, \gamma, \delta ; z)$[^o2] is the solution of **Heun's differential equation** that is holomorphic and 1 at the singular point *z* = 0. The local Heun function is called a **Heun function**, denoted *Hf*, if it is also regular at *z* = 1, and is called a **Heun polynomial**, denoted *Hp*, if it is regular at all three finite singular points *z* = 0, 1, *a*.

:::

It is named after German mathematician [Karl Heun](wikipedia://en/27382617).

## Heun's equation {#Heun's_equation}

Heun's equation is a second-order [linear](wikipedia://en/152111) [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753) (ODE) of the form

$$\frac {d^2w}{dz^2} + \left[\frac{\gamma}{z}+ \frac{\delta}{z-1} + \frac{\epsilon}{z-a} \right] \frac {dw}{dz} + \frac {\alpha \beta z -q} {z(z-1)(z-a)} w = 0.$$

The condition $\epsilon=\alpha+\beta-\gamma-\delta+1$ is taken so that the characteristic exponents for the regular singularity at infinity are α and β (see below).

The complex number *q* is called the **accessory parameter**. Heun's equation has four [regular singular points](wikipedia://en/3166061): 0, 1, *a* and ∞ with exponents (0, 1 − γ), (0, 1 − δ), (0, 1 − ϵ), and (α, β). Every second-order linear ODE on the extended complex plane with at most four regular singular points, such as the [Lamé equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamé_equation) or the [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation), can be transformed into this equation by a change of variable.

Coalescence of various regular singularities of the Heun equation into irregular singularities give rise to several confluent forms of the equation, as shown in the table below.

## Symmetries {#Symmetries}

Heun's equation has a group of symmetries of order 192, isomorphic to the [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) of the [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) *D*<sub>4</sub>, analogous to the 24 symmetries of the [hypergeometric differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equations) obtained by Kummer.

The symmetries fixing the local Heun function form a group of order 24 isomorphic to the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on 4 points, so there are 192/24 = 8 = 2 × 4 essentially different solutions given by acting on the local Heun function by these symmetries, which give solutions for each of the 2 exponents for each of the 4 singular points. The complete list of 192 symmetries was given by (Maier 2007)[^o12] using machine calculation. Several previous attempts by various authors to list these by hand contained many errors and omissions; for example, most of the 48 local solutions listed by Heun contain serious errors.

:::
