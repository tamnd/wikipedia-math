# Principal curvature

*Image: Minimal surface curvature planes-en.svg.* [Saddle surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddle_surface) with normal planes in directions of principal curvatures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principal curvature"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the two **principal curvatures** at a given point of a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) are the maximum and minimum values of the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) as expressed by the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator) at that point. They measure how the surface bends by different amounts in different directions at that point.

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

At each point *p* of a [differentiable](wikipedia://en/2119219) [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) in 3-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) one may choose a unit *[normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector)*. A *[normal plane](wikipedia://en/49965903)* at *p* is one that contains the normal vector, and will therefore also contain a unique direction tangent to the surface and cut the surface in a plane curve, called [normal section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_section). This curve will in general have different [curvatures](wikipedia://en/60770) for different normal planes at *p*. The **principal curvatures** at *p*, denoted *k*<sub>1</sub> and *k*<sub>2</sub>, are the maximum and minimum values of this curvature.

Here the curvature of a curve is by definition the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the [osculating circle](wikipedia://en/1896705). The curvature is taken to be positive if the curve turns in the same direction as the surface's chosen normal, and otherwise negative. The directions in the normal plane where the curvature takes its maximum and minimum values are always perpendicular, if *k*<sub>1</sub> does not equal *k*<sub>2</sub>, a result of [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1760), and are called **principal directions**. From a modern perspective, this theorem follows from the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) because these directions are as the [principal axes](wikipedia://en/11376126) of a [symmetric tensor](wikipedia://en/23087738)—the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078). A systematic analysis of the principal curvatures and principal directions was undertaken by [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux), using [Darboux frames](wikipedia://en/11067444).

The product *k*<sub>1</sub>*k*<sub>2</sub> of the two principal curvatures is the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623), *K*, and the average (*k*<sub>1</sub> + *k*<sub>2</sub>)/2 is the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210), *H*.

If at least one of the principal curvatures is zero at every point, then the [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) will be 0 and the surface is a [developable surface](wikipedia://en/2878111). For a [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734), the mean curvature is zero at every point.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *M* be a surface in Euclidean space with [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078) $I\!I(X,Y)$. Fix a point *p* ∈ *M*, and an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub> of tangent vectors at *p*. Then the principal curvatures are the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix

$$\left[I\!I_{ij}\right] = \begin{bmatrix} I\!I(X_1,X_1)&I\!I(X_1,X_2)\\ I\!I(X_2,X_1)&I\!I(X_2,X_2) \end{bmatrix}.$$

If *X*<sub>1</sub> and *X*<sub>2</sub> are selected so that the matrix $\left[I\!I_{ij}\right]$ is a diagonal matrix, then they are called the **principal directions**. If the surface is [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_%28mathematics%29), then one often requires that the pair (*X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>) be positively oriented with respect to the given orientation.

Without reference to a particular orthonormal basis, the principal curvatures are the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the [shape operator](wikipedia://en/15513875#Shape_operator), and the principal directions are its [eigenvectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector).

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

For hypersurfaces in higher-dimensional Euclidean spaces, the principal curvatures may be defined in a directly analogous fashion. The principal curvatures are the eigenvalues of the matrix of the second fundamental form $I\!I(X_i,X_j)$ in an orthonormal basis of the tangent space. The principal directions are the corresponding eigenvectors.

Similarly, if *M* is a hypersurface in a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *N*, then the principal curvatures are the eigenvalues of its second-fundamental form. If *k*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *k*<sub>n</sub> are the *n* principal curvatures at a point *p* ∈ *M* and *X*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*n*</sub> are corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors (principal directions), then the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) of *M* at *p* is given by

$$K(X_i,X_j) = k_ik_j$$

for all $i,j$ with $i\neq j$.

## Classification of points on a surface {#Classification_of_points_on_a_surface}

- At **elliptical** points, both principal curvatures have the same sign, and the surface is [locally](wikipedia://en/1462457) convex.
  - At **[umbilic points](wikipedia://en/9757480)**, both principal curvatures are equal and every tangent vector can be considered a principal direction. These typically occur in isolated points.
- At **hyperbolic** points, the principal curvatures have opposite signs, and the surface will be locally saddle shaped.
- At **parabolic** points, one of the principal curvatures is zero. Parabolic points generally lie in a curve separating elliptical and hyperbolic regions.
  - At **flat umbilic** points both principal curvatures are zero. A generic surface will not contain flat umbilic points. The [monkey saddle](wikipedia://en/812032) is one surface with an isolated flat umbilic.

## Line of curvature {#Line_of_curvature}

The **lines of curvature** or **curvature lines** are curves which are always tangent to a principal direction (they are [integral curves](wikipedia://en/1322061) for the principal direction fields). There will be two lines of curvature through each non-umbilic point and the lines will cross at right angles. They were first studied by [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840) in the context of the [optimal transport problem](wikipedia://en/6704603#Geometric_interpretation) in $\mathbb{R}^3$,[^o13] and in the context of engineering.[^o14] In a course of lectures given in 1795, Monge proposed that the [legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_assembly) for the government of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) should be a triaxial ellipsoidal dome. The lines of curvature are the guiding curves for the workers to put the stones. The candle lights hang from the two umbilic points. Straight below one umbilic point would be the podium for the speakers. The square-shaped holes between the curves can also be windows, a shape much more rational than the [rose windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_window) of [gothic churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture).[^o15]

In the vicinity of an umbilic the lines of curvature typically form one of three configurations **star**, **lemon** and **monstar** (derived from *lemon-star*).[^o16] These points are also called Darbouxian Umbilics (D<sub>1</sub>, D<sub>2</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>) in honor of [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux), the first to make a systematic study in Vol. 4, p 455, of his Leçons (1896).

In these figures, the red curves are the lines of curvature for one family of principal directions, and the blue curves for the other.

When a line of curvature has a local extremum of the same principal curvature then the curve has a **[ridge point](wikipedia://en/18896093)**. These ridge points form curves on the surface called **ridges**. The ridge curves pass through the umbilics. For the star pattern either 3 or 1 ridge line pass through the umbilic, for the monstar and lemon only one ridge passes through.[^o17]

## Applications {#Applications}

Principal curvature directions, along with the surface normal, define a 3D orientation frame at a surface point. For example, on a cylinder, along a certain unit vector, the surface is flat (parallel to the axis of the cylinder). The implication of such an orientation frame at each surface point means any rotation of the surfaces over time can be determined simply by considering the change in the corresponding orientation frames. This has resulted in single surface point [motion estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_estimation) and [segmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_segmentation) algorithms in computer vision.[^o20]
