# Lemniscate constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lemniscate constant"}

*Image: Lemniscate of Bernoulli.svg.* Lemniscate of Bernoulli

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **lemniscate constant** $\varpi$ is a [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) mathematical constant that is the ratio of the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of [Bernoulli's lemniscate](wikipedia://en/250908) to its [diameter](wikipedia://en/68283333), analogous to the definition of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) for the circle.[^o2] Equivalently, the perimeter of the lemniscate $(x^2+y^2)^2=x^2-y^2$ is $2\varpi$. The lemniscate constant is closely related to the [lemniscate elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/3754835) and is approximately equal to 2.62205755.[^o3] It also appears in evaluation of the [gamma](wikipedia://en/4239318) and [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) at certain rational values. The symbol $\varpi$ is a [cursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursive) variant of $\pi$ known as [variant pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variant_pi) represented in Unicode by the character 03D6.

:::

Sometimes the quantities $2\varpi$ or $\varpi/2$ are referred to as *the* lemniscate constant.[^o4][^o5]

## History {#History}

**Gauss's constant**, denoted by *G*, is equal to $\varpi / \pi \approx 0.8346268$[^o7] and named after [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), who calculated it via the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090) as $1/M\bigl(1,\sqrt{2}\bigr)$.[^n1] By 1799, Gauss had two proofs of the theorem that $M\bigl(1,\sqrt2\bigr)=\pi/\varpi$ where $\varpi$ is the lemniscate constant.[^o9]

[John Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Todd_%28mathematician%29) named two more lemniscate constants, the *first lemniscate constant* $A = \varpi/2 \approx 1.3110287771$ and the *second lemniscate constant* $B = \pi/(2\varpi) \approx 0.5990701173$.[^o10][^o11]

The lemniscate constant $\varpi$ and Todd's first lemniscate constant $A$ were proven [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) by [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) in 1932 and later by [Theodor Schneider](wikipedia://en/10312621) in 1937 and Todd's second lemniscate constant $B$ and Gauss's constant $G$ were proven transcendental by Theodor Schneider in 1941.[^o13][^o14] In 1975, [Gregory Chudnovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Chudnovsky) proved that $\pi$ and $\varpi$ are [algebraically independent](wikipedia://en/287808) over $\mathbb{Q}$, which implies that $A$ and $B$ are algebraically independent as well.[^o15][^o16] But the set $\bigl\{\pi,M\bigl(1,1/\sqrt{2}\bigr),M'\bigl(1,1/\sqrt{2}\bigr)\bigr\}$ (where the prime denotes the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) with respect to the second variable) is not algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.[^o17] In fact,

$$\pi=2\sqrt{2}\frac{M^3\left(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}{M'\left(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}=\frac{1}{G^3 M'\left(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)}.$$

In 1996, [Yuri Nesterenko](wikipedia://en/13129704) proved that $e^{\pi}$, $\pi$, and $\varpi$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.[^o18]

As of 2025 over 2 trillion digits of this constant have been calculated using [y-cruncher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-cruncher).[^o19]

## Forms {#Forms}

Usually, $\varpi$ is defined by the first equality below, but it has many equivalent forms:[^o21]

$$\begin{aligned} \varpi &= 2\int_0^1\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}} = \sqrt2\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1+t^4}} = \int_0^1\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t-t^3}} = \int_1^\infty \frac{dt}{\sqrt{t^3-t}}\\[6mu] &= 4\int_0^\infty\Bigl(\sqrt[4]{1+t^{4}}-t\Bigr)dt = 2\sqrt2\int_0^1 \sqrt[4]{1-t^{4}}\mathop{dt} =3\int_0^1 \sqrt{1-t^4}dt\\[2mu] &= 2K(i) = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{B}\bigl( \tfrac14, \tfrac12\bigr) = \tfrac{1}{2\sqrt2}\mathrm{B}\bigl( \tfrac14, \tfrac14\bigr) = \frac{\Gamma (1/4)^2}{2\sqrt{2\pi}} = \frac{2-\sqrt2}{4}\frac{\zeta(3/4)^2}{\zeta(1/4)^2}\\[5mu] &= 2.62205\;75542\;92119\;81046\;48395\;89891\;11941\ldots, \end{aligned}$$

where $K$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the first kind](wikipedia://en/9960#Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_first_kind) with modulus $k$, $\mathrm{B}$ is the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660), $\Gamma$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316) and $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

The lemniscate constant can also be computed by the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090) $M$,

$$\varpi=\frac{\pi}{M\bigl(1,\sqrt2\bigr)}.$$

Gauss's constant is typically defined as the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090) of 1 and the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682), after his calculation of $M\bigl(1, \sqrt2\bigr)$ published in 1800:[^n2]

$$G = \frac{1}{M\bigl(1, \sqrt2\bigr)}$$

John Todd's lemniscate constants may be given in terms of the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660) B:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac\varpi2 = \tfrac14 \mathrm{B} \bigl(\tfrac14,\tfrac12\bigr), \\[3mu] B &= \frac{\pi}{2\varpi} =\tfrac14\mathrm{B} \bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac34\bigr). \end{aligned}$$

### As a special value of L-functions {#As_a_special_value_of_L-functions}

$$\beta'(0)=\log\frac{\varpi}{\sqrt{\pi}}$$

which is analogous to

$$\zeta'(0)=\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$$

where $\beta$ is the [Dirichlet beta function](wikipedia://en/2216061) and $\zeta$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).[^o24]

Analogously to the [Leibniz formula for π](wikipedia://en/1301559),

$$\beta (1)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi (n)}{n}=\frac{\pi}{4},$$

we have[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28][^o29]

$$L(E,1)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\nu (n)}{n}=\frac{\varpi}{4}$$

where $L$ is the [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) of the [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) $E:\, y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$; this means that $\nu$ is the [multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/20059) given by

$$\nu (p^n)=\begin{cases} p - \mathcal{N}_p, & p\in\mathbb{P}, \, n=1 \\[5mu] 0, & p=2,\, n\ge 2 \\[5mu] \nu (p)\nu (p^{n-1})-p\nu (p^{n-2}), & p\in\mathbb{P}\setminus\{2\},\, n\ge 2 \end{cases}$$

where $\mathcal{N}_p$ is the number of solutions of the congruence

$$a^3-a\equiv b^2 \,(\operatorname{mod}p),\quad p\in\mathbb{P}$$

in variables $a,b$ that are non-negative integers ($\mathbb{P}$ is the set of all primes). Equivalently, $\nu$ is given by

$$F(\tau)=\eta (4\tau)^2\eta (8\tau)^2=\sum_{n=1}^\infty \nu (n) q^n,\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$$

where $\tau\in\mathbb{C}$ such that $\operatorname{\Im}\tau >0$ and $\eta$ is the [eta function](wikipedia://en/447020).[^o30][^o31][^o32] The above result can be equivalently written as

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\nu (n)}{n}e^{-2\pi n/\sqrt{32}}=\frac{\varpi}{8}$$

(the number $32$ is the [conductor](wikipedia://en/43604164) of $E$) and also tells us that the [BSD conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161) is true for the above $E$.[^o33] The first few values of $\nu$ are given by the following table; if $1\le n\le 113$ such that $n$ doesn't appear in the table, then $\nu (n)=0$:

$$\begin{array}{r|r|r|r} n & \nu (n) & n & \nu (n) \\ \hline 1 & 1 & 53 & 14 \\ 5 & -2 & 61 & -10 \\ 9 & -3 & 65 & -12 \\ 13 & 6 & 73 & -6 \\ 17 & 2 & 81 & 9 \\ 25 & -1 & 85 & -4 \\ 29 & -10 & 89 & 10\\ 37 & -2 & 97 & 18 \\ 41 & 10 & 101 & -2 \\ 45 & 6 & 109 & 6 \\ 49 & -7 & 113 & -14 \\ \end{array}$$

### As a special value of other functions {#As_a_special_value_of_other_functions}

Let $\Delta$ be the minimal weight level $1$ new form. Then[^o35]

$$\Delta (i)=\frac{1}{64}\left(\frac{\varpi}{\pi}\right)^{12}.$$

The $q$-coefficient of $\Delta$ is the [Ramanujan tau function](wikipedia://en/1231708).

## Series {#Series}

[Viète's formula](wikipedia://en/1609504) for $\pi$ can be written:

$$\frac2\pi = \sqrt\frac12 \cdot \sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12\sqrt\frac12} \cdot \sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12\sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12\sqrt\frac12}} \cdots$$

An analogous formula for $\varpi$ is:[^o37]

$$\frac2\varpi = \sqrt\frac12 \cdot \sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12 \bigg/ \!\sqrt\frac12} \cdot \sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12 \Bigg/ \!\sqrt{\frac12 + \frac12 \bigg/ \!\sqrt\frac12}} \cdots$$

The [Wallis product](wikipedia://en/1301687) for $\pi$ is:

$$\frac{\pi}{2} = \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{(-1)^{n+1}}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{2n}{2n-1} \cdot \frac{2n}{2n+1}\right) = \biggl(\frac{2}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{3}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{5}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{6}{5} \cdot \frac{6}{7}\biggr) \cdots$$

An analogous formula for $\varpi$ is:[^o38]

$$\frac{\varpi}{2} = \prod_{n=1}^\infty \left(1+\frac{1}{2n}\right)^{(-1)^{n+1}}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{4n-1}{4n-2} \cdot \frac{4n}{4n+1}\right) = \biggl(\frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{5}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{7}{6} \cdot \frac{8}{9}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{11}{10} \cdot \frac{12}{13}\biggr) \cdots$$

A related result for Gauss's constant ($G=\varpi / \pi$) is:[^o39]

$$\frac{\varpi}{\pi} = \prod_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\frac{4n-1}{4n} \cdot \frac{4n+2}{4n+1}\right) = \biggl(\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{6}{5}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{7}{8} \cdot \frac{10}{9}\biggr) \biggl(\frac{11}{12} \cdot \frac{14}{13}\biggr) \cdots$$

An [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) discovered by Gauss is:[^o40]

$$\frac{\varpi}{\pi} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty (-1)^n \prod_{k=1}^n \frac{(2k-1)^2}{(2k)^2} = 1 - \frac{1^2}{2^2} + \frac{1^2\cdot3^2}{2^2\cdot4^2} - \frac{1^2\cdot3^2\cdot5^2}{2^2\cdot4^2\cdot6^2} + \cdots$$

The [Machin formula](wikipedia://en/2724287) for $\pi$ is $\tfrac14\pi = 4 \arctan \tfrac15 - \arctan \tfrac1{239},$ and several similar formulas for $\pi$ can be developed using trigonometric angle sum identities, e.g. Euler's formula $\tfrac14\pi = \arctan\tfrac12 + \arctan\tfrac13$. Analogous formulas can be developed for $\varpi$, including the following found by Gauss: $\tfrac12\varpi = 2 \operatorname{arcsl} \tfrac12 + \operatorname{arcsl} \tfrac7{23}$, where $\operatorname{arcsl}$ is the [lemniscate arcsine](wikipedia://en/3754835#Inverse_functions).[^o41]

The lemniscate constant can be rapidly computed by the series[^o42][^o43]

$$\varpi=2^{-1/2}\pi\biggl(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi n^2}\biggr)^2=2^{1/4}\pi e^{-\pi/12} \biggl(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^n e^{-\pi p_n}\biggr)^2$$

where $p_n=\tfrac12(3n^2-n)$ (these are the [generalized pentagonal numbers](wikipedia://en/504786)). Also[^o44]

$$\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-2\pi (m^2 +mn+n^2)}=\sqrt{1+\sqrt{3}}\dfrac{\varpi}{12^{1/8}\pi}.$$

In a spirit similar to that of the [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303),

$$\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}[i]\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{z^4}=G_4(i)=\frac{\varpi ^4}{15}$$

where $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ are the [Gaussian integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_integers) and $G_4$ is the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458) of weight $4$ (see [Lemniscate elliptic functions § Hurwitz numbers](wikipedia://en/3754835#Hurwitz_numbers) for a more general result).[^o45]

A related result is

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty \sigma_3(n)e^{-2\pi n}=\frac{\varpi^4}{80 \pi^4}-\frac{1}{240}$$

where $\sigma_3$ is the [sum of positive divisors function](wikipedia://en/407249).[^o46]

In 1842, [Malmsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Johan_Malmsten) found

$$\beta'(1)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n+1}\frac{\log (2n+1)}{2n+1}=\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\gamma+2\log\frac{\pi}{\varpi\sqrt{2}}\right)$$

where $\gamma$ is [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594) and $\beta(s)$ is the Dirichlet-Beta function.

The lemniscate constant is given by the rapidly converging series

$$\varpi = \pi\sqrt[4]{32}e^{-\frac{\pi}{3}}\biggl(\sum_{n = -\infty}^\infty (-1)^n e^{-2n\pi(3n+1)} \biggr)^2.$$

The constant is also given by the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006)

$$\varpi = \pi\prod_{m = 1}^\infty \tanh^2 \left( \frac{\pi m}{2}\right).$$

Also[^o47]

$$\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{6635520^n}\frac{(4n)!}{n!^4}=\frac{24}{5^{7/4}}\frac{\varpi^2}{\pi^2}.$$

## Continued fractions {#Continued_fractions}

A (generalized) [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) for $\pi$ is

$$\frac\pi2=1 + \cfrac{1}{1 + \cfrac{1\cdot 2}{1 + \cfrac{2\cdot 3}{1 + \cfrac{3\cdot 4}{1+\ddots}}}}$$

An analogous formula for $\varpi$ is

$$\frac\varpi2= 1 + \cfrac{1}{2 + \cfrac{2\cdot 3}{2 + \cfrac{4\cdot 5}{2 + \cfrac{6\cdot 7}{2+\ddots}}}}$$

Define *[Brouncker](wikipedia://en/1426858)'s continued fraction* by[^o49]

$$b(s)=s + \cfrac{1^2}{2s + \cfrac{3^2}{2s + \cfrac{5^2}{2s+\ddots}}},\quad s>0.$$

Let $n\ge 0$ except for the first equality where $n\ge 1$. Then[^o50][^o51]

$$\begin{aligned}b(4n)&=(4n+1)\prod_{k=1}^n \frac{(4k-1)^2}{(4k-3)(4k+1)}\frac{\pi}{\varpi^2}\\ b(4n+1)&=(2n+1)\prod_{k=1}^n \frac{(2k)^2}{(2k-1)(2k+1)}\frac{4}{\pi}\\ b(4n+2)&=(4n+1)\prod_{k=1}^n \frac{(4k-3)(4k+1)}{(4k-1)^2}\frac{\varpi^2}{\pi}\\ b(4n+3)&=(2n+1)\prod_{k=1}^n \frac{(2k-1)(2k+1)}{(2k)^2}\,\pi.\end{aligned}$$

For example,

$$\begin{aligned} b(1) &= \frac{4}{\pi}, & b(2) &= \frac{\varpi^2}{\pi}, & b(3) &= \pi, & b(4) &= \frac{9\pi}{\varpi^2}. \end{aligned}$$

In fact, the values of $b(1)$ and $b(2)$, coupled with the functional equation

$$b(s+2)=\frac{(s+1)^2}{b(s)},$$

determine the values of $b(n)$ for all $n$.

### Simple continued fractions {#Simple_continued_fractions}

Simple continued fractions for the lemniscate constant and related constants include[^o53][^o54]

$$\begin{aligned} \varpi &= [2,1,1,1,1,1,4,1,2,\ldots], \\[8mu] 2\varpi &= [5,4,10,2,1,2,3,29,\ldots], \\[5mu] \frac{\varpi}{2} &= [1,3,4,1,1,1,5,2,\ldots], \\[2mu] \frac{\varpi}{\pi} &= [0,1,5,21,3,4,14,\ldots]. \end{aligned}$$

## Integrals {#Integrals}

*Image: Lemniscate constant as an integral.png.* A geometric representation of $\varpi/2$ and $\varpi/\sqrt{2}$

The lemniscate constant $\varpi$ is related to the area under the curve $x^4 + y^4 = 1$. Defining $\pi_n \mathrel{:=} \mathrm{B}\bigl(\tfrac1n, \tfrac1n \bigr)$, twice the area in the positive quadrant under the curve $x^n + y^n = 1$ is $2 \int_0^1 \sqrt[n]{1 - x^n}\mathop{\mathrm{d}x} = \tfrac1n \pi_n.$ In the quartic case, $\tfrac14 \pi_4 = \tfrac1\sqrt{2} \varpi.$

In 1842, Malmsten discovered that[^o56]

$$\int_0^1 \frac{\log (-\log x)}{1+x^2}\, dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\log\frac{\pi}{\varpi\sqrt{2}}.$$

Furthermore,

$$\int_0^\infty \frac{\tanh x}{x}e^{-x}\, dx=\log\frac{\varpi^2}{\pi}$$

and[^o57]

$$\int_0^\infty e^{-x^4}\, dx=\frac{\sqrt{2\varpi\sqrt{2\pi}}}{4},\quad\text{analogous to}\,\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\, dx=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2},$$

a form of [Gaussian integral](wikipedia://en/567580).

The lemniscate constant appears in the evaluation of the integrals

$${\frac{\pi}{\varpi}} = \int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sqrt{\sin(x)}\,dx=\int_0^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sqrt{\cos(x)}\,dx$$

$$\frac{\varpi}{\pi} = \int_0^{\infty}{\frac{dx}{\sqrt{\cosh(\pi x)}}}$$

John Todd's lemniscate constants are defined by integrals:

$$A = \int_0^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}$$

$$B = \int_0^1\frac{x^2\, dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}$$

### Circumference of an ellipse {#Circumference_of_an_ellipse}

The lemniscate constant satisfies the equation[^n3]

$$\frac{\pi}{\varpi} = 2 \int_0^1\frac{x^2\, dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}$$

Euler discovered in 1738 that for the rectangular elastica (first and second lemniscate constants)[^o60][^n3]

$$\textrm{arc}\ \textrm{length}\cdot\textrm{height} = A \cdot B = \int_0^1 \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} \cdot \int_0^1 \frac{x^2 \mathop{\mathrm{d}x}}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} = \frac\varpi2 \cdot \frac\pi{2\varpi} = \frac\pi4$$

Now considering the circumference $C$ of the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with axes $\sqrt{2}$ and $1$, satisfying $2x^2 + 4y^2 = 1$, Stirling noted that[^n4]

$$\frac{C}{2} = \int_0^1\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} + \int_0^1\frac{x^2\,dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}}$$

Hence the full circumference is

$$C = \frac{\pi}{\varpi} + \varpi =3.820197789\ldots$$

This is also the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of the [sine](wikipedia://en/5010838) curve on half a period:[^o63]

$$C = \int_0^\pi \sqrt{1+\cos^2(x)}\,dx$$

## Other limits {#Other_limits}

Analogously to

$$2\pi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{(2n)!}{\mathrm{B}_{2n}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2n}}$$

where $\mathrm{B}_n$ are [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964), we have

$$2\varpi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{(4n)!}{\mathrm{H}_{4n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4n}}$$

where $\mathrm{H}_n$ are [Hurwitz numbers](wikipedia://en/3754835#Hurwitz_numbers).
