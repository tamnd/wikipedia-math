# Jean Nicod

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Nicod"}

**Jean George Pierre Nicod** (; 1 June 1893, Paris – 16 February 1924, [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva)) was a French [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), best known for his work on [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154) and [induction](wikipedia://en/393736).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Nicod's main contribution to [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) was to show that [classical](wikipedia://en/55868) [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) could be axiomatized with only one [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) (which is now known as [Nicod's axiom](wikipedia://en/27656359)) and one rule of inference, both formulated using the [Sheffer stroke](wikipedia://en/29388) as only [connective](wikipedia://en/18153). In [inductive logic](wikipedia://en/393736) and [confirmation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), he famously proposed [Nicod's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicod's_criterion), according to which a conditional [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) is confirmed by all and only its positive instances.[^o3] This principle plays a central role in the derivation of [Carl Hempel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Hempel)'s [raven paradox](wikipedia://en/37391).

Nicod died at the age of 30 from [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis).[^o4]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The [Institut Jean Nicod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Jean_Nicod) in Paris (a branch of the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) and a research laboratory at the interface between [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science) and the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science)) was named in honour of Nicod's memory. Jean Nicod's name is also commemorated by the prestigious [Jean Nicod Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Nicod_Prize), which are delivered annually in Paris by a leading [philosopher of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher_of_mind) or philosophically oriented [cognitive scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_scientist).[^o6] The lecturer is awarded the [Jean Nicod Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Nicod_Prize) by the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS), which sponsors the lectures to develop the field of cognitive science in France.[^o6]

## Main works {#Main_works}

- 1917, "A Reduction in the Number of Primitive Propositions of Logic", *Proc. Camb. Phil. Soc.* **19**: 32–41.
- 1921, "La géométrie des sensations de mouvement", *Revue de métaphysique et de morale* **28**: 537–43.
- 1922, "Les tendances philosophiques de M. Bertrand Russell", *Revue de métaphysique et de morale* **29**: 77–84.
- 1922, "Mathematical Logic and the Foundations of Mathematics" in *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica)*: *The New Volumes, vol. 3*, 12th ed. 874–76.
- \1923. *La géométrie dans le monde sensible*. Thèse, Univ. de Paris.
- \1923. *Le problème logique de l'induction*. Thèse complémentaire, Univ. de Paris.
- \1924. "Les relations des valeurs et les relations de sens en logique formelle", *Revue de métaphysique et de morale* **31**: 467–80.
- 1924, "Freedom of Association and Trade Unionism: An Introductory Survey", *International Labor Review* **9**: 467–80.
- \1930. *Foundations of Geometry & Induction, Containing Geometry in a Sensible World and the Logical Problem of Induction*, with prefaces by [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [André Lalande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Lalande_%28philosopher%29). London: Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner & Co. New York: Harcourt, Brace & Co.[^o8] Reprinted 2000, London: Routledge. Translated by [Philip P. Wiener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_P._Wiener).
