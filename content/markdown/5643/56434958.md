# James Michael Gardner Fell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Michael Gardner Fell"}

**James Michael Gardner Fell** (4 December 1923, [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver) – 16 December 2016, [Gladwyne, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladwyne,_Pennsylvania)) was a Canadian-American mathematician, specializing in functional analysis and representation theory. He is known for Fell bundles (*i.e.* Banach \*-algebraic bundles). He was an accomplished linguist who knew [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), [Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

J. M. G. Fell's father was James Pemberton Fell, a colonel in the Canadian Army Corps of Engineers and a real estate developer who helped to lay out the streets of [North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vancouver_%28city%29). J. M. G. Fell attended elementary school in Vancouver and then in 1935 went to England for secondary school at [Eton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College). In 1940 he returned to Canada and matriculated at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), graduating there in 1943 at the age of 19. In 1951 he received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) under [John L. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Kelley) with thesis *On L-spaces*.[^o5]

He was an assistant professor at [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) from 1953 to 1955, a research associate at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 1955 to 1956, and an assistant professor, then associate professor, and then full professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) from 1956 to 1965. He was a full professor from 1965 to 1991 at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he retired as professor emeritus.[^o2]

On 4 July 1957 Fell married Angela Daphne Rachel MacDonald.[^o3]

During his years at Penn he developed a great interest in the Sanskrit language, and attended Sanskrit courses in the South Asian Studies Department. After retirement his passion for languages extended to Icelandic, which he learned to speak, and in his retirement he published six books, most of them translations, on aspects of Icelandic Christianity. He and Daphne spent at least three months a year in Reykjavik every year for twenty years.[^o2]

He was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice). He gave a mathematical lecture at the [University of Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iceland), and in 2000 the University gave him an honorary doctorate in theology.[^o3]

Upon his death he was survived by his wife, two children, and a grandson.[^o2]

## Fell bundles {#Fell_bundles}

In a sense, it has long been understood that Fell bundles provide an important mechanism for illuminating the structure of *C\**-dynamical systems and their associated crossed products.
