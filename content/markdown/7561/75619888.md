# Nikolas Breuckmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolas Breuckmann"}

**Nikolas P. Breuckmann** (born 1988) is a German [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416) affiliated with the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o3][^o4] He is, as of Spring 2024, a visiting scientist and program organizer at the [Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Institute_for_the_Theory_of_Computing) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o5] His research focuses on [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274) theory, in particular [quantum error correction](wikipedia://en/892803) and [quantum complexity theory](wikipedia://en/24092190). He is known for his work (together with [Anurag Anshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anurag_Anshu) and [Chinmay Nirkhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinmay_Nirkhe)) on proving the [NLTS conjecture](wikipedia://en/71478579), a famous open problem in quantum information theory.

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Breuckmann was born in [Duisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duisburg) and grew up in [Waltrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltrop), [North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Rhine-Westphalia), Germany. He earned a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in Mathematics and a BSc, an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Physics from [RWTH Aachen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University). His doctoral thesis was titled "Homological Quantum Codes Beyond the Toric Code" and he was supervised by [Barbara Terhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Terhal).[^o7]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After his PhD, he deferred his [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) Post-Doctoral Fellowship in Quantum Technologies funded by [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_and_Physical_Sciences_Research_Council) for a year to work for [Palo Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto,_California)-based [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220) start-up [PsiQuantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PsiQuantum), which was co-founded by [Jeremy O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_O'Brien) and [Terry Rudolph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Rudolph) (among other scientists).

In 2022, he became Lecturer (Assistant Professor)[^o9] in Quantum Computing Theory at the University of Bristol.

In 2023, he was awarded the [James Clerk Maxwell Medal and Prize](wikipedia://en/75629571) by the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics) for his "outstanding contributions to the quantum error correction field, particularly work on proving the no low-energy trivial state conjecture, a famous open problem in quantum information theory".[^o10][^o11] [Quanta Magazine](wikipedia://en/51804976) described the proof as "one of the biggest developments in theoretical computer science".[^o12][^o13][^o14] This result built on his introduction with [Jens Eberhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Eberhardt) of “Balanced Product Quantum Codes”.[^o15][^o16]

The [NLTS conjecture](wikipedia://en/71478579) posits that there exist families of [Hamiltonians](wikipedia://en/14381) with all low-energy states of non-trivial complexity. It was formulated in 2013 by [Fields Medallist](wikipedia://en/10859) [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) and [Matthew Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Hastings) at [Microsoft Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Research). The conjecture was proven by Breuckmann and colleagues (Anurag Anshu and Chinmay Nirkhe) by showing that the recently discovered families of constant-rate and linear-distance low-density parity-check (LDPC) quantum codes correspond to NLTS local Hamiltonians.[^o17][^o18] This result is a step towards proving the [quantum PCP conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_PCP_conjecture), considered the most important open problem in quantum complexity theory.

He and his former doctoral student [Oscar Higgott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Higgott) are inventors of a U.S. patent titled “Subsystem codes with high thresholds by gauge fixing and reduced qubit overhead”, which concerns a technique to significantly improve the performance of quantum error correction in quantum computers.[^o19] Their related work was included as a major development for computer science in 2023 by Quanta.[^o20][^o21][^o22]
