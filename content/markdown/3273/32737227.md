# Elonka Dunin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elonka Dunin"}

**Elonka Dunin** (; born December 29, 1958[^o15]) is an American video game developer and [cryptologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptologist).[^o5] Dunin worked at [Simutronics Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simutronics) in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis) from 1990–2014, and in 2015 was Senior Producer at Black Gate Games in [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee).[^o3] She is Chairperson Emerita and one of the founders of the [International Game Developers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Game_Developers_Association)'s [Online Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Games) group, has contributed or been editor in chief on multiple [IGDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGDA) State of the Industry [white papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper#Commercial_white_papers), and was one of the Directors of the [Global Game Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Game_Jam) from 2011–2014.[^o4] As of 2020 she works as a management consultant at [Accenture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accenture).[^o3]

:::

Dunin has published a book of exercises on [classical cryptography](wikipedia://en/705892), and maintains cryptography-related websites about topics such as *[Kryptos](wikipedia://en/456619)*, a sculpture at the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency) containing an encrypted message,[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] and another on the world's most famous unsolved codes.[^o9] She has given several lectures on the subject of cryptography,[^o10][^o11] and according to the [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Service) series *[NOVA scienceNOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOVA_scienceNOW)* she is "generally considered the leading *Kryptos* expert in the world".[^o12] In 2010, bestselling author [Dan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Brown) named a character, Nola Kaye, in his novel *[The Lost Symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Symbol)* after her, in an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) pattern.[^o13]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Dunin was born in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California), the older of two children to Stanley Dunin, a [Polish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and [Elsie Ivancich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsie_Ivancich_Dunin), a [Croatian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian-American) dance [ethnologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologist) at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA).[^o15]

Dunin graduated in 1976 from [University High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_High_School_%28Los_Angeles,_California%29), and then enrolled as an undergraduate at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), but dropped out near the end of her freshman year.[^o15]

Dunin joined the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force), working as an [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) technician at [RAF Mildenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mildenhall) in the United Kingdom and [Beale Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beale_Air_Force_Base) in California.[^o20] After choosing not to re-enlist, she studied digital electronics at [Yuba College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuba_College)—a two-year [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college)—but did not obtain a degree.[^o15][^o16]

## Career {#Career}

### Online games {#Online_games}

In the 1980s, she became involved with the growing [BBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_board_system) culture. In 1989, while she was working as a temporary legal secretary in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles), this interest overlapped into the early [multiplayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplayer) games, such as *[British Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Legends)* on [CompuServe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CompuServe) and [Simutronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simutronics)' *[GemStone II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GemStone_II)* on [GEnie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEnie).[^o19]

In 1990, Dunin moved to [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri) and began working for the online game company [Simutronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simutronics).[^o20][^o21] Simutronics launched its own website, play.net, in 1997 with Dunin as General Manager of Online Games,[^o22] managing Simutronics' [online community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_community).[^o23][^o24] Dunin was the product manager for *[GemStone III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GemStone_III)*, executive producer for the *[Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules:_The_Legendary_Journeys)* and *[Xena: Warrior Princess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xena:_Warrior_Princess)*-based multiplayer game *Alliance of Heroes*, and worked in a variety of production and development roles on most of Simutronics' other products, including *[CyberStrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CyberStrike_2)*, *Modus Operandi*, *[DragonRealms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DragonRealms)*, [HeroEngine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HeroEngine), *Fantasy University*, and *Tiny Heroes*. She is a founding member of the [International Game Developers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Game_Developers_Association)'s [Online Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_game) [SIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Interest_Group) and edited four of their annual White Papers on various aspects of the online game industry, such as "Web and Downloadable Games" and "Persistent Worlds".[^o25][^o26]

Dunin held a variety of production and development roles during her 24 years at Simutronics. Games that she worked on include:

### Cryptography {#Cryptography}

*Image: Elonka Dunin.jpg.* in St. Louis, 2006

Dunin has written books and articles about [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and been interviewed on radio and television about related subjects such as *[Kryptos](wikipedia://en/456619)*, the [Smithy Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithy_Code),[^o29] and [Ricky McCormick's encrypted notes](wikipedia://en/31336045).[^o30] In an interview with GIGnews.com, Dunin said that in the year 2000 she cracked the [PhreakNIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhreakNIC) v3.0 Code, an amateur cryptographic puzzle created by a [hacker group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker_%28computer_security%29), and that this launched her public interest in high-profile ciphers.[^o31][^o32] Because of the location of *Kryptos* on [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency) grounds, physical access to the sculpture is restricted. According to *[Wired News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_News)*, in 2002, Dunin gave a presentation to CIA analysts about [steganography](wikipedia://en/28733) and [Al-Qaeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda), and "\[i\]n 2002, Dunin was one of the lucky few who saw \[Kryptos\] in person", and "she also made [rubbings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubbing_%28art%29) of the text".[^o33] Based on her visit, she launched the beginnings of what became a comprehensive website about the sculpture,[^o5][^o34] and also became co-moderator of a [Yahoo Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo_Group) that is attempting to decipher the encrypted messages on the sculpture.[^o35] In 2003, Dunin organized a team which solved the ciphers on *Kryptos**s sister sculpture, the*****[Cyrillic Projector](wikipedia://en/4124072)*.[^o20][^o36][^o37][^o38]***

When *Kryptos* sculptor [Jim Sanborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Sanborn) chose to release information about an error on the sculpture in 2006, he contacted Dunin to make the announcement.[^o5][^o34] In July 2007, Dunin appeared on the [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Broadcasting_Service) program *[NOVA scienceNOW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOVA_scienceNOW)*, as an expert on *Kryptos*, and in 2009, contributed two articles about the sculpture for the book *Secrets of The Lost Symbol: The Unauthorized Guide to the Mysteries Behind The Da Vinci Code Sequel*,[^o39] a companion book to author [Dan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Brown)'s novel *[The Lost Symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lost_Symbol)*. Dunin had assisted Brown with the research for the novel, and Brown named a character in the novel after her. The character "Nola Kaye" is an anagrammed form of "Elonka".[^o39][^o40][^o41]

In 2006, Dunin compiled a book of several hundred exercises in [classical cryptography](wikipedia://en/705892), which was published in the United States as *The Mammoth Book of Secret Codes and Cryptograms*, and in the UK as *The Mammoth Book of Secret Code Puzzles*. The book also includes a few details about several unsolved codes, such as *Kryptos*.[^o34]

In 2013, in response to a [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_%28United_States%29) request by Dunin, the [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) released documents which show the NSA became involved in attempts to solve the *Kryptos* puzzle in 1992, following a challenge by [Bill Studeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Studeman), Deputy Director of the CIA. The documents show that by June 1993, a small group of NSA cryptanalysts had succeeded in solving the first three parts of the sculpture.[^o42][^o43]

## Public speaking {#Public_speaking}

Dunin has given talks on *Kryptos* and the *Cyrillic Projector* at the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939)'s Cryptologic History Symposium,[^o10] [Def Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEF_CON),[^o11] [Shmoocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmoocon),[^o45] [Notacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notacon),[^o46] [PhreakNIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhreakNIC),[^o32] and [Dragon\*Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon*Con),[^o47] and has also given lectures at the [International Game Developers Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Developers_Conference).[^o48][^o49] She has been invited to be a co-host on the [Binary Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_Revolution) webcast three times.[^o50] In October 2012, she was the guest of honor at [Archon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archon_%28convention%29).[^o51]

In 2021, she gave a [TEDx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_%28conference%29#TEDx) talk on cryptography, "[2000 years of ordinary secrets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-DhLKmOQt8Y)".

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Dunin is a Wikipedia editor who has made tens of thousands of edits.[^o53]
