# Hemicube (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hemicube"}

In abstract [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hemicube** is an [abstract](wikipedia://en/1211056), [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299), containing half the [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).

:::

## Realization {#Realization}

*Image: hemicube\_visualisation.svg.* Visualisation of the topology of a hemicube

It can be realized as a [projective polyhedron](wikipedia://en/26952055) (a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) by three quadrilaterals), which can be visualized by constructing the projective plane as a [hemisphere](wikipedia://en/27859#Hemisphere) where opposite points along the boundary are connected and dividing the hemisphere into three equal parts.

It has three square faces, six edges, and four vertices. It has an unexpected property that every face is in contact with every other face on two edges, and every face contains all the vertices, which gives an example of an abstract polytope whose faces are not determined by their vertex sets.

It cannot be embedded into the 3-dimensional Euclidean space.[^o4]

From the point of view of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) the [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) is a [tetrahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_graph), an embedding of *K*<sub>4</sub> (the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) with four vertices) on a [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350).

The hemicube should not be confused with the [demicube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demicube) – the hemicube is a projective polyhedron, while the demicube is an ordinary polyhedron (in Euclidean space). While they both have half the vertices of a cube, the *hemi*cube is a *quotient* of the cube, while the vertices of the *demi*cube are a *subset* of the vertices of the cube.

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

The hemicube is the [Petrie dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrie_dual) to the regular [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), with the four vertices, six edges of the tetrahedron, and three [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) quadrilateral faces.
