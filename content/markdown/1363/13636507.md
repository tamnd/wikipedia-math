# Geodesic map

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geodesic map"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831)—specifically, in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)—a **geodesic map** (or **geodesic mapping** or **geodesic diffeomorphism**) is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) that "preserves [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096)". More precisely, given two ([pseudo](wikipedia://en/287555)-)[Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) (*M*, *g*) and (*N*, *h*), a function *φ* : *M* → *N* is said to be a geodesic map if

:::

- *φ* is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of *M* onto *N*; and
- the image under *φ* of any geodesic arc in *M* is a geodesic arc in *N*; and
- the image under the [inverse function](wikipedia://en/14907) *φ*<sup>−1</sup> of any geodesic arc in *N* is a geodesic arc in *M*.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If (*M*, *g*) and (*N*, *h*) are both the *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **E**<sup>*n*</sup> with its usual flat [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric), then any Euclidean [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) is a geodesic map of **E**<sup>*n*</sup> onto itself.
- Similarly, if (*M*, *g*) and (*N*, *h*) are both the *n*-dimensional unit [sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere) **S**<sup>*n*</sup> with its usual round metric, then any isometry of the sphere is a geodesic map of **S**<sup>*n*</sup> onto itself.
- If (*M*, *g*) is the unit sphere **S**<sup>*n*</sup> with its usual round metric and (*N*, *h*) is the sphere of [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) 2 with its usual round metric, both thought of as subsets of the ambient coordinate space **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>, then the "expansion" map *φ* : **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup> → **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup> given by *φ*(*x*) = 2*x* induces a geodesic map of *M* onto *N*.
- There is no geodesic map from the Euclidean space **E**<sup>*n*</sup> onto the unit sphere **S**<sup>*n*</sup>, since they are not [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660), let alone diffeomorphic.
- The [gnomonic projection](wikipedia://en/1331480) of the hemisphere to the plane is a geodesic map as it takes great circles to lines and its inverse takes lines to great circles.
- Let (*D*, *g*) be the [unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc) *D* ⊂ **R**<sup>2</sup> equipped with the Euclidean metric, and let (*D*, *h*) be the same disc equipped with a [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) metric as in the [Poincaré disc model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_disc_model) of hyperbolic geometry. Then, although the two structures are diffeomorphic via the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) *i* : *D* → *D*, *i* is *not* a geodesic map, since *g*-geodesics are always straight lines in **R**<sup>2</sup>, whereas *h*-geodesics can be curved.
- On the other hand, when the hyperbolic metric on *D* is given by the [Klein model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltrami-Klein_model), the identity *i* : *D* → *D* *is* a geodesic map, because hyperbolic geodesics in the Klein model are (Euclidean) straight line segments.

:::
