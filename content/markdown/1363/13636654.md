# Beltrami's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beltrami's theorem"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), any ([pseudo](wikipedia://en/287555)-)[Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) determines a certain class of paths known as [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096). **Beltrami's theorem**, named for Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Eugenio Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760), is a result on the inverse problem of determining a (pseudo-)Riemannian metric from its geodesics.

:::

It is nontrivial to see that, on any [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) of [constant curvature](wikipedia://en/3638803), there are smooth coordinates relative to which all nonconstant geodesics appear as straight lines. In the *negative curvature* case of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), this is justified by the [Beltrami–Klein model](wikipedia://en/4792791). In the *positive curvature* case of [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), it is justified by the [gnomonic projection](wikipedia://en/1331480). In the language of [projective differential geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_differential_geometry), these charts show that any Riemannian manifold of constant curvature is *locally projectively flat.* More generally, any [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555) of constant curvature is locally projectively flat.

Beltrami's theorem asserts the converse: any connected pseudo-Riemannian manifold which is locally projectively flat must have constant curvature. With the use of [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus), the proof is straightforward. [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) described Beltrami's original proof (done in the two-dimensional Riemannian case) as being much more complicated. Relative to a projectively flat chart, there are functions $\rho_{i}$ such that the [Christoffel symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffel_symbol) take the form

$$\Gamma_{ij}^k=\rho_i\delta_j^k+\rho_j\delta_i^k.$$

Direct calculation then shows that the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) is given by

$$R_{ijkl}=(\partial_i\rho_j-\partial_j\rho_i)g_{kl}+g_{jl}(\partial_i\rho_k-\rho_i\rho_k)-g_{il}(\partial_j\rho_k-\rho_j\rho_k).$$

The curvature symmetry $R_{ijkl} + R_{jikl} = 0$ implies that $\partial_{i} \rho_{j} = \partial_{j} \rho_{i}$. The other curvature symmetry $R_{ijkl} = R_{klij}$, traced over $i$ and $l$, then says that

$$\partial_j\rho_k-\rho_j\rho_k=g_{jk}\frac{g^{il}(\partial_i\rho_l-\rho_i\rho_l)}{n}$$

where $n$ is the dimension of the manifold. It is direct to verify that the left-hand side is a (locally defined) [Codazzi tensor](wikipedia://en/35890862), using only the given form of the Christoffel symbols. It follows from [Schur's lemma](wikipedia://en/22203669) that $g^{il}(\partial_{i} \rho_{l} - \rho_{i} \rho_{l})$ is constant. Substituting the above identity into the Riemann tensor as given above, it follows that the chart domain has constant sectional curvature $-\tfrac{1}{n}g^{il}(\partial_{i} \rho_{l} - \rho_{i} \rho_{l})$. By connectedness of the manifold, this local constancy implies global constancy.

Beltrami's theorem may be phrased in the language of [geodesic maps](wikipedia://en/13636507): if given a geodesic map between pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, one manifold has constant curvature if and only if the other does.
