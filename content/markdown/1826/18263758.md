# Locally normal space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally normal space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is **locally normal** if intuitively it looks locally like a [normal space](wikipedia://en/48629).[^o2] More precisely, a locally normal space satisfies the property that each point of the space belongs to a [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of the space that is normal under the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is said to be locally normal [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) each point, *x*, of *X* has a [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) that is [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) under the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926).[^o5]

Note that not every neighbourhood of *x* has to be normal, but at least one neighbourhood of *x* has to be normal (under the subspace topology).

Note however, that if a space were called locally normal [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) each point of the space belonged to a subset of the space that was normal under the subspace topology, then every topological space would be locally normal. This is because, the singleton {*x*} is [vacuously](wikipedia://en/51672) normal and contains *x*. Therefore, the definition is more restrictive.

:::

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

- Every locally normal [T1 space](wikipedia://en/148721) is locally regular and [locally Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/5075766).
- A [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is always locally normal.
- A normal space is always locally normal.
- A [T1 space](wikipedia://en/148721) need not be locally normal as the set of all real numbers endowed with the [cofinite topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofinite_topology) shows.
