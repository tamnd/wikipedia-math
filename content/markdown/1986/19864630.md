# Dixon's identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dixon's identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Dixon's identity** (or **Dixon's theorem** or **Dixon's formula**) is any of several different but closely related identities [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [A. C. Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Cardew_Dixon), some involving finite sums of products of three [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668), and some evaluating a [hypergeometric sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_sum). These identities famously follow from the [MacMahon Master theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacMahon_Master_theorem), and can now be routinely proven by computer algorithms (Ekhad 1990).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statements"}

## Statements {#Statements}

The original identity, from (Dixon 1891), is

$$\sum_{k=-a}^{a}(-1)^{k}{2a\choose k+a}^3 =\frac{(3a)!}{(a!)^3}.$$

A generalization, also sometimes called Dixon's identity, is

$$\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^k{a+b\choose a+k} {b+c\choose b+k}{c+a\choose c+k} = \frac{(a+b+c)!}{a!b!c!}$$

where *a*, *b*, and *c* are non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) (Wilf 1994, 156). The sum on the left can be written as the terminating well-poised hypergeometric series

$${b+c\choose b-a}{c+a\choose c-a}{}_3F_2(-2a,-a-b,-a-c;1+b-a,1+c-a;1)$$

and the identity follows as a limiting case (as *a* tends to an integer) of Dixon's theorem evaluating a well-poised <sub>3</sub>*F*<sub>2</sub> [generalized hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_hypergeometric_series) at 1, from (Dixon 1902):

$$\;_3F_2 (a,b,c;1+a-b,1+a-c;1)= \frac{\Gamma(1+a/2)\Gamma(1+a/2-b-c)\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(1+a-c)} {\Gamma(1+a)\Gamma(1+a-b-c)\Gamma(1+a/2-b)\Gamma(1+a/2-c)}.$$

This holds for Re(1 + $\tfrac{1}{2}$*a* − *b* − *c*) > 0. As *c* tends to −∞ it reduces to [Kummer's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kummer's_formula) for the hypergeometric function <sub>2</sub>F<sub>1</sub> at −1. Dixon's theorem can be deduced from the evaluation of the [Selberg integral](wikipedia://en/18583020).

:::

## *q*-analogues {#q-analogues}

A *q*-analogue of Dixon's formula for the [basic hypergeometric series](wikipedia://en/2233526) in terms of the [q-Pochhammer symbol](wikipedia://en/11149717) is given by

$$\;_4 \varphi_3 \left[\begin{matrix} a & -qa^{1/2} & b & c \\ &-a^{1/2} & aq/b & aq/c \end{matrix} ; q,qa^{1/2}/bc \right] = \frac{(aq,aq/bc,qa^{1/2}/b,qa^{1/2}/c;q)_\infty}{(aq/b,aq/c,qa^{1/2},qa^{1/2}/bc;q)_\infty}$$

where |*qa*<sup>1/2</sup>/*bc*| \< 1.
