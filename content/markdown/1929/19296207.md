# Hanna Neumann conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hanna Neumann conjecture"}

In the mathematical subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Hanna Neumann conjecture** is a statement about the [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) of the intersection of two [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735). The conjecture was posed by [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308) in 1957.[^o2] In 2011, a strengthened version of the conjecture (see below) was proved independently by Joel Friedman[^o3] and by Igor Mineyev.[^o4]

:::

In 2017, a third proof of the Strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture, based on homological arguments inspired by [pro-p-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-p-group) considerations, was published by Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain. [^o5]

## History {#History}

The subject of the conjecture was originally motivated by a [1954 theorem of Howson](wikipedia://en/55479079)[^o7] who proved that the intersection of any two [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is always finitely generated, that is, has finite [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088). In this paper Howson proved that if *H* and *K* are [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of a free group *F*(*X*) of finite ranks *n* ≥ 1 and *m* ≥ 1 then the rank *s* of *H* ∩ *K* satisfies:

*s* − 1 ≤ 2*mn* − *m* − *n*.

In a 1956 paper[^o8] [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308) improved this bound by showing that:

*s* − 1 ≤ 2*mn* − *2m* − *n*.

In a 1957 addendum,[^o2] Hanna Neumann further improved this bound to show that under the above assumptions

*s* − 1 ≤ 2(*m* − 1)(*n* − 1).

She also conjectured that the factor of 2 in the above inequality is not necessary and that one always has

*s* − 1 ≤ (*m* − 1)(*n* − 1).

This statement became known as the *Hanna Neumann conjecture*.

::: {#o10 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let *H*, *K* ≤ *F*(*X*) be two nontrivial finitely generated subgroups of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*(*X*) and let *L* = *H* ∩ *K* be the intersection of *H* and *K*. The conjecture says that in this case

rank(*L*) − 1 ≤ (rank(*H*) − 1)(rank(*K*) − 1).

Here for a group *G* the quantity rank(*G*) is the [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) of *G*, that is, the smallest size of a [generating set](wikipedia://en/99945) for *G*. Every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is known to be [free](wikipedia://en/59735) itself and the [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is equal to the size of any free basis of that free group.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture"}

## Strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture {#Strengthened_Hanna_Neumann_conjecture}

If *H*, *K* ≤ *G* are two subgroups of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* and if *a*, *b* ∈ *G* define the same [double coset](wikipedia://en/982771) *HaK = HbK* then the [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* ∩ *aKa*<sup>−1</sup> and *H* ∩ *bKb*<sup>−1</sup> are [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) in *G* and thus have the same [rank](wikipedia://en/18720088). It is known that if *H*, *K* ≤ *F*(*X*) are [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) subgroups of a finitely generated [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) *F*(*X*) then there exist at most finitely many double coset classes *HaK* in *F*(*X*) such that *H* ∩ *aKa*<sup>−1</sup> ≠ {1}. Suppose that at least one such double coset exists and let *a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> be all the distinct representatives of such double cosets. The *strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture*, formulated by her son [Walter Neumann](wikipedia://en/49819507) (1990),[^o13] states that in this situation

$$\sum_{i=1}^n [{\rm rank}(H\cap a_iKa_{i}^{-1})-1] \le ({\rm rank}(H)-1)({\rm rank}(K)-1).$$

The strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture was proved in 2011 by Joel Friedman.[^o3] Shortly after, another proof was given by Igor Mineyev.[^o4]

:::

## Partial results and other generalizations {#Partial_results_and_other_generalizations}

- In 1971 Burns improved[^o15] Hanna Neumann's 1957 bound and proved that under the same assumptions as in Hanna Neumann's paper one has

*s* ≤ 2*mn* − 3*m* − 2*n* + 4.

- In a 1990 paper,[^o13] Walter Neumann formulated the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture (see statement above).
- [Tardos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gábor_Tardos) (1992)[^o16] established the strengthened Hanna Neumann Conjecture for the case where at least one of the subgroups *H* and *K* of *F*(*X*) has rank two. As most other approaches to the Hanna Neumann conjecture, Tardos used the technique of [Stallings subgroup graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stallings_subgroup_graph)[^o17] for analyzing subgroups of free groups and their intersections.
- Warren Dicks (1994)[^o18] established the equivalence of the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture and a graph-theoretic statement that he called the *amalgamated graph conjecture*.
- Arzhantseva (2000) proved[^o19] that if *H* is a finitely generated subgroup of infinite index in *F*(*X*), then, in a certain statistical meaning, for a generic finitely generated subgroup $K$ in $F(X)$, we have *H* ∩ *gKg*<sup>−1</sup> = {1} for all *g* in *F*. Thus, the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture holds for every *H* and a generic *K*.
- In 2001 Dicks and [Formanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_W._Formanek) established the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture for the case where at least one of the subgroups *H* and *K* of *F*(*X*) has rank at most three.[^o20]
- Khan (2002)[^o21] and, independently, Meakin and Weil (2002),[^o22] showed that the conclusion of the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture holds if one of the subgroups *H*, *K* of *F*(*X*) is *positively generated*, that is, generated by a finite set of words that involve only elements of *X* but not of *X*<sup>−1</sup> as letters.
- Ivanov[^o23][^o24] and Dicks and Ivanov[^o25] obtained analogs and generalizations of Hanna Neumann's results for the intersection of [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* and *K* of a [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of several groups.
- Wise (2005) claimed[^o26] that the strengthened Hanna Neumann conjecture implies another long-standing group-theoretic conjecture which says that every [one-relator group](wikipedia://en/51159172) with torsion is *coherent* (that is, every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) subgroup in such a group is [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group)).
