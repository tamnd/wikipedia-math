# Finitely generated algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finitely generated algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **finitely generated algebra** (also called an **algebra of finite type**) over a (commutative) ring $R$, or a finitely generated $R$-algebra for short, is a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) *$A$* defined by [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $f:R\to A$, such that every element of *$A$* can be expressed as a polynomial in a finite number of generators $a_1,\dots,a_n\in A$ with coefficients in $f(R)$. Put another way, there is a surjective $R$-[algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism) from the polynomial ring $R[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ to $A$.

:::

If $K$ is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), regarded as a subalgebra of $A$, and $f$ is the natural injection $K\hookrightarrow A$, then a $K$-algebra of finite type is a commutative associative algebra $A$ where there exists a finite set of elements $a_1,\dots,a_n\in A$ such that every element of *$A$* can be expressed as a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in $a_1,\dots,a_n$, with [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) in *$K$*.

Equivalently, there exist elements $a_1,\dots,a_n\in A$ such that the evaluation homomorphism at ${\bf a}=(a_1,\dots,a_n)$

$$\phi_{\bf a}\colon K[X_1,\dots,X_n]\twoheadrightarrow A$$

is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective); thus, by applying the [first isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_isomorphism_theorem), $A \cong K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/{\rm ker}(\phi_{\bf a})$.

[Conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), $A:= K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/I$ for any [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I\subseteq K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ is a $K$-algebra of finite type, indeed any element of $A$ is a polynomial in the cosets $a_i:=X_i+I, i=1,\dots,n$ with coefficients in $K$. Therefore, we obtain the following characterisation of finitely generated $K$-algebras:[^o2]

$A$ is a finitely generated $K$-algebra if and only if it is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) as a $K$-algebra to a [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) of the type $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/I$ by an ideal $I\subseteq K[X_1,\dots,X_n].$

Algebras that are not finitely generated are called **infinitely generated**.

A **finitely generated ring** refers to a ring that is finitely generated when it is regarded as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra.

An algebra being *finitely generated* (*of finite type*) should not be confused with an algebra being *finite* (see below). A **finite algebra** over $R$ is a commutative associative algebra $A$ that is *finitely generated as a module*; that is, an $R$-algebra defined by [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $f:R\to A$, such that every element of $A$ can be expressed as a *linear combination* of a finite number of generators $a_1,\dots,a_n \in A$ with coefficients in $f(R)$. This is a stronger condition than $A$ being expressible as a *polynomial* in a finite set of generators in the case of the algebra being finitely generated.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [polynomial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_algebra) $K[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ is finitely generated. The polynomial algebra in [countably infinitely many](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) generators is infinitely generated.
- The ring of real-coefficient polynomials $\mathbb{R}[X]$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{R}$ but not over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- The field $E=K(T)$ of [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) in one variable over an infinite field *$K$* is *not* a finitely generated algebra over *$K$*. On the other hand, $E$ is generated over $K$ by a single element, *$T$*, *as a field*.
- If $E/F$ is a [finite field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field_extension) then it follows from the definitions that $E$ is a finitely generated algebra over $F$.
- Conversely, if $E/F$ is a field extension and $E$ is a finitely generated algebra over $F$ then the field extension is finite. This is called [Zariski's lemma](wikipedia://en/37279342). See also [integral extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_extension).
- If $G$ is a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) then the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) $KG$ is a finitely generated algebra over $K$.
- "[Quadratic](wikipedia://en/23565253) and [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890) are essentially all the associative R-algebras which are finitely generated [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) R-modules and have a [standard involution](wikipedia://en/293450#Ring_theory)."[^o5]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- A [homomorphic](wikipedia://en/13564) [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a finitely generated algebra is itself finitely generated. However, a similar property for [subalgebras](wikipedia://en/45239) does not hold in general.
- [Hilbert's basis theorem](wikipedia://en/13733): if $A$ is a finitely generated commutative algebra over a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) then every [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of *A* is finitely generated, or equivalently, *$A$* is a Noetherian ring.

:::

## Relation with affine varieties {#Relation_with_affine_varieties}

Finitely generated [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749) [commutative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) are basic objects of consideration in modern [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where they correspond to [affine algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_variety); for this reason, these algebras are also referred to as (commutative) **affine algebras**. More precisely, given an affine algebraic set $V\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ we can associate a finitely generated $K$-algebra

$$\Gamma(V):=K[X_1,\dots,X_n]/I(V)$$

called the affine [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) of $V$; moreover, if $\phi\colon V\to W$ is a [regular map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_map_%28algebraic_geometry%29) between the affine algebraic sets $V\subseteq \mathbb{A}^n$ and $W\subseteq \mathbb{A}^m$, we can define a homomorphism of $K$-algebras

$$\Gamma(\phi)\equiv\phi^*\colon\Gamma(W)\to\Gamma(V),\,\phi^*(f)=f\circ\phi,$$

then, $\Gamma$ is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of affine algebraic sets with regular maps to the category of reduced finitely generated $K$-algebras: this functor turns out[^o9] to be an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051)

$$\Gamma\colon (\text{affine algebraic sets})^{\rm opp}\to(\text{reduced finitely generated }K\text{-algebras}),$$

and, restricting to [affine varieties](wikipedia://en/320468) (i.e. [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_space) affine algebraic sets),

$$\Gamma\colon (\text{affine algebraic varieties})^{\rm opp}\to(\text{integral finitely generated }K\text{-algebras}).$$

## Finite algebras vs algebras of finite type {#Finite_algebras_vs_algebras_of_finite_type}

We recall that a commutative $R$-[algebra](wikipedia://en/2112#from_ring_homomorphisms) $A$ is a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $\phi\colon R\to A$; the $R$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) structure of $A$ is defined by

$$\lambda \cdot a := \phi(\lambda)a,\quad\lambda\in R, a\in A.$$

An $R$-algebra $A$ is called ***finite*** if it is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) as an $R$-module, i.e. there is a surjective homomorphism of $R$-modules

$$R^{\oplus_n}\twoheadrightarrow A.$$

Again, there is a characterisation of [finite algebras](wikipedia://en/62856263) in terms of quotients:[^o11]

An $R$-algebra $A$ is finite if and only if it is isomorphic to a quotient $R^{\oplus_n}/M$ by an $R$-[submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) $M\subseteq R$.

By definition, a finite $R$-algebra is of finite type, but the converse is false: the polynomial ring $R[X]$ is of finite type but not finite. However, if an $R$-algebra is of finite type and [integral](wikipedia://en/9478630), then it is finite. More precisely, $A$ is a finitely generated $R$-module if and only if $A$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by a finite number of elements integral over $R$.

Finite algebras and algebras of finite type are related to the notions of [finite morphisms](wikipedia://en/2139357) and [morphisms of finite type](wikipedia://en/2139357#morphisms_of_finite_type).
