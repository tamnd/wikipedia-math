# John McKay (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John McKay"}

**John K. S. McKay** ( ; 18 November 1939 – 19 April 2022)[^o2][^o3][^o4] was a British-Canadian mathematician and academic who worked at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University_%28Quebec%29), known for his discovery of [monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168), his joint construction of some [sporadic simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group), for the [McKay conjecture](wikipedia://en/51116107) in representation theory, and for the [McKay correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKay_correspondence) relating certain [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) to [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

McKay was educated at [Dulwich College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulwich_College), earned his Bachelor and Diploma in 1961 and 1962 at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), and his PhD in 1971 from the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o6] Since 1974 he worked at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University), since 1979 as a professor in computer science.

He was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 2000, and won the 2003 [CRM-Fields-PIMS prize](wikipedia://en/19284033).

In April 2007 a Joint Conference was organised by the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal) and Concordia University honouring four decades of McKay's work.
