# Monoidal monad

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoidal monad"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **monoidal monad** $(T,\eta,\mu,T_{A,B},T_0)$ is a [monad](wikipedia://en/355140) $(T,\eta,\mu)$ on a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $(C,\otimes,I)$ such that the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $T:(C,\otimes,I)\to(C,\otimes,I)$ is a [lax monoidal functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax_monoidal_functor) and the [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) $\eta$ and $\mu$ are [monoidal natural transformations](wikipedia://en/4585487). In other words, $T$ is equipped with coherence maps $T_{A,B}:TA\otimes TB\to T(A\otimes B)$ and $T_0:I\to TI$ satisfying [certain properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax_monoidal_functor) (again: they are lax monoidal), and the unit $\eta: id \Rightarrow T$ and multiplication $\mu:T^2\Rightarrow T$ are [monoidal natural transformations](wikipedia://en/4585487). By monoidality of $\eta$, the morphisms $T_0$ and $\eta_I$ are necessarily equal.

:::

All of the above can be compressed into the statement that a monoidal monad is a monad in the [2-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_2-category) $\mathsf{MonCat}$ of monoidal categories, lax monoidal functors, and monoidal natural transformations.

## Opmonoidal monads {#Opmonoidal_monads}

Opmonoidal monads have been studied under various names. [Ieke Moerdijk](wikipedia://en/25499812) introduced them as "Hopf monads",[^o3] while in works of Bruguières and Virelizier they are called "bimonads", by analogy to "[bialgebra](wikipedia://en/310887)",[^o4] reserving the term "Hopf monad" for opmonoidal monads with an antipode, in analogy to "[Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714)".

An **opmonoidal monad** is a monad $(T,\eta,\mu)$ *in* the 2-category of $\mathsf{OpMonCat}$ monoidal categories, oplax monoidal functors and monoidal natural transformations. That means a monad $(T,\eta,\mu)$ *on* a monoidal category $(C,\otimes,I)$ together with coherence maps $T^{A,B}:T(A\otimes B) \to TA\otimes TB$ and $T^0:TI\to I$ satisfying three axioms that make an opmonoidal functor, and four more axioms that make the unit $\eta$ and the multiplication $\mu$ into opmonoidal natural transformations. Alternatively, an opmonoidal monad is a monad on a monoidal category such that the category of Eilenberg-Moore algebras has a monoidal structure for which the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) is strong monoidal.[^o3][^o5]

An easy example for the monoidal category $\operatorname{Vect}$ of vector spaces is the monad $- \otimes A$, where $A$ is a [bialgebra](wikipedia://en/310887).[^o4] The multiplication and unit of $A$ define the multiplication and unit of the monad, while the comultiplication and counit of $A$ give rise to the opmonoidal structure. The algebras of this monad are right $A$-modules, which one may tensor in the same way as their underlying vector spaces.

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [Kleisli category](wikipedia://en/3957200) of a monoidal monad has a canonical monoidal structure, induced by the monoidal structure of the monad, and such that the [free functor](wikipedia://en/333835) is strong monoidal. The canonical adjunction between $C$ and the Kleisli category is a [monoidal adjunction](wikipedia://en/4623563) with respect to this monoidal structure, this means that the 2-category $\mathsf{MonCat}$ has Kleisli objects for monads.
- The 2-category of monads in $\mathsf{MonCat}$ is the 2-category of monoidal monads $\mathsf{Mnd(MonCat)}$ and it is isomorphic to the 2-category $\mathsf{Mon(Mnd(Cat))}$ of monoidales (or pseudomonoids) in the category of monads $\mathsf{Mnd(Cat)}$, (lax) monoidal arrows between them and monoidal cells between them.[^o8]
- The [Eilenberg-Moore category](wikipedia://en/355140#Algebras_for_a_monad) of an opmonoidal monad has a canonical monoidal structure such that the forgetful functor is strong monoidal.[^o3] Thus, the 2-category $\mathsf{OpmonCat}$ has Eilenberg-Moore objects for monads.[^o5]
- The 2-category of monads in $\mathsf{OpmonCat}$ is the 2-category of monoidal monads $\mathsf{Mnd(OpmonCat)}$ and it is isomorphic to the 2-category $\mathsf{Opmon(Mnd(Cat))}$ of monoidales (or pseudomonoids) in the category of monads $\mathsf{Mnd(Cat)}$ opmonoidal arrows between them and opmonoidal cells between them.[^o8]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The following monads on the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), with its [cartesian monoidal](wikipedia://en/43676277) structure, are monoidal monads:

- The [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) monad $(\mathbb{P},\varnothing,\cup)$. Indeed, there is a function $\mathbb{P}(X)\times\mathbb{P}(Y)\to\mathbb{P}(X\times Y)$, sending a pair $(X'\subseteq X,Y'\subseteq Y)$ of subsets to the subset $\{(x,y)\mid x\in X'\text{ and } y\in Y'\}\subseteq X\times Y$. This function is natural in *X* and *Y*. Together with the unique function $\{1\}\to\mathbb{P}(\varnothing)$ as well as the fact that $\mu,\eta$ are monoidal natural transformations, $\mathbb{P}$ is established as a monoidal monad.
- The [probability distribution (Giry) monad](wikipedia://en/75998118).

- If $M$ is a monoid, then $X\mapsto X\times M$ is a monad, but in general there is no reason to expect a monoidal structure on it (unless $M$ is commutative).
