# Walter Richard Talbot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Richard Talbot"}

**Walter Richard Talbot** (1909-1977)[^o2] was the fourth [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Americans) to earn a Ph.D. in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) ([Geometric Group Theory](wikipedia://en/955164)) from the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh)[^o3] and [Lincoln University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_University_%28Pennsylvania%29)'s youngest [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy).[^o4] He was a member of [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi)[^o5] and [Pi Tau Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Tau_Phi).[^o6] In 1969 Talbot co-founded the [National Association of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/58449356) (NAM) at [Morgan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_State_University),[^o7] the organization which, nine years later honored him at a memorial luncheon and created a scholarship[^o8] in his name.[^o9] In 1990 the Cox-Talbot lecture[^o10] was inaugurated recognizing his accomplishments together with [Elbert Frank Cox](wikipedia://en/2814552) – the first African-American to get a doctoral degree in mathematics.

:::

Academic positions Talbot held include: Mathematics Department Chair and Professor[^o11] (Morgan State University); assistant professor,[^o12] professor, department chair, dean of men, registrar, acting dean of instruction (Lincoln University).[^o13] Talbot was most widely known for his introduction of computer technology to the school.[^o14]

Talbot's [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) was entitled *Fundamental Regions in S(sub 6) for the Simple Quaternary G(sub 60), Type I.*[^o15]
