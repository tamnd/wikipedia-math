# Amplituhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amplituhedron"}

*Image: Amplituhedron-0c.png.* Notional amplituhedron visualization

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) (especially [twistor string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twistor_string_theory)), an **amplituhedron** is a geometric structure introduced in 2013 by [Nima Arkani-Hamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nima_Arkani-Hamed) and Jaroslav Trnka. It enables simplified calculation of [particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle) interactions in some [quantum field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_field) theories. In [planar *N* = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_Yang–Mills_theory), also equivalent to the perturbative [topological B model string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_string_theory#B-model) in [twistor space](wikipedia://en/2688710), an amplituhedron is defined as a mathematical space known as the positive [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810).[^o2][^o3]

:::

Amplituhedron theory challenges the notion that [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) [locality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_locality) and [unitarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarity_%28physics%29) are necessary components of a model of particle interactions. Instead, they are treated as properties that emerge from an underlying phenomenon.[^o4][^o5]

The connection between the amplituhedron and scattering amplitudes is a conjecture that has passed many non-trivial checks, including an understanding of how locality and unitarity arise as consequences of positivity.[^o2] Research has been led by [Nima Arkani-Hamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nima_Arkani-Hamed). [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) described the work as "very unexpected" and said that "it is difficult to guess what will happen or what the lessons will turn out to be".[^o6]

## Description {#Description}

When [subatomic particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subatomic_particle) interact, different outcomes are possible. The evolution of the various possibilities is called a "tree", and the [probability amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_amplitude) of a given outcome is called its [scattering amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude). According to the principle of [unitarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarity_%28physics%29), the sum of the probabilities (the squared moduli of the probability amplitudes) for every possible outcome is 1.

The [on-shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_shell_and_off_shell) scattering process "tree" may be described by a positive [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810), a structure in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) analogous to a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885), that generalizes the idea of a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135).[^o4] A [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) is the *n*-dimensional analogue of a 3-dimensional [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), the values being calculated in this case are scattering amplitudes, and so the object is called an *amplituhedron*.[^o2]

Using [twistor theory](wikipedia://en/260836), Britto–Cachazo–Feng–Witten recursion ([BCFW recursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCFW_recursion)) relations involved in the scattering process may be represented as a small number of twistor diagrams. These diagrams effectively provide the recipe for constructing the positive Grassmannian, i.e. the amplituhedron, which may be captured in a single equation.[^o4] The scattering amplitude can thus be thought of as the volume of a certain polytope, the positive Grassmannian, in momentum twistor space.[^o2]

When the volume of the amplituhedron is calculated in the [planar limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1/N_expansion) of [*N* = 4 *D* = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_4_super_Yang–Mills), it describes the [scattering amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude) of particles described by this theory.[^o2]

The twistor-based representation provides a recipe for constructing specific cells in the Grassmannian which assemble to form a positive Grassmannian, i.e., the representation describes a specific [cell decomposition](wikipedia://en/25884400) of the positive Grassmannian.

The recursion relations can be resolved in many different ways, each giving rise to a different representation, with the final amplitude expressed as a sum of on-shell processes in different ways as well. Therefore, any given on-shell representation of scattering amplitudes is not unique, but all such representations of a given interaction yield the same amplituhedron.[^o2]

The twistor approach is relatively abstract. While amplituhedron theory provides an underlying geometric model, the geometrical space is not physical spacetime and is also best understood as abstract.[^o8]

## Implications {#Implications}

The twistor approach simplifies calculations of particle interactions. In a conventional [perturbative](wikipedia://en/297069) approach to quantum field theory, such interactions may require the calculation of thousands of [Feynman diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feynman_diagrams), most describing off-shell "virtual" particles which have no directly observable existence. In contrast, [twistor theory](wikipedia://en/260836) provides an approach in which scattering amplitudes can be computed in a way that yields much simpler expressions.[^o10] Amplituhedron theory calculates scattering amplitudes without referring to such virtual particles. This undermines the case for even a transient, unobservable existence for such virtual particles.[^o8]

The geometric nature of the theory suggests in turn that the nature of the universe, in both classical [relativistic](wikipedia://en/30001) [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), may be described with [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o8]

Calculations can be done without assuming the quantum mechanical properties of [locality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_locality) and [unitarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarity_%28physics%29). In amplituhedron theory, locality and unitarity arise as a direct consequence of positivity.[^o5] They are encoded in the positive geometry of the amplituhedron, via the singularity structure of the integrand for scattering amplitudes.[^o2] Arkani-Hamed suggests this is why amplituhedron theory simplifies scattering-amplitude calculations: in the Feynman-diagrams approach, locality is manifest, whereas in the amplituhedron approach, it is implicit.[^o11]
