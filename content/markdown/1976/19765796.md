# Jordan Ellenberg

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Jordan Ellenberg"}

**Jordan Stuart Ellenberg** (born October 30, 1971) is an American mathematician who is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison).[^o5] His research involves [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He is also an author of both fiction and non-fiction writing.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Ellenberg was born in [Potomac, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac,_Maryland). He was a [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy) who taught himself to read at the age of two by watching *[Sesame Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Street)*.[^o7] His mother discovered his ability one day while she was driving on the [Capital Beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_495_%28Capital_Beltway%29) when her toddler informed her, "The sign says '[Bethesda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland) is to the right.'" In second grade, he helped his teenage babysitter with her math homework. By fourth grade, he was participating in high school competitions (such as the [American Regions Mathematics League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Regions_Mathematics_League)) as a member of the [Montgomery County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland) math team. And by eighth grade, he had started college-level work.

He was part of the [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) [Study of Mathematically Precocious Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_of_Mathematically_Precocious_Youth) longitudinal cohort. He scored a perfect 800 on the math portion and a 680 on the verbal portion of the [SAT-I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT-I) exam at the age of 12.[^o8] When he was in eighth grade, he took honors calculus classes at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland); when he was a junior at [Winston Churchill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill_High_School_%28Potomac,_Maryland%29), he earned a perfect score of 1600 on the SAT; and as a high school senior, he placed second in the national [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search).[^o9] He participated in the [International Mathematical Olympiads](wikipedia://en/82208) three times, winning gold medals in 1987 and 1989 (with perfect scores) and a silver medal in 1988.[^o10] He was also a two-time [Putnam fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) (1990 and 1992) while at Harvard.[^o11]

## Career {#Career}

In 2004, he began teaching at the University of Wisconsin-Madison and is currently the John D. MacArthur Professor of Mathematics, a position he has held since 2015.[^o13] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and was a plenary speaker at the 2013 [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143) where he spoke on the subject of number theory and algebraic topology, the study of abstract high-dimensional shapes and the relations between them.[^o14][^o15][^o16] He was named a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 2015.[^o17] He was elected as one of the six A.D. White Professors-at-Large at Cornell in 2019.[^o18] His research focuses on "the fields of number theory and algebraic geometry."[^o29]

In addition to his research articles, he has authored a novel, *The Grasshopper King*,[^o19] which was a finalist for the 2004 [Young Lions Fiction Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Lions_Fiction_Award);[^o20] the "Do the Math" column in [Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_%28magazine%29);[^o21] two non-fiction books, *[How Not to Be Wrong](wikipedia://en/51077606)*[^o22][^o23][^o24] and *Shape: The Hidden Geometry of Information, Biology, Strategy, Democracy, and Everything Else* (2022);[^o25] and articles on mathematical topics in many newspapers and general magazines.

Ellenberg was a mathematics consultant for the 2017 film *[Gifted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifted_%282017_film%29)*, which features a math prodigy as its protagonist;[^o26] he also made a cameo appearance in the film as a professor lecturing on the [partition function](wikipedia://en/312250) and [Ramanujan's congruences](wikipedia://en/6703729).[^o27]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ellenberg lives in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), with his wife and children.[^o29] He is the son of statisticians [Susan S. Ellenberg](wikipedia://en/59133796) and [Jonas H. Ellenberg](wikipedia://en/81441289).[^o3]

He maintains a blog called Quomodocumque which means "after whatever fashion" in Latin.[^o30]
