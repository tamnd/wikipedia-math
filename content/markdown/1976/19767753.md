# Einasto profile

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Einasto profile"}

The **Einasto profile** (or **Einasto model**) is a mathematical function that describes how the density $\rho$ of a spherical stellar system varies with distance $r$ from its center. [Jaan Einasto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaan_Einasto) introduced his model at a 1963 conference in [Alma-Ata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma-Ata), Kazakhstan.[^o2]

:::

*Image: Einasto 2.jpg.* Einasto profiles. The order of $\alpha$ is reversed for large radii.

The Einasto profile possesses a [power law](wikipedia://en/24522) [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/164055) slope of the form:

$$\gamma(r) \equiv -\frac{\operatorname{d}\ln\rho(r)}{\operatorname{d}\ln r} \propto r^{\alpha}$$

which can be rearranged to give

$$\rho(r) \propto \exp{(-Ar^\alpha)} .$$

The parameter $\alpha$ controls the degree of curvature of the profile. This can be seen by computing the slope on a log-log plot:

$$\frac{d \ln\rho}{d \ln r} \propto -r^{\alpha} .$$

The larger $\alpha$, the more rapidly the slope varies with radius (see figure). Einasto's law can be described as a generalization of a power law, $\rho\propto r^{-N}$, which has a constant slope on a log-log plot.

Einasto's model has the same mathematical form as [Sersic's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sersic's_law), which is used to describe the surface brightness (i.e. projected density) profile of galaxies, except that the Einasto model describes a spherically symmetric density distribution in 3 dimensions, whereas the Sersic law describes a circularly symmetric surface density distribution in two dimensions.

Einasto's model has been used to describe many types of system, including [galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy),[^o3] and [dark matter halos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter_halo).[^o4][^o5]
