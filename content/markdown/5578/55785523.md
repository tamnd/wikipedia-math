# Richard H. Stockbridge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard H. Stockbridge"}

**Richard H. Stockbridge** is a [Distinguished Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee). His contributions to research primarily involve [stochastic control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_control_theory), [optimal stopping](wikipedia://en/7189886) and [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680). Most notably, alongside Professors [Thomas G. Kurtz](wikipedia://en/51178615), [Kurt Helmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Helmes), and [Chao Zhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Zhu), he developed the methodology of using [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) to solve stochastic control problems.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Stockbridge obtained his Ph.D. from the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) under the supervision of Thomas G. Kurtz with a dissertation entitled "Time-Average Control of Martingale Problems".[^o4] He also holds a master's degree in mathematics from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and attended [St. Lawrence University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_University) in Canton, New York, for his [baccalaureate studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree).[^o5]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Following the awarding of his Ph.D., Stockbridge served as an assistant professor in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) from 1987 to 1988. He then took an assistant professor position at the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky) from 1988 to 1993, leading to an associate professorship which he held until 2000. Later, Stockbridge began working at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee) and became a full professor in 2002. In 2018, he was awarded the title of "distinguished professor" by the University of Wisconsin Milwaukee Distinguished Faculty Committee.[^o7]

Stockbridge has also held various visiting positions, including:

- *Visiting Scholar,* [Heriot Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University), School of Mathematical and Computer Science, [Edinburgh, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh,_Scotland), March–August 2016
- *Sabbaticant Professor*, [University of Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Botswana), Department of Mathematics, July 2008 – January 2009
- *Visiting Fellow*, [Bath University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath), Department of Mathematical Sciences, [Bath, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath,_Somerset), January–July 1997
- *Visiting Assistant Professor, [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky)*, Department of Mathematics, [Lexington, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky), 1988–89

## Research {#Research}

Professor Stockbridge's research is focused on developing [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) techniques in [stochastic control](wikipedia://en/17058216). These techniques give an alternative formulation to the traditional [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) framework used in stochastic control problems and have been demonstrated in examples including control of the running maximum of a diffusion,[^o9] optimal stopping problems,[^o10] and regime-switching diffusions.[^o11]

Through the completion of his Ph.D. dissertation, Stockbridge examined the relationship between long-term average stochastic control problems and linear programs spanning the space of stationary distributions for that controlled process, ultimately concluding their equivalence. This dissertation served as a basis for significant work in the field.

Following his graduate studies, Stockbridge helped expand the applications of this equivalence between linear programming and stochastic control to include discounted, first-exit and finite horizon problems.
