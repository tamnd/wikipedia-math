# Mary Gibbons Natrella

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Gibbons Natrella"}

**Mary Gibbons Natrella** (September 23, 1922 – May 18, 1988) was an American statistician and "an expert on the application of modern statistical techniques in physical science experimentation and engineering testing". She worked at the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology), where she wrote their Handbook 91, *Experimental Statistics* (1963).[^o2] It became one of their "all-time best selling publications" and has been recognized as "a monumental work" with "deep and long-lasting impact on the application of statistics to the planning and analysis of scientific experiments".

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mary Blanche Gibbons was born in [Scranton, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania). After earlier studies at [Keystone College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_College), she completed a bachelor's degree at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1942. She worked as a mathematician for the [U.S. Army Ordnance Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Ordnance_Corps) from 1942 to 1945, and as a statistician for the Navy beginning in 1945. In 1946, she married Joseph Victor Natrella, a mathematician for the Air Force who later worked for [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA). In 1950, she moved from the Navy to the National Bureau of Standards, where she remained until retiring in 1986.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Before writing her book, NBS Handbook 91 Experimental Statistics, Natrella helped produce defense standard [MIL-STD-105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD-105) for [acceptance sampling](wikipedia://en/25719356). At the National Bureau of Standards, she was responsible for teaching statistics to scientists, and "had a special gift for elucidating difficult statistical concepts".

## Recognition and legacy {#Recognition_and_legacy}

In 1981, Natrella was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association); her brother-in-law, [Vito Natrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Natrella), was also a Fellow. She was also a fellow of the American Society for Test Materials. She was given the [Department of Commerce Bronze Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Commerce_Bronze_Medal) in 1982. She died in 1988 and was buried in [Ivy Hill Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_Hill_Cemetery_%28Alexandria,_Virginia%29) in [Alexandria, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia).[^o6]

A scholarship in her and her husband's name is awarded annually by the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), funded by a gift from her husband when she died.
