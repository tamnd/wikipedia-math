# Nucleus (order theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nucleus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and especially in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924), a **nucleus** is a function $F$ on a [meet-semilattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meet-semilattice) $\mathfrak{A}$ such that (for every $p$ in $\mathfrak{A}$):[^o2]

:::

1. $$p \le F(p)$$
1. $$F(F(p)) = F(p)$$
1. $$F(p \wedge q) = F(p) \wedge F(q)$$

Every nucleus is evidently a monotone function.

## Frames and locales {#Frames_and_locales}

Usually, the term *nucleus* is used in [pointless topology](wikipedia://en/24764) (when the semilattice $\mathfrak{A}$ is a frame).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="Proposition"}

**Proposition:** If $F$ is a nucleus on a frame $\mathfrak{A}$, then the [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) $\operatorname{Fix} F$ of fixed points of $F$, with order inherited from $\mathfrak{A}$, is also a frame.[^o5]

:::
