# Christiane Rousseau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christiane Rousseau"}

**Christiane Rousseau** (born March 30, 1954, in [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_%28city%29), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France)) is a French and Canadian mathematician, a professor in the department of mathematics and statistics at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal). She was president of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) from 2002 to 2004.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rousseau earned her Ph.D. from the Université de Montréal in 1977, under the supervision of Dana Schlomiuk, with a thesis entitled "Topos Theory and Complex Analysis".[^o2] After postdoctoral research at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), she joined the Montréal faculty in 1979, and was promoted to full professor in 1991.[^o2] She was interim Director of [Centre de Recherches Mathématiques](wikipedia://en/3505436) from 2008 to 2009.

She was the first female vice-president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), from 2011 to 2014. She implemented and led the Mathematics on Planet Earth initiative in 2013 (MPE 2013), supported by UNESCO, and played a leading role in having March 14 declared the International Day of Mathematics by UNESCO.[^o5]

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

Rousseau's main areas of research are [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087). This includes work on singularities of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042), [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750) (the part about differential equations)[^o7] as well as analysis of important differential equations in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biology) such as the [predator-prey Lotka–Volterra equations](wikipedia://en/462534). Other scientific interests include applications of [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) to the [Grand Unified Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Unified_Theory).

She actively participates in mathematics popularization programs in high schools and [cégeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEGEP), namely through presentations as well as the publication of over 35 vulgarization articles in the *Accromath* journal.[^o8]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She has received the Adrien-Pouliot Prize and the Abel-Gauthier Prize of the Mathematical Association of Québec,[^o2] and the 2009 Graham Wright Award for Distinguished Service from the Canadian Mathematical Society.[^o10] She also received the 2014 [George Pólya Award](wikipedia://en/36409098) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for her article about a discovery by [Inge Lehmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inge_Lehmann), "How Inge Lehmann Discovered the Inner Core of the Earth".[^o11] In 2012, she became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS).[^o12] In 2017 she became the inaugural recipient of the AMS' [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) prize for furthering human values and the common good through mathematics.[^o13] In 2018 the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) listed her in their inaugural class of fellows.[^o14]

In 2024, she was appointed as an officer to the [Order of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Canada). She lives in [Mont-Saint-Grégoire, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Saint-Grégoire,_Quebec).[^o15]
