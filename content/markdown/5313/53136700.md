# Joseph Fletcher (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Fletcher"}

**Joseph Fletcher** (1813 – 1852) was an English statistical writer and barrister. He worked also on official committees and as a schools inspector.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Fletcher trained as a barrister, entering the [Middle Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Temple) in 1838 and being [called to the bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Called_to_the_bar) in 1841.

## Career {#Career}

Fletcher was the secretary of the [Royal Commission on Hand-Loom Weavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Hand-Loom_Weavers).[^o5] Starting in 1844, he worked as a [schools inspector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schools_inspector).[^o6]

From a young age, Fletcher wrote reports on social and health issues.[^o6] These included one on child employment in lead mines, for the Children's Employment Commission 1842.[^o7] His commission reports influenced legislation. The findings of the children's employment commission in particular laid the basis for parliamentary control. As a schools inspector he wrote also on education.[^o6]

In 1850 Fletcher published a *Summary of the Moral Statistics of England and Wales*; and in the following year a work on *Education: National, Voluntary, and Free*.[^o6] He was unconvinced of the moral superiority of communities that were relatively sparsely developed, such as small towns and farming areas, presaging views later held by [Herbert Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spencer) and [Norbert Elias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Elias).[^o8] He studied foreign educational systems, and issued (1851–2) two treatises on *The Farm School of the Continent, and its Applicability to the Preventive and Reformatory Education of Pauper and Criminal Children in England and Wales*.[^o6]

Fletcher was one of the honorary secretaries of the [Statistical Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Society_of_London); and also editor of the *[Statistical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Journal)*. He was member of the council of the [British Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association), and acted as secretary to its statistical section.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

Fletcher died at [Chirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirk), [Denbighshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denbighshire) on 11 August 1852. He was buried in the graveyard of [All Hallows' Church, Tottenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Hallows'_Church,_Tottenham).[^o6]
