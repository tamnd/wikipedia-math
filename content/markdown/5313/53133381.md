# Crumpling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crumpling"}

Crumpled

*Image: Paperball.png.* Ball of crumpled paper

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), **crumpling** is the process whereby a sheet of [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper) or other two-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) undergoes disordered [deformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_%28engineering%29) to yield a [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3054853) structure comprising a random network of [ridges](wikipedia://en/149926#Ridge_or_.28n-2.29-face) and [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet) with variable density. The geometry of crumpled structures is of interest to mathematicians studying topology.[^o2] Crumpled paper balls have been studied and found to exhibit surprisingly complex structures with [compressive strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressive_strength) resulting from [frictional](wikipedia://en/11062) interactions at [locally](wikipedia://en/1462457) flat facets between folds.[^o3] The unusually high compressive strength of crumpled structures relative to their density is of interest in the disciplines of [materials science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science) and [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering).

:::

## Significance {#Significance}

The packing of a sheet by crumpling is a complex phenomenon that depends on material parameters and the packing protocol. Thus the crumpling behaviour of foil, paper and poly-membranes differs significantly and can be interpreted on the basis of material foldability.[^o5] The high compressive strength exhibited by dense crumple formed [cellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulose) paper is of interest towards impact dissipation applications and has been proposed as an approach to [utilising waste paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_recycling).[^o6]

From a practical standpoint, crumpled balls of paper are commonly used as [toys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_play_and_toys) for [domestic cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat).
