# John Kemp (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Kemp"}

Prof **John Kemp** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) LLD (1763–1812) was a Scottish mathematician, who settled in the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state) of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29) for most of his life.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born on 10 April 1763 at Achlossan near [Aboyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboyne) in [Aberdeenshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeenshire), Scotland, the son of a farmer. He studied Mathematics at [Aberdeen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_University) and graduated in 1783. In the same year he emigrated to the United States of America, settling first in [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia) then moving to New York\] in 1785.

In 1786 he successfully applied to become Professor of Mathematics at [Columbia College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (later Columbia University), aged only 23. In 1795 he began teaching Geography in addition to Mathematics and in 1799 he also began teaching Natural Philosophy (Physics).[^o3]

In 1792 he was elected a Foreign Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [James Gregory](wikipedia://en/220425), [Daniel Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Rutherford) and [John Playfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Playfair). He was not (as some records claim) one of the founders of the Society in 1783.[^o4]

In 1810 he visited [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie) in relation to an assessment of the then-proposed canal project, and considered it wholly viable.

He died in New York on 15 November 1812 following a long illness. He married twice but both wives died. One daughter (by his first marriage) survived him.[^o5]
