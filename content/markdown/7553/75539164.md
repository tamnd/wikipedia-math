# Jorge Nuno Silva

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jorge Nuno Silva"}

*Image: Jorge Nuno Silva.jpg.* Jorge Nuno Silva

**Jorge Nuno Silva** (born 1956) is a Portuguese mathematician who taught at the [University of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lisbon), starting in 1995 and retiring in 2023.[^o2] His interests encompass the pedagogy of mathematics, history of mathematics, history of board games, mathematical games, and [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). He is the chief editor for *Recreational Mathematics Magazine*[^o3] and *Board Game Studies Journal*.[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

In 1974, Silva completed his secondary education at the National Lyceum of Viana do Castelo. Subsequently, in 1976/77, he enrolled at the University of Lisbon School of Medicine. In 1983, he earned his Bachelor of Science degree in Pure Mathematics from the University of Lisbon Faculty of Sciences (FCUL).[^o6] In 1991 he obtained a Master of Arts degree at UC Berkeley, writing *Some Notes on Game Bounds* under the direction of [Elwyn Berlekamp](wikipedia://en/655893).[^o7] In 1994 he got a Ph.D. at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) with the dissertation, "Some Notes on the Theory of Hilbert Spaces of Analytic Functions on the Unit Disc" under doctoral advisor [Donald Erik Sarason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Erik_Sarason).[^o8]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

From 1995 until his retirement in April 2023 he was a professor at the University of Lisbon.[^o6] First at the Department of Mathematics (1995-2006), and then at the Department of History and Philosophy of Science (2006-2023).

He is a teacher trainer for [Associação Ludus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associação_Ludus) and the [Portuguese Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mathematical_Society).

## Mathematics promotion {#Mathematics_promotion}

In 1998 Silva wrote *Berkley Problems of Mathematics*, a compendium of problems which is widely used by PhD candidates as a reference.[^o11] He is president of the Ludus Association (Associação Ludus), an organization for popularizing the culture and history of mathematics.[^o12] He is a member of Centro Interuniversitário de História das Ciências e da Tecnologia (CIUHCT)

Silva has been involved in efforts to popularize mathematics around the world. In a 2009 interview he stated his guiding philosophy: "Mathematics is, by its very nature, the pure joy of thinking, and the same goes for board games. There is a lack of challenging activities in our Western culture. Games can close this gap; there are many interrelationships between mathematics, history, and culture."[^o13] In an interview with [Diário de Notícias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diário_de_Notícias) he said, "One day a great game will be invented to teach Mathematics and the world will change."[^o14]

Silva is the co-founder of the Circo Matemático which, since its founding in 2011, has toured more than a dozen countries on four continents promoting the popularization of mathematics.[^o15]

In Portugal he has had an enormous effect on mathematics in public education[^o21] and he often appears on [Rádio e Televisão de Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rádio_e_Televisão_de_Portugal) (RTP) as an expert on games.[^o16][^o17]
