# Radiodrome

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radiodrome"}

*Image: Dog\_curve.svg.* The path of a dog chasing a hare running along a vertical straight line at a constant speed. The dog runs towards the momentary position of the hare, and will be changing his heading continuously.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **radiodrome** is a specific type of [pursuit curve](wikipedia://en/587650): the path traced by a point that continuously moves toward a target traveling in a straight line at constant speed. The term comes from the Latin radius (ray or spoke) and the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) grc (running or racetrack), reflecting the radial nature of the motion.

:::

The most classic and widely recognized example is the so-called **dog curve**, which describes the path of a dog swimming across a river toward a hare moving along the opposite bank. Because of the current, the dog must constantly adjust its heading, resulting in a longer, curved trajectory. This case was first described by the French mathematician and hydrographer [Pierre Bouguer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Bouguer) in 1732.

Radiodromes are distinguished from other pursuit curves by the assumption that the pursuer always heads directly toward the target’s current position, while the target moves at a constant velocity along a straight path.

## Mathematical analysis {#Mathematical_analysis}

Introduce a [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) with origin at the position of the dog at time zero and with *y*-axis in the direction the hare is running with the constant speed $V_{t}$. The position of the hare at time zero is $(A_{x}, A_{y})$ with $A_{x} > 0$ and at time $t$ it is

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$(T_x\ ,\ T_y)\ =\ (A_x\ ,\ A_y+V_t t)~. \tag{1}$$

:::

The dog runs with the constant speed $V_{d}$ towards the instantaneous position of the hare.

The [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) corresponding to the movement of the dog, $(x(t), y(t))$, is consequently

::: {#o4 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\dot x= V_d\ \frac{T_x-x}{\sqrt{(T_x-x)^2+(T_y-y)^2}} \tag{2}$$

:::

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$\dot y= V_d\ \frac{T_y-y}{\sqrt{(T_x-x)^2+(T_y-y)^2}} ~. \tag{3}$$

:::

It is possible to obtain a closed-form analytic expression $y=f(x)$ for the motion of the dog. From ((2)) and ((3)), it follows that

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

y'(x)=\frac{T_y-y}{T_x-x}$ . (4)

:::

Multiplying both sides with $T_x-x$ and taking the derivative with respect to $x$, using that

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$\frac{dT_y}{dx}\ =\ \frac{dT_y}{dt}\ \frac{dt}{dx}\ =\ \frac{V_t}{V_d}\ \sqrt{{y'}^2+1}~ , \tag{5}$$

:::

one gets

::: {#o8 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$y''=\frac{V_t\ \sqrt{1+{y'}^2}}{V_d(A_x-x)} \tag{6}$$

:::

or

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$\frac{y''}{\sqrt{1+{y'}^2}}=\frac{V_t}{V_d(A_x-x)} ~. \tag{7}$$

:::

From this relation, it follows that

::: {#o10 .equation cue=numblk name="8"}

$$\sinh^{-1}(y')=B-\frac{V_t}{V_d}\ \ln(A_x-x)~, \tag{8}$$

:::

where $B$ is the [constant of integration](wikipedia://en/147939) determined by the initial value of $y$' at time zero, $y' (0)= \sinh(B - (V_{t} /V_{d}) \ln A_{x})$, i.e.,

::: {#o11 .equation cue=numblk name="9"}

$$B=\frac{V_t}{V_d}\ \ln(A_x)+\ln\left(y'(0)+\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right) . \tag{9}$$

:::

From ((8)) and ((9)), it follows after some computation that

::: {#o12 .equation cue=numblk name="10"}

y'= \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(y'(0)+\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right)^{-\frac{V_t}{V_d}} + \left(y'(0)-\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right)^{\frac{V_t}{V_d}}\right]$ . (10)

:::

Furthermore, since $y(0)=0$, it follows from ((1)) and ((4)) that

::: {#o13 .equation cue=numblk name="11"}

y'(0)= \frac{A_y}{A_x}$ . (11)

:::

If, now, $V_{t} \ne V_{d}$, relation ((10)) integrates to

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="12"}

$$y= C - \frac{A_x}{2}\left[ \frac{\left(y'(0)+\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right) ^{1 - \frac{V_t}{V_d}} }{1-\frac{V_t}{V_d}} + \frac{\left(y'(0)-\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right) ^{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}} }{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}} \right], \tag{12}$$

:::

where $C$ is the constant of integration. Since again $y(0)=0$, it's

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="13"}

$$C = \frac{A_x}{2}\left[ \frac{y'(0)+\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}}{1-\frac{V_t}{V_d}} + \frac{y'(0)-\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}}{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}} \right]. \tag{13}$$

:::

The equations ((11)), ((12)) and ((13)), then, together imply

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="14"}

y= \frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{A_y+\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}}{1-\frac{V_t}{V_d}} \left[1-\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right) ^{1 - \frac{V_t}{V_d}}\right] + \frac{A_y-\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}}{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}} \left[1-\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right) ^{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}}\right]\right\}$ . (14)

:::

If $V_{t} = V_{d}$, relation ((10)) gives, instead,

::: {#o17 .equation cue=numblk name="15"}

y= C -\frac{A_x}{2}\left[\left(y'(0)+\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\left(y'(0)-\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right)\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right)^2\right]$ . (15)

:::

Using $y(0)=0$ once again, it follows that

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="16"}

$$C = \frac{A_x}{4}\left(y'(0)-\sqrt{{y'(0)}^2+1}\right) . \tag{16}$$

:::

The equations ((11)), ((15)) and ((16)), then, together imply that

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="17"}

y= \frac{1}{4}\left(A_y-\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}\right)\left[1-\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right)^2\right] - \frac{1}{2}\left(A_y+\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}\right)\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{A_x}\right)$ . (17)

:::

If $V_{t} < V_{d}$, it follows from ((14)) that

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="18"}

$$\lim_{x \to A_x}y(x) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{A_y+\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}}{1-\frac{V_t}{V_d}} + \frac{A_y-\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}}{1 + \frac{V_t}{V_d}}\right) . \tag{18}$$

:::

If $V_{t} \ge V_{d}$, one has from ((14)) and ((17)) that $\lim_{x \to A_x}y(x) = \infty$, which means that the hare will never be caught, whenever the chase starts.
