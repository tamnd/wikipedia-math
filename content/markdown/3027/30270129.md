# Tube domain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tube domain"}

tube In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **tube domain** is a generalization of the notion of a vertical strip (or [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane)) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) to [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). A strip can be thought of as the collection of complex numbers whose [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) lie in a given subset of the real line and whose imaginary part is unconstrained; likewise, a tube is the set of complex vectors whose real part is in some given collection of real vectors, and whose imaginary part is unconstrained.

:::

Tube domains are [domains](wikipedia://en/50263) of the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of a function of several [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) variables (see [multidimensional Laplace transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Laplace_transform)). [Hardy spaces](wikipedia://en/314780) on tubes can be defined in a manner in which a version of the [Paley–Wiener theorem](wikipedia://en/632992) from one variable continues to hold, and characterizes the elements of Hardy spaces as the Laplace transforms of functions with appropriate integrability properties. Tubes over [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292) are [domains of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377). The Hardy spaces on tubes over convex [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monge_cone) have an especially rich structure, so that precise results are known concerning the boundary values of *H*<sup>*p*</sup> functions. In mathematical physics, the [future tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_tube) is the tube domain associated to the interior of the past [null cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_cone) in [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), and has applications in [relativity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relativity_theory) and [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).[^n1] Certain tubes over cones support a [Bergman metric](wikipedia://en/12457310) in terms of which they become [bounded symmetric domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_symmetric_domain). One of these is the [Siegel half-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegel_half-space) which is fundamental in [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118).

## Definition {#Definition}

Let **R**<sup>*n*</sup> denote [real coordinate space](wikipedia://en/1482083) of dimension *n* and **C**<sup>*n*</sup> denote [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) coordinate space. Then any element of **C**<sup>*n*</sup> can be decomposed into real and imaginary parts:

$$a=(z_1,\dots,z_n) = (x_1+iy_1, \dots, x_n+iy_n) = (x_1,\dots,x_n) + i(y_1,\dots,y_n)=x+iy.$$

Let *A* be an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) subset of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. The **tube over *A***, denoted *T*<sub>*A*</sub>, is the subset of **C**<sup>*n*</sup> consisting of all elements whose real parts lie in *A*:[^n2]

$$T_A = \{ z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^n\mid x\in A\}.$$

:::

## Tubes as domains of holomorphy {#Tubes_as_domains_of_holomorphy}

Suppose that *A* is a connected open set. Then any complex-valued function that is [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) in a tube *T*<sub>*A*</sub> can be extended uniquely to a holomorphic function on the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the tube ch *T*<sub>*A*</sub>,[^n2] which is also a tube, and in fact

$$\operatorname{ch} \, T_A = T_{\operatorname{ch}\, A}.$$

Since any convex open set is a [domain of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377) ([holomorphically convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphically_convex)), a convex tube is also a domain of holomorphy. So the [holomorphic envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_envelope) of any tube is equal to its convex hull.[^n3][^n4]

## Hardy spaces {#Hardy_spaces}

Let *A* be an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. The [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) *H*<sup>*p*</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>) is the set of all [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) *F* in *T*<sub>*A*</sub> such that

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |F(x+iy)|^p\, dy < \infty$$

for all *x* in *A*.

In the special case of *p* = 2, functions in *H*<sup>2</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>) can be characterized as follows.[^n5] Let *ƒ* be a complex-valued function on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> satisfying

$$\sup_{x\in A}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|f(t)|^2e^{-4\pi x\cdot t}\,dt < \infty.$$

The Fourier–Laplace transform of *ƒ* is defined by

$$F(x+iy) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n}e^{2\pi z\cdot t}f(t)\,dt.$$

Then *F* is well-defined and belongs to *H*<sup>2</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>). Conversely, every element of *H*<sup>2</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>) has this form.

A corollary of this characterization is that *H*<sup>2</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>) contains a nonzero function if and only if *A* contains no straight line.

### Tubes over cones {#Tubes_over_cones}

Let *A* be an open convex cone in **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. This means that *A* is an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) such that, whenever *x* lies in *A*, so does the entire ray from the origin to *x*. Symbolically,

$$x\in A \implies tx\in A\ \ \ \text{for all}\ t>0.$$

If *A* is a cone, then the elements of *H*<sub>2</sub>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>) have *L*<sup>2</sup> boundary limits in the sense that[^n5]

$$\lim_{y\to 0} F(x+iy)$$

exists in *L*<sup>2</sup>(*B*). There is an analogous result for *H*<sup>*p*</sup>(*T*<sub>*A*</sub>), but it requires additional regularity of the cone (specifically, the [dual cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_cone) *A*\* needs to have nonempty interior).
