# Robert McLachlan (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert McLachlan"}

**Robert Iain McLachlan** (born 1964) is a [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) mathematician and Distinguished Professor in the School of Fundamental Sciences, [Massey University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_University), New Zealand.[^o17] His research in [geometric integration](wikipedia://en/2208188) encompasses both [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), modelling the structure of systems such as liquids, [climate cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_variability), and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). He also writes for the public on the subject of [climate change policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_policy).[^o3]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

McLachlan was born in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch), New Zealand in 1964,[^o5] and studied mathematics at the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury), graduating with a [BSc (Hons)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) First Class in 1984.[^o6] One formative experience was in his last year of high school, where he had free rein to experiment with [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) programming on the school [PDP-11/10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11).[^o6]

McLachlan went on to graduate work in numerical analysis in 1986. He received a PhD from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (the California Institute of Technology) in 1990, supervised by [Herbert Keller](wikipedia://en/24424009) in [computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924), with a thesis titled "Separated Viscous Flows via Multigrid".[^o6] He then worked as a [postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) in what was then the new field of [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298). After meeting [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316), who was visiting [Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_Colorado) at the time, McLachlan spent six months on a postdoctoral fellowship in Switzerland, at the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Institute_of_Technology_in_Zurich).[^o6]

McLachlan joined [Massey University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_University) in [Palmerston North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_North) in 1994, and began a collaboration with [Reinout Quispel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinout_Quispel) at [La Trobe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trobe_University) that resulted in over 26 publications on geometric integration.[^o6] In 2002 he became Professor of Applied Mathematics at Massey, and spent a year's sabbatical at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva), working with [Gerhard Wanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Wanner) and [Ernst Hairer](wikipedia://en/31446016), and the [Norwegian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in Oslo.[^o7] In 2007 he won the prestigious Germund Dahlquist Prize, the first mathematician from the southern hemisphere to do so.[^o8] From 2008 to 2012, along with [Stephen Marsland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Marsland) and [Matt Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Perlmutter), he worked on a [Marsden grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsden_grant) project "Geodesics in diffeomorphism groups: geometry and applications", designing efficient numerical integrators that preserved the geometric properties of systems.[^o9] In 2013, McLachlan was the LMS-NZMS Aitken Lecturer, delivering talks on geometric numerical integration to six UK universities.[^o10][^o11]

McLachlan is a Fellow of the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society) (NZMS), and in 1998 organised the first of the annual Manawatu-Wellington Applied Mathematics Conferences. He was president of the NZMS in 2008–2009 and vice president in 2010, and edited the *New Zealand Journal of Mathematics* for six years.[^o6] Since 2016 he has been a Distinguished Professor in Massey's School of Fundamental Sciences.[^o12]

## Research {#Research}

McLachlan is a world leader in the field of [geometric integration](wikipedia://en/2208188), a technique for the reliable simulation of large-scale complex systems, and in particular the use of [symplectic](wikipedia://en/2063558) techniques in the numerical analysis of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309).[^o6][^o14] This field, which McLachlan helped found in the 1990s, builds into its approach the underlying geometric structure of data sets.[^o12] Because it allows the simulation of large systems, it has the potential for solving practical problems in fields as disparate as the structure of liquids, [climate cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_variability), the motion of the [solar system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System), particles in [circular accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_accelerator), [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295) in dynamical systems, and weather forecasting.[^o6][^o20][^o15] For example, during [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy) in 2012, the [European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Centre_for_Medium-Range_Weather_Forecasts), using geometric integration models, correctly predicted the hurricane would suddenly turn 90 degrees towards New York six days in advance.[^o20] McLachlan's methods have been used in computational science to examine a possible celestial origin of the [ice ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age), biological models, and the dynamics of flexible structures.[^o20] His research contributed to a solar system simulation that revised the dates of geophysical epochs by millions of years.[^o12] Although his work in geometric numerical integration has a wide range of real-world applications, he considers himself a [pure mathematician](wikipedia://en/457210).[^o20]

## Science communication {#Science_communication}

As an advocate for action on climate change McLachlan writes frequently for the public, in media like *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*'s blog,[^o22] the blog *Planetary Ecology* and the [Royal Society Te Apārangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Te_Apārangi)'s *Sciblogs*. His writing focuses on [consequences of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change),[^o22] the benefits of wind turbines,[^o23] electric cars,[^o24] and climate policy.[^o25][^o26]
