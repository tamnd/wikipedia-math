# Remmert–Stein theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Remmert–Stein theorem"}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a field in mathematics, the **Remmert–Stein theorem**, introduced by (txt), gives conditions for the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of an [analytic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_variety) to be analytic.

:::

The theorem states that if *F* is an analytic set of dimension less than *k* in some [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) *D*, and *M* is an analytic subset of *D* – *F* with all components of dimension at least *k*, then the closure of *M* is either analytic or contains *F*.

The condition on the dimensions is necessary: for example, the set of points (1/*n*,0) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) is analytic in the complex plane minus the origin, but its closure in the complex plane is not.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Relations to other theorems"}

## Relations to other theorems {#Relations_to_other_theorems}

A consequence of the Remmert–Stein theorem (also treated in their paper), is [Chow's theorem](wikipedia://en/630505#Chow's_theorem) stating that any projective complex [analytic space](wikipedia://en/8031773) is necessarily a [projective algebraic variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_algebraic_variety).

The Remmert–Stein theorem is implied by a [proper mapping theorem](wikipedia://en/1190262) due to (Bishop 1964), see (Aguilar Verjovsky 2021).

:::
