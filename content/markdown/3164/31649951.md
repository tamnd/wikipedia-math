# Cyclohedron

*Image: Cyclohedron W3.svg.* The 2-dimensional cyclohedron $W_{3}$ and the correspondence between its vertices and edges with a cycle on three vertices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclohedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **cyclohedron** is a $d$-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) where $d$ can be any non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). It was first introduced as a [combinatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial) object by [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) and [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920)[^o2] and, for this reason, it is also sometimes called the **Bott–Taubes polytope**. It was later constructed as a polytope by Martin Markl[^o3] and by [Rodica Simion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodica_Simion).[^o4] Rodica Simion describes this polytope as an [associahedron](wikipedia://en/19698446) of type B.

:::

The cyclohedron appears in the study of [knot invariants](wikipedia://en/166898).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Cyclohedra belong to several larger families of polytopes, each providing a general construction. For instance, the cyclohedron belongs to the generalized associahedra[^o8] that arise from [cluster algebra](wikipedia://en/27038423), and to the graph-associahedra,[^o9] a family of polytopes each corresponding to a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). In the latter family, the graph corresponding to the $d$-dimensional cyclohedron is a cycle on $d+1$ vertices.

In topological terms, the [configuration space](wikipedia://en/54981574) of $d+1$ distinct points on the circle $S^1$ is a $(d+1)$-dimensional [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), which can be [compactified](wikipedia://en/31662076) into a [manifold with corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_with_corners) by allowing the points to approach each other. This [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) can be factored as $S^1 \times W_{d+1}$, where $W_{d+1}$ is the $d$-dimensional cyclohedron.

Just as the associahedron, the cyclohedron can be recovered by removing some of the [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) of the [permutohedron](wikipedia://en/12745947).[^o10]

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: 2-cyclohedron triangulation.svg.* The 2-dimensional cyclohedron as the centrally symmetric triangulations of the regular hexagon

The graph made up of the vertices and edges of the $d$-dimensional cyclohedron is the [flip graph](wikipedia://en/47526601) of the centrally symmetric partial [triangulations](wikipedia://en/679384) of a [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) with $2d+2$ vertices.[^o4] When $d$ goes to infinity, the asymptotic behavior of the diameter $\Delta$ of that graph is given by

$\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta}{d}=\frac{5}{2}$.[^o13]
