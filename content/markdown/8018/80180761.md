# Charles Livingston (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Livingston"}

**Charles Livingston** is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) and [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). He is a professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington).[^o2]

:::

In 1982, he posed a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) on [Seifert surfaces](wikipedia://en/1343951) that remained open for 40 years. It was finally [solved](wikipedia://en/82285) in 2022 in a collaborative effort of five mathematicians: Kyle Hayden, Seungwon Kim, [Maggie Miller](wikipedia://en/70294244), JungHwan Park, and Isaac Sundberg.[^o2]

His undergraduate studies were at [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT).[^o3] Livingston obtained his PhD in 1980 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), advised by [Robion Kirby](wikipedia://en/2932424) (thesis: *The Knotting of Surfaces in 4-Spaces*).[^o4]

He is the author of the textbook *Knot Theory* (MAA).[^o5][^o6] In 2004, he was awarded the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o7]
