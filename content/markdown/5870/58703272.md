# Wilf Malcolm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilf Malcolm"}

**Wilfred Gordon Malcolm** (29 November 1933 – 6 October 2018) was a New Zealand mathematician and university administrator. He was professor of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) at [Victoria University of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington) from the mid 1970s, until serving as vice-chancellor of the [University of Waikato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waikato) between 1985 and 1994.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Feilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feilding) on 29 November 1933, Malcolm was educated at [Feilding Agricultural High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feilding_Agricultural_High_School). He went on to study at [Wellington Teachers' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Teachers'_College) and [Victoria University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_College), graduating [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) with first-class honours in 1957.[^o5][^o6][^o7] He won a Shirtcliffe Fellowship, which enabled him to take parts II and III of the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos), specialising in algebra and topology, at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o6] While in England, Malcolm married Edmée Ruth Prebensen.[^o6]

Malcolm returned to Victoria, where he took up a lecturership in pure mathematics.[^o6] Between 1964 and 1966, he spent time away from the university, working as the general secretary of the [Inter-Varsity Fellowship of Evangelical Unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_Students_Christian_Fellowship).[^o6] However, he returned to lecturing at Victoria in 1967, and was promoted to senior lecturer the following year.[^o6] In 1972, he completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) thesis, titled *Ultraproducts and higher order models*, supervised by [George Hughes](wikipedia://en/18734752) and [Max Cresswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Cresswell) from the Department of Philosophy, and C.J. Seelye from the Mathematics Department.[^o6][^o8]

In 1985, Malcolm moved to the University of Waikato to take up the vice-chancellorship, serving in that role until 1994.[^o9]

Malcolm was awarded the [New Zealand 1990 Commemoration Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_1990_Commemoration_Medal) in 1990.[^o10] In the [1994 Queen's Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Birthday_Honours_%28New_Zealand%29), he was appointed a [Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire), for services to tertiary education. The following year, he was conferred with an honorary doctorate by the University of Waikato.[^o12] The Wilf Malcolm Institute of Educational Research at Waikato was named in his honour in 2002, in recognition of his contribution to education.[^o13]

Malcolm died in Auckland on 6 October 2018.[^o14]
