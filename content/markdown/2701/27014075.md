# Elliptical distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elliptical distribution"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), an **elliptical distribution** is any member of a broad family of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) that generalize the [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347). In the simplified two and three dimensional case, the joint distribution forms an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) and an [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381), respectively, in iso-density plots.

:::

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the normal distribution is used in *classical* [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis), while elliptical distributions are used in *generalized* multivariate analysis, for the study of symmetric distributions with tails that are [heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_tail), like the [multivariate t-distribution](wikipedia://en/7984768), or light (in comparison with the normal distribution). Some statistical methods that were originally motivated by the study of the normal distribution have good performance for general elliptical distributions (with finite variance), particularly for spherical distributions (which are defined below). Elliptical distributions are also used in [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691) to evaluate proposed multivariate-statistical procedures.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Elliptical distributions are defined in terms of the [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) of probability theory. A random vector $X$ on a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) has an *elliptical distribution* if its characteristic function $\phi$ satisfies the following [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377) (for every column-vector $t$)

$$\phi_{X-\mu}(t) = \psi(t' \Sigma t)$$

for some [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516) $\mu$, some [nonnegative-definite matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonnegative-definite_matrix) $\Sigma$ and some scalar function $\psi$.[^o4] The definition of elliptical distributions for *real* random-vectors has been extended to accommodate random vectors in Euclidean spaces over the [field](wikipedia://en/5826#Field_structure) of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), so facilitating applications in [time-series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-series_analysis).[^o5] Computational methods are available for generating [pseudo-random](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-random_number) vectors from elliptical distributions, for use in [Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/56098) [simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) for example.[^o6]

Some elliptical distributions are alternatively defined in terms of their [density functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_function). An elliptical distribution with a density function *f* has the form:

$$f(x)= k \cdot g((x-\mu)'\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu))$$

where $k$ is the [normalizing constant](wikipedia://en/398189), $x$ is an $n$-dimensional [random vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_vector) with [median vector](wikipedia://en/18837#Multivariate_median) $\mu$ (which is also the mean vector if the latter exists), and $\Sigma$ is a [positive definite matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_definite_matrix) which is proportional to the [covariance matrix](wikipedia://en/191752) if the latter exists.[^o7]

Examples include the following multivariate probability distributions:

- [Multivariate *t*-distribution](wikipedia://en/7984768)

- [Symmetric multivariate Laplace distribution](wikipedia://en/54150419)[^o11]
- [Multivariate logistic distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_logistic_distribution)[^o12]
- Multivariate symmetric general [hyperbolic distribution](wikipedia://en/6331574)[^o12]

## Properties {#Properties}

In the 2-dimensional case, if the density exists, each iso-density locus (the set of *x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub> pairs all giving a particular value of $f(x)$) is an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) or a union of ellipses (hence the name elliptical distribution). More generally, for arbitrary *n*, the iso-density loci are unions of [ellipsoids](wikipedia://en/145381). All these ellipsoids or ellipses have the common center μ and are scaled copies (homothets) of each other.

The [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) is the special case in which $g(z)=e^{-z/2}$. While the multivariate normal is unbounded (each element of $x$ can take on arbitrarily large positive or negative values with non-zero probability, because $e^{-z/2}>0$ for all non-negative $z$), in general elliptical distributions can be bounded or unbounded—such a distribution is bounded if $g(z)=0$ for all $z$ greater than some value.

There exist elliptical distributions that have undefined [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean), such as the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003) (even in the univariate case). Because the variable *x* enters the density function quadratically, all elliptical distributions are [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_distribution) about $\mu.$

If two subsets of a jointly elliptical random vector are [uncorrelated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncorrelated), then if their means exist they are [mean independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_independent) of each other (the mean of each subvector conditional on the value of the other subvector equals the unconditional mean).[^o15]

If random vector *X* is elliptically distributed, then so is *DX* for any matrix *D* with full [row rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_rank). Thus any linear combination of the components of *X* is elliptical (though not necessarily with the same elliptical distribution), and any subset of *X* is elliptical.[^o15]

## Applications {#Applications}

Elliptical distributions are used in statistics and in economics. They are also used to calculate the [landing footprints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_footprint) of spacecraft.

In mathematical economics, elliptical distributions have been used to describe [portfolios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_theory) in [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680).[^o18][^o19]

### Statistics: Generalized multivariate analysis {#Statistics:_Generalized_multivariate_analysis}

In statistics, the [multivariate *normal* distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) (of Gauss) is used in *classical* [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis), in which most methods for estimation and hypothesis-testing are motivated for the normal distribution. In contrast to classical multivariate analysis, *generalized* multivariate analysis refers to research on elliptical distributions without the restriction of normality.

For suitable elliptical distributions, some classical methods continue to have good properties.[^o21][^o22] Under finite-variance assumptions, an extension of [Cochran's theorem](wikipedia://en/574337) (on the distribution of quadratic forms) holds.[^o23]

#### Spherical distribution {#Spherical_distribution}

An elliptical distribution with a zero mean and variance in the form $\alpha I$ where $I$ is the identity-matrix is called a *spherical distribution*.[^o25] For spherical distributions, classical results on parameter-estimation and hypothesis-testing hold have been extended.[^o26][^o27] Similar results hold for [linear models](wikipedia://en/877698),[^o28] and indeed also for complicated models (especially for the [growth curve](wikipedia://en/44286896) model). The analysis of multivariate models uses [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946) (particularly [Kronecker products](wikipedia://en/712430) and [vectorization](wikipedia://en/7136985)) and [matrix calculus](wikipedia://en/1765852).[^o22][^o29][^o30]

#### Robust statistics: Asymptotics {#Robust_statistics:_Asymptotics}

Another use of elliptical distributions is in [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691), in which researchers examine how statistical procedures perform on the class of elliptical distributions, to gain insight into the procedures' performance on even more general problems,[^o32] for example by using the [limiting theory of statistics](wikipedia://en/26024794) ("asymptotics").[^o33]

### Economics and finance {#Economics_and_finance}

Elliptical distributions are important in [portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_theory) because, if the returns on all assets available for portfolio formation are jointly elliptically distributed, then all portfolios can be characterized completely by their location and scale – that is, any two portfolios with identical location and scale of portfolio return have identical distributions of portfolio return.[^o35][^o15] Various features of portfolio analysis, including [mutual fund separation theorems](wikipedia://en/29928965) and the [Capital Asset Pricing Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Asset_Pricing_Model), hold for all elliptical distributions.[^o15]
