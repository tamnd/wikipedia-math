# Developable roller

*Image: Sphericon.stl.* [STL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STL_%28file_format%29) model of a [sphericon](wikipedia://en/572813)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Developable roller"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **developable roller** is a [convex solid](wikipedia://en/1817885) whose [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) consists of a single [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122), [developable](wikipedia://en/2878111) face.[^o2][^o3] While [rolling](wikipedia://en/1156993) on a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), most developable rollers develop their entire surface so that all the points on the surface touch the rolling plane. All developable rollers have [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158). Four families of developable rollers have been described to date: the prime poly[sphericons](wikipedia://en/572813),[^o4] the [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634) of the two disc rollers (TDR convex hulls),[^o5] the [polycons](wikipedia://en/65261724) [^o6][^o2] and the Platonicons.[^o3][^o7]

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: comparison\_oloid\_sphericon\_3D.svg.* Comparison of an oloid (left) and sphericon (right) — in \[ the SVG image\], move over the image to rotate the shapes

Each developable roller family is based on a different construction principle. The **prime polysphericons** are a subfamily of the [polysphericon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polysphericon) family.[^o10] They are based on bodies made by [rotating](wikipedia://en/329549) [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) around one of their longest [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251). These bodies are cut in two at their symmetry plane and the two halves are reunited after being rotated at an offset angle relative to each other.[^o6] All prime polysphericons have two edges made of one or more circular arcs and four vertices. All of them, but the [sphericon](wikipedia://en/572813), have surfaces that consist of one kind of [conic](wikipedia://en/782427) surface and one, or more, conical or [cylindrical](wikipedia://en/859283) [frustum](wikipedia://en/89405) surfaces.[^o2] **Two-disc rollers** are made of two [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) symmetrical [circular](wikipedia://en/6220) or [elliptical](wikipedia://en/9277) [sectors](wikipedia://en/1967249). The sectors are joined to each other such that the planes in which they lie are perpendicular to each other, and their axes of symmetry coincide.[^o5] The convex hulls of these structures constitute the members of the TDR convex hull family. All members of this family have two edges (the two circular or elliptical [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29)). They may have either 4 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), as in the sphericon (which is a member of this family as well) or none, as in the [oloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oloid). Like the prime polysphericons the **polycons** are based on regular polygons but consist of identical pieces of only one type of cone with no frustum parts. The cone is created by rotating two adjacent edges of a regular polygon (and in most cases their extensions as well) around the polygon's axis of symmetry that passes through their common vertex. A polycon based on an *n*-gon (a polygon with n edges) has *n* edges and *n* + 2 vertices. The sphericon, which is a member of this family as well, has circular edges. The hexacon's edges are [parabolic](wikipedia://en/23231). All other polycons' edges are [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/14052).[^o2] Like the polycons, the **Platonicons** are made of only one type of conic surface. Their unique feature is that each one of them circumscribes one of the five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905). Unlike the other families, this family is not infinite. 14 Platonicons have been discovered to date.[^o3]

## Rolling motion {#Rolling_motion}

Unlike [axially symmetrical](wikipedia://en/6025673) bodies that, if unrestricted, can perform a [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_motion) [rolling](wikipedia://en/1156993) motion (like the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) or the cylinder) or a circular one ([like the cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_cone_motion)), developable rollers meander while rolling.[^o2] Their motion is linear only on average. In the case of the polycons and Platonicons, as well as some of the prime polysphericons, the path of their [center of mass](wikipedia://en/173961) consists of circular arcs. In the case of the prime polysphericons that have surfaces that contain cylindrical parts the path is a combination of circular arcs and straight lines. A general expression for the shape of the path of the TDR convex hulls center of mass has yet to be derived.[^o5] In order to maintain a smooth rolling motion the center of mass of a rolling body must maintain a constant height. All prime polysphericons, polycons, and platonicons and some of the TDR convex hulls share this property.[^o2][^o4] Some of the TDR convex hulls, like the oloid, do not possess this property. In order for a TDR convex hull to maintain constant height the following must hold:

$$\ c^2=4a^2-2b^2$$

Where a and b are the half minor and major axes of the elliptic arcs, respectively, and c is the distance between their centers.[^o5] For example, in the case where the skeletal structure of the convex hull TDR consists of two circular segments with radius **r**, for the center of mass to be kept at constant height, the distance between the sectors' centers should be equal to $\sqrt{2}$**r**.[^o12]
