# Ernst Mally

::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Mally"}

**Ernst Mally** (; ; 11 October 1879 – 8 March 1944) was an Austrian [analytic philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_philosopher),[^o5][^o3] initially affiliated with [Alexius Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong)'s [Graz School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_School) of [object theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object_theory). Mally was one of the founders of [deontic logic](wikipedia://en/1343980) and is mainly known for his contributions in that field of research. In [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), he is known for introducing a distinction between two kinds of [predication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29), better known as the [dual predication approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_predication_approach).[^o8]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Mally was born in the town of [Kranj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranj) (Krainburg) in the [Duchy of Carniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Carniola), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (now in [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia)). His father was of [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenes) origin, but identified himself with [Austrian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_German) culture (he also [Germanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanize) the orthography of his surname, originally spelled Mali, a common Slovene surname of [Upper Carniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Carniola)). After his death, the family moved to the [Carniolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carniola) capital of [Ljubljana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana) (Laibach). There, Ernst attended the prestigious [Ljubljana German-language Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poljane_Grammar_School). Already at a young age, Mally became a fervent supporter of the [pan-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-German) nationalist movement of [Georg von Schönerer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_von_Schönerer). In the same time, he developed an interest in philosophy.

In 1898, he enrolled in the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz), where he studied philosophy under the supervision of [Alexius Meinong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Meinong), as well as [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and mathematics, specializing in [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic). He received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1903 under the supervision of Meinong with the dissertation *Untersuchungen zur Gegenstandstheorie des Messens* (*Investigations in the Object Theory of Measurement*; published as a book in 1904). In 1906 he started teaching at a high school in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz), at the same time collaborating with Adalbert Meingast and working as Meinong's assistant at the university. He also maintained close contacts with the [Graz Psychological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_Psychological_Institute), founded by Meinong. In 1912, he published his [habilitation thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation_thesis) entitled *Gegenstandstheoretische Grundlagen der Logik und Logistik* (*Object-theoretic Foundations for Logics and Logistics*) at Graz (with Meinong as supervisor) and earned his habilitation in 1913.

From 1915 to 1918 he served as an officer in the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army). After the end of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), Mally joined the [Greater German People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_German_People's_Party) (GDVP), which called the unification of [German Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Austria) with Germany. In the same period, he started teaching at the university and in 1925 he took over Meinong's chair. In 1938, he became a member of the [National Socialist Teachers League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Teachers_League) and two months after the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)* he joined the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party). He continued teaching during the [Nazi administration of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_in_the_time_of_National_Socialism) until 1942 when he retired.

He died in 1944 in [Schwanberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwanberg,_Styria).

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

### Mally's deontic logic {#Mally's_deontic_logic}

Mally was the first logician ever to attempt an [axiomatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatization) of [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) (Mally 1926). He used five [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms), which are given below. They form a [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory) that quantifies over [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094), and there are several predicates to understand first. !x means that x ought to be the case. Ux means that x is unconditionally obligatory, i.e. that !x is [necessarily true](wikipedia://en/3986852). ∩x means that x is unconditionally forbidden, i.e. U(¬x). A f B is the [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) A requires B, i.e. A materially implies !B. (All [entailment](wikipedia://en/37531624) in the axioms is [material conditional](wikipedia://en/658808).) It is defined by axiom III, whereas all other terms are defined as a preliminary.

$$\begin{array}{rl} \mbox{I.} & ((A\; \operatorname{f}\; B) \And (B \to C)) \to (A\; \operatorname{f}\; C) \\ \mbox{II.} & ((A\; \operatorname{f}\; B) \And (A\;\operatorname{f}\;C)) \to (A\; \operatorname{f}\; (B \And C)) \\ \mbox{III.} & (A\; \operatorname{f}\; B) \leftrightarrow\; !(A \to B) \\ \mbox{IV.} & \exists U\; !U \\ \mbox{V.} & \neg (U\; \operatorname{f}\; \cap) \end{array}$$

Note the implied [universal quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_quantifier) in the above axioms.

The fourth axiom has confused some logicians because its formulation is not as they would have expected, since Mally gave each axiom a description in words also, and he said that axiom IV meant "the unconditionally obligatory is obligatory", i.e. (as many logicians have insisted) UA → !A. Meanwhile, axiom 5 lacks an object to which the predicates apply, a [typo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typo). However, it turns out these are the least of Mally's worries (see below).

### Failure of Mally's deontic logic {#Failure_of_Mally's_deontic_logic}

Theorem: This axiomatization of [deontic logic](wikipedia://en/1343980) implies that !x [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) x is true, OR !x is unsatisfiable. (This makes it useless to deontic logicians.)

Proof: Using axiom III, axiom I may be rewritten as (!(A → B) & (B → C)) → !(A → C). Since B → C holds whenever C holds, one immediate consequence is that (!(A → B) → (C → !(A → C))). In other words, if A requires B, it requires any true statement. In the special case where A is a [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335), the theorem has consequence (!B → (C → !C)). Thus, if at least one statement ought be true, every statement must [materially entail](wikipedia://en/658808) it ought be true, and so every true statement ought be true. As for the converse (i.e. if some statement ought be true then all statements that ought be true are true), consider the following logic: ((U → !A) & (A → ∩)) → (U → !∩) is a special case of axiom I, but its [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778) contradicts axiom V, and so ¬((U → !A) & (A → ∩)). The result !A → A can be shown to follow from this, since !A implies that U → !A and ¬A implies that A → ∩; and, since these are not both true, we know that !A → A.

Mally thought that axiom I was self-evident, but he likely confused it with an alternative in which the implication B → C is *logical*, which would indeed make the axiom self-evident. The theorem above, however, would then not be demonstrable. The theorem was proven by [Karl Menger](wikipedia://en/149431), the next deontic logician. Neither Mally's original axioms nor a modification that avoids this result remains popular today. Menger did not suggest his own axioms.

### Metaphysics {#Metaphysics}

In [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), Mally is known for introducing a distinction between two kinds of [predication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29), a strategy better known as the [dual predication approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_predication_approach), for solving the problem of [nonexistent objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonexistent_object) (Mally 1912).[^o8] He also introduced a similar strategy, the [dual property strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_property_strategy), but did not endorse it.[^o5] The dual property strategy was eventually adopted by Meinong.[^o5]

Mally developed a [realistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism) approach to [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) (Mally 1935) and saw himself in opposition to the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle) and the [logical positivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivists).[^o2]

### Legacy {#Legacy}

Mally's metaphysical work influences some contemporary metaphysicians and logicians working in [abstract object theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object_theory), especially [Edward Zalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Zalta).[^o16]

The analytic philosopher [David Kellogg Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kellogg_Lewis) argued forcefully that the name of the fictional Australian poet [Ern Malley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ern_Malley), created by [James McAuley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_McAuley) and [Harold Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Stewart), was an allusion to Mally.[^o17]
