# Graetz number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graetz number"}

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), the **Graetz number** (**Gz**) is a [dimensionless number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_number) that characterizes [laminar flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminar_flow) in a conduit. The number is defined as:[^o2]

:::

$$\mathrm{Gz} = {D_H \over L} \mathrm{Re}\, \mathrm{Pr}$$

where

*D<sub>H</sub>* is the diameter in round tubes or [hydraulic diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_diameter) in arbitrary cross-section ducts

*L* is the length

Re is the [Reynolds number](wikipedia://en/23868856) and

Pr is the [Prandtl number](wikipedia://en/58283).

This number is useful in determining the thermally developing flow entrance length in ducts. A Graetz number of approximately 1000 or less is the point at which flow would be considered thermally fully developed.[^o3]

When used in connection with mass transfer the Prandtl number is replaced by the [Schmidt number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt_number), Sc, which expresses the ratio of the momentum diffusivity to the mass diffusivity.

$$\mathrm{Gz} = {D_H \over L} \mathrm{Re}\, \mathrm{Sc}$$

The quantity is named after the physicist [Leo Graetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Graetz).
