# Elliott–Halberstam conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elliott–Halberstam conjecture"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Elliott–Halberstam conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) about the distribution of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) in [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389). It has many applications in [sieve theory](wikipedia://en/1035915). It is named for [Peter D. T. A. Elliott](wikipedia://en/36492978) and [Heini Halberstam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heini_Halberstam), who stated a specific version of the conjecture in 1968.[^o2]

:::

One version of the conjecture is as follows. Let $\pi(x)$, the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826), denote the number of primes less than or equal to $x$. If $q$ is a [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_and_positive_numbers) [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) and $a$ is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to $q$, we let $\pi(x;q,a)$ denote the number of primes less than or equal to $x$ which are equal to $a$ modulo $q$. [Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet's_theorem_on_primes_in_arithmetic_progressions) then tells us that

$$\pi(x;q,a) \sim \frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\ \ (x\rightarrow\infty)$$

where $\varphi$ is [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452). If we then define the error function

$$E(x;q) = \max_{\text{gcd}(a,q) = 1} \left|\pi(x;q,a) - \frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right|$$

where the max is taken over all $a$ coprime to $q$, then the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture is the assertion that for every $\theta < 1$ and $A > 0$ there exists a constant $C > 0$ such that

$$\sum_{1 \leq q \leq x^\theta} E(x;q) \leq \frac{C x}{\log^A x}$$

for all $x > 2$.

This conjecture was proven for all $\theta < 1/2$ by [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475)[^o3] and [A. I. Vinogradov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._I._Vinogradov)[^o4] (the [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](wikipedia://en/4632596), sometimes known simply as "Bombieri's theorem"); this result is already quite useful, being an averaged form of the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567). It is known that the conjecture fails at the endpoint $\theta = 1$.[^o5] In 1986, Bombieri, Friedlander and Iwaniec generalized the Elliott-Halberstam conjecture, using [Dirichlet convolution](wikipedia://en/179017) of arithmetic functions related to the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862).[^o6]

The Elliott–Halberstam conjecture has several consequences. A striking one is the result announced by [Dan Goldston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Goldston), [János Pintz](wikipedia://en/22191053), and [Cem Yıldırım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_Yıldırım),[^o7][^o8] which shows (assuming this conjecture) that there are infinitely many pairs of primes which differ by at most 16. In November 2013, [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102) showed that subject to the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, one can show the existence of infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes that differ by at most 12.[^o9] In August 2014, [Polymath](wikipedia://en/34858344) group showed that subject to the generalized Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, one can show the existence of infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes that differ by at most 6.[^o10] Without assuming any form of the conjecture, the lowest proven bound is 240.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Original conjecture"}

## Original conjecture {#Original_conjecture}

The original Elliott-Halberstam conjecture is not clearly stated in their paper,[^o2] but can be inferred there from (1) page 59 and the comment above the Theorem on page 62. It says that

$$\sum_{q\le x}\varphi(q)\max_{(h,q)=1} \left(\pi(x,q,h)-\frac{\operatorname{li}x}{\varphi(q)}\right)^2\ll\frac{x^2}{\log^Ax}$$

provided $X<x(\log x)^{-A-1}$, where $\operatorname{li} x$ denotes the [logarithmic integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_integral) and $\varphi$ denotes [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452).
