# Morihiko Saito

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morihiko Saito"}

**Morihiko Saitō** (斎藤 盛彦, *Saitō Morihiko*, born 1961) is a Japanese mathematician, specializing in [algebraic analysis](wikipedia://en/20846707) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After graduating from Aiko High School in [Matsuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsuyama), Saito completed undergraduate study in mathematics at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) and in 1979 completed the master's program there. In 1986 he received his D.Sc. from [Kyoto University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University). After working as a research assistant at Kyoto University's [Research Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1978962), he was appointed there an associate professor.

In 1988/1990 he introduced the theory of [mixed Hodge modules](wikipedia://en/10389277), based on the theory of [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755) in algebraic analysis, the theory of [perverse sheaves](wikipedia://en/1714012), and the theory of [variation of Hodge structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variation_of_Hodge_structures) and [mixed Hodge structures](wikipedia://en/64743403) (introduced by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544)) in algebraic geometry. This led, among other things, to a generalization of the fundamental decomposition theorems of [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), Deligne, and [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber) about perverse sheaves in positive [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) to characteristic 0.[^o3] The theory of Hodge D-modules forms the starting point for the theory of the twistor D-modules developed by [Claude Sabbah](wikipedia://en/41608298) and [Takurō Mochizuki](wikipedia://en/47405836), which lead to led to another generalization of the Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber theorem by Mochizuki.

In 2006 Saito, with Nero Budur and [Mircea Mustață](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Mustață), generalized the notion of a [Bernstein–Sato polynomial](wikipedia://en/1636721) (*aka* b-function or b-polynomial) to an arbitrary variety.[^o4]

Saito's research deals with "applications of the theory of mixed Hodge modules to algebraic geometry, including the theories of singularities, algebraic cycles, characteristic classes, and so on."[^o5]

In 1990 he was an Invited Speaker with talk *Mixed Hodge Modules and Applications* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto). In 1991 he was awarded the Spring Prize of the [Mathematical Society of Japan](wikipedia://en/18376786).[^o6]
