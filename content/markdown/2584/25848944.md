# Transformation between distributions in time–frequency analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transformation between distributions in time–frequency analysis"}

In the field of [time–frequency analysis](wikipedia://en/857897), several signal formulations are used to represent the signal in a joint time–frequency domain.[^o2]

:::

There are several methods and transforms called "time-frequency distributions" (TFDs), whose interconnections were organized by Leon Cohen.[^o3] [^o4][^o5][^o6] The most useful and popular methods form a class referred to as "quadratic" or [bilinear time–frequency distributions](wikipedia://en/15249674). A core member of this class is the [Wigner–Ville distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner–Ville_distribution) (WVD), as all other TFDs can be written as a smoothed or convolved versions of the WVD. Another popular member of this class is the [spectrogram](wikipedia://en/263317) which is the square of the magnitude of the [short-time Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/436912) (STFT). The spectrogram has the advantage of being positive and is easy to interpret, but also has disadvantages, like being irreversible, which means that once the spectrogram of a signal is computed, the original signal can't be extracted from the spectrogram. The theory and methodology for defining a TFD that verifies certain desirable properties is given in the "Theory of Quadratic TFDs".[^o7]

The scope of this article is to illustrate some elements of the procedure to transform one distribution into another. The method used to transform a distribution is borrowed from the [phase space formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_space_formulation) of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), even though the subject matter of this article is "[signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324)". Noting that a signal can be recovered from a particular distribution under certain conditions, given a certain TFD *ρ*<sub>1</sub>(*t*,*f*) representing the signal in a joint time–frequency domain, another, different, TFD *ρ*<sub>2</sub>(*t*,*f*) of the same signal can be obtained to calculate any other distribution, by simple smoothing or filtering; some of these relationships are shown below. A full treatment of the question can be given in Cohen's book.

## General class {#General_class}

If we use the variable $\omega = 2\pi f$, then, borrowing the notations used in the field of quantum mechanics, we can show that [time–frequency representation](wikipedia://en/857710), such as [Wigner distribution function](wikipedia://en/4110803) (WDF) and other [bilinear time–frequency distributions](wikipedia://en/15249674), can be expressed as

::: {#o9 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$C(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iiint s^*\left(u-\dfrac{1}{2}\tau\right) s\left(u+\dfrac{1}{2}\tau\right)\phi(\theta,\tau)e^{-j\theta t-j\tau\omega+j\theta u}\, du\,d\tau\,d\theta , \tag{1}$$

:::

where $\phi(\theta,\tau)$ is a two dimensional function called the kernel, which determines the distribution and its properties (for a signal processing terminology and treatment of this question, the reader is referred to the references already cited in the introduction).

The kernel of the [Wigner distribution function](wikipedia://en/4110803) (WDF) is one. However, no particular significance should be attached to that, since it is possible to write the general form so that the kernel of any distribution is one, in which case the kernel of the [Wigner distribution function](wikipedia://en/4110803) (WDF) would be something else.

## Characteristic function formulation {#Characteristic_function_formulation}

The characteristic function is the double [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the distribution. By inspection of Eq. ((1)), we can obtain that

::: {#o11 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$C(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iint M(\theta,\tau)e^{-j\theta t-j\tau\omega}\, d\theta\,d\tau \tag{2}$$

:::

where

::: {#o12 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$\begin{alignedat}{2} M(\theta,\tau) & = \phi(\theta,\tau)\int s^*\left(u-\dfrac{1}{2}\tau\right)s\left(u+\dfrac{1}{2}\tau\right)e^{j\theta u}\,du \\ & = \phi(\theta,\tau)A(\theta,\tau) \\ \end{alignedat} \tag{3}$$

:::

and where $A(\theta,\tau)$ is the symmetrical [ambiguity function](wikipedia://en/1135333). The characteristic function may be appropriately called the generalized ambiguity function.

## Transformation between distributions {#Transformation_between_distributions}

To obtain that relationship suppose that there are two distributions, $C_1$ and $C_2$, with corresponding kernels, $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$. Their characteristic functions are

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$M_1(\phi,\tau) = \phi_1(\theta,\tau)\int s^*\left(u-\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)s\left(u+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)e^{j\theta u}\, du \tag{4}$$

:::

::: {#o15 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$M_2(\phi,\tau) = \phi_2(\theta,\tau)\int s^*\left(u-\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)s\left(u+\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)e^{j\theta u}\, du \tag{5}$$

:::

Divide one equation by the other to obtain

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="6"}

$$M_1(\phi,\tau) = \dfrac{\phi_1(\theta,\tau)}{\phi_2(\theta,\tau)}M_2(\phi,\tau) \tag{6}$$

:::

This is an important relationship because it connects the characteristic functions. For the division to be proper the kernel cannot to be zero in a finite region.

To obtain the relationship between the distributions take the double [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of both sides and use Eq. ((2))

::: {#o17 .equation cue=numblk name="7"}

$$C_1(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iint \dfrac{\phi_1(\theta,\tau)}{\phi_2(\theta,\tau)}M_2(\theta,\tau)e^{-j\theta t-j\tau\omega}\, d\theta\,d\tau \tag{7}$$

:::

Now express $M_2$ in terms of $C_2$ to obtain

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="8"}

$$C_1(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iiiint \dfrac{\phi_1(\theta,\tau)}{\phi_2(\theta,\tau)}C_2(t,\omega')e^{j\theta(t'-t)+j\tau(\omega'-\omega)}\, d\theta\,d\tau\,dt'\,d\omega' \tag{8}$$

:::

This relationship can be written as

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="9"}

$$C_1(t,\omega) = \iint g_{12}(t'-t,\omega'-\omega)C_2(t,\omega')\,dt'\,d\omega' \tag{9}$$

:::

with

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="10"}

$$g_{12}(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iint \dfrac{\phi_1(\theta,\tau)}{\phi_2(\theta,\tau)}e^{j\theta t+j\tau\omega}\, d\theta\, d\tau \tag{10}$$

:::

## Relation of the spectrogram to other bilinear representations {#Relation_of_the_spectrogram_to_other_bilinear_representations}

Now we specialize to the case where one transform from an arbitrary representation to the spectrogram. In Eq. ((9)), both $C_1$ to be the spectrogram and $C_2$ to be arbitrary are set. In addition, to simplify notation, $\phi_{SP} = \phi_1, \phi = \phi_2$, and $g_{SP} = g_{12}$ are set and written as

::: {#o22 .equation cue=numblk name="11"}

$$C_{SP}(t,\omega) = \iint g_{SP} \left (t'-t,\omega'-\omega \right)C \left (t,\omega' \right )\,dt'\,d\omega' \tag{11}$$

:::

The kernel for the spectrogram with window, $h(t)$, is $A_h(-\theta,\tau)$ and therefore

$$\begin{aligned} g_{SP}(t,\omega) & = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iint \dfrac{A_h(-\theta,\tau)}{\phi(\theta,\tau)}e^{j\theta t+j\tau\omega}\, d\theta\,d\tau \\ & = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iiint \dfrac{1}{\phi(\theta,\tau)}h^*(u-\tfrac{\tau}{2})h(u+\tfrac{\tau}{2})e^{j\theta t+j\tau\omega-j\theta u}\, du\,d\tau\,d\theta \\ & = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iiint h^*(u-\tfrac{\tau}{2})h(u+\tfrac{\tau}{2})\dfrac{\phi(\theta,\tau)}{\phi(\theta,\tau)\phi(-\theta,\tau)}e^{-j\theta t+j\tau\omega+j\theta u}\, du\,d\tau\,d\theta \\ \end{aligned}$$

If we only consider kernels for which $\phi(-\theta,\tau)\phi(\theta,\tau) = 1$ holds then

$$g_{SP}(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iiint h^*(u-\tfrac{\tau}{2})h(u+\tfrac{\tau}{2}) \phi(\theta,\tau) e^{-j\theta t+j\tau\omega+j\theta u}\, du\,d\tau\,d\theta = C_h(t,-\omega)$$

and therefore

$$C_{SP}(t,\omega) = \iint C_s(t',\omega')C_h(t'-t,\omega'-\omega)\,dt'\,d\omega'$$

This was shown by Janssen.[^o5] When $\phi(-\theta,\tau)\phi(\theta,\tau)$ does not equal one, then

$$C_{SP}(t,\omega) = \iiiint G(t'',\omega'')C_s(t',\omega')C_h(t''+t'-t,-\omega''+\omega-\omega')\,dt'\,dt''\,d\omega'\,d\omega''$$

where

$$G(t,\omega) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi^2}\iint \dfrac{e^{-j\theta t-j\tau\omega}}{\phi(\theta,\tau)\phi(-\theta,\tau)}\, d\theta\,d\tau$$
