# Robert Forsyth Scott

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Forsyth Scott"}

*Image: Robert Forsyth Scott.png.*

**Sir Robert Forsyth Scott** (28 July 1849 – 18 November 1933) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister) and Master of [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge)

:::

## Life {#Life}

Scott was born in [Leith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith), near [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), the eldest son of Reverend George Scott, a Minister in the church at [Dairsie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairsie) and Mary Forsyth, daughter of the Edinburgh advocate Robert Forsyth.[^o3]

Scott was educated at the High School, Edinburgh, then in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) before becoming a student at [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London). In 1870, while a student at University College, London, he was awarded a Whitworth Exhibition. He went on to read mathematics at St John's College, where he was fourth [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in the [Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos) in 1875 and was elected to a fellowship in 1877.[^o5]

After publishing *The Theory of Determinants and Their Applications* in 1880, Scott turned his attention to the law, become a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister) in 1883, and to institutional history, including histories of St. John's College, Cambridge, published between 1882 and 1907.[^o3][^o5] In 1908 he was appointed as the Master of St John's College, a position he held until his death in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge) in 1933, and from 1910 to 1912 he served as [Vice-chancellor of the University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Vice-Chancellors_of_the_University_of_Cambridge). On his death he left the library of St John's one of the largest collection of [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) manuscripts in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe).[^o5]

He was the elder brother of Sir [James George Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_George_Scott).
