# Blake canonical form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blake canonical form"}

*Image: Karnaugh map KV 4mal4 Gruppe01a.svg.* [Karnaugh map](wikipedia://en/10854684) of green + red + blue, a sum of all prime implicants (each rendered in a different color). Deleting blue results in a minimal sum.

In [Boolean logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic), a [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28mathematical_logic%29) for a Boolean function *f* is in **Blake canonical form** (**BCF**),[^o8] also called the **complete sum of prime implicants**,[^o9] the **complete sum**,[^o10] or the **disjunctive prime form**,[^o11] when it is a [disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962) of all the [prime implicants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_implicant) of *f*.[^o8]

:::

## Relation to other forms {#Relation_to_other_forms}

The Blake canonical form is a special case of [disjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73341).

The Blake canonical form is not necessarily [minimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_minimization) (upper diagram), however all the terms of a minimal sum are contained in the Blake canonical form.[^o10] On the other hand, the Blake canonical form is a [canonical form](wikipedia://en/515096), that is, it is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) reordering, whereas there can be multiple minimal forms (lower diagram). Selecting a minimal sum from a Blake canonical form amounts in general to solving the [set cover problem](wikipedia://en/870399),[^o12] so is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard).[^o13][^o14]

## History {#History}

[Archie Blake](wikipedia://en/69419362) presented his canonical form at a meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1932,[^o15] and in his 1937 dissertation. He called it the "simplified canonical form";[^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19] it was named the "Blake canonical form" by Frank Markham Brown and Sergiu Rudeanu in 1986–1990.[^o24][^o8] Together with [Platon Poretsky](wikipedia://en/3033920)'s work[^o25] it is also referred to as "Blake–Poretsky laws".[^o26]

## Methods for calculation {#Methods_for_calculation}

Blake discussed three methods for calculating the canonical form: exhaustion of implicants, iterated [consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_%28boolean_algebra%29), and multiplication. The iterated consensus method was rediscovered[^o8] by Edward W. Samson and Burton E. Mills,[^o20] [Willard Quine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_van_Orman_Quine),[^o21] and Kurt Bing.[^o22][^o23] In 2022, Milan Mossé, Harry Sha, and Li-Yang Tan discovered a near-optimal algorithm for computing the Blake canonical form of a formula in [conjunctive normal form](wikipedia://en/73342).[^o5]
