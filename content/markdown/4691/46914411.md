::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="List of mathematical artists"}

This is a list of artists who actively explored [mathematics in their artworks](wikipedia://en/22468661).[^o12] [Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art) forms practised by these artists include [painting](wikipedia://en/22468661), [sculpture](wikipedia://en/47304362), [architecture](wikipedia://en/279293), [textiles](wikipedia://en/13410380) and [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami).

:::

Some artists such as [Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_della_Francesca) and [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473) went so far as to write books on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in art. Della Francesca wrote books on [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960) and the emerging field of [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405), including *De Prospectiva Pingendi (On Perspective for Painting)*, *Trattato d’Abaco (Abacus Treatise)*, and *De corporibus regularibus (Regular Solids)*,[^o5][^o6][^o7] while Pacioli wrote *[De divina proportione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_divina_proportione) (On Divine Proportion)*, with illustrations by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci), at the end of the fifteenth century.[^o8]

Merely making accepted use of some aspect of mathematics such as [perspective](wikipedia://en/14909067) does not qualify an artist for admission to this list.

The term "fine art" is used conventionally to cover the output of artists who produce a combination of paintings, drawings and sculptures.
