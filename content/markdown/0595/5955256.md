# Bourbaki dangerous bend symbol

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bourbaki dangerous bend symbol"}

*Image: Borne Michelin Virages.JPG.* French "virages dangereux" road sign, before 1949

The **dangerous bend** or **caution** symbol **☡** (2621) was created by the [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) group of mathematicians and appears in the margins of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) books written by the group. It resembles a [road sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_sign) that indicates a "dangerous bend" in the road ahead, and is used to mark passages tricky on a first reading or with an especially difficult argument.[^o2]

:::

Certains passages sont destinés à prémunir le lecteur contre des erreurs graves, où il risquerait de tomber; ces passages sont signalés en marge par le signe ☡ (« tournant dangereux »)

Some passages are designed to forewarn the reader against serious errors, where he risks falling; these passages are indicated in the margin with the sign ☡ ("dangerous bend")

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

Others have used variations of the symbol in their books. The computer scientist [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) introduced an American-style road sign depiction in his [Metafont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metafont) and [TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX) systems, with a pair of adjacent signs indicating doubly dangerous passages.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9]

:::

## Typography {#Typography}

*Image: Knuth's dangerous bend symbol.svg.* Knuth's "Dangerous Bend" sign, with yellow color added.

In the [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) typesetting system, Knuth's dangerous bend symbol can be produced by first loading the font manfnt (a font with extra symbols used in Knuth's TeX manual) with `\usepackage{manfnt}` and then typing `\dbend`.

There are several variations given by `\lhdbend`, `\reversedvideodbend`, `\textdbend`, `\textlhdbend`, and `\textreversedvideodbend`.
