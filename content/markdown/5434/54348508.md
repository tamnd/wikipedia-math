# Gene Grabeel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gene Grabeel"}

**Gene Grabeel** (June 5, 1920 – January 30, 2015) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [cryptanalyst](wikipedia://en/5715) who founded the [Venona project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venona_project).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Grabeel was born on June 5, 1920, in [Rose Hill, Lee County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Hill,_Lee_County,_Virginia), where she grew up; her mother raised chickens and her father farmed tobacco.[^o7] She graduated from [Mars Hill College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Hill_College) and [Farmville State Teachers College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmville_State_Teachers_College) and initially worked as a high school [home economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_economics) teacher in [Madison Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Heights,_Virginia) to teenage girls.[^o7][^o8][^o9]

## Career {#Career}

On the recommendation of a friend of hers, [Frank Rowlett](wikipedia://en/477018), Grabeel joined the [Signal Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Intelligence_Service) on December 28, 1942, where she was assigned to attack intercepted Soviet ciphertext.[^o7][^o9] Her father gave her his permission to do so and "push some papers around" during the next few months.[^o9] Thus Grabeel began her 36-year career with the [Signal Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Intelligence_Service).

On February 1, 1943, she founded the [Venona project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venona_project), a counterintelligence program aimed at decrypting Soviet communications.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14] She and others spent months sifting through stored and incoming Soviet telegrams.[^o15] Grabeel initially worked with [Leonard Zubko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Zubko), a Russian speaker, but he was soon replaced.[^o7]

In his book *Code Warriors: NSA's Codebreakers and the Secret Intelligence War Against the Soviet Union*, [Stephen Budiansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Budiansky) describes how she came into the opportunity to work as a U.S. government cryptanalyst: Grabeel retired from service in 1978, as the [Venona project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venona_project) came to a close.[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Grabeel attended Blackstone Baptist Church. She was a member of the [Daughters of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_the_American_Revolution) and of the 17th Century Colonial Dames.[^o8] Later in life, she was a fan of [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) basketball.[^o7] She dated men, but was not interested in marriage, similarly to her colleagues.[^o7]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Grabeel died at age 94 on January 30, 2015, in [Blackstone, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone,_Virginia). [^o8] After the 1995 [declassification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information_in_the_United_States#Declassification) of the Venona project, Grabeel was recognized by the [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency) as an "American Hero". [^o8] She was memorialized with a historical highway marker along with [Frank Rowlett](wikipedia://en/477018) in Virginia.[^o18]
