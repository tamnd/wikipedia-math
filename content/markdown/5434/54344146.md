# Gail Wolkowicz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gail Wolkowicz"}

**Gail Susan Kohl Wolkowicz** is a Canadian researcher in [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), and [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology) who works as a professor of mathematics and statistics at [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University). She is known, among other contributions, for her proof that the [competitive exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/451120) holds for inter-species competition in the [chemostat](wikipedia://en/4796040).

:::

After earning bachelor's and master's degrees at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), Wolkowicz completed her doctorate in 1984 at the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), under the supervision of Geoffrey J. Butler. Her dissertation was entitled "An Analysis of Mathematical Models Related to the Chemostat." After postdoctoral studies at [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), she joined the McMaster faculty in 1986. One of her PhD students was [Stacey Smith?](wikipedia://en/77279852).

Wolkowicz won the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) in 2014. One of her papers, "Mathematical model of anaerobic digestion in a chemostat: effects of syntrophy and inhibition" (with Marion Weedermann and Gunog Seo) won the biennial Lord Robert May Best Paper Prize of the *Journal of Biological Dynamics*, in which it was published.
