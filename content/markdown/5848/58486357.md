# Soft configuration model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Soft configuration model"}

In applied mathematics, the **soft configuration model (SCM)** is a [random graph](wikipedia://en/358477) model subject to the [principle of maximum entropy](wikipedia://en/201718) under constraints on the [expectation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectation_value) of the [degree sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_sequence) of sampled [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806).[^o2] Whereas the [configuration model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Configuration_model) (CM) uniformly samples random graphs of a specific degree sequence, the SCM only retains the specified degree sequence on average over all network realizations; in this sense the SCM has very relaxed constraints relative to those of the CM ("soft" rather than "sharp" constraints[^o3]). The SCM for graphs of size $n$ has a nonzero probability of sampling any graph of size $n$, whereas the CM is restricted to only graphs having precisely the prescribed connectivity structure.

:::

## Model formulation {#Model_formulation}

The SCM is a [statistical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_ensemble) of random graphs $G$ having $n$ vertices ($n=|V(G)|$) labeled $\{v_j\}_{j=1}^n=V(G)$, producing a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on $\mathcal{G}_n$ (the set of graphs of size $n$). Imposed on the ensemble are $n$ constraints, namely that the [ensemble average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_average) of the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) $k_j$ of vertex $v_j$ is equal to a designated value $\widehat{k}_j$, for all $v_j\in V(G)$. The model is fully [parametrized](wikipedia://en/453198) by its size $n$ and expected degree sequence $\{\widehat{k}_j\}_{j=1}^n$. These constraints are both local (one constraint associated with each vertex) and soft (constraints on the ensemble average of certain observable quantities), and thus yields a [canonical ensemble](wikipedia://en/1137672) with an [extensive](wikipedia://en/204464) number of constraints.[^o3] The conditions $\langle k_j \rangle = \widehat{k}_j$ are imposed on the ensemble by the [method of Lagrange multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_Lagrange_multipliers) (see [Maximum-entropy random graph model](wikipedia://en/58472531)).

## Derivation of the probability distribution {#Derivation_of_the_probability_distribution}

The probability $\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)$ of the SCM producing a graph $G$ is determined by maximizing the [Gibbs entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_entropy) $S[G]$ subject to constraints $\langle k_j \rangle = \widehat{k}_j, \ j=1,\ldots,n$ and normalization $\sum_{G\in \mathcal{G}_n}\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)=1$. This amounts to [optimizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimizing) the multi-constraint [Lagrange function](wikipedia://en/159974#Multiple_constraints) below:

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathcal{L}\left(\alpha,\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^n\right) \\[6pt] = {} & -\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_n}\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)\log\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G) + \alpha\left(1-\sum_{G\in \mathcal{G}_n}\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G) \right)+\sum_{j=1}^n\psi_j\left(\widehat{k}_j-\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_n}\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)k_j(G)\right), \end{aligned}$$

where $\alpha$ and $\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^n$ are the $n+1$ multipliers to be fixed by the $n+1$ constraints (normalization and the expected degree sequence). Setting to zero the derivative of the above with respect to $\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)$ for an arbitrary $G\in \mathcal{G}_n$ yields

$$0 = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}\left(\alpha,\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^n\right)}{\partial \mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)}= -\log \mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G) -1-\alpha-\sum_{j=1}^n\psi_j k_j(G) \ \Rightarrow \ \mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left[-\sum_{j=1}^n\psi_jk_j(G)\right],$$

the constant $Z:=e^{\alpha+1}=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_n}\exp\left[-\sum_{j=1}^n\psi_jk_j(G)\right]=\prod_{1\le i < j \le n}\left(1+e^{-(\psi_i+\psi_j)}\right)$[^o6] being the [partition function](wikipedia://en/16846849) normalizing the distribution; the above exponential expression applies to all $G\in\mathcal{G}_n$, and thus is the probability distribution. Hence we have an [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174) parameterized by $\{\psi_j\}_{j=1}^n$, which are related to the expected degree sequence $\{\widehat{k}_j\}_{j=1}^n$ by the following equivalent expressions:

$$\langle k_q \rangle = \sum_{G\in \mathcal{G}_n}k_q(G)\mathbb{P}_\text{SCM}(G) = -\frac{\partial \log Z}{\partial \psi_q} =\sum_{j\ne q}\frac{1}{e^{\psi_q+\psi_j}+1} = \widehat{k}_q, \ q=1,\ldots,n.$$
