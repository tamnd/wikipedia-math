# Differential coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential coefficient"}

In physics and mathematics, the **differential coefficient** of a function *f*(*x*) is what is now called its [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) *df*(*x*)/*dx*, the (not necessarily constant) multiplicative factor or *[coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203)* of the [differential](wikipedia://en/677191) *dx* in the differential *df*(*x*).[^o2][^o3]

:::

A *coefficient* is usually a [constant](wikipedia://en/24758132) quantity, but the *differential coefficient* of *f* is a *constant function* only if *f* is a [linear function](wikipedia://en/152111). When *f* is *not* linear, its differential coefficient is a function, call it *f*, *derived* by the [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921#Differentiation_and_the_derivative) of *f*, hence, the modern term, derivative.

The older usage is now rarely seen.

Early editions of [Silvanus P. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvanus_P._Thompson)'s *[Calculus Made Easy](wikipedia://en/14020714)* use the older term.[^o4] In his 1998 update of this text, [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) lets the first use of "differential coefficient" stand, along with Thompson's criticism of the term as a needlessly obscure phrase that should not intimidate students, and substitutes "derivative" for the remainder of the book.
