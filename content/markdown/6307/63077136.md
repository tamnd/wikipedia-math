# Cartier isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartier isomorphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Cartier isomorphism** is a certain [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between the [cohomology sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_of_sheaves) of the [de Rham complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_de_Rham_cohomology) of a [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [positive characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_characteristic), and the sheaves of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on the [Frobenius twist](wikipedia://en/1088270#Frobenius_for_schemes) of the variety. It is named after [Pierre Cartier](wikipedia://en/1470549). Intuitively, it shows that de Rham cohomology in positive characteristic is a much larger object than one might expect. It plays an important role in the approach of [Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and [Illusie](wikipedia://en/27206965) to the degeneration of the [Hodge–de Rham spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/43662331).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *k* be a field of characteristic *p* > 0, and let $f: X \to S$ be a morphism of [*k*-schemes](wikipedia://en/364754). Let $X^{(p)} = X \times_{S,\varphi} S$ denote the Frobenius twist and let $F: X \to X^{(p)}$ be the [relative Frobenius](wikipedia://en/1088270#Relative_Frobenius). The **Cartier map** is defined to be the unique morphism

$$C^{-1}: \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} \Omega^i_{X^{(p)}/S} \to \bigoplus_{i \geq 0} \mathcal{H}^i(F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})$$

of graded $\mathcal{O}_{X^{(p)}}$-algebras such that $C^{-1}(d(x \otimes 1)) = [x^{p-1} dx]$ for any local section *x* of $\mathcal{O}_X$. (Here, for the Cartier map to be well-defined in general it is essential that one takes cohomology sheaves for the codomain.) The **Cartier isomorphism** is then the assertion that the map $C^{-1}$ is an isomorphism if $f$ is a [smooth morphism](wikipedia://en/7837041).

In the above, we have formulated the Cartier isomorphism in the form it is most commonly encountered (e.g., in the 1970 paper of [Katz](wikipedia://en/1451427)).[^o5] In his original paper, Cartier actually considered the inverse map in a more restrictive setting, whence the notation $C^{-1}$ for the Cartier map.[^o6]

The smoothness assumption is not essential for the Cartier map to be an isomorphism. For instance, one has it for ind-smooth morphisms since both sides of the Cartier map commute with [filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit). By [Popescu's theorem](wikipedia://en/44549263), one then has the Cartier isomorphism for a [regular morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_morphism) of [noetherian](wikipedia://en/2783891) *k*-schemes.[^o7] [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber) has also proven a Cartier isomorphism for [valuation rings](wikipedia://en/966856).[^o8] In a different direction, one can dispense with such assumptions entirely if one instead works with derived de Rham cohomology (now taking the associated graded of the conjugate filtration) and the exterior powers of the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860).[^o9]

:::
