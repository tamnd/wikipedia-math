# History of representation theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="History of representation theory"}

The history of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) concerns the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) development of the study of objects in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), notably [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), by describing these objects more concretely, particularly using [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). In some ways, representation theory predates the mathematical objects it studies: for example, [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634) (in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)) and [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group) (in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)) were studied long before the notion of an abstract group was formalized by [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) in 1854.[^o2][^o3] Thus, in the [history of algebra](wikipedia://en/9550030), there was a process in which, first, mathematical objects were [abstracted](wikipedia://en/454446), and then the more abstract algebraic objects were realized or *represented* in terms of the more concrete ones, using [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564), [actions](wikipedia://en/12781) and [modules](wikipedia://en/276410).

:::

An early pioneer of the [representation theory of finite groups](wikipedia://en/874400) was [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048).[^o4] At first this method was not widely appreciated, but with the development of [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741) and the proof of [Burnside's $p^\alpha q^\beta$ solvability criterion](wikipedia://en/1912650) using such methods,[^o5] its power was soon appreciated.[^o6] Later [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) and others developed [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685).[^o7]
