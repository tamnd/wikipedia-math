# Leggett inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leggett inequality"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Leggett inequalities**,[^o2] named for [Anthony James Leggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_James_Leggett), who derived them, are a related pair of mathematical expressions concerning the correlations of properties of [entangled](wikipedia://en/25336) particles. (As published by Leggett, the inequalities were exemplified in terms of relative angles of elliptical and linear [polarizations](wikipedia://en/41564).)

:::

## Inequalities {#Inequalities}

They are fulfilled by a large class of physical theories based on particular [non-local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_nonlocality) and [realistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfactual_definiteness) assumptions, that may be considered to be plausible or intuitive according to common physical [reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning).

The Leggett inequalities are violated by [quantum mechanical theory](wikipedia://en/25202). The results of experimental tests in 2007 and 2010 have shown agreement with quantum mechanics rather than the Leggett inequalities.[^o4][^o5] Given that experimental tests of [Bell's inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell's_inequalities) have ruled out [local realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_realism) in quantum mechanics, the violation of Leggett's inequalities is considered to have falsified [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism) in quantum mechanics.[^o6] In quantum mechanics "realism" means "notion that physical systems possess complete sets of definite values for various parameters prior to, and independent of, measurement".[^o7]
