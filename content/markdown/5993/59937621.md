# Van Schooten's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Van Schooten's theorem"}

*Image: Satz von van schooten.svg.* $|PA| = |PB| + |PC|$

**Van Schooten's theorem**, named after the Dutch mathematician [Frans van Schooten](wikipedia://en/66858), describes a property of [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285). It states:

:::

*For an equilateral triangle $\triangle ABC$ with a point $P$ on its [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) the length of longest of the three line segments $PA$, $PB$, $PC$ connecting $P$ with the vertices of the triangle equals the sum of the lengths of the other two.*

The theorem is a consequence of [Ptolemy's theorem](wikipedia://en/1521971) for [cyclic quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/194131). Let $a$ be the side length of the equilateral triangle $\triangle ABC$ and $PA$ the longest line segment. The triangle's vertices together with $P$ form a cyclic quadrilateral $\square ABPC$. By Ptolemy's theorem,

$$|BC| \cdot |PA| = |AC| \cdot |PB| + |AB| \cdot |PC|.$$

But, since the triangle is equilateral, $|BC| = |AC| = |AB| = a$, so

$$\begin{aligned} a \cdot |PA| &= a \cdot |PB| + a \cdot |PC| \\[3mu] |PA| &= |PB| + |PC|. \end{aligned}$$
