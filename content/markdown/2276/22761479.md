# Quinn McNemar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quinn McNemar"}

**Quinn Michael McNemar** (February 20, 1900 – July 3, 1986)[^o3] was an American [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He is known for his work on [IQ tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IQ_tests), for his book *Psychological Statistics* (1949) and for [McNemar's test](wikipedia://en/3305230), the statistical test he introduced in 1947.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

McNemar was born in [Greenland, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland,_West_Virginia) in 1900. He obtained his bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1925 from [Juniata College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniata_College), studied for his doctorate in psychology under [Lewis Terman](wikipedia://en/1583393) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and joined the faculty at Stanford in 1931. In 1942 he published *The Revision of the [Stanford–Binet Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford–Binet_Scale)*, the IQ test released in 1916 by Terman. By the time he retired from Stanford in 1965 he held professorships in psychology, statistics and education. He taught for another five years at the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) before retiring to [Palo Alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto), where he died in 1986.[^o5]

He was president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) in 1951 and of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association) in 1964.[^o7][^o8][^o9]
