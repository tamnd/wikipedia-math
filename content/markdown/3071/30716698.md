# Pierre Gabriel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Gabriel"}

**Pierre Gabriel** (1 August 1933[^o3] – 24 November 2015), also known as **Peter Gabriel**, was a French mathematician who worked as a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) (1962–1970), [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) (1970–1974) and [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zürich) (1974–1998). He was an [algebraist](wikipedia://en/18716923) with fundamental contributions to [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), the theory of [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063), [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) and the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [algebras](wikipedia://en/2112).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Gabriel was born in the French town of [Bitche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitche) in [Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorraine), near the German border, a region that historically had been contested between France and Germany. Gabriel was bilingual and variantly used the first names *Pierre* and *Peter* in his publications. Throughout his life, he would forcefully argue for bilingualism and was active in several organizations supporting this goal.[^o13]

From 1953 to 1957 he studied mathematics at the prestigious [École normale supérieure Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) and afterwards became a research fellow at [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) from 1957 to 1960. Mathematically, he was heavily influenced by [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536), [Alexandre Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), who all worked in Paris at the time.

His doctoral thesis, written at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) under the direction of Serre, was defended in 1960[^o3] and published in 1962.[^o5] It contained important work about [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063), a concept introduced by [Buchsbaum](wikipedia://en/30781131) and popularized by Grothendieck a few years earlier. In his thesis, Gabriel studied the proper concept of [localization of abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_quotient), known as the Serre quotient or Gabriel quotient. With Michel Zisman he later proposed a more generally applicable concept of localization of categories and applied it to [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), thereby axiomatizing simplicial homotopy theory.[^o6] The thesis also contains an early "reconstruction theorem" allowing (under certain assumptions) to reconstruct a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *X* from the abelian category of [quasi-coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-coherent_sheaves) on *X,* by considering the spectrum of [indecomposable](wikipedia://en/810549) [injective objects](wikipedia://en/473779) in the category. This theorem, later vastly generalized by [Alexander L. Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/57780441) and now known as the [Gabriel-Rosenberg reconstruction theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel-Rosenberg_reconstruction_theorem), forms a starting point for [non-commutative algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/25493347).

In the early 1960s, Grothendieck and Gabriel collaborated in compiling the volumes [SGA](wikipedia://en/394508) 1 and SGA 3. In particular, they established the [Grothendieck construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_construction).

Gabriel collaborated with [Nicolae Popescu](wikipedia://en/1358791) in the study of abelian categories. In 1964 they published the [Gabriel-Popescu theorem](wikipedia://en/35884241), showing that every [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538) is a Serre quotient of the category of all (right) modules over some (not necessarily commutative) unital ring.[^o7]

With [Michel Demazure](wikipedia://en/32535595) he published a volume on [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) in 1970, emphasizing homological methods and algebraic geometry in the style of Grothendieck. An English translation of the first two chapters of the book appeared in 1980.[^o8]

In 1972 Gabriel published a fundamental result in representation theory, now known as [Gabriel's theorem](wikipedia://en/25274336). He gives a complete list of those [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100) that admit (up to isomorphism) only finitely many indecomposable linear representations over a given field and furthermore describes those representations. His proof uses [root systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_systems) and the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332).

Another important result in the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras, a collaborative effort of Gabriel, [Andrei Roiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Roiter), [Raymundo Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymundo_Bautista) and Leonardo Salmerón, appeared in 1985. Extending an earlier result by Gabriel's former doctoral student [Klaus Bongartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Bongartz), it established the existence of multiplicative bases for algebras of finite representation type (i.e. those associative algebras over a field admitting only finitely many isomorphism types of indecomposable modules).[^o9]

Having relocated to Switzerland in 1974, he served as the president of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society) in 1980–1981.

Gabriel and Bongartz wrote about [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323) in representation theory in 1982, building on earlier work by Gabriel's former doctoral student [Christine Riedtmann](wikipedia://en/54816867). This led to effective algorithms for algebras of finite representation type.[^o10]

Gabriel, his former doctoral student [Bernhard Keller](wikipedia://en/50017260) and A. V. Roiter published a book on the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras in 1997.[^o11]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

He was awarded the [Prix Francoeur](wikipedia://en/57185095) in 1972. In 1986 he was a [plenary speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenary_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) 1986 in Berkeley, explaining his work in finite-dimensional representation theory. He was elected a correspondent member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in November 1986.[^o13]
