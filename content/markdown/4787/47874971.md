# Arnold Emch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arnold Emch"}

**Arnold Frederick Emch** (24 March 1871 in [Hessigkofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessigkofen), Switzerland[^o2]–1959) was a Swiss-American mathematician, known for his work on the [inscribed square problem](wikipedia://en/27631070).

:::

After studying at [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) from 1890 to 1893, Emch traveled to America,[^o2] and received his Ph.D. in 1895 at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) under the supervision of Henry Byron Newson.[^o4] In the late 1890s until 1905, he was an assistant professor of graphic mathematics in the school of engineering at the Kansas State Agricultural College (now [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University)).[^o5] In 1905, Emch became a professor of mathematics at the Kantonsschule in [Solothurn, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn).[^o6] In 1908, Emch gave a lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome).[^o6] From 1911 to 1939, he was a professor at the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign).[^o7]

His wife was Hilda Walters Emch and they had two sons, Walter Emch and Arnold Frederick Emch, a well-known management consultant.[^o8]
