# Kenneth O. May

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kenneth O. May"}

**Kenneth Ownsworth May**[^o3] (July 8, 1915 – December 1, 1977)[^o4] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics), who developed [May's theorem](wikipedia://en/719575).

:::

May was a prime mover behind the [International Commission on the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/35011217), and was the first editor of its journal *[Historia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/10841658)*. Every four years the ICHM awards the [Kenneth O. May Prize](wikipedia://en/25758309) for outstanding contributions to the history of mathematics.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kenneth was born in 1915, the son of Samuel Chester "Sam" May (1887–1955) and Eleanor Ownsworth Perkin. His father, an alumnus of the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) and [Yale Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_Law_School), practised law in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon) beginning in 1913. After earning a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree from Columbia University, Sam in 1920 began teaching [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), and later at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o6]

At the University of California, Kenneth May was elected to [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) as a junior, and studied under [Griffith C. Evans](wikipedia://en/20086983). May joined the [Communist Party USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_USA) (CPUSA) and the [Institute of Pacific Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Pacific_Relations), of which he served as secretary from 1933. May's mother died in a gas heater explosion at home in 1935. The following year he was granted the [A.B. degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) and named to the [Order of the Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Golden_Bear). In 1937 May was granted a master's degree and was selected by the [Institute of Current World Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Current_World_Affairs) for foreign study. He participated in a Russian seminar and expected to study in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia), but those plans fell through and he took up study at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics).[^o6]

May was married on July 25, 1938. His wife, Ruth, had a leave of absence from her school for study at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), so the couple studied there for a year. In 1939 they travelled, including to Moscow where May visited the Kharkov Engineering-Economics Institute.

## Career {#Career}

Returning to California, May became a teaching assistant, assigned to [mathematics of finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_finance) and calculus with [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202).[^o6] May's work with the CPUSA led to alienation from his father and dismissal from his job. The affair received attention nationwide and was featured in a [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times) article.[^o8] In 1942 he ran for [California State Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Treasurer) under the Communist banner. May's associations with [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer) were used as evidence in [Oppenheimer's security clearance hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_security_clearance_hearing). In World War II May attempted to join up, but until Ruth filed for divorce in June 1942, he could not be drafted. May's unit was sent to [Kiska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiska) Island in the Aleutian chain. In May 1944, May married Jacqueline Bromley. His career in the military was recounted on page 4 of *[Stars and Stripes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stars_and_Stripes_%28newspaper%29)* (Mediterranean Rome) on March 3, 1945.[^o6]

In 1946 May submitted his thesis, *Mathematical Theory of Employment* for the Ph.D. He then received a teaching appointment at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College) in Northfield, Minnesota. As a protest against the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War), in 1966 May signed a vow of tax resistance.[^o9] He moved to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) the same year.

May is remembered for his *Bibliography and Research Manual of the History of Mathematics* (1973) published by [University of Toronto Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto_Press). He made editorial contributions to the [Encyclopedic Dictionary of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/29902421). He managed the compilation of an index for the first 80 volumes of [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) and wrote numerous book reviews. Philip Enros compiled a bibliography of May's writings that was published in 1984.[^o10]
