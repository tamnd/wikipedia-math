# Richard Courant

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Courant"}

**Richard Courant** (; January 8, 1888 – January 27, 1972) was a German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is best known by the general public for the book *[What is Mathematics?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_is_Mathematics?)*, co-written with [Herbert Robbins](wikipedia://en/745193). His research focused on the areas of [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). He wrote textbooks widely used by generations of students of physics and mathematics. He is also known for founding the [institute now bearing his name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Courant was born in [Lublinitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubliniec), in the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) [Province of Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Silesia) (now in Poland). His parents were Siegmund Courant and Martha Freund of [Oels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oels). [Edith Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Stein) was Richard's cousin on the maternal side. During his youth his parents moved often, including to [Glatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kłodzko), then to [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław) and in 1905 to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin). He stayed in Breslau and entered the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) there, then continued his studies at the [University of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zürich) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). He became [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s assistant in Göttingen and obtained his doctorate there in 1910. He was obliged to serve in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), but was wounded shortly after enlisting and therefore dismissed from the military. Courant left the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) in 1921 to take over [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990)'s position at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o5] There he founded the Mathematical Institute, which he headed as director from 1928 until 1933.

Courant left [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) in 1933, earlier than many [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) escapees. He did not lose his position due to being Jewish, as his previous service as a front-line soldier exempted him; however, his public membership in the [social-democratic left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany) was reason enough (for the Nazis) for dismissal.[^o6]

In 1936, after one year at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), Courant accepted a professorship at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in New York City. There he founded an institute for graduate studies in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969). The [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) (as it was renamed in 1964) is now one of the most respected research centers in applied mathematics.[^o7]

Courant and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) authored the influential [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) *[Methoden der mathematischen Physik](wikipedia://en/3203945)*, which, with its revised editions, is still current and widely used since its publication in 1924. With [Herbert Robbins](wikipedia://en/745193), Courant coauthored a popular overview of higher mathematics, intended for the general public, titled *[What is Mathematics?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_is_Mathematics?)*. With [Fritz John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_John), he coauthored the two-volume work *Introduction to Calculus and Analysis,* first published in 1965.[^o8]

*Image: 330-ps-9039-usn-710199 15641315399 o.jpg.* [Garrison Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Norton), Assistant Secretary of the Navy for Air, congratulates Courant (right) after presenting him with the [Distinguished Public Service Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Public_Service_Award) during [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) ceremonies on July 18, 1958

Courant's name is also attached to the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581),[^o9] with his numerical treatment of the plain torsion problem for multiply-connected domains, published in 1943.[^o10] This method is now one of the standard ways to solve [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) [numerically](wikipedia://en/21506). Courant is a namesake of the [Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy condition](wikipedia://en/2029932) and the [Courant minimax principle](wikipedia://en/16704670).

Courant was an elected member of both the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) (1953) and the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (1955).[^o11][^o12] In 1965, the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) recognized his contributions with its Award for Distinguished Service to Mathematics.[^o13][^o14]

Courant died of a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke) in [New Rochelle, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York) on January 27, 1972, aged 84.[^o15]

## Perspective on mathematics {#Perspective_on_mathematics}

Commenting upon his analysis of experimental results from in-laboratory soap film formations, Courant explained why the existence of a physical solution does not obviate [mathematical proof](wikipedia://en/82285). Here is a quote from Courant on his mathematical perspective:

Empirical evidence can never establish mathematical existence–nor can the mathematician's demand for existence be dismissed by the physicist as useless rigor. Only a mathematical existence proof can ensure that the mathematical description of a physical phenomenon is meaningful.[^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1912, Courant married [Nelly Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelly_Neumann), who had earned her doctorate at Breslau in [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484) in 1909. They lived together in Göttingen until they divorced in 1916. She was later murdered by the Nazis in 1942 for being Jewish.[^o19]

In 1919, Courant married Nerina (Nina) Runge (1891–1991), daughter of Göttingen professor for Applied Mathematics, [Carl Runge](wikipedia://en/747636) (of [Runge–Kutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runge–Kutta) fame) and sister of [Iris Runge](wikipedia://en/41305556), [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist).

Richard and Nerina had four children: [Ernest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Courant) (1920–2020), a [particle physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) and innovator in particle accelerators; Gertrude (1922–2014), a biologist who married mathematician [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316) (1928–1999); [Hans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Courant) (1924-2019),[^o20][^o21] a physicist who participated in the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project); and Leonore (known as "Lori", 1928–2015), a professional violist who married first mathematician Jerome Berkowitz (1928–1998) and secondly mathematician [Peter Lax](wikipedia://en/486365).[^o22]
