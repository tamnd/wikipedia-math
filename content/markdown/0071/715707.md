# Gyroelongated square bipyramid

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated square bipyramid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated square bipyramid** is a polyhedron with 16 triangular faces. it can be constructed from a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045) by attaching two [equilateral square pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid) to each of its square faces. The same shape is also called **hexakaidecadeltahedron**, **heccaidecadeltahedron**, or **tetrakis square antiprism**; these last names mean a polyhedron with 16 triangular faces. It is an example of a [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995), and of a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

The dual polyhedron of the gyroelongated square bipyramid is a truncated square trapezohedron with eight pentagons and two squares as its faces. The gyroelongated square pyramid appears in chemistry as the basis for the [bicapped square antiprismatic molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicapped_square_antiprismatic_molecular_geometry), and in mathematical optimization as a solution to the [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018).

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Like other [gyroelongated bipyramids](wikipedia://en/5506771), the gyroelongated square bipyramid can be constructed by attaching two [equilateral square pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid) onto the square faces of a [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045); this process is known as [gyroelongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongation). These pyramids cover each square, replacing it with four [equilateral triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_triangles), so that the resulting polyhedron has 16 equilateral triangles as its faces. A polyhedron with only equilateral triangles as faces is called a [deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995). There are only eight different convex deltahedra, one of which is the gyroelongated square bipyramid. More generally, the convex polyhedron in which all faces are regular is the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), and every convex deltahedron is a Johnson solid. The gyroelongated square bipyramid is numbered among the Johnson solids as $J_{17}$.

One possible system of [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a gyroelongated square bipyramid, giving it edge length 2, is:

$$\begin{aligned} \left(\pm 1, \pm 1, 2^{-1/4} \right),\qquad &\left(\pm \sqrt{2}, 0, -2^{-1/4} \right), \\ \left(0, \pm \sqrt{2}, -2^{-1/4} \right),\qquad &\left(0, 0, \pm \left(2^{-1/4} + \sqrt{2}\right)\right). \end{aligned}$$

## Properties {#Properties}

The surface area of a gyroelongated square bipyramid is 16 times the area of an equilateral triangle, that is:

$$4\sqrt{3}a^2 \approx 6.928a^2,$$

and the volume of a gyroelongated square bipyramid is obtained by slicing it into two equilateral square pyramids and one square antiprism, and then adding their volume:

$$\frac{\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{4 + 3\sqrt{2}}}{3}a^3 \approx 1.428a^3.$$

*Image: J17 gyroelongated square bipyramid.stl.* 3D model of a gyroelongated square bipyramid

It has the same [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525) as the square antiprism, the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) of $D_{4d}$ of order 8. Its [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) is similar to the [gyroelongated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1123470), by calculating the sum of the equilateral square pyramid and the square antiprism's angle in the following:

- the dihedral angle of an equilateral square pyramid between two adjacent triangles, approximately $109.47^\circ$
- the dihedral angle of a square antiprism between two adjacent triangles, approximately $127.55^\circ$
- the dihedral angle between two adjacent triangles, on the edge where an equilateral square pyramid is attached to a square antiprism, is $158.57^\circ$, which is obtained by adding the dihedral angles between a square and a triangle of both the pyramid and the antiprism.

*Image: Square truncated trapezohedron.png.* Square truncated trapezohedron

The [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of a gyroelongated square bipyramid is the square truncated trapezohedron. As suggested by the name, it is the family of [truncated trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/3521844) by means of truncating two axial vertices of a square trapezohedron. The resulting polyhedron has eight pentagons and two squares.

## Application {#Application}

Gyroelongated square bipyramid can be visualized in the geometry of [chemical compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_compounds) as the [atom cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_cluster) surrounding a central atom as a polyhedron, and the compound of such cluster is the [bicapped square antiprismatic molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicapped_square_antiprismatic_molecular_geometry). It has 10 vertices and 24 edges, corresponding to the [*closo* polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_skeletal_electron_pair_theory#4n_rules) with $2n + 2$ skeletal electrons. An example is [nickel carbonyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel_carbonyl) [carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbide) [anion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anion) Ni<sub>10</sub>C(CO), a 22 skeletal electron chemical compound with ten Ni(CO)<sub>2</sub> vertices and the deficiency of two [carbon monoxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide).

The [Thomson problem](wikipedia://en/3104018) concerns the minimum-energy configuration of $n$ charged particles on a sphere. The minimum solution known for $n = 10$ places the points at the vertices of a gyroelongated square bipyramid, [inscribed in a sphere](wikipedia://en/991784).
