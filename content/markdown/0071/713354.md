# Epicycloid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Epicycloid"}

*Image: EpitrochoidOn3-generation.gif.* The red curve is an epicycloid traced as the small circle (radius $r = 1)$ rolls around the outside of the large circle (radius $R = 3)$.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **epicycloid** (also called **hypercycloid**)[^o2] is a [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) produced by tracing the path of a chosen point on the circumference of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220)—called an *[epicycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deferent_and_epicycle)*—which rolls without slipping around a fixed circle. It is a particular kind of [roulette](wikipedia://en/846500).

:::

An epicycloid with a minor radius (R2) of 0 is a circle. This is a [degenerate form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form).

## Equations {#Equations}

If the rolling circle has radius $r$, and the fixed circle has radius $R = kr$, then the [parametric equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_equations) for the curve can be given by either:

$$\begin{aligned} & x (\theta) = (R + r) \cos \theta \ - r \cos \left( \frac{R + r}{r} \theta \right) \\ & y (\theta) = (R + r) \sin \theta \ - r \sin \left( \frac{R + r}{r} \theta \right) \end{aligned}$$

or:

$$\begin{aligned} & x (\theta) = r (k + 1) \cos \theta - r \cos \left( (k + 1) \theta \right) \\ & y (\theta) = r (k + 1) \sin \theta - r \sin \left( (k + 1) \theta \right). \end{aligned}$$

This can be written in a more concise form using complex numbers as[^o4]

$$z(\theta) = r \left( (k + 1)e^{ i\theta} - e^{i(k+1)\theta} \right)$$

where

- the angle $\theta \in [0, 2\pi],$
- the rolling circle has radius $r$, and
- the fixed circle has radius $kr$.

## Area and arc length {#Area_and_arc_length}

Assuming the initial point lies on the larger circle, when $k$ is a positive integer, the area $A$ and arc length $s$ of this epicycloid are

$$A=(k+1)(k+2)\pi r^2,$$

$$s=8(k+1)r.$$

It means that the epicycloid is $\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{k^2}$ larger in area than the original stationary circle.

If $k$ is a positive integer, then the curve is closed, and has $k$ [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758) (i.e., sharp corners).

If $k$ is a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024), say $k = p/q$ expressed as [irreducible fraction](wikipedia://en/14822), then the curve has $p$ cusps.

::: {#o6 .table cue=table}

| To close the curve and |
|---|
| complete the 1st repeating pattern : |
| $\theta = 0$ to $q$ rotations |
| $\alpha = 0$ to $p$ rotations |
| total rotations of outer rolling circle = $p + q$ rotations |

:::

Count the animation rotations to see $p$ and $q$

If $k$ is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689), then the curve never closes, and forms a [dense subset](wikipedia://en/23523594) of the space between the larger circle and a circle of radius $R + 2r$.

The distance $\overline{OP}$ from the origin to the point $p$ on the small circle varies up and down as

$$R \leq \overline{OP} \leq R+2r$$

where

- $R$ = radius of large circle and
- $2r$ = diameter of small circle .

The epicycloid is a special kind of [epitrochoid](wikipedia://en/1547292).

An epicycle with one cusp is a [cardioid](wikipedia://en/642101), two cusps is a [nephroid](wikipedia://en/843192).

An epicycloid and its [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) are [similar](wikipedia://en/39377).[^o7]

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

*Image: Epizykloide herleitung.svg.* sketch for proof

Assuming that the position of $p$ is what has to be solved, $\alpha$ is the angle from the tangential point to the moving point $p$, and $\theta$ is the angle from the starting point to the tangential point.

Since there is no sliding between the two cycles, then

$$\ell_R=\ell_r$$

By the definition of angle (which is the rate arc over radius), then

$$\ell_R= \theta R$$

and

$$\ell_r= \alpha r.$$

From these two conditions, the following identity is obtained

$$\theta R=\alpha r.$$

By calculating, the relation between $\alpha$ and $\theta$ is obtained, which is

$$\alpha =\frac{R}{r} \theta.$$

From the figure, the position of the point $p$ on the small circle is clearly visible.

$$x=\left( R+r \right)\cos \theta -r\cos\left( \theta+\alpha \right) =\left( R+r \right)\cos \theta -r\cos\left( \frac{R+r}{r}\theta \right)$$

$$y=\left( R+r \right)\sin \theta -r\sin\left( \theta+\alpha \right) =\left( R+r \right)\sin \theta -r\sin\left( \frac{R+r}{r}\theta \right)$$

:::
