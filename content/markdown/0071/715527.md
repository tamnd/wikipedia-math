# Norman Johnson (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Norman Johnson"}

**Norman Woodason Johnson** (November 12, 1930 – July 13, 2017) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) at [Wheaton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheaton_College,_Massachusetts), [Norton, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton,_Massachusetts).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Norman Johnson was born on November in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago). His father had a bookstore and published a local newspaper.

Johnson earned his undergraduate mathematics degree in 1953 at [Carleton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_College) in [Northfield, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield,_Minnesota) followed by a master's degree from the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh). After graduating in 1953, Johnson did [alternative civilian service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_civilian_service) as a [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector). He earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1966 with a dissertation title of *The Theory of Uniform Polytopes and Honeycombs* under the supervision of [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328).T66 From there, he accepted a position in the Mathematics Department of Wheaton College in Massachusetts and taught until his retirement in 1998.

## Career {#Career}

In 1966, Johnson published a paper titled *Convex Polyhedra with Regular Faces*.A66 Inside, it contained the list of 92 convex polyhedra with regular faces, along with the coined prefixes on naming some solids; encompassing the family of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), and other [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) brings up 112 solids in total. [Victor Zalgaller](wikipedia://en/4235763) later proved in 1969 that Johnson's list of 92 solids was complete, and the set is now known as the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) or Johnson–Zalgaller solid.

Johnson is also credited with naming all the [uniform star polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_star_polyhedra) and their [duals](wikipedia://en/609737), as published in [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)'s model building books: *Polyhedron models* (1971) and *Dual models* (1983).

## Death and final works {#Death_and_final_works}

He completed final edits for his book *Geometries and Transformations*B18 just before his death on July, and nearly completed his manuscript on [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).
