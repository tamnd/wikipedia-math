# Newman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newman"}

**Newman** is an English-language [surname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) of [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) origins. Newman is the [modern English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_English) form of the name used in [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) and among people of [British ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_ancestry) around the world (as is 'Numan'), while [Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumann) (with variant spellings) is used in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), and [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria), and to some degree in The [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands) and [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium).[^o2] Both have their origins in the pre-7th-century ([Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English)) word *neowe* meaning "new", with *mann*, meaning [man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man). Its first recorded uses were Godwin Nieweman in [Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordshire), England, in 1169, and in Germany, Herman Nyeman of [Barth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barth,_Germany) in 1325.[^o3] It was mostly likely originally used as a [nickname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickname) for a recent arrival or settler.[^o4] Related surnames include [Neuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuman), [Naumann(s)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naumann), [Numan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numan), [Nauman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauman), and [Neiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiman).[^o5]
