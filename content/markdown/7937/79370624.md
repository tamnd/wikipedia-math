# Density topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Density topology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **density topology** on the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on the real line that is different (strictly [finer](wikipedia://en/393069)), but in some ways analogous, to the [usual topology](wikipedia://en/287188#As_a_topological_space). It is sometimes used in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) to express or relate properties of the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) in topological terms.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ be a Lebesgue-measurable set. By the [Lebesgue density theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_density_theorem), almost every point $x$ of $U$ is a density point of $U$, i.e., satisfies

$$\frac{\lambda(U \, \cap \, (x-h,x+h))}{2h} \ \underset{h \to 0^+}{\longrightarrow} \ 1$$

where $\lambda$ is the Lebesgue measure and $(x-h,x+h)$ is the open interval of length $2h$ centered at $x$.

When *all* points of $U$ are density points of $U$, it is said to be **density open**.[^o4]

It can be shown that the density open sets of $\mathbb{R}$ form a topology (in other words, they are stable under arbitrary unions and finite intersections):[^o5] this constitutes the density topology.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every open set in the usual topology of $\mathbb{R}$ (i.e., a union of open intervals) is density open, but the converse is not true. For example, the subset $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1/n : n > 0\}$ is not open in the usual sense (since every open neighborhood of 0 contains some $1/n$ and is thus not contained in the set), but it *is* density open (the only problematic point being 0 and the set has density 1 at 0). More generally, any subset of [full measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_measure_%28mathematics%29) is density open. This includes, for example, the complements of $\mathbb{Q}$ and the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172).

Less trivially, and perhaps more instructively, let us show that the set $U := \mathbb{R} \setminus \bigcup_{n=1}^{+\infty} \left[\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2^n}\right]$ (which, again, is not open in the usual topology) is density open. Again, at every point $x\in U$ other than 0 this is clear because it is even neighborhood of *x* for the usual topology, so the only point to consider is 0. But if $h>0$ and we let $k := \left\lfloor\frac{1}{h}\right\rfloor$, then each interval $\left[\frac{1}{n}, \frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{2^n}\right]$ that intersects $(-h,h)$ has $n>k$ so their total measure is $\leq 2^{-k}$, and $\frac{\lambda((-h,+h) \setminus U)}{2h} \leq \frac{2^{-k}}{2h} \leq \frac{2^{-1/h}}{h} \underset{h \to 0^+}{\longrightarrow} 0$ proving that 0 is indeed a density point of *U*.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ denote the real line endowed with the density topology.

- Like $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology, $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ is a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) (*T<sub>2</sub>*) and [Tychonoff](wikipedia://en/30554) (*T<sub>$3\tfrac{1}{2}$</sub>*) topological space but *unlike* the usual topology, it is not [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) (*T<sub>4</sub>*).[^o10][^o11]
- A subset $Y \subseteq \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ is [nowhere dense](wikipedia://en/48634) (for the *density* topology) *iff* it is [meagre](wikipedia://en/226975) (ditto) *iff* it is closed and discrete (ditto) *iff* it is a [null set](wikipedia://en/21520) (in the sense of Lebesgue measure).[^o12][^o13]
- The [Borel subsets](wikipedia://en/43327) of $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ (for the *density* topology) are precisely the Lebesgue-measurable sets;[^o14] and the [complete Boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/1839944) of [regular open sets](wikipedia://en/47306) of $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ can be identified with the "reduced measure algebra", i.e., the Boolean algebra of Lebesgue-measurable sets modulo null sets.
- Like $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology, $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ is connected.[^o15][^o16]
- Like $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology, $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550); in fact, unlike the usual topology, it is even *hereditarily Baire* in the sense that every subspace of $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ is a Baire space.[^o17]
- The [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) subspaces of $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}$ are precisely its finite subsets.[^o18]
- The [approximately continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximately_continuous) functions $f\colon \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ are precisely the continuous functions $f : \mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{d}}\to\mathbb{R}$ (i.e., placing the density topology at the source but the usual topology at the target).[^o19]

:::
