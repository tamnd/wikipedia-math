# Factoriangular number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Factoriangular number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **factoriangular number** is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) formed by adding a [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) and a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) with the same index. The name is a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of "factorial" and "triangular."

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For $n \ge 1$, the $n$th *factoriangular number*, denoted $\operatorname{Ft}_n$, is defined as the sum of the $n$th [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) and the $n$th [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915):[^o4]

$$\operatorname{Ft}_n = n! + T_n = n! + \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.$$

The first few factoriangular numbers are:

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| $n$ | $n!$ | $T_n$ | $\operatorname{Ft}_n = n! + T_n$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| 3 | 6 | 6 | 12 |
| 4 | 24 | 10 | 34 |
| 5 | 120 | 15 | 135 |
| 6 | 720 | 21 | 741 |
| 7 | 5,040 | 28 | 5,068 |
| 8 | 40,320 | 36 | 40,356 |
| 9 | 362,880 | 45 | 362,925 |
| 10 | 3,628,800 | 55 | 3,628,855 |

:::

These numbers form the [integer sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) [A101292](https://oeis.org/A101292) in the [Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences) (OEIS).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Recurrence relations {#Recurrence_relations}

Factoriangular numbers satisfy several [recurrence relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrence_relations). For $n \geq 1$,

$$\operatorname{Ft}_{n+1} = (n+1)\left(\operatorname{Ft}_n - \frac{n^2-2}{2}\right)$$

And for $n \geq 2$,

$$\operatorname{Ft}_n = n\left(\operatorname{Ft}_{n-1} - \frac{n^2-2n-1}{2}\right)$$

These are linear [non-homogeneous recurrence relations](wikipedia://en/146806#Solving_first-order_non-homogeneous_recurrence_relations_with_variable_coefficients) with variable coefficients of order 1.

### Generating functions {#Generating_functions}

The [exponential generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_generating_function) $E(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \operatorname{Ft}_n \tfrac{x^n}{n!}$ for factoriangular numbers is (for $-1 < x < 1$)

$$E(x) = \frac{2 + (2-5x^2+2x^3+x^4)e^x}{2(1-x)^2}$$

If the sequence is extended to include $\operatorname{Ft}_0 = 1$, then the exponential generating function becomes

$$E(x) = \frac{2 + (2x-x^2-x^3)e^x}{2(1-x)}.$$

### Representations as sums of triangular numbers {#Representations_as_sums_of_triangular_numbers}

Factoriangular numbers can sometimes be expressed as sums of two triangular numbers:

- $\operatorname{Ft}_n = 2T_n$ if and only if $n = 1$ or $n = 3$.
- $\operatorname{Ft}_n = T_x + T_n$ if and only if $8n! + 1$ is a perfect square. For $n \neq x$, the only known solution is $(\operatorname{Ft}_5, T_{15}) = (135, 120)$, giving $\operatorname{Ft}_5 = T_5 + T_{15}$.
- $\operatorname{Ft}_n = T_x + T_y$ if and only if $8\operatorname{Ft}_n + 2$ is a sum of two squares.

### Representations as sums of squares {#Representations_as_sums_of_squares}

Some factoriangular numbers can be expressed as the sum of two squares. For $n \leq 20$, the factoriangular numbers that can be written as $a^2 + b^2$ for some integers $a$ and $b$ include:

- $$\operatorname{Ft}_1 = 2 = 1^2 + 1^2$$
- $$\operatorname{Ft}_2 = 5 = 1^2 + 2^2$$
- $$\operatorname{Ft}_4 = 34 = 3^2 + 5^2$$
- $$\operatorname{Ft}_9 = 362,925 = 195^2 + 570^2$$

This result is related to the [sum of two squares theorem](wikipedia://en/46248976), which states that a positive integer can be expressed as a sum of two squares if and only if its [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) contains no prime factor of the form $4k+3$ raised to an odd power.

### Fibonacci factoriangular numbers {#Fibonacci_factoriangular_numbers}

A *Fibonacci factoriangular number* is a number that is both a [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) and a factoriangular number. There are exactly three such numbers:

- $$\operatorname{Ft}_1 = 2 = F_3$$
- $$\operatorname{Ft}_2 = 5 = F_5$$
- $$\operatorname{Ft}_4 = 34 = F_9$$

This result was conjectured by Romer Castillo and later proved by Ruiz and Luca.[^o13][^o4]

### Pell factoriangular numbers {#Pell_factoriangular_numbers}

A *Pell factoriangular number* is a number that is both a [Pell number](wikipedia://en/1355482) and a factoriangular number.[^o15] Luca and Gómez-Ruiz proved that there are exactly three such numbers: $\operatorname{Ft}_1 = 2$, $\operatorname{Ft}_2 = 5$, and $\operatorname{Ft}_3 = 12$.[^o15]

### Catalan factoriangular numbers {#Catalan_factoriangular_numbers}

A *Catalan factoriangular number* is a number that is both a [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488) and a factoriangular number. The only such numbers are 1, 2 and 5.[^o17] They also proved in the same paper that the only [central binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_binomial_coefficients) which are also factoriangular numbers are 1 and 2.

:::

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The concept of factoriangular numbers can be generalized to $(n,k)$-factoriangular numbers, defined as $\operatorname{Ft}_{n,k} = n! + T_k$ where $n$ and $k$ are positive integers. The original factoriangular numbers correspond to the case where $n = k$. This generalization gives rise to factoriangular triangles, which are [Pascal-like triangular arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_triangle) of numbers. Two such triangles can be formed:

- A triangle with entries $\operatorname{Ft}_{n,k}$ where $k \leq n$, yielding the sequence: 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 25, 27, 30, 34, ...
- A triangle with entries $\operatorname{Ft}_{n,k}$ where $k \geq n$, yielding the sequence: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 12, 11, 12, 16, 34, ...

In both cases, the diagonal entries (where $n = k$) correspond to the original factoriangular numbers.
