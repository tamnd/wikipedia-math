# Chang graphs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Chang graphs"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Chang graphs** are three 12-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph), each with 28 vertices and 168 edges. They are [strongly regular](wikipedia://en/1399856), with the same parameters and [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) as the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) *L*(*K*<sub>8</sub>) of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>*8*</sub>.

:::

Each of these three graphs may be obtained by [graph switching](wikipedia://en/2705947) from *L*(*K*<sub>8</sub>). That is, a subset *S* of the vertices of *L*(*K*<sub>8</sub>) is chosen, each edge that connects a vertex in *S* with a vertex not in *S* is deleted from *L*(*K*<sub>8</sub>), and an edge is added for each pair of vertices (with again one in *S* and one not in *S*) that were not already connected by an edge. Among the graphs that can be generated in this way, three of them are the Chang graphs.

The Chang graphs are named after Chang Li-Chien, who proved that, with only these exceptions, every line graph of a complete graph is uniquely determined by its parameters as a strongly regular graph.[^o3]
