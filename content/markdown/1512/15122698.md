# Richard V. Southwell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard V. Southwell"}

**Sir Richard Vynne Southwell**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o5] (2 July 1888 – 9 December 1970) was a British mathematician who specialised in [applied mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mechanics) as an engineering science academic.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Richard Southwell was educated at [Norwich School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwich_School) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where in 1912 he achieved [first class degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_degree) results in both the mathematical and [mechanical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) [tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripos).[^o9] In 1914, he became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_%28college%29) of Trinity, and a lecturer in Mechanical Sciences.

Southwell was in the [Royal Naval Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Air_Service) during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I). After World War I, he was head of the Aerodynamics and Structures Divisions at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment), [Farnborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnborough_Airfield).

In 1920, he moved to the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_%28United_Kingdom%29). He then returned to Trinity College in 1925 as Fellow and Mathematics Lecturer in 1925. Next, in 1929, he moved to [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) as Professor of [Engineering Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_Science) and Fellow of [Brasenose College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford). Here, he developed a research group, including [Derman Christopherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derman_Christopherson), with whom he worked on his [relaxation method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxation_method). He became a member of a number of UK governmental technical committees, including for the [Air Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Ministry), at the time when the [R100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R100) and [R101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R101) [airships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airship) were being conceived.

Southwell was [Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28college%29) at [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London) from 1942 until his retirement in 1948.[^o6][^o10][^o11] He continued his research at Imperial College. He was also involved in the opening a new student residence, Selkirk Hall.

## Scientific contribution {#Scientific_contribution}

As a scientist, Southwell developed [relaxation methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxation_method) for solving [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) in engineering and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) during the 1930 and the 1940s. The equations had first to be discretised by the finite difference methods. Then, the values of the function of the grids would have to be iteratively adjusted so that the discretised equation would be satisfied. At the time, digital computers did not exist, and the computations had to be done by hand. Southwell developed various techniques to speed up the calculations. For instance, in 1935, he used multiple grids for that purpose, a technique which would later be elaborated into the multigrid method.[^o13]

## Honours {#Honours}

Southwell received the following honours and recognition for his achievements:

- Professor at [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) (1925)
- Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (1925)[^o5]
- Worcester Reed Warner medal, [ASME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASME) (1941)
- Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (1943)
- [Timoshenko Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timoshenko_Medal) (1959)
- [Elliott Cresson Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Cresson_Medal) (1964)

Southwell was also honoured with a knighthood.
