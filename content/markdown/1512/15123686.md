# Leonard Gillman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leonard Gillman"}

**Leonard E. Gillman** (January 8, 1917 – April 7, 2009) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) professor at the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin). He was also an accomplished classical [pianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianist).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gillman was born in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio) in 1917. His family moved to [Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania) in 1922. It was there that he started taking [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano) lessons at age six. They moved to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) in 1926, and he began intensive training as a pianist. Upon graduation from [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) in 1933, Gillman won a [fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) to the [Juilliard Graduate School of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juilliard_School).

### Career {#Career}

After one semester at Juilliard, he enrolled in evening classes in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He received a diploma in piano from Juilliard in 1938, then continued his studies at Columbia, graduating with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics in 1941. He stayed on as a graduate student, and completed the [coursework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coursework) for a mathematics [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) by 1943.

In 1943, Gillman accepted a position at [Tufts College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_College), working on a special project for the [Navy Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy). While there he wrote a [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) based on their work on [pursuit curves](wikipedia://en/587650), and he received his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) from Columbia in 1945. He moved to [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) where he continued doing Navy work for the Operations Evaluation Group (OEG), affiliated with the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). After five years he took a one-year [sabbatical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbatical) at MIT to write a doctoral thesis. Originally he intended for it to be on [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), but he happened to read a book by [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) and became suddenly interested in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553). With no specialists to advise him, Gillman wrote and published a paper that became his thesis: "On Intervals of Ordered Sets". He also sent the paper to [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), beginning a correspondence that led Tarski to claim Gillman as "my Ph.D. by mail".[^o6] In 1952, Gillman accepted an instructorship at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), and in 1953 he finally received his Ph.D. in mathematics from Columbia.

At Purdue, he began to do research in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), in collaboration with [Melvin Henriksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Henriksen), [Meyer Jerison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meyer_Jerison), and others. This work concentrated on the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of all [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) whose [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) is a given [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). They explored the relationships between topological properties of the space and algebraic properties of the ring. Gillman and Henriksen defined and characterized the classes of [P-spaces](wikipedia://en/41775284) and F-spaces, and Gillman and Jerison published an entire textbook on the subject: *Rings of Continuous Functions*, .

In 1958, Gillman was awarded a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship),[^o8] and he spent the next two years as a visiting member at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He and former OEG colleague [Nathan Fine](wikipedia://en/6042215) defined [remote points](wikipedia://en/63619636) and showed that if the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) holds, then the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) (or any [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Tychonoff space](wikipedia://en/30554) that is not [pseudocompact](wikipedia://en/5075659)) has remote points.

In 1960, he became chairman of the department of mathematics at the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester). He was active in recruiting top mathematicians to the department, including [Arthur Harold Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Harold_Stone) and his wife [Dorothy Maharam](wikipedia://en/13180716). At Rochester, Gillman also became involved in activities of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA). In 1969 he was appointed a regional Associate Secretary of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), but he had to give it up after moving to the University of Texas that same year. He chaired the UT mathematics department until 1973, when he was elected [Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer) of the MAA. He held this office for 13 years. Gillman retired from UT in 1987 and served as President of the MAA for the term 1987–1988.[^o9] Leonard Gillman received a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 1994[^o10] and again in 2003.[^o11]

### Music aspirations {#Music_aspirations}

Gillman was involved in local [classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_music) everywhere he worked, and performed four times at the [Joint Mathematics Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Mathematics_Meeting), twice with [William Browder](wikipedia://en/6646431). Gillman died on April 7, 2009[^o13] in [Austin, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin,_Texas).[^o14]
