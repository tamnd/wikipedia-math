# Simon Antoine Jean L'Huilier

*Image: Lhuilier, Simon Antoine Jean – Principiorum calculi differentialis et integralis expositio elementaris, 1795 – BEIC 749741.jpg.* *Principiorum calculi differentialis et integralis expositio elementaris*, 1795

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simon Antoine Jean L'Huilier"}

**Simon Antoine Jean L'Huilier** (or **L'Huillier**) (24 April 1750 in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) – 28 March 1840 in Geneva) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) descent. He is known for his work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and in particular the generalization of [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/148420) for [planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_graphs).[^o2]

:::

He won the mathematics section prize of the [Berlin Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences) for 1784 in response to a question on the foundations of the [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176). The work was published in his 1787 book *Exposition elementaire des principes des calculs superieurs*. (A [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) version was published in 1795.) Although L'Huilier won the prize, [Joseph Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange), who had suggested the question and was the lead judge of the submissions, was disappointed in the work, considering it "the best of a bad lot." Lagrange would go on to publish his own work on foundations.[^o10]

## L'Huilier and Cauchy {#L'Huilier_and_Cauchy}

L'Huilier introduced the abbreviation "lim" for limit, using the first three letters of the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) limite, with a [full stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_stop)) to denote the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) of a variable [expression](wikipedia://en/609125). This reappeared in 1821 in [Cours d'Analyse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cours_d'Analyse) by [Augustin Louis Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Louis_Cauchy), who would later create his approach based on [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) defined in terms of variable quantities.[^o8][^o9] L'Huilier first employed this symbol on page 24 of the essay and explained its convenience for indicating the limiting value of simultaneous changes in variables on page 31. This represents the earliest recorded use of a limit notation in European analysis and laid the groundwork for the modern "lim" symbol.[^o10]

## Royal Society fellow {#Royal_Society_fellow}

He was elected in May, 1791 a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o5]

Note that this [surname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) is sometimes rendered as *Lhuilier* or *Lhuillier*.
