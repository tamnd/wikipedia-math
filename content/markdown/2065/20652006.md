# Tomasz Mrowka

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tomasz Mrowka"}

**Tomasz Stanislaw Mrowka** (born September 8, 1961) is an American mathematician specializing in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155). He is the Singer Professor of Mathematics and former head of the [Department of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mathematics_Department) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).

:::

Mrowka is the son of Polish mathematician Stanisław Mrówka,[^o3] and is married to MIT mathematics professor [Gigliola Staffilani](wikipedia://en/44916025).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

A 1983 graduate of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, he received the PhD from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1988 under the direction of [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920) and [Robion Kirby](wikipedia://en/2932424). He joined the MIT mathematics faculty as professor in 1996, following faculty appointments at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (professor 1994–96).[^o6] At MIT, he was the Simons Professor of Mathematics from 2007–2010. Upon Isadore Singer's retirement in 2010 the name of the chair became the Singer Professor of Mathematics which Mrowka held until 2017. He was named head of the Department of Mathematics in 2014 and held that position for 3 years.[^o7]

A prior [Sloan fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellows) and Young Presidential Investigator, in 1994 he was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich). In 2007, he received the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) from the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) jointly with [Peter Kronheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kronheimer), "for their joint contributions to both three- and four-dimensional [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) through the development of deep analytical techniques and applications."[^o8] He was named a Guggenheim Fellow in 2010, and in 2011 he received the [Doob Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doob_Prize) with [Peter B. Kronheimer](wikipedia://en/3845647) for their book *Monopoles and Three-Manifolds* ([Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press), 2007).[^o9][^o10] In 2018 he gave a plenary lecture at the ICM in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro), together with Peter Kronheimer. In 2023 he was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research (with Peter Kronheimer).[^o11]

He became a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts & Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_&_Sciences) in 2007,[^o12] and a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2015.[^o13]

## Research {#Research}

Mrowka's work combines analysis, geometry, and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), specializing in the use of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), such as the [Yang-Mills equations](wikipedia://en/672202) from particle physics to analyze low-dimensional mathematical objects.[^o7] Jointly with [Robert Gompf](wikipedia://en/42024874), he discovered four-dimensional models of space-time topology.[^o15]

In joint work with Peter Kronheimer, Mrowka settled many long-standing conjectures, three of which earned them the 2007 Veblen Prize. The award citation mentions three papers that Mrowka and Kronheimer wrote together. The first paper in 1995 deals with [Donaldson's polynomial invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson's_polynomial_invariants) and introduced [Kronheimer–Mrowka basic class](wikipedia://en/35131387), which have been used to prove a variety of results about the topology and geometry of [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795), and partly motivated Witten's introduction of the [Seiberg–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/14407762).[^o16] The second paper proves the so-called [Thom conjecture](wikipedia://en/3846986) and was one of the first deep applications of the then brand new Seiberg–Witten equations to four-dimensional topology.[^o17] In the third paper in 2004, Mrowka and Kronheimer used their earlier development of [Seiberg–Witten monopole Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856#Seiberg–Witten_Floer_homology) to prove the [Property P conjecture](wikipedia://en/1294354) for [knots](wikipedia://en/153008).[^o18] The citation says: "The proof is a beautiful work of synthesis which draws upon advances made in the fields of gauge theory, [symplectic](wikipedia://en/294298) and [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398), and [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227) over the past 20 years."[^o8]

In further recent work with Kronheimer, Mrowka showed that a certain subtle combinatorially defined knot invariant introduced by [Mikhail Khovanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khovanov) can detect “[unknottedness](wikipedia://en/4956355).”[^o19]
