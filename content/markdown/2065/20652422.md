# Yakov Eliashberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Yakov Eliashberg"}

**Yakov Matveevich Eliashberg** (also **Yasha Eliashberg**; Яков Матвеевич Элиашберг; born 11 December 1946) is an American mathematician who was born in Leningrad, USSR. He is the Herald L. and Caroline L. Ritch Professor of Mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). His research interests are [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology), and [contact topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_topology). He was awarded many prizes for his work, including the [Wolf Prize](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2020 (shared with [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305)).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

*Image: Yakov\_Eliashberg.jpg.* Yakov Eliashberg in 1988

*Image: Yakov Eliashberg 2016 03.jpg.* Yakov Eliashberg, Crafoord Prize Laureate in Mathematics 2016, with King [Carl XVI Gustaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_XVI_Gustaf) at the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in Stockholm, Sweden, May 2016

Eliashberg received his PhD, entitled *Surgery of Singularities of Smooth Mappings*, from [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_University) in 1972, under the direction of [Vladimir Rokhlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Rokhlin_%28Soviet_mathematician%29).

Due to the growing [anti-Semitism in the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism_in_the_Soviet_Union), from 1972 to 1979 he had to work at the [Syktyvkar State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syktyvkar_State_University) in the isolated [Komi Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_Republic). In 1980 Eliashberg returned to Leningrad and applied for a visa, but his request was denied and he became a [refusenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik) until 1987. He was cut off from mathematical life and was prevented to work in academia, but due to a friend's intercession, he managed to secure a job in industry as the head of a computer software group.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

In 1988 Eliashberg managed to move to the United States, and since 1989 he has been Herald L. and Caroline L. Ritch professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o8] Between 2001 and 2002 he was Distinguished Visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o9]

, he supervised 43 PhD students, including [Eric Katz](wikipedia://en/54434627), [Emmy Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Murphy) and [John Pardon](wikipedia://en/48651594).[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Eliashberg's research interests are in [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), especially in [symplectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology) and [contact topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_topology).[^o7]

In the 80's he developed a combinatorial technique[^o25] which he used to prove that the group of [symplectomorphisms](wikipedia://en/312304) is $\mathcal{C}^0$-[closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) in the [diffeomorphism group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphism_group).[^o11] This fundamental result, proved also in a different way by [Gromov](wikipedia://en/771562),[^o12] is now called the Eliashberg–Gromov theorem, and is one of the first manifestations of symplectic rigidity.

In 1990 he discovered a complete topological characterization of [Stein manifolds](wikipedia://en/1053909) of [complex dimension](wikipedia://en/2783513) greater than 2.[^o13]

Eliashberg classified [contact structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_structure) into "tight" and "overtwisted" ones.[^o14] Using this dichotomy, he gave the complete classification of contact structures on the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792).[^o26] Together with [Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190), he developed the theory of confoliations, which unifies [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227) and contact structures.[^o15]

Eliashberg worked on various aspects of the [h-principle](wikipedia://en/481119), introduced by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29), and he wrote in 2002 an introductory book on the subject.[^o16]

Together with [A. Givental](wikipedia://en/8488782) and [H. H. W. Hofer](wikipedia://en/34920327), Eliashberg pioneered the foundations of [symplectic field theory](wikipedia://en/2672856#Symplectic_field_theory_%28SFT%29).[^o17]

## Awards {#Awards}

Eliashberg received the "Young Mathematician" Prize from the [Leningrad Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_Mathematical_Society) in 1972.[^o19] He was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1986,[^o20] 1998[^o21] and 2006 (plenary lecture).[^o22] In [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1995) he was a recipient of the [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o23]

In 2001 Eliashberg was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) from the [AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_mathematical_society) for his work in [symplectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology) and [contact topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_topology),[^o24] in particular for his proof of the [symplectic rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_rigidity)[^o25] and the development of 3-dimensional [contact topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_topology).[^o26]

In 2002 Eliashberg was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) of the US[^o27] and in 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o28] He also was a member of the Selection Committee in mathematical sciences of the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize).[^o29] He received a [Doctorat Honoris Causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorat_Honoris_Causa) from the [ENS Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENS_Lyon) in 2009[^o30] and from the [University of Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Uppsala) in 2017.[^o31]

In 2013 Eliashberg shared with [Helmut Hofer](wikipedia://en/34920327) the [Heinz Hopf Prize](wikipedia://en/24560211) from the [ETH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH), Zurich, for their pioneering research in symplectic topology.[^o32] In 2016 Yakov Eliashberg was awarded the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in Mathematics from the [Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) *for the development of contact and symplectic topology and groundbreaking discoveries of rigidity and flexibility phenomena.*[^o33]

In 2020 he received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) (jointly with [Simon K. Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_K._Donaldson)).[^o5][^o34][^o35] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2021.[^o36] For 2023 he was awarded the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in Basic Sciences (jointly with [Claire Voisin](wikipedia://en/5980475)).[^o37]
