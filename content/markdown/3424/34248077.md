# Bel–Robinson tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bel–Robinson tensor"}

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Bel–Robinson tensor** is a tensor defined in the [abstract index notation](wikipedia://en/1674987) by:

:::

$$T_{abcd}=C_{aecf}C_{b} {}^{e} {}_{d} {}^{f} + \frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{ae}{}^{hi} \epsilon_{b}{}^{ej}{}_{k} C_{hicf} C_{j}{}^{k}{}_{d}{}^{f}$$

Alternatively,

$$T_{abcd} = C_{aecf}C_{b} {}^{e} {}_{d} {}^{f} - \frac{3}{2} g_{a[b} C_{jk]cf} C^{jk}{}_{d}{}^{f}$$

where $C_{abcd}$ is the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848). It was introduced by Lluís Bel in 1959.[^o2][^o3] The Bel–[Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Robinson_%28physicist%29) tensor is constructed from the Weyl tensor in a manner analogous to the way the [electromagnetic stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/4601361) is built from the [electromagnetic tensor](wikipedia://en/1912367). Like the electromagnetic stress–energy tensor, the Bel–Robinson tensor is totally symmetric and traceless:

$$\begin{aligned} T_{abcd} &= T_{(abcd)} \\ T^{a}{}_{acd} &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

In general relativity, there is no unique definition of the local energy of the gravitational field. The Bel–Robinson tensor is a possible definition for local energy,[^o4] since it can be shown that whenever the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) vanishes (i.e. in vacuum), the Bel–Robinson tensor is divergence-free:

$$\nabla^{a} T_{abcd} = 0$$
