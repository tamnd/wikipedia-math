# Aissa Wade

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Aissa Wade"}

**Aissa Wade** is a professor of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University). She was the President of the [African Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1575286) centre in Senegal (from 2016 to 2018).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wade was born in [Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar), [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal).[^o4] She studied mathematics at [Cheikh Anta Diop University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheikh_Anta_Diop_University) and graduated in 1993.[^o5] She had to leave [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal) to earn a Ph.D. as there were no opportunities in Africa.[^o6] Wade earned her Ph.D. at the [University of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier) in 1996.[^o5] Her thesis, *"Normalisation formelle de structures de Poisson"*, considered [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298).[^o7][^o8] Her doctoral advisor was Jean Paul Dufour.[^o9][^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Wade became a postdoctoral researcher at the [Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam_International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics), where she worked on conformal Dirac structures.[^o5][^o12] She held visiting faculty positions at [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), [African University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_University_of_Science_and_Technology) and [Paul Sabatier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sabatier_University).[^o13] Wade joined [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) and was appointed full professor in 2016.

She served as a managing editor of The African Diaspora Journal of Mathematics.[^o14] She is editor of Afrika Mathematika.[^o13] She is on the scientific committee of the [NextEinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NextEinstein) forum, an initiative to connect science, society and policy in Africa.[^o15] As the President of the [African Institute for Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/1575286), Wade was the first woman to hold this role.[^o16][^o17] She has been awarded funding from the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) to support the Senegal Workshop on Geometric Structures.[^o18][^o19] She has been involved with [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) activities to enhance African [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663) research, including the provision of evidence-based metrics, case studies and policy recommendations.[^o20][^o21] In 2017 Wade was named a fellow of the [African Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o22][^o17]

Wade's accomplishments earned her recognition by *[Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006),* where she was featured as a Black History Month 2020 Honoree.[^o23]
