# Ken Ribet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ken Ribet"}

**Kenneth Alan Ribet** (; born June 28, 1948) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is known for the [Herbrand–Ribet theorem](wikipedia://en/1078637) and [Ribet's theorem](wikipedia://en/700981), which were key ingredients in the [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), as well as for his service as President of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 2017 to 2019. He is currently a professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Kenneth Ribet was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to parents David Ribet and Pearl Ribet, both Jewish, on June 28, 1948.[^o4] As a student at [Far Rockaway High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Rockaway_High_School), Ribet was on a competitive mathematics team, but his first field of study was [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry).[^o5]

Ribet earned his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) and [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1969.[^o6][^o20] In 1973, Ribet received his Ph.D. from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the supervision of [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933).[^o6][^o20]

## Career {#Career}

After receiving his doctoral degree, Ribet taught at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) for three years before spending two years doing research in Paris.[^o6] In 1978, Ribet joined the Department of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he served three separate terms as supervisor of the department's graduate program, supervisor of the department's undergraduate program, and supervisor of the department's development.[^o6]

Ribet has served as an editor for several mathematics journals, a book series editor for the [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press), and a book series editor for [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media).[^o6] He also served on the United States National Committee for Mathematics, representing the United States at the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), and was the Chair of the Mathematics section of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o6]

From February 1, 2017 to January 31, 2019, Ribet was [President of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822#2001.E2.80.93present).[^o17]

## Research {#Research}

Ribet's contributions in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and algebraic geometry were described by [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863) and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) as being "key to our understanding of the connections between the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) and the [ℓ-adic representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ℓ-adic_representation) of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of the field of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024)."[^o6]

Ribet is credited with paving the way towards [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)'s proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953). In 1986, Ribet proved that the *[epsilon conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_conjecture)* formulated by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) was true, and thereby proved that Fermat's Last Theorem would follow from the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture). Crucially it also followed that the full conjecture was not needed, but a special case, that of [semistable elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_elliptic_curve), sufficed.[^o10] An earlier theorem of Ribet's, the [Herbrand–Ribet theorem](wikipedia://en/1078637), is the converse to [Herbrand](wikipedia://en/159901)'s theorem on the divisibility properties of [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964) and is also related to Fermat's Last Theorem.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Ribet received the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195) in 1989 jointly with [Abbas Bahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Bahri).[^o17][^o6] He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1997 and the National Academy of Sciences in 2000.[^o17][^o6][^o20] In 2012, he became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16][^o17] In 2017, Ribet received the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624).[^o18] For 2025 Ribet was awarded the AMS [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research.[^o19]

In 1988, Ribet was inducted as a vigneron d'honneur by the Jurade de [Saint-Émilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Émilion_AOC).[^o20] In 1998, Ribet received an honorary doctorate from Brown University.[^o20]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Ribet is married to statistician [Lisa Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Goldberg).[^o22]
