# Shreeram Shankar Abhyankar

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Shreeram Shankar Abhyankar"}

**Shreeram Shankar Abhyankar** (22 July 1930 – 2 November 2012)[^o3][^o4] was an [Indian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). At the time of his death, he held the Marshall [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor) of Mathematics Chairman at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University), and was also a professor of computer science and industrial engineering. He is known for [Abhyankar's conjecture](wikipedia://en/3832555) of finite [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). His later research was in the area of [computational](wikipedia://en/5926) and [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Abhyankar was born during the late [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), 22 July 1930, in a [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_people) [Chitpavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitpavan) [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) family in [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), [Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India). He earned his B.Sc. from the [Royal Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Science) of [University of Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mumbai) in 1951, his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1952, and his Ph.D. at Harvard in 1955. His thesis, written under the direction of [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352), was titled *Local uniformization on algebraic surfaces over modular ground fields*.[^o7] Before going to [Purdue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue), he was an associate [professor of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_mathematics) at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University).

Abhyankar was appointed the Marshall Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at Purdue in 1967. His research topics include [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) (particularly [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115), a field in which he made significant progress over fields of finite characteristic), [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), [local algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_algebra), [valuation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_theory), theory of functions of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268), [circuit theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_theory), [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [computer-aided design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_design), and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics). He popularized the [Jacobian conjecture](wikipedia://en/1193823).

## Death {#Death}

Abhyankar died of a heart condition on 2 November 2012 at his residence near Purdue University.[^o9]

## Honours {#Honours}

Abhyankar has won numerous awards and honours.

- Abhyankar received the Herbert Newby McCoy Award from Purdue University in 1973 .
- Fellow of the [Indian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Academy_of_Sciences)
- Editorial board member of the *Indian Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics*
- [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (1978)[^o16]
- Honorary Doctorate Degree (Docteur Honoris Causa) by the [University of Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Angers) in France (29 October 1998)
- Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (2012)[^o17]

## Bhaskaracharya Pratishthana {#Bhaskaracharya_Pratishthana}

**Bhaskaracharya Pratishthana** is a research and education institute for [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in [Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), founded by Abhyankar. The institute is named after the great ancient Indian Mathematician [Bhaskaracharya](wikipedia://en/480509) (Born in 1114 A.D). It was founded in 1976.[^o21] It has researchers working in many areas of mathematics, particularly in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o22]

Since 1992, the Pratishthan has also been a recognized center for conducting [Regional Mathematics Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Mathematics_Olympiad) (RMO) under the [National Board for Higher Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Board_for_Higher_Mathematics) (NBHM) for Maharashtra and Goa Region.[^o23]

Pratishthana publishes the mathematics periodical *Bona Mathematica* and has published texts in higher and Olympiad mathematics.[^o24] Besides this the Pratishthan holds annual / biennial conferences/ Workshops in some research areas in higher mathematics attended by Indian/Foreign scholars and Professors. The Pratishthan has organized a number of workshops for research students and college teachers under the aegis of NBHM/NCM. The National Board for Higher Mathematics has greatly helped Pratishthan to enrich its library. The [Department of Atomic Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Atomic_Energy) and the Mathematics Department of S. P. [Pune University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule_Pune_University) have rendered active co-operation in holding conferences/workshops.

It also conducts the BMTSC exam which is a school-level mathematics competition for students studying in the 5th and the 6th grade. The objectives of the competition are listed below: 1. Identify good students of mathematics at an early age. 2. A pre Olympiad type competition. 3. To enhance Mathematical ability and logical thinking. 4. Nurture programs for successful students to improve their ability.
