# Thomas Fale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Fale"}

**Thomas Fale** (; [fl.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floruit) 1604) was an English mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Fale matriculated as a [sizar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizar) of [Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonville_and_Caius_College,_Cambridge), in November 1578, removed to [Corpus Christi College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge) in 1582, went out B.A. in 1582–3, commenced M.A. in 1586, proceeded B.D. in 1597, and in 1604 had a licence from the university to practise medicine.

## Publications {#Publications}

His only known publication is *Horologiographia* (1593).[^o5] It is dedicated in Latin to all lovers of mathematics in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). There is also a prefatory letter to 'my loving kinsman,’ Thomas Osborne, who had invented the instrument mentioned in the beginning of the book 'for the triall of plats,’ dated from London, 3 January 1593. The table of [sines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) which it contains is probably the earliest specimen of a [trigonometrical table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometrical_table) printed in England.
