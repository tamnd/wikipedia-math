# George David Birkhoff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George David Birkhoff"}

**George David Birkhoff** (March 21, 1884 – November 12, 1944) was an American mathematician. He is considered one of the top American mathematicians of his generation. He made valuable contributions to the theory of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the [four-color problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-color_problem), the [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420), and [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Today, Birkhoff is best remembered for the [ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_theorem).[^o3] The [George D. Birkhoff House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_D._Birkhoff_House), his residence in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), has been designated a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in [Overisel Township, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overisel_Township,_Michigan),[^o5] the son of two Dutch immigrants, David Birkhoff, who arrived in the United States in 1870, and Jane Gertrude Droppers.[^o6][^o7] Birkhoff's father worked as a [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) in Chicago while he was a child.[^o7] From 1896 to 1902, he would attend the [Lewis Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Institute) as a teenager.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Birkhoff was part of a generation of American mathematicians who were the first to study entirely within the United States and not participate in academics within Europe.[^n1] Following his time at the [Lewis Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Institute), Birkhoff would spend a year at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o7] He then obtained his A.B. and A.M. from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), returned to the University of Chicago in 1905, and at the age of twenty-three, graduated *[summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude)* with his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1907 in [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).[^o7] While [E. H. Moore](wikipedia://en/1532860) was his supervisor,[^n2] he was most influenced by the writings of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740). After teaching at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) from 1907 to 1909 and at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1909 to 1912, he taught at Harvard from 1912 until his death.[^o7] Being the only American familiar with the three main mathematical institutions within the United States—Chicago, Harvard and Princeton—he was held in high regard by his colleagues.[^n1]

### Service {#Service}

During his membership in the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), Birkhoff served multiple positions in the organization. In 1919, he served as vice president of the society. He was editor of *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)* from 1920 to 1924.

From 1925 to 1926, he was President of the American Mathematical Society. During his tenure as president of the society, Birkhoff sought to create a lectureship program to travel the United States to promote mathematics.[^n3] In 1926, he travelled Europe to serve as an unofficial representative of the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation)'s International Education Board.[^n1] During his time in Europe, Birkhoff attempted to create links between American and French institutions, especially due to his affection for Paris.[^n4]

In 1937, he served as president of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), a rare occurrence for mathematicians and was proof of his respect amongst the scientific community.[^n5]

## Work {#Work}

In 1912, attempting to solve the [four color problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_color_problem), Birkhoff introduced the [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126). Even though this line of attack did not prove fruitful, the [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) itself became an important object of study in [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216).

In 1913, he proved Poincaré's "[Last Geometric Theorem](wikipedia://en/35737249),"[^o18] a special case of the [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420), a result that made him world-famous and improved the international recognition of American mathematics.[^n1]

Birkhoff was also a [contributor](wikipedia://en/2388068) to the development of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). He wrote on the foundations of relativity and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), publishing (with [R. E. Langer](wikipedia://en/38442527)) the monograph *Relativity and Modern Physics* in 1923. In 1923, Birkhoff also [proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhoff's_theorem_%28relativity%29) that the [Schwarzschild geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzschild_geometry) is the unique spherically symmetric solution of the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540). A consequence is that [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650) are not merely a mathematical curiosity, but could result from any spherical star having sufficient mass. His theorem was later used to develop the [Oppenheimer–Snyder model](wikipedia://en/74503991). In 1927, he published his *[Dynamical Systems](https://web.archive.org/web/20060422022803/http://www.ams.org/online_bks/coll9/)*.

Birkhoff's most durable result has been his 1931 discovery of what is now called the [ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_theorem). Combining insights from [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) on the [ergodic hypothesis](wikipedia://en/258980) with [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), this theorem solved, at least in principle, a fundamental problem of [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481). The ergodic theorem has also had repercussions for dynamics, [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [group theory](wikipedia://en/19447), and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). He also worked on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the [Riemann–Hilbert problem](wikipedia://en/9710396), and the [four colour problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_colour_problem). He proposed an axiomatization of [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) different from Hilbert's (see [Birkhoff's axioms](wikipedia://en/828320)); this work culminated in his text *Basic Geometry* (1941).

His 1933 *[Aesthetic Measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetic_Measure)* proposed a [mathematical theory of aesthetics](wikipedia://en/22468661).[^o20] While writing this book, he spent a year studying the art, music and poetry of various cultures around the world. His 1938 *Electricity as a Fluid* combined his ideas on philosophy and science. His 1943 theory of gravitation is also puzzling since Birkhoff knew (but didn't seem to mind) that his theory allows as sources only matter which is a [perfect fluid](wikipedia://en/2137509) in which the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) must equal the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light).

## Influence on selection process {#Influence_on_selection_process}

Birkhoff believed that fellowships from foreign nations would improve the mathematic standards in the United States,[^n6] though he wanted to raise the standards for fellowships, believing that the qualifications in less-developed countries were different and that access should be limited.[^n7] He also was adamant that foreign fellows be able to meet language requirements.[^n8]

[Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) and [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185), among others, accused[^o25][^o26][^o27] Birkhoff of advocating [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) selection processes. During the 1930s, when many Jewish mathematicians fled Europe and tried to obtain positions in the United States, Birkhoff is alleged to have influenced the selection process at American institutions to exclude Jews.[^o26] [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574), who was at Harvard at the time, would call Einstein's allegations "worthless" as he was not familiar with American processes and that the two had competing ideas regarding general relativity while also rebutting the reports of antisemitism against Wiener, writing "Birkhoff clearly listened to Norbert's ideas. There could be many reasons why Birkhoff did not take steps to appoint him".[^o28] Mac Lane also stated that Birkhoff's efforts were motivated less by animus towards Jews than by a desire to find jobs for home-grown American mathematicians.[^o29] However, in some of the correspondence that came to light in the book *A History in Sum: 150 Years of Mathematics at Harvard (1825-1975)*, it is obvious that his views of Jews were antisemitic and he regarded them as suspicious elements of the American society. [^o30] [^o31]

Birkhoff was, on the other hand, close to a Jewish mathematician [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam). [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891) writes: "Like other persons rumored to be anti-Semitic, he would occasionally feel the urge to shower his protective instincts on some good-looking young Jew. Ulam's sparkling manners were diametrically opposite to Birkhoff's hard-working, aggressive, touchy personality. Birkhoff tried to keep Ulam at Harvard, but his colleagues balked at the idea."[^o32]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1923, he was awarded the inaugural [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for his paper in 1917 containing, among other things, what is now called the [Birkhoff curve shortening process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhoff_curve_shortening_process).[^o34]

He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in Paris, the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o35] and the London and Edinburgh Mathematical Societies.

The [George David Birkhoff Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_David_Birkhoff_Prize) in applied mathematics is awarded jointly by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) in his honor.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Birkhoff married Margaret Elizabeth Graftus in 1908.[^o7] They had three children, Barbara, mathematician [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) (1911–1996) and Rodney.[^o7]
