# Argumentum ad populum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Argumentum ad populum"}

la In [argumentation theory](wikipedia://en/1317383), an **argumentum ad populum** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for 'appeal to the people')[^o2] is an [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450) that asserts a claim is true, good, or correct because many people allegedly think so.[^o3]

:::

## Description {#Description}

*Argumentum ad populum* is a type of [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450), specifically a [fallacy of relevance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacy_of_relevance),[^o9][^o10] and is similar to an [argument from authority](wikipedia://en/37568781) (*argumentum ad verecundiam*).[^o11][^o12][^o13] It uses an appeal to the beliefs, tastes, or values of a group of people,[^o14] stating that because a certain opinion or attitude is held by a majority, or even everyone, it is therefore correct.[^o15]

Appeals to popularity are common in commercial advertising that portrays products as desirable because they are used by many people or associated with popular sentiments[^o16] instead of communicating the merits of the products themselves.

The [inverse](wikipedia://en/155942) argument, that something that is unpopular must be flawed, is also a form of this fallacy.[^o17]

The fallacy is similar in structure to certain other fallacies that involve a confusion between the "justification" of a belief and its "widespread acceptance" by a given group of people. When an argument uses the appeal to the beliefs of a group of experts, it takes on the form of an appeal to authority; if the appeal relates to the beliefs of a group of respected elders or the members of one's community over a long time, then it takes on the form of an [appeal to tradition](wikipedia://en/173387).

### Scholarship {#Scholarship}

The philosopher [Irving Copi](wikipedia://en/4352005) defined *argumentum ad populum* differently from an appeal to popular opinion itself,[^o19] as an attempt to rouse the "emotions and enthusiasms of the multitude".[^o20]

[Douglas N. Walton](wikipedia://en/5725671) argues that appeals to popular opinion can be logically valid in some cases, such as in political dialogue within a [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).[^n1]

## Reversals {#Reversals}

In some circumstances, a person may argue that the fact that Y people believe X to be true implies that X is *false*. This line of thought is closely related to the [appeal to spite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal_to_spite) fallacy given that it invokes a person's contempt for the general populace or something about the general populace to persuade them that most are wrong about X. This *ad populum* reversal commits the same logical flaw as the original fallacy given that the idea "X is true" is inherently separate from the idea that "Y people believe X": "Y people believe in X as true, purely because Y people believe in it, and not because of any further considerations. Therefore X must be false." While Y people can believe X to be true for fallacious reasons, X might still be true. Their motivations for believing X do not affect whether X is true or false.

Y = most people, a given quantity of people, people of a particular demographic.

X = a statement that can be true or false.

Examples:

- "Are you going to be a [mindless conformist drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformity) drinking milk and water like everyone else, or will you wake up and drink my product?"
- "Everyone likes [The Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beatles) and that probably means that they didn't have nearly as much talent as \<Y band>, which didn't [sell out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selling_out)."
- "The [German people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) today consists of the [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz) [generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation), with every person in power being guilty in some way. How on earth can we buy the generally held propaganda that the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) is imperialistic and totalitarian? Clearly, it must not be."
- "Everyone loves \<A actor>. \<A actor> must be nowhere near as talented as the devoted and serious [method actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_actor) that aren't so popular like \<B actor>."

In general, the reversal usually goes: "Most people believe A and B are both true. B is false. Thus, A is false." The similar fallacy of [chronological snobbery](wikipedia://en/2432024) is not to be confused with the *ad populum* reversal. Chronological snobbery is the claim that if belief in both X and Y was popularly held in the past and if Y was recently proved to be untrue then X must also be untrue. That line of argument is based on a belief in historical progress and not—like the *ad populum* reversal is—on whether or not X and/or Y is currently popular.

## Valid uses {#Valid_uses}

Appeals to public opinion are valid in situations where consensus is the determining factor for the validity of a statement, such as linguistic usage and definitions of words.

### Language {#Language}

[Linguistic descriptivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_descriptivist) argue that correct grammar, spelling, and expressions are defined by the language's speakers, especially in languages which do not have a central governing body. According to this viewpoint, if an incorrect expression is commonly used, it becomes correct. In contrast, [linguistic prescriptivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_prescriptivist) believe that incorrect expressions are incorrect regardless of how many people use them.[^o26]

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

[Special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) are [mathematical functions](wikipedia://en/185427) that have well-established names and mathematical notations due to their significance in mathematics and other scientific fields.

There is no formal definition of what makes a function a special function; instead, the term *special function* is defined by consensus. Functions generally considered to be special functions include [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860), [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367), and the [Bessel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_functions).
