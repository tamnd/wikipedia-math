# Naum Meiman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Naum Meiman"}

**Naum Natanovich (Nokhim Sanalevich) Meiman** (Нау́м Ната́нович (Но́хим Са́нелевич) Ме́йман; 12 May 1912, [Bazar, Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazar,_Ukraine) – 31 March 2001, [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv)) was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and [dissident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_dissidents).[^o3] He is known for his work in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), as well as for his dissident activity, in particular, for being a member of the [Moscow Helsinki Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Helsinki_Group).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Bazar, Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazar,_Ukraine) on 12 May 1912.[^o3] In 1932 he graduated from [Kazan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University) as an [extern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extern). In 1937 he submitted his Ph.D. under the supervision of [Nikolai Chebotaryov](wikipedia://en/3033696) and was awarded the degree [Doktor nauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doktor_nauk). In 1939 he became a full professor at [Kazan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University).[^o3]

He worked for two years in the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kharkiv), where he became friends with [Lev Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Landau) with whom he collaborated for many years. After the Second World War, he went to [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) and worked at the [Institute for Physical Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Physical_Problems), where he was a head of the mathematics lab. Then he worked in the [Institute for Theoretical and Experimental Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Theoretical_and_Experimental_Physics). In 1953, he was awarded a [Stalin prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) for his work in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028). He made important contributions in the development of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons) in the USSR.

Starting in 1968, Meiman became active in politics and signed several letters of protest against political trials in the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR).[^o3]

In 1971, he retired and applied for permission to emigrate to Israel. Denied on grounds of knowing state secrets, he soon became a [refusenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik). Gradually he became more active in politics, and was a member of the [Moscow Helsinki Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Helsinki_Group) beginning in 1977. Later he became deputy chairman and the last active free member, writing hundreds of the group's documents. He also participated in a Refusenik scientific seminar. He was permanently under surveillance by the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB), who also bugged his telephone and searched his home.

In 1982, Naum Meiman and [Andrei Dmitrievich Sakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Dmitrievich_Sakharov) published a letter in defence of [Yuri Fyodorovich Orlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Fyodorovich_Orlov).[^o5]

Meiman also struggled for the right of his wife [Inna Meiman-Kitrossky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inna_Meiman-Kitrossky) to go to the USA for medical treatment since she had been diagnosed with cancer. After several years of struggle, she was allowed to go to the US and she died in February 1987 in [Georgetown (Washington, D.C.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_%28Washington,_D.C.%29).[^o6][^o7][^o8] Meiman was not allowed to attend her funeral in Washington D.C.[^o9][^o10]

In 1988 Meiman was finally allowed to emigrate to Israel, where he became a professor emeritus in [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University). In 1992, in Tel-Aviv, there was a conference in his honor dedicated to his 80th birthday. Meiman died there in 2001.[^o3]
