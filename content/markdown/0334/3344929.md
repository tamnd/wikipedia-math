# Nicolaas Kuiper

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolaas Kuiper"}

**Nicolaas Hendrik Kuiper** (; 28 June 1920 – 12 December 1994) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for [Kuiper's test](wikipedia://en/16720) and proving [Kuiper's theorem](wikipedia://en/2993692). He also contributed to the [Nash embedding theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_embedding_theorem).

:::

Kuiper studied at University of Leiden in 1937-41, and worked as a secondary school teacher of mathematics in [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht) in 1942-47. He completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) from the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden) in 1946 under the supervision of [Willem van der Woude](wikipedia://en/47912777).[^o3] In 1947 he came to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) at the invitation of Oscar Veblen, where he stayed at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) for one year as Veblen's assistant, and the second year as member of the IAS, meeting [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), and he also went to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) at Ann Arbor. In February to June 1954, he went for a second time to Ann Arbor where he met [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) and his student [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294). In 1950 he was appointed professor of mathematics (and statistics) at the [Agricultural University of Wageningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wageningen_University_and_Research).

In 1957, he was notably one of the six participants to the first [Arbeitstagung](wikipedia://en/1186588), an informal seminar animated by [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495), which later became very popular among mathematicians; he saw at this occasion [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) presenting his first revolutionary works in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o4] In 1960 he visited [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in Evanston for half a year.

He became professor of pure mathematics at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) in 1962. In 1969-70 he made a second visit at the Institute for Advanced Study. At his return from [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), he gave a talk at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) organised in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice),[^o5] during which he was appointed in the executive committee of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) for 1971–1975.[^o6] He finally served as director of the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) from 1971 until his retirement in 1985, then stayed there as a long-term visitor for six years.[^o7] In 1990, he was appointed chairman of the program committee of the International Congress of Mathematicians held at [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto).[^o8] In 1991, he returned to the Netherlands to live in [Heteren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteren), and continued to participate in mathematical colloquia at the [University of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utrecht).[^o9]
