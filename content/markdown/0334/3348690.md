# Alexander Aitken

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Aitken"}

**Alexander Craig Aitken** FRS FRSNZ (1 April 1895–3 November 1967) was one of [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand)'s most eminent mathematicians.[^o5] In a 1935 paper he introduced the concept of [generalized least squares](wikipedia://en/4504135), along with now standard [vector/matrix notation](wikipedia://en/20556859) for the [linear regression model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_regression_model).[^o7] Another influential paper co-authored with his student [Harold Silverstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Silverstone) established the lower bound on the variance of an [estimator](wikipedia://en/10043),[^o8] now known as [Cramér–Rao bound](wikipedia://en/581124).[^o9] He was elected to the [Royal Society of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Literature) for his [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) memoir, *Gallipoli to the Somme*.[^o10]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Aitken was born on 1 April 1895 in [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin), the eldest of the seven children of Elizabeth Towers and William Aitken. He was of Scottish descent, his grandfather having emigrated from [Lanarkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanarkshire) in 1868. His mother was from [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton).[^o12]

He was educated at [Otago Boys' High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otago_Boys'_High_School) in Dunedin (1908–13) where he was school [dux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dux) and won the Thomas Baker Calculus Scholarship in his last year at school. He saw active service during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) enlisting in April 1915 with the [New Zealand Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Expeditionary_Force), and serving in [Gallipoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallipoli_Campaign) from November 1915, in Egypt, and at the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_%28World_War_I%29). He was seriously wounded at the [Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Somme).[^o13] He spent several months in hospital in [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_London) before being invalided out of the army and shipped home to New Zealand in March 1917.[^o12]

Resuming his studies Aitken graduated with an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree from the [University of Otago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Otago) in 1920, then worked as a schoolmaster at Otago Boys' High School from 1920 to 1923.

Aitken studied for a doctorate ([PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy)) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), in Scotland, under [Edmund Taylor Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Taylor_Whittaker) where his dissertation, "The Graduation of Observational Data", was considered so impressive that he was awarded a [DSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degree in 1925.[^o14][^o5] Aitken's impact at the university had been so great that he had been elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (FRSE) the year before the award of his degree, upon the proposal of [Sir Edmund Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Edmund_Whittaker), [Sir Charles Galton Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Charles_Galton_Darwin), [Edward Copson](wikipedia://en/8918906) and [David Gibb](wikipedia://en/50969533). In 1927, Aitken published what became known as [Aitken delta-squared process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitken_delta-squared_process) as part of an extension to [Bernoulli's method](wikipedia://en/79735921).[^o15][^o16] Aitken was awarded the Makdougall-Brisbane Prize for 1930–32, and was active in the affairs of the RSE, serving as Councillor (1934–36), Secretary to Ordinary Meetings (1936–40), and vice-president (1948–51; 1956–59). He was also an active member of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) and a Fellow of the [Faculty of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/2380461).

Aitken spent his entire career at the University of Edinburgh, working as lecturer in Actuarial Mathematics & Statistics (1925–36), Reader in Statistics (1936–46), and finally Professor of Mathematics (1946–65).

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he worked in [Hut 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_6) [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) decrypting [ENIGMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENIGMA) code.[^o17]

Aitken was one of the best [mental calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_calculator) known, and had a prodigious memory. This ability was researched by the psychologist [Ian M.L. Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_M.L._Hunter). He knew the first 1000 digits of $\pi$, the 96 recurring digits of 1/97, and memorised the *[Aeneid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeneid)* in high school. However, his inability to forget the horrors he witnessed in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) led to recurrent [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder) throughout his life.[^o5]

Aitken was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 1936[^o3] and an [Honorary Fellow of the Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand) (Hon FRSNZ) in 1940,[^o18] both for his work in statistics, [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He was an accomplished writer, being elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Literature) (FRSL) in 1964 in response to the publication of his war memoirs, *Gallipoli to the Somme*.[^o19] His book was the basis of an [oratorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oratorio) of the same name by the New Zealand composer [Anthony Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ritchie).[^o20] He was also an excellent musician, being described by [Eric Fenby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Fenby) as the most accomplished amateur musician he had ever known, and was a champion athlete in his younger days.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

The [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society) and [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) [Aitken Lectureship](wikipedia://en/75557217) occurs every two years (in odd-numbered years) when a mathematician from New Zealand is invited by both Societies to give lectures at different universities around the UK over a period of several weeks.[^o22][^o23]

An annual "Aitken Prize" is awarded by the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society) for the best student talk at their colloquium. The prize was inaugurated in 1995 at the [University of Otago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Otago)'s Aitken Centenary Conference, a joint mathematics and statistics conference held to remember Aitken 100 years after his birth.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He married [Winifred Betts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winifred_Betts), a lecturer in biology and the first female lecturer appointed to the University of Otago, in 1920. They had a daughter and a son. Aitken died on 3 November 1967, in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh).
