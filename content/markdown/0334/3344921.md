# Positive and negative parts

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Positive and negative parts"}

*Image: Positive and Negative Parts of f(x) = x^2 - 4.png.* Positive and Negative Parts of $f(x) = x^{2} - 4$

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **positive part** of a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [extended real](wikipedia://en/51698)-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) is defined by the formula

$$f^+(x) = \max(f(x),0) = \begin{cases} f(x) & \text{ if } f(x) > 0 \\ 0 & \text{ otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

:::

Intuitively, the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of $f^+$ is obtained by taking the graph of $f$, 'chopping off' the part under the $x$-axis, and letting $f^+$ take the value zero there.

Similarly, the **negative part** of $f$ is defined as

$$f^-(x) = \max(-f(x),0) = -\min(f(x),0) = \begin{cases} -f(x) & \text{ if } f(x) < 0 \\ 0 & \text{ otherwise} \end{cases}$$

Note that both $f^{+}$ and $f^{-}$ are non-negative functions. A peculiarity of terminology is that the 'negative part' is not negative (like the imaginary part of a [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) is not imaginary).

The function $f$ can be expressed in terms of $f^{+}$ and $f^{-}$ as

$$f = f^+ - f^-.$$

Also note that

$$|f| = f^+ + f^-.$$

Using these two equations one may express the positive and negative parts as

$$\begin{aligned} f^+ &= \frac{|f| + f}{2} \\ f^- &= \frac{|f| - f}{2}. \end{aligned}$$

Another representation, using the [Iverson bracket](wikipedia://en/1721092) is

$$\begin{aligned} f^+ &= [f>0]f \\ f^- &= -[f<0]f. \end{aligned}$$

One may define the positive and negative part of any function with values in a [linearly ordered group](wikipedia://en/349014).

The unit [ramp function](wikipedia://en/5092636) is the positive part of the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069).

## Measure-theoretic properties {#Measure-theoretic_properties}

Given a [measurable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra) $(X, \Sigma)$, an extended real-valued function $f$ is [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its positive and negative parts are. Therefore, if such a function $f$ is measurable, so is its absolute value $\lvert f\rvert$, being the sum of two measurable functions. The converse, though, does not necessarily hold: for example, taking $f$ as

$$f = 1_V - \frac{1}{2},$$

where $V$ is a [Vitali set](wikipedia://en/424189), it is clear that $f$ is not measurable, but its absolute value is, being a constant function.

The positive part and negative part of a function are used to define the [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288) for a real-valued function. Analogously to this decomposition of a function, one may decompose a [signed measure](wikipedia://en/2985811) into positive and negative parts — see the [Hahn decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/2985858).
