# Leo Koenigsberger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Koenigsberger"}

**Leo Koenigsberger** (15 October 1837 – 15 December 1921) was a German mathematician and [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science). He is best known for his three-volume biography[^o3] of [Hermann von Helmholtz](wikipedia://en/53311), which remains the standard reference on the subject.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Leo Koenigsberger was born in 1837 in Posen (now [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poznań), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)) to the wealthy merchant Jakob Löb (1800–1882) and Henriette Kantorowicz (1810–1895) and had eleven siblings.[^o5] He studied at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) with [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), where he taught mathematics and physics (1860–64). He taught at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald) (assistant professor, 1864–66; professor, 1866–69), the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) (1869–75), the [Technische Universität Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Dresden) (1875–77), and the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) (1877–84) before returning to [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg) in 1884, where remained until his retirement in 1914.[^n1]

Whilst at Heidelberg, he was elected to honorary membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society) on 17 April 1895.[^o7]

In 1904 he was a Plenary Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Heidelberg.[^o8] In 1919 he published his autobiography, *Mein Leben* ('My Life'). The biography of Helmholtz was published in 1902 and 1903. He also wrote a biography of [C. G. J. Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^n1]

Koenigsberger's own research was primarily on [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). He worked closely with [Lazarus Fuchs](wikipedia://en/2817087), a childhood friend.[^n1]

In 1873, he married in Heidelberg Sophie Beral-Kappel, daughter of the merchant Iwanowitsch Beral of Kharkov and Anna Grigoriewna Tschaikowski. They had a son and a daughter, [Johann Koenigsberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Koenigsberger) (1874), professor of physics in Freiburg, and Ani Koenigsberger (1876).[^o5][^o11]
