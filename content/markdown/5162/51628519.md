# CECPQ1

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CECPQ1"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **CECPQ1** (**combined elliptic-curve and post-quantum 1**) is a [post-quantum](wikipedia://en/26605226) [key-agreement protocol](wikipedia://en/63944) developed by [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) as a limited experiment[^o2] for use in [Transport Layer Security](wikipedia://en/187813) (TLS) by [web browsers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser).[^o4] It was succeeded by [CECPQ2](wikipedia://en/61861645).

:::

## Details {#Details}

CECPQ1 was designed to test algorithms that can provide confidentiality even against an attacker who possesses a large [quantum computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_computer). It is a [key-agreement algorithm](wikipedia://en/63944) for TLS that combines [X25519](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X25519) and [NewHope](wikipedia://en/62068372), a [ring learning with errors](wikipedia://en/45588368) primitive. Even if NewHope were to turn out to be compromised, the parallel X25519 key-agreement ensures that CECPQ1 provides at least the security of existing connections.[^o4]

It was available in [Google Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome) 54 beta.[^o5] In 2016, its experimental use in Chrome ended and it was planned to be disabled in a later Chrome update.[^o6]

It was succeeded by CECPQ2.
