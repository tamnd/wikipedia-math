# Anna Mullikin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anna Mullikin"}

**Anna Margaret Mullikin** (1893–1975) was an American mathematician who was one of the early investigators of [point set theory](wikipedia://en/6233). She was one of the few women to earn a PhD in math before World War II.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Anna Margaret Mullikin was born in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), Maryland, March 7, 1893, as the youngest of four children of Sophia Ridgely (Battee) and William Lawrence Mullikin. Anna received her BA from [Goucher College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goucher_College), in [Towson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towson,_Maryland), Maryland, in 1915. After she graduated Mullikin taught in private schools including [Science Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Hill_School_%28Shelbyville,_Kentucky%29) in [Shelbyville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelbyville,_Kentucky), Kentucky, (1915–1917) and at [Mary Baldwin Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Baldwin_University) in [Staunton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staunton,_Virginia), Virginia, 1917–1918.[^o5]

Mullikin went on to attend [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia) for doctoral work. She was [Robert Lee Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133)'s third student, graduating with her PhD in 1922 with a dissertation titled *Certain Theorems Relating to Plane Connected Point Sets*. Her dissertation was published that year in [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)[^o7] and subsequently became the catalyst for significant advances in the field of topology.[^o3] Her main result was initially referred to as "Miss Mullikin's Theorem" but by 1928 it was called the "Janiszewski-Mullikin theorem."[^o5]

During 1921–1922, while studying for her doctorate, she taught at [Oak Lane Country Day School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Lane_Day_School), which served preschool and elementary-aged children in Pennsylvania.[^o8]

As a PhD, she spent her career as a secondary school mathematics teacher, beginning at [William Penn High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn_High_School_%28Philadelphia%29) in Philadelphia for one year (1922–1923) before moving to [Germantown High School (Philadelphia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germantown_High_School_%28Philadelphia%29). There she became a mentor to [Mary-Elizabeth Hamstrom](wikipedia://en/62619108), who would go on to become a student of Moore and professional mathematician herself.[^o8] At Germantown High School she was named department head of the mathematics in 1952, and she remained there until her retirement in 1959.[^o5]

Anna Mullikin died August 24, 1975, in Philadelphia at 82 and was interred in [Mt. Olivet Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olivet_Cemetery_%28Baltimore%29) in Baltimore, Maryland.[^o5]
