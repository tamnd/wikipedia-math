# Stufe (algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stufe"}

In [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Stufe** (; German: "level") *s*(*F*) of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* is the least number of squares that sum to −1. If −1 cannot be written as a sum of squares, *s*(*F*) = $\infty$. In this case, *F* is a [formally real field](wikipedia://en/1067223). [Albrecht Pfister](wikipedia://en/35551178) proved that the Stufe, if finite, is always a power of 2, and that conversely every power of 2 occurs.[^n1]

:::

## Powers of 2 {#Powers_of_2}

If $s(F)\ne\infty$ then $s(F)=2^k$ for some [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $k$.[^n1][^n2]

*Proof:* Let $k \in \mathbb N$ be chosen such that $2^k \leq s(F) < 2^{k+1}$. Let $n = 2^k$. Then there are $s = s(F)$ elements $e_1, \ldots, e_s \in F\setminus\{0\}$ such that

$$0 = \underbrace{1 + e_1^2 + \cdots + e_{n-1}^2 }_{=:\,a} + \underbrace{e_n^2 + \cdots + e_s^2}_{=:\,b}\;.$$

Both $a$ and $b$ are sums of $n$ squares, and $a \ne 0$, since otherwise $s(F)< 2^k$, contrary to the assumption on $k$.

According to the theory of [Pfister forms](wikipedia://en/7204602), the product $ab$ is itself a sum of $n$ squares, that is, $ab = c_1^2 + \cdots + c_n^2$ for some $c_i \in F$. But since $a+b=0$, we also have $-a^2 = ab$, and hence

$$-1 = \frac{ab}{a^2} = \left(\frac{c_1}{a} \right)^2 + \cdots + \left(\frac{c_n}{a} \right)^2,$$

and thus $s(F) = n = 2^k$.

## Positive characteristic {#Positive_characteristic}

Any field $F$ with positive [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) has $s(F) \leq 2$.[^n3]

*Proof:* Let $p = \operatorname{char}(F)$. It suffices to prove the claim for $\mathbb F_p$.

If $p = 2$ then $-1 = 1 = 1^2$, so $s(F)=1$.

If $p>2$ consider the set $S=\{x^2 : x \in \mathbb F_p\}$ of squares. $S\setminus\{0\}$ is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) $2$ in the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $\mathbb F_p^\times$ with $p-1$ elements. Thus $S$ contains exactly $\tfrac{p+1}2$ elements, and so does $-1-S$. Since $\mathbb F_p$ only has $p$ elements in total, $S$ and $-1-S$ cannot be [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359), that is, there are $x,y\in\mathbb F_p$ with $S\ni x^2=-1-y^2\in-1-S$ and thus $-1=x^2+y^2$.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Stufe *s*(*F*) is related to the [Pythagoras number](wikipedia://en/36818246) *p*(*F*) by *p*(*F*) ≤ *s*(*F*) + 1.[^n4] If *F* is not formally real then *s*(*F*) ≤ *p*(*F*) ≤ *s*(*F*) + 1.[^n5][^n6] The additive order of the form (1), and hence the [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent_of_a_group) of the [Witt group](wikipedia://en/5578523) of *F* is equal to 2*s*(*F*).[^n7][^n8]

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The Stufe of a [quadratically closed field](wikipedia://en/36823185) is 1.[^n8]
- The Stufe of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) is $\infty$, 1, 2 or 4 (Siegel's theorem).[^n9] Examples are $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})$.[^n7]
- The Stufe of a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbb{F}_q$ is 1 if *q* ≡ 1 mod 4 and 2 if *q* ≡ 3 mod 4.[^n3][^n8][^n10]
- The Stufe of a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) of odd residue characteristic is equal to that of its residue field. The Stufe of the 2-adic field $\mathbb{Q}_2$ is 4.[^n9]

:::
