# Carlitz exponential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carlitz exponential"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Carlitz exponential** (named after [Leonard Carlitz](wikipedia://en/10356778)) is a [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p* analogue to the usual [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) studied in [real](wikipedia://en/26478) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). It is used in the definition of the [Carlitz module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlitz_module) – an example of a [Drinfeld module](wikipedia://en/5578871).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

We work over the polynomial ring **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*T*\] of one variable over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) **F**<sub>*q*</sub> with *q* elements. The [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28metric_space%29) **C**<sub>∞</sub> of an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of the field **F**<sub>*q*</sub>((*T*<sup>−1</sup>)) of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) in *T*<sup>−1</sup> will be useful. It is a complete and algebraically closed field.

First we need analogues to the [factorials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorials), which appear in the definition of the usual exponential function. For *i* > 0 we define

$$[i] := T^{q^i} - T, \,$$

$$D_i := \prod_{1 \le j \le i} [j]^{q^{i - j}}$$

and *D*<sub>0</sub> := 1. Note that the usual factorial is inappropriate here, since *n*! vanishes in **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*T*\] unless *n* is smaller than the characteristic of **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*T*\].

Using this we define the Carlitz exponential *e*<sub>*C*</sub>:**C**<sub>∞</sub> → **C**<sub>∞</sub> by the convergent sum

$$e_C(x) := \sum_{i = 0}^\infty \frac{x^{q^i}}{D_i}.$$

:::

## Relation to the Carlitz module {#Relation_to_the_Carlitz_module}

The Carlitz exponential satisfies the functional equation

$$e_C(Tx) = Te_C(x) + \left(e_C(x)\right)^q = (T + \tau)e_C(x), \,$$

where we may view $\tau$ as the power of $q$ map or as an element of the ring $F_q(T)\{\tau\}$ of [noncommutative polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_polynomials). By the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of polynomial rings in one variable this extends to a ring homomorphism *ψ*:**F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*T*\]→**C**<sub>∞</sub>{*τ*}, defining a Drinfeld **F**<sub>*q*</sub>\[*T*\]-module over **C**<sub>∞</sub>{*τ*}. It is called the Carlitz module.
