# Moses Schönfinkel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Moses Schönfinkel"}

**Moses Ilyich Schönfinkel** (Моисей Эльевич Шейнфинкель; 29 September 1888 – 1942) was a [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for the invention of [combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Moses Schönfinkel was born on 1888-09-29 in Ekaterinoslav, Russian Empire (now Dnipro, Ukraine)[^n1] to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family. His father was Ilya Girshevich Schönfinkel, a merchant of first guild, who was in the grocery trade; his mother, Maria “Masha” Gertsovna Schönfinkel (née Lurie), came from the prominent [Lurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurie) family. Moses had siblings named Deborah, Natan, Israel and Grigoriy.[^n2] Schönfinkel attended [Novorossiysk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_University) of [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa), studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) under [Samuil Osipovich Shatunovskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuil_Osipovich_Shatunovskii) (1859–1929), who worked in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358). From 1914 to 1924, Schönfinkel was a member of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s group at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).[^n3] On 7 December 1920 he delivered a talk entitled *Elemente der Logik* ("Elements of Logic") to the group where he outlined the concept of [combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848). [Heinrich Behmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Behmann), a member of Hilbert's group, later revised the text and published it in 1924.[^n4] In 1928, Schönfinkel had one other paper published, on special cases of the decision problem (*[Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672)*), which was prepared by [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580).[^n5]

After he left Göttingen, Schönfinkel returned to Moscow. By 1927 he was reported to be mentally ill and in a sanatorium.[^n4][^n5] His later life was spent in poverty, and he died in Moscow some time in 1942. His papers were burned by his neighbors for heating.[^n5]

## Work {#Work}

Schönfinkel developed a formal system that avoided the use of [bound variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_variables). His system was essentially equivalent to a combinatory logic based upon the combinators **B**, **C**, **I**, **K**, **S** and a combinator for a universally quantified **nand** function which he called **U**. Schönfinkel stated that the system could be reduced to just **K**, **S**, and **U** (a colleague stated that **U** could be factored to the end of any expression and thus not always explicitly written) and outlined a proof that a version of this system had the same power as [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic).[^n4]

His paper also showed that functions of two or more arguments could be replaced by functions taking a single argument.[^n6][^n7][^n8] This replacement mechanism simplifies work in both combinatory logic and [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203) and would later be called [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), after [Haskell Curry](wikipedia://en/42182). While Curry attributed the concept to Schönfinkel, it had already been used by [Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) (an example of [Stigler's law](wikipedia://en/682348)).

The complete known published output of Schönfinkel consists of just two papers: his 1924 *On the Building Blocks of Mathematical Logic*, and another, 31-page paper written in 1927 and published 1928, coauthored with Paul Bernays, entitled *Zum Entscheidungsproblem der mathematischen Logik* (*On the Decision Problem of Mathematical Logic*).
