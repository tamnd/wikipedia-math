# Deltoid curve

*Image: Deltoid2.gif.* blue solid 2px black solid 2px red solid 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deltoid curve"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **deltoid curve**, also known as a **tricuspoid curve** or **Steiner curve**, is a [hypocycloid](wikipedia://en/592735) of three [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758). In other words, it is the [roulette](wikipedia://en/846500) (type of [curve](wikipedia://en/89246)) created by a point on the [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of a circle as it [rolls](wikipedia://en/1156993) without slipping along the inside of a circle with three or one-and-a-half times its [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815). It is named after the capital Greek letter [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_%28letter%29) (Δ) which it resembles.

:::

More broadly, a *deltoid* can refer to any closed figure with three vertices connected by curves that are concave to the exterior, making the interior points a non-[convex set](wikipedia://en/6292).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

### Equations {#Equations}

A hypocycloid can be represented (up to [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789) and [translation](wikipedia://en/204682)) by the following [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108)

$$x=(b-a)\cos(t)+a\cos\left(\frac{b-a}at\right) \,$$

$$y=(b-a)\sin(t)-a\sin\left(\frac{b-a}at\right) \, ,$$

where *a* is the radius of the rolling circle, *b* is the radius of the circle within which the aforementioned circle is rolling and *t* ranges from zero to 6$\pi$. (In the illustration above *b = 3a* tracing the deltoid.)

In complex coordinates this becomes

$$z=2ae^{it}+ae^{-2it}.$$

The variable *t* can be eliminated from these equations to give the Cartesian equation

$$(x^2+y^2)^2+18a^2(x^2+y^2)-27a^4 = 8a(x^3-3xy^2),\,$$

so the deltoid is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of degree four. In [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates) this becomes

$$r^4+18a^2r^2-27a^4=8ar^3\cos 3\theta\,.$$

The curve has three singularities, cusps corresponding to $t=0,\, \pm\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The parameterization above implies that the curve is rational which implies it has [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) zero.

A line segment can slide with each end on the deltoid and remain tangent to the deltoid. The point of tangency travels around the deltoid twice while each end travels around it once.

The [dual curve](wikipedia://en/3127321) of the deltoid is

$$x^3-x^2-(3x+1)y^2=0,\,$$

which has a double point at the origin which can be made visible for plotting by an imaginary rotation y ↦ iy, giving the curve

$$x^3-x^2+(3x+1)y^2=0\,$$

with a double point at the origin of the real plane.

:::

## Area and perimeter {#Area_and_perimeter}

The area of the deltoid is $2\pi a^2$ where again *a* is the radius of the rolling circle; thus the area of the deltoid is twice that of the rolling circle.[^o7]

The perimeter (total arc length) of the deltoid is 16*a*.[^o7]

The incircle of the deltoid has radius R/2, this means 3/2 *a*.

## History {#History}

Ordinary [cycloids](wikipedia://en/41638) were studied by [Galileo Galilei](wikipedia://en/29688374) and [Marin Mersenne](wikipedia://en/72947) as early as 1599 but cycloidal curves were first conceived by [Ole Rømer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_Rømer) in 1674 while studying the best form for gear teeth. [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) claims first consideration of the actual deltoid in 1745 in connection with an optical problem.

## Applications {#Applications}

Deltoids arise in several fields of mathematics. For instance:

- The set of complex eigenvalues of [unistochastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unistochastic) matrices of order three forms a deltoid.
- A cross-section of the set of [unistochastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unistochastic) matrices of order three forms a deltoid.
- The set of possible traces of unitary matrices belonging to the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) SU(3) forms a deltoid.
- The intersection of two deltoids parametrizes a family of [complex Hadamard matrices](wikipedia://en/5157854) of order six.
- The set of all [Simson lines](wikipedia://en/3307757) of given triangle, form an [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) in the shape of a deltoid. This is known as the Steiner deltoid or Steiner's hypocycloid after [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) who described the shape and symmetry of the curve in 1856.[^o11]
- The [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of the [area bisectors](wikipedia://en/152547#Triangle_area_bisectors) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is a deltoid (in the broader sense defined above) with vertices at the midpoints of the [medians](wikipedia://en/1072006). The sides of the deltoid are arcs of [hyperbolas](wikipedia://en/14052) that are [asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic) to the triangle's sides.[^o12] <http://www.se16.info/js/halfarea.htm>
- A deltoid was proposed as a solution to the [Kakeya needle problem](wikipedia://en/588260#Kakeya_needle_problem).
