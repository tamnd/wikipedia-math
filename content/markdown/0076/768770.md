# George Kingsley Zipf

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Kingsley Zipf"}

**George Kingsley Zipf** ( ;[^o3] January 7, 1902 – September 25, 1950) was an American [linguist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philologist) who studied [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) occurrences in different [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language).[^o4]

:::

Zipf earned his bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and also studied at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) and the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin).[^o4]

He was chairman of the German department and university lecturer (meaning he could teach any subject he chose) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o4] He worked with [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) and [demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics), and much of his effort can explain properties of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), distribution of income within nations, and many other collections of data.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Zipf's law"}

## Zipf's law {#Zipf's_law}

He is the [eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of [Zipf's law](wikipedia://en/43218), which states that while only a few words are used very often, many or most are used rarely,

$$P_n \sim 1/n^a$$

where *P<sub>n</sub>* is the frequency of a word ranked *n*<sup>th</sup> and the exponent *a* is almost 1. This means that the second item occurs approximately 1/2 as often as the first, and the third item 1/3 as often as the first, and so on. Zipf's discovery of this law in 1935 was one of the first academic studies of [word frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_frequency).[^o8]

Although he originally intended it as a model for linguistics, Zipf later generalized his law to other disciplines. In particular, he observed that the rank vs. frequency distribution of individual incomes in a unified nation approximates this law, and in his 1941 book *National Unity and Disunity*, he theorized that breaks in this "normal curve of income distribution" portend social pressure for change or [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution).

:::
