# Émile Léger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Émile Léger"}

**Émile Léger** (1795–1838) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Leger studied at *Lycée de Mayence* (now [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz) in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), capital of the French department of [Mont-Tonnerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont-Tonnerre) during the [French First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic)), where his father Claude was professor of rhetoric. In 1813 he entered the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique). With other students, he helped defend Paris during the [Hundred Days of Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days) in March 1815, and was decorated for bravery.[^o5] In 1816, he left school to go to [Montmorency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montmorency,_Val-d'Oise) where his father founded an institution to prepare young people for the entrance exams to Paris universities. After his father retired, he managed the institution.[^o4]

Léger only published four papers on mathematics,[^o5] but one of them seems to be the first to recognize the worst case in the [euclidean algorithm](wikipedia://en/10377): when the inputs are proportional to consecutive [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers).[^o4]
