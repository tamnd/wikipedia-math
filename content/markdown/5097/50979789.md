# Decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne"}

In mathematics, especially [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne and Gabber** or **BBDG decomposition** theorem is a set of results concerning the cohomology of [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808). It was originally conjectured by Gelfand and MacPherson.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

### Decomposition for smooth proper maps {#Decomposition_for_smooth_proper_maps}

The first case of the decomposition theorem arises via the [hard Lefschetz theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Lefschetz_theorem) which gives isomorphisms, for a smooth [proper map](wikipedia://en/1190262) $f: X \to Y$ of relative dimension *d* between two projective varieties[^o6]

$$- \cup \eta^i : R^{d-i}f_* (\mathbb Q) \stackrel \cong \to R^{d+i} f_*(\mathbb Q).$$

Here $\eta$ is the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of a [hyperplane section](wikipedia://en/3459767), $f_*$ is the [direct image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_image) (pushforward) and $R^n f_*$ is the *n*-th [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) of the direct image. This derived functor measures the *n*-th cohomologies of $f^{-1}(U)$, for $U \subset Y$. In fact, the particular case when *Y* is a point, amounts to the isomorphism

$$- \cup \eta^i : H^{d-i} (X, \mathbb Q) \stackrel \cong \to H^{d+i} (X, \mathbb Q).$$

This hard Lefschetz isomorphism induces canonical isomorphisms

$$Rf_* (\mathbb Q) \stackrel \cong \to \bigoplus_{i=-d}^{d} R^{d+i} f_*(\mathbb Q)[-d-i].$$

Moreover, the sheaves $R^{d+i} f_* \mathbb Q$ appearing in this decomposition are [local systems](wikipedia://en/3461103), i.e., locally free sheaves of **Q**-vector spaces, which are moreover semisimple, i.e., a direct sum of local systems without nontrivial local subsystems.

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="BBD decomposition"}

### Decomposition for proper maps {#Decomposition_for_proper_maps}

The decomposition theorem generalizes this fact to the case of a proper, but not necessarily smooth map $f: X \to Y$ between varieties. In a nutshell, the results above remain true when the notion of local systems is replaced by [perverse sheaves](wikipedia://en/1714012).

The hard Lefschetz theorem above takes the following form:[^o9][^o10] there is an isomorphism in the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of sheaves on *Y*:

$${}^p H^{-i} (Rf_* \mathbb Q) \cong {}^p H^{+i} (Rf_* \mathbb Q),$$

where $Rf_*$ is the total derived functor of $f_*$ and ${}^p H^i$ is the *i*-th truncation with respect to the *perverse* [t-structure](wikipedia://en/1798923).

Moreover, there is an isomorphism

$$Rf_* IC_X^\bullet \cong \bigoplus_i {}^p H^i (Rf_* IC_X^\bullet)[-i].$$

where the summands are semi-simple perverse-sheaves, meaning they are direct sums of push-forwards of intersection cohomology sheaves.[^o11]

If *X* is not smooth, then the above results remain true when $\mathbb Q[\dim X]$ is replaced by the [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) complex $IC$.[^o9]

## Proofs {#Proofs}

The decomposition theorem was first proved by Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne and Gabber.[^o14] Their proof is based on the usage of weights on l-adic sheaves in positive characteristic. A different proof using [mixed Hodge modules](wikipedia://en/10389277) was given by Saito. A more geometric proof, based on the notion of **semismall maps** was given by de Cataldo and Migliorini.[^o15]

For semismall maps, the decomposition theorem also applies to [Chow motives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chow_motive).[^o16]

## Applications of the theorem {#Applications_of_the_theorem}

### Cohomology of a Rational Lefschetz Pencil {#Cohomology_of_a_Rational_Lefschetz_Pencil}

Consider a rational morphism $f:X \rightarrow \mathbb{P}^1$ from a smooth quasi-projective variety given by $[f_1(x):f_2(x)]$. If we set the vanishing locus of $f_1,f_2$ as $Y$ then there is an induced morphism $\tilde{X} = Bl_Y(X) \to \mathbb{P}^1$. We can compute the cohomology of $X$ from the intersection cohomology of $Bl_Y(X)$ and subtracting off the cohomology from the blowup along $Y$. This can be done using the perverse [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894)

$$E_2^{l,m} = H^l(\mathbb{P}^1; {}^\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{H}^m(IC_{\tilde{X}}^\bullet(\mathbb{Q})) \Rightarrow IH^{l + m}(\tilde{X};\mathbb{Q}) \cong H^{l+m}(X;\mathbb{Q})$$

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Local invariant cycle theorem"}

### Local invariant cycle theorem {#Local_invariant_cycle_theorem}

Let $f : X \to Y$ be a [proper morphism](wikipedia://en/603782) between complex algebraic varieties such that $X$ is smooth. Also, let $y_0$ be a regular value of $f$ that is in an open ball *B* centered at $y$. Then the restriction map

$$\operatorname{H}^*(f^{-1}(y), \mathbb{Q}) = \operatorname{H}^*(f^{-1}(B), \mathbb{Q}) \to \operatorname{H}^*(f^{-1}(y_0), \mathbb{Q})^{\pi_{1, \textrm{loc}}}$$

is surjective, where $\pi_{1, \textrm{loc}}$ is the fundamental group of the intersection of $B$ with the set of regular values of *f*.[^o22]
