# Yvette Amice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yvette Amice"}

**Yvette Amice** (4 June 1936 – 4 July 1993) was a French mathematician who carried out research in the areas of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [$p$-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327).[^o3][^o4] The Amice transform and Amice theorem for *p*-adic functions are named after her.[^o5][^o6] She headed academic societies and was the second woman president of the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205). In addition to her academic work, she promoted recognition of women mathematicians and their achievements.[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born on 4 June 1936, following her father's death from war injuries, she was brought up by her mother, a school teacher, in [Indre et Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indre_et_Loire).[^o3]

Amice studied mathematics at the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles) in [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sèvres), from 1956, earning her [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in 1959.[^o3] She became an assistant at the Faculté des sciences de Paris until 1964, when she completed a state doctorate under the supervision of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) and [Charles Pisot](wikipedia://en/10796294).[^o4] Her dissertation was titled *p-adic interpolation* (Interpolation p-adique).[^o3][^o10] Her doctoral work addressed [*p*-adic zeta functions](wikipedia://en/22059815) and the arithmetic of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375).[^o4] Her procedure (now known as Amice theorem) generalized [Mahler's theorem](wikipedia://en/408111).[^o11]

In 1965, she gave one of the [Peccot Lectures](wikipedia://en/60897406) (for distinguished mathematicians under 30 years old) at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o12]

### Career {#Career}

On completing her doctorate, Amice became [maître de conférences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maître_de_conférences) (lecturer) at the [University of Poitiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poitiers). In 1966, she was appointed professor at the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux) and gave the [Cours Peccot](wikipedia://en/60897406) lecture series at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o10] She was an advisor to Jean Fresnel (1967) and Daniel Barksy (1974).[^o14]

Amice returned to Poitiers in 1968, then in 1970 became one of the founding professors of [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University), where she was vice president from 1978 to 1981. She was director of a seminar on number theory at the university.[^o10]

She participated in a number of significant bodies, including the CCU (1968–1976) and the CBESER (1976–1983). She headed the mathematics training and research unit (UFR) at [Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_VII) (1975.1978), after which she became responsible for all teaching at Paris VII (1978–1981).[^o3]

In 1975 she became president of the [Société mathématique de France](wikipedia://en/1691205).[^o3] She had been one of three vice-presidents to the society in 1974, and was the only woman on the 25-member council. She was the second woman president of the society, after [Marie-Louise Dubreil-Jacotin](wikipedia://en/46727148) in 1952.[^o10] The third woman president, [Mireille Martin-Deschamps](wikipedia://en/64593933), did not achieve that role until 1988.[^o10] As president of the Société mathématique, together with [Georges Poitou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Poitou), she created the [Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques](wikipedia://en/54507572) which was founded in Luminy, Marseille, in the early 1980s.[^o15][^o3] Together with [Jean Giraud](wikipedia://en/572753). she was also instrumental in creating the SCFCIEM, the French subcommission of the [International Commission on Mathematical Instruction](wikipedia://en/8818410).[^o3]

Amice died from cancer in [Passy, Haute-Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passy,_Haute-Savoie), on 4 July 1993, aged 57, following a decade-long illness.[^o10][^o3]

## Advocacy {#Advocacy}

Amice was a supporter of the role of women in science at a time when academic posts were reserved principally for men. She maintained that women scientists and mathematicians should no longer be considered an exception to the general rule. As a result, she actively advocated for the support of women in a number of relevant committees and high-level academic bodies.[^o4]

## Textbook {#Textbook}

Amice was the author of a textbook on the [*p*-adic number system](wikipedia://en/51423), *Les nombres p-adiques* (Presses Universitaires de France, 1975).[^o18] Various reviews at the time of release considered it to be a well-written introduction to [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327) covering discussion of *p*-adic numbers, [non-archimedean fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-archimedean_field) and [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478), and [rationality theorems](wikipedia://en/26426).[^o19]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

In 1963, Amice was awarded the Albert Châtelet Medal for her works in *p*-adic analysis.[^o21]

She was decorated as a knight of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) in January 1991.[^o22]

In 2026, Amice was announced as one of 72 historical women in STEM whose names have been proposed to be added to the [72 men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower) already celebrated on the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower). The plan was conceived by a student and tour guide named Bernard Rigaud and it was taken up by Sorbonne University's President [Nathalie Drach-Temam](wikipedia://en/83485079).[^o23] and it was announced by the Mayor of Paris, [Anne Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) following the recommendations of a committee led by [Isabelle Vauglin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Vauglin) of [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences) and Jean-François Martins, representing the operating company which runs the Eiffel Tower.[^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27]
