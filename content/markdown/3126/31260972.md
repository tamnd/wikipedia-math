# Otto Schilling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Otto Schilling"}

**Otto Franz Georg Schilling** (3 November 1911 – 20 June 1973) was a German-American mathematician known as one of the leading [algebraists](wikipedia://en/18716923) of his time.[^o2]

:::

He was born in [Apolda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apolda) and studied in the 1930s at the [Universität Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universität_Jena) and the [Universität Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universität_Göttingen) under [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896). After Noether was forced to leave Germany by the Nazis, he found a new advisor in [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118),[^o3] and obtained his Ph.D. from [Marburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_University) in 1934 on the thesis *Über gewisse Beziehungen zwischen der Arithmetik hyperkomplexer Zahlsysteme und algebraischer Zahlkörper*.[^o5] He then was [post doc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_doc) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) before moving to [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) 1935–37[^o4] and the [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) 1937–39. He became an instructor with the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1939,[^o3] promoted to assistant professor 1943, associate 1945 and full professor in 1958. In 1961 he moved to [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University). He died in [Highland Park, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_Illinois). His students were, among others, the game theorist [Anatol Rapoport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatol_Rapoport) and the mathematician [Harley Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_Flanders).
