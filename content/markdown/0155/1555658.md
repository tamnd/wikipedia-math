# Digit (unit)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Digit"}

*Image: Coudée-turin detail.jpg.* Detail of the Ancient Egyptian cubit rod in the [Museo Egizio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_Egizio) of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), showing digit, palm, hand and fist lengths

*Image: Hand Units of Measurement.PNG.* Some hand-based measurements, including the digit (6)

The **digit** or **finger** is an ancient and obsolete non-[SI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI) [unit of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Units_of_measure) of [length](wikipedia://en/18542). It was originally based on the breadth of a human finger.[^o17] It was a fundamental unit of length in the [Ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_units_of_measurement), [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Mesopotamian_units_of_measurement), [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Hebrew_units_of_measurement), [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_units_of_measurement) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_units_of_measurement) systems of measurement.

:::

In astronomy a digit is one twelfth of the diameter of the sun or the moon.[^o16]

## History {#History}

### Ancient Egypt {#Ancient_Egypt}

The digit, also called a finger or fingerbreadth, is a unit of measurement originally based on the breadth of a human finger. In Ancient Egypt it was the basic unit of subdivision of the [cubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubit).[^o17]

On surviving [Ancient Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_units_of_measurement) [cubit-rods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measuring_rod#Ancient_Egypt), the [royal cubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubit#Ancient_Egyptian_royal_cubit) is divided into seven [palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_%28length%29) of four digits or fingers each.[^o26] The royal cubit measured approximately 525 mm,[^o22] so the length of the ancient Egyptian digit was about 19 mm.

### Mesopotamia {#Mesopotamia}

In the classical [Akkadian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire) system instituted in about 2250 BC during the reign of [Naram-Sin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naram-Sin_of_Akkad), the finger was one-thirtieth of a cubit length. The cubit was equivalent to approximately 497 mm, so the finger was equal to about 17 mm. Basic length was used in architecture and field division.

### Britain {#Britain}

A **digit** ([lat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language) *digitus*, "finger"), when used as a unit of length, is usually a sixteenth of a [foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_%28unit%29) or 3/4" ([\1.905 cm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_E-2_m) for the international [inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch)).[^o12] The width of an adult human male finger tip is indeed about 2 centimetres. In English this unit has mostly fallen out of use, as do others based on the human arm: [finger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_%28unit%29) (7/6 digit), [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_%28unit%29) (4 digits), [hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_%28unit%29) (16/3 digits), [shaftment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaftment) (8 digits), [span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Span_%28length%29) (12 digits), [cubit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubit) (24 digits) and [ell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ell_%28unit%29) (60 digits).

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

In astronomy a digit is, or was until recently, one twelfth of the diameter of the sun or the moon.[^o16][^o18] This is found in the [Moralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moralia) of [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch), XII:23,[^o24] but the definition as exactly one twelfth of the diameter may be due to [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979). [Sosigenes of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosigenes_of_Alexandria) had observed in the 1st century AD that on a [dioptra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dioptra), a disc with a diameter of 11 or 12 digits (of length) was needed to cover the moon.[^o23]

The unit was used in Arab or Islamic astronomical works such as those of [Ṣadr al‐Sharīʿa al‐Thānī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ṣadr_al‐Sharīʿa_al‐Thānī) (d.1346/7),[^o21] where it is called إصبع *iṣba'*, digit or finger.[^o25]

The astronomical digit was in use in Britain for centuries. Heath, writing in 1760, explains that 12 digits are equal to the diameter in eclipse of the sun, but that 23 may be needed for the Earth's shadow as it eclipses the moon, those over 12 representing the extent to which the Earth's shadow is larger than the Moon.[^o20] The unit is apparently not in current use, but is found in recent dictionaries.[^o18]
