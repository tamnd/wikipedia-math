# Open-label trial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Open-label trial"}

An **open-label trial**, or **open trial**, is a type of [clinical trial](wikipedia://en/241717) in which information is not withheld from trial participants.[^o2] In particular, both the researchers and participants know which treatment is being administered.[^o2] This contrasts with a [double-blinded trial](wikipedia://en/277248), where information is withheld both from the researchers and the participants to reduce bias.[^o3][^o4]

:::

Open-label trials may be appropriate for comparing two similar treatments to determine which is most effective, such as a comparison of different [prescription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescription_drug) [anticoagulants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticoagulant),[^o5] or possible relief from [symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symptom) of some disorders when a [placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo) is given.[^o6]

An open-label trial may still be [randomized](wikipedia://en/163180). Open-label trials may also be uncontrolled (without a [placebo group](wikipedia://en/21017052)), with all participants receiving the same treatment.[^o5] This would be a [single-arm study design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-arm_study_design).
