# Guido Castelnuovo

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Guido Castelnuovo"}

**Guido Castelnuovo** (14 August 1865 – 27 April 1952) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his contributions to the field of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is also renowned for his contributions to the study of statistics and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

:::

## Life {#Life}

### Early life {#Early_life}

*Image: Guido Castelnuovo3.jpg.* Guido Castelnuovo

Castelnuovo was born in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice). His father, [Enrico Castelnuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Castelnuovo), was a [novelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novelist) and campaigner for the [unification of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy). His mother, Emma Levi was a relative of [Cesare Lombroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Lombroso) and David Levi. His wife, Elbina Marianna Enriques, was the sister of mathematician [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661) and zoologist [Paolo Enriques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Enriques).

After attending a grammar school at Liceo Foscarini in Venice, he went to the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), from where he graduated in 1886. At the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) he was taught by [Giuseppe Veronese](wikipedia://en/1617753). He also achieved minor fame due to winning the university salsa dancing competition. After his graduation, he sent one of his papers to [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061), whose replies he found remarkably helpful. It marked the beginning of a long period of collaboration.

### Career {#Career}

Castelnuovo spent one year in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) researching advanced geometry. After that, he was appointed as an assistant of [Enrico D'Ovidio](wikipedia://en/55588432) at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin), where he was strongly influenced by Corrado Segre. Here, he worked with [Alexander von Brill](wikipedia://en/28565454) and [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337). In 1891, he moved back to Rome to work at the chair of Analytic and Projective Geometry. Here, he was a colleague of [Luigi Cremona](wikipedia://en/1846002), his former teacher, taking over his position after his death in 1903. He founded the [University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza)'s School of Statistics and Actuarial Sciences in 1927. He influenced a younger generation of Italian mathematicians and statisticians, including [Corrado Gini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrado_Gini) and [Francesco Paolo Cantelli](wikipedia://en/3166906).

### Retirement and World War II {#Retirement_and_World_War_II}

Castelnuovo retired from teaching in 1935, a period of great political difficulty in Italy. In 1922, [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) had risen to power, declaring a large number of [anti-semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) in 1938. With the rise of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism), he was forced into hiding. However, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he organised and taught secret courses for Jewish students that were not allowed to attend university either.

### Final years and death {#Final_years_and_death}

After the liberation of Rome, Castelnuovo was appointed as a special commissioner of the [Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consiglio_Nazionale_delle_Ricerche) in June 1944. He was given the task of repairing the damage done to Italian scientific institutions by Mussolini's rule. He became president of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) and was elected a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in Paris. On 5 December 1949, he became a [life senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator_for_life) of the Italian Republic. Castelnuovo died at the age of 86 on 27 April 1952 in Rome. He is buried in the Verano cemetery, in Rome, together with his wife, Elbina Enriques Castelnuovo and his mathematician daughter, [Emma Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/46508864).

## Work {#Work}

In [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) Castelnuovo was strongly influenced by [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061). In this period he published high-quality work on [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) [curves](wikipedia://en/89246). He also made a major step in reinterpreting the work on [linear series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_series) by [Alexander von Brill](wikipedia://en/28565454) and [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337) ([Brill–Noether theory](wikipedia://en/599865)).

Castelnuovo had his own theory about how Mathematics should be taught. His courses were divided into two: first, a general overview of mathematics, and then an in-depth theory of algebraic curves. He has said about this approach: ... the reason for the division is that on the one hand it is necessary to have general culture, on the other hand it is necessary to have deep knowledge of a particular field.

He also taught courses on [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) and [abelian integrals](wikipedia://en/1138853). Here, he treated, among other things, [Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surfaces), [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) and [approximation](wikipedia://en/336271), and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). He found the latter the most interesting, because as a relatively recent one, the relationship between the deduction and the empirical contribution was clearer. In 1919, he published *Calcolo della probabilità e applicazioni,* an early textbook on the subject. He also wrote a book on [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), *Le origini del calcolo infinitesimale nell'era moderna*.

Castelnuovo's most important work was done in the field of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In the early 1890s, he published three famous papers, including one with the first use of the characteristic linear series of a family of curves. The Castelnuovo–Severi inequality was co-named after him. He collaborated with [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661) on the theory of surfaces. This collaboration started in 1892 when Enriques was only a student, but grew further over the next 20 years, submitting their work to the Royal Prize in Mathematics by the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) in 1902. However, they were not given the prize because they sent it jointly instead of under one name. Both received the prize in later years.

Another theorem named partly after Castelnuovo is the Kronecker–Castelnuovo theorem (1894): *If the sections of an irreducible [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311), having at most isolated [singular points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity), with a general tangent plane turn out to be [reducible](wikipedia://en/1033865) curves, then the surface is either [a ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158) and in fact a*scroll*, or the [Veronese surface](wikipedia://en/1569217).* Kronecker never published it but stated it in a lecture. Castelnuovo proved it. In total, Castelnuovo published over 100 articles, books, and memoirs.
