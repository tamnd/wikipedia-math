# Alex Eskin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alex Eskin"}

**Alex Eskin** (Александр Григорьевич Эскин, born May 19, 1965)[^o3] is an American mathematician. He is the Arthur Holly Compton distinguished service professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o4] His research focuses on [rational billiards](wikipedia://en/2160429) and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Eskin was born in [Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev) on May 19, 1965.[^o4][^o6] He is the son of a Russian-Jewish mathematician [Gregory I. Eskin](wikipedia://en/48460676), a professor at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles). The family emigrated to Israel in 1974 and in 1982 to the United States.[^o7]

Eskin earned his doctorate from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1993, under the supervision of [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak).

Eskin has been a professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) since 1999.[^o14]

## Awards {#Awards}

Eskin gave invited talks at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin in 1998,[^o10] and in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad) in 2010.[^o11]

For his contribution to joint work with [David Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fisher_%28mathematician%29) and [Kevin Whyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Whyte) establishing the [quasi-isometric](wikipedia://en/12747259) rigidity of solvable groups, Eskin was awarded the 2007 [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167).[^o12] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o13] In April 2015, Eskin was elected a member of the United States [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o14][^o15] Eskin won the 2020 [Breakthrough Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakthrough_Prize)[^o16][^o17] in mathematics for his classification of $P$-invariant and stationary measures for the moduli of translation surfaces,[^o18] in joint work with [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655).
