# Summability kernel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Summability kernel"}

In mathematics, a **summability kernel** is a family or sequence of periodic integrable functions satisfying a certain set of properties, listed below. Certain kernels, such as the [Fejér kernel](wikipedia://en/693816), are particularly useful in [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659). Summability kernels are related to [approximation of the identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_of_the_identity); definitions of an approximation of identity vary,[^o2] but sometimes the definition of an approximation of the identity is taken to be the same as for a summability kernel.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. A **summability kernel** is a sequence $(k_n)$ in $L^1(\mathbb{T})$ that satisfies

1. $$\int_\mathbb{T}k_n(t)\,dt=1$$
1. $\int_\mathbb{T}|k_n(t)|\,dt\le M$ (uniformly bounded)
1. $\int_{\delta\le|t|\le\frac{1}{2}}|k_n(t)|\,dt\to0$ as $n\to\infty$, for every $\delta>0$.

Note that if $k_n\ge0$ for all $n$, i.e. $(k_n)$ is a **positive summability kernel**, then the second requirement [follows automatically](wikipedia://en/37531624) from the first.

With the more usual convention $\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, the first equation becomes $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_\mathbb{T}k_n(t)\,dt=1$, and the upper limit of integration on the third equation should be extended to $\pi$, so that the condition 3 above should be

$\int_{\delta\le|t|\le\pi}|k_n(t)|\,dt\to0$ as $n\to\infty$, for every $\delta>0$.

This expresses the fact that the mass concentrates around the origin as $n$ increases.

One can also consider $\mathbb{R}$ rather than $\mathbb{T}$; then (1) and (2) are integrated over $\mathbb{R}$, and (3) over $|t|>\delta$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [Fejér kernel](wikipedia://en/693816)
- The [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583) (continuous index)
- The [Landau kernel](wikipedia://en/72287736)
- The [Dirichlet kernel](wikipedia://en/26611926) is *not* a summability kernel, since it fails the second requirement.

:::

## Convolutions {#Convolutions}

Let $(k_n)$ be a summability kernel, and $*$ denote the [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) operation.

- If $(k_n),f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})$ (continuous functions on $\mathbb{T}$), then $k_n*f\to f$ in $\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T})$, i.e. uniformly, as $n\to\infty$. In the case of the Fejér kernel this is known as [Fejér's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fejér's_theorem).
- If $(k_n),f\in L^1(\mathbb{T})$, then $k_n*f\to f$ in $L^1(\mathbb{T})$, as $n\to\infty$.
- If $(k_n)$ is radially decreasing symmetric and $f\in L^1(\mathbb{T})$, then $k_n*f\to f$ [pointwise a.e.](wikipedia://en/340198#Almost_everywhere_convergence), as $n\to\infty$. This uses the [Hardy–Littlewood maximal function](wikipedia://en/10094198). If $(k_n)$ is not radially decreasing symmetric, but the decreasing symmetrization $\widetilde{k}_n(x):=\sup_{|y|\ge|x|}k_n(y)$ satisfies $\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|\widetilde{k}_n\|_1<\infty$, then a.e. convergence still holds, using a similar argument.
