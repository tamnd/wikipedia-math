# Property P conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Property P conjecture"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **Property P conjecture** is a statement about [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) obtained by [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) on a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) in the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792). A knot in the 3-sphere is said to have **Property P** if every 3-manifold obtained by performing (non-trivial) Dehn surgery on the knot is not [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected).[^o2] The conjecture states that all knots, except the unknot, have Property P.

:::

Research on Property P was started by [R. H. Bing](wikipedia://en/967353), who popularized the name and conjecture.

This conjecture can be thought of as a first step to resolving the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798), since the [Lickorish–Wallace theorem](wikipedia://en/1240641) says any closed, orientable 3-manifold results from Dehn surgery on a link.[^o3] If a knot $K \subset \mathbb{S}^{3}$ has Property P, then one cannot construct a counterexample to the Poincaré conjecture by surgery along $K$.

A proof was announced in 2004, as the combined result of efforts of mathematicians working in several different fields.

## Algebraic Formulation {#Algebraic_Formulation}

Let $[l], [m] \in \pi_{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3} \setminus K)$ denote elements corresponding to a preferred longitude and meridian of a tubular neighborhood of $K$.

$K$ has Property P if and only if its [Knot group](wikipedia://en/1164126) is never trivialised by adjoining a relation of the form $m = l^{a}$ for some $0 \ne a \in \mathbb{Z}$.
