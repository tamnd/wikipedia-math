# Optimal experimental design

*Image: Theb1982.jpg.* [Gustav Elfving](wikipedia://en/31600984) developed the optimal design of experiments, and so minimized surveyors' need for [theodolite measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodolite) *(pictured)*, while trapped in his tent in storm-ridden [Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland).[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Optimal experimental design"}

In the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), **optimal experimental designs** (or **optimum designs**[^o3]) are a class of [experimental designs](wikipedia://en/9541) that are [optimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29) with respect to some [statistical](wikipedia://en/27579) [criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_function). The creation of this field of statistics has been credited to Danish statistician [Kirstine Smith](wikipedia://en/40337435).[^o4][^o5]

:::

In the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541) for [estimating](wikipedia://en/1565926) [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576), **optimal designs** allow parameters to be [estimated without bias](wikipedia://en/8450479) and with [minimum variance](wikipedia://en/1583470). A non-optimal design requires a greater number of [experimental runs](wikipedia://en/10306262) to [estimate](wikipedia://en/1565926) the [parameters](wikipedia://en/2883137) with the same [precision](wikipedia://en/27250866) as an optimal design. In practical terms, optimal experiments can reduce the costs of experimentation.

The optimality of a design depends on the [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) and is assessed with respect to a statistical criterion, which is related to the variance-matrix of the estimator. Specifying an appropriate model and specifying a suitable criterion function both require understanding of [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579) and practical knowledge with [designing experiments](wikipedia://en/9541).

## Advantages {#Advantages}

Optimal designs offer three advantages over sub-optimal [experimental designs](wikipedia://en/9541):[^o7]

1. Optimal designs reduce the costs of experimentation by allowing [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576) to be estimated with fewer experimental runs.
1. Optimal designs can accommodate multiple types of factors, such as process, mixture, and discrete factors.
1. Designs can be optimized when the design-space is constrained, for example, when the mathematical process-space contains factor-settings that are practically infeasible (e.g. due to safety concerns).

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="D-optimal"}

## Minimizing the variance of estimators {#Minimizing_the_variance_of_estimators}

Experimental designs are evaluated using statistical criteria.[^o10]

It is known that the [least squares](wikipedia://en/82359) estimator minimizes the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of [mean](wikipedia://en/9653)-[unbiased](wikipedia://en/8450479) [estimators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimators) (under the conditions of the [Gauss–Markov theorem](wikipedia://en/170353)). In the [estimation](wikipedia://en/36687154) theory for [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576) with one [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [parameter](wikipedia://en/25065), the [reciprocal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_%28mathematics%29) of the variance of an (["efficient"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_efficiency)) estimator is called the "[Fisher information](wikipedia://en/598971)" for that estimator.[^o11] Because of this reciprocity, ***minimizing* the *variance*** corresponds to ***maximizing* the *information***.

When the [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) has several [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065), however, the [mean](wikipedia://en/9653) of the parameter-estimator is a [vector](wikipedia://en/879358) and its [variance](wikipedia://en/191752) is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859). The [inverse matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix) of the variance-matrix is called the "information matrix". Because the variance of the estimator of a parameter vector is a matrix, the problem of "minimizing the variance" is complicated. Using [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579), statisticians compress the information-matrix using real-valued [summary statistics](wikipedia://en/27587); being real-valued functions, these "information criteria" can be maximized.[^o12] The traditional optimality-criteria are [invariants](wikipedia://en/363762) of the [information](wikipedia://en/598971) matrix; algebraically, the traditional optimality-criteria are [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948) of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the information matrix.

- **A**-optimality ("**average**" or **trace**)
  - One criterion is **A-optimality**, which seeks to minimize the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of the [inverse](wikipedia://en/217122) of the information matrix. This criterion results in minimizing the average variance of the estimates of the regression coefficients.
- **C**-optimality
  - This criterion minimizes the variance of a [best linear unbiased estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_linear_unbiased_estimator) of a predetermined linear combination of model parameters.
- **D**-optimality (**determinant**)
  - A popular criterion is **D-optimality**, which seeks to minimize |(X'X)<sup>−1</sup>|, or equivalently maximize the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the [information matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_matrix) X'X of the design. This criterion results in maximizing the [differential Shannon information](wikipedia://en/3504168) content of the parameter estimates.
- **E**-optimality (**eigenvalue**)
  - Another design is **E-optimality**, which maximizes the minimum [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the information matrix.
- **S**-optimality[^o13]
  - This criterion maximizes a quantity measuring the mutual column orthogonality of X and the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the information matrix.
- **T**-optimality
  - This criterion maximizes the discrepancy between two proposed models at the design locations.[^o14]

Other optimality-criteria are concerned with the variance of [predictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictive_inference):

- **G**-optimality
  - A popular criterion is **G-optimality**, which seeks to minimize the maximum entry in the [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251#Matrices) of the [hat matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_matrix) X(X'X)<sup>−1</sup>X'. This has the effect of minimizing the maximum variance of the predicted values.
- **I**-optimality (**integrated**)
  - A second criterion on prediction variance is **I-optimality**, which seeks to minimize the average prediction variance *over the design space*.
- **V**-optimality (**variance**)
  - A third criterion on prediction variance is **V-optimality**, which seeks to minimize the average prediction variance over a set of m specific points.[^o15]

### Contrasts {#Contrasts}

In many applications, the statistician is most concerned with a ["parameter of interest"](wikipedia://en/979528) rather than with ["nuisance parameters"](wikipedia://en/979528). More generally, statisticians consider [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632) of parameters, which are estimated via linear combinations of treatment-means in the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541) and in the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634); such linear combinations are called [contrasts](wikipedia://en/5611564). Statisticians can use appropriate optimality-criteria for such [parameters of interest](wikipedia://en/979528) and for [contrasts](wikipedia://en/5611564).[^o17]

## Implementation {#Implementation}

Catalogs of optimal designs occur in books and in software libraries.

In addition, major [statistical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_statistical_packages) like [SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_System) and [R](wikipedia://en/376707) have procedures for optimizing a design according to a user's specification. The experimenter must specify a [model](wikipedia://en/27576) for the design and an optimality-criterion before the method can compute an optimal design.[^o20]

## Practical considerations {#Practical_considerations}

Some advanced topics in optimal design require more [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579) and practical knowledge in designing experiments.

### Model dependence and robustness {#Model_dependence_and_robustness}

Since the optimality criterion of most optimal designs is based on some function of the information matrix, the 'optimality' of a given design is *[model](wikipedia://en/27576) dependent*: While an optimal design is best for that [model](wikipedia://en/27576), its performance may deteriorate on other [models](wikipedia://en/27576). On other [models](wikipedia://en/27576), an *optimal* design can be either better or worse than a non-optimal design.[^o23] Therefore, it is important to [benchmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmarking) the performance of designs under alternative [models](wikipedia://en/27576).[^o24]

::: {#o26 .statement cue=named-heading name="Choosing an optimality criterion and robustness"}

### Choosing an optimality criterion and robustness {#Choosing_an_optimality_criterion_and_robustness}

The choice of an appropriate optimality criterion requires some thought, and it is useful to benchmark the performance of designs with respect to several optimality criteria. Cornell writes that

since the \[traditional optimality\] criteria . . . are variance-minimizing criteria, . . . a design that is optimal for a given model using one of the . . . criteria is usually near-optimal for the same model with respect to the other criteria.

Indeed, there are several classes of designs for which all the traditional optimality-criteria agree, according to the theory of "universal optimality" of [Kiefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kiefer_%28mathematician%29).[^o28] The experience of practitioners like Cornell and the "universal optimality" theory of Kiefer suggest that robustness with respect to changes in the *optimality-criterion* is much greater than is robustness with respect to changes in the *model*.

#### Flexible optimality criteria and convex analysis {#Flexible_optimality_criteria_and_convex_analysis}

High-quality statistical software provide a combination of libraries of optimal designs or iterative methods for constructing approximately optimal designs, depending on the model specified and the optimality criterion. Users may use a standard optimality-criterion or may program a custom-made criterion.

All of the traditional optimality-criteria are [convex (or concave) functions](wikipedia://en/245568), and therefore optimal-designs are amenable to the mathematical theory of [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) and their computation can use specialized methods of [convex minimization](wikipedia://en/1674411).[^o30] The practitioner need not select *exactly one* traditional, optimality-criterion, but can specify a custom criterion. In particular, the practitioner can specify a convex criterion using the maxima of convex optimality-criteria and [nonnegative combinations](wikipedia://en/19634578) of optimality criteria (since these operations preserve [convex functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_functions)). For *convex* optimality criteria, the [Kiefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kiefer_%28mathematician%29)-[Wolfowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Wolfowitz) [equivalence theorem](https://books.google.com/books?id=5ZcfDZUJ4F8C&dq=Kiefer+Wolfowitz&pg=PA212) allows the practitioner to verify that a given design is globally optimal.[^o31] The [Kiefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kiefer_%28mathematician%29)-[Wolfowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Wolfowitz) [equivalence theorem](https://books.google.com/books?id=5ZcfDZUJ4F8C&dq=Kiefer+Wolfowitz&pg=PA212) is related with the [Legendre](wikipedia://en/390273)-[Fenchel](wikipedia://en/3077734) [conjugacy](wikipedia://en/1001490) for [convex functions](wikipedia://en/245568).[^o32]

If an optimality-criterion lacks [convexity](wikipedia://en/5781226), then finding a [global optimum](wikipedia://en/563854) and verifying its optimality often are difficult.

:::

### Model uncertainty and Bayesian approaches {#Model_uncertainty_and_Bayesian_approaches}

::: {#o35 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Optimal design model selection"}

#### Model selection {#Model_selection}

When scientists wish to test several theories, then a statistician can design an experiment that allows optimal tests between specified models. Such "discrimination experiments" are especially important in the [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) supporting [pharmacokinetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacokinetics) and [pharmacodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacodynamics), following the work of [Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_R._Cox) and Atkinson.[^o36]

:::

#### Bayesian experimental design {#Bayesian_experimental_design}

When practitioners need to consider multiple [models](wikipedia://en/27576), they can specify a [probability-measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on the models and then select any design maximizing the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of such an experiment. Such probability-based optimal-designs are called optimal [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/49571) [designs](wikipedia://en/1706860). Such [Bayesian designs](wikipedia://en/1706860) are used especially for [generalized linear models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_linear_models) (where the response follows an [exponential-family](wikipedia://en/339174) distribution).[^o38]

The use of a [Bayesian design](wikipedia://en/1706860) does not force statisticians to use [Bayesian methods](wikipedia://en/49571) to analyze the data, however. Indeed, the "Bayesian" label for probability-based experimental-designs is disliked by some researchers.[^o39] Alternative terminology for "Bayesian" optimality includes "on-average" optimality or "population" optimality.

## Iterative experimentation {#Iterative_experimentation}

Scientific experimentation is an iterative process, and statisticians have developed several approaches to the optimal design of sequential experiments.

### Sequential analysis {#Sequential_analysis}

[Sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530) was pioneered by [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430).[^o42] In 1972, [Herman Chernoff](wikipedia://en/1181131) wrote an overview of optimal sequential designs,[^o43] while [adaptive designs](wikipedia://en/65297106) were surveyed later by S. Zacks.[^o44] Of course, much work on the optimal design of experiments is related to the theory of [optimal decisions](wikipedia://en/16414027), especially the [statistical decision theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_decision_theory) of [Abraham Wald](wikipedia://en/1239430).[^o45]

### Response-surface methodology {#Response-surface_methodology}

Optimal designs for [response-surface models](wikipedia://en/4481194) are discussed in the textbook by Atkinson, Donev and Tobias, and in the survey of Gaffke and Heiligers and in the mathematical text of Pukelsheim. The [blocking](wikipedia://en/1822339) of optimal designs is discussed in the textbook of Atkinson, Donev and Tobias and also in the monograph by Goos.

The earliest optimal designs were developed to estimate the parameters of regression models with continuous variables, for example, by [J. D. Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Diaz_Gergonne) in 1815 (Stigler). In English, two early contributions were made by [Charles S. Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) and [Kirstine Smith](https://web.archive.org/web/20090810040534/http://www.webdoe.cc/publications/kirstine.php).

Pioneering designs for multivariate [response-surfaces](wikipedia://en/4481194) were proposed by [George E. P. Box](wikipedia://en/2741338). However, Box's designs have few optimality properties. Indeed, the [Box–Behnken design](wikipedia://en/11086228) requires excessive experimental runs when the number of variables exceeds three.[^o47] Box's ["central-composite" designs](wikipedia://en/6252669) require more experimental runs than do the optimal designs of Kôno.[^o48]

### System identification and stochastic approximation {#System_identification_and_stochastic_approximation}

The optimization of sequential experimentation is studied also in [stochastic programming](wikipedia://en/501898) and in [systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_analysis) and [control](wikipedia://en/7039). Popular methods include [stochastic approximation](wikipedia://en/8979437) and other methods of [stochastic optimization](wikipedia://en/7325543). Much of this research has been associated with the subdiscipline of [system identification](wikipedia://en/552466).[^o50] In computational [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), D. Judin & A. Nemirovskii and [Boris Polyak](https://web.archive.org/web/20071031081747/http://www.ipu.ru/labs/lab7/eng/staff/polyak.htm) has described methods that are more efficient than the ([Armijo-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armijo_rule)) [step-size rules](wikipedia://en/9891368#Step_size_rules) introduced by [G. E. P. Box](wikipedia://en/2741338) in [response-surface methodology](wikipedia://en/4481194).[^o51]

[Adaptive designs](wikipedia://en/65297106) are used in [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trials), and optimal [adaptive designs](wikipedia://en/65297106) are surveyed in the *Handbook of Experimental Designs* chapter by Shelemyahu Zacks.

## Specifying the number of experimental runs {#Specifying_the_number_of_experimental_runs}

### Using a computer to find a good design {#Using_a_computer_to_find_a_good_design}

There are several methods of finding an optimal design, given an *a priori* restriction on the number of experimental runs or replications. Some of these methods are discussed by Atkinson, Donev and Tobias and in the paper by Hardin and [Sloane](wikipedia://en/400062). Of course, fixing the number of experimental runs *a priori* would be impractical. Prudent statisticians examine the other optimal designs, whose number of experimental runs differ.

### Discretizing probability-measure designs {#Discretizing_probability-measure_designs}

In the mathematical theory on optimal experiments, an optimal design can be a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) that is [supported](wikipedia://en/6591796) on an infinite set of observation-locations. Such optimal probability-measure designs solve a mathematical problem that neglected to specify the cost of observations and experimental runs. Nonetheless, such optimal probability-measure designs can be [discretized](wikipedia://en/330017) to furnish [approximately](wikipedia://en/336271) optimal designs.[^o55]

In some cases, a finite set of observation-locations suffices to [support](wikipedia://en/6591796) an optimal design. Such a result was proved by Kôno and [Kiefer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kiefer_%28mathematician%29) in their works on [response-surface designs](wikipedia://en/4481194) for quadratic models. The Kôno–Kiefer analysis explains why optimal designs for response-surfaces can have discrete supports, which are very similar as do the less efficient designs that have been traditional in [response surface methodology](wikipedia://en/4481194).[^o56]

## History {#History}

In 1815, an article on optimal designs for [polynomial regression](wikipedia://en/21893202) was published by [Joseph Diaz Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Diaz_Gergonne), according to [Stigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_M._Stigler).

[Charles S. Peirce](wikipedia://en/6117) proposed an economic theory of scientific experimentation in 1876, which sought to maximize the precision of the estimates. Peirce's optimal allocation immediately improved the accuracy of gravitational experiments and was used for decades by Peirce and his colleagues. In his 1882 published lecture at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), Peirce introduced experimental design with these words:

> Logic will not undertake to inform you what kind of experiments you ought to make in order best to determine the acceleration of gravity, or the value of the Ohm; but it will tell you how to proceed to form a plan of experimentation. \[....\] Unfortunately practice generally precedes theory, and it is the usual fate of mankind to get things done in some boggling way first, and find out afterward how they could have been done much more easily and perfectly.[^o58]

[Kirstine Smith](wikipedia://en/40337435) proposed optimal designs for polynomial models in 1918. (Kirstine Smith had been a student of the Danish statistician [Thorvald N. Thiele](wikipedia://en/701256) and was working with [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) in London.)

For several parameters, the covariance-matrices and information-matrices are elements of the convex cone of nonnegative-definite symmetric matrices in a [partially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) [ordered vector space](wikipedia://en/8099221), under the [Loewner](wikipedia://en/9337483) (Löwner) order. This cone is closed under matrix-matrix addition, under matrix-inversion, and under the multiplication of positive real-numbers and matrices. An exposition of matrix theory and the Loewner-order appears in Pukelsheim.
