# Wilhelm Traugott Krug

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilhelm Traugott Krug"}

*Image: Wilhelm Traugott Krug.jpg.* Wilhelm Traugott Krug

**Wilhelm Traugott Krug** (22 June 1770 – 12 January 1842) was a German philosopher and writer. He is considered to be part of the [Kantian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantian) school of logic.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Krug was born on June 22, 1770 in [Radis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radis) near [Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittenberg) to a farming family.[^o4] He studied at the [University of Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wittenberg) under [Franz Volkmar Reinhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Volkmar_Reinhard) and Karl Gottfried Jehnichen, at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) under [Karl Leonhard Reinhold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Leonhard_Reinhold), and at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^n1] After finishing his studies, he was employed as an [adjunct professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunct_professor) at the University of Wittenberg.[^o4]

From 1801 to 1804, Krug was professor of philosophy at [Frankfurt (Oder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_%28Oder%29), after which he succeeded [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant) in the chair of logic and metaphysics at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg). From 1809 till his death he was professor of philosophy at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig).[^n1] He fought in the [War of Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition) (1813–14) as captain of mounted chasseurs. He became Kant's successor at the University of Königsberg after the faculty of philosophy elected him to Kant's chair of logic and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics).[^o7]

He died in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) on January 12, 1842.[^n1]

## Views {#Views}

In philosophy, Krug's method was psychological; he attempted to explain the [Ego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Id,_ego_and_super-ego#Ego) by examining the nature of its reflection upon the facts of consciousness. Being is known to us only through its presentation in [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness); consciousness only in its relation to [Being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being). Both Being and Consciousness, however, are immediately known to us, as also the relation existing between them. By this Transcendental Synthesis he proposed to reconcile [Realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism) and [Idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism), and to destroy the traditional difficulty between transcendental, or pure, thought and things in themselves.[^n1]

Krug challenged Schelling to deduce his quill or pen from [German Idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Idealism)'s [Philosophy of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_Nature). It was part of his empiricist objections to the new idealist philosophy.[^o7][^o11] In so doing, he challenged the thinking that particular, perceptually real things could be logically known from general concepts. It prompted [Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel](wikipedia://en/12598) to issue a critical response and forced him to deal with the issue of the knowledge of singulars.[^o7]

*Beiträge zur Geschichte der Philosophie des XIX. Jahrhunderts* (1835–1838) contains criticisms of Hegel and [Schelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_Joseph_Schelling).[^n2]

Krug was a prolific writer on a great variety of subjects, excelling as a popularizer rather than as an original thinker. His work stimulated freedom of thought in religion and politics,[^n1] and he was a firm supporter of [Jewish emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_emancipation).[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1804, Krug married [Wilhelmine von Zenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmine_von_Zenge) (1780–1852),[^o16] the eldest daughter of a Prussian major-general. They had six children. Wilhelmine had previously been engaged to [Heinrich von Kleist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Kleist).[^o17]

## Principal works {#Principal_works}

- *Briefe über den neuesten Idealism. Eine Fortsetzung der Briefe über die Wissenschaftslehre* (1801)
- *Entwurf eines neuen Organon's der Philosophie oder Versuch über die Principien der philosophischen Erkenntniss* (1801)
- *Fundamentalphilosophie* (1803)
- *System der theoretischen Philosophie* (1806–1810):
  - *Denklehre oder Logik* (1806)
  - *Erkenntnisslehre oder Metaphysik* (1808)
  - *Geschmackslehre oder Aesthetik* (1810)
- *Geschichte der Philosophie alter Zeit, vornehmlich unter Griechen und Römern* (1815; 2nd ed., 1825)
- *System der praktischen Philosophie* (1817–1819):
  - *Dikäologie oder philosophische Rechtslehre* (1817)
  - *Aretologie oder philosophische Tugendlehre* (1818)
  - *Eusebiologie oder philosophische Religionslehre* (1819)
- *Handbuch der Philosophie und der philosophischen Literatur* (1820-1821; 3rd ed., 1828)
- *Meine Lebensreise* (1825, 2nd ed., 1842), autobiography[^n3]
- *Allgemeines Handwörterbuch der philosophischen Wissenschaften* (1827-1834; 2nd ed., 1832-1838)
- *Gesammelte Schriften* (1830-1841; Collected works in 12 volumes):
  - 1-2: *Theologische Schriften* (1830)
  - 3-6: *Politische und juridische Schriften* (1834-1836)
  - 7-9: *Philosophische Schriften* (1839)
  - 10-12: *Enzyklopädische und vermischte Schriften* (1841)
- *Universalphilosophische Vorlesungen für Gebildete beiderlei Geschlechts* (1831)
- Beiträge zur Geschichte der Philosophie des XIX. Jahrhunderts (1835-1838):
  - *Schelling und Hegel oder die neueste Philosophie im Vernichtungskriege mit sich selbst begriffen* (1835)
  - *Ueber das Verhältniß der Philosophie zum gesunden Menschenverstande, zur öffentlichen Meinung und zum Leben selbst, mit besonderer Hinsicht auf Hegel* (1835)
  - *Der hallische Löwe und die marzialischen Philosophen unserer Zeit oder neuester Krieg auf dem Gebiete der Philosophie* (1838)

For a bibliography of Krug's writings see his autobiography *Lebensreise* (1842, pp. 343–360).
