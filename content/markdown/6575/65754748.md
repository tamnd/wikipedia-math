# Irene Moroz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Irene Moroz"}

**Irene Margaret Moroz** is a British [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) whose research interests include [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) including the [Schrödinger–Newton equation](wikipedia://en/1905371), [attractors](wikipedia://en/191094), [synchronization of chaos](wikipedia://en/10867794), and applications to [geophysical fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/28996847), [voice analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_analysis), the [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) of [plankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plankton), and [dynamo theory](wikipedia://en/255217). She is Professor of Mathematics and Applied Mathematics in the [Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford](wikipedia://en/7905850) and Senior Mathematics Fellow at [St Hilda's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hilda's_College,_Oxford).

:::

Moroz was educated at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds), and was formerly affiliated with the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia) before becoming Applied Mathematics Fellow at St. Hilda's in 1992. At the Mathematical Institute, she is group lead for the Mathematical Geoscience Group.

Her work in dynamo theory, with collaborators including [Raymond Hide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Hide) and [Andrew Soward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Soward), involved the derivation of simple systems of coupled differential equations for dynamos such as the [Earth's magnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetic_field), that can model phenomena involving intermittent collapses of these fields.
