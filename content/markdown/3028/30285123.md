# Formally étale morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formally étale morphism"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a morphism is called **formally étale** if it has a [lifting property](wikipedia://en/53659730) that is analogous to being a [local diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/1728483).

:::

## Formally étale homomorphisms of rings {#Formally_étale_homomorphisms_of_rings}

Let *A* be a [topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097), and let *B* be a topological *A*-algebra. Then *B* is **formally étale** if for all [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) *A*-algebras *C*, all [nilpotent ideals](wikipedia://en/6389159) *J* of *C*, and all continuous *A*-homomorphisms *u* : *B* → *C*/*J*, there exists a unique continuous *A*-algebra map *v* : *B* → *C* such that *u* = *pv*, where *p* : *C* → *C*/*J* is the canonical projection.[^o3]

Formally étale is equivalent to [formally smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_smooth) plus [formally unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_unramified).[^o4]

## Formally étale morphisms of schemes {#Formally_étale_morphisms_of_schemes}

Since the [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf) of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) naturally carries only the discrete topology, the notion of formally étale for schemes is analogous to formally étale for the discrete topology for rings. That is, a morphism of schemes *f* : *X* → *Y* is **formally étale** if for every affine *Y*-scheme *Z*, every [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/6389159) [sheaf of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_ideals) *J* on *Z* with *i* : *Z*<sub>0</sub> → *Z* be the closed immersion determined by *J*, and every *Y*-morphism *g* : *Z*<sub>0</sub> → *X*, there exists a unique *Y*-morphism *s* : *Z* → *X* such that *g* = *si*.[^o6]

It is equivalent to let *Z* be any *Y*-scheme and let *J* be a [locally nilpotent](wikipedia://en/3100179) sheaf of ideals on *Z*.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- [Open immersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_immersion) are formally étale.[^o10]
- The property of being formally étale is preserved under composites, base change, and [fibered products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibered_product).[^o11]
- If *f* : *X* → *Y* and *g* : *Y* → *Z* are morphisms of schemes, *g* is formally unramified, and *gf* is formally étale, then *f* is formally étale. In particular, if *g* is formally étale, then *f* is formally étale if and only if *gf* is.[^o12]
- The property of being formally étale is [local](wikipedia://en/1462457#Properties_of_commutative_rings) on the source and target.[^o13]
- The property of being formally étale can be checked on stalks. One can show that a morphism of rings *f* : *A* → *B* is formally étale if and only if for every prime *Q* of *B*, the induced map *A* → *B*<sub>*Q*</sub> is formally étale.[^o14] Consequently, *f* is formally étale if and only if for every prime *Q* of *B*, the map *A*<sub>*P*</sub> → *B*<sub>*Q*</sub> is formally étale, where *P* = *f*<sup>−1</sup>(*Q*).

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Localizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) are formally étale.
- Finite separable field extensions are formally étale. More generally, any (commutative) [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) [separable *A*-algebra](wikipedia://en/13205367) *B* is formally étale.[^o17]
