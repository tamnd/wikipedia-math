# Heegner point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heegner point"}

Heegner-Punkt In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Heegner point** is a point on a [modular curve](wikipedia://en/669552) that is the image of a quadratic imaginary point of the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826). Heegner points were defined by [Bryan Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Birch) and named after [Kurt Heegner](wikipedia://en/621799), who used similar ideas to prove [Gauss's conjecture](wikipedia://en/596282) on imaginary [quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_fields) of class number one.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Gross–Zagier theorem"}

## Gross–Zagier theorem {#Gross–Zagier_theorem}

The **Gross–Zagier theorem** (Gross Zagier 1986) describes the [height](wikipedia://en/9236652) of Heegner points in terms of a derivative of the [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) of the elliptic curve at the point *s* = 1. In particular if the elliptic curve has (analytic) rank 1, then the Heegner points can be used to construct a rational point on the curve of infinite order (so the [Mordell–Weil group](wikipedia://en/5841813) has rank at least 1). More generally, (Gross Kohnen Zagier 1987) showed that Heegner points could be used to construct [rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200) on the curve for each positive integer *n*, and the heights of these points were the coefficients of a modular form of weight 3/2. [Shou-Wu Zhang](wikipedia://en/3582724) generalized the Gross–Zagier theorem from elliptic curves to the case of modular [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) ((nb), (nb)).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture"}

## Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture {#Birch_and_Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture}

[Kolyvagin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolyvagin) later used Heegner points to construct [Euler systems](wikipedia://en/1052176), and used this to prove much of the [Birch&ndash;Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch&ndash;Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture) for rank 1 elliptic curves. Brown proved the [Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer_conjecture) for most rank 1 elliptic curves over global fields of positive characteristic (Brown 1994).

:::

::: {#o12 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation"}

## Computation {#Computation}

Heegner points can be used to compute very large rational points on rank 1 elliptic curves (see (Watkins 2006) for a survey) that could not be found by naive methods. Implementations of the algorithm are available in [Magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_computer_algebra_system), [PARI/GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PARI/GP), and [Sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sage_%28computer_algebra_system%29).

:::
