# Alexander Ostrowski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Ostrowski"}

**Alexander Markowich Ostrowski** (Олександр Маркович Островський; Алекса́ндр Ма́ркович Остро́вский; 25 September 1893 – 20 November 1986) was a mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

His father Mark having been a merchant, Alexander Ostrowski attended the Kiev College of Commerce, not a high school, and thus had an insufficient qualification to be admitted to university. However, his talent did not remain undetected: Ostrowski's mentor, [Dmitry Grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Aleksandrovich_Grave), wrote to [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) and [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565) for help.

Subsequently, Ostrowski began to study mathematics at [Marburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_University) under Hensel's supervision in 1912. During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) he was interned, but thanks to the intervention of Hensel, the restrictions on his movements were eased somewhat, and he was allowed to use the university library.

*Image: Otto Toeplitz and Alexander Ostrowski.jpg.* Ostrowski (right) with [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682)

After the war ended, Ostrowski moved to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) where he wrote his doctoral dissertation and was influenced by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), and Landau. In 1920, after having obtained his doctorate from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), Ostrowski moved to [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg) where he worked as [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990)'s assistant and finished his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1922. In 1923, he returned to Göttingen and, in 1928, became Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Basel), until retirement in 1958. In 1950, Ostrowski obtained Swiss citizenship. After retirement, he still published scientific papers until his late eighties.

His work was mainly, but not exclusively, in algebra and number theory, and he had a great interest in numerical methods as well as abstract mathematics.[^o5]
