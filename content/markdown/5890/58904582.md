# Tetradic number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tetradic number"}

A **tetradic** **number**, also known as a **four-way** **number**, is a number that remains the same when flipped back to front, flipped front to back, mirrored up-down, or flipped up-down. The only numbers that remain the same which turned up-side-down or mirrored are 0, 1, and 8, so a tetradic number is a [palindromic number](wikipedia://en/105956) containing only 0, 1, and 8 as digits. (This is dependent on the use of a handwriting style or font in which these digits *are* symmetrical, as well on the use of [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) in the first place.) The first few tetradic numbers are 1, 8, 11, 88, 101, 111, 181, 808, 818, ... ([OEIS](wikipedia://en/500004) A006072).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

Tetradic numbers are also known as four-way numbers because they have four-way [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2714149) and can flipped back to front, flipped front to back, mirrored up-down, or flipped up-down and always stay the same. The four-way symmetry explains the name, due to [tetra-](wikipedia://en/1705121) being the Greek prefix for four. Tetradic numbers are both [strobogrammatic](wikipedia://en/2906596) and [palindromic](wikipedia://en/105956).[^o4][^o5]

A larger tetradic number can always be generated by adding another tetradic number to each end, retaining the symmetry.

## Tetradic primes {#Tetradic_primes}

Tetradic primes are a specific type of tetradic number defined as tetradic numbers that are also [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). The first few tetradic primes are 11, 101, 181, 18181, 1008001, 1180811, 1880881, 1881881, ... ([OEIS](wikipedia://en/500004) A068188).[^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12]
