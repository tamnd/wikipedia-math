# Point-pair separation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Point-pair separation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), two pairs of points in a [cyclic order](wikipedia://en/434673) such as the [real projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102) **separate** each other when they occur alternately in the order. Thus the ordering *a b c d* of four points has (*a,c*) and (*b,d*) as separating pairs. This **point-pair separation** is an invariant of projectivities of the line.

:::

## Concept {#Concept}

The concept was described by [G. B. Halsted](wikipedia://en/3992708) at the outset of his *Synthetic Projective Geometry*: With regard to a pair of different points of those on a straight, all remaining fall into two classes, such that every point belongs to one and only one. If two points belong to different classes with regard to a pair of points, then also the latter two belong to different classes with regard to the first two. Two such point pairs are said to 'separate each other.' Four different points on a straight can always be partitioned in one and only one way into pairs separating each other.[^o3]

Given any pair of points on a projective line, they separate a third point from its [harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/16094600).

A pair of lines in a [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) separates another pair when a [transversal](wikipedia://en/13295107) crosses the pairs in separated points.

The point-pair separation of points was written AC//BD by [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) in his textbook *The Real Projective Plane*.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

### Application {#Application}

The relation may be used in showing the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) is a [complete space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space). The axiom of continuity used is "Every monotonic sequence of points has a limit." The point-pair separation is used to provide definitions:

- {*A*<sub>n</sub>} is **monotonic** ≡ ∀ *n* > 1 $A_0 A_n // A_1 A_{n+1}.$
- *M* is a **limit** ≡ (∀ *n* > 2 $A_1 A_n // A_2 M$) ∧ (∀ P $A_1P // A_2 M$ ⇒ ∃ *n* $A_1 A_n // P M$ ).

:::

## Unoriented circle {#Unoriented_circle}

Whereas a [linear order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order) endows a set with a positive end and a negative end, an other relation forgets not only which end is which, but also where the ends are located. In this way it is a final, further weakening of the concepts of a [betweenness relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betweenness_relation) and a [cyclic order](wikipedia://en/434673). There is nothing else that can be forgotten: up to the relevant sense of interdefinability, these three relations are the only nontrivial [reducts](wikipedia://en/12747972) of the ordered set of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).[^o8]

A [quaternary relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary_relation) *S(a, b, c, d)* is defined satisfying certain axioms, which is interpreted as asserting that *a* and *c* separate *b* from *d*.[^o9][^o10]

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/axiom cue=named-heading name="Axioms"}

### Axioms {#Axioms}

The separation relation was described with axioms in 1898 by [Giovanni Vailati](wikipedia://en/2452856).[^o13]

- *abcd* = *badc*
- *abcd* = *adcb*
- *abcd* ⇒ ¬ *acbd*
- *abcd* ∨ *acdb* ∨ *adbc*
- *abcd* ∧ *acde* ⇒ *abde*.

:::
