# Superperfect number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superperfect number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **superperfect number** is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ that satisfies

:::

$$\sigma^2(n)=\sigma(\sigma(n))=2n\, ,$$

where $\sigma$ is the [sum-of-divisors function](wikipedia://en/407249). Superperfect numbers are not a generalization of [perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/23670) but have a common generalization. The term was coined by D. Suryanarayana (1969).[^o3]

The first few superperfect numbers are:

[2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%28number%29), [16](wikipedia://en/208254), [64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64_%28number%29), [4096](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4096_%28number%29), [65536](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65536_%28number%29), 262144, 1073741824, ... .

To illustrate: it can be seen that 16 is a superperfect number as σ(16) = 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31, and σ(31) = 1 + 31 = 32, thus σ(σ(16)) = 32 = 2 × 16.

If $n$ is an *[even](wikipedia://en/143135)* superperfect number, then $n$ must be a [power of 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_2), $2^{k}$, such that $2^{k+1} - 1$ is a [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908).[^o3]

It is not known whether there are any [odd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number) superperfect numbers. An odd superperfect number $n$ would have to be a [square number](wikipedia://en/213919) such that either $n$ or $\sigma(n)$ is divisible by at least three distinct primes.[^o2] There are no odd superperfect numbers below 7.[^o3]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Perfect and superperfect numbers are examples of the wider class of *m*-superperfect numbers, which satisfy

$$\sigma^m(n) = 2n ,$$

corresponding to *m* = 1 and 2 respectively. For *m* ≥ 3 there are no even *m*-superperfect numbers.[^o3]

The *m*-superperfect numbers are in turn examples of (*m*,*k*)-perfect numbers which satisfy[^o6]

$$\sigma^m(n)=kn\, .$$

With this notation, perfect numbers are (1,2)-perfect, [multiperfect numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiperfect_number) are (1,*k*)-perfect, superperfect numbers are (2,2)-perfect and *m*-superperfect numbers are (*m*,2)-perfect.[^o7] Examples of classes of (*m*,*k*)-perfect numbers are:
