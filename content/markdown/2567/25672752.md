# Quantum inverse scattering method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum inverse scattering method"}

In [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics), the **quantum inverse scattering method**, similar to the closely related **algebraic Bethe ansatz**, is a method for solving [integrable models](wikipedia://en/2573213) in 1+1 dimensions, introduced by [Leon Takhtajan](wikipedia://en/48468716) and [L. D. Faddeev](wikipedia://en/4854639) in 1979.[^o2]

:::

It can be viewed as a quantized version of the classical [inverse scattering method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_scattering_method) pioneered by [Norman Zabusky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Zabusky) and [Martin Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kruskal)[^o3] used to investigate the [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) and later other [integrable](wikipedia://en/2573213) [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). In both, a [Lax matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax_matrix) features heavily and [scattering data](wikipedia://en/164483) is used to construct solutions to the original system.

While the classical inverse scattering method is used to solve integrable partial differential equations which model [continuous media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_media) (for example, the KdV equation models shallow water waves), the quantum inverse scattering method is used to solve [many-body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_problem) quantum systems, sometimes known as [spin chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_chain), of which the [Heisenberg spin chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_spin_chain) is the best-studied and most famous example. These are typically discrete systems, with particles fixed at different points of a lattice, but limits of results obtained by the quantum inverse scattering method can give predictions even for [field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) defined on a continuum, such as the quantum [sine-Gordon model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine-Gordon_model).

## Discussion {#Discussion}

The quantum inverse scattering method relates two different approaches:

1. The [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884), a method of solving integrable quantum models in one space and one time dimension.[^o5]
1. The [inverse scattering transform](wikipedia://en/4916481), a method of solving classical integrable differential equations of the evolutionary type.[^o6]

This method led to the formulation of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678), in particular the [Yangian](wikipedia://en/15272567).[^o7] The center of the Yangian, given by the [quantum determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_determinant), plays a prominent role in the method.[^o8]

An important concept in the [inverse scattering transform](wikipedia://en/4916481) is the [Lax representation](wikipedia://en/5527029). The quantum inverse scattering method starts by the [quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29) of the Lax representation and reproduces the results of the Bethe ansatz. In fact, it allows the Bethe ansatz to be written in a new form: the *algebraic Bethe ansatz*.[^o9] This led to further progress in the understanding of quantum [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213), such as the [quantum Heisenberg model](wikipedia://en/4899779), the quantum [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748) (also known as the [Lieb–Liniger model](wikipedia://en/24952147) or the [Tonks–Girardeau gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonks–Girardeau_gas)) and the [Hubbard model](wikipedia://en/1702398).[^o15]

The theory of [correlation functions](wikipedia://en/1651916) was developed, relating determinant representations, descriptions by differential equations and the [Riemann–Hilbert problem](wikipedia://en/9710396). Asymptotics of correlation functions which include space, time and temperature dependence were evaluated in 1991.[^o15]

Explicit expressions for the higher [conservation laws](wikipedia://en/6956) of the integrable models were obtained in 1989.[^o14]

Essential progress was achieved in study of [ice-type models](wikipedia://en/20096022): the bulk free energy of the six vertex model depends on boundary conditions even in the [thermodynamic limit](wikipedia://en/2262550).[^o14]

## Procedure {#Procedure}

The steps can be summarized as follows (after [Evgeny Sklyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Sklyanin)):[^o14]

1. Take an [*R*-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996) which solves the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810).
1. Take a [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_%28group_theory%29) of an algebra $\mathcal{T}_R$ satisfying the RTT relations.[^o13]
1. Find the spectrum of the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) $t(u)$ of the [centre](wikipedia://en/7125) of $\mathcal{T}_R$.
1. Find correlators.
