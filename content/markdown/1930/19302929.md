# Gerard Laman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerard Laman"}

**Gerard Laman** (August 22, 1924 – September 22, 2009) was a Dutch mathematician who worked on [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).

:::

*Image: Gerard Laman (mathematician).jpg.* Gerard Laman.

## Early life {#Early_life}

He completed high school studies at the [Stedelijk Gymnasium Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stedelijk_Gymnasium_Leiden) in 1942. His study of Mathematics at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) was delayed by a period in hiding to evade enforced labor during the Nazi occupation of the Netherlands. He completed a degree in Mathematics with a minor in Mechanics in 1952. From 1949 onwards, he was a scientific assistant to J. Haantjes.

He received private instruction in the [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400) of [fiber spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_space) in [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) from G. Hirsch of the Agricultural [University of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghent) in 1953. During this period he received a stipend from the Dutch-Belgian Cultural Accord.

From 1954 to 1957 he taught mathematics at the Delft high school 'Gemeentelijke Hogere Burgerschool HBS'.

In 1959 he completed his PhD thesis[^n1] at Leiden University. W. T. van Est acted as his supervisor, once the original supervisor J. Haantjes was deceased.

### Career {#Career}

From 1957 to 1967 he worked as a lecturer at the 'Technische Hogeschool' of [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven) (now [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology)).

From 1967 to his retirement in 1989, he worked as a lecturer at the Mathematical Institute of the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), teaching [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and mathematics for students in [Econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390). Laman regarded himself mostly as a teacher. Clear thinking, as well as brevity in speech and writing, were his forte.

Laman is often credited with proving, in 1970,[^n2] that a particular family of sparse graphs, since named [Laman graphs](wikipedia://en/13790456), are precisely those that are minimally generically rigid in the plane. This result, the [Geiringer–Laman theorem](wikipedia://en/66490111), had already been proven by [Hilda Geiringer](wikipedia://en/7553047) back in 1927.[^o6]

Laman's original publication in 1970 went largely unnoticed at first. Only when [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Geoffrey Colin Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532) wrote about Laman's paper in their Lectures on lost mathematics[^n3] did this work receive more attention.

Towards the end of his life, Laman worked to lift the original 'Laman graph' from its original two dimensions to three, inspired by a simple counterexample, the 'double banana graph'.[^n4]
