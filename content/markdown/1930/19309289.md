# Zlil Sela

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zlil Sela"}

**Zlil Sela** (צליל סלע) is an Israeli [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the area of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). He is a Professor of Mathematics at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). Sela is known for the solution[^o15] of the [isomorphism problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_problem_for_groups) for [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) [word-hyperbolic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) and for the solution of the [Tarski conjecture](wikipedia://en/59735) about equivalence of [first-order theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theories) of [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [non-abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735).[^o6]

:::

## Biographical data {#Biographical_data}

Sela received his Ph.D. in 1991 from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), where his [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) was [Eliyahu Rips](wikipedia://en/673693). Prior to his current appointment at the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University), he held an Associate Professor position at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in New York.[^o4] While at Columbia, Sela won the [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) from the [Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Foundation).[^o4][^o5]

Sela gave an Invited Address at the 2002 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Beijing.[^o6][^o7] He gave a plenary talk at the 2002 annual meeting of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438),[^o8] and he delivered an AMS Invited Address at the October 2003 meeting of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o9] and the 2005 [Tarski Lectures](wikipedia://en/54104355) at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley).[^o10] He was also awarded the 2003 [Erdős Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_Prize) from the [Israel Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/59341674).[^o11] Sela also received the 2008 [Carol Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize) from the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) for his work on the Tarski conjecture and on discovering and developing new connections between [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).[^o12][^o13]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Sela's early important work was his solution[^o15] in mid-1990s of the [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026) for torsion-free [word-hyperbolic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group). The machinery of [group actions](wikipedia://en/12781) on [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914), developed by [Eliyahu Rips](wikipedia://en/673693), played a key role in Sela's approach. The solution of the isomorphism problem also relied on the notion of *canonical representatives* for elements of hyperbolic groups, introduced by Rips and Sela in a joint 1995 paper.[^o16] The machinery of the canonical representatives allowed Rips and Sela to prove[^o16] algorithmic solvability of finite systems of equations in torsion-free hyperbolic groups, by reducing the problem to solving equations in [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735), where the Makanin–Razborov algorithm can be applied. The technique of canonical representatives was later generalized by Dahmani[^o17] to the case of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/21993809) and played a key role in the solution of the isomorphism problem for *toral* relatively hyperbolic groups.[^o18]

In his work on the isomorphism problem Sela also introduced and developed the notion of a JSJ-decomposition for word-hyperbolic groups,[^o19] motivated by the notion of a [JSJ decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599) for [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257). A JSJ-decomposition is a representation of a word-hyperbolic group as the [fundamental group of a graph of groups](wikipedia://en/18673708) which encodes in a canonical way all possible [splittings](wikipedia://en/18673708#Splittings) over [infinite cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic_group) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011). The idea of JSJ-decomposition was later extended by Rips and Sela to torsion-free [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group)[^o20] and this work gave rise a systematic development of the JSJ-decomposition theory with many further extensions and generalizations by other mathematicians.[^o21][^o22][^o23][^o24] Sela applied a combination of his JSJ-decomposition and [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) techniques to prove that torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups are [Hopfian](wikipedia://en/11486001).[^o25] This result and Sela's approach were later generalized by others to [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of hyperbolic groups[^o26] and to the setting of relatively hyperbolic groups.

Sela's most important work came in early 2000s when he produced a solution to a famous [Tarski conjecture](wikipedia://en/59735#Tarski's_problems). Namely, in a long series of papers,[^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30][^o31][^o32][^o33] he proved that any two non-abelian [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735) have the same [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory). His work relied on applying his earlier JSJ-decomposition and [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) techniques as well as developing new ideas and machinery of "[algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997)" over free groups.

Sela pushed this work further to study first-order theory of arbitrary torsion-free word-hyperbolic groups and to characterize all groups that are elementarily equivalent to (that is, have the same first-order theory as) a given torsion-free word-hyperbolic group. In particular, his work implies that if a finitely generated group *G* is elementarily equivalent to a word-hyperbolic group then *G* is word-hyperbolic as well.

Sela also proved that the first-order theory of a finitely generated free group is [stable](wikipedia://en/15828771) in the model-theoretic sense, providing a brand-new and qualitatively different source of examples for the stability theory.

An alternative solution for the Tarski conjecture has been presented by [Olga Kharlampovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Kharlampovich) and [Alexei Myasnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Myasnikov).[^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37]

The work of Sela on first-order theory of free and word-hyperbolic groups substantially influenced the development of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), in particular by stimulating the development and the study of the notion of [limit groups](wikipedia://en/78938535) and of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/21993809).[^o38]

## Sela's classification theorem {#Sela's_classification_theorem}

Theorem. Two non-abelian torsion-free hyperbolic groups are elementarily equivalent [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) their cores are isomorphic.[^o41]

:::
