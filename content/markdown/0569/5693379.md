# Robin Hartshorne

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robin Hartshorne"}

**Robin Cope Hartshorne** ( ; born March 15, 1938) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is known for his work in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Hartshorne was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) in Fall 1958 while he was an undergraduate at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University)[^o4] (under the name Robert C. Hartshorne[^o5]). He received a Ph.D. in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1963 after completing a doctoral dissertation titled *Connectedness of the Hilbert scheme* under the supervision of [John Coleman Moore](wikipedia://en/13433862) and [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352).[^o6] He then became a [Junior Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Fellow) at Harvard University, where he taught for several years.[^o8] In 1972, he was appointed to the faculty at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley),[^o8] where he is a Professor Emeritus as of 2020.[^o9]

Hartshorne is the author of the text *[Algebraic Geometry](wikipedia://en/6921017)*.[^o10][^o11]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1979, Hartshorne was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for "his expository research article Equivalence relations on algebraic cycles and subvarieties of small codimension, Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics, volume 29, American Mathematical Society, 1975, pp. 129-164; and his book *Algebraic geometry*, Springer-Verlag, Berlin and New York, 1977."[^o13] In 2012, Hartshorne became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hartshorne attended high school at [Phillips Exeter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy), graduating in 1955. Hartshorne is married to Edie Churchill and has two sons and an adopted daughter.[^o8] He is a mountain climber and amateur flute and *[shakuhachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakuhachi)* player.[^o8]
