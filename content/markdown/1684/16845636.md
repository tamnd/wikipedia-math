# Size functor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Size functor"}

Given a [size pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Size_pair) $(M,f)\$ where $M\$ is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) of dimension $n\$ and $f\$ is an arbitrary real [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) defined on it, the $i$-th **size functor**,[^o2] with $i=0,\ldots,n\$, denoted by $F_i\$, is the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) in $Fun(\mathrm{Rord},\mathrm{Ab})\$, where $\mathrm{Rord}\$ is the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of ordered real numbers, and $\mathrm{Ab}\$ is the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [Abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_groups), defined in the following way. For $x\le y\$, setting $M_x=\{p\in M:f(p)\le x\}\$, $M_y=\{p\in M:f(p)\le y\}\$, $j_{xy}\$ equal to the inclusion from $M_x\$ into $M_y\$, and $k_{xy}\$ equal to the [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) in $\mathrm{Rord}\$ from $x\$ to $y\$,

:::

- for each $x\in\mathbb{R}\$, $F_i(x)=H_i(M_x);\$
- $$F_i(k_{xy})=H_i(j_{xy}).\$$

In other words, the size functor studies the process of the birth and death of homology classes as the lower level set changes. When $M\$ is smooth and compact and $f\$ is a [Morse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function), the functor $F_0\$ can be described by oriented trees, called $H_0\$ − trees.

The concept of size functor was introduced as an extension to [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) of the idea of [size function](wikipedia://en/16637535). The main motivation for introducing the size functor originated by the observation that the [size function](wikipedia://en/16637535) $\ell_{(M,f)}(x, y)\$ can be seen as the rank of the image of $H_0(j_{xy}) : H_0(M_x) \rightarrow H_0(M_y)$.

The concept of size functor is strictly related to the concept of [persistent homology group](wikipedia://en/73493845),[^o3] studied in [persistent homology](wikipedia://en/31069689). It is worth to point out that the $i\$-th persistent homology group coincides with the image of the [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $F_i(k_{xy})=H_i(j_{xy}): H_i(M_x) \rightarrow H_i(M_y)$.
