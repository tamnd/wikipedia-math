# Unknown key-share attack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unknown key-share attack"}

As defined by (Blake-Wilson Menezes 1999), an **unknown key-share** (**UKS**) **attack** on an authenticated [key agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_agreement_protocol) (AK) or authenticated key agreement with [key confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_confirmation) (AKC) [protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_%28computing%29) is an attack whereby an entity $A$ ends up believing she shares a key with $B$, and although this is in fact the case, $B$ mistakenly believes the key is instead shared with an entity $E \neq A$.

:::

In other words, in a UKS, an [opponent](wikipedia://en/144676#Example), say Eve, coerces honest parties Alice and Bob into establishing a [secret key](wikipedia://en/53039) where at least one of Alice and Bob does not know that the secret key is shared with the other. For example, Eve may coerce Bob into believing he shares the key with Eve, while he actually shares the key with Alice. The “key share” with Alice is thus unknown to Bob.
