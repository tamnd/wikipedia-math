# Stephen Twinoburyo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stephen Twinoburyo"}

**Stephen Twinoburyo** (8 January 1970 – 1 January 2019)[^o6] was a Ugandan scientist, mathematician, lecturer, and entrepreneur. He was the [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer) of Scimatic Solutions, a South African company which helps students with maths and science tuition.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Twinoburyo was born on 8 January 1970,[^o4] in [Mbarara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbarara), Uganda.[^o5] He was the second of seven children, and his father worked as a [town clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_clerk).[^o4] He attended [Ntare School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ntare_School) and was [head prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_girl_and_head_boy) there in 1989.[^o6]

In 1990, he started studying engineering at [Makerere University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makerere_University), and relocated to South Africa.[^o6][^o5] During his time there, he was chairman of Lumumba Hall.[^o6] He later studied mathematics as a [part-time degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part-time_learner_in_higher_education) at the [University of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Africa), completing the course in 2007.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

In 1994, Twinoburyo visited [Soweto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto), South Africa,[^o5] and it inspired him to move to the country in 1997.[^o4][^o5] He lectured at the [University of Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pretoria),[^o4] and taught in colleges in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) and [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town).[^o8]

In 2008, Twinoburyo decided to found Uganda Professionals Living in South Africa (AUPSA),[^o6] and worked as their chairman.[^o8][^o9] In 2009, he organised a meeting of Ugandan [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) in South Africa. The meeting was held in [Sandton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandton), South Africa.[^o8] AUPSA was set up to connect Ugandan expatriates living in South Africa.[^o6][^o8] Twinoburyo also worked for the Ugandan Civil Alliance Network.[^o10]

In 2010, Twinoburyo said that Ugandans were unhappy about the ticket prices for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup) in South Africa.[^o11] In 2011, he condemned alleged [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights) abuses in Uganda, and asked South African president [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma) not to attend the inauguration of Ugandan president [Yoweri Museveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoweri_Museveni).[^o10]

In 2014, Twinoburyo set up and became the CEO of Scimatic Solutions, a South African company which helps students with maths and science tuition.[^o4][^o12] He was inspired to set up the company after visiting the [California Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Science_Center) and [National Air and Space Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_and_Space_Museum) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o12] The company is based in [Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatfield,_Pretoria), Pretoria.[^o12]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Twinoburyo and his wife had three children.[^o6] He died in South Africa on 1 January 2019 of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack), one week before his 49th birthday.[^o4][^o5][^o9] His body was [repatriated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repatriation) to Uganda.[^o4]
