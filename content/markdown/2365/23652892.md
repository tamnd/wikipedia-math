# Hitchin system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hitchin system"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hitchin integrable system** is an [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213) depending on the choice of a complex [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) and a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181), introduced by [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164) in 1987. It lies on the crossroads of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the theory of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) and [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213) theory. It also plays an important role in the [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178) over the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) through [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913).

:::

A [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) zero analogue of the Hitchin system, the [Garnier system](wikipedia://en/72988820), was discovered by René Garnier somewhat earlier as a certain limit of the [Schlesinger equations](wikipedia://en/16987249#Fuchsian_systems_and_Schlesinger's_equation), and Garnier solved his system by defining spectral curves. (The Garnier system is the classical limit of the [Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317). In turn, the Schlesinger equations are the classical limit of the [Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations](wikipedia://en/16119106)).

Almost all integrable systems of [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) can be obtained as particular cases of the Hitchin system or their common generalization defined by Bottacin and Markman in 1994.

## Description {#Description}

Using the language of algebraic geometry, the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101) of the system is a partial [compactification](wikipedia://en/6617) of the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) to the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of [stable *G*-bundles](wikipedia://en/64244772) for some [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G*, on some compact [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260). This space is endowed with a [canonical symplectic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_symplectic_form). Suppose for simplicity that $G = \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C})$, the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564); then the [Hamiltonians](wikipedia://en/198319) can be described as follows: the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) to the moduli space of *G*-bundles at the bundle *F* is

$$H^1(\operatorname{End}(F)),$$

which by [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175) is dual to

$$\Phi \in H^0(\operatorname{End}(F)\otimes K),$$

where $K$ is the [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893), so a pair

$$(F,\Phi)$$

called a Hitchin pair or [Higgs bundle](wikipedia://en/34143255), defines a point in the cotangent bundle. Taking

$$\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi^k),\qquad k=1,\ldots,\operatorname{rank}(G)$$

one obtains elements in

$$H^0( K^{\otimes k} ),$$

which is a vector space which does not depend on $(F,\Phi)$. So taking any basis in these vector spaces we obtain functions *H<sub>i</sub>*, which are Hitchin's hamiltonians. The construction for general reductive group is similar and uses [invariant polynomials](wikipedia://en/5352569) on the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of *G*.

For trivial reasons these functions are algebraically independent, and some calculations show that their number is exactly half of the dimension of the phase space. The nontrivial part is a proof of [Poisson commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_commutativity) of these functions. They therefore define an integrable system in the symplectic or [Arnol'd–Liouville](wikipedia://en/40071018) sense.

## Hitchin fibration {#Hitchin_fibration}

The **Hitchin fibration** is the map from the moduli space of Hitchin pairs to [characteristic polynomials](wikipedia://en/218268), a higher genus analogue of the map Garnier used to define the spectral curves. (txt) used Hitchin fibrations over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) in his proof of the [fundamental lemma](wikipedia://en/25431009).

To be more precise, the version of Hitchin fibration that is used by Ngô has source the [moduli stack](wikipedia://en/361609#Moduli_stacks) of Hitchin pairs, instead of the moduli space. Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be the Lie algebra of the reductive algebraic group $G$. We have the adjoint action of $G$ on $\mathfrak{g}$. We can then take the [stack quotient](wikipedia://en/40579087) $\mathfrak{g}/G$ and the [GIT quotient](wikipedia://en/41139312) $\mathfrak{g}/\!/G$, and there is a natural morphism $\chi:\mathfrak{g}/G\to \mathfrak{g}/\!/G$. There is also the natural scaling action of the multiplicative group $\mathbb{G}_m$ on $\mathfrak{g}$, which descends to the stack and GIT quotients. Furthermore, the morphism $\chi$ is equivariant with respect to the $\mathbb{G}_m$-actions. Therefore, given any [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) $L$ on our curve $C$, we can [twist](wikipedia://en/41910974#The_contracted_product) the morphism $\chi$ by the $\mathbb{G}_m$-[torsor](wikipedia://en/41910974), and obtain a morphism $\chi_L: (\mathfrak{g}/G)_L\to (\mathfrak{g}/\!/G)_L$ of stacks over $C$. Finally, the moduli stack of $L$-twisted Higgs bundles is recovered as the section stack $Higgs=Sect(C,(\mathfrak{g}/G)_L)$; the corresponding Hitchin base is recovered as $A(C,L):=Sect(C,(\mathfrak{g}/\!/G)_L)$, which is represented by a vector space; and the Hitchin morphism at the stack level $h:Higgs\to A(C,L)$ is simply the morphism induced by the morphism $\chi_L$ above. Note that this definition is not relevant to semistability. To obtain the Hitchin fibration mentioned above, we need to take $L$ to be the canonical bundle, restrict to the semistable part of $Higgs$, and then take the induced morphism on the moduli space. To be even more precise, the version of $Higgs$ that is used by Ngô often has the restriction that $\deg(L)\geq 2g$, so that it cannot be the canonical bundle. This condition is added to guarantee that the topology of the Hitchin morphism is, [in a precise sense](wikipedia://en/1714012), determined by its restriction to the smooth part, see Chaudouard for the vector bundle case.
