# Otto M. Nikodym

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Otto M. Nikodym"}

**Otto Marcin Nikodym** (3 August 1887 – 4 May 1974) (also Otton Martin Nikodým) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He contributed mainly to the areas of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) and [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619). In 1919, he was among the founders of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Nikodym studied mathematics at the University of Lemberg (today's [University of Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lviv)). Immediately after his graduation in 1911, he started his teaching job at a high school in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) where he remained until 1924. He eventually obtained his doctorate in 1925 from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw); he also spent an academic year (1926-1927) in [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). Nikodym taught at the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) and [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) and at the [Akademia Górnicza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademia_Górniczo-Hutnicza) in Kraków in the years that followed. He moved to the United States in 1948 and joined the faculty of [Kenyon College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyon_College). He retired in 1966 and moved to [Utica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utica,_New_York), New York, where he continued his research until his death.[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Nikodym was born in 1887 in Demycze, a suburb of [Zabłotów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabłotów) (in modern-day [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine)), to a family with Polish, Czech, Italian and French roots.[^o6] Orphaned at a young age, he was brought up by his maternal grandparents. In 1924, he married [Stanisława Nikodym](wikipedia://en/60079820), the first Polish woman to obtain a PhD in mathematics.

## Research works {#Research_works}

Nikodym worked in a wide range of areas, but his best-known early work was his contribution to the development of the Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym integral (see [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746)).[^o8] His work in measure theory led him to an interest in abstract [Boolean lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_lattice). His work after coming to the United States centered on the theory of operators in [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), based on Boolean lattices, culminating in his *The Mathematical Apparatus for Quantum-Theories*. He was also interested in the teaching of mathematics.
