# Graded Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graded Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **graded Lie algebra** is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) endowed with a [gradation](wikipedia://en/1155734) which is compatible with the [Lie bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_bracket). In other words, a graded Lie algebra is a Lie algebra which is also a [nonassociative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonassociative_algebra) [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) under the bracket operation. A choice of [Cartan decomposition](wikipedia://en/1139145) endows any [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) with the structure of a graded Lie algebra. Any [parabolic Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/10538204) is also a graded Lie algebra.

:::

A **graded Lie superalgebra**[^o2] extends the notion of a graded Lie algebra in such a way that the Lie bracket is no longer assumed to be necessarily [anticommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutative). These arise in the study of [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) on [graded algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra), in the [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of [Murray Gerstenhaber](wikipedia://en/30237327), [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326), and [Donald C. Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer), and in the theory of [Lie derivatives](wikipedia://en/294437).

A **supergraded Lie superalgebra**[^o3] is a further generalization of this notion to the category of [superalgebras](wikipedia://en/469950) in which a graded [Lie superalgebra](wikipedia://en/297501) is endowed with an additional super $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-gradation. These arise when one forms a graded Lie superalgebra in a classical (non-supersymmetric) setting, and then tensorizes to obtain the [supersymmetric](wikipedia://en/224636) analog.[^o4]

Still greater generalizations are possible to Lie algebras over a class of [braided monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/1470466) equipped with a [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889) and some notion of a gradation compatible with the braiding in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759). For hints in this direction, see [Lie superalgebra#Category-theoretic definition](wikipedia://en/297501#Category-theoretic_definition).

## Graded Lie algebras {#Graded_Lie_algebras}

In its most basic form, a graded Lie algebra is an ordinary Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, together with a [gradation of vector spaces](wikipedia://en/1155734)

$${\mathfrak g}=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}} {\mathfrak g}_i,$$

such that the Lie bracket respects this gradation:

$$[{\mathfrak g}_i,{\mathfrak g}_j]\subseteq {\mathfrak g}_{i+j}.$$

The [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a graded Lie algebra inherits the grading.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### sl(2) {#sl(2)}

For example, the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ of [trace-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace-free) 2 × 2 [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) is graded by the generators:

$$X = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad Y = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\textrm{and}\quad H = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.$$

These satisfy the relations $[X,Y] = H$, $[H,X] = 2X$, and $[H,Y] = -2Y$. Hence with $\mathfrak{g}_{-1} = \textrm{span}(X)$, $\mathfrak{g}_{0} = \textrm{span}(H)$, and $\mathfrak{g}_{1} = \textrm{span}(Y)$, the decomposition $\mathfrak{sl}(2) = \mathfrak{g}_{-1} \oplus \mathfrak{g}_0 \oplus \mathfrak{g}_{1}$ presents $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ as a graded Lie algebra.

#### Free Lie algebra {#Free_Lie_algebra}

The [free Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/3174646) on a set *X* naturally has a grading, given by the minimum number of terms needed to generate the group element. This arises for example as the associated graded Lie algebra to the [lower central series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_central_series) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735).

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

If $\Gamma$ is any [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid) [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), then the notion of a **$\Gamma$-graded Lie algebra** generalizes that of an ordinary ($\mathbb{Z}$-) graded Lie algebra so that the defining relations hold with the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z}$ replaced by $\Gamma$. In particular, any semisimple Lie algebra is graded by the root spaces of its [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_algebra).

:::

## Graded Lie superalgebras {#Graded_Lie_superalgebras}

A **graded Lie superalgebra** over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k* (not of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) 2) consists of a [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) *E* over *k*, along with a [bilinear](wikipedia://en/449477) **bracket** operation

$$[-,-] : E \otimes_k E \to E$$

such that the following axioms are satisfied.

- \[-, -\] respects the gradation of *E*: $$[E_i,E_j]\subseteq E_{i+j}.$$
- (*Symmetry*) For all *x* in *E*<sub>*i*</sub> and *y* in *E*<sub>*j*</sub>, $$[x,y]=-(-1)^{ij}\,[y,x]$$
- (*Jacobi identity*) For all *x* in *E*<sub>*i*</sub>, *y* in *E*<sub>*j*</sub>, and *z* in *E*<sub>*k*</sub>, $$(-1)^{ik}[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{ij}[y,[z,x]]+(-1)^{jk}[z,[x,y]]=0.$$ (If *k* has characteristic 3, then the Jacobi identity must be supplemented with the condition $[x,[x,x]] = 0$ for all *x* in *E*<sub>odd</sub>.)

Note, for instance, that when *E* carries the trivial gradation, a graded Lie superalgebra over *k* is just an ordinary Lie algebra. When the gradation of *E* is concentrated in even degrees, one recovers the definition of a (**Z**-)graded Lie algebra.

### Examples and Applications {#Examples_and_Applications}

The most basic example of a graded Lie superalgebra occurs in the study of derivations of graded algebras. If *A* is a [graded *k*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) with gradation

$$A = \bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}} A_i,$$

then a graded *k*-derivation *d* on *A* of degree *l* is defined by

1. $dx= 0$ for $x\in k$,
1. $d\colon A_i \to A_{i+l}$, and
1. $d(xy) = (dx)y+(-1)^{il}x(dy)$ for $x\in A_i$.

The space of all graded derivations of degree *l* is denoted by $\operatorname{Der}_l(A)$, and the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of these spaces,

$$\operatorname{Der}(A) = \bigoplus_l \operatorname{Der}_l(A),$$

carries the structure of an *A*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). This generalizes the notion of a derivation of [commutative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) to the graded category.

On Der(*A*), one can define a bracket via:

\[*d*, *δ*\] = *dδ* − (−1)<sup>*ij*</sup>*δd*, for *d* ∈ Der<sub>*i*</sub>(*A*) and *δ* ∈ Der<sub>*j*</sub>(*A*).

Equipped with this structure, Der(*A*) inherits the structure of a graded Lie superalgebra over *k*.

**Further examples:**

- The [Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/3291722) is an example of a graded Lie algebra arising naturally in the study of [connections](wikipedia://en/308815) in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).
- The [Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket](wikipedia://en/3291647) arises in connection with the deformations of Lie algebras.

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The notion of a graded Lie superalgebra can be generalized so that their grading is not just the integers. Specifically, a **signed semiring** consists of a pair $(\Gamma, \epsilon)$, where $\Gamma$ is a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) and $\epsilon \colon \Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) of [additive groups](wikipedia://en/332229). Then a graded Lie supalgebra over a signed semiring consists of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *E* graded with respect to the additive structure on $\Gamma$, and a bilinear bracket \[-, -\] which respects the grading on *E* and in addition satisfies:

1. $[x,y] = - (-1)^{\epsilon(\deg x)\epsilon(\deg y)}[y,x]$ for all [homogeneous elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_element) *x* and *y*, and
1. $$(-1)^{\epsilon(\deg x)\epsilon(\deg z)}[x,[y,z]] + (-1)^{\epsilon(\deg y)\epsilon(\deg x)}[y,[z,x]] + (-1)^{\epsilon(\deg z)\epsilon(\deg y)}[z,[x,y]]=0.$$

**Further examples:**

- A [Lie superalgebra](wikipedia://en/297501) is a graded Lie superalgebra over the signed semiring $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}, \epsilon)$, where $\epsilon$ is the [identity map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_map) for the additive structure on the ring $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$.

:::

[^o2]: The "super" prefix for this is not entirely standard, and some authors may opt to omit it entirely in favor of calling a graded Lie superalgebra just a *graded Lie algebra*. This dodge is not entirely without warrant, since graded Lie superalgebras may have nothing to do with the algebras of [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636). They are only super insofar as they carry a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ gradation. This gradation occurs naturally, and not because of any underlying superspaces. Thus in the sense of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), they are properly regarded as ordinary non-super objects.
[^o3]: In connection with [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636), these are often called just *graded Lie superalgebras*, but this conflicts with the previous definition in this article.
[^o4]: Thus supergraded Lie superalgebras carry a *pair* of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$-gradations: one of which is supersymmetric, and the other is classical. [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) calls the supersymmetric one the *super gradation*, and the classical one the *cohomological gradation*. These two gradations must be compatible, and there is often disagreement as to how they should be regarded. See [Deligne's discussion](https://www.math.ias.edu/QFT/fall/bern-appen1.ps) of this difficulty.
