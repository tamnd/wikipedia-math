# Universal quadratic form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Universal quadratic form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **universal quadratic form** is a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that represents every element of the ring.[^o2] A non-singular form over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) which represents zero non-trivially is universal.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), the form $x^2$ in one variable is not universal, as it cannot represent negative numbers: the two-variable form $x^2-y^2$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is universal.
- [Lagrange's four-square theorem](wikipedia://en/326395) states that every positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) is the sum of four [squares](wikipedia://en/213919). Hence the form $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + t^2 - u^2$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ is universal.
- Over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), any non-singular quadratic form of dimension 2 or more is universal.[^o6]

:::

## Forms over the rational numbers {#Forms_over_the_rational_numbers}

The [Hasse–Minkowski theorem](wikipedia://en/1109126) implies that a form is universal over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if it is universal over [$\mathbf{Q}_{p}$](wikipedia://en/51423) for all [primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primes) $p$ (where we include $p=\infty$, letting $\mathbb{Q}_\infty$ denote $\mathbb{R}$).[^o8] A form over $\mathbb{R}$ is universal if and only if it is not [definite](wikipedia://en/20437320); a form over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is universal if it has dimension at least 4.[^o9] One can conclude that all indefinite forms of dimension at least 4 over $\mathbb{Q}$ are universal.[^o8]

## See also {#See_also}

- The [15 and 290 theorems](wikipedia://en/5405018) give conditions for a quadratic form to represent all positive integers.
