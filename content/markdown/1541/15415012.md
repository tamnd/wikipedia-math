# Robert R. Sokal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert R. Sokal"}

**Robert Reuven Sokal** (January 13, 1926, in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) – April 9, 2012, in [Stony Brook, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook,_New_York))[^o3] was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria)–[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) and [entomologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomologist). Distinguished Professor Emeritus at the [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University), Sokal was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences). He promoted the use of statistics in biology and co-founded the field of [numerical taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_taxonomy), together with [Peter H. A. Sneath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_H._A._Sneath).[^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Sokal was born in 1926 in a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Jews) family in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria). In 1939, following the [annexation of Austria by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss), he escaped with his family to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China). He earned his bachelor's degree at [St. John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John's_University,_Shanghai) in [Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai), where he married, and from there moved with his wife Julie to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where he also worked as a [librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Librarian) to complement his scholarship. He took his Ph.D. degree under the supervision of the well-known termite systematist [Alfred E. Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_E._Emerson). He was also strongly influenced by [Sewall Wright](wikipedia://en/1700593), who served on his [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) committee. Sokal developed an interest for [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and quantitative biology.

In 1959, Sokal moved to the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) where he developed—initially in collaboration with [Charles Duncan Michener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Duncan_Michener)—quantitative techniques for classifying organisms and building dendrograms, which later came to be called [numerical taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_taxonomy) methods. At the [State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York), Stony Brook, in collaboration with [F. James Rohlf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._James_Rohlf), Sokal worked on new statistical methods for the analysis of geographic variation. His interests shifted to [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) and [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268), and he directed studies on the population history of [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) as inferred from genetic and ethnohistorical data. Along with [Luca Cavalli-Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Cavalli-Sforza), Sokal pioneered the comparative study of linguistic and genetic variation.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- Fellow, [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), 1983
- Fellow, [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), 1986
- [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship), 1975, 1983
- Member, [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), 1987
- Doctor of Science (honorary), [University of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Crete), Iraklion, 1990
- Distinguished Statistical Ecologist Award, [International Association for Ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_for_Ecology), 1994
- Darwin Lifetime Achievement Award, [American Association of Physical Anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Physical_Anthropologists), 2004[^o7]
