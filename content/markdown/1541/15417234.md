# Edna Kramer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edna Kramer"}

**Edna Ernestine Kramer Lassar** (May 11, 1902 – July 9, 1984), born **Edna Ernestine Kramer**, was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and author of mathematics books.

:::

Kramer was born in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) to Jewish immigrants.[^o3] She earned her [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) *summa cum laude* in mathematics from [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) in 1922.[^o4] While teaching at local high schools, she earned her [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1925 and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1930 in mathematics (with a minor in physics) from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) with [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner) as her advisor.

She wrote *The Nature and Growth of Modern Mathematics*, *A First Course in Educational Statistics,* *Mathematics Takes Wings: An Aviation Supplement to Secondary Mathematics*, and *The Main Stream of Mathematics*.[^o5]

Kramer married the French teacher Benedict Taxier Lassar on July 2, 1935. Kramer-Lassar died at the age of 82 in [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan) of [Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease).[^o5]
