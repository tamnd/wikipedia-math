# COCONUT98

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="COCONUT98"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **COCONUT98 (Cipher Organized with Cute Operations and N-Universal Transformation)** is a [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) designed by [Serge Vaudenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Vaudenay) in 1998. It was one of the first concrete applications of Vaudenay's [decorrelation theory](wikipedia://en/12930296), designed to be [provably secure](wikipedia://en/1182348) against [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536), [linear cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/59469), and even certain types of undiscovered cryptanalytic attacks.

:::

The cipher uses a [block size](wikipedia://en/294065) of 64 bits and a [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) of 256 bits. Its basic structure is an 8-round [Feistel network](wikipedia://en/352709), but with an additional operation after the first 4 rounds, called a *decorrelation module*. This consists of a key-dependent [affine transformation](wikipedia://en/38449) in the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) GF(2<sup>64</sup>). The round function makes use of [modular multiplication and addition](wikipedia://en/20087), [bit rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_rotation), [XORs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XOR), and a single 8×24-bit [S-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_box). The entries of the S-box are derived using the binary expansion of [e](wikipedia://en/9633) as a source of "[nothing up my sleeve numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothing_up_my_sleeve_number)".[^o4]

Despite Vaudenay's proof of COCONUT98's security, in 1999 [David Wagner](wikipedia://en/298253) developed the [boomerang attack](wikipedia://en/2137332) against it.[^o5] This attack, however, requires both [chosen plaintexts](wikipedia://en/63852) and [adaptive chosen ciphertexts](wikipedia://en/426533), so is largely theoretical.[^o6] Then in 2002, Biham, et al. applied [differential-linear cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential-linear_cryptanalysis), a purely chosen-plaintext attack, to break the cipher.[^o7] The same team has also developed what they call a *[related-key](wikipedia://en/1516144) boomerang attack*, which [distinguishes](wikipedia://en/11563117) COCONUT98 from random using one related-key adaptive chosen plaintext and ciphertext quartet under two keys.[^o8]
