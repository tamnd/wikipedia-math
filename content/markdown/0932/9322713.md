# Indicators of spatial association

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Indicators of spatial association"}

**Indicators of spatial association** are [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) that evaluate the existence of [clusters](wikipedia://en/669675) in the [spatial](wikipedia://en/27667) arrangement of a given variable. For instance, if we are studying [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) rates among [census tracts](wikipedia://en/105254) in a given city local clusters in the rates mean that there are areas that have higher or lower rates than is to be expected by chance alone; that is, the values occurring are above or below those of a random distribution in space.

:::

## Global indicators {#Global_indicators}

*Image: UnitedStates2022 Population MoransI Report.png.* Spatial Autocorrelation Report generated by ArcGIS Pro for 2022 U.S. county population

Notable global indicators of spatial association include:[^o3]

- **Global [Moran's *I*](wikipedia://en/15308227)**: The most commonly used measure of global [spatial autocorrelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_autocorrelation) or the overall clustering of the spatial data developed by [Patrick Alfred Pierce Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Alfred_Pierce_Moran).[^o4][^o5]
- **[Geary's *C*](wikipedia://en/15300799)** (Geary's Contiguity Ratio): A measure of global spatial autocorrelation developed by [Roy C. Geary](wikipedia://en/15112696) in 1954.[^o6][^o7] It is inversely related to Moran's *I*, but more sensitive to local autocorrelation than Moran's *I*.
- **Getis–Ord *G*** (Getis–Ord global G, Geleral G-Statistic): Introduced by [Arthur Getis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Getis) and [J. Keith Ord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Keith_Ord) in 1992 to supplement Moran's *I*.[^o8]

## Local indicators {#Local_indicators}

*Image: USA Contiguous Poverty 2020 clusters.jpg.* Clusters of the estimated percent of people in poverty by county in the contiguous United States in 2020

*Image: USA Contiguous PopulationwithoutInsurance 2020.jpg.* HotSpot map of the estimated percent of non-institutionalized population without health insurance, by county in the contiguous United States in 2020

Notable local indicators of spatial association (LISA) include:[^o3]

- **Local Moran's *I***: Derived from Global Moran's *I*, it was introduced by [Luc Anselin](wikipedia://en/12921821) in 1995[^o10] and can be computed using [GeoDa](wikipedia://en/9322583).[^o11]
- **Getis–Ord *G<sub>i</sub>*** (local *G<sub>i</sub>*): Developed by Getis and Ord based on their global *G*.
- **INDICATE's I<sub>N</sub>**: Originally developed to assess the spatial distribution of stars,[^o12] can be computed for any discrete 2+D dataset using python-based INDICATE tool available from GitHub.[^o13]
