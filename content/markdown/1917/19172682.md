# Boerdijk–Coxeter helix

*Image: Boerdijk Coxeter Helices.webm.* Edges can be colored into three groups, one helix (cyan) connecting every vertex, two helices (magenta) connecting vertices in steps of two vertices, and three helices (orange) connecting vertices in steps of three vertices[^n1] (see the video's Wikimedia Commons page for a detailed description of its contents).

*Image: Boerdijk helical sphere packing smooth shading.png.* A Boerdijk helical [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621) has each [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) centered at a [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) of the Coxeter helix. Each sphere is in contact with 6 neighboring spheres.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Boerdijk–Coxeter helix"}

The **Boerdijk–Coxeter helix**, named after [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) and Arie Hendrick Boerdijk, is a linear stacking of regular [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606), arranged so that the edges of the complex that belong to only one tetrahedron form three intertwined [helices](wikipedia://en/179924). There are two [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160) forms, with either right-handed or left-handed windings. Unlike any other stacking of [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids), the Boerdijk–Coxeter helix is not rotationally repetitive in 3-dimensional space. Even in an infinite string of stacked tetrahedra, no two tetrahedra will have the same orientation, because the helical pitch per cell is not a rational fraction of the circle. However, modified forms of this helix have been found which are rotationally repetitive,[^n2] and in 4-dimensional space this helix repeats in rings of exactly 30 tetrahedral cells that tessellate the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) surface of the [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447), one of the six regular convex [polychora](wikipedia://en/24979).

:::

[Buckminster Fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller) named it a *tetrahelix* and considered them with regular and irregular tetrahedral elements.[^n3]

## Geometry {#Geometry}

The vertices of the Boerdijk–Coxeter helix composed of tetrahedra with unit edge length can be written in the coordinates:

$$(r\cos n\theta,r\sin n\theta,n h)$$

where $r=3\sqrt{3}/10$, $\theta=\pm\cos^{-1}(-2/3) \approx 131.81^\circ$, $h=1/\sqrt{10}$ and $n$ is an arbitrary integer. The two different values of $\theta$ correspond to the two chiral forms. All vertices are located on the cylinder with radius $r$ along the z-axis. Given how the tetrahedra alternate, this gives an *apparent* twist of $2\theta - \frac{4}{3}\pi \approx 23.62^\circ$ every *two* tetrahedra. There is another inscribed cylinder with radius $1/\sqrt{6}$ inside the helix.[^o6]

## Higher-dimensional geometry {#Higher-dimensional_geometry}

The [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) partitions into [20 rings](wikipedia://en/501447#Boerdijk–Coxeter_helix_rings) of 30 [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606), each a Boerdijk–Coxeter helix.[^n4] When superimposed onto the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) curvature it becomes periodic, with a period of ten vertices, encompassing all 30 cells. The collective of such helices in the 600-cell represent a discrete [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384).[^n5] While in 3 dimensions the edges are helices, in the imposed 3-sphere [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) they are [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) and have no [torsion](wikipedia://en/3039281). They spiral around each other naturally due to the Hopf fibration.[^n6] The collective of edges forms another discrete Hopf [fibration of 12 rings](wikipedia://en/501447#Decagons) with 10 vertices each. These correspond to [rings of 10 dodecahedrons](wikipedia://en/717358#Intertwining_rings) in the dual [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358).

In addition, the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) partitions into two [8-tetrahedron rings](wikipedia://en/716422#Helical_construction), four edges long, and the [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) partitions into a single degenerate [5-tetrahedron ring](wikipedia://en/513821#Boerdijk–Coxeter_helix).

## Related polyhedral helices {#Related_polyhedral_helices}

Equilateral [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) can also be chained together as a helix, with two [vertex configurations](wikipedia://en/3603745), 3.4.3.4 and 3.3.4.3.3.4. This helix exists as finite ring of [30 pyramids in a 4-dimensional polytope](wikipedia://en/2720121#Related_polytopes).

*Image: Square\_pyramid\_helix.png.*

And equilateral [pentagonal pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128461) can be chained with 3 vertex configurations, 3.3.5, 3.5.3.5, and 3.3.3.5.3.3.5:

*Image: Penta\_pyramid\_helix.png.*

## In architecture {#In_architecture}

The [Art Tower Mito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Tower_Mito) is based on a Boerdijk–Coxeter helix.
