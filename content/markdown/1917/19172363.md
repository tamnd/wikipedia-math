# Total curvature

*Image: Winding Number Around Point.svg.* This curve has total curvature 6$\pi$, and index/turning number 3, though it only has [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) 2 about $p$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Total curvature"}

In [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) study of the [differential geometry of curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry_of_curves), the **total curvature** of an [immersed](wikipedia://en/6993953) [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) is the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) of [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) along a curve taken with respect to [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401):

:::

$$\int_a^b k(s)\,ds = 2\pi N.$$

The total curvature of a closed curve is always an integer multiple of 2$\pi$, where *N* is called the *[index of the curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_the_curve)* or *[turning number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_number)* – it is the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) of the unit [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) about the origin, or equivalently the [degree](wikipedia://en/3094450) of the [map](wikipedia://en/516931) to the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) assigning to each point of the curve, the unit tangent vector at that point (a kind of [Gauss map](wikipedia://en/378881)).

## Comparison to surfaces {#Comparison_to_surfaces}

This relationship between a local geometric invariant, the curvature, and a global [topological invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant), the index, is characteristic of results in higher-dimensional [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) such as the [Gauss–Bonnet theorem](wikipedia://en/139229).

## Invariance {#Invariance}

According to the [Whitney–Graustein theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney–Graustein_theorem), the total curvature is invariant under a [regular homotopy](wikipedia://en/6013248) of a curve: it is the degree of the [Gauss map](wikipedia://en/378881). However, it is not invariant under homotopy: passing through a kink (cusp) changes the turning number by 1.

By contrast, [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) about a point is invariant under homotopies that do not pass through the point, and changes by 1 if one passes through the point.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

*Image: Closed polygonal line.svg.* A closed [polygonal chain](wikipedia://en/12056032), with total curvature 2$\pi$.

A finite generalization is that the exterior angles of a triangle, or more generally any [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530), add up to 360° = 2$\pi$ radians, corresponding to a turning number of 1. More generally, [polygonal chains](wikipedia://en/12056032) that do not go back on themselves (no 180° angles) have well-defined total curvature, interpreting the curvature as point masses at the angles.

The [total absolute curvature](wikipedia://en/48503145) of a curve is defined in almost the same way as the total curvature, but using the absolute value of the curvature instead of the signed curvature. It is 2$\pi$ for [convex curves](wikipedia://en/29747989) in the plane, and larger for non-convex curves.[^o6] It can also be generalized to curves in higher dimensional spaces by flattening out the [tangent developable](wikipedia://en/9841185) to $\gamma$ into a plane, and computing the total curvature of the resulting curve. That is, the total curvature of a curve in $n$-dimensional space is

$$\int_a^b \left|\gamma''(s)\right|\operatorname{sgn} \kappa_{n-1}(s)\,ds$$

where $\kappa_{n-1}$ is last Frenet curvature (the [torsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_of_curves) of the curve) and $\operatorname{sgn}$ is the [signum function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signum_function).

The minimum total absolute curvature of any three-dimensional curve representing a given [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) of the knot. This invariant has the value 2$\pi$ for the unknot, but by the [Fáry–Milnor theorem](wikipedia://en/3133127) it is at least 4$\pi$ for any other knot.[^o7]
