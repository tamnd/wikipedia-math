# Harries–Wong graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harries–Wong graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Harries–Wong graph** is a 3-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 70 vertices and 105 edges.

:::

The Harries–Wong graph has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 2, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, radius 6, diameter 6, girth 10 and is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph). It is also a 3-[vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) and 3-[edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph) [non-planar](wikipedia://en/24314) [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508). It has [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o4]

The characteristic polynomial of the Harries–Wong graph is

$$(x-3) (x-1)^4 (x+1)^4 (x+3) (x^2-6) (x^2-2) (x^4-6x^2+2)^5 (x^4-6x^2+3)^4 (x^4-6x^2+6)^5. \,$$

## History {#History}

In 1972, A. T. Balaban published a (3-10)-cage graph, a cubic graph that has as few vertices as possible for girth 10.[^o6] It was the first (3-10)-cage discovered but it was not unique.[^o7]

The complete list of (3-10)-cages and the proof of minimality was given by O'Keefe and Wong in 1980.[^o8] There exist three distinct (3-10)-cage graphs—the [Balaban 10-cage](wikipedia://en/23714047), the [Harries graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harries_graph) and the Harries–Wong graph.[^o9] Moreover, the Harries–Wong graph and Harries graph are [cospectral graphs](wikipedia://en/534914).
