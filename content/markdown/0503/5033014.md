# Igor Frenkel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Igor Frenkel"}

**Igor Borisovich Frenkel** (Игорь Борисович Френкель; born 22 April 1952) is a Russian-American mathematician at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) working in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

Frenkel emigrated to the United States in 1979. He received his PhD from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1980 with a dissertation on the "Orbital Theory for Affine Lie Algebras". He held positions at the [IAS](wikipedia://en/184179) and [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), and a tenured professorship at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), before taking his current job of tenured professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2018.[^o3] He is also a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o4]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

In collaboration with [James Lepowsky](wikipedia://en/5033025) and [Arne Meurman](wikipedia://en/5033047), he constructed the [monster vertex algebra](wikipedia://en/5032859), a vertex algebra which provides a representation of the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422).[^o6][^o7]

Around 1990, as a member of the School of Mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), Frenkel worked on the mathematical [theory of knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_knots), hoping to develop a theory in which the knot would be seen as a physical object. He continued to develop the idea with his student [Mikhail Khovanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khovanov), and their collaboration ultimately led to the discovery of [Khovanov homology](wikipedia://en/2639906), a refinement of the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642), in 2002.[^o8]

A detailed description of Igor Frenkel's research over the years can be found in "Perspectives in Representation Theory". <http://www.math.sunysb.edu/~frenkel60/Frenkel/Conference.html>
