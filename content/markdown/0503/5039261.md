# Pierre Frédéric Sarrus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Frédéric Sarrus"}

**Pierre Frédéric Sarrus** (; 10 March 1798, [Saint-Affrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Affrique) – 20 November 1861) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3]

:::

Sarrus was a professor at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), France (1826–1856) and a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in Paris (1842).[^o4] He is the author of several treatises, including one on the solution of numeric equations with multiple unknowns (1842); one on multiple [integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrals) and their integrability conditions; and one on the determination of the orbits of the [comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comets).

Sarrus discovered a [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) rule for solving the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of a 3-by-3 [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859), named [rule of Sarrus](wikipedia://en/14568414). He also demonstrated the fundamental [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) of the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). Additionally, he also developed the [Sarrus linkage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarrus_linkage), the first linkage capable of transforming rotary motion into perfect straight-line motion, and vice versa. [Sarrus numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarrus_numbers) ([Fermat pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/45891#Definition) to base 2) are also named after Sarrus, who discovered the first number (341).
