# Robert Griess

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Griess"}

**Robert Louis Griess Jr.** (born 1945) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working on [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) and [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebras).[^o3] He is currently the John Griggs Thompson Distinguished University Professor of mathematics at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Griess developed a keen interest in mathematics prior to entering undergraduate studies at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in the fall of 1963.[^o6] There, he eventually earned a Ph.D. in 1971 after defending a dissertation on the [Schur multipliers](wikipedia://en/1121587) of the then-known [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group).[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Griess' work has focused on [group extensions](wikipedia://en/345807), [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) and Schur multipliers, as well as on [vertex operator algebras](wikipedia://en/1287577) and the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), with emphasis on constructions of the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422).[^o9][^o10]

### Friendly Giant {#Friendly_Giant}

In 1982, Robert Griess published the first construction of the largest sporadic group, the [monster group](wikipedia://en/47422) $\mathbb M$ (or $F_1$).[^o15] He first successfully constructed this [group](wikipedia://en/19447) while at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1979–80, 1981, and later in 1994), through rotations of an [object](wikipedia://en/19453961) in 196,883-dimensional space.[^o12]

Originally, Griess called the monster group the **"Friendly Giant"**;[^o15] however, a paper published in 1979 by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Simon P. Norton](wikipedia://en/1641166) in the *[Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_of_the_London_Mathematical_Society)* titled "[Monstrous Moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168)" formally coins the term.[^o13] [Bernd Fischer](wikipedia://en/29628162) and Griess independently predicted the existence of the monster group earlier in the decade, by 1973.[^o12]

Conway, speaking about the monster group on one occasion (in 2011), expressed Griess' dissatisfaction with his choice in naming the Fischer-Griess 196,883-dimensional group, as the "Monster group":[^o14]

By the way, Griess criticized me very strongly for using the term *monster* for it, ... I wrote a postcard to [Fischer](wikipedia://en/29628162), who is the first discoverer of the Monster, saying something like, "I've been thinking a lot about your monster group"... he liked that name, and ever since then, it's been called *the Monster* \[...\] Griess made the point — and I... I agree with him in a way — that to call it a monster suggests it's sort of ugly, or frightening, or something; and I wasn't thinking of that at all, I was just thinking of it as being *tremendously big*. You know, maybe a 'bit \[*sic*\] frightening in a way, but not ugly, really a beautiful thing...

In the same landmark 1982 paper where he published his construction, Griess detailed an organization of the twenty-six sporadic groups into two general families of groups (the [Happy Family](wikipedia://en/324806#Happy_Family) and the [pariahs](wikipedia://en/16569533)).[^o15]

### Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1983 Griess was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Warsaw to give a lecture on the [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) and his construction of the monster group, as the largest of these geometric groups.[^o17] In 2010, he was awarded the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research for his construction of the monster group.[^o18][^o15]

He became a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2007, and a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o19][^o20] In 2020 he became a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o21] Since 2006, Robert Griess has been an editor for Electronic Research Announcements of the AIMS (ERA-AIMS), a peer-review journal.[^o22]
