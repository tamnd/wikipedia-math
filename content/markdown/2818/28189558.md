# Brouwer–Haemers graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Brouwer–Haemers graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Brouwer–Haemers graph** is a 20-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 81 vertices and 810 edges. It is a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856), a [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222), and a [Ramanujan graph](wikipedia://en/849412). Although its construction is folklore, it was named after [Andries Brouwer](wikipedia://en/22709916) and Willem H. Haemers, who proved its uniqueness as a strongly regular graph.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The Brouwer–Haemers graph has several related algebraic constructions. One of the simplest is as a degree-4 generalized [Paley graph](wikipedia://en/5719307): it can be defined by making a vertex for each element in the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $GF(81)$ and an edge for every two elements that differ by a [fourth power](wikipedia://en/665027).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Brouwer–Haemers graph is the unique [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with parameters (81, 20, 1, 6). This means that it has 81 vertices, 20 edges per vertex, 1 triangle per edge, and 6 length-two paths connecting each non-adjacent pair of distinct vertices. As a strongly regular graph with the third parameter equal to 1, the Brouwer–Haemers graph has the property that every edge belongs to a unique triangle; that is, it is [locally linear](wikipedia://en/59938063). Finding large dense graphs with this property is one of the formulations of the [Ruzsa–Szemerédi problem](wikipedia://en/59842050).

As well as being strongly regular it is a [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222).

:::

## History {#History}

Although Brouwer writes that this graph's "construction is folklore", and cites as an early reference a 1964 paper on [Latin squares](wikipedia://en/37447) by Dale M. Mesner, it is named after [Andries Brouwer](wikipedia://en/22709916) and Willem H. Haemers, who in 1992 published a proof that it is the only strongly regular graph with the same parameters.

## Related graphs {#Related_graphs}

The Brouwer–Haemers graph is the first in an infinite family of [Ramanujan graphs](wikipedia://en/849412) defined as generalized [Paley graphs](wikipedia://en/5719307) over fields of characteristic three. With the $3\times 3$ [Rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959) and the [Games graph](wikipedia://en/59961913), it is one of only three possible strongly regular graphs whose parameters have the form $\bigl((n^2+3n-1)^2,n^2(n+3),1,n(n+1)\bigr)$.

It should be distinguished from the [Sudoku graph](wikipedia://en/59925424), a different 20-regular 81-vertex graph. The Sudoku graph is derived from [Sudoku](wikipedia://en/1365807) puzzles by making a vertex for each square of the puzzle and connecting two squares by an edge when they belong to the same row, column, or $3\times 3$ block of the puzzle. It has many 9-vertex [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) and requires 9 colors in any [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743); a 9-coloring of this graph describes a solved Sudoku puzzle. In contrast, for the Brouwer–Haemers graph, the largest cliques are the triangles and the number of colors needed is 7.
