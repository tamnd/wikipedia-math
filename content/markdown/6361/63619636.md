# Remote point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Remote point"}

In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), a **remote point** is a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) $p$ that belongs to the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) $\beta X$ of a [Tychonoff space](wikipedia://en/30554) $X$ but that does not belong to the [topological closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_closure) within $\beta X$ of any [nowhere dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere_dense) subset of $X$.[^o2]

:::

Let $\mathbb{R}$ be the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) with the standard topology. In 1962, [Nathan Fine](wikipedia://en/6042215) and [Leonard Gillman](wikipedia://en/15123686) proved that, assuming the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705): There exists a point $p$ in $\beta \mathbb{R}$ that is not in the closure of *any* [discrete subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_subset) of $\mathbb{R}$ ...[^o3]

Their proof works for any Tychonoff space that is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) and not [pseudocompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudocompact).[^o2]

Chae and [Smith](wikipedia://en/2350459) proved that the existence of remote points is independent, in terms of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214), of the continuum hypothesis for a class of topological spaces that includes [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces).[^o4] Several other mathematical theorems have been proved concerning remote points.[^o5][^o6]
