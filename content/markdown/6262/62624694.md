# Mabel Minerva Young

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mabel Minerva Young"}

**Mabel Minerva Young** (1872 – 1963) was an American mathematician active at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Young was born July 18, 1872, in [Worcester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts). She began study at Wellesley College in 1894. Going to graduate study at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), she graduated with a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1899. First she taught [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_%28academic_discipline%29) at [Northfield Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northfield_Seminary). In 1904 she began her long service at Wellesley College, beginning as an assistant in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and becoming a full professor.

Taking a leave of absence, she studied for her Ph.D. with [Frank Morley](wikipedia://en/589167) at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). Her thesis was titled "Dupin's cyclide as a self-dual surface".[^o4] With her doctoral degree, Young was eventually promoted to [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) and became Lewis Attenbury Stimson Professor of Mathematics at Wellesley College.[^o5]

In 1933 Young contributed an article to [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) on a configuration of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) associated with a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) π.[^o6] Let π be a parabola, *p* and *q* fixed [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482) to π that intersect at T. Then a variable tangent to π forms a triangle with *p* and *q*. The variability of this tangent describes the "single infinity of triangles". The corresponding [orthocenters](wikipedia://en/161244), [circumcenters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter), [centroids](wikipedia://en/187926), and centers of the [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243) are approached using projective properties of the triangles.

Young became [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor) in 1941. She died March 4, 1963, at Wellesley.

## Solutions of AMM problems {#Solutions_of_AMM_problems}

One of the features of *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* is a section devoted to problems articulated by readers, and eventual solutions of said problems. The published solutions are chosen for their [elegance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elegance_%28mathematics%29), and five involving [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) were by Mabel Young.

Given a point and a circle, find the locus of second circles where the [radical axis](wikipedia://en/2093844) of the two circles lies on the given point. Young's [analytical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_geometry) solution established a condition on the radii.[^o8]

A given segment subtends an angle from a point on another line. As the point moves along its line, find the [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of the bisectors of the angles. Young's solution established the class of the envelope curve using [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).[^o9]

Let a point and a pair of intersecting planes be fixed. Then as a variable line lies on the point, find the locus of the midpoint of the segment determined by the planes. Young's solution starts with a line *p* through the point and parallel to the intersection of the planes. She identified the locus as a [hyperbolic cylinder](wikipedia://en/145570) through use of a third parallel midway between the others that is the [projective harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/16094600) of a line at infinity.[^o10]

In a triangle *ABC* the feet of the [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) and midpoints of the sides are used to define three [involutions](wikipedia://en/293450#Projective_geometry). The problem was to show that the [double points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point_%28involution%29) of these involutions are three pairs of opposite vertices of a [complete quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_quadrilateral). Young's solution used the radical axis of the circumcircle and nine-point circle of the triangle.[^o11]

Young proposed construction of a [strophoid](wikipedia://en/2738060): Form triangle *AOB* from a fixed point *A* and a variable *B* on circle centered at *O*. Then the locus of the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of *AOB* is a strophoid.[^o12]

Another problem required the [concurrence of three lines](wikipedia://en/2206157) determined by a triangle's altitudes and angle bisectors. Young's solution pointed to the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point) and [Nagel point](wikipedia://en/9489914) of the triangle to obtain the concurrence.[^o13]
