# The Book of Numbers (math book)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Book of Numbers"}

***The Book of Numbers*** is a 1996 mathematics book by [John H. Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308). It discusses individual numbers, and types of number, that have proved conceptually significant. Topics include the origin of the nursery rhyme "[Hickory Dickory Dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hickory_Dickory_Dock)", [figurate numbers](wikipedia://en/325260), the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918), [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) numbers, the [Metonic cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonic_cycle), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/5826), [nimbers](wikipedia://en/164501), and [surreal numbers](wikipedia://en/51432).[^o4][^o7][^o8]

:::

The Basic Library List Committee of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) has recommended that it be included in undergraduate mathematics libraries.[^o7]

## Reception {#Reception}

Andrew Bremner called the book "a delight" and opined that readers of [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) would appreciate it.[^o4] A. Robert Pargeter found it "fascinating" both for systematic reading and for browsing, and he recommended that school and college libraries carry it.[^o5]

Sarah Gourlie called the book "organized and enlightening", while observing that some topics were considerably more demanding than others.[^o6] Likewise, reviewing the book for the Mathematical Association of America, Allen Stenger noted that while the book only presumed knowledge of high-school [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), it also in places demanded a "high level of mathematical reasoning". Stenger expected that many readers would be unable to follow all of the explanations unaided.[^o7] A retrospective by [Ezra Brown](wikipedia://en/64600200) also commented on the "more than a little sophistication" required to follow some of Conway and Guy's discussions, while finding that the authors' joy "comes through on every page".[^o8]

The [MacTutor History of Mathematics Archive](wikipedia://en/1234374) quotes a review of this book in its biography of Conway, saying that "the publishers should have been required to post a warning label on the front cover indicating that this book contains extremely addictive material."
