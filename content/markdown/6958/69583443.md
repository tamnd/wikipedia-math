# Circumcevian triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Circumcevian triangle"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **circumcevian triangle** is a special [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) associated with a reference triangle and a point in the plane of the triangle. It is also associated with the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the reference triangle.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: CircumCevianTriangle.png.* solid blue red solid #222222 solid brown

Let $P$ be a point in the plane of the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$. Let the lines $AP, BP, CP$ intersect the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of $\triangle ABC$ at $A', B', C'$. The triangle $\triangle A'B'C\boldsymbol{}$ is called the **circumcevian triangle** of $P$ with reference to $\triangle ABC$.[^o4]

:::

## Coordinates {#Coordinates}

Let $a,b,c$ be the side lengths of triangle $\triangle ABC$ and let the [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) of $P$ be $\alpha : \beta : \gamma$. Then the trilinear coordinates of the vertices of the circumcevian triangle of $P$ are as follows:[^o6]

$$\begin{array}{rccccc} A' =& -a\beta\gamma &:& (b\gamma+c\beta)\beta &:& (b\gamma+c\beta)\gamma \\ B' =& (c\alpha +a\gamma)\alpha &:& - b\gamma\alpha &:& (c\alpha +a\gamma) \gamma \\ C' =& (a\beta +b\alpha)\alpha &:& (a\beta +b\alpha)\beta &:& - c\alpha\beta \end{array}$$

## Some properties {#Some_properties}

- Every triangle inscribed in the circumcircle of the reference triangle ABC is congruent to exactly one circumcevian triangle.[^o6]
- The circumcevian triangle of P is similar to the [pedal triangle](wikipedia://en/152983) of P.[^o6]
- The [McCay cubic](wikipedia://en/69592861) is the locus of point P such that the circumcevian triangle of P and ABC are [orthologic](wikipedia://en/69531104).[^o8]
