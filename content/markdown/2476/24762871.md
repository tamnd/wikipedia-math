# Cosocle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cosocle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the term **cosocle** (socle meaning *pedestal* in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)) has several related meanings.

:::

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **cosocle** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G*, denoted by Cosoc(*G*), is the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of all [maximal](wikipedia://en/1472649) [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*.[^o2] If *G* is a [quasisimple group](wikipedia://en/1642729), then Cosoc(*G*) = [Z(*G*)](wikipedia://en/7125).[^o2]

In the context of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), a **cosocle** of a [symmetric Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_Lie_algebra) is the [eigenspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace) of its structural [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) that corresponds to the [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) +1. (A symmetric Lie algebra decomposes into the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of its [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350) and cosocle.)[^o3]

In the context of [module theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_theory), the **cosocle of a module** over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is defined to be the maximal [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1001361) [quotient](wikipedia://en/364820) of the module.[^o4]
