# Malnormal subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Malnormal subgroup"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H$ of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ is termed **malnormal** if for any group element $x$ not in $H$, $H$ and $xHx^{-1}$ [intersect](wikipedia://en/23476429) only in the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962).[^n1]

:::

Some facts about malnormality:

- The intersection of malnormal subgroups is malnormal.[^n2]
- Malnormality is [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463); that is, a malnormal subgroup of a malnormal subgroup is malnormal.[^n3]
- The trivial subgroup and the whole group are malnormal subgroups. A [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) that is also malnormal must be one of these.[^n4]
- Every malnormal subgroup is a special type of [C-group](wikipedia://en/29529118) called a trivial intersection subgroup, or TI subgroup.

When $G$ is [finite](wikipedia://en/323707), a malnormal subgroup $H$ distinct from 1 and $G$ is called a "Frobenius complement".[^n4] The set $N$ of elements of $G$ which are either equal to 1 or non-conjugate to any element of $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, called the "Frobenius kernel", and $G$ is the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of $H$ and $N$.[^n5]
