# Francis Sowerby Macaulay

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Francis Sowerby Macaulay"}

**Francis Sowerby Macaulay** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (11 February 1862, [Witney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witney) – 9 February 1937, [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge)) was an [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) mathematician who made significant contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is known for his 1916 book *The Algebraic Theory of Modular Systems* (an old term for [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977)), which greatly influenced the later course of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). [Cohen–Macaulay rings](wikipedia://en/497407), [Macaulay duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaulay_duality), the [Macaulay resultant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaulay_resultant) and the [Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaulay_computer_algebra_system) and [Macaulay2](wikipedia://en/3184149) [computer algebra systems](wikipedia://en/54427) are named for Macaulay.[^o5]

:::

Macaulay was educated at [Kingswood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswood_School) and graduated with distinction from [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge). He taught the top mathematics class in [St Paul's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Paul's_School_%28London%29) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) from 1885 to 1911. His students included [J. E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) and [G. N. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._N._Watson).

In 1928 Macaulay was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o3]
