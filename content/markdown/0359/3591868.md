# Robert Calderbank

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Calderbank"}

**Robert Calderbank** (born 28 December 1954) is a professor of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and director of the Information Initiative at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).[^o3] He received a [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSc) from [Warwick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) in 1975, an [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSc) from [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1976, and a PhD from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1980, all in mathematics. He joined [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) in 1980, and retired from [AT&T Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Labs) in 2003 as Vice President for Research and [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) and [network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) systems. He then went to [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as a professor of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and [Applied and Computational Mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), before moving to Duke in 2010 to become Dean of Natural Sciences.[^o4]

:::

His contributions to [coding](wikipedia://en/321869) and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) won the [IEEE Information Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Information_Theory_Society) Paper Award in 1995 and 1999.[^o5]

He was elected as a member into the US [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 2005 for leadership in communications research, from advances in algebraic coding theory to signal processing for wire-line and wireless modems.[^o6] He also became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o7]

Calderbank won the 2013 [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal)[^o8] and the 2015 [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award).

He was named a [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow) in the 2021 class of fellows, "for deep contributions to information theory".[^o9]

He is married to [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989).[^o10]
