# Fermi–Walker transport

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fermi–Walker transport"}

**Fermi–Walker transport** is a process in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) used to define a [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) or [reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference) such that all [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) in the frame is due to the presence of mass/energy density and not due to arbitrary spin or rotation of the frame. It was discovered by Fermi in 1921 and rediscovered by Walker in 1932.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Fermi-Walker differentiation"}

## Fermi–Walker differentiation {#Fermi–Walker_differentiation}

In the theory of [Lorentzian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold), Fermi–Walker differentiation is a generalization of [covariant differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_differentiation). In general relativity, Fermi–Walker derivatives of the [spacelike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacelike_vector) vector fields in a frame field, taken with respect to the [timelike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timelike_vector) unit vector field in the frame field, are used to define non-inertial and non-rotating frames, by stipulating that the Fermi–Walker derivatives should vanish. In the special case of [inertial frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_frame), the Fermi–Walker derivatives reduce to covariant derivatives.

With a $(-+++)$ sign convention, this is defined for a vector field *X* along a curve $\gamma(s)$:

$$\frac{D_F X}{d s}=\frac{DX}{d s} - \left(X,\frac{DV}{d s}\right) V + (X,V)\frac{DV}{d s},$$

where $V$ is four-velocity, $D$ is the covariant derivative, and $( \cdot , \cdot )$ is the scalar product. If

$$\frac{D_F X}{d s}=0,$$

then the vector field $X$ is Fermi–Walker transported along the curve.[^o5] Vectors perpendicular to the space of [four-velocities](wikipedia://en/226829) in [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488), e.g., polarization vectors, under Fermi–Walker transport experience [Thomas precession](wikipedia://en/1868229).

Using the Fermi derivative, the [Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargmann–Michel–Telegdi_equation)[^o6] for spin precession of electron in an external electromagnetic field can be written as follows:

$$\frac{D_Fa^{\tau}}{ds} = 2\mu (F^{\tau \lambda} - u^{\tau} u_{\sigma} F^{\sigma \lambda})a_{\lambda},$$

where $a^{\tau}$ and $\mu$ are polarization four-vector and [magnetic moment](wikipedia://en/540979), $u^{\tau}$ is four-velocity of electron, $a^{\tau}a_{\tau} = -u^{\tau}u_{\tau} = -1$, $u^{\tau} a_{\tau}=0$, and $F^{\tau \sigma}$ is the [electromagnetic field strength tensor](wikipedia://en/1912367). The right side describes [Larmor precession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larmor_precession).

:::

## Co-moving coordinate systems {#Co-moving_coordinate_systems}

A coordinate system co-moving with a particle can be defined. If we take the unit vector $v^{\mu}$ as defining an axis in the co-moving coordinate system, then any system transforming with proper time is said to be undergoing Fermi–Walker transport.[^o8]

## Generalised Fermi–Walker differentiation {#Generalised_Fermi–Walker_differentiation}

Fermi–Walker differentiation can be extended for any $V$ where $(V,V)\ne0$ (that is, not a [light-like](wikipedia://en/28758) vector). This is defined for a vector field $X$ along a curve $\gamma(s)$:

$\frac{\mathcal D X}{d s}=\frac{D X}{d s} + \left(X,\frac{DV}{d s}\right)\frac{V}{(V,V)} - \frac{(X,V)}{(V,V)}\frac{DV}{d s} - \left(V,\frac{DV}{d s}\right)\frac{(X,V)}{(V,V)^2} V ,$[^o10]

Except for the last term, which is new, and basically caused by the possibility that $(V, V)$ is not constant, it can be derived by taking the previous equation, and dividing each $V^2$ by $(V,V)$.

If $(V,V)=-1$, then we recover the Fermi–Walker differentiation:

$\left(V,\frac{DV}{d s}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{ds}(V,V)=0\ ,$ and $\frac{\mathcal{D} X}{d s}=\frac{D_F X}{d s} .$
