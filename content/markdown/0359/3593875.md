# Business mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Business mathematics"}

**Business mathematics** are [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) used by [commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) enterprises to record and manage business operations. Commercial organizations use mathematics in [accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting), [inventory management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_inventory_management), [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing), sales [forecasting](wikipedia://en/246074), and [financial analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analysis).[^o2]

:::

Mathematics typically used in commerce includes [elementary arithmetic](wikipedia://en/1567386), [elementary algebra](wikipedia://en/9710), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). For some management problems, more advanced mathematics - [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), [matrix algebra](wikipedia://en/20556859#Basic_operations), and [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) - may be applied.

## High school {#High_school}

*Business mathematics,* sometimes called *commercial math* or *consumer math*, is a group of practical subjects used in [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce) and everyday life. In schools, these subjects are often taught to students who are not planning a university education. In the United States, they are typically offered in [high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_schools) and in schools that grant [associate's degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate's_degrees); elsewhere they may be included under [business studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_studies). These courses often fulfill [the general math credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_the_United_States#Core) for high school students.[^o4]

The emphasis in these courses is on computational skills and their practical application, with practice being predominant. A (U.S.) business math course typically[^o4] includes a review of [elementary arithmetic](wikipedia://en/1567386), including [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions), [decimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimals), and [percentages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentages); elementary [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) is included also. The practical applications of these techniques include [revenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenues), [checking accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checking_accounts), [price discounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discounts_and_allowances), [markups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markup_%28business%29), [payroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payroll) calculations, [simple and compound interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_%28economics%29), [consumer and business credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_%28finance%29), and [mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgages).

## University level {#University_level}

### Undergraduate {#Undergraduate}

*Business mathematics* comprises mathematics [credits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_credit) taken at an [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate) level by [business students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_education#Undergraduate_education). The course[^o7] is often organized around the various [business sub-disciplines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_of_business_management#Areas_of_management_application), including the above applications, and usually includes a separate module on [interest calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_value_of_money); the mathematics itself comprises mainly [algebraic techniques](wikipedia://en/9710#Solving_algebraic_equations).[^o2] Many programs, as mentioned, extend to more sophisticated mathematics. Common credits are *Business Calculus*[^o8] and *[Business Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_statistics)*.[^o9] Programs may also cover matrix operations, as above. At many US universities, business students (instead) study "[finite mathematics](wikipedia://en/226981)",[^o10] a course combining several applicable topics, including basic probability theory, an introduction to linear programming, some theory of matrices, and introductory [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924); the course sometimes includes a high level treatment of calculus.

Since these courses are focused on problems from the business world, the syllabus is then adjusted re [standard courses](wikipedia://en/326471#Content_and_age_levels) in the mathematics or science fields.[^o11] The calculus course especially emphasizes [differentiation](wikipedia://en/7921), leading to [optimization of costs and revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit_maximization). [Integration](wikipedia://en/15532) is less emphasized, as its business applications are fewer — used here[^o12] [in some interest calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_compounding), and for (theoretically) [aggregating costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_curve) and / or [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_revenue) — and it is more technically demanding. Relatedly, in a regular calculus course students study [trigonometric functions](wikipedia://en/30367), whereas business calculus courses often omit them. As regards formal theory, as these are applied courses, very few [proofs or derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_%28mathematics%29) are included, unlike in standard courses.[^o11] (Although see [Bachelor of Science in Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science_in_Business_Administration) and [Bachelor of Business Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Business_Science).) [Intermediate or college algebra](wikipedia://en/9710) is often the only prerequisite for a business calculus course. The statistics syllabus, similarly of an applied nature, is described [under that article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_statistics).

Note that [economics majors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Economics), especially those planning to pursue [graduate study in the field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Economics), are encouraged to instead take regular calculus, as well as [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and other advanced math courses, especially [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478). Some economics programs (instead) include a module in "mathematics for economists",[^o17] providing a bridge between the above "Business Mathematics" courses and [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390). Programs in [management accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_accounting), [operations management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_management), [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management) and [credit management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_management), may similarly include supplementary coursework in relevant quantitative techniques, generally [regression](wikipedia://en/48758386), and often linear programming as above, as well as other optimization techniques and various probability topics.[^o13]

### Postgraduate {#Postgraduate}

[At the postgraduate level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_education#Postgraduate_education), *generalist* management and finance programs include quantitative topics which are foundational for the study in question - often exempting students with an appropriate background. These are usually "interest mathematics" and statistics, both at the above level. [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBA) programs often also include basic [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) (linear programming, as above) with the emphasis on practice, and may combine the topics as "quantitative analysis"; [MSF programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_science_in_finance) may similarly cover applied / [financial econometrics](wikipedia://en/1626747).

More technical [Master's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in these areas, such as those in [management science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_science) and [in quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Quantitative_Finance), will entail a deeper, more theoretical study of operations research and econometrics, and extend to further advanced topics such as [mathematical programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_programming), [Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098), and [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001).[^o15][^o16] These programs, then, do not include or entail "Business mathematics" *per se*.

Where [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) is not a degree requirement, [graduate economics programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Economics) often include "quantitative techniques", which covers (applied) [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), [multivariate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_calculus), and [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033), and may include [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and analysis;[^o17] regardless, econometrics is usually a separate course, and is dealt with in depth. Similarly, [Master of Financial Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Financial_Economics) (and *MSc* Finance) programs often include a (supplementary) course covering the calculus, optimization, linear algebra, and probability techniques required for specific topics.
