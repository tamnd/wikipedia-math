# The Design of Experiments

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Design of Experiments"}

***The Design of Experiments*** is a 1935 book by the English [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685), [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), on experimental design, considered to be a foundational work in modern statistics and experimental methodology.[^o3] The book introduced concepts such as randomization, replication, blocking, and contains Fisher’s influential discussion of the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis), illustrated in the context of the [Lady tasting tea experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_tasting_tea_experiment). The book has had a lasting impact on the development of statistical science, shaping diverse fields such as agriculture, psychology, and medical research. It remains an important reference in the history of applied statistics and the philosophy of scientific testing.[^o3]

:::

## Background {#Background}

At the time of publication, Fisher was a statistician at [Rothamsted Research](wikipedia://en/165688) (formally known as Rothamsted Experimental Station) where he developed statistical methods to analyze agricultural data.[^o5] His broader career included fundamental contributions to statistics, genetics, and evolutionary biology. *The Design of Experiments* built on Fisher’s earlier work, including his book *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](wikipedia://en/1645218)* (1925), and laid the groundwork for the practical application of statistical tests in experimental science.[^o3]

Much of Fisher’s approach grew out of earlier studies at [Rothamsted](wikipedia://en/165688), particularly a 1923 paper with [Winifred Mackenzie](wikipedia://en/73714107) on crop variation.[^o6] In this work, Fisher tied the validity of experimental tests directly to the principle of randomization, arguing that randomly allocating treatments to plots could simulate the independence required for valid inference. This innovation established randomization as the “physical basis of the validity of the test” and became Fisher’s first principle of experimental design.[^o6]

Fisher went on to integrate randomization with two further principles, replication and blocking, to form a general framework for designing experiments. These ideas, first applied to agricultural trials, were presented systematically in *The Design of Experiments*, helping to formalize experimental design as a statistical discipline.[^o6]

## Origin of the Null Hypothesis and the Lady Tasting Tea {#Origin_of_the_Null_Hypothesis_and_the_Lady_Tasting_Tea}

One of the most notable sections of the book describes the origin of the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis). Fisher illustrated it with a wager, a woman claimed to be able to tell whether milk or tea had been poured first into a cup.[^o3] In order to test this, Fisher designed an experiment in which she was presented with eight cups, four of each preparation.[^o9] This came to be known as the [Lady tasting tea experiment](wikipedia://en/26297705).[^o10] He defined the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) as the assumption that she has no such ability to make the distinction, meaning her answers would be due to chance. Under this assumption, the probability of guessing all eight cups correctly was calculated to be 1/70. Fisher argued that if she succeeded to distinguish whether milk or tea had been poured first into a cup, the null hypothesis could be rejected. Fisher stated “the null hypothesis is never proved or established, but is possibly disproved, in the course of experimentation. Every experiment may be said to exist only in order to give the facts a chance of disproving the null hypothesis,”[^o11] thus the concept of a null hypothesis was born. This discussion became central to the philosophy of [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577).[^o11]

### Further Developments in Experimental Designs {#Further_Developments_in_Experimental_Designs}

Beyond establishing the principles of [randomization](wikipedia://en/65462), [replication](wikipedia://en/10306262) and [blocking](wikipedia://en/1822339), *The Design of Experiments* advanced the statistical understanding of error estimation in field trials.[^o13] Before Fisher’s work, agricultural experiments often lacked a coherent theory for estimating experimental error, except in the simplest comparisons. Fisher formalized this process, allowing errors to be estimated directly from the data rather than assumed. His methods revealed the unreliability of earlier experiments that failed to account for natural variability, such as differences in soil fertility.[^o13] Later editions of his related work, *[Statistical Methods for Research Workers](wikipedia://en/1645218)*, introduced further refinements, including the analysis of [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059), a technique developed to improve precision by adjusting for variation.[^o13] Together, these innovations helped organize earlier agricultural practices into a coherent framework for experimental design and analysis.

## Controversies {#Controversies}

Fisher has faced multiple criticisms and controversies through his decades-long career. His work had established a foundation for significance testing that stressed the indication of evidence against the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis), using [p-values](wikipedia://en/554994).[^o15] Ronald Fisher argued that [inductive inference](wikipedia://en/393736) was crucial to the progression of scientific knowledge and that this is “experimentally demonstrable when we know how to conduct an experiment which will rarely fail to give us a statistically significant result.”[^o16] Skeptics, such as Paul L. Morgan and [Jacob Cohen](wikipedia://en/10856724), expressed that there were considerable irregularities in Fisher's disagreements with [probability](wikipedia://en/22934).[^o15][^o17] Cohen had a specific critique regarding Fisher, “who emphatically rejected [Bayesian theory](wikipedia://en/4890) of inverse probability but slipped into invalid Bayesian interpretations of NHST \[null hypothesis significance testing\].”[^o18] Despite Fisher’s statistical legacy, the discourse has argued that his study has contributed to the extensive misinterpretation of [NHST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis_significance_testing) in modern research.[^o18]

In addition to critiques of his work, Fisher has been known to be controversial due to his views on race and history of supporting [eugenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics) policies.[^o18]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

*Image: Ronald Fisher.jpg.* The founding father of *The Design of Experiments*, Ronald A. Fisher, at the time of his scientific research in the 1930s.

After Fisher’s death in 1962, the methods of experimental design and analysis he had established decades earlier had transformed research across the physical, biological and statistical sciences.[^o20] *The Design of Experiments* (1935) and its later editions provided a systematic account of the logic of experimentation, as well as the formulation of the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) into a unified framework for scientific use.[^o3] The book advanced the use of [factorial designs](wikipedia://en/1827237), which allowed researchers to study multiple factors and their interactions simultaneously.[^o20] While ideas of combining treatments in this way was not entirely new, Fisher demonstrated its logical and statistical advantage over older approaches that favoured observation of one factor at a time.[^o20] His framework later influenced fields such as [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) and [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education), where experimental designs gradually replaced purely [correlational](wikipedia://en/157057) studies.[^o20] Fisher’s concepts of [randomization](wikipedia://en/65462), [error analysis](wikipedia://en/44281046), and [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) remain foundational.[^o5]

### Impact of Fisher's Book and Research {#Impact_of_Fisher's_Book_and_Research}

In *The Design of Experiments*, Ronald A. Fisher has made noteworthy inputs within the field of [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) and biological [assay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assay). The book discusses his study of systematically arranged experiments which demonstrated and fully articulated the incorrect assumptions about the independence of plot errors that can be properly eliminated using the process of [randomization](wikipedia://en/65462).[^o13] This is now commonly known as the principle of [randomization](wikipedia://en/65462).

Since laying the groundwork in experimental design and analysis, many of Fisher’s initial works were advanced by other notable researchers, such as [Frank Yates](wikipedia://en/165733).[^o13] [Rothamsted Research](wikipedia://en/165688) was particularly fond of developing the newer concepts of experimental design and analysis. Fisher’s studies have advanced sampling methods for experimental plots, effectively modernizing yield estimates in [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and survey methods in a variety of fields.[^o13] By 1939, studies involving highly variable data had included these strategies which were adapted by [agronomists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agronomists) worldwide.[^o13] Decades after its publication, *The Design of Experiments* continues to shape modern research methodology.[^o13]
