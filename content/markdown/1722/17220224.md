# Bartlett's method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bartlett's method"}

In [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis), **Bartlett's method** (also known as the **method of averaged periodograms**[^o7]), is used for estimating [power spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectrum). It provides a way to reduce the variance of the [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) in exchange for a reduction of resolution, compared to standard [periodograms](wikipedia://en/485457).[^o5][^o6] A final estimate of the spectrum at a given frequency is obtained by averaging the estimates from the periodograms (at the same frequency) derived from non-overlapping portions of the original series.

:::

The method is used in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and applied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). Common applications of Bartlett's method are frequency response measurements and general spectrum analysis.

The method is named after [M. S. Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M_S_Bartlett) who first proposed it.[^o5][^o6]

## Definition and procedure {#Definition_and_procedure}

*Image: Bartlett's method.svg.* The linear spectrum calculated by Bartlett's method.

Bartlett's method consists of the following steps:

1. The original N point data segment is split up into K (non-overlapping) data segments, each of length M
1. For each segment, compute the [periodogram](wikipedia://en/485457) by computing the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811) (DFT version which does not divide by M), then computing the squared magnitude of the result and dividing this by M.
1. Average the result of the [periodograms](wikipedia://en/485457) above for the K data segments.
   1. The averaging reduces the variance, compared to the original N point data segment.

The result is an array of power measurements vs. frequency "bin".

## Related methods {#Related_methods}

- The [Welch method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welch_method): this is a method that uses a modified version of Bartlett's method in which the portions of the series contributing to each periodogram are allowed to overlap.
- Periodogram smoothing.
