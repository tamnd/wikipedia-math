# Engel group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Engel group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an element *x* of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) or a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is called an ***n*-Engel element**,[^o2] named after [Friedrich Engel](wikipedia://en/5686283), if it satisfies the ***n*-Engel condition** that the repeated commutator \[...\[\[*x*,*y*\],*y*\], ..., *y*\][^o3] with *n* copies of *y* is trivial (where \[*x*, *y*\] means *xyx*<sup>−1</sup>*y*<sup>−1</sup> or the [Lie bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_bracket)). It is called an **Engel element** if it satisfies the **[Engel condition](wikipedia://en/36147939)** that it is *n*-Engel for some *n*.

:::

A Lie group or Lie algebra is said to satisfy the **Engel** or ***n*-Engel** conditions if every element does. Such groups or algebras are called **Engel groups**, ***n*-Engel groups**, **Engel algebras**, and ***n*-Engel algebras**.

Every [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569) or Lie algebra is Engel. [Engel's theorem](wikipedia://en/1341657) states that every finite-dimensional Engel algebra is nilpotent. (Cohn 1955) gave examples of non-nilpotent Engel groups and algebras.
