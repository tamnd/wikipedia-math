# Restricted sumset

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Restricted sumset"}

In [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392), a **restricted sumset** has the form

:::

$$S=\{a_1+\cdots+a_n:\ a_1\in A_1,\ldots,a_n\in A_n \ \mathrm{and}\ P(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\not=0\},$$

where $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ are finite [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* and $P(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) over *F*.

If $P$ is a constant non-zero function, for example $P(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=1$ for any $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, then $S$ is the usual [sumset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumset) $A_1+\cdots+A_n$ which is denoted by $nA$ if $A_1=\cdots=A_n=A.$

When

$$P(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \prod_{1 \le i < j \le n} (x_j-x_i),$$

*S* is written as $A_1\dotplus\cdots\dotplus A_n$ which is denoted by $n^{\wedge} A$ if $A_1=\cdots=A_n=A.$

Note that |*S*| > 0 if and only if there exist $a_1\in A_1,\ldots,a_n\in A_n$ with $P(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\not=0.$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Cauchy–Davenport theorem"}

## Cauchy–Davenport theorem {#Cauchy–Davenport_theorem}

The **Cauchy–Davenport theorem**, named after [Augustin Louis Cauchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustin_Louis_Cauchy) and [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111), asserts that for any [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* and nonempty subsets *A* and *B* of the prime-[order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ we have the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489)[^o4][^o5][^o6]

$$|A+B| \ge \min\{p,\, |A|+|B|-1\}$$

where $A+B := \{a+b \pmod p \mid a \in A, b \in B\}$, i.e. using [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087). It can be generalised to arbitrary (not necessarily abelian) groups using a [Dyson transform](wikipedia://en/21871152). If $A, B$ are subsets of a group $G$, then[^o7]

$$|AB| \ge \min\{p(G),\, |A|+|B|-1\}$$

where $p(G)$ is the size of the smallest nontrivial subgroup of $G$ (we set it to $\infty$ if there is no such subgroup).

This can be used to deduce the [Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv_theorem): given any sequence of 2*n*−1 elements in the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, there are *n* elements that sum to zero modulo *n*. (Here *n* does not need to be prime.)[^o8][^o9]

A direct consequence of the Cauchy-Davenport theorem is: Given any sequence *S* of *p*−1 or more nonzero elements, not necessarily distinct, of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, every element of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ can be written as the sum of the elements of some [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) (possibly empty) of *S*.[^o10]

[Kneser's theorem](wikipedia://en/36237048) generalises this to general [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974).[^o11]

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture"}

## Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture {#Erdős–Heilbronn_conjecture}

The **Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture** posed by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Hans Heilbronn](wikipedia://en/4388848) in 1964 states that $|2^\wedge A| \ge \min\{p,\, 2|A|-3\}$ if *p* is a prime and *A* is a nonempty subset of the field **Z**/*p***Z**.[^o14] This was first confirmed by J. A. Dias da Silva and Y. O. Hamidoune in 1994[^o15] who showed that

$$|n^\wedge A| \ge \min\{p(F),\ n|A|-n^2+1\},$$

where *A* is a finite nonempty subset of a field *F*, and *p*(*F*) is a prime *p* if *F* is of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*, and *p*(*F*) = ∞ if *F* is of characteristic 0. Various extensions of this result were given by [Noga Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651), M. B. Nathanson and [I. Ruzsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Z._Ruzsa) in 1996,[^o22] Q. H. Hou and [Zhi-Wei Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhi-Wei_Sun) in 2002,[^o16] and G. Karolyi in 2004.[^o17]

:::

::: {#o19 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Combinatorial Nullstellensatz"}

## Combinatorial Nullstellensatz {#Combinatorial_Nullstellensatz}

A tool in the study of lower bounds for [cardinalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinalities) of various restricted sumsets is the following principle: the combinatorial [Nullstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullstellensatz).[^o20] Let $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be a polynomial over a field $F$. Suppose that the [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) of the [monomial](wikipedia://en/357416) $x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_n^{k_n}$ in $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ is nonzero and $k_1+\cdots+k_n$ is the [total degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_degree) of $f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. If $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ are finite subsets of $F$ with $|A_i|>k_i$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$, then there are $a_1\in A_1,\ldots,a_n\in A_n$ such that $f(a_1,\ldots,a_n) \neq 0$.

This result was rooted in a paper of [N. Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651) and M. Tarsi in 1989,[^o21] and developed by Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa in 1995–1996,[^o22] and reformulated by Alon in 1999.[^o20]
