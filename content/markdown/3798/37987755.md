# Microlife

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Microlife"}

A **microlife** is a unit of [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk) representing half an hour change of [life expectancy](wikipedia://en/18669).[^o3] It is calculated as being roughly a millionth of a 57-year lifetime of adult exposure to the world.[^o3]

:::

Discussed by [David Spiegelhalter](wikipedia://en/2803355) and Alejandro Leiva, and also used by Lin et al.[^o2] for decision analysis, microlives are intended as a simple way of communicating the impact of a lifestyle or environmental risk factor, based on the associated daily proportional effect on expected length of life. Similar to the [micromort](wikipedia://en/13028883) (one in a million probability of death) the microlife is intended for "rough but fair comparisons between the sizes of chronic risks".[^o3] This is to avoid the biasing effects of describing risks in relative hazard ratios, converting them into somewhat tangible units. Similarly they bring long-term future risks into the here-and-now as a gain or loss of time.

The microlife exploits the fact that for small hazard ratios the change in life expectancy is roughly linear.[^o4] They are by necessity rough estimates, based on averages over population and lifetime. Effects of individual variability, short-term or changing habits, and causal factors are not taken into account.(May 2014)
