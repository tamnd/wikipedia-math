# Leonard E. Baum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leonard E. Baum"}

**Leonard Esau Baum** (August 23, 1931 – August 14, 2017) was an American mathematician, known for the [Baum–Welch algorithm](wikipedia://en/822778) and [Baum–Sweet sequence](wikipedia://en/20236431). He graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1953,[^o2] and earned a Ph.D. in mathematics from Harvard in 1958, with a dissertation titled *Derivations in Commutative Semi-Simple Banach Algebras*.[^o3]

:::

He developed the Baum–Welch Algorithm with [Lloyd Welch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_R._Welch) while working for the [Communications Research Division of IDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses#Center_for_Communications_and_Computing).[^o4] It enabled the development of [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition) and had applications in [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) and [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics). He coined the motto of the Communications Research Division: "Bad ideas is good, good ideas is terrific, no ideas is terrible."[^o5][^o6]

Later, in the late 1970s and early 1980s, Baum used mathematical models for [currency trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market), working with [Monemetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies#Monemetrics), a predecessor of [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) management company [Renaissance Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies).[^o7] He left the firm in 1984 amid steep losses.[^o6] In his later years, he would participate in [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29) tournaments and work on mathematical problems relating to prime numbers and the Riemann hypothesis.[^o5] He died at his home in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), on August 14, 2017, at the age of 86.[^o5]
