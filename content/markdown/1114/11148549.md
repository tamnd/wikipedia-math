# Abstract family of languages

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abstract family of languages"}

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), in particular in the field of [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939) theory, an **abstract family of languages** is an abstract mathematical notion generalizing characteristics common to the [regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723), the [context-free languages](wikipedia://en/6867) and the [recursively enumerable languages](wikipedia://en/54789), and other families of formal languages studied in the scientific literature.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definitions"}

## Formal definitions {#Formal_definitions}

A *formal language* is a set $L$ for which there exists a finite set of abstract symbols $\Sigma$ such that $L \subseteq\Sigma^*$, where \* is the [Kleene star](wikipedia://en/16750) operation.

A *family of languages* is an ordered pair $(\Sigma,\Lambda)$, where

1. $\Sigma$ is an infinite set of symbols;
1. $\Lambda$ is a set of formal languages;
1. For each $L$ in $\Lambda$ there exists a finite subset $\Sigma_1 \subseteq \Sigma$ such that $L \subseteq \Sigma_1^*$; and
1. $L \ne \text{Ø}$ for some $L$ in $\Lambda$.

A *trio* is a family of languages [closed](wikipedia://en/293802) under [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) that do not introduce the empty word, inverse homomorphisms, and intersections with a regular language.

A *full trio,* also called a *[cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28formal_languages%29),* is a trio closed under arbitrary homomorphism.

A *(full) semi-AFL* is a (full) trio closed under [union](wikipedia://en/47949).

A *(full) AFL* is a *(full) semi-AFL* closed under [concatenation](wikipedia://en/64474) and the [Kleene plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleene_plus).

:::

## Some families of languages {#Some_families_of_languages}

The following are some simple results from the study of abstract families of languages.

Within the [Chomsky hierarchy](wikipedia://en/6011), the regular languages, the context-free languages, and the recursively enumerable languages are all full AFLs. However, the [context sensitive languages](wikipedia://en/6212) and the [recursive languages](wikipedia://en/23290990) are AFLs, but not full AFLs because they are not closed under arbitrary homomorphisms.

The family of regular languages are contained within any cone (full trio). Other categories of abstract families are identifiable by closure under other operations such as shuffle, reversal, or substitution.

## Origins {#Origins}

[Seymour Ginsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Ginsburg) of the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) and [Sheila Greibach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Greibach) of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) presented the first AFL theory paper at the IEEE Eighth Annual [Symposium on Switching and Automata Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Switching_and_Automata_Theory) in 1967.[^o8]
