# Analytically irreducible ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analytically irreducible ring"}

In algebra, an **analytically irreducible ring** is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) whose [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) has no [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441). Geometrically this corresponds to a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) with only one analytic branch at a point.

:::

(Zariski 1948) proved that if a local ring of an algebraic variety is a [normal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ring), then it is analytically irreducible. There are many examples of reduced and irreducible local rings that are analytically reducible, such as the local ring of a [node](wikipedia://en/4230456) of an irreducible curve, but it is hard to find examples that are also normal. (txt) gave such an example of a normal [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) local ring that is analytically reducible.

## Nagata's example {#Nagata's_example}

Suppose that *K* is a field of characteristic not 2, and *K* *x*,*y* is the [formal power series ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_power_series_ring) over *K* in 2 variables. Let *R* be the subring of *K* *x*,*y* generated by *x*, *y*, and the elements *z*<sub>*n*</sub> and localized at these elements, where

$w=\sum_{m>0} a_mx^m$ is [transcendental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_element) over *K*(*x*)

$$z_1=(y+w)^2$$

$$z_{n+1}=(z_1-(y+\sum_{0<m<n}a_mx^m)^2)/x^n.$$

Then *R*\[*X*\]/(*X*<sup>2</sup>–*z*<sub>1</sub>) is a normal Noetherian local ring that is analytically reducible.
