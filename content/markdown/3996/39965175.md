# Alan Edelman

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Edelman"}

**Alan Stuart Edelman** (born June 1963) is an American mathematician and computer scientist. He is a professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) and a Principal Investigator at the [MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory) (CSAIL) where he leads a group in applied computing. In 2004, he founded a business called Interactive Supercomputing which was later acquired by [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft). Edelman is a fellow of [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS), [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM), [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers) (IEEE), and [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) (ACM), for his contributions in numerical linear algebra, computational science, parallel computing, and random matrix theory. He is one of the creators of the technical programming language [Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Edelman received B.S. and M.S. degrees in mathematics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1984, and a Ph.D. in applied mathematics from MIT in 1989 under the direction of [Lloyd N. Trefethen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_N._Trefethen). Following a year at [Thinking Machines Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_Machines_Corporation), and at CERFACS[^o5] in France, Edelman went to [U.C. Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.C._Berkeley) as a Morrey Assistant Professor and Levy Fellow, 1990–93. He joined the MIT faculty in applied mathematics in 1993.

## Research {#Research}

Edelman's research interests include high-performance computing, numerical computation, linear algebra, and [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory).

- In random matrix theory, Edelman is known for the Edelman distribution of the smallest singular value of random matrices (also known as Edelman's law[^o7]), the invention of [beta ensembles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_ensemble),[^o8] and the introduction of the stochastic operator approach,[^o9] and some of the earliest computational approaches.
- In high performance computing, Edelman is known for his work on parallel computing, as the co-founder of Interactive Supercomputing, as an inventor of the [Julia programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_%28programming_language%29) and for his work on the Future [Fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512). As the leader of the Julialab, he supervises work on scientific machine learning and compiler methodologies.
- In [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660), Edelman is known for eigenvalues and condition numbers of random matrices, the geometry of algorithms with orthogonality constraints, the geometry of the [generalized singular value decomposition](wikipedia://en/1007903) (GSVD), and applications of [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) to matrix factorizations.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

A [Sloan fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship), Edelman received a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) Faculty Career award in 1995. He has received numerous other awards, among them the

- 1989 [Gordon Bell Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Bell_Prize)[^o11][^o12]
- 1990 Householder Prize
- 1998 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize)[^o13]
- 1999 Edgerly Science Partnership Award
- 2000 SIAM Activity Group on Linear Algebra Prize
- 2005 [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award)[^o14] (with [Gilbert Strang](wikipedia://en/2613188))
- 2011 Fellow of SIAM[^o15]
- 2015 Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o16]
- 2017 Member of IEEE Fellow Class of 2018[^o17]
- 2019 Winner [Sidney Fernbach Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Fernbach_Award) by the [IEEE Computer Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Computer_Society)[^o18]
- 2021 ACM Fellow of Class 2020[^o19]
- 2024 Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)[^o20]
