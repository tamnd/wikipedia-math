# Ralph Greenberg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph Greenberg"}

**Ralph Greenberg** (born 1944) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who has made contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), in particular [Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/396150).

:::

He was born in [Chester, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester,_Pennsylvania)[^o3] and studied at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), earning a B.A. in 1966,[^o3] after which he attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), earning his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1971 under the supervision of [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa).[^o4]

Greenberg's results include a proof (joint with [Glenn Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Stevens_%28mathematician%29)) of the [Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum_conjecture) as well as a formula for the derivative of a [*p*-adic Dirichlet *L*-function](wikipedia://en/22059815) at $s=0$ (joint with Bruce Ferrero). Greenberg is also well known for [his many conjectures](wikipedia://en/60008412). In his PhD thesis, he conjectured that the Iwasawa μ- and λ-invariants of the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of a [totally real field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) are zero, a conjecture that remains open as of September 2012. In the 1980s, he introduced the notion of a [Selmer group](wikipedia://en/1970862) for a [*p*-adic Galois representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Galois_representation) and generalized the ["main conjectures"](wikipedia://en/22091332) of Iwasawa and [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) to this setting. He has since generalized this setup to present Iwasawa theory as the theory of *p*-adic deformations of [motives](wikipedia://en/430680). He also provided an arithmetic theory of *L*-invariants generalizing his aforementioned work with Stevens.

Greenberg was an invited speaker in [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) 2010, [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad,_India) on the topic of "*Number Theory.*"[^o5]

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]

In the late 1990s and early 2000s, Greenberg publicly disputed [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) [conspiracy theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theorist) and [pseudoscientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscientist) [Richard C. Hoagland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_C._Hoagland)'s mathematical interpretations of the so-called "D&M Pyramid" and surrounding features found on the [Cydonia Planitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cydonia_%28Mars%29) region of Mars as being conclusive signs of extraterrestrial intelligence and challenged him to a public debate. Hoagland has yet to respond.[^o7]
