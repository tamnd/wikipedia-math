# Farrell–Markushevich theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Farrell–Markushevich theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Farrell–Markushevich theorem**, proved independently by O. J. Farrell (1899–1981)[^o2] and [A. I. Markushevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._I._Markushevich) (1908–1979) in 1934, is a result concerning the approximation in mean square of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on a bounded open set in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) by complex polynomials. It states that complex polynomials form a dense subspace of the [Bergman space](wikipedia://en/3205927) of a domain bounded by a simple closed [Jordan curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_curve). The [Gram–Schmidt process](wikipedia://en/82361) can be used to construct an orthonormal basis in the Bergman space and hence an explicit form of the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923), which in turn yields an explicit [Riemann mapping function](wikipedia://en/26215) for the domain.

:::

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Let Ω be the bounded Jordan domain and let Ω<sub>*n*</sub> be bounded Jordan domains decreasing to Ω, with Ω<sub>*n*</sub> containing the closure of Ω<sub>*n* + 1</sub>. By the Riemann mapping theorem there is a conformal mapping *f*<sub>*n*</sub> of Ω<sub>*n*</sub> onto Ω, normalised to fix a given point in Ω with positive derivative there. By the [Carathéodory kernel theorem](wikipedia://en/34145780) *f*<sub>*n*</sub>(*z*) converges uniformly on compacta in Ω to *z*.[^o5] In fact Carathéodory's theorem implies that the inverse maps tend uniformly on compacta to *z*. Given a subsequence of *f*<sub>*n*</sub>, it has a subsequence, convergent on compacta in Ω. Since the inverse functions converge to *z*, it follows that the subsequence converges to *z* on compacta. Hence *f*<sub>*n*</sub> converges to *z* on compacta in Ω.

As a consequence the derivative of *f*<sub>*n*</sub> tends to 1 uniformly on compacta.

Let *g* be a square integrable holomorphic function on Ω, i.e. an element of the Bergman space A<sup>2</sup>(Ω). Define *g*<sub>*n*</sub> on Ω<sub>*n*</sub> by *g*<sub>*n*</sub>(*z*) = *g*(*f*<sub>*n*</sub>(*z*))*f*<sub>*n*</sub>'(*z*). By change of variable

$$\displaystyle{\|g_n\|^2_{\Omega_n} =\|g\|_\Omega^2.}$$

Let *h*<sub>*n*</sub> be the restriction of *g*<sub>*n*</sub> to Ω. Then the norm of *h*<sub>*n*</sub> is less than that of *g*<sub>*n*</sub>. Thus these norms are uniformly bounded. Passing to a subsequence if necessary, it can therefore be assumed that *h*<sub>*n*</sub> has a weak limit in A<sup>2</sup>(Ω). On the other hand, *h*<sub>*n*</sub> tends uniformly on compacta to *g*. Since the evaluation maps are continuous linear functions on A<sup>2</sup>(Ω), *g* is the weak limit of *h*<sub>*n*</sub>. On the other hand, by [Runge's theorem](wikipedia://en/3199591), *h*<sub>*n*</sub> lies in the closed subspace *K* of *A*<sup>2</sup>(Ω) generated by complex polynomials. Hence *g* lies in the weak closure of *K*, which is *K* itself.[^o6]

:::
