# Nernst heat theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nernst heat theorem"}

*Image: Walther Nernst.jpg.* Walther Nernst

The **Nernst heat theorem** was formulated by [Walther Nernst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_Nernst) early in the twentieth century and was used in the development of the [third law of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/225617).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="The theorem"}

## The theorem {#The_theorem}

The Nernst heat theorem says that as absolute zero is approached, the [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891) change Δ*S* for a chemical or physical transformation approaches 0. This can be expressed mathematically as follows:

$$\lim_{T \to 0} \Delta S = 0$$

The above equation is a modern statement of the theorem. Nernst often used a form that avoided the concept of entropy.[^o4]

*Image: Nernst Theorem graph.jpg.* Enthalpy and free energy both have a slope of zero and become equal as temperature approaches absolute zero

Another way of looking at the theorem is to start with the definition of the [Gibbs free energy](wikipedia://en/238181) (*G*), $G = H-TS$, where *H* stands for [enthalpy](wikipedia://en/10274). For a change from reactants to products at constant temperature and pressure the equation becomes $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$.

In the limit of *T* = 0 the equation reduces to just Δ*G* = Δ*H*, as illustrated in the figure shown here, which is supported by experimental data.[^o5] However, it is known from [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/204469) that the slope of the Δ*G* curve is −Δ*S*. Since the slope shown here reaches the horizontal limit of 0 as *T* → 0 then the implication is that Δ*S* → 0, which is the Nernst heat theorem.

The significance of the Nernst heat theorem is that it was later used by [Max Planck](wikipedia://en/19848) to give the [third law of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/225617), which is that the entropy of all pure, perfectly crystalline homogeneous materials in complete internal equilibrium is 0 at [absolute zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_zero).
