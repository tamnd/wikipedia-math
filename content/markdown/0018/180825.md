# Bruno de Finetti

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bruno de Finetti"}

**Bruno de Finetti** (13 June 1906 – 20 July 1985) was an Italian [probabilist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_probabilists) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405), noted for the "operational subjective" conception of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). The classic exposition of his distinctive theory is the 1937 “La prévision: ses lois logiques, ses sources subjectives",[^o3] which discussed probability founded on the coherence of betting odds and the consequences of [exchangeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchangeability).

:::

## Life {#Life}

De Finetti was born in [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck), Austria-Hungary, and studied mathematics at [Politecnico di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politecnico_di_Milano). He graduated in 1927, writing his thesis under the supervision of [Giulio Vivanti](wikipedia://en/34327454). After graduation, he worked as an actuary and a statistician at the Istituto Nazionale di Statistica ([National Institute of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Statistics_%28Italy%29)) in Rome and, from 1931, the [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) insurance company [Assicurazioni Generali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assicurazioni_Generali). In 1936 he won a competition for Chair of Financial Mathematics and Statistics, but was not nominated due to a fascist law barring access to unmarried candidates;[^o5] he was appointed as ordinary professor at the [University of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste) only in 1950.

He published extensively (17 papers in 1930 alone, according to Lindley) and acquired an international reputation in the small world of probability mathematicians. He taught [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) in Padua and then won a chair in Financial Mathematics at [Trieste University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste_University) (1939). In 1954, he moved to the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), first to another chair in Financial Mathematics and then, from 1961 to 1976, one in the Calculus of Probabilities. De Finetti developed his ideas on subjective probability in the 1920s independently of [Frank P.&nbsp;Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_P.&nbsp;Ramsey).[^o6] Later, according to the preface of his *Theory of Probability*, he drew on ideas of [Harold Jeffreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Jeffreys), [I.&nbsp;J.&nbsp;Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.&nbsp;J.&nbsp;Good) and [B. O. Koopman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Koopman). He also reasoned about the connection of economics and probability, and thought that guiding principles to be [Paretian optimum](wikipedia://en/45468) were further inspired by "fairness" criteria.[^o7] De Finetti held different social and political beliefs through his life: following [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) during his youth, then moving to [Christian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_socialism) and finally adhering to the [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_%28Italy%29).[^o5][^o8]

De Finett became known in the Anglo-American statistical world only in the 1950s when [L.&nbsp;J.&nbsp;Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.&nbsp;J.&nbsp;Savage), who had independently adopted [subjectivism](wikipedia://en/49571), drew him into it; another great champion was [Dennis Lindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Lindley). De Finetti died in Rome in 1985.

## Work and impact {#Work_and_impact}

De Finetti emphasized a [predictive inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictive_inference) approach to statistics; he proposed a [thought experiment](wikipedia://en/49535) along the following lines (described in greater detail at [coherence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_%28philosophical_gambling_strategy%29)): *You* must set the price of a promise to pay \$1 if there was life on Mars 1 billion years ago, and \$0 if there was not, and tomorrow the answer will be revealed. You know that *your opponent* will be able to choose either to buy such a promise from you at the price you have set, or require you to buy such a promise from your opponent, still at the same price. In other words: you set the odds, but your opponent decides which side of the bet will be yours. The price you set is the "operational subjective probability" that you assign to the proposition on which you are betting. This price has to obey the probability axioms if you are not to face certain loss, as you would if you set a price above \$1 (or a negative price). By considering bets on more than one event, de Finetti could justify additivity. Prices, or equivalently odds, that do not expose you to certain loss through a [Dutch book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_book) are called *coherent*.

De Finetti is also noted for [de Finetti's theorem](wikipedia://en/180835) on exchangeable sequences of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685). De Finetti was not the first to study exchangeability, but he brought the subject to greater visibility. He started publishing on exchangeability in the late 1920s, but his 1937 article "La Prévision" (see bibliography) is his most famous treatment.

In 1929, de Finetti introduced the concept of [infinitely divisible probability distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitely_divisible_probability_distribution).

He also introduced [de Finetti diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Finetti_diagram) for graphing [genotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype) frequencies.

The 1974 English translation of his book is credited with reviving interest in predictive inference in the Anglophone world and bringing the idea of exchangeability to its attention.[^o10]

In 1961, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o11] The de Finetti Award, presented annually by the [European Association for Decision Making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Association_for_Decision_Making), is named after him. The Department of Mathematics, Statistics and Economics of the [University of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste) is named after him too.

In the 21st century [quantum extensions](wikipedia://en/180835#Extensions) of de Finetti's representation theorem have been found to be useful in [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274),[^o12][^o13][^o14] in topics like [quantum key distribution](wikipedia://en/51910)[^o15] and [entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336) detection.[^o16]
