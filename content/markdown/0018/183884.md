# Rhombus

*Image: Symmetries of square.svg.* The rhombus has a square as a special case, and is a special case of a [kite](wikipedia://en/84840) and [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496).

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rhombus"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rhombus** (: **rhombi** or **rhombuses**) is an **equilateral quadrilateral**, a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) whose four sides all have the same length. Other names for rhombus include **diamond**, **[lozenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_%28shape%29)**, and **[calisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calisson)**.

:::

Every rhombus is a [simple polygon](wikipedia://en/1059530) (having no self-intersections). A rhombus is a special case of a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) and a [kite](wikipedia://en/84840). A rhombus with right angles is a [square](wikipedia://en/659939). A non-square rhombus has two opposite [acute angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle) and two opposite [obtuse angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_angle).

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *rhombus* comes from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ῥόμβος grc, meaning something that spins, such as a [bullroarer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullroarer) or an ancient precursor of the [button whirligig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Button_whirligig).[^o4] The word was used both by [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) and [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), who also used the term "solid rhombus" for a [bicone](wikipedia://en/13953265), two right circular [cones](wikipedia://en/782427) sharing a common base.[^o5] A planar *rhombus* is a [cross section](wikipedia://en/1337370) of a bicone.

The name *diamond* comes from the shape of an [octahedral](wikipedia://en/22458) [diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond) gemstone; the [diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamonds_%28suit%29) suit in [playing cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_card) is named after the shape – it was originally called *carreaux* (lit. "squares") in French.[^o6] In the context of [polyiamonds](wikipedia://en/185271), shapes like [polyominos](wikipedia://en/72634) but constructed from equilateral triangles, a *diamond* is a rhombus with a 60° angle.

The etymology of *[lozenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozenge_%28shape%29)* is uncertain. It might come from a shape of some [lauzinaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauzinaj) almond pastries, or from the shape of tombstones. A lozenge is often specifically to a rhombus with a 45° angle.

A *[calisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calisson)* is a type of rhombus-shaped French sweet.[^o7]

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

*Image: Haltern am See, Naturpark Hohe Mark, Hohemarkenbusch, Baumstamm -- 2024 -- 4411 (kreativ 2).jpg.* An [ICM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentional_camera_movement) photo with a diamond-shaped composition.

A [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530) (non-[self-intersecting](wikipedia://en/50055439)) quadrilateral is a rhombus [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is any one of the following:[^o10][^o11]

- a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) in which a [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) bisects an [interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_and_external_angle)
- a parallelogram in which at least two consecutive sides are equal in length
- a parallelogram in which the diagonals are perpendicular (an [orthodiagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodiagonal) parallelogram)
- a quadrilateral with four sides of equal length (by definition)
- a quadrilateral in which the diagonals are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) and [bisect](wikipedia://en/152547) each other
- a quadrilateral in which each diagonal bisects two opposite interior angles
- a quadrilateral *ABCD* possessing a point *P* in its plane such that the four triangles *ABP*, *BCP*, *CDP*, and *DAP* are all [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330)[^o12]
- a quadrilateral *ABCD* in which the [incircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle_and_excircles_of_a_triangle) in triangles *ABC*, *BCD*, *CDA* and *DAB* have a common point[^o13]

:::

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

Every rhombus has two [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) connecting pairs of opposite vertices, and two pairs of parallel sides. Using [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), one can [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) that the rhombus is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/53741) across each of these diagonals. It follows that any rhombus has the following properties:

- Opposite [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) of a rhombus have equal measure.
- The two diagonals of a rhombus are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944); that is, a rhombus is an [orthodiagonal quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/30425383).
- Its diagonals bisect opposite angles.

The first property implies that every rhombus is a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496). A rhombus therefore has all of the [properties of a parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496#Properties): for example, opposite sides are parallel; adjacent angles are [supplementary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplementary_angles); the two diagonals [bisect](wikipedia://en/152547) one another; any line through the midpoint bisects the area; and the sum of the squares of the sides equals the sum of the squares of the diagonals (the [parallelogram law](wikipedia://en/362953)). Thus denoting the common side as *a* and the diagonals as *p* and *q*, in every rhombus

$$\displaystyle 4a^2=p^2+q^2.$$

Not every parallelogram is a rhombus, though any parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals (the second property) is a rhombus. In general, any quadrilateral with perpendicular diagonals, one of which is a line of symmetry, is a [kite](wikipedia://en/84840). Every rhombus is a kite, and any quadrilateral that is both a kite and parallelogram is a rhombus.

A rhombus is a [tangential quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/9176412). That is, it has an [inscribed circle](wikipedia://en/4470203) that is tangent to all four sides.

*Image: Rhombus1.svg.* A rhombus. The height *h* is the perpendicular distance between any two non-adjacent sides, which equals the diameter of the circle inscribed. The diagonals of lengths *p* and *q* are the red dotted line segments.

:::

## Diagonals {#Diagonals}

The length of the diagonals *p = AC* and *q = BD* can be expressed in terms of the rhombus side *a* and one vertex angle *α* as

$$p=a\sqrt{2+2\cos{\alpha}}$$

and

$$q=a\sqrt{2-2\cos{\alpha}}.$$

These formulas are a direct consequence of the [law of cosines](wikipedia://en/19480890).

## Inradius {#Inradius}

The inradius (the radius of a circle [inscribed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed) in the rhombus), denoted by $r$, can be expressed in terms of the diagonals $p$ and $q$ as[^o16]

$$r = \frac{p \cdot q}{2\sqrt{p^2+q^2}},$$

or in terms of the side length $a$ and any vertex angle $\alpha$ or $\beta$ as

$$r = \frac{a\sin\alpha}{2} = \frac{a\sin\beta}{2}.$$

## Area {#Area}

As for all [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496), the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) *K* of a rhombus is the product of its [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) and its [height](wikipedia://en/239972) (*h*). The base is simply any side length *a*:

$$K = a \cdot h .$$

The area can also be expressed as the [base](wikipedia://en/12158034) [squared](wikipedia://en/659942) times the sine of any angle:

$$K = a^2 \cdot \sin \alpha = a^2 \cdot \sin \beta ,$$

or in terms of the height and a [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) [angle](wikipedia://en/1196):

$$K=\frac{h^2}{\sin\alpha} ,$$

or as half the product of the [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) *p*, *q*:

$$K = \frac{p \cdot q}{2} ,$$

or as the [semiperimeter](wikipedia://en/886617) times the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) [inscribed](wikipedia://en/4470203) in the rhombus (inradius):

$$K = 2a \cdot r .$$

Another way, in common with parallelograms, is to consider two adjacent sides as vectors, forming a [bivector](wikipedia://en/884040), so the area is the magnitude of the bivector (the magnitude of the vector product of the two vectors), which is the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the two vectors' Cartesian coordinates: *K* = *x*<sub>1</sub>*y*<sub>2</sub> – *x*<sub>2</sub>*y*<sub>1</sub>.

:::

## Dual properties {#Dual_properties}

The [dual polygon](wikipedia://en/19865549) of a rhombus is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048):[^o22]

- A rhombus has all sides equal, while a rectangle has all angles equal.
- A rhombus has opposite angles equal, while a rectangle has opposite sides equal.
- A rhombus has an inscribed circle, while a rectangle has a [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432).
- A rhombus has an axis of symmetry through each pair of opposite vertex angles, while a rectangle has an axis of symmetry through each pair of opposite sides.
- The diagonals of a rhombus intersect at equal angles, while the diagonals of a rectangle are equal in length.
- The figure formed by joining the midpoints of the sides of a rhombus is a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048), and vice versa.

## Cartesian equation {#Cartesian_equation}

The sides of a rhombus centered at the origin, with diagonals each falling on an axis, consist of all points (*x, y*) satisfying

$$\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|\! + \left|\frac{y}{b}\right|\! = 1.$$

The vertices are at $(\pm a, 0)$ and $(0, \pm b).$ This is a special case of the [superellipse](wikipedia://en/52211), with exponent 1.

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

- One of the five 2D [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) types is the rhombic lattice, also called [centered rectangular lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centered_rectangular_lattice).
- Rhombi can tile the 2D plane edge-to-edge and periodically in three different ways, including, for the 60° rhombus, the [rhombille tiling](wikipedia://en/2867012).

### As the faces of a polyhedron {#As_the_faces_of_a_polyhedron}

Convex polyhedra with rhombi include the infinite set of rhombic [zonohedrons](wikipedia://en/669402), which can be seen as projective envelopes of [hypercubes](wikipedia://en/39783).

- A [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406) (also called a rhombic hexahedron) is a three-dimensional figure like a [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802) (also called a rectangular parallelepiped), except that its 3 pairs of parallel faces are up to 3 types of rhombi instead of rectangles.
- The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with 12 [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) rhombi as its [faces](wikipedia://en/149926).
- The [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with 30 [golden rhombi](wikipedia://en/20825292) (rhombi whose diagonals are in the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)) as its faces.
- The [great rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/20823563) is a nonconvex [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120), [isotoxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotoxal) [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with 30 intersecting rhombic faces.
- The [rhombic hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/43733185) is a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the rhombic triacontahedron. It is nonconvex with 60 [golden rhombic](wikipedia://en/20825292) faces with [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844).
- The [rhombic enneacontahedron](wikipedia://en/1707682) is a polyhedron composed of 90 rhombic faces, with three, five, or six rhombi meeting at each vertex. It has 60 broad rhombi and 30 slim ones.
- The [rhombic icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3691443) is a polyhedron composed of 20 rhombic faces, of which three, four, or five meet at each vertex. It has 10 faces on the polar axis with 10 faces following the equator.
