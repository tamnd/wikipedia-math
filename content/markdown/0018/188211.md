# Pidgin (software)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Pidgin"}

**Pidgin** (formerly named **Gaim**) is a [free and open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software) [multi-platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-platform) [instant messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_messaging) client, based on a [library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_%28computing%29) named **libpurple** that has support for many instant messaging [protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_%28computing%29), allowing the user to simultaneously log in to various services from a single application, with a single interface for both popular and obsolete protocols (from [AIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM_%28software%29) to [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord)), thus avoiding the hassle of having to deal with new software for each device and protocol.

:::

In , the number of Pidgin users was estimated to be over three million.[^o4]

Pidgin is for its [Off-the-Record Messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-Record_Messaging) (OTR) plugin, which offers [end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362).

## History {#History}

*Image: Gaim-buddylist-2.0.0b6.png.* Gaim 2.0.0 beta 6 running under [GNOME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNOME) 2.16.0

The program was originally written by [Mark Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Spencer_%28computer_engineer%29), an [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University) sophomore, as an emulation of AOL's IM program [AOL Instant Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL_Instant_Messenger) on Linux using the [GTK+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTK+) toolkit.[^o6] The earliest archived release was on December 31, 1998.[^o7] It was named GAIM (*GTK+ AOL Instant Messenger*) accordingly. The emulation was not based on [reverse engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering), but instead relied on information about the protocol that AOL had published on the web. Development was assisted by some of AOL's technical staff.[^o6][^o8] Support for other IM protocols was added soon thereafter.[^o6]

In response to pressure from [AOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOL), the program was renamed to the [acronymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym)-but-lowercase *gaim*. As AOL Instant Messenger gained popularity, AOL trademarked its acronym, "AIM", leading to a lengthy legal struggle with the creators of GAIM, who kept the matter largely secret.[^o9]

On April 6, 2007, the project development team announced the results of their settlement with AOL, which included a series of name changes: *Gaim* became *Pidgin*, *libgaim* became *libpurple*, and *gaim-text* (the [command-line interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface) version) became *[Finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finch_%28software%29)*. The name Pidgin was chosen in reference to the term "[pidgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pidgin)", which describes communication between people who do not share a common language.[^o10] The name "purple" refers to "prpl", the internal libgaim name for an *IM protocol plugin*.[^o11]

Due to the legal issues, version 2.0 of the software was frozen in beta stages. Following the settlement, it was announced that the first official release of Pidgin 2.0.0 was hoped to occur during the two weeks from April 8, 2007.[^o10] However, Pidgin 2.0 was not released as scheduled; Pidgin developers announced on April 22, 2007, that the delay was due to the preferences directory "`.gaim`".[^o12]

Pidgin 2.0.0 was released on May 3, 2007. Other visual changes were made to the interface in this version, including updated icons.[^o13]

On 6 July 2015, Pidgin scored seven out of seven points on the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation)'s secure messaging scorecard.[^o14] They have received points for having communications encrypted in transit, having communications encrypted with keys the providers don't have access to ([end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362)), making it possible for users to independently verify their correspondent's identities, having past communications secure if the keys are stolen ([forward secrecy](wikipedia://en/2191130)), having their code open to independent review ([open source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software)), having their security designs well-documented, and having recent independent security audits.[^o14]

Pidgin 3.0.0 Experimental 1, a preview release of Pidgin 3 versioned as 2.90, was announced and subsequently released on December 31, 2024 after many years of development.[^o15][^o16] It was shipped with IRC support, with more protocols being expected to be added in future versions.[^o15] In April 2025, version 2.91, a second preview release of Pidgin 3, was released.[^o17]

## Features {#Features}

*Image: Pidgin Screenshot Ubuntu.png.* Pidgin running on [Ubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_%28operating_system%29)

Pidgin provides a graphical [front-end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front-end_and_back-end#Computer_science) for libpurple using [GTK+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTK+).[^o19] Libpurple supports many instant-messaging [protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_protocol).

Pidgin supports multiple [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system), including [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows) and many [Unix-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix-like) systems such as [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux), the [BSDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSD), and [AmigaOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AmigaOS). It is included by default in the operating systems [Tails](wikipedia://en/31672755), [Trisquel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisquel) and [Xubuntu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xubuntu).

### Pluggability {#Pluggability}

The program is designed to be extended with [plugins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_%28computing%29). Plugins are often written by [third-party developers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-party_software_component). They can be used to add support for protocols, which is useful for those such as [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype) or [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord) which have licensing issues (however, the users' data and interactions are still subject to their policies and eavesdropping). They can also add other significant features. For example, the "[Off-the-Record Messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-Record_Messaging)" (OTR) plugin provides [end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362).

The TLS encryption system is pluggable, allowing different TLS libraries to be easily substituted. [GnuTLS](wikipedia://en/949758) is the default, and [NSS](wikipedia://en/5137674) is also supported. Some operating systems' ports, such as [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD)'s, choose to use [OpenSSL](wikipedia://en/438819) or [LibreSSL](wikipedia://en/42552765) by default instead.

### Contacts {#Contacts}

Contacts with multiple protocols can be grouped into one single contact instead of managing multiple protocols, and contacts can be given aliases or placed into groups.

To reach users as they log on or a status change occurs (such as moving from "Away" to "Available"), Pidgin supports on-action automated scripts called *Buddy Pounces* to automatically reach the user in customizable ways.

### File transfer {#File_transfer}

Pidgin supports file transfers for many protocols. Direct, peer-to-peer file transfers are supported over protocols such as XMPP.

### Voice and video chat {#Voice_and_video_chat}

As of version 2.6 (released on August 18, 2009), Pidgin supports voice/video calls using [Farstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farstream).[^o24] , calls can only be initiated through the XMPP protocol.[^o25]

### Miscellaneous {#Miscellaneous}

Further features include support for themes, [emoticons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emoticon), [spell checking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spell_checker), and notification area integration.[^o27]

### Supported protocols {#Supported_protocols}

The following protocols are supported by Pidgin 2.14.14 without any extensions or plugins:[^o29]

- [Gadu-Gadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadu-Gadu)
- [IRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Relay_Chat)
- [Novell GroupWise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novell_GroupWise) (to be discontinued in 3.0)[^o30]
- [SIMPLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIMPLE_%28instant_messaging_protocol%29)
- [XMPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XMPP)
- [Zephyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zephyr_%28protocol%29) (to be discontinued in 3.0)[^o30]

Some XMPP servers provide [transports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensible_Messaging_and_Presence_Protocol#Connecting_to_other_protocols), which allow users to access networks using non-XMPP protocols without having to install plugins or additional software. Pidgin's support for XMPP means that these transports can be used to communicate via otherwise unsupported protocols, including not only instant messaging protocols, but also protocols such as SMS or E-mail.

Additional protocols, supported by third-party plugins, include [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord),[^o31] [Telegram](wikipedia://en/41501305),[^o32] Microsoft OCS/LCS (extended SIP/SIMPLE),[^o33] [Facebook Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Messenger),[^o34] [QQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent_QQ),[^o35] [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867),[^o36] and [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28messaging_app%29).[^o37]

### Plugins {#Plugins}

Various other features are supported using third-party [plugins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_%28computing%29).[^o39] Such features include:

- [Discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discord) text chat via the purple-discord plugin[^o31]
- [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook) chat via purple-facebook
- [Google Chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chat) via purple-googlechat
- microblogs ([GNU social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_social), [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter))
- [Slack (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slack_%28software%29) via slack-libpurple
- [Telegram (software)](wikipedia://en/41501305) via tdlib-purple
- [End-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362), through [Off-the-Record Messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-Record_Messaging) (OTR)
- Adding [mathematical formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_formula) written in [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) to conversations
- Notifications (such as showing "toaster" popups or [Snarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snarl_%28software%29) notifications, or lighting LEDs on laptops)
- Showing contacts what the user is listening to in various media players
- Watching videos directly into a conversation when receiving a video sharing website link ([YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube), [Vimeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimeo))

## Criticisms {#Criticisms}

- As observed by Wired in 2015, the libpurple codebase is "known for its bountiful security bugs".[^o41] In 2011, security vulnerabilities were already discovered in popular OTR plugins using libpurple.[^o42]
- As of version 2.4 and later, the ability to manually resize the text input box of conversations was removed. This led to a [fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_%28software%29), Carrier (originally named Funpidgin).[^o43][^o44][^o45]
- [Passwords](wikipedia://en/24304) are stored in a [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935) file, readable by any person or program that can access the user's files. Version 3.0 of Pidgin (no announced release date)[^o46] will support password storage in system keyrings such as [KWallet](wikipedia://en/12534741) and the [GNOME Keyring](wikipedia://en/3105966) for Linux, [Keychain](wikipedia://en/3049527) for macOS, and winCred API for Windows.[^o47][^o30]
- Pidgin does not currently support pausing or reattempting file transfers.[^o48][^o49][^o50]
- Pidgin does not allow disabling the group sorting on the contact list.[^o51]

## Other notable software based on libpurple {#Other_notable_software_based_on_libpurple}

- [Adium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adium) and the discontinued [Proteus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteus_%28instant_messenger%29) (both for [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS))
- [Meebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meebo) (web-based, no longer available)[^o53]
- Telepathy Haze (a Tube[^o54] for some of the protocols supported by the [Telepathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepathy_%28software%29) framework)[^o55]
- [QuteCom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QuteCom) (cross-platform, focused on VoIP and video. Discontinued)
- [Instantbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instantbird) (discontinued) [^o56] (cross-platform, based on [Mozilla's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozilla_Foundation) [Gecko engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko_%28software%29))

[BitlBee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BitlBee) and [Minbif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbif) are IRCd-like gateways to multiple IM networks, and can be compiled with libpurple to increase functionality.
