# List of unsolved problems in mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of unsolved problems in mathematics"}

Many [mathematical problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_problems) have been stated but not yet solved. These problems come from many [areas of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_mathematics), such as [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1997), [differential](wikipedia://en/8625), [discrete](wikipedia://en/386468) and [Euclidean geometries](wikipedia://en/9417), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). Some problems belong to more than one discipline and are studied using techniques from different areas. Prizes are often awarded for the solution to a long-standing problem, and some lists of unsolved problems, such as the [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234), receive considerable attention.

:::

This list is a composite of notable unsolved problems mentioned in previously published lists, including but not limited to lists considered authoritative, and the problems listed here vary widely in both difficulty and importance.

For over a century, various mathematicians and organizations have published and promoted lists of unsolved mathematical problems. In some cases, the lists have been associated with prizes for the discoverers of solutions, with the Millennium Prize Problems each containing a reward of one million dollars.

### Millennium Prize Problems {#Millennium_Prize_Problems}

Of the original seven [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234) listed by the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) in 2000, six remain unsolved to date:[^o26]

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture"}

- [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161)

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hodge conjecture"}

- [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518)

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness"}

- [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](wikipedia://en/6013654)

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="P versus NP"}

- [P versus NP](wikipedia://en/6115)

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=list-entry name="Riemann hypothesis"}

- [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125)

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=list-entry name="Yang–Mills existence and mass gap"}

- [Yang–Mills existence and mass gap](wikipedia://en/2393975)

:::

The seventh problem, the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798), was solved by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) in 2003.[^o33]

Contrary to most of the Hilbert problems and many other problems described in this article which allow for varying interpretations, the Millennium Prize problem are precisely described to provide clear conditions to award winners. After Perelman's resolution of the Poincaré conjecture, the official descriptions of the remaining six problems were codified by six specialist and released publicly. While the problems can touch upon greater research topics, and can themselves be only special cases of more general open problems, the Clay Institute decided upon these particular cases of the problems:

::: {#o34 .entry cue=list-entry name="Generalized Poincaré conjecture"}

- The **Poincaré conjecture** can be formulated in terms of the [generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216) for different categories of manifolds. In the category of [piecewise linear manifolds](wikipedia://en/2174184), the generalized conjecture is still an open problem for 4-dimensional manifolds. For [differential manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_manifold), it is known to be false generally, but it is an open problem to determine in which dimensions it is true. In other words, it is not yet fully known on which spheres there exist no [exotic smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1114931). The Millennium Prize was offered for the topological version of the conjecture, that remained open only for 3-dimensional manifolds during the publication of the list.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Elliptic curves"}

- The **Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture** can be formulated for [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) over any [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), and generalizations for more general arithmetic objects have been proposed. The Millennium Prize is offered for resolving the conjecture for elliptic curves over [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers).

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Volume viscosity"}

- The **Navier–Stokes equations** have a general form that can take into account many physical phenomena including the difference between [volume viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_viscosity) and [dynamic viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_viscosity), [compressible flow](wikipedia://en/219144), change of viscosities in the flow, and an external force. The Millennium Prize is offered for proving any one of four statements, two of which deal with the Navier–Stokes equations with no external force while the other two deal with the Navier–Stokes equations with a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) external force.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=list-entry name="many generalizations"}

- The **Riemann hypothesis** has [many generalizations](wikipedia://en/152567) for different types of [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) that are expected to be true. The Millennium Prize problem is offered for showing the original Riemann hypothesis for the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::

### Notebooks {#Notebooks}

- The [Kourovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourovka,_Sverdlovsk_Oblast) Notebook (Коуровская тетрадь) is a collection of unsolved problems in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), first published in 1965 and updated many times since.[^o40]

::: {#o41 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sverdlovsk"}

- The [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg) Notebook (Свердловская тетрадь) is a collection of unsolved problems in [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory), first published in 1965 and updated every 2 to 4 years since.[^o42][^o43][^o44]

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dniester"}

- The [Dniester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniester) Notebook (Днестровская тетрадь) lists several hundred unsolved problems in algebra, particularly [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) and [modulus theory](wikipedia://en/18510677).[^o46][^o47]

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="Erlagol"}

- The [Erlagol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlagol) Notebook (Эрлагольская тетрадь) lists unsolved problems in algebra and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).[^o49]

## Unsolved problems {#Unsolved_problems}

### Algebra {#Algebra}

*Image: Regular tetrahedron inscribed in a sphere.svg.* In the [Bloch sphere](wikipedia://en/729572) representation of a [qubit](wikipedia://en/25284), a [SIC-POVM](wikipedia://en/20159695) forms a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). Zauner conjectured that analogous structures exist in complex [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) of all finite dimensions.

::: {#o53 .entry cue=list-entry name="Birch–Tate conjecture"}

- [Birch–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/7774402) on the relation between the order of the [center](wikipedia://en/7125) of the [Steinberg group](wikipedia://en/14994781) of the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of a [number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) to the field's [Dedekind zeta function](wikipedia://en/1131243).

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Casas-Alvero conjecture"}

- [Casas-Alvero conjecture](wikipedia://en/37236964): if a polynomial of degree $d$ defined over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) $0$ has a factor in common with its first through $d - 1$-th derivative, then must $f$ be the $d$-th power of a linear polynomial?

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=list-entry name="Connes embedding problem"}

- [Connes embedding problem](wikipedia://en/25228957) in [Von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) theory

:::

::: {#o56 .entry cue=list-entry name="Determinantal conjecture"}

- [Determinantal conjecture](wikipedia://en/34708696) on the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the sum of two [normal matrices](wikipedia://en/170366).

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture"}

- [Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture](wikipedia://en/8211999): a group with [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) 2 also has a 2-dimensional [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(G, 1)$.

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=list-entry name="Farrell–Jones conjecture"}

- [Farrell–Jones conjecture](wikipedia://en/20643804) on whether certain [assembly maps](wikipedia://en/21311179) are [isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphisms).

:::

  - [Bost conjecture](wikipedia://en/20643804#Bost_conjecture): a specific case of the Farrell–Jones conjecture

::: {#o59 .entry cue=list-entry name="Finite lattice representation problem"}

- [Finite lattice representation problem](wikipedia://en/38231808): is every finite [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) isomorphic to the [congruence lattice](wikipedia://en/310953#Congruence_lattice) of some finite [algebra](wikipedia://en/45200)?[^o60]

:::

::: {#o61 .entry cue=list-entry name="Goncharov conjecture"}

- [Goncharov conjecture](wikipedia://en/34710876) on the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of certain [motivic complexes](wikipedia://en/809314).

:::

::: {#o62 .entry cue=list-entry name="Green's conjecture"}

- [Green's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green's_conjecture): the [Clifford index](wikipedia://en/2980541) of a non-[hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361) is determined by the extent to which it, as a [canonical curve](wikipedia://en/734893), has [linear syzygies](wikipedia://en/11063114).

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Grothendieck–Katz p-curvature conjecture"}

- [Grothendieck–Katz p-curvature conjecture](wikipedia://en/3135542): a conjectured [local–global principle](wikipedia://en/489707) for [linear ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868).

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hadamard conjecture"}

- [Hadamard conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadamard_conjecture): for every positive integer $k$, a [Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553) of order $4k$ exists.

:::

  - [Williamson conjecture](wikipedia://en/60801185): the problem of finding Williamson matrices, which can be used to construct Hadamard matrices.
  - [Lander's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2992310#Ryser_and_Lander_conjectures): if an abelian group of order $v$ contains a difference set of order $n$, and a prime $p$ divides both $v$ and $n$, must the Sylow $p$-subgroup be non-cyclic? This implies Ryser's cyclic difference-set conjecture, and hence the circulant Hadamard conjecture.
  - [Ryser's conjecture on circulant Hadamard matrices](wikipedia://en/82859189): no real circulant Hadamard matrix has order greater than 4.
    - [Barker sequence conjecture](wikipedia://en/4507652#Barker_sequence_conjecture): no Barker sequence has length greater than 13; equivalently, none has even length greater than 4.[^o65]

::: {#o66 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hadamard's maximal determinant problem"}

- [Hadamard's maximal determinant problem](wikipedia://en/30556829): what is the largest [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of a matrix with entries all equal to 1 or −1?

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hilbert's fifteenth problem"}

- [Hilbert's fifteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336205): put [Schubert calculus](wikipedia://en/3061740) on a rigorous foundation.

:::

- [Pierce–Birkhoff conjecture](wikipedia://en/22052605): every piecewise-polynomial $f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is the maximum of a finite set of minimums of finite collections of polynomials.

::: {#o76 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rota's basis conjecture"}

- [Rota's basis conjecture](wikipedia://en/38405915): for matroids of rank $n$ with $n$ disjoint bases $B_{i}$, it is possible to create an $n \times n$ matrix whose rows are $B_{i}$ and whose columns are also bases.

- [Serre's conjecture II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_conjecture_II_%28algebra%29): if $G$ is a [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879) [semisimple algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) over a perfect [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) at most $2$, then the [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) set $H^{1}(F, G)$ is zero.

::: {#o78 .entry cue=list-entry name="Serre's positivity conjecture"}

- [Serre's positivity conjecture](wikipedia://en/472700) that if $R$ is a commutative [regular local ring](wikipedia://en/506959), and $P, Q$ are [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of $R$, then $\dim (R/P) + \dim (R/Q) = \dim (R)$ implies $\chi(R/P, R/Q) > 0$.

::: {#o79 .entry cue=list-entry name="Uniform boundedness conjecture for rational points"}

- [Uniform boundedness conjecture for rational points](wikipedia://en/66851658): do [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) of [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) $g \geq 2$ over [number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822) $K$ have at most some bounded number $N(K, g)$ of $K$-[rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200)?

- [Wild problems](wikipedia://en/49690041): problems involving classification of pairs of $n\times n$ matrices under simultaneous conjugation.

:::

::: {#o81 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zariski–Lipman conjecture"}

- [Zariski–Lipman conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski–Lipman_conjecture): for a [complex algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/39219687) $V$ with [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) $R$, if the [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28algebra%29) of $R$ are a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over $R$, then $V$ is [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_algebraic_variety).

#### Group theory {#Group_theory}
