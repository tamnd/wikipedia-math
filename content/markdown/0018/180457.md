# Keygen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Keygen"}

A **key generator**, commonly abbreviated as **keygen** , is a [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program) that generates a [product key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_key) and/or [serial number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_number) necessary for [product activation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_activation) of an [application software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_software). Keygens may be legitimately distributed by software manufacturers for licensing software in commercial environments where software has been licensed in bulk for an entire site or enterprise, or they may be developed and distributed illegitimately in circumstances of [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement) or software piracy.

:::

Illegitimate key generators are typically programmed and distributed by [software crackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_cracking) in the [warez scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warez_scene). These keygens often play music (taking from the tradition of [cracktros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracktro)), which may include the genres [dubstep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubstep), [chiptunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiptunes), sampled loops, or anything that the programmer desires. Such chiptunes are often made in [music trackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_tracker), and are mostly preferred due to their small size. Such keygens can have artistic user interfaces or kept simple and display only a cracking group or cracker's logo.

## Software licensing {#Software_licensing}

A [software license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_license) is a legal instrument that governs the usage and distribution of computer software.[^o3] Often, such licenses are enforced by implementing in the software a [product activation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_activation) or [digital rights management](wikipedia://en/18938226) (DRM) mechanism,[^o4] seeking to prevent unauthorized use of the software by issuing a code sequence that must be entered into the application when prompted or stored in its configuration.

## Key verification {#Key_verification}

Many programs attempt to verify or validate licensing keys over the Internet by establishing a session with a licensing application of the [software publisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_publisher). Advanced keygens bypass this mechanism, and include additional features for key verification, for example by generating the validation data which would otherwise be returned by an activation server. If the software offers phone activation then the keygen could generate the correct activation code to finish activation. Another method that has been used is activation server emulation, which patches the program memory to "see" the keygen as the *de facto* activation server.

## Multi-keygen {#Multi-keygen}

A multi-keygen is a keygen that offers key generation for multiple software applications. Multi-keygens are sometimes released over singular keygens if a series of products requires the same [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for generating [product keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_key).

These tools simplify the process of obtaining activation keys for users who need access to various software products within the same suite or developed by the same company. By integrating the algorithms for multiple applications into one interface, multi-keygens eliminate the need to manage separate keygens for each program. However, the use of multi-keygens often violates software licensing agreements or constitutes [copyright infringement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_infringement) when unauthorized, and may pose risks such as malware or compromised system security.

## Authors and distribution {#Authors_and_distribution}

Unauthorized keygens that typically violate software licensing terms are written by programmers who engage in [reverse engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) and [software cracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_cracking), often called *crackers*, to circumvent [copy protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copy_protection) of software or [digital rights management](wikipedia://en/18938226) for [multimedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia). Reverse engineering of software often involves disassembly of the software with a [disassembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disassembler). The software is then analyzed and studied for its behavior. This reveals the algorithms or formulas used to verify the keys, which can then be used to create keys that will be accepted by the software, passing its verification process for the keys and activating the software, without obtaining a key from the software developer or publisher.

Keygens are available for download on [warez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warez) sites or through [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) (P2P) networks.

## Malware keygens {#Malware_keygens}

Keygens, available through P2P networks or otherwise, can contain [malicious payloads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malware).[^o9] These key generators may or may not generate a valid key, but the embedded malware loaded invisibly at the same time may, for example, be a version of [CryptoLocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CryptoLocker#Imitators) ([ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware)).[^o10][^o11]

[Antivirus software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antivirus_software) may discover malware embedded in keygens; such software often also identifies unauthorized keygens which do not contain a payload as [potentially unwanted software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potentially_unwanted_software), often labelling them with a name such as Win32/Keygen or Win32/Gendows.[^o9]

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="CRCK KEYGEN"}

### HackTool.Win32.HackAV {#HackTool.Win32.HackAV}

A program designed to assist [hacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker_%28computer_security%29) is defined as HackTool.Win32.HackAV or not-a-virus:Keygen from [Kaspersky Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaspersky_Labs) or as HackTool:Win32/Keygen by [Microsoft Malware Protection Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Malware_Protection_Center). According to the *Microsoft Malware Protection Center*, its first known detection dates back to 16 July 2009.[^o14] The following security threats were most often found on PCs that have been related to these tools:

- *[Blackhole exploit kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackhole_exploit_kit)*
- *Win32/Autorun*
- *Win32/Dorkbot*
- *Win32/Obfuscator*[^o14]

:::

::: {#o16 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Key changer (software)"}

## Keychan {#Keychan}

A key changer or keychan is a variation of a keygen. A keychan is a small piece of software that changes the license key or serial number of a particular piece of [proprietary software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software) installed on a computer.

:::
