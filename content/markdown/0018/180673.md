# Heptagram

Regular star polygon stat table Regular star polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heptagram"}

A **heptagram**, **septagram**, **septegram** or **septogram** is a [seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven)-point [star](wikipedia://en/313303) drawn with seven straight strokes.

:::

The name *heptagram* combines a [numeral prefix](wikipedia://en/1705121), *hepta‑*, with the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) suffix *‑gram*. The *‑gram* suffix derives from γραμμῆ (grc) meaning a line.[^o2]

## Geometry {#Geometry}

In general, a heptagram is any self-intersecting [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349) (7-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621)).

There are two *regular* heptagrams, labeled as {7/2} and {7/3}, with the second number representing the vertex interval step from a regular [heptagon](wikipedia://en/365349), {7/1}.

This is the smallest star polygon that can be drawn in two forms, as [irreducible fractions](wikipedia://en/14822). The two heptagrams are sometimes called the *heptagram* (for {7/2}) and the *great heptagram* (for {7/3}).

The previous one, the regular [hexagram](wikipedia://en/302441) {6/2}, is a compound of two triangles. The smallest star polygon is the {5/2} [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544).

The next one is the {8/3} [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613) and its related {8/2} star figure (a compound of two squares), followed by the regular [enneagram](wikipedia://en/34963576), which also has two forms: {9/2} and {9/4}, as well as one compound of three triangles {9/3}.

## Uses {#Uses}

### Flags and heraldry {#Flags_and_heraldry}

- The [Bennington flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennington_flag), a historical American Flag, has thirteen seven-pointed stars along with the numerals "76" in the canton.
- The [Flag of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Australia) employs five heptagrams and one [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) to depict the [Southern Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crux) constellation and the [Commonwealth Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Star).
- Some old versions of the [coat of arms of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Georgia) including the [Georgian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) used the {7/2} heptagram as an element.

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Law enforcement"}

### Law enforcement {#Law_enforcement}

Some police badges in the US have a heptagram outline.

### Religious and occult symbolism {#Religious_and_occult_symbolism}

*Image: Babalon seal.png.* Seal of Babalon and the A∴A∴

- The heptagram became a traditional symbol for warding off evil in [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church).
- The symbol is also used in [Kabbalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabbalah) [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism).
- The heptagram is used in the symbol for [Babalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babalon) in Aleister Crowley's occult system [Thelema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thelema).
- The heptagram is known among [neopagans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_paganism) as the *Elven Star* or *Fairy Star*. It is treated as a sacred symbol in various modern pagan and witchcraft traditions. [Blue Star Wicca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Star_Wicca) also uses the symbol, where it is referred to as a *septegram*. The second heptagram is a symbol of [magical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_%28paranormal%29) in some [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan) spiritualities.
- In [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), a seven-sided star can refer to the [seven planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_planet) which were known to early alchemists, and also, the seven alchemical substances: fire, water, air, earth, sulphur, salt and mercury.
- In [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology). For example, the [planetary hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_hours), from which comes the [names of the days of the week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_of_the_days_of_the_week).
- In Polynesia, the seven-pointed star is used often in imagery, basket making, tattoos, and is considered to be a symbol of Kanaloa, the first Polynesian navigator.[^o11][^o12]
- The seven-sided star is an important symbol of the [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee) people of southern Appalachia, representing the [seven clans of the Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_clans) and the sacred number seven.

### In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

*Image: Maersk Group Logo.svg.* Logo of [Maersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maersk)

- The logo of American shoe brand [DC Shoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Shoes) features a {7/3} heptagram in the letter C.
- The seven-pointed star is used as the logo for the international Danish shipping company A.P. Møller – Mærsk A/S, sometimes known simply as [Maersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maersk).
- In [George R. R. Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_R._R._Martin)'s novel series *[A Song of Ice and Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Song_of_Ice_and_Fire)* and its TV version *[Game of Thrones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_Thrones)*, a seven-pointed star serves as the symbol of the [Faith of the Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Themes_in_A_Song_of_Ice_and_Fire#Faith_of_the_Seven).
- In the manga series *[MeruPuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeruPuri)*, a magical mirror/portal is in the shape of a heptagram. The symbol is also seen during spellcasting.
- English singer [Damon Albarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Albarn) uses a heptagram as a symbol in his solo performances and with his band [Blur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blur_%28band%29).
- The {7/3} heptagram is used by some members of the [otherkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otherkin) subculture as an identifier.
- The American progressive rock metal band [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_%28band%29) uses an 'open' seven pointed symbol for their fan group. It is said to be 'open' to signify an invitation into the collective unconscious.
- An alternate logo of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago)-based [Children's Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholastic_Corporation#Imprints_and_corporate_divisions) from 1945 to 1970, having the slogan "Children's Books Are Important".
