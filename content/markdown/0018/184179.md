# Institute for Advanced Study

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Institute for Advanced Study"}

The **Institute for Advanced Study** (**IAS**) is a center for theoretical research and intellectual inquiry, designed and funded by the [U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States), the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) and the [Carnegie Endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Endowment), located in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). It has served as the academic home of internationally preeminent scholars, including [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer), [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), [Michael Walzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Walzer), [Clifford Geertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Geertz), [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397) and [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), many of whom had emigrated from Europe to the United States.

:::

It was founded in 1930 by American educator [Abraham Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Flexner), together with philanthropists [Louis Bamberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bamberger) and [Caroline Bamberger Fuld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Bamberger_Fuld). Despite collaboration and neighboring geographic location, the institute is independent and has no formal ties to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o7] Having no students, the institute does not charge tuition or fees.[^o8]

Flexner's guiding principle in founding the institute was the pursuit of knowledge for its own sake.[^o9] The faculty have no classes to teach. There are no degree programs or experimental facilities at the institute. Research is never contracted or directed. It is left to each individual researcher to pursue their own goals.[^o10][^o11] Established during the rise of [fascism in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism_in_Europe), the institute played a key role in the [transfer of intellectual capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital_flight) from Europe to America. It quickly earned its reputation as the pinnacle of academic and scientific life—a reputation it has retained.[^o12][^o41][^o13]

The institute consists of four schools: Historical Studies, Mathematics, Natural Sciences, and Social Sciences. The institute also has a program in [Systems Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_Biology).

It is supported entirely by endowments, grants, and gifts. It is one of eight American mathematics institutes funded by the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation).[^o14] It is the model for all ten members of the consortium [Some Institutes for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Institutes_for_Advanced_Study).[^o13]

## History {#History}

### Founding {#Founding}

*Image: Picture of Abraham Flexner.jpg.* [Abraham Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Flexner)

*Image: LouisBamberger.jpg.* [Louis Bamberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bamberger)

*Image: CarolineBamberger.jpg.* [Caroline Bamberger Fuld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Bamberger_Fuld)

*Image: OswaldVeblen1915.jpg.* [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen) (c. 1915)

The institute was founded in 1930 by [Abraham Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Flexner), together with [philanthropists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy) [Louis Bamberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bamberger) and [Caroline Bamberger Fuld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Bamberger_Fuld).[^o17][^o18] Flexner was interested in education generally, and as early as 1890 he had founded an experimental school which had no formal curriculum, exams, or grades. It was a great success at preparing students for prestigious colleges, and this same philosophy would later guide him in the founding of the Institute for Advanced Study.[^o19]

Flexner's study of medical schools, the 1910 [Flexner Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexner_Report), played a major role in the reform of medical education.[^o20] Flexner had studied European schools such as [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University), [All Souls College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Souls_College,_Oxford), and the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France)–and he wanted to establish a similar advanced research center in the United States.[^o21][^o22][^o100]

In his autobiography, Abraham Flexner reports a phone call which he received in the fall of 1929 from representatives of the Bamberger siblings that led to their partnership and the eventual founding of the IAS:[^o23]

The Bamberger siblings wanted to use the proceeds from the sale of their [Bamberger's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamberger's) department store in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), to fund a [dental school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_school) as an expression of gratitude to the state of [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey).[^o24] Flexner convinced them to put their money in the service of more abstract research.[^o25] (There was a brush with near-disaster when the Bambergers pulled their money out of the market just before the [Crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_of_1929).)[^o26][^o27] The eminent topologist [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen)[^o28] at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), who had long been trying to found a high-level research institute in mathematics, urged Flexner to locate the new institute near Princeton where it would be close to an existing center of learning and a world-class library.[^o29] In 1932 Veblen resigned from Princeton and became the first professor in the new Institute for Advanced Study. He selected most of the original faculty and also helped the institute acquire land in Princeton for both the original facility and future expansion.[^o30][^o31]

Flexner and Veblen set out to recruit the best mathematicians and physicists they could find.[^o30] The rise of [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) and the associated [anti-semitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) forced many prominent mathematicians to flee Europe and some, such as [Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) and [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) (whose wife was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews)), found a home at the new institute.[^o32] Weyl as a condition of accepting insisted that the institute also appoint the thirty-year-old Austrian-Hungarian [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942). Indeed, the IAS became the key lifeline for scholars fleeing Europe.[^o33] Einstein was Flexner's first coup and shortly after that he was followed by Veblen's brilliant student [James Alexander](wikipedia://en/9848523) and the wunderkind of logic [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736).[^o34][^o35] Flexner was fortunate in the luminaries he directly recruited but also in the people that they brought along with them.[^o36] Thus, by 1934 the fledgeling institute was led by six of the most prominent mathematicians in the world. In 1935 quantum physics pioneer [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli) became a faculty member.[^o37] With the opening of the Institute for Advanced Study, [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) replaced [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) as the leading center for mathematics in the twentieth century.[^o76][^o38]

### Early years {#Early_years}

For the six years from its opening in 1933, until Fuld Hall was finished and opened in 1939, the institute was housed within [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University)—in Fine Hall, which housed Princeton's mathematics department.[^o40] Princeton University's science departments are less than two miles away and informal ties and collaboration between the two institutions occurred from the beginning.[^o41] This helped start an incorrect impression that it was part of the university, one that has never been completely eradicated.[^o42]

Bamberger's policy did not prevent racial discrimination by Princeton. When African-American mathematician [William S. Claytor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Claytor) applied to the IAS in 1937, Princeton University said they "would not permit any colored person to go to the Institute for Advanced Study." In 1939, when the institute had moved into its own building, Veblen was able to offer Claytor a position; but Claytor turned it down on principle.[^o44] The first African-American mathematician to visit the IAS was [David Blackwell](wikipedia://en/2202900) in 1941, with another visiting the next year.[^o45][^o46] Six Chinese physicists visited the IAS by 1949.[^o47]

Women joined the IAS since its opening in 1933, and faced discrimination as they tried to pursue scientific careers.[^o48][^o49] [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) and [Anna Stafford Henriques](wikipedia://en/60673295) were two of the earliest women to study at the IAS.[^o50][^o51] The first and only woman professor at the IAS from 1936 until 1972 was [Hetty Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetty_Goldman), in the School of Humanistic Studies.[^o52][^o53] In 1945, [Cheng-Shu Wang Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Chengshu) became the first non-white woman to visit the IAS.[^o54][^o55]

### Expansion {#Expansion}

*Image: Albert Einstein and Abraham Flexner at the Institute for Advanced Study.jpg.* left to right: [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), [Abraham Flexner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Flexner), [John R. Hardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_R._Hardin), and [Herbert Maass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Maass) at the IAS on May 22, 1939

Flexner had successfully assembled a faculty of unrivaled prestige[^o57] in the School of Mathematics which officially opened in 1933. He sought to equal this success in the founding of schools of economics and humanities but this proved to be more difficult. The School of Humanistic Studies and the School of Economics and Politics were established in 1935. All three schools along with the office of the director moved into the newly built Fuld Hall in 1939.[^o58] (Ultimately the schools of Humanistic Studies and Economics and Politics were merged into the present day School of Historical Studies established in 1949.)[^o59] In the beginning, the School of Mathematics included physicists as well as mathematicians. A separate School of Natural Sciences was not established until 1966.[^o60][^o61] The School of Social Science was founded in 1973.[^o62]

Faculty representation continued to change throughout the 2000s. The IAS's first African-American permanent faculty member joined in 2007.[^o63] In 2024, the School of Mathematics hired its first woman permanent faculty member.[^o64]

## Mission {#Mission}

*Image: Fuld Hall, Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, NJ.jpg.* Fuld Hall, built 1939

In a 1939 essay Flexner emphasized how [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696), driven only by a desire to know, did abstruse calculations in the field of magnetism and electricity and that these investigations led in a direct line to the entire electrical development of modern times.[^o9] Citing Maxwell and other theoretical scientists such as [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), [Michael Faraday](wikipedia://en/19727), [Paul Ehrlich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ehrlich) and Einstein, Flexner said, "Throughout the whole history of science most of the really great discoveries which have ultimately proved to be beneficial to mankind have been made by men and women who were driven not by the desire to be useful but merely the desire to satisfy their curiosity."[^o66]

## Impact {#Impact}

*Image: Institute for Advanced Study Campus.jpg.* Bird's eye view of the Institute for Advanced Study campus

From the day it opened the IAS had a major impact on mathematics, physics, economic theory, and world affairs.[^o68] In mathematics forty-two out of sixty-one [Fields Medalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medalist) have been affiliated with the institute.[^o69] Thirty-four [Nobel Laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate) have worked at the IAS.[^o70] Of the sixteen [Abel Prizes](wikipedia://en/159285) awarded since the establishment of that award in 2003, nine were garnered by Institute professors or visiting scholars.[^o71][^o72] Of the fifty-six [Cole Prizes](wikipedia://en/1372177) awarded since the establishment of that award in 1928, thirty-nine have gone to scholars associated with the IAS at some point in their career.[^o73] IAS people have won 20 [Wolf Prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in mathematics and physics.[^o74] Its more than 6,000 former members hold positions of intellectual and scientific leadership throughout the academic world.[^o75]

*Image: Oppenheimer & Neumann.jpg.* J. Robert Oppenheimer (left) and John von Neumann in front of the IAS machine (1952)

Pioneering work on the theory of the [stored-program computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stored-program_computer) as laid down by [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) was done at the IAS by John von Neumann, and the [IAS machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAS_machine) built in the basement of the Fuld Hall from 1942 to 1951 under von Neumann's direction introduced the basic architecture of most modern digital computers.[^o76][^o77][^o78] The IAS is the leading center of research in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and its generalization [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406) introduced by [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) at the IAS in 1995.[^o79] The [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905), a far-reaching approach which unites parts of geometry, [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), and number theory was introduced by [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986), the mathematician who now occupies Albert Einstein's old office at the institute.[^o80][^o81] Langlands was inspired by the work of [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), and [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190), all scholars with wide-ranging ties to the institute, and the IAS maintains the key repository for the papers of Langlands and the Langlands program.[^o82] The IAS is a main center of research for [homotopy type theory](wikipedia://en/33974223), a modern approach to the foundations of mathematics which is not based on classical set theory. A special year organized by Institute professor [Vladimir Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482) and others resulted in a benchmark book in the subject which was published by the institute in 2013.[^o83][^o88]

The institute is or has been the academic home of scholars like [James Waddell Alexander II](wikipedia://en/9848523), [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475), [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397), [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), [Clifford Geertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Geertz), [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), [Albert Hirschman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hirschman), [George F. Kennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_F._Kennan), [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee), [Avishai Margalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avishai_Margalit), [J. Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Robert_Oppenheimer), [Erwin Panofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Panofsky), [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021), [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544), [Frank Wilczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wilczek), [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894), [Chen-Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen-Ning_Yang) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137).[^o84]

## Special Year programs {#Special_Year_programs}

## Other Institutes for Advanced Study {#Other_Institutes_for_Advanced_Study}

The IAS in Princeton is widely recognized as the world's first Institute for Advanced Study.[^o12] Despite later imitators of the institute's model, it took years before any similar institutions were founded. The Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences at Stanford was the first such spinoff in 1954. This was followed by the National Humanities Center founded in North Carolina in 1978.[^o96] These two institutions eventually became the core of a consortium known as *[Some Institutes for Advanced Study (SIAS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some_Institutes_for_Advanced_Study)*. The SIAS consortium includes the original institute in Princeton and nine other institutes founded explicitly to emulate the model of the original IAS. These ten Institutes for Advanced Study are:[^o13][^o97][^o98]

- [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States)
- [Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Study_in_the_Behavioral_Sciences) in [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California), U.S.
- [National Humanities Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Humanities_Center) in [Durham County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_County,_North_Carolina), [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina), U.S.
- [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts), U.S.
- [Netherlands Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands)
- [Swedish Collegium for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Collegium_for_Advanced_Study) in [Uppsala, Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala,_Sweden)
- [Berlin Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) in [Berlin, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin,_Germany)
- [Israel Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies) in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem)
- [Nantes Institute for Advanced Study Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantes_Institute_for_Advanced_Study_Foundation) in [Nantes, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantes,_France)
- [Stellenbosch Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) in [Stellenbosch, South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch,_South_Africa)

In recent years there have been other institutes loosely based on the Princeton original, in some cases established with help from IAS professors. In 1997 IAS professor [Chen-Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen-Ning_Yang) helped the Chinese set up the [Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study,_Tsinghua_University) at [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University) in Beijing.[^o99] The [Freiburg Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies) in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), Germany was founded in 2007, with IAS director at the time [Peter Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Goddard_%28physicist%29) giving the inaugural address.[^o100] Princeton IAS professors [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) and [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) helped to establish close contacts with the [Ramanujan Institute for Advanced Study in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/42083445), founded in 1967 as part of the [University of Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Madras) in India.[^o101]

The [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) (IHÉS) founded in 1958 just south of Paris was inspired by the IAS in Princeton.[^o102][^o103] Princeton Institute director [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer) had a close relationship with IHÉS founder [Léon Motchane](wikipedia://en/39434117) and played a major role in helping to get it established.[^o104] The [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies), which focuses on [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), [cosmic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_physics), and [Celtic studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_studies), was also based on the IAS, and was the second such institute when it was founded in 1940.[^o105][^o106]

Neither the Princeton IAS nor SIAS is connected with, and should not be confused with, the *Consortium of Institutes of Advanced Studies* which comprises some twenty research institutes located throughout Great Britain and Ireland.[^o107] The name Institute for Advanced Study, along with the acronym IAS, is also used by various other independent institutions throughout the world, some having little to do with the Princeton model.[^o108] See [Institute for Advanced Study (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Study_%28disambiguation%29) for a complete list.

## Directors, faculty and members {#Directors,_faculty_and_members}

At any given time, the IAS has a faculty consisting of twenty-eight eminent academics who are appointed for life. Although the faculty do not teach classes (because there are none), they often do give lectures at their own initiative and have the title Professor along with the prestige associated with that title. Furthermore, they direct research and serve as the nucleus of a larger and generally younger group of scholars, whom they have the power to select and invite.[^o110] Each year fellowships are awarded to about 190 visiting members from over 100 universities and research institutions who come to the institute for periods from one term to a few years. Individuals must apply to become members of the institute, and each of the schools has its own application procedures and deadlines.[^o111]

## Campus, lands, Olden Farm and Olden Manor {#Campus,_lands,_Olden_Farm_and_Olden_Manor}

*Image: Olden Manor, 97 Olden Lane, Princeton, NJ.jpg.* Olden Manor

The IAS owns over 600 acres of land, most of which was acquired between 1936 and 1945. Since 1997 the institute has preserved 589 acres of woods, wetlands, and farmland.[^o124] By 1936, for total of \$290,000, the founding trustees of the IAS had purchased 256 acres, including the two-hundred-acre Olden Farm with Olden Manor, which was the former home of William Olden.[^o125][^o126][^o127] Olden Manor, with its extensive gardens,[^o128] has been, since 1940, the residence of the institute's director.[^o125]
