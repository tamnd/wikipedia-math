# Peter Gavin Hall

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Gavin Hall"}

**Peter Gavin Hall** [^o3] (20 November 1951 – 9 January 2016) was an Australian researcher in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711).[^o4] The [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) described him as one of the most influential and prolific theoretical statisticians in the history of the field.[^o5] The School of Mathematics and Statistics Building at [The University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Melbourne) was renamed the Peter Hall building in his honour on 9 December 2016.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hall attended [Sydney Technical High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Technical_High_School) in Bexley, NSW during the years 1964–1969. He placed consistently high in examination results and in his final year, was among the top achievers in his form, and the winner of Old Boys' Union Mathematics prize.[^o12]

Hall earned his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1976 for research supervised by [John Kingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kingman).[^o13]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Hall was an author in probability and statistics. [MathSciNet](wikipedia://en/2580683) lists 606 publications as of January 2016. He made contributions to nonparametric statistics, in particular for curve estimation and resampling: the bootstrap method, smoothing, density estimation, and bandwidth selection. He worked on numerous applications across fields of economics, engineering, physical science and biological science. Hall also made contributions to surface roughness measurement using fractals. In probability theory he made many contributions to limit theory, spatial processes and [stochastic geometry](wikipedia://en/22037813). His paper "Theoretical comparison of bootstrap confidence intervals" (Annals of Statistics, 1988) has been reprinted in the *Breakthroughs in Statistics* collection.

He was an [Australian Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Research_Council) (ARC) Laureate Fellow at the School of Mathematics and Statistics, [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne),[^o16] and also had a joint appointment at [University of California Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Davis).[^o17] He previously held a professorship at the Centre for Mathematics and its Applications at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Peter Hall was born to [radiophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiophysics) and [radio astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_astronomy) pioneer [Ruby Payne-Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Payne-Scott) and telephone technician William Holman Hall. His younger sister is artistic photographer and sculptor, [Fiona Margaret Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Margaret_Hall).[^o39] Hall was a keen photographer with a special interest in train photography.[^o5] He enjoyed travel and was a regular visitor to many universities around the world. He died of leukaemia in Melbourne on 9 January 2016. He is survived by his wife, Jeannie.
