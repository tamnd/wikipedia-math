# Craige Schensted

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Craige Schensted"}

**Craige Schensted** (1927-04-12),[^o2] who formally changed his name to **Ea Ea**, was an American physicist and mathematician who first formulated the insertion algorithm (Schensted 1961) that defines the [Robinson–Schensted correspondence](wikipedia://en/1901026). Under a different form, that correspondence had earlier been described by [Gilbert de Beauregard Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_de_Beauregard_Robinson) in 1938, but it is due to the Schensted insertion algorithm that the correspondence has become widely known in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). Schensted also designed several board games including [\*Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/*Star), [Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_%28board_game%29), and [Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_%28game%29).

:::

In 1995, he changed his name to **Ea**, the Babylonian name for the Sumerian god [Enki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enki), interested in the god's creative and empathetic nature.[^o2] This became **Ea Ea** in 1999, in anticipation of the [millennium computer glitches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2000_problem). He lived for forty years on [Peaks Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaks_Island,_Maine) in [Portland, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Maine).[^o4]
