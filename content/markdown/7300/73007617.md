# Daniel Rothman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Rothman"}

**Daniel H. Rothman** is an American geophysicist and Professor of Geophysics in the Department of Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Sciences at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology).[^o3]

:::

Having made significant contributions to [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), much of his work has contributed to understanding how the natural environment is organized. From such research, Rothman has made fundamental advances in topics ranging from [seismology](wikipedia://en/28935) and [geobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geobiology) to fluid flow and [biogeochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biogeochemistry).[^o4]

Rothman's recent research interests lay in understanding the dynamics of the Earth's carbon cycle, with other research interests relating to: the physical foundation of natural geometric forms, thresholds of catastrophe in the [climate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_system), and the co-evolution of life and environment.[^o5]

## Biography {#Biography}

Rothman received his A.B. in applied mathematics from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1979, before completing a PhD in geophysics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1986. Rothman then joined the faculty at MIT later that year.[^o7] Since 1986, he has held visiting appointments at Harvard's Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study, [The University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Chicago), and [Ecole Normale Superieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecole_Normale_Superieure).

Rothman is a Fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) and the [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union) and is the recipient of the 2016 [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

He is also co-founder and co-director of MIT's Lorenz Center with atmospheric scientist [Kerry Emanuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Emanuel), an interdisciplinary research center devoted to learning how climate works with a particular interest in [nonlinear dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_dynamics).[^o9]

With Stiphane Zaleski, he is the co-author of *Lattice-Gas Cellular Automata: Simple Models of Complex Hydrodynamics*.[^o10]
