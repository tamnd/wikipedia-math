# Frances Harshbarger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frances Harshbarger"}

**Frances Harshbarger** (16 August 1902, [Quimby, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quimby,_Iowa) – 11 February 1987, [Cuyahoga Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Falls), [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio)) was an American mathematician.[^o9]

:::

## Education {#Education}

She obtained her [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) with honors in 1923 at [Grinnell College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinnell_College),[^o4][^o5] and went to [West Virginia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_University) to serve as a half-time teacher and simultaneously work on her mathematics graduation; in 1925 she finished her [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) After that she became head of the mathematics department of [Potomac State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_State_College) in [Keyser, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyser,_West_Virginia). In 1927 to 1929, she was an assistant, in 1929 to 1930 a fellow, at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois). In 1930, she obtained her Ph.D. in mathematics with a thesis in algebraic geometry,[^o6][^o7] advised by [A. B. Coble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._B._Coble). She was one of the first American women who obtained a mathematics Ph.D. degree.[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

Thereafter, she worked as a professor in the American College for Girls, the university section of [Robert College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_College), in [Istanbul, Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul,_Turkey).[^o9] She attended the 1932 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) as an official delegate.[^o11] In 1934, she returned to the United States to teach at the high school associated with the University of Chicago. For the rest of her career, she served at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University) in Ohio, as an appointed instructor since 1935, an assistant professor since 1936, an associate professor since 1942, and a professor since 1946, until she retired in 1972, as emeritus professor.[^o9]
