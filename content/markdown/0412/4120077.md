# Dominating decision rule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dominating decision rule"}

[In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instagram) [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), a decision rule is said to **dominate** another if the performance of the former is sometimes better, and never worse, than that of the latter.

:::

Formally, let $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ be two [decision rules](wikipedia://en/446216), and let $R(\theta, \delta)$ be the [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_function) of rule $\delta$ for parameter $\theta$. The decision rule $\delta_1$ is said to dominate the rule $\delta_2$ if $R(\theta,\delta_1)\le R(\theta,\delta_2)$ for all $\theta$, and the inequality is strict for some $\theta$.[^o2]

This defines a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) on decision rules; the [maximal elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_element) with respect to this order are called *[admissible decision rules](wikipedia://en/1776726).*[^o2]
