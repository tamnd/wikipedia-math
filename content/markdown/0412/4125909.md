# Schottky problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schottky problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Schottky problem,** named after [Friedrich Schottky](wikipedia://en/5872505), is a classical question of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), asking for a characterisation of [Jacobian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_varieties) amongst [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties).

:::

## Geometric formulation {#Geometric_formulation}

More precisely, one should consider [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) $C$ of a given [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) $g$, and their Jacobians $\operatorname{Jac}(C)$. There is a [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) $\mathcal{M}_g$ of such curves, and a [moduli space of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_space_of_abelian_varieties), $\mathcal{A}_g$, of dimension $g$, which are *principally polarized*. There is a morphism

> $$\operatorname{Jac}: \mathcal{M}_g \to \mathcal{A}_g$$

which on points ([geometric points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_point), to be more accurate) takes isomorphism class $[C]$ to $[\operatorname{Jac}(C)]$. The content of [Torelli's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli's_theorem) is that $\operatorname{Jac}$ is injective (again, on points). The **Schottky problem** asks for a description of the image of $\operatorname{Jac}$, denoted $\mathcal{J}_g = \operatorname{Jac}(\mathcal{M}_g)$.[^o3]

The dimension of $\mathcal{M}_g$ is $3g - 3$,[^o4] for $g \geq 2$, while the dimension of *$\mathcal{A}_g$* is *g*(*g* + 1)/2. This means that the dimensions are the same (0, 1, 3, 6) for *g* = 0, 1, 2, 3. Therefore $g = 4$ is the first case where the dimensions change, and this was studied by F. Schottky in the 1880s. Schottky applied the [theta constants](wikipedia://en/44466777), which are [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) for the [Siegel upper half-space](wikipedia://en/1141106), to define the **Schottky locus** in *$\mathcal{A}_g$*. A more precise form of the question is to determine whether the image of $\operatorname{Jac}$ essentially coincides with the Schottky locus (in other words, whether it is [Zariski dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_dense) there).

### Dimension 1 case {#Dimension_1_case}

All elliptic curves are the Jacobian of themselves, hence the [moduli stack of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/49905706) $\mathcal{M}_{1,1}$ is a model for $\mathcal{A}_1$.

### Dimensions 2 and 3 {#Dimensions_2_and_3}

In the case of Abelian surfaces, there are two types of Abelian varieties:[^o7] the Jacobian of a genus 2 curve, or the product of Jacobians of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). This means the moduli spaces

> $$\mathcal{M}_2, \mathcal{M}_{1,1}\times \mathcal{M}_{1,1}$$

embed into $\mathcal{A}_2$. There is a similar description for dimension 3 since an Abelian variety can be the product of Jacobians.

## Period lattice formulation {#Period_lattice_formulation}

If one describes the moduli space *$\mathcal{A}_g$* in intuitive terms, as the parameters on which an abelian variety depends, then the Schottky problem asks simply what condition on the parameters implies that the abelian variety comes from a curve's Jacobian. The classical case, over the [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) field, has received most of the attention, and then an abelian variety *A* is simply a [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042) of a particular type, arising from a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in **C**<sup>*g*</sup>. In relatively concrete terms, it is being asked which lattices are the *period lattices* of [compact Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface).

## Riemann's matrix formulation {#Riemann's_matrix_formulation}

*Note that a Riemann matrix is quite different from any [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor)*

One of the major achievements of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) was his theory of complex tori and [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745). Using the [Riemann theta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_theta_function), necessary and sufficient conditions on a lattice were written down by Riemann for a lattice in **C**<sup>*g*</sup> to have the corresponding torus embed into [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). (The interpretation may have come later, with [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384), but Riemann's theory was definitive.) The data is what is now called a **Riemann matrix**. Therefore, the complex Schottky problem becomes the question of characterising the [period matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_matrix) of compact [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) of genus *g*, formed by integrating a basis for the [abelian integrals](wikipedia://en/1138853) round a basis for the first [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group), amongst all Riemann matrices. It was solved by [Takahiro Shiota](wikipedia://en/32594980) in 1986.[^o10]

## Geometry of the problem {#Geometry_of_the_problem}

There are a number of geometric approaches, and the question has also been shown to implicate the [Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation](wikipedia://en/4211219), related to [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) theory.
