# Krull's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Krull's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), **Krull's theorem**, named after [Wolfgang Krull](wikipedia://en/964328), asserts that a [nonzero](wikipedia://en/1872874) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404)[^o2] has at least one [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164). The theorem was proved in 1929 by Krull, who used [transfinite induction](wikipedia://en/30461). The theorem admits a [simple proof using Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442#Every_nontrivial_ring_with_unity_contains_a_maximal_ideal), and in fact is equivalent to [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442),[^o3] which in turn is equivalent to the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

## Variants {#Variants}

- For [noncommutative rings](wikipedia://en/24062516), the analogues for maximal left ideals and maximal right ideals also hold.
- For [pseudo-rings](wikipedia://en/51508407), the theorem holds for [regular ideals](wikipedia://en/24159446).
- An *apparently* slightly stronger (but equivalent) result, which can be proved in a similar fashion, is as follows:

Let *R* be a ring, and let *I* be a [proper ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_ideal) of *R*. Then there is a maximal ideal of *R* containing *I*.

The statement of the original theorem can be obtained by taking *I* to be the [zero ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) (0). Conversely, applying the original theorem to *R*/*I* leads to this result.

To prove the "stronger" result directly, consider the set *S* of all proper ideals of *R* containing *I*. The set *S* is nonempty since *I* ∈ *S*. Furthermore, for any chain *T* of *S*, the union of the ideals in *T* is an ideal *J*, and a union of ideals not containing 1 does not contain 1, so *J* ∈ *S*. By Zorn's lemma, *S* has a maximal element *M*. This *M* is a maximal ideal containing *I*.
