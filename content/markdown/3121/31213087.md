# List of numeral systems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of numeral systems"}

There are many different [numeral systems](wikipedia://en/21170), that is, [writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) for expressing [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690).

:::

## By culture / time period {#By_culture_/_time_period}

"A *base* is a natural number B whose *powers* (B multiplied by itself some number of times) are specially designated within a numerical system."[^o3] The term is not equivalent to [radix](wikipedia://en/701207), as it applies to all numerical notation systems (not just positional ones with a radix) and most systems of spoken numbers.[^o3] Some systems have two bases, a smaller (subbase) and a larger (base); an example is Roman numerals, which are organized by fives (V=5, L=50, D=500, the subbase) and tens (X=10, C=100, M=1,000, the base).

## By type of notation {#By_type_of_notation}

Numeral systems are classified here as to whether they use [positional notation](wikipedia://en/437052) (also known as place-value notation), and further categorized by [radix](wikipedia://en/701207) or base.

### Standard positional numeral systems {#Standard_positional_numeral_systems}

*Image: Binary clock.svg.* A [binary clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_clock) might use [LEDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode) to express binary values. In this clock, each column of LEDs shows a [binary-coded decimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary-coded_decimal) numeral of the traditional [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) time.

The common names are derived [somewhat arbitrarily](wikipedia://en/13263#Etymology) from a mix of [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), in some cases including roots from both languages within a single name.[^o85] There have been some proposals for standardisation.[^o86]

### [Non-standard positional numeral systems](wikipedia://en/4204408) {#Non-standard_positional_numeral_systems}

#### [Bijective numeration](wikipedia://en/2260933) {#Bijective_numeration}

[Unary](wikipedia://en/32316), or bijective base1, is used in [Tally marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tally_mark), and [Counting](wikipedia://en/275768). Unary numbering is used as part of some data compression algorithms such as [Golomb coding](wikipedia://en/147230). It also forms the basis for the [Peano axioms](wikipedia://en/25005) for formalizing arithmetic within [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). A form of unary notation called [Church encoding](wikipedia://en/2989409) is used to represent numbers within [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203). Some [email](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email) [spam filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spam_filter) tag messages with a number of [asterisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk) in an [e-mail header](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail_header) such as *X-Spam-Bar* or *X-SPAM-LEVEL*. The larger the number, the more likely the email is considered spam.
