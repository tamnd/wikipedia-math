# Gila Sher

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gila Sher"}

**Gila Sher** is an American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego). She has worked extensively in the theory of [truth](wikipedia://en/29920) and [philosophy of logic](wikipedia://en/21368888). Sher is a leading advocate of foundational [holism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holism), a holistic theory of [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1989, Sher earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in philosophy from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University),[^o4] where she studied the works of [Willard Quine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Quine) and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920). Her dissertation was directed by [Charles Parsons](wikipedia://en/1821365).

Since 2017, Sher has been an editor of *[The Journal of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Journal_of_Philosophy)*, and from 2012 to 2017, she was an editor-in-chief of the journal *[Synthese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthese)*.[^o5] Sher has also served as the president of the International Society for Formal Ontology since 2017.[^o6]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Her early work focused on Tarski's definition of truth. Her reformulation of this definition has been influential in modern truth theory. Her [dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) was expanded into the book *The Bounds of Logic* (1991),[^o8] in which Sher also formalized definitions for unique second-order quantifiers such as 'most'.[^o8]

Sher has pursued research into [logical positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) and logical [foundationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundationalism). She has argued that strict-ordering foundationalism, in the vein of [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987), is untenable, supporting Quine's argument from *[Two Dogmas of Empiricism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Dogmas_of_Empiricism)*. She has, however, resisted the mainstream move toward all-or-nothing and [semantic holism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_holism). The former view she considers unexplanatory, and the latter she considers untenable (see: [Jerry Fodor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Fodor)).[^o9]

Sher has also written more generally on the [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) of truth. She put forward a criticism of [John Etchemendy](wikipedia://en/5503538) in the article "Did Tarski Commit Tarski's Fallacy?"[^o10] This article defended Tarskian truth theory from the radical attack posed by Etchemendy. She is also a leading Quine scholar, writing about the place of philosophy in his theory of naturalized epistemology.[^o11]
