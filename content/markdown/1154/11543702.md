# Carathéodory metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carathéodory metric"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Carathéodory metric** is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) defined on the [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) of a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) that has many similar properties to the [Poincaré metric](wikipedia://en/1487868) of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291). It is named after the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (*X*, || ||) be a complex Banach space and let *B* be the open unit ball in *X*. Let Δ denote the open unit disc in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) **C**, thought of as the [Poincaré disc model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_disc_model) for 2-dimensional real/1-dimensional complex hyperbolic geometry. Let the Poincaré metric *ρ* on Δ be given by

$$\rho (a, b) = \tanh^{-1} \frac{| a - b |}{|1 - \bar{a} b |}$$

(thus fixing the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) to be −4). Then the **Carathéodory metric** *d* on *B* is defined by

$$d (x, y) = \sup \{ \rho (f(x), f(y)) | f : B \to \Delta \mbox{ is holomorphic} \}.$$

What it means for a function on a Banach space to be holomorphic is defined in the article on [Infinite dimensional holomorphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_dimensional_holomorphy).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For any point *x* in *B*,

$$d(0, x) = \rho(0, \| x \|).$$

- *d* can also be given by the following formula, which Carathéodory attributed to [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191):

$$d(x, y) = \sup \left\{ \left. 2 \tanh^{-1} \left\| \frac{f(x) - f(y)}{2} \right\| \right| f : B \to \Delta \mbox{ is holomorphic} \right\}$$

- For all *a* and *b* in *B*,

$$\| a - b \| \leq 2 \tanh \frac{d(a, b)}{2}, \qquad \qquad (1)$$

with equality [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) either *a* = *b* or there exists a [bounded linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_functional) ℓ ∈ *X*<sup>∗</sup> such that ||ℓ|| = 1, ℓ(*a* + *b*) = 0 and

$$\rho (\ell (a), \ell (b)) = d(a, b).$$

Moreover, any ℓ satisfying these three conditions has |ℓ(*a* − *b*)| = ||*a* − *b*||.

- Also, there is equality in (1) if ||*a*|| = ||*b*|| and ||*a* − *b*|| = ||*a*|| + ||*b*||. One way to do this is to take *b* = −*a*.
- If there exists a unit vector *u* in *X* that is not an [extreme point](wikipedia://en/454968) of the closed unit ball in *X*, then there exist points *a* and *b* in *B* such that there is equality in (1) but *b* ≠ ±*a*.

:::

## Carathéodory length of a tangent vector {#Carathéodory_length_of_a_tangent_vector}

There is an associated notion of Carathéodory length for [tangent vectors](wikipedia://en/191999) to the ball *B*. Let *x* be a point of *B* and let *v* be a tangent vector to *B* at *x*; since *B* is the open unit ball in the vector space *X*, the tangent space T<sub>*x*</sub>*B* can be identified with *X* in a natural way, and *v* can be thought of as an element of *X*. Then the **Carathéodory length** of *v* at *x*, denoted *α*(*x*, *v*), is defined by

$$\alpha (x, v) = \sup \big\{ | \mathrm{D} f(x) v | \big| f : B \to \Delta \mbox{ is holomorphic} \big\}.$$

One can show that *α*(*x*, *v*) ≥ ||*v*||, with equality when *x* = 0.
