# Evan O'Dorney

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Evan O'Dorney"}

**Evan Michael O'Dorney** (born September 16, 1993) is an American mathematician who is a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral) associate at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University). His specialization is [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). As a [home-schooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home-schooled) high school student and college student, he won many contests in mathematics and other subjects, including the 2007 [Scripps National Spelling Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_National_Spelling_Bee), 2011 [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search), four [International Math Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Math_Olympiad) medals, and three [Putnam Fellowships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow). A 2013 report by the [National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_%28United_States%29) called him "as famous for academic excellence as any student can be".[^o3]

:::

## Education and competitions {#Education_and_competitions}

O'Dorney grew up in [Danville, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danville,_California). When he was a child, he was assessed as "'profoundly gifted' with [autistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autism) behaviors", but he has never been formally diagnosed with a [neurodevelopmental disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodevelopmental_disorder).[^o15][^o5] As a home-schooled high school student, he attended classes at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 2007 to 2011. He was the winner of the 2007 [Scripps National Spelling Bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_National_Spelling_Bee);[^o6] an interview on [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN) with [Kiran Chetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiran_Chetry) after his win, in which he misspelled *scombridae* due to the interviewer's pronunciation, later became a [viral video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_video).[^o7][^o8] During this time he was a four-time [International Math Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Math_Olympiad) medalist, with two gold and two silver medals.

In 2010, he won \$10,000 (half for himself and half for the Berkeley Mathematics Circle) in a national "Who Wants to Be a Mathematician" contest, held at that year's [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143) in San Francisco.[^o10] In 2011, he won the top prize in the [Intel Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regeneron_Science_Talent_Search) for a project entitled "continued fraction convergents and linear fractional transformations".[^o11][^o12][^o13]

O'Dorney started attending [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 2011, where he studied mathematics.[^o23] He enrolled in graduate classes in mathematics, skipping the undergraduate-level classes. While at Harvard, he was a three-time [Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) fellow.[^o14] (His first Putnam was as a high school student.) After graduating *summa cum laude*,[^o15] in 2015–16 he studied [Part III of the Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos) at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge),[^o16] on a [Churchill Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Scholarship).[^o24][^o17] In 2016 he received honorable mention for the [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) in mathematics.

In 2021, he earned a PhD in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University),[^o15] with a dissertation titled "Reflection theorems for number rings".[^o18]

## Career {#Career}

He held a two-year post-doctoral position at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame)[^o20] and then took up a similar appointment at Carnegie Mellon University.[^o15] His specialization is number theory.[^o15]

## Other interests {#Other_interests}

Although his primary interest is mathematics, O'Dorney has had a strong interest in music. In 2007, he composed a song to help [memorize the digits](wikipedia://en/617167) of [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601).[^o22] At Harvard, he studied music as well as mathematics,[^o23] and continued to compose music, as well as singing in a chamber music group and playing the organ and piano. He has [absolute pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_pitch).[^o24] O’Dorney finds spirituality in his mathematical studies, and his meeting with Eric Riedl at a Catholic Mass led him to Notre Dame.[^o25]
