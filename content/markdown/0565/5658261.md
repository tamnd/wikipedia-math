# Apeirogon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirogon"}

*Image: Regular 2-apeirogon.svg.* A partition of the Euclidean line into infinitely many equal-length segments can be understood as a regular apeirogon.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apeirogon** (grc) or **infinite polygon** is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) with an [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) number of sides. Apeirogons are the rank-2 case of [infinite polytopes](wikipedia://en/12768648). In some literature, the term "apeirogon" may refer only to the [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) apeirogon, with an [infinite dihedral group](wikipedia://en/4296050) of [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741).[^o6]

:::

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Geometric apeirogon {#Geometric_apeirogon}

Given a point *A*<sub>0</sub> in a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) and a [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) *S*, define the point *A<sub>i</sub>* to be the point obtained from *i* applications of the translation *S* to *A*<sub>0</sub>, so *A<sub>i</sub>* = *S<sup>i</sup>*(*A*<sub>0</sub>). The set of vertices *A<sub>i</sub>* with *i* any integer, together with edges connecting adjacent vertices, is a sequence of equal-length segments of a line, and is called the **regular apeirogon** as defined by [H. S. M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter).[^o6]

A **regular apeirogon** can be defined as a partition of the Euclidean line *E*<sup>1</sup> into infinitely many equal-length segments. It generalizes the regular [*n*-gon](wikipedia://en/23621), which may be defined as a partition of the circle *S*<sup>1</sup> into *finitely* many equal-length segments.[^o7]

### Hyperbolic pseudogon {#Hyperbolic_pseudogon}

The **regular pseudogon** is a partition of the hyperbolic line *H*<sup>1</sup> (instead of the Euclidean line) into segments of length 2λ, as an analogue of the regular apeirogon.[^o7]

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Abstract apeirogon"}

### Abstract apeirogon {#Abstract_apeirogon}

An [abstract polytope](wikipedia://en/1211056) is a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* (whose elements are called *faces*) with properties modeling those of the inclusions of faces of [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885). The *rank* (or dimension) of an abstract polytope is determined by the length of the maximal ordered chains of its faces, and an abstract polytope of rank *n* is called an abstract *n*-polytope.[^o11]p. 22–25

For abstract polytopes of rank 2, this means that: A) the elements of the partially ordered set are sets of vertices with either zero vertex (the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566)), one vertex, two vertices (an [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034)), or the entire vertex set (a two-dimensional face), ordered by inclusion of sets; B) each vertex belongs to exactly two edges; C) the [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) formed by the vertices and edges is connected.[^o11]p. 22–25[^o12]p. 224

An abstract polytope is called an abstract [apeirotope](wikipedia://en/12768648) if it has infinitely many elements; an abstract 2-apeirotope is called an **abstract apeirogon**.[^o11]p. 25

A realization of an abstract polytope is a mapping of its vertices to points a geometric space (typically a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697)).[^o11]p. 121 A faithful realization is a realization such that the vertex mapping is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective).[^o11]p. 122[^o13] Every geometric apeirogon is a realization of the abstract apeirogon.

## Symmetries {#Symmetries}

*Image: Hyperbolic apeirogon example.png.* The [order-3 apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/38758064), {∞,3}, fills the hyperbolic plane with apeirogons whose vertices lie along horocyclic paths.

The infinite dihedral group *G* of symmetries of a regular geometric apeirogon is generated by two reflections, the product of which translates each vertex of *P* to the next.[^o11]p. 140–141[^o12]p. 231 The product of the two reflections can be decomposed as a product of a non-zero translation, finitely many rotations, and a possibly trivial reflection.[^o11]p. 141[^o12]p. 231

In an abstract polytope, a *flag* is a collection of one face of each dimension, all incident to each other (that is, comparable in the partial order); an abstract polytope is called *regular* if it has symmetries (structure-preserving permutations of its elements) that take any flag to any other flag. In the case of a two-dimensional abstract polytope, this is automatically true; the symmetries of the apeirogon form the [infinite dihedral group](wikipedia://en/4296050).[^o11]p. 31

A **symmetric realization** of an abstract apeirogon is defined as a mapping from its vertices to a finite-dimensional geometric space (typically a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697)) such that every symmetry of the abstract apeirogon corresponds to an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) of the images of the mapping.[^o11]p. 121[^o12]p. 225

:::

## Moduli space {#Moduli_space}

Generally, the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of a faithful realization of an abstract polytope is a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) of infinite dimension.[^o11]p. 127[^o12]p. 229–230 The realization cone of the abstract apeirogon has uncountably infinite [algebraic dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_dimension) and cannot be [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) in the [Euclidean topology](wikipedia://en/1482085).[^o11]p. 141[^o12]p. 232

## Classification of Euclidean apeirogons {#Classification_of_Euclidean_apeirogons}

The symmetric realization of any regular polygon in Euclidean space of dimension greater than 2 is [reducible](wikipedia://en/385982), meaning it can be made as a blend of two lower-dimensional polygons.[^o11] This characterization of the regular polygons naturally characterizes the regular apeirogons as well. The discrete apeirogons are the results of blending the 1-dimensional apeirogon with other polygons.[^o12]p. 231 Since every polygon is a quotient of the apeirogon, the blend of any polygon with an apeirogon produces another apeirogon.[^o11]

In two dimensions the discrete regular apeirogons are the [infinite zigzag polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_apeirogon),[^o18] resulting from the blend of the 1-dimensional apeirogon with the [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728), represented with the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{\infty\}\#\{2$, $\{\infty\}\#$, or $\left\{\dfrac{2}{0,1}\right\}$.[^o11]

In three dimensions the discrete regular apeirogons are the infinite helical polygons,[^o18] with vertices spaced evenly along a [helix](wikipedia://en/179924). These are the result of blending the 1-dimensional apeirogon with a 2-dimensional polygon, $\{\infty\}\#p/q$ or $\left\{\dfrac{p}{0,q}\right\}$.[^o11]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

### Higher rank {#Higher_rank}

[Apeirohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apeirohedron) are the rank 3 analogues of apeirogons, and are the infinite analogues of [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470).[^o22] More generally, *n*-[apeirotopes](wikipedia://en/12768648) or infinite *n*-polytopes are the *n*-dimensional analogues of apeirogons, and are the infinite analogues of *n*-[polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).[^o11]p. 22–25
