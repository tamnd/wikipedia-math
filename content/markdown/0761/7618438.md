# Bill Parry (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bill Parry"}

**William Parry** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (3 July 1934 – 20 August 2006) was an English mathematician who worked in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), and, in particular, [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986). In particular, he studied [subshifts of finite type](wikipedia://en/1986265) nilflows.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Bill Parry was born in [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry) in the [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire) (now the [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_%28county%29)), England, the sixth of seven children. Although he failed the [eleven-plus exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleven_plus_exam), Parry was persuaded by his mathematics teacher at [Coventry Junior Technical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_Junior_Technical_School), specialising in metalwork and woodwork, to aim for university. To get appropriate tuition, he had to travel to [Birmingham Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Technical_College). He won a place at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). Following an MSc at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), he returned to London to study at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) with [Yael Dowker](wikipedia://en/48217935), obtaining his PhD in 1960, with thesis *Ergodic and mixing transformations*.

Having served in lecturing positions at [Birmingham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_University) and the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex), Parry was appointed to a readership at the recently created [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) in 1968; his was the first appointment in analysis. Two years later, he gave a particularly well-received address at the Sixteenth [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Nice, France, and was promoted to professor.

He played a key role in the Warwick Mathematics Department, and was Chair of the Department for 2 years. The rapid rise of the Department's international reputation was due to many, among whom Parry featured prominently. His great mathematical achievements were recognized by his early election to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1984; however, he rarely used the title "Fellow of the Royal Society" except to aid specific causes of interest.

Parry continued to contribute to the mathematical community and others' learning up until his death: he taught the University of Warwick's undergraduate course on ergodic theory as late as 2003.

His published works include more than 80 research articles and four books. His doctoral students include [Mark Pollicott](wikipedia://en/14732052) and [Mary Rees](wikipedia://en/18517772).

He died in [Marton, Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton,_Warwickshire), of cancer exacerbated by [MRSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methicillin-resistant_Staphylococcus_aureus), at the age of 72, on 20 August 2006.

## Research {#Research}

In 1975, Parry and [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) introduced the topological [Parry–Sullivan invariant](wikipedia://en/7740082) for flows in one-dimensional dynamical systems.[^o6][^o7]
