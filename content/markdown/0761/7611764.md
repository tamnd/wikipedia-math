# Hall algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hall algebra** is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) with a basis corresponding to isomorphism classes of finite abelian [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664). It was first discussed by (Steinitz 1901) but forgotten until it was rediscovered by (txt), both of whom published no more than brief summaries of their work. The **Hall polynomials** are the [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) of the **Hall algebra**. The Hall algebra plays an important role in the theory of [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209) and [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig) regarding [canonical bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_basis) in [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678). (Ringel 1990) generalized Hall algebras to more general [categories](wikipedia://en/5869), such as the category of representations of a [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100).

:::

## Construction {#Construction}

A [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) *M* is a direct sum of [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) *p*-power components $C_{p^{\lambda_i}},$ where $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots)$ is a [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) of $n$ called the *type* of *M*. Let $g^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(p)$ be the number of subgroups *N* of *M* such that *N* has type $\nu$ and the quotient *M/N* has type $\mu$. Hall proved that the functions *g* are [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) functions of *p* with integer coefficients. Thus we may replace *p* with an indeterminate *q*, which results in the **Hall polynomials**

$$g^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]. \,$$

Hall next constructs an [associative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_ring) $H$ over $\mathbb{Z}[q]$, now called the **Hall algebra**. This ring has a basis consisting of the symbols $u_\lambda$ and the structure constants of the multiplication in this basis are given by the Hall polynomials:

$$u_\mu u_\nu = \sum_\lambda g^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(q) u_\lambda. \,$$

It turns out that *H* is a commutative ring, freely generated by the elements $u_{\mathbf1^n}$ corresponding to the [elementary *p*-groups](wikipedia://en/4843202). The linear map from *H* to the algebra of [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359) defined on the generators by the formula

$$u_{\mathbf 1^n} \mapsto q^{-n(n-1)/2}e_n \,$$

(where *e*<sub>*n*</sub> is the *n*th [elementary symmetric function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_function)) uniquely extends to a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) and the images of the basis elements $u_\lambda$ may be interpreted via the [Hall–Littlewood symmetric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall–Littlewood_polynomial). Specializing *q* to 1, these symmetric functions become [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062), which are thus closely connected with the theory of Hall polynomials.
