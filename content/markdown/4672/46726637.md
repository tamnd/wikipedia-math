# Paulette Libermann

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paulette Libermann"}

**Paulette Libermann** (14 November 1919 – 10 July 2007) was a French mathematician who became a full professor at the [University of Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rennes) in 1958. As a researcher, she specialized in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498), in particular [G-structures](wikipedia://en/1277699), [Cartan's equivalence method](wikipedia://en/1841654), and [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398). In 1987 she wrote together with [Charles-Michel Marle](wikipedia://en/62842956) one of the first textbooks on symplectic geometry and analytical mechanics.[^o15][^o21]

:::

In [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), the Libermann foliation is named after her.[^o3]

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born on 14 November 1919 in Paris, Libermann was one of three sisters belonging to a family of Russian-Ukrainian [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish)-speaking Jewish immigrants to France.[^o7]

After attending the [Lycée Lamartine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Lamartine), she began her university studies in 1938 at the [École normale supérieure de jeunes filles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_jeunes_filles), a college in [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sèvres) for training women to become school teachers. Due to the reforms of the new director [Eugénie Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugénie_Cotton), who wanted her school to be at the same level of [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29), Libermann was taught by leading mathematicians including [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274), [Jacqueline Ferrand](wikipedia://en/35256327) and [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188).[^o6][^o8][^o9]

Two years later, upon completion of her studies, she was prevented from taking the [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) and becoming a teacher because of the anti-Jewish laws instituted by the [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II). However, thanks to a scholarship provided by Cotton, she began doing research under Cartan's supervision.[^o6][^o9]

In 1942, she and her family escaped Paris for [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon), where they hid from the persecutions by [Klaus Barbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Barbie) for two years.[^o6] After the [liberation of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris) in 1944, she returned to Sèvres and completed her studies, obtaining the agrégation.[^o8][^o9] From 1945 to 1947, she studied at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University).[^o20]

### Career {#Career}

Libermann taught briefly in a school at [Douai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douai), and then got a scholarship to study at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) between 1945 and 1947, where she obtained a bachelor's degree under the supervision of [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179).[^o8]

From 1947 to 1951 she held a teaching position at a school for girls in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg). However, at the encouragement of Élie Cartan, during this period she also continued her research at [Université Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur_University) under the leadership of [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108).[^o8] Together with Ehresmann, she published a number of papers.[^o20]

In 1951 she left teaching for a research position at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique), and in 1953 she completed her doctoral thesis, entitled *Sur le problème d’équivalence de certaines structures infinitésimales* \[On the equivalence problem of certain infinitesimal structures\], under the supervision of [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108).[^o6] The thesis documents her investigation of foliations of [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) and defined what would later be known as the affine structure of a [Lagrangian foliation](wikipedia://en/1879769).[^o20]

After her PhD, Libermann was appointed assistant professor at the [University of Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rennes) in 1954 and full professor at the same university in 1958.[^o8] The university's maths department was different in 1960 because it had a relatively high percentage of women. There were fifteen professors and three of them were women. Libermann was one of them and the other two were [Marie Charpentier](wikipedia://en/55849176) and [Huguette Delavault](wikipedia://en/34230274). Charpentier taught mechanics and Huguette Delavault was to be known for championing the role of women.[^o12]

In 1966 Libermann moved to the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), becoming one of the first few women to receive a full professorship in a French university, and when the university split in 1968, she joined [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University), from which she retired in 1986.[^o8][^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Libermann's research involved many different aspects of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498).[^o6][^o14][^o15] In particular, she worked on [G-structures](wikipedia://en/1277699) and [Cartan's equivalence method](wikipedia://en/1841654),[^o16] [Lie groupoids](wikipedia://en/310923) and [Lie pseudogroups](wikipedia://en/1255458),[^o17] higher-order [connections](wikipedia://en/308815),[^o18] and [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398).[^o19]

After her appointment as a full professor in Paris. Libermann continued her work as an influential researcher, developing the work of Cartan and Ehlermann and publishing some 70 papers until her death in 2007.[^o20]

In 1987, together with [Charles-Michel Marle](wikipedia://en/62842956), she wrote one of the first textbooks on [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [analytical mechanics](wikipedia://en/454450).[^o20][^o21]

Libermann travelled widely, speaking at many conferences and visiting universities and research associations, including Oxford University, the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley), the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) and the [National Institute of Pure and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2334708) in Brazil.[^o20][^o22]

## Honors {#Honors}

In 2026, Libermann was announced as one of 72 historical women in STEM whose names have been proposed to be added to the [72 men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_72_names_on_the_Eiffel_Tower) already celebrated on the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower). The plan was announced by the Mayor of Paris, [Anne Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) following the recommendations of a committee led by Isabelle Vauglin of [Femmes et Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femmes_et_Sciences) and Jean-François Martins, representing the operating company which runs the Eiffel Tower.[^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27]
