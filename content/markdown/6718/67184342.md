# Non-numerical words for quantities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-numerical words for quantities"}

The English language has a number of [words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word) that denote specific or approximate [quantities](wikipedia://en/691277) that are themselves not [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690).[^o2] Along with numerals, and special-purpose words like some, any, much, more, every, few, several, and all, they are [quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantifier_%28linguistics%29). Quantifiers are a kind of determiner and occur in many constructions with other determiners, like articles: e.g., two dozen or more than a score. Scientific non-numerical quantities are represented as [SI units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units).

:::

| [Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartet) | 4 | Referring to people working or collaborating especially in musical performance; also: [Quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintet), [Sextet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextet), [Septet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septet), [Octet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octet_%28music%29) |
| [Great gross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1728_%28number%29) | 1,728 | A dozen gross (12x144) |
