# Local convergence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local convergence"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), an [iterative method](wikipedia://en/15237) is called **locally convergent** if the successive [approximations](wikipedia://en/336271) produced by the method are guaranteed to [converge](wikipedia://en/285773) to a solution when the initial approximation is already close enough to the solution. Iterative methods for [nonlinear equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_equation) and their systems, such as [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) are usually only locally convergent.[^o2]

:::

An iterative method that converges for an arbitrary initial approximation is called **globally convergent**. Iterative methods for systems of [linear equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equations) are usually globally convergent.[^o2]
