# Möbius ladder

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Möbius ladder"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Möbius ladder** $M_{n}$, for even numbers $n$, is formed from an $n$-[cycle](wikipedia://en/878962) by adding edges (called "rungs") connecting opposite pairs of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) in the cycle. It is a [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508), [circulant graph](wikipedia://en/3498787), so-named because (with the exception of $M_{6}$ (the [utility graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_graph) $K_{3,3}$), $M_{n}$ has exactly $n/2$ four-cycles[^n1] which link together by their shared edges to form a topological [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817). Möbius ladders were named and first studied by (txt).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

For every even $n > 4$, the Möbius ladder $M_{n}$ is a [nonplanar](wikipedia://en/24314) [apex graph](wikipedia://en/28228942), meaning that it cannot be drawn without crossings in the plane but removing one vertex allows the remaining graph to be drawn without crossings. These graphs have [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) one, and can be embedded without crossings on a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) or [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350). Thus, they are examples of [toroidal graphs](wikipedia://en/1633057). (Li 2005) explores embeddings of these graphs onto higher genus surfaces.

Möbius ladders are [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175) – they have symmetries taking any vertex to any other vertex – but (with the exceptions of $M_{4}$ and $M_{6}$) they are not [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive). The edges from the cycle from which the ladder is formed can be distinguished from the rungs of the ladder, because each cycle edge belongs to a single 4-cycle, while each rung belongs to two such cycles. Therefore, there is no symmetry taking a cycle edge to a rung edge or vice versa.

When *n* ≡ [2 (mod 4)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28mod_4%29), *M*<sub>*n*</sub> is [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431). When *n* ≡ [0 (mod 4)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28mod_4%29), it is not bipartite. The endpoints of each rung are an even distance apart in the initial cycle, so adding each rung creates an odd cycle. In this case, because the graph is 3-regular but not bipartite, by [Brooks' theorem](wikipedia://en/21042117) it has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 3. (De Mier Noy 2004) show that the Möbius ladders are uniquely determined by their [Tutte polynomials](wikipedia://en/3892303).

The Möbius ladder *M*<sub>8</sub> has 392 [spanning trees](wikipedia://en/455770); it and *M*<sub>6</sub> have the most spanning trees among all cubic graphs with the same number of vertices.[^o9] However, the 10-vertex cubic graph with the most spanning trees is the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), which is not a Möbius ladder.

The [Tutte polynomials](wikipedia://en/3892303) of the Möbius ladders may be computed by a simple [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806).[^n2]

## Graph minors {#Graph_minors}

*Image: Wagner graph ham.svg.* The Wagner graph *M*<sub>8</sub>

Möbius ladders play an important role in the theory of [graph minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_%28graph_theory%29). The earliest result of this type is a theorem of (txt) that graphs with no *K*<sub>5</sub> minor can be formed by using [clique-sum](wikipedia://en/17598460) operations to combine planar graphs and the Möbius ladder *M*<sub>8</sub>; for this reason *M*<sub>8</sub> is called the [Wagner graph](wikipedia://en/17598450).

(Gubser 1996) defines an *almost-planar graph* to be a nonplanar graph for which every nontrivial minor is planar; he shows that 3-connected almost-planar graphs are Möbius ladders or members of a small number of other families, and that other almost-planar graphs can be formed from these by a sequence of simple operations.

(Maharry 2000) shows that almost all graphs that do not have a [cube](wikipedia://en/6706815) minor can be derived by a sequence of simple operations from Möbius ladders.

## Chemistry and physics {#Chemistry_and_physics}

(Walba Richards Haltiwanger 1982) first synthesized molecular structures in the form of a Möbius ladder, and since then this structure has been of interest in chemistry and chemical stereography,[^n3] especially in view of the ladder-like form of DNA molecules. With this application in mind, (txt) studies the mathematical symmetries of embeddings of Möbius ladders in **R**<sup>3</sup>. In particular, as she shows, every three-dimensional embedding of a Möbius ladder with an odd number of rungs is topologically [chiral](wikipedia://en/32703814): it cannot be converted into its mirror image by a continuous deformation of space without passing one edge through another. On the other hand, Möbius ladders with an [even number](wikipedia://en/143135) of rungs all do have embeddings into **R**<sup>3</sup> that can be deformed into their mirror images.

Möbius ladders have also been used as the shape of a [superconducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity) ring in experiments to study the effects of conductor topology on electron interactions.[^o19]

## Combinatorial optimization {#Combinatorial_optimization}

Möbius ladders have also been used in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), as part of [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215) approaches to problems of [set packing](wikipedia://en/2017636) and linear ordering. Certain configurations within these problems can be used to define facets of the [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) describing a [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) [relaxation](wikipedia://en/6368430) of the problem; these facets are called Möbius ladder constraints.[^o21]
