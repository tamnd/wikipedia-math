# Both/and reasoning

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Both/and reasoning"}

**Both/and** is an [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy) concept which refers to a form of reasoning which resists [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_thinking) or [either/or](wikipedia://en/17687) styles of thinking.[^o2][^o3]

:::

Unlike dualistic styles of reasoning, both/and means that between two options, both can be valid, or that their opposition may present opportunities for [dialectical](wikipedia://en/52813) synthesis, rather than a complete rejection of one of the premises in favor of the other.

Both/and is associated with dialectical thinking, which means investigating contradictions in order to attain higher understanding. However, it also appears in broader systems of thought, such as the concept of [nondualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondualism), in which the distinction between self and other is transcended.[^o4][^o5]

The term has been used in a texts on [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_management), [literary theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_theory), classroom research, [religious studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_studies), [methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodology), and [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations).[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]
