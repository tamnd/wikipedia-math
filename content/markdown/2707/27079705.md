# Ronald Brown (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald Brown"}

**Ronald Brown [FLSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_Wales)** (born 4 January 1935 – 6 December 2024) was an English mathematician. He was a [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in the School of Computer Science at [Bangor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_University).[^o3] He authored many books and more than 160 journal articles. Brown died on 6 December 2024, at the age of 89.[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born on 4 January 1935 in London, Brown attended [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), obtaining a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1956 and a [D.Phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Phil.) in 1962.[^o6] Brown began his teaching career during his doctorate work, serving as an assistant lecturer at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) before assuming the position of Lecturer. In 1964, he took a position at the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull), serving first as a Senior Lecturer and then as a Reader before becoming a Professor of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) at Bangor University, then a part of the [University of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wales), in 1970.

Brown served as Professor of Pure Mathematics for 30 years; he also served during the 1983–84 term as a Professor for one month at [Louis Pasteur University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur_University) in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg).[^o6] In 1999, Brown took a half-time research professorship until he became Professor Emeritus in 2001. He was elected as a Fellow of the [Learned Society of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Society_of_Wales) in 2016.

## Editing and writing {#Editing_and_writing}

Brown served as an [editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor) or on the [editorial board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board) for a number of print and [electronic journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_journal). He began in 1968 with the *[Chapman & Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_&_Hall) Mathematics Series*, contributing through 1986.[^o6] In 1975, he joined the editorial advisory board of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), remaining through 1994. Two years later, he joined the editorial board of [Applied Categorical Structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media),[^o8] continuing through 2007. From 1995 and 1999, respectively, he has been active with the electronic journals *[Theory and Applications of Categories](http://www.tac.mta.ca/tac/)*[^o9] and *[Homology, Homotopy and Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology,_Homotopy_and_Applications)*,[^o10] which he helped found. Since 2006, he has been involved with *[Journal of Homotopy and Related Structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv)*.[^o11] His mathematical research interests ranged from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543), to [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [higher-dimensional algebra](wikipedia://en/19515158).[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16]

Brown authored or edited a number of books and over 160 academic papers published in academic journals or collections. His first published paper was "Ten topologies for X × Y", which was published in the *Quarterly Journal of Mathematics* in 1963.[^o17] Since then, his publications have appeared in many journals, including but not limited to the *[Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra)*, *[Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)*, *[Mathematische Zeitschrift](wikipedia://en/7457514)*, *[College Mathematics Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Mathematics_Journal)*, and *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)*. He was also known for several recent co-authored papers on [categorical ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology).[^o18]

Among his several books and standard topology and algebraic topology textbooks are: *Elements of Modern Topology* (1968), *Low-Dimensional Topology* (1979, co-edited with T.L. Thickstun), *Topology: a geometric account of general topology, homotopy types, and the fundamental groupoid* (1998),[^o19][^o20] *Topology and Groupoids* (2006)[^o21] and *Nonabelian Algebraic Topology: Filtered Spaces, Crossed Complexes, Cubical Homotopy Groupoids* (EMS, 2010).[^o21][^o22][^o23][^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30]

His recent fundamental results that extend the classical [Van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert-van_Kampen_theorem) to higher homotopy in higher dimensions (HHSvKT) are of substantial interest for solving several problems in algebraic topology, both old and new.[^o31] Moreover, developments in algebraic topology have often had wider implications, as for example in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and also in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). Such higher-dimensional (HHSvKT) theorems are about homotopy invariants of structured spaces, and especially those for filtered spaces or *n*-cubes of spaces. An example is the fact that the relative [Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385) theorem is a consequence of HHSvKT, and this then suggested a triadic [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587).
