# Arthur Lee Dixon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Lee Dixon"}

**Arthur Lee Dixon** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (27 November 1867 — 20 February 1955) was a British mathematician and holder of the [Waynflete Professorship of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorship) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Dixon was born on 27 November 1867 in [Pickering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickering,_North_Yorkshire), [North Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Riding_of_Yorkshire) to G.T. Dixon, and was the younger brother of [Alfred Cardew Dixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Cardew_Dixon). From 1879 to 1885 he studied at [Kingswood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswood_School), before matriculating at [Worcester College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_College,_Oxford) as a scholar to study mathematics.[^o7]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Dixon became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbridge_Fellow) of [Merton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford) in 1891, and [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorship#Waynflete_Professors_of_Pure_Mathematics) in 1922.[^o9][^o10]

His research was focused on algebra and its application to geometry, [elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_functions) and [hyperelliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_function). From 1908 onwards he published a series of papers on [algebraic eliminants](wikipedia://en/245978). He also published a dozen joint papers with W.L. Ferrar on [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).

Dixon was the last mathematical professor at Oxford to hold a life tenure,[^o11] and although he was not particularly noted for his mathematical innovations he did publish many papers on [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and the application of algebra to geometry, [elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_functions) and [hyperelliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperelliptic_function).

He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1912 and served as president of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 1924 to 1926.[^o7]

Dixon died on 20 February 1955.[^o3]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1902 Dixon married Catherine Rieder. Catherine found the atmosphere in Oxford difficult for her health, and spent a lot of time in [Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau,_Pyrénées-Atlantiques) to recover. The couple had one child, a daughter, who later married F.J. Baden Fuller; when Catherine died in 1930, Dixon moved in with his daughter and her husband in [Sandgate, Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandgate,_Kent), where he spent the rest of his life.[^o3]
