# Sample exclusion dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sample exclusion dimension"}

In [computational learning theory](wikipedia://en/387537), **sample exclusion dimensions** arise in the study of exact [concept learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_learning) with queries.[^o2]

:::

In [algorithmic learning theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_learning_theory), a *concept* over a domain *X* is a [Boolean function](wikipedia://en/753349) over *X*. Here we only consider finite domains. A *partial approximation* *S* of a concept *c* is a Boolean function over $Y\subseteq X$ such that *c* is an extension to *S*.

Let *C* be a class of concepts and *c* be a concept (not necessarily in *C*). Then a *specifying set* for c w.r.t. *C*, denoted by *S* is a partial approximation *S* of *c* such that *C* contains at most one extension to *S*. If we have observed a specifying set for some concept w.r.t. *C*, then we have enough information to verify a concept in *C* with at most one more mind change.

The *exclusion dimension*, denoted by *XD*(*C*), of a concept class is the maximum of the size of the minimum specifying set of *c*' with respect to *C*, where *c*' is a concept not in *C*.
