# Veronese map

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Veronese map"}

The **Veronese map** of degree 2 is a mapping from $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ to the space of [symmetric matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_matrices) $(n+1){\times}(n+1)$ defined by the formula:[^o2]

:::

$$V\colon(x_0,\dots,x_n)\to \begin{pmatrix} x_0\cdot x_0&x_0\cdot x_1&\dots&x_0\cdot x_n \\ x_1\cdot x_0&x_1\cdot x_1&\dots&x_1\cdot x_n \\ \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots \\ x_n\cdot x_0&x_n\cdot x_1&\dots&x_n\cdot x_n \end{pmatrix}.$$

Note that $V(x)=V(-x)$ for any $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$.

In particular, the restriction of $V$ to the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^n$ factors through the [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$, which defines the **Veronese embedding** of $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$. The image of the Veronese embedding is called the **Veronese submanifold**, and for $n=2$ it is known as the [Veronese surface](wikipedia://en/1569217).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The matrices in the image of the Veronese embedding correspond to projections onto one-dimensional subspaces in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$. They can be described by the equations:

$$A^T=A,\quad \mathrm{tr}\,A=1,\quad A^2=A.$$

In other words, the matrices in the image of $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^n$ have unit [trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_trace) and unit norm. Specifically, the following is true:

  - The image lies in an [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) of dimension $n+\tfrac {n\cdot(n+1)}2$.
  - The image lies on an $(n-1+\tfrac {n\cdot(n+1)}2)$-sphere with radius $r_n= \sqrt{1-\tfrac1{n+1}}$.
    - Moreover, the image forms a [minimal submanifold](wikipedia://en/276734) in this sphere.
- The Veronese embedding induces a [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) $2\cdot g$, where $g$ denotes the canonical metric on $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n-1}$.
- The Veronese embedding maps each geodesic in $\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n-1}$ to a circle with radius $\tfrac1{\sqrt{2}}$.
  - In particular, all the [normal curvatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_curvature) of the image are equal to $\sqrt{2}$.
- The Veronese manifold is **extrinsically symmetric**, meaning that reflection in any of its normal spaces maps the manifold onto itself.

:::

## Variations and generalizations {#Variations_and_generalizations}

Analogous Veronese embeddings are constructed for complex and quaternionic projective spaces, as well as for the [Cayley plane](wikipedia://en/9410951).
