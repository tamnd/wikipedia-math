# Alfred Horn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Horn"}

**Alfred Horn** (February 17, 1918 – April 16, 2001) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) notable for his work in [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory) and [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200). His 1951 paper "On sentences which are true of direct unions of algebras" described [Horn clauses](wikipedia://en/333824) and [Horn sentences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horn_sentence), which later became used in [logic programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_programming).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Horn was born on [Lower East Side, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_East_Side,_Manhattan). His parents were both [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf), and his father died when Horn was three years old. At this point, the children moved in with their grandparents on the mother's side. They would later move to [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) where Horn spent most of his childhood, raised by his extended family.

Horn attended the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York), and later, [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) where he earned a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He went on to earn his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1946. A year later, he started work at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), where he stayed until his retirement in 1988. He died in 2001 in [Pacific Palisades, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Palisades,_Los_Angeles).
