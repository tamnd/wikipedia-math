# Kirillov character formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kirillov character formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), for a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$, the [Kirillov orbit method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirillov_orbit_method) gives a heuristic method in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200). It connects the [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) of [coadjoint orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coadjoint_orbit), which lie in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of *G*, to the [infinitesimal characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_character) of the [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982). The method got its name after the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515).

:::

At its simplest, it states that a character of a Lie group may be given by the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021) [supported](wikipedia://en/381013) on the coadjoint orbits, weighted by the square-root of the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290), denoted by $j$. It does not apply to all Lie groups, but works for a number of classes of [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) Lie groups, including [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235), some [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_Lie_group) groups, and [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326).

The Kirillov orbit method has led to a number of important developments in Lie theory, including the [Duflo isomorphism](wikipedia://en/34050946) and the [wrapping map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrapping_map).

## Character formula for compact Lie groups {#Character_formula_for_compact_Lie_groups}

Let $\lambda \in \mathfrak{t}^*$ be the [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) of an [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/12695) $\pi$, where $\mathfrak{t}^*$ is the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28mathematics%29) of the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136), and let $\rho$ be half the sum of the positive [roots](wikipedia://en/277087).

We denote by $\mathcal{O}_{\lambda + \rho}$ the coadjoint orbit through $\lambda + \rho \in \mathfrak{t}^*$ and by $\mu_{\lambda + \rho}$ the $G$-invariant [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on $\mathcal{O}_{\lambda + \rho}$ with total mass $\dim \pi = d_\lambda$, known as the [Liouville measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville_measure). If $\chi_\lambda$ is the character of the [representation](wikipedia://en/12695), the**Kirillov's character formula** for compact Lie groups is given by

$$j^{1/2}(X) \chi_\lambda (\exp X) = \int_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda + \rho}} e^{i\beta (X)}d\mu_{\lambda + \rho} (\beta), \; \forall \; X \in \mathfrak{g},$$

where $j(X)$ is the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of the exponential map.

:::

## Example: SU(2) {#Example:_SU(2)}

For the case of [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29), the [highest weights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) are the positive half integers, and $\rho = 1/2$. The coadjoint orbits are the two-dimensional [spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spheres) of radius $\lambda + 1/2$, centered at the origin in 3-dimensional space.

By the theory of [Bessel functions](wikipedia://en/4700), it may be shown that

$$\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda + 1/2}} e^{i\beta (X)}d\mu_{\lambda + 1/2} (\beta) = \frac{\sin((2\lambda + 1)X)}{X/2}, \; \forall \; X \in \mathfrak{g},$$

and

$$j(X) = \frac{\sin X/2}{X/2}$$

thus yielding the characters of *SU*(2):

$$\chi_\lambda (\exp X) = \frac{\sin((2\lambda + 1)X)}{\sin X/2}$$
