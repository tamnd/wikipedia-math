# Percival Frost

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Percival Frost"}

**Percival Frost** (1817–1898), was an English mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Percival Frost was born at [Kingston upon Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_upon_Hull) on 1 September 1817, the second son of Charles Frost. He was educated at Beverley and Oakham, and entered [St. John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_College,_Cambridge), in October 1835, graduating B.A. as [second wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_wrangler) in 1839 and M.A. in 1842. He was chosen first Smith prizeman in 1839, beating the senior wrangler, [Benjamin Morgan Cowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Morgan_Cowie), his fellow-collegian, and he was elected to a fellowship at St. John's College on 19 March. In 1841 he was ordained deacon, and in the same year vacated his fellowship by marriage. He held a mathematical lectureship in [Jesus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Cambridge) from 1847 to 1859, and in King's College from 1859 to 1889; but his chief work consisted in the tuition of private pupils, among whom were [John Rigby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rigby_%28politician%29), [William Kingdon Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820), and [Joseph Wolstenholme](wikipedia://en/98915).

He was a man of wide interests and varied attainments, an accomplished pianoforte player, and a successful painter in water-colours. On 2 June 1841 he was married at Finchley to Jennett Louisa, daughter of Richard Dixon of Oak Lodge, Finchley.

In 1854, Frost edited the first three sections of [Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophiae_Naturalis_Principia_Mathematica) by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627). New editions were published in 1863, 1878, and 1883. In 1863 he prepared, in conjunction with [Joseph Wolstenholme](wikipedia://en/98915), *A Treatise on Solid Geometry*, of which second and third editions, by Frost alone, appeared in 1875 and 1886. *Hints for the Solution of Problems* in the Third Edition of *Solid Geometry* was published in 1887. His third work, *An Elementary Treatise on Curve Tracing*, appeared in 1872.

On 7 June 1883, Frost was admitted as a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), and in the same year he was elected by King's College, Cambridge, to a fellowship, which he retained until his death. In 1883 Frost proceeded to the recently established degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science).

Frost died at Cambridge on 5 June 1898, at his house in Fitzwilliam Street, and was buried on 10 June in the [Mill Road cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Road_Cemetery,_Cambridge), Cambridge.

Besides the works already mentioned, Frost was the author of numerous papers in the *[Cambridge Mathematical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Journal)*, the *[Oxford and Cambridge Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_and_Cambridge_Journal_of_Mathematics)*, and the *[Quarterly Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterly_Journal_of_Mathematics)*.
