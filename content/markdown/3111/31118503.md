# Beta negative binomial distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beta negative binomial distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **beta negative binomial distribution** is the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of a [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $X$ equal to the number of failures needed to get $r$ successes in a sequence of [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) [Bernoulli trials](wikipedia://en/102653). The probability $p$ of success on each trial stays constant within any given experiment but varies across different experiments following a [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074). Thus the distribution is a [compound probability distribution](wikipedia://en/28897820).

:::

This distribution has also been called both the **inverse Markov-Pólya distribution** and the **generalized Waring distribution**[^o2] or simply abbreviated as the **BNB** distribution. A shifted form of the distribution has been called the **beta-Pascal distribution**.[^o2]

If parameters of the beta distribution are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and if

$$X \mid p \sim \mathrm{NB}(r,p),$$

where

$$p \sim \textrm{B}(\alpha,\beta),$$

then the marginal distribution of $X$ (i.e. the [posterior predictive distribution](wikipedia://en/35052447)) is a beta negative binomial distribution:

$$X \sim \mathrm{BNB}(r,\alpha,\beta).$$

In the above, $\mathrm{NB}(r,p)$ is the [negative binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/45177) and $\textrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)$ is the [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074).

## Definition and derivation {#Definition_and_derivation}

Denoting $f_{X|p}(k|q), f_{p}(q|\alpha,\beta)$ the densities of the negative binomial and beta distributions respectively, we obtain the PMF $f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)$ of the BNB distribution by marginalization:

$$\begin{aligned} f(k|\alpha,\beta,r) \; =& \; \int_0^1 f_{X|p}(k|r,q) \cdot f_{p}(q|\alpha,\beta) \mathrm{d} q \\ =& \; \int_0^1 \binom{k+r-1}{k} (1-q)^k q^r \cdot \frac{q^{\alpha-1}(1-q)^{\beta-1}} {\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)} \mathrm{d} q \\ =& \; \frac{1}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)} \binom{k+r-1}{k} \int_0^1 q^{\alpha+r-1}(1-q)^{\beta+k-1} \mathrm{d} q \end{aligned}$$

Noting that the integral evaluates to:

$$\int_0^1 q^{\alpha+r-1}(1-q)^{\beta+k-1} \mathrm{d} q = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+r)\Gamma(\beta+k)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+k+r)}$$

we can arrive at the following formulas by relatively simple manipulations.

If $r$ is an integer, then the PMF can be written in terms of the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660),:

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\binom{r+k-1}k\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+r,\beta+k)}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}.$$

More generally, the PMF can be written

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\frac{\Gamma(r+k)}{k!\;\Gamma(r)}\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+r,\beta+k)}{\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}$$

or

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\frac{\mathrm{B}(r+k,\alpha+\beta)}{\mathrm{B}(r,\alpha)}\frac{\Gamma(k+\beta)}{k!\;\Gamma(\beta)}.$$

### PMF expressed with Gamma {#PMF_expressed_with_Gamma}

Using the properties of the [Beta function](wikipedia://en/245660), the PMF with integer $r$ can be rewritten as:

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\binom{r+k-1}k\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+r)\Gamma(\beta+k)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+r+\beta+k)\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}.$$

More generally, the PMF can be written as

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\frac{\Gamma(r+k)}{k!\;\Gamma(r)}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+r)\Gamma(\beta+k)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha+r+\beta+k)\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}.$$

### PMF expressed with the rising Pochammer symbol {#PMF_expressed_with_the_rising_Pochammer_symbol}

The PMF is often also presented in terms of the [Pochammer symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pochammer_symbol) for integer $r$

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r)=\frac{r^{(k)}\alpha^{(r)}\beta^{(k)}}{k!(\alpha+\beta)^{(r+k)}}$$

## Properties {#Properties}

### Factorial Moments {#Factorial_Moments}

The $k$-th [factorial moment](wikipedia://en/790823) of a beta negative binomial random variable $X$ is defined for $k < \alpha$ and in this case is equal to

$$\operatorname{E}\bigl[(X)_k\bigr] = \frac{\Gamma(r+k)}{\Gamma(r)}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+k)}{\Gamma(\beta)}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha-k)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.$$

### Non-identifiable {#Non-identifiable}

The beta negative binomial is [non-identifiable](wikipedia://en/22884398) which can be seen easily by simply swapping $r$ and $\beta$ in the above density or [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) and noting that it is unchanged. Thus [estimation](wikipedia://en/1565926) demands that a [constraint](wikipedia://en/7418540) be placed on $r$, $\beta$ or both.

### Relation to other distributions {#Relation_to_other_distributions}

The beta negative binomial distribution contains the beta geometric distribution as a special case when either $r=1$ or $\beta=1$. It can therefore approximate the [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922) arbitrarily well. It also approximates the negative binomial distribution arbitrarily well for large $\alpha$. It can therefore approximate the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) arbitrarily well for large $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $r$.

### Heavy tailed {#Heavy_tailed}

By [Stirling's approximation](wikipedia://en/151783) to the beta function, it can be easily shown that for large $k$

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta,r) \sim \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+r)}{\Gamma(r)\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}\frac{k^{r-1}}{(\beta+k)^{r+\alpha}}$$

which implies that the beta negative binomial distribution is [heavy tailed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_tail_distribution) and that [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) less than or equal to $\alpha$ do not exist.

:::

## Beta geometric distribution {#Beta_geometric_distribution}

The beta geometric distribution is an important special case of the beta negative binomial distribution occurring for $r=1$. In this case the pmf simplifies to

$$f(k|\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\mathrm{B}(\alpha+1,\beta+k)} {\mathrm{B}(\alpha,\beta)}.$$

This distribution is used in some [Buy Till you Die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Till_you_Die) (BTYD) models.

Further, when $\beta=1$ the beta geometric reduces to the [Yule–Simon distribution](wikipedia://en/1119623). However, it is more common to define the Yule-Simon distribution in terms of a shifted version of the beta geometric. In particular, if $X \sim BG(\alpha,1)$ then $X+1 \sim YS(\alpha)$.

## Beta negative binomial as a Pólya urn model {#Beta_negative_binomial_as_a_Pólya_urn_model}

In the case when the 3 parameters $r, \alpha$ and $\beta$ are positive integers, the Beta negative binomial can also be motivated by an [urn model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urn_model) - or more specifically a basic [Pólya urn model](wikipedia://en/28955783). Consider an urn initially containing $\alpha$ red balls (the stopping color) and $\beta$ blue balls. At each step of the model, a ball is drawn at random from the urn and replaced, along with one additional ball of the same color. The process is repeated over and over, until $r$ red colored balls are drawn. The random variable $X$ of observed draws of blue balls are distributed according to a $\mathrm{BNB}(r, \alpha, \beta)$. Note, at the end of the experiment, the urn always contains the fixed number $r+\alpha$ of red balls while containing the random number $X+\beta$ blue balls.

By the non-identifiability property, $X$ can be equivalently generated with the urn initially containing $\alpha$ red balls (the stopping color) and $r$ blue balls and stopping when $\beta$ red balls are observed.
