# Herbert Grötzsch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Grötzsch"}

**Camillo Herbert Grötzsch** (21 May 1902 – 15 May 1993) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known primarily for his work on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), specifically [univalent functions](wikipedia://en/1534382) and [conformal mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping), and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). He was the first to introduce the concept of a [quasiconformal mapping](wikipedia://en/2749226). He was the discoverer and eponym of the [Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783), a [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996) that requires four colors in any [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743), and [Grötzsch's theorem](wikipedia://en/24917736), the result that every triangle-free [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) requires at most three colors.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Camillo Herbert Grötzsch was born in [Döbeln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Döbeln) in 1902. His father, Emil Camillo Grötzsch, received his doctorate under [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) and was a mathematics teacher. Grötzsch studied mathematics at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) from 1922 to 1926 under [Paul Koebe](wikipedia://en/4313643). He followed Koebe to the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) and received his doctorate there in 1929.

In 1931 Grötzsch obtained his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen), where he taught until his termination for refusing to join the [Sturmabteilung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) in 1935. he subsequently worked in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) on [J. C. Poggendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Christian_Poggendorf)'s *Biographisch-literarisches Handworterbuch zur Geschichte der exacten Wissenschaften* until 1939.

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Grötzsch served in the artillery until 1942 and the home service until 1944. In 1944 he worked on [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) research at the [Aerodynamics Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamics_Research_Institute) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen).

After the war, Grötzsch joined at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) in 1945. In 1948 he moved to the [Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_Halle-Wittenberg), where he would remain until his retirement in 1967.

Grötzsch was elected to the [Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina) in 1959. In 1967 he received the [National Prize of the German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Prize_of_the_German_Democratic_Republic). The keynote speech in honor of his 75th birthday was given by [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972).

In 1951 Grötzsch married Annemarie Jung, daughter of mathematician [Heinrich Jung](wikipedia://en/37050659); they had three children.

Grötzsch died in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt) in 1993.
