# Wladimir Seidel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wladimir Seidel"}

**Wladimir P. Seidel** (21 December 1907 – 12 January 1981) was a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians)-born [German-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Doctor of Mathematics. He held a fellowship as a Benjamin Peirce Professor in [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). During World War II, he was with the *Montreal Theory* group for the [National Research Council of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_of_Canada).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) on 21 December 1907.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

He obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) on 26 February 1930 with a dissertation entitled *Über die Ränderzuordnung bei konformen Abbildungen*, advised by [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625).[^o6]

He joined the faculty of Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as [Benjamin Peirce](wikipedia://en/364270) Instructor from 1932 to 1933,[^o7][^o8] at [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) from 1941 to 1955, at [The Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) from 1952 to 1953, at [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) from 1955 to 1963, and at [Wayne State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_State_University) in [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit) since 1963.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was with the *Montreal Theory* group for the [National Research Council of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_of_Canada).[^o9]

The [Seidel class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seidel_class) is named after him.[^o10]

He was married to Leah Lappin-Seidel (1904–1999).[^o11]
