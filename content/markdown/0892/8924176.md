# Principle of nonvacuous contrast

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principle of nonvacuous contrast"}

The **principle of nonvacuous contrast** is a logical or methodological principle which requires that a genuine [predicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28grammar%29) never refer to everything, or to nothing, within its [universe of discourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe_of_discourse). The implications of this is that for a predicate to be meaningful it can neither apply to all members of a group or to no members of the group.

:::
