# Abraham Ziv

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abraham Ziv"}

**Abraham Ziv** (אברהם זיו; 1940-3-6–2013-3-5) was an Israeli mathematician, known for his contributions to the [Zero-sum problem](wikipedia://en/2546047) as one of the discoverers of the [Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv_theorem).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Abraham Zubkowski (later Ziv) was born in [Avihayil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avihayil) to Haim and Zila Zubkovski. In the 1950s, he changed his surname as part of the widespread [Hebraization of surnames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebraization_of_surnames) trend. He studied at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology), where he earned his Ph.D. in mathematics, after receiving his master's degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1961, at the age of 21, Ziv proved along with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Abraham Ginzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Ginzburg) the general result that every sequence of $2n - 1\$ elements of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ contains $n$ terms that sum to zero.[^o5]

In 1972 Ziv was part of the founding team of [IBM R&D Labs in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_R&D_Labs_in_Israel), where he stayed until retirement. In his time at IBM he wrote 21 more publications[^o6] and 6 patents.[^o7]
