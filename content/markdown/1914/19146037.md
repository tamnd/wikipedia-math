# Uniform tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform tree"}

*Image: Uniform tree.svg.* Section of the uniform tree for graph $G$

In mathematics, a **uniform tree** is a locally finite [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) which is the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of a [finite graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_graph). Equivalently, the full [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) $G=Aut(X)$ of the tree, which is a [locally compact topological group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_topological_group), is [unimodular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodular_group) and $G/X$ is finite. Also equivalent is the existence of a uniform [X-lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_lattice) in $G$.

:::

For a graph $G$ which contains no [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609), $G$ is its own uniform tree. If $G$ contains at least 1 cycle, its uniform tree is an infinite tree.

**Leighton's Graph Covering Theorem** states that any two finite graphs that share a common covering must also share a common finite covering. Walter D. Neumann expanded on this in 2011, proving any two graphs that have a common covering necessarily have the same universal covering. This means that every uniform tree corresponds to a unique family of finite graphs.
