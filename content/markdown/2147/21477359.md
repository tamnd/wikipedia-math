# Falling cat problem

*Image: Cat fall 150x300 6fps.gif.* A falling cat modeled as two independently rotating parts turns around while maintaining zero net angular momentum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Falling cat problem"}

The **falling cat problem** is a problem that consists of explaining the underlying [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) behind the observation of the [cat righting reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat_righting_reflex).

:::

Although amusing and trivial to pose, the solution of the problem is not as straightforward as its statement would suggest. The apparent contradiction with the law of conservation of angular momentum is resolved because the cat is not a [rigid body](wikipedia://en/455769), but instead is permitted to change its shape during the fall owing to the cat's flexible [backbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column) and non-functional [collar-bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collar-bone). The behavior of the cat is thus typical of the mechanics of [deformable bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformable_bodies).

Several explanations have been proposed for this phenomenon since the late 19th century:

- Cats rely on conservation of [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839).[^n1]
- The rotation angle of the front body is larger than that of the rear body.[^n2]
- The dynamics of the falling cat have been explained using the [Udwadia–Kalaba equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udwadia–Kalaba_equation).[^n3]

## History {#History}

The falling cat problem has elicited interest from scientists including [George Gabriel Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gabriel_Stokes), [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696), and [Étienne-Jules Marey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne-Jules_Marey). In a letter to his wife, Katherine Mary Clerk Maxwell, Maxwell wrote, "There is a tradition in [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) that when I was here I discovered a method of throwing a cat so as not to light on its feet, and that I used to throw cats out of windows. I had to explain that the proper object of research was to find how quick the cat would turn round, and that the proper method was to let the cat drop on a table or bed from about two inches, and that even then the cat lights on her feet."[^n4]

*Image: Falling cat 1894.jpg.* *[Falling Cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_Cat)* – chronophotographic sequence which appeared in the journal *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* in 1894, recorded by [Marey's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne-Jules_Marey). The editor of *Nature* wrote, "The expression of offended dignity shown by the cat at the end of the first series indicates a want of interest in scientific investigation."

Whereas the cat-falling problem was regarded as a mere curiosity by Maxwell, Stokes, and others, a more rigorous study of the problem was conducted by [Étienne-Jules Marey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne-Jules_Marey) who applied [chronophotography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronophotography) to capture the cat's descent. Marey deduced that, as the cat had no [rotational motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_motion) at the start of its descent, the cat was not "cheating" by using the cat handler's hand as a [fulcrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lever). This in itself posed a problem as it implied that it was possible for a body in free fall to acquire angular momentum. Marey also showed that [air resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_resistance) played no role in facilitating the righting of the cat's body.

His investigations were subsequently published in *[Comptes Rendus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptes_rendus_de_l'Académie_des_sciences),*[^n5] and a summary of his findings were published in the science magazine *[La Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Nature).*[^n6] The article's summary in *La Nature* appeared thus:

M. Marey thinks that it is the inertia of its own mass that the cat uses to right itself. The torsion couple which produces the action of the muscles of the vertebra acts at first on the forelegs which have a very small motion of inertia on account of the front feet being foreshortened and pressed against the neck. The hind legs, however, being stretched out and almost perpendicular to the axis of the body, possesses a moment of inertia which opposes motion in the opposite direction to that which the torsion couple tends to produce. In the second phase of the action, the attitude of the feet is reversed, and it is the inertia of the forepart that furnishes a fulcrum for the rotation of the rear.

Despite the publication of the images, many physicists at the time maintained that the cat was still "cheating" by using the handler's hand from its starting position to right itself, as the cat's motion would otherwise seem to imply a [rigid body](wikipedia://en/455769) acquiring angular momentum.[^n7]

## Solution {#Solution}

The problem was initially solved in 1969 by modelling the cat as a pair of cylinders (the front and back halves of the cat) capable of changing their relative orientations and has been described in terms of a [connection](wikipedia://en/470632) in the configuration space that encapsulates the relative motions of the two parts of the cat permitted by the physics.[^n8][^n9] We can model the simplified "cat" as two cylinders connected by a flexible spine in the middle. We also assume that the flexible spine disallows twisting, so both cylinders can only rotate by the same degree. Thus, the configuration space of the system has only three dimensions:

- The overall angle of the cat around the horizontal axis.
- The bending angle of the spine
- The turning angle of the cylinders

Framed in this way, the dynamics of the falling cat problem is a prototypical example of a [nonholonomic system](wikipedia://en/554248),[^n10] the study of which is among the central preoccupations of [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039). A solution of the falling cat problem is a curve in the configuration space that is *horizontal* with respect to the connection (that is, it is admissible by the physics) with prescribed initial and final configurations. Finding an optimal solution is an example of optimal [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning).[^n11][^n12]

In the language of physics, Montgomery's connection is a certain [Yang–Mills field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_field) on the configuration space, and is a special case of a more general approach to the dynamics of deformable bodies as represented by [gauge fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_field),[^n9][^n10] following the work of Shapere and Wilczek.[^n13][^n14]
