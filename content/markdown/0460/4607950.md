# Magic formula investing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magic formula investing"}

**Magic formula investing** is an investment technique outlined by [Joel Greenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Greenblatt) that uses the principles of [value investing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_investing).

:::

## Development {#Development}

Greenblatt introduced "magic formula investing" in his book, *The Little Book That Beats the Market*, a method for determining which stocks to buy: "cheap and good companies" with a high [earnings yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_yield) and a high [return on invested capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_on_invested_capital). His strategy is featured in *The Guru Investor* by [John P. Reese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Reese).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] He suggests purchasing 30 "good companies": cheap stocks with a high [earnings yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_yield) and a high [return on capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_on_capital). He describes this as a simplified version of the strategy employed by [Warren Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett) and [Charlie Munger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Munger) of [Berkshire Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire_Hathaway).

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

According to Greenblatt's 2005 book *The Little Book That Beats the Market* (John Wiley & Sons), he used the following criteria:

1. Establish a minimum [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) (usually greater than \$50 million).
1. Exclude [utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_utility) and [financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial) stocks.
1. Exclude foreign companies ([American Depositary Receipts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Depositary_Receipt)).
1. Determine company's [earnings yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_yield) = [EBIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_before_interest_and_taxes) / [enterprise value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_value).
1. Determine company's [return on capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_on_capital) = [EBIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_before_interest_and_taxes) / (net [fixed assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_assets) + [working capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_capital)).
1. Rank all companies above chosen [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) by highest earnings yield and highest return on capital (ranked as [percentages](wikipedia://en/64493)).
1. Invest in 20–30 highest ranked companies, accumulating 2–3 positions per month over a 12-month period.
1. Re-balance [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_%28finance%29) once per year, selling losers one week before the year-mark and winners one week after the year mark.
1. Continue over a long-term (5–10+ year) period.

:::

## Critical analysis {#Critical_analysis}

A number of studies have found merit in Greenblatt's "magic investing formula" in various markets around the world. However, the studies have also often noted increased volatility, short-term underperformance and other potential risks.

- A 2009 study of stock markets in the [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) from 1998 to 2008 found Greenblatt's formula led to outperformance of market averages.[^o10] However, the authors advised the formula was best used as a screening tool and should not be applied dogmatically, as the outperformance associated with Greenblatt's formula might be accounted for by data outlined in the [capital asset pricing model](wikipedia://en/163062) and the [Fama–French three-factor model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fama–French_three-factor_model).
- A 2016 study from the stock market in [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) found the magic formula "yields higher risk-adjusted returns on average". The authors also proposed that a modified form of Greenblatt's strategy, additionally emphasizing companies with better than average [free cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_cash_flow), was best suited to [bull markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_markets).[^o11]
- A 2016 study found possible confirmation of Greenblatt's formula in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil)'s stock market, but cautioned "we could not assure with a high level of certainty that the strategy is \[an\] [alpha generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_generation_platform), and that our results were not due to randomness."[^o12]
- A 2017 study from the markets in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) found application of the Greenblatt formula resulted in long-term outperformance of market averages in the periods 2005 to 2015, and 2007 to 2017. The authors also found the "magic formula" was also associated with short-term underperformance in some periods, and significantly increased volatility.[^o13]
- In 2018, a paper presented at a professional conference found validation for the Greenblatt formula in the [Chinese stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_stock_market).[^o14]
- Independent scholar Robert Andrew Martin conducted a [backtest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backtest) analysis of Greenblatt's magic investing formula for the US market, published June 2020.[^o15] His analysis revealed that from 2003 to 2015 application of Greenblatt's formula to U.S. stocks resulted in an annualized average return of 11.4%. This outperformed the S&P 500's annualized return of 8.7%. However, Martin also found that the formula underperformed the S&P 500 slightly during the 2007-2011 period and actually went negative for a time. Additionally, over the entire 2003-2015 period, the strategy exhibited more volatility compared to the S&P 500. In his evaluation, Martin found that the almost 3% outperformance was remarkable, though it fell short of the 30% returns claimed in Greenblatt's book. Different sample periods are used by Greenblatt and Martin. Moreover, Martin highlighted the presence of "significant psychological risk" associated with under-performance after the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).
- A 2022 study of the stock market in [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) found that the magic formula generates risk-adjusted excess returns. Over the sample period (2003-2022) the strategy had a CAGR of 21.56%. However, these returns may not be achievable in real-world conditions due to the impact of transaction costs. The study also found that the magic formula could be improved by using operating cash flows instead of EBIT.[^o16]
- The strategy also outperforms the Indian stock market over the period July 2012 - Feb 2020, according to a 2022 paper. Over this period the average return was 13.9% of 30-stock Magic Formula portfolio versus 9.3% for the BSE Sensex.[^o17]
- An analysis of the [Hong Kong stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_stock_market) from 2001 to 2014 found Greenblatt's formula was associated with long-term outperformance of market averages by 6-15% depending on company size and other variables.
- An empirical study of the French stock market for the period 1999-2019 shows that Joel Greenblatt's formula was able to beating the market by 5%-9% per year using various quality definitions.[^o18]

::: {#o20 .statement cue=named-heading name="Comparison to other Investing formulas"}

## Comparison to other Investing formulas {#Comparison_to_other_Investing_formulas}

A 2024 study evaluates the formula for the U.S. market from 1963 to 2022 and compares it with the performance of the [Piotroski F-Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotroski_F-Score), Acquirer's Multiple, and [Conservative Formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Formula_Investing). The study finds that all four formulas generate significant raw and risk-adjusted returns, primarily by providing efficient exposure to well-established style factors. However, no single formula consistently outperforms across all performance metrics. While the Acquirer's Multiple achieves the highest returns for top decile portfolios and the Conservative Formula leads in CAPM alpha and return spread, the Magic Formula exhibits the highest remaining alpha after adjusting for common factors.[^o21]

:::
