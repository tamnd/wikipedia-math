# Diagonal morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diagonal morphism"}

*Image: Diagonal and co-diagonal.svg.*

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), for every [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $A$ in every [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathcal{C}$ where the [product](wikipedia://en/237868) $A\times A$ exists, there exists the **diagonal morphism**[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

$$\delta_A : A \rightarrow A \times A$$

satisfying

$\pi_k \circ \delta_A = \operatorname{id}_A$ for $k \in \{ 1,2 \},$

where $\pi_k$ is the [canonical projection morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_projection_morphism) to the $k$-th component. The existence of this [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) is a consequence of the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) that [characterizes](wikipedia://en/780886) the product ([up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)). The restriction to binary products here is for ease of notation; diagonal morphisms exist similarly for arbitrary products. The [image](wikipedia://en/342899) of a diagonal morphism in the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), as a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095), is a [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558) on the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263), namely [equality](wikipedia://en/90446).

For [concrete categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_categories), the diagonal morphism can be simply described by its action on elements $x$ of the object $A$. Namely, $\delta_A(x) = \langle x,x \rangle$, the [ordered pair](wikipedia://en/22362) formed from $x$. The reason for the name is that the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of such a diagonal morphism is diagonal (whenever it makes sense), for example the image of the diagonal morphism $\mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) is given by the line that is the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of the equation $y=x$. The diagonal morphism into the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) $X^\infty$ may provide an [injection](wikipedia://en/45196) into the [space of sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_of_sequences) valued in $X$; each element maps to the constant [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) at that element. However, most notions of sequence spaces have [convergence](wikipedia://en/1743431) restrictions that the image of the diagonal map will fail to satisfy.

The dual notion of a diagonal morphism is a **codiagonal morphism**. For every object $B$ in a category $\mathcal{C}$ where the [coproducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coproducts) $B \sqcup B$ exists, the codiagonal[^o8] is the canonical morphism

$$\delta_B \colon B \sqcup B \stackrel{[Id,Id]} \to B$$

satisfying

$\delta_B \circ \tau_l = \operatorname{id}_B$ for $l \in \{ 1,2 \}.$

where $\tau_l$ is the injection morphism to the $l$-th component.
