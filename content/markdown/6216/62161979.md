# Jonathan Rosenhead

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jonathan Rosenhead"}

**Jonathan Vivian Rosenhead** (born 21 September 1938)[^o4] is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [operational researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research) and [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28UK%29) activist.[^o2]

:::

## Early life and career {#Early_life_and_career}

Jonathan Rosenhead is the son of mathematician [Louis Rosenhead](wikipedia://en/39261792).[^o4] He studied at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), where he received a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in mathematics in 1959.[^o2] He continued his studies at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) where he received an [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc.) degree in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in 1961, and an [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) from Cambridge in 1963. He worked as an operational researcher at [United Steel Companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Steel_Companies) in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield) in 1961-63 and at Science in General Management Ltd. (SIGMA) in [Croydon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croydon) in 1963–66 before returning to the academic world.

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Rosenhead spent 1966–67 at the [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania) and its [Management Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_Science) Centre, where [Russell L. Ackoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_L._Ackoff) was professor. In 1967, he joined the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) as a lecturer in operational research. He became a senior lecturer in 1981 and professor of operational research in 1987.[^o2] He retired in 2003.

Within operational research (OR), Rosenhead is primarily associated with the development of "soft OR" from the late 1970s, which resulted in the development of a number of new OR methods. He was the editor of the first book to gather a number of [problem structuring methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_structuring_methods) within one volume, *Rational analysis for a problematic world*, published in 1989.[^o6]

He was president of the [Operational Research Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Research_Society) in 1986–87.

## Political activism {#Political_activism}

Rosenhead was a [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_%28UK%29) candidate for [Kensington South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_South_%28UK_Parliament_constituency%29) at the [1966 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_United_Kingdom_general_election).[^o2][^o8] His candidacy was endorsed by notable local residents, including playwright [Keith Waterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Waterhouse), designer [Misha Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misha_Black), writer [Baroness Stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Stocks,_Baroness_Stocks), architect [Sir Hugh Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Casson), writer [Brigid Brophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigid_Brophy) and novelist [Lord Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Snow).[^o8] However, Rosenhead came a distant second to the [Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_%28UK%29), who held the seat with a large majority.[^o9]

He was active in the [British Society for Social Responsibility in Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Society_for_Social_Responsibility_in_Science) over a 20-year period, including a stint as chair of the society. His political activities has included being chairman of the [British Committee for the Universities of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Committee_for_the_Universities_of_Palestine) (BRICUP) and being information officer for [Jewish Voice for Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Voice_for_Labour).

Rosenhead was involved in the campaign against South African [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid). He was arrested at a May 1972 demonstration against the English rugby team which was departing for a tour in South Africa. He was convicted of a public order offence and fined. In 2020, Rosenhead learned that a fellow protester, who was also arrested at the demonstration, was an undercover police officer working in the [Special Demonstration Squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Demonstration_Squad).[^o10] The fact that the police had told neither the defence nor the court about the officer made the convictions a [miscarriage of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage_of_justice). Rosenhead and several others had their convictions quashed in 2023.[^o11]

In May 2020, Rosenhead was one of ten Labour Party members identified in dossiers which the shadow Secretary of State for Communities and Local Government [Steve Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Reed_%28politician%29) submitted to the party's Governance and Legal Unit, calling for them to be investigated for engaging in antisemitic conduct. The subsequent investigation found that he had no case to answer.[^o12]
