# CM-field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CM-field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **CM-field** is a particular type of [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), so named for a close connection to the theory of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183). Another name used is **J-field**.

:::

The abbreviation "CM" was introduced by [Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724) and [Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843).[^n1]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A number field $K$ is a CM-field if it is a [quadratic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) $K/F$ where the base field $F$ is [totally real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) but $K$ is [totally imaginary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_imaginary_field); i.e., every embedding of $F$ into $\mathbb{C}$ lies entirely within $\mathbb{R}$, but there is no embedding of $K$ into $\mathbb{R}$.

In other words, there is a subfield $F$ of $K$ such that $K$ is generated over $F$ by a single square root of an element, say $\beta = \sqrt{\alpha}$, in such a way that the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of $\beta$ over the [rational number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number_field) $\mathbb{Q}$ has all its roots non-real [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). For this α should be chosen *totally negative*, so that for each embedding σ of $F$ into the real number field, $\sigma(\alpha) < 0$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

One feature of a CM-field is that [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation) on $\mathbb{C}$ induces an automorphism on the field which is independent of its embedding into $\mathbb C$. In the notation given, it must negate $\beta$.

A number field $K$ is a CM-field if and only if it has a "units defect", i.e. if it contains a proper subfield $F$ whose unit group has the same $\mathbb Z$-rank as that of $K$.[^n2] In fact, $F$ is the totally real subfield of $K$ mentioned above. This follows from [Dirichlet's unit theorem](wikipedia://en/460227).

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The simplest, and motivating, example of a CM-field is an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field), for which the totally real subfield is just the field of rationals.
- One of the most important examples of a CM-field is the [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, which is generated by a primitive $n$th [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). It is a totally imaginary [quadratic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of the [totally real field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n +\zeta_n^{-1})$. The latter is the fixed field of [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation), and $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ is obtained from it by adjoining a square root of $(\zeta_n+\zeta_n^{-1})^2-4 = (\zeta_n - \zeta_n^{-1})^2.$
- The union $\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{CM}$ of all CM fields is similar to a CM field except that it has infinite degree. It is a quadratic extension of the union of all totally real fields $\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{R}$. The [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{R})$ is generated (as a closed subgroup) by all elements of order 2 in $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{CM})$ is a subgroup of index 2. The Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{CM}/\mathbb{Q})$ has a [center](wikipedia://en/7125) generated by an element of order 2 (complex conjugation) and the quotient by its center is the group $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^\mathrm{R}/\mathbb{Q})$.
- If $V$ is a complex [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) of dimension $n$, then any abelian algebra $F$ of endomorphisms of $V$ has rank at most $2n$ over $\mathbb{Z}$. If it has rank $2n$ and $V$ is simple then $F$ is an order in a CM-field. Conversely any CM field arises like this from some simple complex abelian variety, unique up to [isogeny](wikipedia://en/1085176).
- One example of a totally imaginary field which is not CM is the number field defined by the polynomial $x^4 + x^3 - x^2 - x + 1$.

:::
