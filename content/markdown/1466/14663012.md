# Brocard circle

*Image: Brocard Circle.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brocard circle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Brocard circle** (or **seven-point circle**) is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) derived from a given triangle. It passes through the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) of the triangle, and is centered at the midpoint of the line segment joining them (so that this segment is a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007)).

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

In terms of the side lengths $a$, $b$, and $c$ of the given triangle, and the [areal coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areal_coordinates) $(x,y,z)$ for points inside the triangle (where the $x$-coordinate of a point is the area of the triangle made by that point with the side of length $a$, etc), the Brocard circle consists of the points satisfying the equation[^o3]

$$b^2 c^2 x^2 + a^2 c^2 y^2 + a^2 b^2 z^2 - a^4 y z - b^4 x z - c^4 x y=0.$$

## Related points {#Related_points}

The two [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586) lie on this circle, as do the vertices of the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335).[^o5] These five points, together with the other two points on the circle (the circumcenter and symmedian), justify the name "seven-point circle".

The Brocard circle is concentric with the [first Lemoine circle](wikipedia://en/15956333#Lemoine_point_and_circle).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

If the triangle is [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285), the circumcenter and symmedian coincide and therefore the Brocard circle reduces to a single point.[^o9]

:::

## History {#History}

The Brocard circle is named for [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496),[^o11] who presented a paper on it to the French Association for the Advancement of Science in [Algiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers) in 1881.
