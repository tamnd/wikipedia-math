# Neil Chriss

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neil Chriss"}

**Neil A. Chriss** is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff), [hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) manager,[^o2] [philanthropist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropist) and a founding board member of the charity organization "[Math for America](wikipedia://en/2182307)" which seeks to improve math education in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o3] Chriss also serves on the board of trustees of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o4]

:::

## Early career {#Early_career}

Chriss learned programming at the age of 11. He developed a videogame called D' Fuse and sold it to Tymac when he was a sophomore in high school. The game quickly faded when the Commodore 64 with 64K of memory and much better graphics appeared.[^o6]

Chriss went to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where he majored in mathematics. Following his junior year in college, he worked at [Fermilab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermilab) with Myron Campbell and Bruce Denby; he developed a neural network to find [b-quark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottom_quark) jets.[^o7] He then earned his master's degree in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech).[^o8]

Chriss studied [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), working in the [Langlands Program](wikipedia://en/171905). He received a Ph.D. in 1993, with the thesis *A Geometric Construction of the Iwahori-Hecke Algebra*.[^o9] With [Victor Ginzburg](wikipedia://en/14995490), he wrote a book on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).[^o10]

## Academia {#Academia}

Chriss's first academic job (1993–1994) was at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), where he wrote "Representation Theory and Complex Geometry" with Ginzburg. At Toronto, [John M. Liew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Liew) introduced Chriss to "[quant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analyst)" finance, [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [stochastic calculus](wikipedia://en/228001) and [Black–Scholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black–Scholes) option pricing theory.

At the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1994–1995, Chriss began the book "Black–Scholes and Beyond: Option Pricing Models" (Irwin, 1996). In 1995, he was hired for the summer in the Quantitative Strategies group of [Emanuel Derman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_Derman) at [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs). In 1994, Derman and Kani published a paper[^o12] that showed how to fit a [binomial tree](wikipedia://en/250074) to price all options trading in the market at that time. Chriss helped extend their work from binomial to [trinomial trees](wikipedia://en/20262149).[^o13]

Chriss received a grant from NSF and went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) Mathematics Department in 1996. Despite the offer of an assistant professorship at Harvard in 1997, he moved to Wall Street.

## Wall Street {#Wall_Street}

Risk Magazine named Chriss one of the "Top Ten to Watch in the next Ten Years" in 1997.[^o15]

In 1997, Chriss joined the quant research group in [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) to work on portfolio trading for their cash equities operational dealing desk. He wrote a paper "Optimal execution of portfolio transactions" with [Robert Almgren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Almgren).[^o16] The Institutional Investor[^o17] published an article about Algorithmic Trading in its November 2004 issue, titled "The Orders Battle", which noted that Chriss's paper "helped lay the groundwork for arrival-price algorithms being developed on Wall Street." The work has been widely cited since.[^o18][^o19] Chriss also wrote articles on the following topics: "Competitive bids for principal program trades",[^o20] "Value under liquidation".[^o21] At Morgan Stanley, Peter Muller inspired Chriss to pursue quantitative trading.

In 1998, Chriss moved into [portfolio management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_management), joining the Goldman Sachs Asset Management (GSAM) Quantitative Strategies group to develop a new trading strategy, after Cliff Asness, [John M. Liew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Liew) and Bob Krail left to form [AQR Capital Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AQR_Capital_Management).

In 2000, Chriss left Goldman Sachs to found ICor Brokerage Inc., a derivatives trading firm.[^o8] In 2001, ICor joined forces with [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters), forming a joint venture, ICor Brokerage Ltd.[^o22] Reuters bought out ICor in 2004.[^o23]

## Mathematical finance education {#Mathematical_finance_education}

Chriss was asked by [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) to be the first (part-time) director of the Program in Mathematics in Finance.[^o25] At Courant from 1997 to 2003, Chriss recruited [Jim Gatheral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gatheral), Steve Allen, Peter Fraenkel (now head of Quantitative IT at [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS)) and [Nassim Taleb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassim_Taleb).

In 2003 Chriss became executive director of the University of Chicago Financial Mathematics Program.

## Hedge funds {#Hedge_funds}

In 2003, Chriss joined the Stamford, Connecticut hedge fund [SAC Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAC_Capital), working there until early 2007.

Chriss then founded the hedge fund "Hutchin Hill Capital". [Renaissance Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Technologies)' Meritage Fund provided \$300 million of capital to Hutchin Hill.

## Recent research {#Recent_research}

With R. Almgren, Chriss wrote a paper on optimizing a portfolio.[^o28] They submitted a [patent application](http://www.freshpatents.com/Method-and-system-for-portfolio-optimization-from-ordering-information-dt20060824ptan20060190371.php) on the method.
