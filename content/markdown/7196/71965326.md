# Oper (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oper"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **oper** is a [principal connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_connection), or in more elementary terms a type of [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886). They were first defined and used by [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) and Vladimir Sokolov[^o2] to study how the [KdV equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KdV_equation) and related integrable PDEs correspond to algebraic structures known as [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292). Their modern formulation is due to Drinfeld and [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613).[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

Opers were first defined, although not named, in a 1981 Russian paper by Drinfeld and Sokolov on *Equations of Korteweg–de Vries type, and simple Lie algebras*. They were later generalized by Drinfeld and Beilinson in 1993, later published as an e-print in 2005.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

### Abstract {#Abstract}

Let $G$ be a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) over the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) $\mathbb{C}$, with a distinguished [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) $B = B_G \subset G$. Set $N = [B,B]$, so that $H = B/N$ is the [Cartan group](wikipedia://en/360136).

Denote by $\mathfrak{n} < \mathfrak{b} < \mathfrak{g}$ and $\mathfrak{h} = \mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{n}$ the corresponding [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). There is an open $B$-orbit $\mathbf{O}$ consisting of vectors stabilized by the radical $N\subset B$ such that all of their negative simple-root components are non-zero.

Let $X$ be a smooth curve.

A **G-oper** on $X$ is a triple $(\mathfrak{F}, \nabla, \mathfrak{F}_B)$ where $\mathfrak{F}$ is a principal $G$-bundle, $\nabla$ is a connection on $\mathfrak{F}$ and $\mathfrak{F}_B$ is a $B$-[reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_of_the_structure_group) of $\mathfrak{F}$, such that the [one-form](wikipedia://en/314692) $\nabla/\mathfrak{F}_B$ takes values in $\mathbf{O}_{\mathfrak{F}_B}$.

:::

## Example {#Example}

Fix $X = \mathbb{P}^1 = \mathbb{CP}^1$ the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799). Working at the level of the algebras, fix $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}(2, \mathbb{C})$, which can be identified with the space of traceless $2\times 2$ complex matrices. Since $\mathbb{P}^1$ has only one (complex) dimension, a one-form has only one component, and so an $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$-valued one form is locally described by a matrix of functions

$$A(z) = \begin{pmatrix}a(z) & b(z) \\ c(z) & -a(z)\end{pmatrix}$$

where $a, b, c$ are allowed to be [meromorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic) functions.

Denote by $\text{Conn}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{P}^1)$ the space of $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ valued meromorphic functions together with an action by $g(z)$, meromorphic functions valued in the associated [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G = SL(2, \mathbb{C})$. The action is by a formal [gauge transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation):

$$g(z) * A(z) = g(z)A(z)g(z)^{-1} - g'(z) g(z)^{-1}.$$

Then opers are defined in terms of a subspace of these connections. Denote by $\text{op}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{P}^1)$ the space of connections with $c(z) \equiv 1$. Denote by $N$ the subgroup of meromorphic functions valued in $SL(2, \mathbb{C})$ of the form $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & f(z) \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with $f(z)$ meromorphic.

Then for $g(z) \in N, A(z) \in \text{op}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{P}^1),$ it holds that $g(z) * A(z) \in \text{op}_{\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})}(\mathbb{P}^1)$. It therefore defines an action. The orbits of this action concretely characterize opers. However, generally this description only holds locally and not necessarily globally.

:::

## Gaudin model {#Gaudin_model}

Opers on $\mathbb{P}^1$ have been used by [Boris Feigin](wikipedia://en/53823921), [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel) and [Nicolai Reshetikhin](wikipedia://en/7487626) to characterize the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206137) of the [Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317).[^o11]

Specifically, for a $\mathfrak{g}$-Gaudin model, and defining $^L\mathfrak{g}$ as the [Langlands dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) algebra, there is a bijection between the spectrum of the Gaudin algebra generated by operators defined in the Gaudin model and an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) of $^L\mathfrak{g}$ opers.
