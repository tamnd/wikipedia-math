# Patrick Billingsley

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick Billingsley"}

**Patrick Paul Billingsley** (May 3, 1925 – April 22, 2011[^o3][^o4]) was an American mathematician noted for his books in advanced [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Billingsley was born and raised in [Sioux Falls, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_Falls,_South_Dakota), and graduated from the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) in 1946. After earning a Ph.D. in mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1955, he was attached to the [NSA](wikipedia://en/21939) until his discharge from the Navy in 1957. In 1958 he became a professor of mathematics and statistics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where he served as chair of the Department of Statistics from 1980 to 1983, and retired in 1994. In 1964–65 he was a [Fulbright Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) and visiting professor at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen). In 1971–72 he was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) and visiting professor at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) ([Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge)). From 1976 to 1979 he edited the [Annals of Probability](wikipedia://en/34673748). In 1983 he was [president](wikipedia://en/15315059) of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). He was given the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) for his article "[Prime Numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and [Brownian Motion](wikipedia://en/4436)."[^o6] He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1986.[^o7]

In *[Young Men and Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Men_and_Fire)*, fellow University of Chicago professor [Norman Maclean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Maclean) wrote about Billingsley that "he is a distinguished statistician and one of the best amateur actors I have ever seen".[^o8]

## Death {#Death}

He died in 2011, aged 85, in his [Hyde Park, Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Chicago) home. He was survived by his children, [Franny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franny_Billingsley), Patty, Julie, Marty and Paul, and his companion, Florence Weisblatt. His wife of nearly 50 years, social activist Ruth Billingsley, died in 2000.
