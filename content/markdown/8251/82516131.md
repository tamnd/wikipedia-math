# Spectral gap conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectral gap conjecture"}

In [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), a branch of mathematics, the **spectral gap conjecture** of [Alexander Lubotzky](wikipedia://en/22754130), [Ralph S. Phillips](wikipedia://en/27858707), and [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak) is a statement on the spectral gaps of certain actions of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on the sphere $S^{2}$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Any matrix $U \in SU(2)$ defines an isometry of the sphere $S^{2}$, which in turn defines an operator $\phi_{U}$ on the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $L^{2}(SU(2))$. The spectral gap conjecture states that for any integer $n > 2$, if $n$ isometries $U_{1}, \dots, U_{n}$ are chosen uniformly at random, then the operator $\phi_{U_{1}} + \phi_{U_{1}}^{-1} + \cdots + \phi_{U_{n}} + \phi_{U_{n}}^{-1}$ has a nontrivial [spectral gap](wikipedia://en/7999250) with probability 1.[^o5]

:::

## Progress {#Progress}

In 2007, [Jean Bourgain](wikipedia://en/1024131) and Alex Gamburd proved that when the matrices $U_{i}$ have entries which are all [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) up to simultaneous conjugation, the resulting operator has a spectral gap.[^o7] This result was later generalized to the case of $SU(d)$.[^o8] It is known that either there is a nontrivial spectral gap with probability 1 or that the spectral gap is trivial with probability 1.[^o9] If true, the statement would have applications to [quantum computing](wikipedia://en/25220) and the design of universal quantum gate sets.[^o10][^o11]
