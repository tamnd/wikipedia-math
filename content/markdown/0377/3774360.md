# Bracket polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bracket polynomial"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **bracket polynomial** (also known as the **Kauffman bracket**) is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) invariant of [framed links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_link). Although it is not an invariant of knots or links (as it is not invariant under type I [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374)), a suitably "normalized" version yields the famous [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) called the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642). The bracket polynomial plays an important role in unifying the Jones polynomial with other [quantum invariants](wikipedia://en/22624170). In particular, Kauffman's interpretation of the Jones polynomial allows generalization to invariants of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

:::

The bracket polynomial was discovered by [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman) in 1987.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The bracket polynomial of any (unoriented) link diagram $L$, denoted $\langle L \rangle$, is a polynomial in the variable $A$, characterized by the three rules:

- $\langle \bigcirc \rangle = 1$, where $\bigcirc$ is the standard diagram of the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075)
- *Image: kauffman\_bracket2.png.*
- $$\langle \bigcirc \sqcup L \rangle = (-A^2 - A^{-2}) \langle L \rangle$$

The pictures in the second rule represent brackets of the link diagrams which differ inside a disc as shown but are identical outside. The third rule means that adding a circle disjoint from the rest of the diagram multiplies the bracket of the remaining diagram by $-A^2 - A^{-2}$.
