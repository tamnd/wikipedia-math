# Salva veritate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Salva veritate"}

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy), **salva veritate** (or **intersubstitutivity**) is the logical condition by which two expressions may be interchanged without altering the [truth-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth-value) of statements in which the expressions occur. Substitution *salva veritate* of co-extensional terms can fail in [opaque contexts](wikipedia://en/4532008).[^o2]

:::

The literal translation of the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) "***salva veritate***" is "with (or by) unharmed truth", using [ablative of manner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablative): "*salva*" meaning "rescue," "salvation," or "welfare," and "*veritate*" meaning "reality" or "truth".

## Leibniz {#Leibniz}

The phrase occurs in two fragments from [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz)'s *General Science. Characteristics*:

- In Chapter 19, Definition 1, Leibniz writes: "Two terms are the same (*eadem*) if one can be substituted for the other *without altering the truth of any statement* (*salva veritate*)."
- In Chapter 20, Definition 1, Leibniz writes: "Terms which can be substituted for one another wherever we please *without altering the truth of any statement* (*salva veritate*), are the same (*eadem*) or coincident (*coincidentia*). For example, '[triangle](wikipedia://en/30654)' and 'trilateral', for in every proposition demonstrated by [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) concerning 'triangle', 'trilateral' can be substituted *without loss of [truth](wikipedia://en/29920)* (*salva veritate*)."

## Quine {#Quine}

[W.V.O. Quine](wikipedia://en/33833) takes substitutivity *salva veritate* to be the same as the "indiscernibility of identicals". Given a true statement, one of its two terms may be substituted for the other in any true statement and the result will be true.[^o5] He continues to show that depending on context, the statement may change in value. In fact, the whole [quantified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantification_%28logic%29) [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) of necessity is dependent on context and empty otherwise; for it collapses if [essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence) is withdrawn.[^o6]

For example, the statements:

| (1) | Giorgione = Barbarelli, |
|---|---|
| (2) | Giorgione was so-called because of his size |

are true; however, replacement of the name 'Giorgione' by the name 'Barbarelli' turns (2) into the falsehood:

|  | Barbarelli was so-called because of his size.[^o7] |
|---|---|

Quine's example here refers to [Giorgio Barbarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Barbarelli)'s [sobriquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriquet) "Giorgione", an Italian name roughly glossed as "Big George."
