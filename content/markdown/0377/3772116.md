# Vector-valued differential form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector-valued differential form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **vector-valued differential form** on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* is a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) on *M* with values in a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V*. More generally, it is a differential form with values in some [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) *E* over *M*. Ordinary differential forms can be viewed as **R**-valued differential forms.

:::

An important case of vector-valued differential forms are [Lie algebra-valued forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra-valued_forms) (a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) is an example of such a form.)

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and *E* → *M* be a smooth [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over *M*. We denote the space of [smooth sections](wikipedia://en/367621) of a bundle *E* by Γ(*E*). An ***E*-valued differential form** of degree *p* is a smooth section of the [tensor product bundle](wikipedia://en/46418707) of *E* with Λ<sup>*p*</sup>(*T*<sup>∗</sup>*M*), the *p*-th [exterior power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) of the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) of *M*. The space of such forms is denoted by

$$\Omega^p(M,E) = \Gamma(E\otimes\Lambda^pT^*M).$$

Because Γ is a [strong monoidal functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_monoidal_functor),[^o4] this can also be interpreted as

$$\Gamma(E\otimes\Lambda^pT^*M) = \Gamma(E) \otimes_{\Omega^0(M)} \Gamma(\Lambda^pT^*M) = \Gamma(E) \otimes_{\Omega^0(M)} \Omega^p(M),$$

where the latter two tensor products are the [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) over the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) Ω<sup>0</sup>(*M*) of smooth **R**-valued functions on *M* (see the seventh example [here](wikipedia://en/276410#Examples)). By convention, an *E*-valued 0-form is just a section of the bundle *E*. That is,

$$\Omega^0(M,E) = \Gamma(E).\,$$

Equivalently, an *E*-valued differential form can be defined as a [bundle morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_bundle_morphism)

$$TM\otimes\cdots\otimes TM \to E$$

which is totally [skew-symmetric](wikipedia://en/174055).

Let *V* be a fixed [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). A ***V*-valued differential form** of degree *p* is a differential form of degree *p* with values in the [trivial bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle) *M* × *V*. The space of such forms is denoted Ω<sup>*p*</sup>(*M*, *V*). When *V* = **R** one recovers the definition of an ordinary differential form. If *V* is finite-dimensional, then one can show that the natural homomorphism

$$\Omega^p(M) \otimes_\mathbb{R} V \to \Omega^p(M,V),$$

where the first tensor product is of vector spaces over **R**, is an isomorphism.[^o5]

:::

## Operations on vector-valued forms {#Operations_on_vector-valued_forms}

### Pullback {#Pullback}

One can define the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of vector-valued forms by [smooth maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) just as for ordinary forms. The pullback of an *E*-valued form on *N* by a smooth map φ : *M* → *N* is an (φ\**E*)-valued form on *M*, where φ\**E* is the [pullback bundle](wikipedia://en/1665392) of *E* by φ.

The formula is given just as in the ordinary case. For any *E*-valued *p*-form ω on *N* the pullback φ\*ω is given by

$$(\varphi^*\omega)_x(v_1,\cdots, v_p) = \omega_{\varphi(x)}(\mathrm d\varphi_x(v_1),\cdots,\mathrm d\varphi_x(v_p)).$$

### Wedge product {#Wedge_product}

Just as for ordinary differential forms, one can define a [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) of vector-valued forms. The wedge product of an *E*<sub>1</sub>-valued *p*-form with an *E*<sub>2</sub>-valued *q*-form is naturally an (*E*<sub>1</sub>⊗*E*<sub>2</sub>)-valued (*p*+*q*)-form:

$$\wedge : \Omega^p(M,E_1) \times \Omega^q(M,E_2) \to \Omega^{p+q}(M,E_1\otimes E_2).$$

The definition is just as for ordinary forms with the exception that real multiplication is replaced with the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844):

$$(\omega\wedge\eta)(v_1,\cdots,v_{p+q}) = \frac{1}{p! q!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{p+q}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\omega(v_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(p)})\otimes \eta(v_{\sigma(p+1)},\cdots,v_{\sigma(p+q)}).$$

In particular, the wedge product of an ordinary (**R**-valued) *p*-form with an *E*-valued *q*-form is naturally an *E*-valued (*p*+*q*)-form (since the tensor product of *E* with the trivial bundle *M* × **R** is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturally_isomorphic) to *E*). In terms of local frames {*e*<sub>*α*</sub>} and {*l*<sub>*β*</sub>} for *E*<sub>1</sub> and *E*<sub>2</sub> respectively, the wedge product of an *E*<sub>1</sub>-valued *p*-form *ω* = *ω*<sup>*α*</sup> *e*<sub>*α*</sub>, and an *E*<sub>2</sub>-valued *q*-form *η* = *η*<sup>*β*</sup> *l*<sub>*β*</sub> is

$$\omega \wedge \eta = \sum_{\alpha, \beta} (\omega^\alpha \wedge \eta^\beta) (e_\alpha \otimes l_\beta),$$

where *ω*<sup>*α*</sup> ∧ *η*<sup>*β*</sup> is the ordinary wedge product of $\mathbb{R}$-valued forms. For ω ∈ Ω<sup>*p*</sup>(*M*) and η ∈ Ω<sup>*q*</sup>(*M*, *E*) one has the usual commutativity relation:

$$\omega\wedge\eta = (-1)^{pq}\eta\wedge\omega.$$

In general, the wedge product of two *E*-valued forms is *not* another *E*-valued form, but rather an (*E*⊗*E*)-valued form. However, if *E* is an [algebra bundle](wikipedia://en/1244529) (i.e. a bundle of [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) rather than just vector spaces) one can compose with multiplication in *E* to obtain an *E*-valued form. If *E* is a bundle of [commutative](wikipedia://en/245990), [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) then, with this modified wedge product, the set of all *E*-valued differential forms

$$\Omega(M,E) = \bigoplus_{p=0}^{\dim M}\Omega^p(M,E)$$

becomes a [graded-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded-commutative) associative algebra. If the fibers of *E* are not commutative then Ω(*M*,*E*) will not be graded-commutative.

### Exterior derivative {#Exterior_derivative}

For any vector space *V* there is a natural [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) on the space of *V*-valued forms. This is just the ordinary exterior derivative acting component-wise relative to any [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of *V*. Explicitly, if {*e*<sub>α</sub>} is a basis for *V* then the differential of a *V*-valued *p*-form ω = ω<sup>α</sup>*e*<sub>α</sub> is given by

$$d\omega = (d\omega^\alpha)e_\alpha.\,$$

The exterior derivative on *V*-valued forms is completely characterized by the usual relations:

$$\begin{aligned} &d(\omega+\eta) = d\omega + d\eta\\ &d(\omega\wedge\eta) = d\omega\wedge\eta + (-1)^p\,\omega\wedge d\eta\qquad(p=\deg\omega)\\ &d(d\omega) = 0. \end{aligned}$$

More generally, the above remarks apply to *E*-valued forms where *E* is any [flat vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_vector_bundle) over *M* (i.e. a vector bundle whose transition functions are constant). The exterior derivative is defined as above on any [local trivialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) of *E*.

If *E* is not flat then there is no natural notion of an exterior derivative acting on *E*-valued forms. What is needed is a choice of [connection](wikipedia://en/391816) on *E*. A connection on *E* is a linear [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) taking sections of *E* to *E*-valued one forms:

$$\nabla : \Omega^0(M,E) \to \Omega^1(M,E).$$

If *E* is equipped with a connection ∇ then there is a unique [covariant exterior derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_exterior_derivative)

$$d_\nabla: \Omega^p(M,E) \to \Omega^{p+1}(M,E)$$

extending ∇. The covariant exterior derivative is characterized by [linearity](wikipedia://en/91591) and the equation

$$d_\nabla(\omega\wedge\eta) = d_\nabla\omega\wedge\eta + (-1)^p\,\omega\wedge d_\nabla\eta$$

where ω is a *E*-valued *p*-form and η is an ordinary *q*-form. In general, one need not have *d*<sub>∇</sub><sup>2</sup> = 0. In fact, this happens [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the connection ∇ is flat (i.e. has vanishing [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440)).

## Basic or tensorial forms on principal bundles {#Basic_or_tensorial_forms_on_principal_bundles}

Let *E* → *M* be a smooth vector bundle of rank *k* over *M* and let *π* : F(*E*) → *M* be the ([associated](wikipedia://en/398874)) [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of *E*, which is a [principal](wikipedia://en/310950) GL<sub>*k*</sub>(**R**) bundle over *M*. The [pullback](wikipedia://en/1665392) of *E* by *π* is canonically isomorphic to F(*E*) ×<sub>ρ</sub> **R**<sup>*k*</sup> via the inverse of \[*u*, *v*\] →*u*(*v*), where ρ is the standard representation. Therefore, the pullback by *π* of an *E*-valued form on *M* determines an **R**<sup>*k*</sup>-valued form on F(*E*). It is not hard to check that this pulled back form is [right-equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) with respect to the natural [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of GL<sub>*k*</sub>(**R**) on F(*E*) × **R**<sup>*k*</sup> and vanishes on [vertical vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_bundle) (tangent vectors to F(*E*) which lie in the kernel of d*π*). Such vector-valued forms on F(*E*) are important enough to warrant special terminology: they are called *basic* or *tensorial forms* on F(*E*).

Let *π* : *P* → *M* be a (smooth) [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) and let *V* be a fixed vector space together with a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) *ρ* : *G* → GL(*V*). A **basic** or **tensorial form** on *P* of type ρ is a *V*-valued form ω on *P* that is **equivariant** and **horizontal** in the sense that

1. $(R_g)^*\omega = \rho(g^{-1})\omega\,$ for all *g* ∈ *G*, and
1. $\omega(v_1, \ldots, v_p) = 0$ whenever at least one of the *v*<sub>*i*</sub> are vertical (i.e., d*π*(*v*<sub>*i*</sub>) = 0).

Here *R*<sub>*g*</sub> denotes the right action of *G* on *P* for some *g* ∈ *G*. Note that for 0-forms the second condition is [vacuously true](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuously_true).

Example: If ρ is the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of *G* on the Lie algebra, then the connection form ω satisfies the first condition (but not the second). The associated [curvature form](wikipedia://en/489440) Ω satisfies both; hence Ω is a tensorial form of adjoint type. The "difference" of two connection forms is a tensorial form.

Given *P* and *ρ* as above one can construct the [associated vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_vector_bundle) *E* = *P* ×<sub>*ρ*</sub> *V*. Tensorial *q*-forms on *P* are in a natural one-to-one correspondence with *E*-valued *q*-forms on *M*. As in the case of the principal bundle F(*E*) above, given a *q*-form $\overline{\phi}$ on *M* with values in *E*, define φ on *P* fiberwise by, say at *u*,

$$\phi = u^{-1}\pi^*\overline{\phi}$$

where *u* is viewed as a linear isomorphism $V \overset{\simeq}\to E_{\pi(u)} = (\pi^*E)_u, v \mapsto [u, v]$. φ is then a tensorial form of type ρ. Conversely, given a tensorial form φ of type ρ, the same formula defines an *E*-valued form $\overline{\phi}$ on *M* (cf. the [Chern–Weil homomorphism](wikipedia://en/970447).) In particular, there is a natural isomorphism of vector spaces

$$\Gamma(M, E) \simeq \{ f: P \to V | f(ug) = \rho(g)^{-1}f(u) \}, \, \overline{f} \leftrightarrow f.$$

Example: Let *E* be the tangent bundle of *M*. Then identity bundle map id<sub>*E*</sub>: *E* →*E* is an *E*-valued one form on *M*. The [tautological one-form](wikipedia://en/1855974) is a unique one-form on the frame bundle of *E* that corresponds to id<sub>*E*</sub>. Denoted by θ, it is a tensorial form of standard type.

Now, suppose there is a connection on *P* so that there is an [exterior covariant differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_covariant_differentiation) *D* on (various) vector-valued forms on *P*. Through the above correspondence, *D* also acts on *E*-valued forms: define ∇ by

$$\nabla \overline{\phi} = \overline{D \phi}.$$

In particular for zero-forms,

$$\nabla: \Gamma(M, E) \to \Gamma(M, T^*M \otimes E).$$

This is exactly the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) for the [connection on the vector bundle *E*](wikipedia://en/391816).[^o11]
