# Eisenstein ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eisenstein ideal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Eisenstein ideal** is an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) in the [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of a [modular curve](wikipedia://en/669552), consisting roughly of elements of the [Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907189) of [Hecke operators](wikipedia://en/521837) that annihilate the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458). It was introduced by (txt), in studying the rational points of modular curves. An **Eisenstein prime** is a prime in the support of the Eisenstein ideal (this has nothing to do with primes in the Eisenstein integers).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *N* be a rational prime, and define

*J*<sub>0</sub>(*N*) = *J*

as the Jacobian variety of the modular curve

*X*<sub>0</sub>(*N*) = *X*.

There are endomorphisms *T*<sub>*l*</sub> of *J* for each prime number *l* not dividing *N*. These come from the Hecke operator, considered first as an [algebraic correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_correspondence) on *X*, and from there as acting on [divisor classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class), which gives the action on *J*. There is also a [Fricke involution](wikipedia://en/37607968) *w* (and [Atkin–Lehner involutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atkin–Lehner_involution) if *N* is composite). The Eisenstein ideal, in the (unital) subring of End(*J*) generated as a ring by the *T*<sub>*l*</sub>, is generated as an ideal by the elements

*T*<sub>*l*</sub> − *l* - 1

for all *l* not dividing *N*, and by

*w* + 1.

:::

## Geometric definition {#Geometric_definition}

Suppose that *T*\* is the ring generated by the Hecke operators acting on all modular forms for Γ<sub>0</sub>(*N*) (not just the cusp forms). The ring *T* of Hecke operators on the cusp forms is a quotient of *T*\*, so Spec(*T*) can be viewed as a subscheme of Spec(*T*\*). Similarly Spec(*T*\*) contains a line (called the Eisenstein line) isomorphic to Spec(**Z**) coming from the action of Hecke operators on the Eisenstein series. The Eisenstein ideal is the ideal defining the intersection of the Eisenstein line with Spec(*T*) in Spec(*T*\*).

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

- The Eisenstein ideal can also be defined for higher weight modular forms. Suppose that *T* is the full Hecke algebra generated by Hecke operators *T*<sub>*n*</sub> acting on the 2-dimensional space of modular forms of level 1 and weight 12.This space is 2 dimensional, spanned by the Eigenforms given by the [Eisenstein series](wikipedia://en/1270458) *E*<sub>12</sub> and the [modular discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_discriminant) Δ. The map taking a Hecke operator *T*<sub>*n*</sub> to its eigenvalues (σ<sub>11</sub>(*n*),τ(n)) gives a homomorphism from *T* into the ring **Z**×**Z** (where τ is the [Ramanujan tau function](wikipedia://en/1231708) and σ<sub>11</sub>(*n*) is the sum of the 11th powers of the divisors of *n*). The image is the set of pairs (*c*,*d*) with *c* and *d* congruent mod 691 because of Ramanujan's congruence σ<sub>11</sub>(*n*) ≡ τ(n) mod 691. The Hecke algebra of Hecke operators acting on the cusp form Δ is just isomorphic to **Z**. If we identify it with **Z** then the Eisenstein ideal is (691).

:::
