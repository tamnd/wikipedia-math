# George Berzsenyi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="George Berzsenyi"}

**George Berzsenyi** (; August 17, 1938 – June 4, 2026) was a Hungarian-American mathematician and professor, known for his contributions to mathematics education and his mentorship of high-school mathematics students. He founded and chaired the [American Invitational Mathematics Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination) (AIME), founded the [United States of America Mathematical Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America_Mathematical_Talent_Search) (USAMTS), and helped create several other problem-solving programs for mathematically gifted students. For his contributions, he received the [Paul Erdős Award](wikipedia://en/28001680) in 1996 and the [Gung and Hu Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yueh-Gin_Gung_and_Dr._Charles_Y._Hu_Award_for_Distinguished_Service_to_Mathematics) in 2016.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Berzsenyi was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), Hungary, on August 17, 1938, as György de Vargha. Both parents came from old Hungarian nobility. His mother Kornélia descended from the poet [Dániel Berzsenyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dániel_Berzsenyi). György and his father, Colonel Miklós de Vargha, were among the many thousands who emigrated to the United States in 1957 after Soviet troops crushed the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956).[^o6][^o5]

György soon officially changed his name to George Berzsenyi.[^o6] He earned his doctorate in mathematics from [Texas Christian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Christian_University) in 1969,[^o7] with a specialization in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).[^o8]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Berzsenyi taught mathematics at [Lamar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_University) in Beaumont, Texas, for 19 years.[^o10] He later joined [Rose–Hulman Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose–Hulman_Institute_of_Technology) (RHIT) in Terre Haute, Indiana, where he continued to teach and promote mathematics enrichment for 12 years[^o10] until his retirement in 1999.

## Competitions and outreach {#Competitions_and_outreach}

### Mathematics Day and Texas Mathematical Olympiad {#Mathematics_Day_and_Texas_Mathematical_Olympiad}

At Lamar University, Berzsenyi organized an annual "Mathematics Day" (1977–1982) for high-school students. He also created the Texas Mathematical Olympiad during this period.[^o3]

### In-person coaching {#In-person_coaching}

Berzsenyi was the first coach of the Texas team for the [American Regions Mathematics League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Regions_Mathematics_League) (ARML) in 1980.[^o10] He also mentored students for the Westinghouse/Intel [Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Talent_Search) and similar competitions.[^o3]

### AIME and AMC involvement {#AIME_and_AMC_involvement}

In 1983, Berzsenyi became the founding chair of the [American Invitational Mathematics Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Invitational_Mathematics_Examination) (AIME) committee and served for six years. He also contributed problems to the [American Mathematics Competitions](wikipedia://en/665435) (AMC), including the earlier American High School Mathematics Examination (AHSME).[^o3] These annual exams have been the primary selectors of students for the [USA Mathematical Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Mathematical_Olympiad), leading in turn to the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208).

### USAMTS and IMTS {#USAMTS_and_IMTS}

In 1989, Berzsenyi founded the [USA Mathematical Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Mathematical_Talent_Search) (USAMTS), a nationwide proof-based contest by mail. Modeled on Hungary's *[Középiskolai Matematikai Lapok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Középiskolai_Matematikai_Lapok)* (*KöMaL*), it provided high-school students with extended time and personalized feedback for their work. Berzsenyi directed the contest through the 1990s, and later co-edited problem sets for the International Mathematical Talent Search (IMTS).[^o16] For much of the 1990s, he invited 30 of the strongest USAMTS solvers to spend three weeks at the [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation)-funded Young Scholars Program at RHIT, where they studied various topics from classical geometry to chaos theory.

### The Competition Corner {#The_Competition_Corner}

Berzsenyi edited "The Competition Corner" in *The Mathematics Student* from 1978 until the journal ended in 1981. This was the first of his Hungarian-style proof-by-mail efforts (eventually becoming USAMTS) that helped develop hundreds of American high-school mathematicians through year-round creative problem-solving.[^o3]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Berzsenyi died June 4, 2026, at the age of 87. He was survived by his wife Kay after 61 years of marriage, and by their children and grandchildren.[^o19]

His mathematical protégés include numerous academics and scientists. Among them are [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999), [Kiran Kedlaya](wikipedia://en/31688876), and [Vamsi Mootha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamsi_Mootha), who credited Berzsenyi's mentorship in an [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPR) feature.[^o20]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1996, Berzsenyi received the [Paul Erdős Award](wikipedia://en/28001680) from the [World Federation of National Mathematics Competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_National_Mathematics_Competitions) for his contributions to national competitions and educational enrichment.[^o22] In 2016, he received the [Gung and Hu Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gung_and_Hu_Award) from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for a lifetime of distinguished service.[^o3]
