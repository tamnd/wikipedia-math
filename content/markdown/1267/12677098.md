# Geodesic convexity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geodesic convexity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) — **geodesic convexity** is a natural generalization of [convexity for sets](wikipedia://en/6292) and [functions](wikipedia://en/245568) to [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652). It is common to drop the prefix "geodesic" and refer simply to "convexity" of a set or function.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let (*M*, *g*) be a Riemannian manifold.

- A subset *C* of *M* is said to be a **geodesically convex set** if, given any two points in *C*, there is a unique minimizing [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) contained within *C* that joins those two points.
- Let *C* be a geodesically convex subset of *M*. A function $f:C\to\mathbf{R}$ is said to be a (**strictly**) **geodesically convex function** if the composition

$$f \circ \gamma : [0, T] \to \mathbf{R}$$

is a (strictly) convex function in the usual sense for every unit speed geodesic arc *γ* : \[0, *T*\] → *M* contained within *C*.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A geodesically convex (subset of a) Riemannian manifold is also a [convex metric space](wikipedia://en/10788068) with respect to the geodesic distance.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A subset of *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **E**<sup>*n*</sup> with its usual flat metric is geodesically convex [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is convex in the usual sense, and similarly for functions.
- The "northern hemisphere" of the 2-dimensional sphere **S**<sup>2</sup> with its usual metric is geodesically convex. However, the subset *A* of **S**<sup>2</sup> consisting of those points with [latitude](wikipedia://en/17616) further north than 45° south is *not* geodesically convex, since the minimizing geodesic ([great circle](wikipedia://en/48082)) arc joining two distinct points on the southern boundary of *A* leaves *A* (e.g. in the case of two points 180° apart in [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude), the geodesic arc passes over the south pole).

:::
