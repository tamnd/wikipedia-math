# Fast sweeping method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fast sweeping method"}

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), the **fast sweeping method** is a [numerical method](wikipedia://en/1678715) for solving [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) of the [Eikonal equation](wikipedia://en/4557698).

:::

$$|\nabla u(\mathbf{x})| = \frac 1 {f(\mathbf{x})} \text{ for } \mathbf{x} \in \Omega$$

$$u(\mathbf{x}) = 0 \text{ for } \mathbf{x} \in \partial \Omega$$

where $\Omega$ is an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in $\mathbb{R}^n$, $f(\mathbf{x})$ is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) with positive values, $\partial \Omega$ is a well-behaved [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the open set and $|\cdot|$ is the [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm).

The fast sweeping method is an iterative method which uses upwind difference for discretization and uses [Gauss–Seidel iterations](wikipedia://en/4046824) with alternating sweeping ordering to solve the discretized Eikonal equation on a rectangular grid. The origins of this approach lie in the paper by Boue and Dupuis.[^o2] Although fast sweeping methods have existed in control theory, it was first proposed for Eikonal equations[^o3] by [Hongkai Zhao](wikipedia://en/59796930), an applied mathematician at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine).

Sweeping algorithms are highly efficient for solving Eikonal equations when the corresponding [characteristic curves](wikipedia://en/751933) do not change direction very often.[^o4]
