# Sheila May Edmonds

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sheila May Edmonds"}

**Sheila May Edmonds** (1 April 1916 – 2 September 2002) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a [Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and Vice-Principal of [Newnham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College,_Cambridge) from 1960 to 1981.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Kingston, Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Kent), Edmonds studied at [Wimbledon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_High_School) and entered Newnham College, Cambridge in 1935 to study for the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos).[^o4][^o8] At this time women could attend lectures and sit examinations but were not permitted to graduate with a degree.[^o5] She had an excellent undergraduate career and finished Part II as a "[Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29)", Cambridge nomenclature for a student achieving a First Class award. Edmonds followed this with a Distinction in [Part III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos), and then studied for a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) with [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599). During her doctoral research she spent a year at [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College), London, and a year at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). She was awarded PhD for her dissertation "Some Multiplication Problems" in 1944.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Edmonds's first papers were published while she was studying for her PhD, with two in 1942 on [infinite series](wikipedia://en/15287) and on [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247). These led to a series of papers over the following years, exploring these topics and building on her PhD research into [Parseval's theorem](wikipedia://en/706435).

She was a dedicated teacher, supervising students in all branches of [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), as well as lecturing courses in the Mathematical Tripos. Edmonds served on the University Faculty Board of Mathematics for many years, and was its chair in 1975 and 1976.[^o4]

In 1960, Edmonds was appointed Vice-Principal of Newnham, one of the senior offices of the college, a post which she held until her retirement in 1981. She also served on the [University of Cambridge Local Examinations Syndicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge_Local_Examinations_Syndicate) and the boards of governors of several schools including [Rodean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roedean_School).[^o9]
