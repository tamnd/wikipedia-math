# Essential manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Essential manifold"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **essential manifold** is a special type of closed manifold. The notion was first introduced explicitly by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A closed [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* is called essential if its [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) \[*M*\] defines a nonzero element in the [homology](wikipedia://en/248635#Group_homology) of its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi$, or more precisely in the homology of the corresponding [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) *K*($\pi$, 1), via the natural homomorphism

$$H_n(M)\to H_n(K(\pi,1)),$$

where *n* is the dimension of *M*. Here the fundamental class is taken in homology with integer coefficients if the manifold is orientable, and in coefficients modulo 2, otherwise.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- All closed surfaces (i.e. 2-dimensional manifolds) are essential with the exception of the 2-sphere *S<sup>2</sup>*.
- Real projective space *RP<sup>n</sup>* is essential since the inclusion

$$\mathbb{RP}^n \to \mathbb{RP}^\infty$$

is injective in homology, where

$$\mathbb{RP}^\infty = K(\mathbb{Z}_2, 1)$$

is the Eilenberg–MacLane space of the finite cyclic group of order 2.

- All compact [aspherical manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspherical_manifold) are essential (since being aspherical means the manifold itself is already a *K*($\pi$, 1))
  - In particular all compact [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) are essential.
- All [lens spaces](wikipedia://en/885725) are essential.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) of essential manifolds is essential.
- Any manifold which admits a map of nonzero degree to an essential manifold is itself essential.

:::
