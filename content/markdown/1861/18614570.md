# Data binning

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Data binning"}

**Data binning**, also called **data discrete binning** or **data bucketing**, is a [data pre-processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_pre-processing) technique used to reduce the effects of minor [observation errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_error). The original data values which fall into a given small interval, a *[bin](wikipedia://en/13764454)*, are replaced by a value representative of that interval, often a [central value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency) ([mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) or [median](wikipedia://en/18837)). It is related to [quantization](wikipedia://en/317018): data binning operates on the [abscissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abscissa) axis while quantization operates on the [ordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinate) axis. Binning is a generalization of [rounding](wikipedia://en/169945).

:::

**Statistical data binning** is a way to group numbers of more-or-less continuous values into a smaller number of "bins". For example, if you have data about a group of people, you might want to arrange their ages into a smaller number of age intervals (for example, grouping every five years together). It can also be used in [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384), binning in several dimensions at once.

In [digital image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image_processing), "binning" has a very different meaning. [Pixel binning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel_binning) is the process of combining blocks of adjacent [pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixel) throughout an image, by summing or averaging their values, during or after readout. It reduces the amount of data; also the relative noise level in the result is lower.

## Example usage {#Example_usage}

[Histograms](wikipedia://en/13266) are an example of data binning used in order to observe underlying [frequency distributions](wikipedia://en/4839019). They typically occur in [one-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/25584215) and in [equal](wikipedia://en/32533#Equality) [intervals](wikipedia://en/49172) for ease of visualization.

Data binning may be used when small instrumental shifts in the spectral dimension from [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry) (MS) or [nuclear magnetic resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance) (NMR) experiments will be falsely interpreted as representing different components, when a collection of data profiles is subjected to [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706) analysis. A straightforward way to cope with this problem is by using binning techniques in which the spectrum is reduced in resolution to a sufficient degree to ensure that a given peak remains in its bin despite small spectral shifts between analyses. For example, in [NMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_magnetic_resonance) the [chemical shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_shift) axis may be discretized and coarsely binned, and in [MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry) the spectral accuracies may be rounded to an integer multiple of the [dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_%28unit%29). Also, several [digital camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_camera) systems incorporate an automatic pixel binning function to improve image contrast.[^o3]

Binning is also used in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) to speed up[^o4] the decision-tree [boosting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boosting_%28machine_learning%29) method for supervised classification and regression in algorithms such as [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft)'s [LightGBM](wikipedia://en/64785522) and [scikit-learn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scikit-learn)'s [Histogram-based Gradient Boosting Classification Tree](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingClassifier.html).
