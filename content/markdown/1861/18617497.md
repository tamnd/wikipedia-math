# Fuchs's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fuchs's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fuchs's theorem**, named after [Lazarus Fuchs](wikipedia://en/2817087), states that a [second-order differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-order_differential_equation) of the form

$$y'' + p(x)y' + q(x)y = g(x)$$

has a solution expressible by a generalised [Frobenius series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_series) when $p(x)$, $q(x)$ and $g(x)$ are [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) at $x = a$ or $a$ is a [regular singular point](wikipedia://en/3166061). That is, any solution to this second-order differential equation can be written as

$$y = \sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n (x - a)^{n + s}, \quad a_0 \neq 0$$

for some positive real *s*, or

$$y = y_0 \ln(x - a) + \sum_{n=0}^\infty b_n(x - a)^{n + r}, \quad b_0 \neq 0$$

for some positive real *r*, where *y*<sub>0</sub> is a solution of the first kind.

:::

Its radius of convergence is at least as large as the minimum of the radii of convergence of $p(x)$, $q(x)$ and $g(x)$.
