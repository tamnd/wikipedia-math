# Logic and dialectic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logic and dialectic"}

[Formal scientists](wikipedia://en/3694774) have attempted to combine [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) (the science of [deductively valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) inferences or of [logical truths](wikipedia://en/3986852)) and [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813) (a form of reasoning based upon dialogue of [arguments](wikipedia://en/13018310) and counter-arguments) through [formalisation](wikipedia://en/396102) of dialectic. These attempts include pre-formal and partially formal treatises on argument and [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813), systems based on [defeasible reasoning](wikipedia://en/2628057), and systems based on [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121) and [dialogical logic](wikipedia://en/31402297).

:::

## History {#History}

Since the late 20th century, European and American [logicians](wikipedia://en/46426065) have attempted to provide mathematical foundations for dialectic through formalisation,[^o3] although logic has been related to dialectic since ancient times.[^o3] There have been pre-formal and partially-formal treatises on argument and dialectic, from authors such as [Stephen Toulmin](wikipedia://en/1543748) (*The Uses of Argument*, 1958),[^o4][^o5][^o3] [Nicholas Rescher](wikipedia://en/576633) (*Dialectics: A Controversy-Oriented Approach to the Theory of Knowledge*, 1977),[^o6][^o7][^o3] and [Frans H. van Eemeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frans_H._van_Eemeren) and [Rob Grootendorst](wikipedia://en/27220545) ([pragma-dialectics](wikipedia://en/2938955), 1980s).[^o3] One can include works of the communities of [informal logic](wikipedia://en/14122398) and [paraconsistent logic](wikipedia://en/421085).[^o3]

## Defeasibility {#Defeasibility}

Building on theories of [defeasible reasoning](wikipedia://en/2628057) (see [John L. Pollock](wikipedia://en/24911273)), systems have been built that define well-formedness of arguments, rules governing the process of introducing arguments based on fixed assumptions, and rules for shifting burden.[^o3] Many of these logics appear in the special area of [artificial intelligence and law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_and_law), though the computer scientists' interest in formalizing dialectic originates in a desire to build [decision support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_support) and computer-supported collaborative work systems.[^o9]

## Dialogue games {#Dialogue_games}

Dialectic itself can be formalised as moves in a game, where an advocate for the truth of a proposition and an opponent argue.[^o3] Such games can provide a [semantics of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_logic), one that is very general in applicability.[^o3]
