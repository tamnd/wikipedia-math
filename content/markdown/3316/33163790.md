# Samuel Verblunsky

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Verblunsky"}

**Samuel Verblunsky** (22 June 1906, London - 1996, Belfast) was a British mathematician who introduced [Verblunsky's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verblunsky's_theorem) and [Verblunsky coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verblunsky_coefficients). His early work on orthogonal polynomials and harmonic functions was neglected for many years, until publicized by [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Verblunsky's entered [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge) in 1924 having won a scholarship to study mathematics. His teachers at Cambridge included [G H Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_H_Hardy) and [J E Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371), while fellow students included [Donald Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Coxeter) and [Raymond Paley](wikipedia://en/2788568). He got his PhD at Cambridge in 1930 under [John Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371), and spend the rest of the 1930s at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester). He then worked for three decades at [Queen's University Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_Belfast), where he rose to the rank of dean.[^o2]
