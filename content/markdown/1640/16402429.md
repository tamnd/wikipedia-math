# Lateral surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lateral surface"}

*Image: Kreiszylinder.svg.* Straight circular cylinder with unrolled lateral surface

The **lateral surface** of a [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3054853) object is the surface of all its sides, excluding its [base](wikipedia://en/9579379) and top (when they exist).[^o2] The distinction between lateral and non-lateral surfaces is most commonly applied to [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29), [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), and [cones](wikipedia://en/782427), where there is a natural separation between the base face(s) and the remaining faces or curved surface.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **lateral surface area** (**LSA**) is the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of the lateral surface. This is distinguished from the **total surface area** (**TSA**), which is the lateral surface area together with the areas of the base and top.[^o2] In practical applications, the lateral surface area is relevant when calculating quantities such as the amount of material needed to wrap the sides of a container, the area of a wall to be painted, or the label for a cylindrical can.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formulas"}

## Formulas {#Formulas}

### Prism {#Prism}

The lateral surface area of a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) is the sum of the areas of its lateral faces (the faces that are not bases).[^o2] For a **right prism** — one whose lateral edges are perpendicular to the bases — the lateral surface area equals the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the base multiplied by the height of the prism:[^o9]

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = P \cdot h$$

where $P$ is the perimeter of the base and $h$ is the height (the perpendicular distance between the two bases).

As a special case, for a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with edge length $a$, the lateral surface area consists of four square faces: $A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = 4a^{2}$.

For a [rectangular prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangular_prism) (cuboid) with base dimensions $l$ and $w$ and height $h$, the lateral surface area is:

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = 2(l + w) \cdot h$$

### Cylinder {#Cylinder}

*Image: Kreiszylinder.svg.* A cylinder's lateral surface unrolls into a rectangle

For a **[right circular cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29)** with radius $r$ and height $h$, the lateral surface is the curved surface connecting the two circular bases. When this surface is "unrolled" (i.e. developed into a plane), it forms a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) with width equal to the [circumference](wikipedia://en/5916) of the base ($2\pi r$) and height $h$.[^o5] Thus:

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = 2\pi rh$$

The total surface area of the cylinder is obtained by adding the areas of the two circular bases:

$$A_{\operatorname{total}} = 2\pi rh + 2\pi r^{2} = 2\pi r(h + r)$$

### Pyramid {#Pyramid}

For a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045), the lateral surface area is the sum of the areas of all the triangular lateral faces, excluding the base.[^o2]

For a **regular pyramid** — one whose base is a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) and whose apex lies directly above the centre of the base — the lateral faces are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428). In this case, the lateral surface area is:[^o5]

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = \tfrac{1}{2} P \cdot l$$

where $P$ is the perimeter of the base and $l$ is the [slant height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_height) (the distance from the apex to the midpoint of a base edge, measured along a lateral face).

### Cone {#Cone}

For a **right circular [cone](wikipedia://en/782427)** with base radius $r$ and slant height $l$, the lateral surface area is:[^o5]

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = \pi rl$$

The slant height can be found from the radius and the (vertical) height $h$ of the cone using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034):

$$l = \sqrt{r^{2} + h^{2}}$$

This formula can be understood by cutting the lateral surface of the cone along a slant line from the apex to the base and unrolling it into a flat [sector](wikipedia://en/1967249) of a circle with radius $l$ and arc length $2\pi r$.[^o5]

### Frustum {#Frustum}

A **[frustum](wikipedia://en/89405)** is the portion of a cone or pyramid that lies between two parallel planes cutting through it. For a right circular [conical frustum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frustum_of_a_cone) with top radius $r_{1}$, bottom radius $r_{2}$, and slant height $l$, the lateral surface area is:[^o5]

$$A_{\operatorname{lateral}} = \pi(r_{1} + r_{2}) \cdot l$$

where the slant height is given by:

$$l = \sqrt{(r_{2} - r_{1})^{2} + h^{2}}$$

This generalises the cone formula (setting $r_{1} = 0$) and can also be seen as the difference between the lateral areas of two nested cones.

## Summary of formulas {#Summary_of_formulas}

::: {#o16 .table cue=table}

| Solid | Lateral surface area | Variables |
|---|---|---|
| [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) | $4a^{2}$ | $a$ = edge length |
| Right [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) | $P \cdot h$ | $P$ = base perimeter, $h$ = height |
| Right circular [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) | $2\pi rh$ | $r$ = radius, $h$ = height |
| Regular [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) | $\tfrac{1}{2} P \cdot l$ | $P$ = base perimeter, $l$ = slant height |
| Right circular [cone](wikipedia://en/782427) | $\pi rl$ | $r$ = radius, $l$ = slant height |
| Right circular [frustum](wikipedia://en/89405) | $\pi(r_{1} + r_{2}) \cdot l$ | $r_{1}$, $r_{2}$ = radii, $l$ = slant height |
