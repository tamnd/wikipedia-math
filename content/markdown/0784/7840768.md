# Injective metric space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Injective metric space"}

In [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), an **injective metric space**, or equivalently a **hyperconvex metric space**, is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) with certain properties generalizing those of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) and of [L<sub>∞</sub> distances](wikipedia://en/1503963) in higher-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370). These properties can be defined in two seemingly different ways: hyperconvexity involves the intersection properties of [closed balls](wikipedia://en/160556) in the space, while injectivity involves the [isometric embeddings](wikipedia://en/254777) of the space into larger spaces. However it is a theorem of (Aronszajn Panitchpakdi 1956) that these two different types of definitions are equivalent.[^o3]

:::

## Hyperconvexity {#Hyperconvexity}

A metric space $X$ is said to be **hyperconvex** if it is [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_metric) and its [closed balls](wikipedia://en/160556) have the binary [Helly property](wikipedia://en/670453). That is:

1. Any two points $x$ and $y$ can be connected by the [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) image of a line segment of length equal to the distance between the points (i.e. $X$ is a path space).
1. If $F$ is any family of closed balls $${\bar B}_r(p) = \{q \mid d(p,q) \le r\}$$ such that each pair of balls in $F$ meets, then there exists a point $x$ common to all the balls in $F$.

Equivalently, a metric space $X$ is hyperconvex if, for any set of points $p_i$ in $X$ and radii $r_i>0$ satisfying $r_i+r_j\ge d(p_i,p_j)$ for each $i$ and $j$, there is a point $q$ in $X$ that is within distance $r_i$ of each $p_i$ (that is, $d(p_i,q) \le r_i$ for all $i$).

## Injectivity {#Injectivity}

A [retraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retract_%28metric_geometry%29) of a metric space $X$ is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ mapping $X$ to a subspace of itself, such that

1. for all $x \in X$ we have that $f(f(x))=f(x)$; that is, $f$ is the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) on its image (i.e. it is [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent)), and
1. for all $x, y \in X$ we have that $d(f(x),f(y))\le d(x,y)$; that is, $f$ is [nonexpansive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonexpansive_mapping).

A *retract* of a space $X$ is a subspace of $X$ that is an image of a retraction. A metric space $X$ is said to be **injective** if, whenever $X$ is [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) to a subspace $Z$ of a space $Y$, that subspace $Z$ is a retract of $Y$.

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of hyperconvex metric spaces include

- The real line
- $\mathbb{R}^d$ with the [$\ell$<sup>∞</sup> distance](wikipedia://en/45194)
- [Manhattan distance](wikipedia://en/408354) (*L*<sub>1</sub>) in the plane (which is equivalent up to rotation and scaling to the *L*<sub>∞</sub>), but not in higher dimensions
- The [tight span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_span) of a metric space
- Any complete [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914)
- $\operatorname{Aim}(X)$ – see [Metric space aimed at its subspace](wikipedia://en/14114496)

Due to the equivalence between hyperconvexity and injectivity, these spaces are all also injective.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

In an injective space, the radius of the [minimum ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) that contains any set $S$ is equal to half the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of $S$. This follows since the balls of radius half the diameter, centered at the points of $S$, intersect pairwise and therefore by hyperconvexity have a common intersection; a ball of radius half the diameter centered at a point of this common intersection contains all of $S$. Thus, injective spaces satisfy a particularly strong form of [Jung's theorem](wikipedia://en/7503941).

Every injective space is a [complete space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space),[^n1] and every [metric map](wikipedia://en/469760) (or, equivalently, [nonexpansive mapping, or short map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_map)) on a bounded injective space has a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/1011848).[^o11] A metric space is injective [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is an [injective object](wikipedia://en/473779) in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of [metric spaces and metric maps](wikipedia://en/535617).[^o12]
