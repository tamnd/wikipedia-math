# Thomas William Edmondson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas William Edmondson"}

**Thomas William Edmondson** (June 26, 1869 – November 5, 1938) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was a physics and mathematics professor at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) from 1896 to his retirement in 1934.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Thomas William Edmondson was born on June 26, 1869, in [Skipton-in-Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipton), [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), to Sarah (née Dodgson) and Thomas Edmondson Sr.[^o4][^o5] His father was a printer and publisher.[^o6]

Edmondson attended elementary school in Skipton. He attended [Skipton Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipton_Grammar_School) and was a head boy.[^o4][^o6] He graduated from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1888. He received the Ackroyd Scholarship and attended the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o4][^o6][^o5] In 1891, he graduated from Cambridge with a Bachelor of Arts and received the 18th wrangler mark in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos).[^o4][^o7] He then worked as an assistant tutor in mathematics and physics at Cambridge's University Correspondence College.[^o4] In 1893, he left England for America. He graduated from [Clark University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_University) with a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1896.[^o4][^o6][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

In 1896, Edmondson joined [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) as an assistant professor of physics. He worked alongside Daniel W. Hering. He also assisted Pomeroy Ladue in the mathematics department. In 1903, he became an assistant professor of mathematics.[^o4][^o6][^o8] He took over as head of the mathematics department following Ladue's retirement in 1906. He remained as department head until his retirement on August 31, 1934.[^o4][^o6][^o8] He was acting dean of the New York University graduate school from 1916 to 1921.[^o5][^o6] He was secretary of the faculty committee of the university council from 1903 to 1933.[^o4] He was chairman of the Washington Square Collegiate Division in 1913.[^o4] He also served as college bursar, treasurer of the athletic association, and was a member of the Beta chapter of [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa).[^o6]

Edmondson was a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the American Mathematical Association, the [American Association of University Professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors), and the [English Place-Name Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Place-Name_Society).[^o6][^o8] He was a member of the Craven Naturalists and Scientific Association.[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Edmondson married Minnie Ramsden of [Perth, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Ontario), in 1896. They had no children.[^o4][^o6][^o5] He lived on Franklin Avenue in [Mount Vernon, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vernon,_New_York).[^o8] He attended the First Baptist Church in Mount Vernon.[^o8] His hobbies included [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) and archaeological research.[^o6]

Edmondson died on November 4, 1938, at Mount Vernon Hospital.[^o8]

## Works {#Works}

Edmondson wrote books on topics, including spark discharge in dielectrics; potential, spherical harmonic analysis; and on curves and elliptic functions.[^o5]

His publications included:

- *Worked Examples in Coördinate Geometry* (1891)
- *Mensuration and Spherical Geometry* (1893), with W. Briggs
- *Deductions in Euclid* (1901)
