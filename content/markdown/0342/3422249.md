# Ado's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ado's theorem"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), **Ado's theorem** is a theorem characterizing finite-dimensional [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Ado's theorem states that every finite-dimensional [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) *L* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero) can be viewed as a Lie algebra of [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) under the [commutator bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutator_bracket). More precisely, the theorem states that *L* has a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) ρ over *K*, on a [finite-dimensional vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional_vector_space) *V*, that is a [faithful representation](wikipedia://en/2617872), making *L* isomorphic to a subalgebra of the [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of *V*.

:::

## History {#History}

The theorem was proved in 1935 by [Igor Dmitrievich Ado](wikipedia://en/61582053) of [Kazan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University), a student of [Nikolai Chebotaryov](wikipedia://en/3033696).

The restriction on the characteristic was later removed by [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa) (see also the below [Gerhard Hochschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Hochschild) paper for a proof).

## Implications {#Implications}

While for the Lie algebras associated to [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878) there is nothing new in this, the general case is a deeper result. Applied to the real Lie algebra of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G*, it does not imply that *G* has a faithful linear representation (which is not true in general), but rather that *G* always has a linear representation that is a [local isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_isomorphism) with a [linear group](wikipedia://en/3983656).
