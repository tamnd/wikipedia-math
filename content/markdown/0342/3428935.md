# Density on a manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Density on a manifold"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and specifically [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **density** is a spatially varying quantity on a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) that can be [integrated](wikipedia://en/15532) in an intrinsic manner. Abstractly, a density is a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of a certain [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366), called the **density bundle**. An element of the density bundle at *x* is a function that assigns a volume for the [parallelotope](wikipedia://en/23975) spanned by the *n* given tangent vectors at *x*.

:::

From the operational point of view, a density is a collection of functions on [coordinate charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_chart) which become multiplied by the absolute value of the [Jacobian determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_determinant) in the change of coordinates. Densities can be generalized into ***s*-densities**, whose coordinate representations become multiplied by the *s*-th power of the absolute value of the jacobian determinant. On an [oriented manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriented_manifold), 1-densities can be canonically identified with the [*n*-forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on *M*. On non-orientable manifolds this identification cannot be made, since the density bundle is the tensor product of the orientation bundle of *M* and the *n*-th exterior product bundle of *T*${}^{\ast}$*M* (see [pseudotensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudotensor)).

## Motivation (densities in vector spaces) {#Motivation_(densities_in_vector_spaces)}

In general, there does not exist a natural concept of a "volume" for a parallelotope generated by vectors *v*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *v<sub>n</sub>* in a *n*-dimensional vector space *V*. However, if one wishes to define a function *μ* : *V* × ... × *V* → **R** that assigns a volume for any such parallelotope, it should satisfy the following properties:

- If any of the vectors *v<sub>k</sub>* is multiplied by *λ* ∈ **R**, the volume should be multiplied by |*λ*|.
- If any linear combination of the vectors *v*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *v*<sub>*j*−1</sub>, *v*<sub>*j*+1</sub>, ..., *v<sub>n</sub>* is added to the vector *v<sub>j</sub>*, the volume should stay invariant.

These conditions are equivalent to the statement that *μ* is given by a translation-invariant measure on *V*, and they can be rephrased as

$$\mu(Av_1,\ldots,Av_n)=\left|\det A\right|\mu(v_1,\ldots,v_n), \quad A\in \operatorname{GL}(V).$$

Any such mapping *μ* : *V* × ... × *V* → **R** is called a **density** on the vector space *V*. Note that if (*v<sub>1</sub>*, ..., *v<sub>n</sub>*) is any basis for *V*, then fixing *μ*(*v<sub>1</sub>*, ..., *v<sub>n</sub>*) will fix *μ* entirely; it follows that the set Vol(*V*) of all densities on *V* forms a one-dimensional vector space. Any *n*-form *ω* on *V* defines a density $\lvert \omega\rvert$ on *V* by

$$|\omega|(v_1,\ldots,v_n) := |\omega(v_1,\ldots,v_n)|.$$

### Orientations on a vector space {#Orientations_on_a_vector_space}

The set Or(*V*) of all functions *o* : *V* × ... × *V* → **R** that satisfy

$$o(Av_1,\ldots,Av_n)=\operatorname{sign}(\det A)o(v_1,\ldots,v_n), \quad A\in \operatorname{GL}(V)$$

if $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ are linearly independent and $o(v_1,\ldots,v_n) = 0$ otherwise

forms a one-dimensional vector space, and an **orientation** on *V* is one of the two elements *o* ∈ Or(*V*) such that $\lvert o(v_{1}, ..., v_{n})\rvert$ = 1 for any linearly independent *v*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *v<sub>n</sub>*. Any non-zero *n*-form *ω* on *V* defines an orientation *o* ∈ Or(*V*) such that

$$o(v_1,\ldots,v_n)|\omega|(v_1,\ldots,v_n) = \omega(v_1,\ldots,v_n),$$

and vice versa, any *o* ∈ Or(*V*) and any density *μ* ∈ Vol(*V*) define an *n*-form *ω* on *V* by

$$\omega(v_1,\ldots,v_n)= o(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\mu(v_1,\ldots,v_n).$$

In terms of [tensor product spaces](wikipedia://en/30844),

$$\operatorname{Or}(V)\otimes \operatorname{Vol}(V) = \bigwedge^n V^*, \quad \operatorname{Vol}(V) = \operatorname{Or}(V)\otimes \bigwedge^n V^*.$$

### *s*-densities on a vector space {#s-densities_on_a_vector_space}

The *s*-densities on *V* are functions *μ* : *V* × ... × *V* → **R** such that

$$\mu(Av_1,\ldots,Av_n)=\left|\det A\right|^s\mu(v_1,\ldots,v_n), \quad A\in \operatorname{GL}(V).$$

Just like densities, *s*-densities form a one-dimensional vector space *Vol<sup>s</sup>*(*V*), and any *n*-form *ω* on *V* defines an *s*-density |*ω*|<sup>*s*</sup> on *V* by

$$|\omega|^s(v_1,\ldots,v_n) := |\omega(v_1,\ldots,v_n)|^s.$$

The product of *s*<sub>1</sub>- and *s*<sub>2</sub>-densities *μ*<sub>1</sub> and *μ*<sub>2</sub> form an (*s*<sub>1</sub>+*s*<sub>2</sub>)-density *μ* by

$$\mu(v_1,\ldots,v_n) := \mu_1(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\mu_2(v_1,\ldots,v_n).$$

In terms of [tensor product spaces](wikipedia://en/30844) this fact can be stated as

$$\operatorname{Vol}^{s_1}(V)\otimes \operatorname{Vol}^{s_2}(V) = \operatorname{Vol}^{s_1+s_2}(V).$$

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Formally, the *s*-density bundle *Vol<sup>s</sup>*(*M*) of a differentiable manifold *M* is obtained by an [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874) construction, intertwining the one-dimensional [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695)

$$\rho(A) = \left|\det A\right|^{-s},\quad A\in \operatorname{GL}(n)$$

of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) with the [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550) of *M*.

The resulting line bundle is known as the bundle of *s*-densities, and is denoted by

$$\left|\Lambda\right|^s_M = \left|\Lambda\right|^s(TM).$$

A 1-density is also referred to simply as a **density.**

More generally, the associated bundle construction also allows densities to be constructed from any [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) *E* on *M*.

In detail, if (*U*<sub>α</sub>,φ<sub>α</sub>) is an [atlas](wikipedia://en/47436) of [coordinate charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_chart) on *M*, then there is associated a [local trivialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) of $\left|\Lambda\right|^s_M$

$$t_\alpha : \left|\Lambda\right|^s_M|_{U_\alpha} \to \phi_\alpha(U_\alpha)\times\mathbb{R}$$

subordinate to the open cover *U*<sub>α</sub> such that the associated GL(1)-cocycle satisfies

$$t_{\alpha\beta} = \left|\det (d\phi_\alpha\circ d\phi_\beta^{-1})\right|^{-s}.$$

:::

## Integration {#Integration}

Densities play a significant role in the theory of [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) on manifolds. Indeed, the definition of a density is motivated by how a measure dx changes under a change of coordinates (Folland 1999, Section 11.4, pp. 361-362).

Given a 1-density ƒ supported in a coordinate chart *U*<sub>α</sub>, the integral is defined by

$$\int_{U_\alpha} f = \int_{\phi_\alpha(U_\alpha)} t_\alpha\circ f\circ\phi_\alpha^{-1}d\mu$$

where the latter integral is with respect to the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) on **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. The transformation law for 1-densities together with the [Jacobian change of variables](wikipedia://en/193748) ensures compatibility on the overlaps of different coordinate charts, and so the integral of a general [compactly supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support) 1-density can be defined by a [partition of unity](wikipedia://en/48636) argument. Thus 1-densities are a generalization of the notion of a volume form that does not necessarily require the manifold to be oriented or even orientable. One can more generally develop a general theory of [Radon measures](wikipedia://en/521933) as [distributional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) sections of $|\Lambda|^1_M$ using the [Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesz-Markov-Kakutani_representation_theorem).

The set of *1/p*-densities such that $|\phi|_p = \left( \int|\phi|^p \right)^{1/p} < \infty$ is a normed linear space whose completion $L^p(M)$ is called the **intrinsic [*L<sup>p</sup>* space](wikipedia://en/45194)** of *M*.

## Conventions {#Conventions}

In some areas, particularly [conformal geometry](wikipedia://en/295907), a different weighting convention is used: the bundle of *s*-densities is instead associated with the character

$$\rho(A) = \left|\det A\right|^{-s/n}.$$

With this convention, for instance, one integrates *n*-densities (rather than 1-densities). Also in these conventions, a conformal metric is identified with a [tensor density](wikipedia://en/1763396) of weight 2.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [dual vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_bundle) of $|\Lambda|^s_M$ is $|\Lambda|^{-s}_M$.
- [Tensor densities](wikipedia://en/1763396) are sections of the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of a density bundle with a tensor bundle.

:::
