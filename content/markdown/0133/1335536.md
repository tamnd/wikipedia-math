# Butterfly theorem

*Image: Butterfly theorem.svg.* Butterfly theorem: $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{XY}$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Butterfly theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **butterfly theorem** is a classical result which can be stated as follows:[^o2]

:::

Let $M$ be the [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of a [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) $\overline{PQ}$ of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), through which two other chords $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ are drawn; $\overline{AD}$ and $\overline{BC}$ intersect chord $\overline{PQ}$ at $X$ and $Y$ correspondingly. Then $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{XY}$.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

*Image: Butterfly1.svg.* Proof of Butterfly theorem solid red 2px solid magenta 2px solid cyan 2px solid lime 2px To be proved: $\overline{MX} = \overline{MY}$.

A formal proof of the theorem is as follows: Let the [perpendiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendiculars) $\overline{XX'}$ and $\overline{XX''}$ be dropped from the point $X$ on the straight lines $\overline{AM}$ and $\overline{DM}$ respectively. Similarly, let $\overline{YY'}$ and $\overline{YY''}$ be dropped from the point $Y$ perpendicular to the straight lines $\overline{BM}$ and $\overline{CM}$ respectively.

$$\begin{aligned} \triangle MXX' &\sim \triangle MYY', \\[2pt] \therefore {\overline{MX} \over \overline{MY}} &= {\overline{XX'} \over \overline{YY'}}. \\[8pt] \triangle MXX'' &\sim \triangle MYY'', \\[2pt] \therefore {\overline{MX} \over \overline{MY}} &= {\overline{XX''} \over \overline{YY''}}. \\[8pt] \triangle AXX' &\sim \triangle CYY'', \\[2pt] \therefore {\overline{XX'} \over \overline{YY''}} &= {\overline{AX} \over \overline{CY}}. \\[8pt] \triangle DXX'' &\sim \triangle BYY', \\[2pt] \therefore {\overline{XX''} \over \overline{YY'}} &= {\overline{DX} \over \overline{BY}}. \end{aligned}$$

From the preceding equations and the [intersecting chords theorem](wikipedia://en/19359050), it can be seen that

$$\begin{aligned} \left({\overline{MX} \over \overline{MY}}\right)^2 &= {\overline{XX'} \over \overline{YY'}} \cdot {\overline{XX''} \over \overline{YY''}}, \\[2pt] &= {\overline{AX} \cdot \overline{DX} \over \overline{CY} \cdot \overline{BY}}, \\[2pt] &= {\overline{PX} \cdot \overline{QX} \over \overline{PY} \cdot \overline{QY}}, \\[2pt] &= { \bigl(\overline{PM} - \overline{XM} \bigr) \cdot \bigl(\overline{MQ} + \overline{XM}\bigr) \over \bigl(\overline{PM} + \overline{MY}\bigr) \cdot \bigl(\overline{QM} - \overline{MY}\bigr)}, \\[2pt] &= { \overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MX}^2 \over \overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MY}^2}, \end{aligned}$$

since $\overline{PM} = \overline{MQ}$. Thus,

$${ \overline{MX}^2 \over \overline{MY}^2} = {\overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MX}^2 \over \overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MY}^2}.$$

Cross-multiplying the latter equation and cancelling out common terms,

$$\begin{aligned} \overline{MX}^2 \cdot \overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MX}^2 \cdot \overline{MY}^2 &= \overline{MY}^2 \cdot \overline{PM}^2 - \overline{MX}^2 \cdot \overline{MY}^2, \\[4pt] \Rightarrow \quad \overline{MX}^2 \cdot \overline{PM}^2 &= \overline{MY}^2 \cdot \overline{PM}^2, \\[4pt] \Rightarrow \quad \overline{MX}^2 &= \overline{MY}^2, \\[4pt] \Rightarrow \quad \overline{MX} &= \overline{MY}. \end{aligned}$$

Thus, $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{XY}$.

Other proofs exist,[^o5] including one using [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).[^o6]

:::

## History {#History}

Proving the butterfly theorem was posed as a problem by [William Wallace](wikipedia://en/16955407) in *The Gentleman's Mathematical Companion* (1803). Three solutions were published in 1804, and in 1805 [Sir William Herschel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Herschel) posed the question again in a letter to Wallace. Reverend Thomas Scurr asked the same question again in 1814 in the *Gentleman's Diary or Mathematical Repository*.[^o8]
