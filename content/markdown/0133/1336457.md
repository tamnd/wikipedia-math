# Loop algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Loop algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **loop algebras** are certain types of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), of particular interest in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field $K$, if $K[t,t^{-1}]$ is the space of [Laurent polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_polynomials), then

$$L\mathfrak{g} := \mathfrak{g}\otimes K[t,t^{-1}],$$

with the inherited bracket

$$[X\otimes t^m, Y\otimes t^n] = [X,Y]\otimes t^{m+n}.$$

### Geometric definition {#Geometric_definition}

If $\mathfrak{g}$ is a Lie algebra, the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of $\mathfrak{g}$ with $C^{\infty}(S^{1})$, the [algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) of (complex) [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) over the [circle](wikipedia://en/39782) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $S^{1}$ (equivalently, smooth complex-valued [periodic functions](wikipedia://en/76848) of a given period),

$$\mathfrak{g}\otimes C^\infty(S^1),$$

is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra with the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799) given by

$$[g_1\otimes f_1,g_2 \otimes f_2]=[g_1,g_2]\otimes f_1 f_2.$$

Here $g_{1}$ and $g_{2}$ are elements of $\mathfrak{g}$ and $f_{1}$ and $f_{2}$ are elements of $C^{\infty}(S^{1})$.

This isn't precisely what would correspond to the [direct product](wikipedia://en/8887) of infinitely many copies of $\mathfrak{g}$, one for each point in $S^{1}$, because of the smoothness restriction. Instead, it can be thought of in terms of [smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) from $S^{1}$ to $\mathfrak{g}$; a smooth parametrized loop in $\mathfrak{g}$, in other words. This is why it is called the **loop algebra**.

:::

## Gradation {#Gradation}

Defining $\mathfrak{g}_i$ to be the [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) $\mathfrak{g}_i = \mathfrak{g}\otimes t^i < L\mathfrak{g},$ the bracket restricts to a product

$$[\cdot\, , \, \cdot]: \mathfrak{g}_i \times \mathfrak{g}_j \rightarrow \mathfrak{g}_{i+j},$$

hence giving the loop algebra a $\mathbb{Z}$-[graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) structure.

In particular, the bracket restricts to the 'zero-mode' subalgebra $\mathfrak{g}_0 \cong \mathfrak{g}$.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

There is a natural derivation on the loop algebra, conventionally denoted $d$ acting as

$$d: L\mathfrak{g} \rightarrow L\mathfrak{g}$$

$$d(X\otimes t^n) = nX\otimes t^n$$

and so can be thought of formally as $d = t\frac{d}{dt}$.

It is required to define [affine Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1350865), which are used in physics, particularly [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774).

## Loop group {#Loop_group}

Similarly, a set of all smooth maps from $S^{1}$ to a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ forms an infinite-dimensional Lie group (Lie group in the sense we can define [functional derivatives](wikipedia://en/301504) over it) called the **[loop group](wikipedia://en/1390288)**. The Lie algebra of a loop group is the corresponding loop algebra.

## Affine Lie algebras as central extension of loop algebras {#Affine_Lie_algebras_as_central_extension_of_loop_algebras}

If $\mathfrak{g}$ is a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587), then a nontrivial [central extension](wikipedia://en/345807#Central_extension) of its loop algebra $L\mathfrak g$ gives rise to an [affine Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1350865). Furthermore, this central extension is unique.[^o9]

The central extension is given by adjoining a central element $\hat k$, that is, for all $X\otimes t^n \in L\mathfrak{g}$,

$$[\hat k, X\otimes t^n] = 0,$$

and modifying the bracket on the loop algebra to

$$[X\otimes t^m, Y\otimes t^n] = [X,Y] \otimes t^{m + n} + mB(X,Y) \delta_{m+n,0} \hat k,$$

where $B(\cdot, \cdot)$ is the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386).

The central extension is, as a vector space, $L\mathfrak{g} \oplus \mathbb{C}\hat k$ (in its usual definition, as more generally, $\mathbb{C}$ can be taken to be an arbitrary field).

### Cocycle {#Cocycle}

Using the language of [Lie algebra cohomology](wikipedia://en/2096669), the central extension can be described using a 2-[cocycle](wikipedia://en/2870898) on the loop algebra. This is the map

$$\varphi: L\mathfrak g \times L\mathfrak g \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$$

satisfying

$$\varphi(X\otimes t^m, Y\otimes t^n) = mB(X,Y)\delta_{m+n,0}.$$

Then the extra term added to the bracket is $\varphi(X\otimes t^m, Y\otimes t^n)\hat k.$

### Affine Lie algebra {#Affine_Lie_algebra}

In physics, the central extension $L\mathfrak g \oplus \mathbb C \hat k$ is sometimes referred to as the affine Lie algebra. In mathematics, this is insufficient, and the full affine Lie algebra is the vector space[^o12]

$$\hat \mathfrak{g} = L\mathfrak{g} \oplus \mathbb C \hat k \oplus \mathbb C d$$

where $d$ is the derivation defined above.

On this space, the Killing form can be extended to a non-degenerate form, and so allows a root system analysis of the affine Lie algebra.
