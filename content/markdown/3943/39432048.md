# Luis Davidson San Juan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Luis Davidson San Juan"}

**Luis J. Davidson San Juan** (1921-09-10 – 2011) was a Cuban mathematician, professor and Doctor in Mathematics, known for his contributions to the fields of mathematics and [pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy). He is renowned for receiving the [Paul Erdős Award](wikipedia://en/28001680) in 1992 by the [International Commission on Mathematical Instruction](wikipedia://en/8818410), and for being awarded the distinction of Maestro Founder of Mathematics in Ibero-America by the [Organization of Ibero-American States for Education, Science and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Ibero-American_States). He was the Vice President of the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) Site Committee in 1988. And he is the author of many books, including *Equations and mathematicians* (2008), and *Problems of elementary mathematics* (1987).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba) on September 10, 1921, he earned the Doctorate in Physics and Mathematical Sciences from the \[\[University of Havana\]\] in 1944 with the thesis entitled *Developments on the series of analytic functions*.[^o3][^o4]

From 1945 to 1961 he taught at the Instituto de Segunda Enseñanza in [Matanzas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matanzas), and in 1950 he was part of the delegation that represented Cuba at the International Congress of Mathematicians at Harvard University.[^o3][^o4]

In 1960, he held the position of National Inspector of Mathematics. And starting in 1963, he organized competitions in mathematics for pre-collegiate students throughout Cuba.

In 1971, he represented Cuba, as Head of the national delegation in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), and in 1988, while a member of the organization, he became the Vice President for the IMO Site Committee.[^o3][^o4]

In 1990 he participated in the First Congress of The World Federation of National Mathematics Competitions at Waterloo, and in 1992 he was awarded the [Paul Erdős Award](wikipedia://en/28001680) from the International Commission of Mathematical Instruction that was held in Québec, Canada.[^o3][^o5]

Some of his books that were published in Spanish are *Contests in mathematics* (1974), *Problems in elementary mathematics* (1987), and *Equations and mathematicians* (1988). The latter was part of a series that he planned to write about: a story about mathematicians and the problems they have solved.

He died on November 10, 2011.[^o3][^o4]
