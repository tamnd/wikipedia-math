# Dold–Kan correspondence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dold–Kan correspondence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more precisely, in the theory of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784), the **Dold–Kan correspondence** (named after [Albrecht Dold](wikipedia://en/31337135) and [Daniel Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155)) states[^o2] that there is an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051) between the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of (nonnegatively graded) [chain complexes](wikipedia://en/138404) and the category of [simplicial abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicial_abelian_group). Moreover, under the equivalence, the $n$th [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) of a chain complex is the $n$th [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the corresponding simplicial abelian group, and a [chain homotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_homotopy) corresponds to a [simplicial homotopy](wikipedia://en/46853133). (In fact, the correspondence preserves the respective standard [model structures](wikipedia://en/2895304).) The correspondence is an example of the nerve and realization paradigm.

:::

There is also an [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category)-version of the Dold–Kan correspondence.[^o4] The book "Nonabelian Algebraic Topology"[^o5] has a Section 14.8 on [cubical](wikipedia://en/46250924) versions of the Dold–Kan theorem, and relates them to a previous equivalence of categories between cubical omega-groupoids and crossed complexes, which is fundamental to the work of that book.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For a chain complex *C* that has an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *A* in degree *n* and zero in all other degrees, the corresponding simplicial group is the [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(A, n)$.

:::

## Detailed construction {#Detailed_construction}

The Dold–Kan correspondence between the category **sAb** of simplicial abelian groups and the category $\text{Ch}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{Ab})$ of nonnegatively graded chain complexes can be constructed explicitly through a pair of [functors](wikipedia://en/10987)[^o2]<sup>pg 149</sup> so that these functors form an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051). The first functor is the normalized chain complex functor

> $$N\colon s\textbf{Ab} \to \text{Ch}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{Ab})$$

and the second functor is the "simplicialization" functor

> $$\Gamma\colon \text{Ch}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{Ab}) \to s\textbf{Ab}$$

constructing a simplicial abelian group from a chain complex. The formed equivalence is an instance of a special type of adjunction, called the **nerve-realization paradigm**[^o9] (also called a **nerve-realization context**) where the data of this adjunction is determined by what's called a cosimplicial object $dk\colon \Delta \to \text{Ch}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{Ab})$, and the adjunction then takes the form

> $$\Gamma = \mathrm{Lan}_y dk : \text{Ch}_{\geq 0}(\textbf{Ab}) \dashv s\textbf{Ab} : \mathrm{Lan}_{dk} y = N$$

where we take the left [Kan extension](wikipedia://en/2502518) and $y$ is the [Yoneda embedding](wikipedia://en/34359#The_Yoneda_embedding).

### Normalized chain complex {#Normalized_chain_complex}

Given a simplicial abelian group $A_\bullet \in \text{Ob}(\text{s}\textbf{Ab})$ there is a chain complex $NA_\bullet$ called the **normalized chain complex** (also called the **Moore complex**) with terms

> $$NA_n = \bigcap^{n-1}_{i=0}\ker(d_i) \subset A_n$$

and differentials given by

> $$NA_n \xrightarrow{(-1)^nd_n} NA_{n-1}$$

These differentials are well defined because of the [simplicial identity](wikipedia://en/1027784)

> $$d_i \circ d_n = d_{n-1}\circ d_i : A_n \to A_{n-2}$$

showing the image of $d_n \colon NA_n \to A_{n-1}$ is in the kernel of each $d_i\colon NA_{n-1} \to NA_{n-2}$. This is because the definition of $NA_n$ gives $d_i(NA_n) = 0$.

Now, composing these differentials gives a commutative diagram

> $$NA_n \xrightarrow{(-1)^nd_n} NA_{n-1} \xrightarrow{(-1)^{n-1}d_{n-1}} NA_{n-2}$$

and the composition map $(-1)^n(-1)^{n-1}d_{n-1}\circ d_n$. This composition is the zero map because of the [simplicial identity](wikipedia://en/1027784)

> $$d_{n-1}\circ d_n = d_{n-1}\circ d_{n-1}$$

and the inclusion $\text{Im}(d_n) \subset NA_{n-1}$, hence the normalized chain complex is a chain complex in $\text{Ch}_{\geq 0 }(\textbf{Ab})$. Because a simplicial abelian group is a functor

> $$A_\bullet \colon \text{Ord} \to \textbf{Ab}$$

and morphisms $A_\bullet \to B_\bullet$ are given by natural transformations, meaning the maps of the simplicial identities still hold, the normalized chain complex construction is functorial.
