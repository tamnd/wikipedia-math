# Léon Motchane

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Léon Motchane"}

**Léon Motchane** (19 June 1900 – 17 January 1990) was a French industrialist and mathematician and the founder of the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in [Bures-sur-Yvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bures-sur-Yvette).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Léon Motchane was of mixed Russian and Swiss parentage, of Jewish descent.[^o4][^o5] He left [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) after the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) in 1918, emigrated to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) and then to [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) in 1924.[^o6] Encouraged by the French mathematician [Paul Montel](wikipedia://en/1796347), Motchane eventually received a doctorate in mathematics at age 54 under the direction of [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193). In 1958 [Cécile DeWitt-Morette](wikipedia://en/26556512) invited Léon Motchane to see the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in the USA[^o7] which inspired Léon Motchane to establish an institute dedicated to fundamental research in three areas: mathematics, theoretical physics, and the methodology of human sciences (the latter area never really took root at the IHÉS).[^o6] By the moral support of the American physicist [Robert Oppenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Oppenheimer), President of the [IAS](wikipedia://en/184179) at that time, and the financial support of several major private companies he managed to create the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in 1958. It moved to its present location in Bois-Marie in [Bures-sur-Yvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bures-sur-Yvette) in 1962. He attended the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in August 1966 held in Moscow and accepted [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042)'s [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) on his behalf as Grothendieck boycotted the Congress to protest over the treatment of the dissident writers.[^o8] Motchane remained the IHÉS director from 1958 until he retired in 1971 when, the Dutch mathematician [Nicolaas Kuiper](wikipedia://en/3344929), took over as the director of [IHÉS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques).[^o6][^o9]

One of Motchane's sons, [Didier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Motchane), became a politician.
