# Additive identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Additive identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **additive identity** of a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) that is equipped with the [operation](wikipedia://en/4140245) of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) is an [element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) which, when added to any element $x$ in the set, yields $x$. One of the most familiar additive identities is the number [0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29) from [elementary mathematics](wikipedia://en/1775329), but additive identities occur in other mathematical structures where addition is defined, such as in [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) and [rings](wikipedia://en/48404).

:::

## Elementary examples {#Elementary_examples}

- The additive identity familiar from [elementary mathematics](wikipedia://en/1775329) is zero, denoted [0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29). For example,

$$5+0 = 5 = 0+5.$$

- In the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) $\mathbb{N}$ (if 0 is included), the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z},$ the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb{Q},$ the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R},$ and the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C},$ the additive identity is 0. This says that for a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) $n$ belonging to any of these sets,

$$n+0 = n = 0+n.$$

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $N$ be a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is closed under the [operation](wikipedia://en/4140245) of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), denoted [+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+). An additive identity for $N$, denoted $e$, is an element in $N$ such that for any element $n$ in $N$,

$$e+n = n = n+e.$$

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Further examples"}

## Further examples {#Further_examples}

- In a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), the additive identity is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of the group, is often denoted 0, and is unique (see below for proof).
- A [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) or [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is a group under the operation of addition and thus these also have a unique additive identity 0. This is defined to be different from the [multiplicative identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_identity) [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29) if the ring (or field) has more than one element. If the additive identity and the multiplicative identity are the same, then the ring is [trivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_%28mathematics%29) (proved below).
- In the ring $M_{m \times n}(R)$ of $m$-by-$n$ [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over a ring $R$, the additive identity is the zero matrix,[^o7] denoted $\mathbf{O}$ or $\mathbf{0}$, and is the $m$-by-$n$ matrix whose entries consist entirely of the identity element 0 in $R$. For example, in the 2×2 matrices over the integers $\operatorname{M}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ the additive identity is

$$0 = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$$

- In the [quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions), 0 is the additive identity.
- In the ring of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) from $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, the function [mapping](wikipedia://en/516931) every number to 0 is the additive identity.
- In the [additive group](wikipedia://en/332229) of [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) in $\mathbb{R}^n,$ the origin or [zero vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_vector) is the additive identity.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="The additive identity is unique in a group"}

### The additive identity is unique in a group {#The_additive_identity_is_unique_in_a_group}

Let $(G, +)$ be a group and let $0$ and $0'$ in $G$ both denote additive identities, so for any $g$ in $G$,

$$0+g = g = g+0, \qquad 0'+g = g = g+0'.$$

It then follows from the above that

$${\color{green}0'} = {\color{green}0'} + 0 = 0' + {\color{red}0} = {\color{red}0}.$$

:::

::: {#o13 .statement cue=named-heading name="The additive identity annihilates ring elements"}

### The additive identity annihilates ring elements {#The_additive_identity_annihilates_ring_elements}

In a system with a multiplication operation that [distributes](wikipedia://en/103118) over addition, the additive identity is a multiplicative [absorbing element](wikipedia://en/14206224), meaning that for any $s$ in $S$, $s \cdot 0 = 0$. This follows because:

$$\begin{aligned} s \cdot 0 &= s \cdot (0 + 0) = s \cdot 0 + s \cdot 0 \\ \Rightarrow s \cdot 0 &= s \cdot 0 - s \cdot 0 \\ \Rightarrow s \cdot 0 &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

:::

### The additive and multiplicative identities are different in a non-trivial ring {#The_additive_and_multiplicative_identities_are_different_in_a_non-trivial_ring}

Let $R$ be a ring and suppose that the additive identity 0 and the multiplicative identity 1 are equal, i.e. 0 = 1. Let $r$ be any element of $R$. Then

$$r = r \times 1 = r \times 0 = 0$$

proving that $R$ is trivial, i.e. $R = \{0\}.$ The [contrapositive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrapositive), that if $R$ is non-trivial then 0 is not equal to 1, is therefore shown.

:::
