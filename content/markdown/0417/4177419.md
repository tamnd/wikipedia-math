# Sidney Siegel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sidney Siegel"}

**Sidney Siegel** (4 January 1916, [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) – 29 November 1961, [Stanford, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California)) was an American [psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychologist) who became especially well known for his work in popularizing [non-parametric statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics) for use in the behavioral sciences. He was a co-developer of the statistical test known as the [Siegel–Tukey test](wikipedia://en/13890643).

:::

In 1951 Siegel completed a [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in vocational arts at [San Jose State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) (now San Jose State University),[^o2] then in 1953 a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in Psychology at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). Except for a year spent at the [Center for Advanced Study in the Behavioral Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Study_in_the_Behavioral_Sciences) at Stanford, he thereafter taught at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), until his death in November 1961 of a [coronary thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_thrombosis).

His parents, Jacob and Rebecca Siegel, were Jewish immigrants from [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania).[^o3]
