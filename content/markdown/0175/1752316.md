# Jürgen Moser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jürgen Moser"}

**Jürgen Kurt Moser** (July 4, 1928 – December 17, 1999) was a German–American mathematician, honored for work spanning over four decades, including [Hamiltonian dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/1197531) and [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Moser's mother Ilse Strehlke was a niece of the violinist and composer [Louis Spohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Spohr). His father was the neurologist Kurt E. Moser (July 21, 1895 – June 25, 1982), who was born to the merchant Max Maync (1870–1911) and Clara Moser (1860–1934). The latter descended from 17th century French [Huguenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) immigrants to [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia). Jürgen Moser's parents lived in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), [German empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_empire) and resettled in [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) as a result of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

Moser attended the [Wilhelmsgymnasium (Königsberg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmsgymnasium_%28Königsberg%29) in his hometown, a high school specializing in mathematics and natural sciences education, from which [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) had graduated in 1880. His older brother Friedrich Robert Ernst (Friedel) Moser (August 31, 1925 – January 14, 1945) served in the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_%28Wehrmacht%29) and died in [Schloßberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrovolsk) during the [East Prussian offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussian_offensive).

Moser married the biologist Dr. Gertrude C. Courant ([Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238)'s daughter, [Carl Runge](wikipedia://en/747636)'s granddaughter and great-granddaughter of [Emil DuBois-Reymond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_DuBois-Reymond)) on September 10, 1955, and took up permanent residence in [New Rochelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle), New York in 1960, commuting to work in New York City. In 1980 he moved to Switzerland, where he lived in [Schwerzenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerzenbach) near [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). He was a member of the Akademisches Orchester Zürich.

He was survived by his younger brother, the photographic printer and processor Klaus T. Moser-Maync from [Northport, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northport,_New_York), his wife, Gertrude Moser from [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle), their daughters, the theater designer Nina Moser from Seattle and the mathematician Lucy I. Moser-Jauslin from [Dijon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijon), and his stepson, the lawyer Richard D. Emery from New York City.

Moser played the piano and the cello, performing [chamber music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_music) since his childhood in the tradition of a musical family, where his father played the violin and his mother the piano. He was a lifelong [amateur astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_astronomer) and took up [paragliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding) in 1988 during a visit at [IMPA](wikipedia://en/2334708) in Rio de Janeiro.

## Work {#Work}

Moser completed his undergraduate education at and received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.) from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_August_University_of_Göttingen) in 1952, studying under [Franz Rellich](wikipedia://en/4172897). After his thesis, he came under the influence of [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602), with whom he coauthored the second and considerably expanded English language edition of a monography on [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182).

Having spent the year 1953 at the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) as a [Fulbright scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_scholar), he emigrated to the United States in 1955 becoming a citizen in 1959.[^o5] He became a professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and later at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He served as director of the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in the period of 1967–1970. In 1970 he declined the offer of a chair at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). After 1980 he was at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich), becoming [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) in 1995.

He was director (sharing office with [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) in the first two years) of the Forschungsinstitut für Mathematik at ETH Zürich in 1984–1995, where he succeeded [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365). He led a rebuilding of the ETH Zürich mathematics faculty. Moser was president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) in 1983–1986.

## Research {#Research}

In 1967, [Neil Trudinger](wikipedia://en/15173259) identified a new function space embedding which could be viewed as a borderline case of the [Sobolev embedding theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_embedding_theorem).[^o9] Moser found the sharp constant in Trudinger's inequality, with the corresponding result often known as the [Moser–Trudinger inequality](wikipedia://en/2971303).[^o7]

### Elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations {#Elliptic_and_parabolic_partial_differential_equations}

In the late 1950s, [Ennio De Giorgi](wikipedia://en/1752401) and [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash) independently discovered the fundamental [elliptic regularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_regularity) theory for general second-order [elliptic](wikipedia://en/662787) and [parabolic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/7466971), in which (unlike the [Schauder estimates](wikipedia://en/23547303)) no differentiability or continuity is assumed of the coefficients. In the 1960s, Moser identified a new approach to their basic regularity theory, introducing the technique of *Moser iteration*. He developed it for both elliptic and parabolic problems, and beyond recovering De Giorgi and Nash's results, he was able to use it to prove a new [Harnack inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harnack_inequality).[^o9][^o10] In his original work, a key role was played by an extension of the [John–Nirenberg lemma](wikipedia://en/4694026). [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475) later found an argument avoiding this lemma in the elliptic case, which Moser was able to adapt to the parabolic case. The collection of these regularity results are often known as De Giorgi–Nash–Moser theory, although the original results were due solely to De Giorgi and Nash.

### Differential geometry {#Differential_geometry}

In 1965, Moser found new results showing that any two [volume forms](wikipedia://en/1855811) on a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) are related to one another by scaling and pullback by a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564), so that geometrically the total volume is the only invariant of a volume form.[^o12] He was able to apply the same techniques to [symplectic forms](wikipedia://en/28356), thereby proving that a [cohomologous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomologous) family of symplectic forms are related to one another by diffeomorphisms: this is also known as [Moser's stability theorem](wikipedia://en/73989969#Application:_Moser_stability_theorem).[^o13] Moser also analyzed the case of manifolds with boundary, although his argument was mistaken. Later, with [Bernard Dacorogna](wikipedia://en/49177384), Moser fully carried out the analysis of the boundary case.

Moser also made an early contribution to the [prescribed scalar curvature problem](wikipedia://en/15901488), showing that in any [conformal class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_class) of [Riemannian metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), every function except for those which are nonpositive arises as a [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622).[^o14] Moser's prior analysis of the Moser–Trudinger inequality was important for this work, highlighting the geometric significance of optimal constants in functional inequalities.

Research of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) and [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) in the early twentieth century had clarified the two-dimensional [CR geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_geometry), dealing with three-dimensional [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101) of smooth four-dimensional manifolds which are also equipped with a [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742). They had identified local invariants distinguishing two such structures, analogous to prior work identifying the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) and its covariant derivatives as fundamental invariants of a Riemannian metric. With [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), Moser extended Poincaré and Cartan's work to arbitrary dimensions. Their work has had a significant influence on CR geometry.[^o15][^o16]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Moser won the first [George David Birkhoff Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_David_Birkhoff_Prize) in 1968 for contributions to the theory of [Hamiltonian dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/1197531), the [James Craig Watson Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Craig_Watson_Medal) in 1969 for his contributions to dynamical [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), the [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624) of the [Royal Dutch Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/43504918) in 1984, the Cantor Medal of the [Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung) in 1992 and the [Wolf Prize](wikipedia://en/714278) in 1995 for his work on stability in Hamiltonian systems and on nonlinear differential equations.

He was elected to membership of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1973 and was corresponding member of numerous foreign academies such as the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) and the Akademie der Wissenschaften und Literatur, [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz). At three occasions he was an invited speaker at the quadrennial [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), namely in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) (1962) in the section on [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) (1978) in the section on [Complex Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_Analysis),[^o18] and a plenary speaker in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) (1998).[^o19]

In 1990 he was awarded honorary doctorates from [University of Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bochum) and from [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) in Paris. The [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) established a lecture prize in his honor in 2000.
