# Contractible space

*Image: Contractibility figure.png.* Illustration of some contractible and non-contractible spaces. Spaces A, B, and C are contractible; spaces D, E, and F are not.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Contractible space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is **contractible** if the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) on *X* is null-homotopic, i.e. if it is [homotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopic) to some [constant map](wikipedia://en/320861).[^o2][^o3] Intuitively, a contractible space is one that can be continuously shrunk to a point within that space.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A contractible space is precisely one with the [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type) of a point. It follows that all the [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of a contractible space are [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059). Therefore any space with a nontrivial homotopy group cannot be contractible. Similarly, since [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) is a homotopy invariant, the [reduced homology groups](wikipedia://en/3110036) of a contractible space are all trivial.

For a nonempty topological space *X* the following are all equivalent:

- *X* is contractible (i.e. the identity map is null-homotopic).
- *X* is homotopy equivalent to a one-point space.
- *X* [deformation retracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) onto a point. (However, there exist contractible spaces which do not *strongly* deformation retract to a point.)
- For any path-connected space *Y*, any two maps *f*,*g*: *X* → *Y* are homotopic.
- For any nonempty space *Y*, any map *f*: *Y* → *X* is null-homotopic.

The [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) on a space *X* is always contractible. Therefore any space can be embedded in a contractible one (which also illustrates that subspaces of contractible spaces need not be contractible).

Furthermore, *X* is contractible [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) there exists a [retraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) from the cone of *X* to *X*.

Every contractible space is [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected) and [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected). Moreover, since all the higher homotopy groups vanish, every contractible space is [*n*-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-connected) for all *n* ≥ 0.

:::

## Locally contractible spaces {#Locally_contractible_spaces}

A topological space *X* is **locally contractible at a point** *x* if for every [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28topology%29) *U* of *x* there is a neighborhood *V* of *x* contained in *U* such that the inclusion of *V* is nulhomotopic in *U*. A space is **locally contractible** if it is locally contractible at every point. This definition is occasionally referred to as the "geometric topologist's locally contractible," though is the most common usage of the term. In [Hatcher's](wikipedia://en/1112366) standard Algebraic Topology text, this definition is referred to as "weakly locally contractible," though that term has other uses.[^o8]

If every point has a [local base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_base) of contractible neighborhoods, then we say that *X* is **strongly locally contractible**. Contractible spaces are not necessarily locally contractible nor vice versa. For example, the [comb space](wikipedia://en/17381118) is contractible but not locally contractible (if it were, it would be locally connected which it is not). Locally contractible spaces are locally *n*-connected for all *n* ≥ 0. In particular, they are [locally simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_simply_connected), [locally path connected](wikipedia://en/6959754), and [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected). The circle is (strongly) locally contractible but not contractible.

Strong local contractibility is a strictly stronger property than local contractibility; the counterexamples are sophisticated, the first being given by [Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk) and [Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203) in their paper *Sur les rétractes absolus indécomposables*, C.R.. Acad. Sci. Paris 199 (1934), 110-112).

There is some disagreement about which definition is the "standard" definition of local contractibility ; the first definition is more commonly used in geometric topology, especially historically, whereas the second definition fits better with the typical usage of the term "local" with respect to topological properties. Care should always be taken regarding the definitions when interpreting results about these properties.

:::

## Examples and counterexamples {#Examples_and_counterexamples}

- Any [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is contractible, as is any [star domain](wikipedia://en/5983414) on a Euclidean space.
- The [Whitehead manifold](wikipedia://en/1192013) is contractible.
- [Spheres](wikipedia://en/39782) of any finite dimension are not contractible, although they are [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) in dimension at least 2.
- The [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) in an infinite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) [is contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractibility_of_unit_sphere_in_Hilbert_space).
- The [house with two rooms](wikipedia://en/4446017) is a standard example of a space which is contractible, but not intuitively so.
- The [Dunce hat](wikipedia://en/5148246) is contractible, but not [collapsible](wikipedia://en/20814602).
- The cone on a [Hawaiian earring](wikipedia://en/910263) is contractible (since it is a cone), but not locally contractible or even locally simply connected.
- All [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) and [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) are *locally* contractible, but in general not contractible.
- The [Warsaw circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_circle) is obtained by "closing up" the [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) by an arc connecting (0,−1) and (1,sin(1)). It is a one-dimensional continuum whose [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) are all trivial, but it is not contractible.
