# Hans Lewy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Lewy"}

**Hans Lewy** (20 October 1904 – 23 August 1988) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and on the [theory of functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).[^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Lewy was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau), [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia), on October 20, 1904. He began his studies at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1922, after being advised to avoid the more local [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław) because it was too old-fashioned,[^o8][^o7] supporting himself during the [Weimar hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation_in_the_Weimar_Republic) by a side job doing railroad track maintenance.[^o7] At Göttingen, he studied both mathematics and physics; his teachers there included [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born), [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), [James Franck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franck), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), and [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091). He earned his doctorate in 1926, at which time he and his friend [Kurt Otto Friedrichs](wikipedia://en/3626171) both became assistants to Courant and [privatdozents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at Göttingen. The famous [Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy_condition) originated from that time in 1928.[^o8][^o7]

At the recommendation of Courant, Lewy was granted a [Rockefeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation), which he used in 1929 to travel to Rome and study [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) with [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180) and [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661), and then in 1930 to travel to Paris, where he attended the seminar of [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675). After [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler)'s election as chancellor in 1933, Lewy was advised by [Herbert Busemann](wikipedia://en/25491802) to leave Germany again. He was offered a position in Madrid, but declined it, fearing for the future there under [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco). He revisited Italy and France, but then at the invitation of the [Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Committee_in_Aid_of_Displaced_Foreign_Scholars) and with the assistance of Hadamard found a two-year position in America at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). At the end of that term, in 1935, he moved to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o8][^o7]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Lewy obtained a pilot's license, but then worked at the [Aberdeen Proving Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Proving_Ground). He married Helen Crosby in 1947.[^o7]

In 1950, Lewy was fired from Berkeley for refusing to sign a loyalty oath.[^o7][^o10][^o9] He taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1952 and 1953[^o7] before being reinstated by the [California Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Supreme_Court) case *[Tolman v. Underhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolman_v._Underhill)*.[^o10][^o9]

He retired from Berkeley in 1972, and in 1973 became one of two Ordway Professors of Mathematics at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). He died on August 23, 1988, in Berkeley.[^o7][^o10][^o11]

Lewy is known for his contributions to partial differential equations. In 1957, [his famous example](wikipedia://en/5473255) of a first-order linear partial differential operator, $L= (\partial_{x_1}+i\partial_{x_2})-2i(x_1+ix_2)\partial_{y_1}$, with complex coefficients for which the equation $Lu=f$ has no *local* solutions unless $f$ is real analytic was so stunning and unexpected that it redirected the field of partial differential equations, as well as shaping modern analysis in a significant way.[^o12] Based on this example, [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725), [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958), [François Trèves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Trèves), [Nils Dencker](wikipedia://en/31870949), etc. developed a theory of local solvability for pseudo-differential equations.

He also worked on several complex variables in relation to nonlinear hyperbolic equations and elliptic equations, well-posedness for initial value problems of wave fronts (now commonly called [Sobolev spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_spaces)) in the early 1930s, solutions of the classical problems of [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) for analytical data (the original problem was solved by Louis Nirenberg in 1949 as part of his PhD thesis), the extendibility of [minimal surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surfaces) on and analytical nature of its boundaries which is fully free or in part, free boundary problems of water wave fronts in hydrodynamics, and the proof of quadratic reciprocity theorem in number theory from 'hydrodynamical' perspective.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Lewy was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1964, and was also a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o10] He became a foreign member of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) in 1972.[^o7] He was awarded a [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) in 1979,[^o7] and a [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 1986 for his work on partial differential equations.[^o4] In 1986, the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) gave him an honorary doctorate.[^o11]
