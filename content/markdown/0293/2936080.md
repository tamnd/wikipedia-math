# Noetherian topological space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noetherian topological space"}

In mathematics, a **Noetherian topological space**, named for [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) in which closed subsets satisfy the [descending chain condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descending_chain_condition). Equivalently, we could say that the open subsets satisfy the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189), since they are the complements of the closed subsets. The Noetherian property of a topological space can also be seen as a strong [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness) condition, namely that every open subset of such a space is compact, and in fact it is equivalent to the seemingly stronger statement that *every* subset is compact.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A topological space $X$ is called **Noetherian** if it satisfies the [descending chain condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descending_chain_condition) for [closed subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset): for any [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838)

$$Y_1 \supseteq Y_2 \supseteq \cdots$$

of closed subsets $Y_i$ of $X$, there is an integer $m$ such that $Y_m=Y_{m+1}=\cdots.$

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A topological space $X$ is Noetherian if and only if every [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) of $X$ is compact (i.e., $X$ is hereditarily compact), and if and only if every open subset of $X$ is compact.[^o6]
- Every subspace of a Noetherian space is Noetherian.
- The continuous image of a Noetherian space is Noetherian.[^o7]
- A finite union of Noetherian subspaces of a topological space is Noetherian.[^o8]
- Every [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) Noetherian space is finite with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology).

Proof: *Every subset of X is compact in a Noetherian space and every compact subset is closed in a Hausdorff space; hence all subsets of X are closed and X has the discrete topology. As X is discrete and compact it must be finite.*

- Every Noetherian space *X* has a finite number of [irreducible components](wikipedia://en/2652476).[^o9] If the irreducible components are $X_1,...,X_n$, then $X=X_1\cup\cdots\cup X_n$, and none of the components $X_i$ is contained in the union of the other components.

:::

## From algebraic geometry {#From_algebraic_geometry}

Many examples of Noetherian topological spaces come from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where for the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) an [irreducible set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_set) has the intuitive property that any closed proper subset has smaller dimension. Since dimension can only 'jump down' a finite number of times, and [algebraic sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set) are made up of finite unions of irreducible sets, descending chains of Zariski closed sets must eventually be constant.

A more algebraic way to see this is that the associated [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) defining algebraic sets must satisfy the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189). That follows because the rings of algebraic geometry, in the classical sense, are [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129). This class of examples therefore also explains the name.

If *R* is a commutative Noetherian ring, then Spec(*R*), the [prime spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_spectrum) of *R*, is a Noetherian topological space. More generally, a [Noetherian scheme](wikipedia://en/2783891) is a Noetherian topological space. The converse does not hold, since there are non-Noetherian rings with only one prime ideal, so that Spec(*R*) is not a Noetherian scheme, but consists of exactly one point and therefore is a Noetherian space.

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The space $\mathbb{A}^n_k$ (affine $n$-space over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$) under the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) is an example of a Noetherian topological space. By properties of the [ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_ideal) of a subset of $\mathbb{A}^n_k$, we know that if

$$Y_1 \supseteq Y_2 \supseteq Y_3 \supseteq \cdots$$

is a descending chain of Zariski-closed subsets, then

$$I(Y_1) \subseteq I(Y_2) \subseteq I(Y_3) \subseteq \cdots$$

is an ascending chain of ideals of $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n].$ Since $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is a Noetherian ring, there exists an integer $m$ such that

$$I(Y_m)=I(Y_{m+1})=I(Y_{m + 2})=\cdots.$$

Since $V(I(Y))$ is the closure of *Y* for all *Y*, $V(I(Y_i))=Y_i$ for all $i.$ Hence

$Y_m=Y_{m+1}=Y_{m + 2}=\cdots$ as required.

:::
