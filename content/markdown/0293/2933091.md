# Klein quadric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Klein quadric"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the lines of a 3-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135), *S*, can be viewed as points of a 5-dimensional projective space, *T*. In that 5-space, the points that represent each line in *S* lie on a [quadric](wikipedia://en/145570) *Q,* known as the **Klein quadric**. Thus, the space of lines is a 4-dimensional projective variety. Its subvarieties are [line complexes](wikipedia://en/23445252).

:::

If the underlying [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of *S* is the 4-dimensional vector space *V*, then *T* has as the underlying vector space the 6-dimensional [exterior square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_square) Λ<sup>2</sup>*V* of *V*. The [line coordinates](wikipedia://en/21252759) obtained this way are known as [Plücker coordinates](wikipedia://en/1168486).

These Plücker coordinates satisfy the quadratic relation

$$p_{12} p_{34}+p_{13}p_{42}+p_{14} p_{23} = 0$$

defining *Q*, where

$$p_{ij} = u_i v_j - u_j v_i$$

are the coordinates of the line [spanned](wikipedia://en/56353) by the two vectors *u* and *v*.

The 3-space, *S*, can be reconstructed again from the quadric, *Q*: the planes contained in *Q* fall into two [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260), where planes in the same class meet in a point, and planes in different classes meet in a line or in the empty set. Let these classes be *C* and *C*′. The [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of *S* is retrieved as follows:

1. The points of *S* are the planes in *C*.
1. The lines of *S* are the points of *Q*.
1. The planes of *S* are the planes in *C*′.

The fact that the geometries of *S* and *Q* are isomorphic can be explained by the [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of the [Dynkin diagrams](wikipedia://en/277109) *A*<sub>3</sub> and *D*<sub>3</sub>.
