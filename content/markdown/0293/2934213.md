# Quasi-algebraically closed field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-algebraically closed field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* is called **quasi-algebraically closed** (or ***C*<sub>1</sub>**) if every non-constant [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) *P* over *F* has a non-trivial zero provided the number of its variables is more than its degree. The idea of quasi-algebraically closed fields was investigated by [C. C. Tsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._C._Tsen), a student of [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), in a 1936 paper (Tsen 1936); and later by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) in his 1951 [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) dissertation and in his 1952 paper (Lang 1952). The idea itself is attributed to Lang's advisor [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).

:::

Formally, if *P* is a non-constant homogeneous polynomial in variables

*X*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*N*</sub>,

and of degree *d* satisfying

*d* \< *N*

then it has a non-trivial zero over *F*; that is, for some *x*<sub>*i*</sub> in *F*, not all 0, we have

*P*(*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*N*</sub>) = 0.

In geometric language, the [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101) defined by *P*, in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of degree *N* − 2, then has a point over *F*.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) is quasi-algebraically closed. In fact, any homogeneous polynomial in at least two variables over an algebraically closed field has a non-trivial zero.[^o6]
- Any [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) is quasi-algebraically closed by the [Chevalley–Warning theorem](wikipedia://en/2934314).[^o7][^o8][^o9]
- [Algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645) of dimension 1 over algebraically closed fields are quasi-algebraically closed by [Tsen's theorem](wikipedia://en/10129446).[^o8][^o10]
- The maximal unramified extension of a complete field with a discrete valuation and a [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088) residue field is quasi-algebraically closed.[^o8]
- A complete field with a discrete valuation and an algebraically closed residue field is quasi-algebraically closed by a result of Lang.[^o8][^o11]
- A [pseudo algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/3696313) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero is quasi-algebraically closed.[^o12]

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Any [algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125) of a quasi-algebraically closed field is quasi-algebraically closed.
- The [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of a finite extension of a quasi-algebraically closed field is trivial.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
- A quasi-algebraically closed field has [cohomological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomological_dimension_of_a_field) at most 1.[^o17]

:::

## *C*<sub>*k*</sub> fields {#Ck_fields}

Quasi-algebraically closed fields are also called *C*<sub>1</sub>. A ***C*<sub>*k*</sub> field**, more generally, is one for which any homogeneous polynomial of degree *d* in *N* variables has a non-trivial zero, provided

*d*<sup>*k*</sup> \< *N*,

for *k* ≥ 1.[^o19] The condition was first introduced and studied by Lang.[^o17] If a field is *C*<sub>*i*</sub> then so is a finite extension.[^o19][^o20] The *C*<sub>0</sub> fields are precisely the algebraically closed fields.[^o25][^o21]

Lang and Nagata proved that if a field is *C*<sub>*k*</sub>, then any extension of [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) *n* is *C*<sub>*k*+*n*</sub>.[^o22][^o23][^o24] The smallest *k* such that *K* is a *C*<sub>*k*</sub> field ($\infty$ if no such number exists), is called the **[diophantine dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_dimension)** dd(*K*) of *K*.[^o25]

### *C*<sub>1</sub> fields {#C1_fields}

Every finite field is *C*<sub>1</sub>.[^o12]

### *C*<sub>2</sub> fields {#C2_fields}

::: {#o29 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

Suppose that the field *k* is *C*<sub>2</sub>.

- Any [skew field](wikipedia://en/9067) *D* finite over *k* as centre has the property that the [reduced norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_norm) *D*<sup>∗</sup> → *k*<sup>∗</sup> is surjective.[^o23]
- Every quadratic form in 5 or more variables over *k* is [isotropic](wikipedia://en/10917170).[^o23]

:::

::: {#o31 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Artin's conjecture"}

#### Artin's conjecture {#Artin's_conjecture}

Artin conjectured that [*p*-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field) were *C*<sub>2</sub>, but [Guy Terjanian](wikipedia://en/7354718) found [*p*-adic counterexamples](wikipedia://en/5911859) for all *p*.[^o32][^o33] The [Ax–Kochen theorem](wikipedia://en/5911859) applied methods from [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) to show that Artin's conjecture was true for **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> with *p* large enough (depending on *d*).

:::

### Weakly *C*<sub>*k*</sub> fields {#Weakly_Ck_fields}

A field *K* is **weakly *C*<sub>*k*,*d*</sub>** if for every homogeneous polynomial of degree *d* in *N* variables satisfying

*d*<sup>*k*</sup> \< *N*

the [Zariski closed](wikipedia://en/241744) set *V*(*f*) of **P**<sup>*n*</sup>(*K*) contains a [subvariety](wikipedia://en/248808) which is Zariski closed over *K*.

A field that is weakly *C*<sub>*k*,*d*</sub> for every *d* is **weakly *C*<sub>*k*</sub>**.[^o7]

::: {#o36 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

#### Properties {#Properties}

- A *C*<sub>*k*</sub> field is weakly *C*<sub>*k*</sub>.[^o7]
- A [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088) PAC weakly *C*<sub>*k*</sub> field is *C*<sub>*k*</sub>.[^o7]
- A field *K* is weakly *C*<sub>*k*,*d*</sub> [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) every form satisfying the conditions has a point **x** defined over a field which is a [primary extension](wikipedia://en/30505550) of *K*.[^o37]
- If a field is weakly *C*<sub>*k*</sub>, then any extension of transcendence degree *n* is weakly *C*<sub>*k*+*n*</sub>.[^o24]
- Any extension of an algebraically closed field is weakly *C*<sub>1</sub>.[^o38]
- Any field with procyclic [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) is weakly *C*<sub>1</sub>.[^o38]
- Any field of positive characteristic is weakly *C*<sub>2</sub>.[^o38]
- If the field of rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$ and the function fields $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$ are weakly *C*<sub>1</sub>, then every field is weakly *C*<sub>1</sub>.[^o38]

:::
