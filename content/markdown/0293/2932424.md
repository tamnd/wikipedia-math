# Robion Kirby

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robion Kirby"}

**Robion Cromwell Kirby** (born February 25, 1938) is a [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) who specializes in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). Together with [Laurent C. Siebenmann](wikipedia://en/13498931) he developed the [Kirby–Siebenmann invariant](wikipedia://en/2180593) for classifying the [piecewise linear structures](wikipedia://en/2174184) on a [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193). He also proved the fundamental result on the [Kirby calculus](wikipedia://en/1105826), a method for describing [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifolds) and smooth [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795) by surgery on [framed links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_link). Along with his significant mathematical contributions, he has over 50 doctoral students and is the editor of an influential problem list.[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1965, with thesis "Smoothing Locally Flat Imbeddings" written under the direction of [Eldon Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldon_Dyer). He soon became an assistant professor at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles). While there he developed his "[torus trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torus_trick)" which enabled him to solve, in dimensions greater than four (with additional joint work with Siebenmann), four of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632)'s seven most important problems in geometric topology.[^o6]

In 1971, he was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

In 1995 he became the first mathematician to receive the [NAS Award for Scientific Reviewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_for_Scientific_Reviewing) from the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) for his problem list in low-dimensional topology.[^o7] He was elected to the National Academy of Sciences in 2001. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

Kirby is also the President of [Mathematical Sciences Publishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Publishers), a small non-profit academic publishing house that focuses on mathematics and engineering journals.
