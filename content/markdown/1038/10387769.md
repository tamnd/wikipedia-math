# Tangential and normal components

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangential and normal components"}

*Image: Surface normal tangent.svg.* Illustration of tangential and normal components of a vector to a surface

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), given a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) at a point on a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246), that vector can be decomposed uniquely as a sum of two vectors, one [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the curve, called the **tangential component** of the vector, and another one [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the curve, called the **normal component** of the vector. Similarly, a vector at a point on a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) can be broken down the same way.

:::

More generally, given a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) *N* of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M*, and a vector in the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) to *M* at a point of *N*, it can be decomposed into the component tangent to *N* and the component normal to *N*.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

### Surface {#Surface}

More formally, let $S$ be a surface, and $x$ be a point on the surface. Let $\mathbf{v}$ be a vector at $x$. Then one can write uniquely $\mathbf{v}$ as a sum

$$\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_\parallel + \mathbf{v}_\perp$$

where the first vector in the sum is the tangential component and the second one is the normal component. It follows immediately that these two vectors are perpendicular to each other.

To calculate the tangential and normal components, consider a [unit normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_normal) to the surface, that is, a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) $\hat\mathbf{n}$ perpendicular to $S$ at $x$. Then,

$$\mathbf{v}_\perp = \left(\mathbf{v} \cdot \hat\mathbf{n}\right) \hat\mathbf{n}$$

and thus

$$\mathbf{v}_\parallel = \mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_\perp$$

where "$\cdot$" denotes the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093). Another formula for the tangential component is

$$\mathbf{v}_\parallel = -\hat\mathbf{n} \times (\hat\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{v}),$$

where "$\times$" denotes the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092).

These formulas do not depend on the particular unit normal $\hat\mathbf{n}$ used (there exist two unit normals to any surface at a given point, pointing in opposite directions, so one of the unit normals is the negative of the other one).

### Submanifold {#Submanifold}

More generally, given a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) *N* of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* and a point $p \in N$, we get a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) involving the [tangent spaces](wikipedia://en/30807):

$$T_p N \to T_p M \to T_p M / T_p N$$

The [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $T_p M / T_p N$ is a generalized space of normal vectors.

If *M* is a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), the above sequence [splits](wikipedia://en/1531404), and the tangent space of *M* at *p* decomposes as a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_spaces) of the component tangent to *N* and the component normal to *N*:

$$T_p M = T_p N \oplus N_p N := (T_p N)^\perp$$

Thus every [tangent vector](wikipedia://en/191999) $v \in T_p M$ splits as $v = v_\parallel + v_\perp$, where $v_\parallel \in T_p N$ and $v_\perp \in N_p N := (T_p N)^\perp$.

:::

## Computations {#Computations}

Suppose *N* is given by non-degenerate equations.

If *N* is given explicitly, via [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108) (such as a [parametric curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_curve)), then the derivative gives a spanning set for the tangent bundle (it is a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) if and only if the parametrization is an [immersion](wikipedia://en/6993953)).

If *N* is given [implicitly](wikipedia://en/1527655) (as in the above description of a surface, (or more generally as) a [hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101)) as a [level set](wikipedia://en/559622) or intersection of [level surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Level_surface) for $g_i$, then the gradients of $g_i$ span the normal space.

In both cases, we can again compute using the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093); the cross product is special to 3 dimensions however.

## Applications {#Applications}

- [Lagrange multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_multipliers): constrained [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) are where the tangential component of the [total derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_derivative) vanish.
