# Waldhausen category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Waldhausen category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Waldhausen category** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *C* equipped with some additional data, which makes it possible to construct the [K-theory](wikipedia://en/42083967) [spectrum](wikipedia://en/1033697) of *C* using a so-called **S-construction**. It's named after [Friedhelm Waldhausen](wikipedia://en/20163961), who introduced this notion (under the term **category with cofibrations and weak equivalences**) to extend the methods of [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) to categories not necessarily of algebraic origin, for example the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *C* be a category, co(*C*) and we(*C*) two classes of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) in *C*, called cofibrations and weak equivalences respectively. The triple (*C*, co(*C*), we(*C*)) is called a **Waldhausen category** if it satisfies the following axioms, motivated by the similar properties for the notions of [cofibrations](wikipedia://en/1697216) and [weak homotopy equivalences](wikipedia://en/34615176#Topological_spaces) of topological spaces:

- *C* has a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object), denoted by 0;
- [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) are included in both co(*C*) and we(*C*);
- co(*C*) and we(*C*) are closed under composition;
- for each object *A* ∈ *C* the unique map 0 → *A* is a cofibration, i.e. is an element of co(*C*);
- co(*C*) and we(*C*) are compatible with [pushouts](wikipedia://en/582024) in a certain sense.

For example, if $\scriptstyle A\, \rightarrowtail\, B$ is a cofibration and $\scriptstyle A\,\to\, C$ is any map, then there must exist a pushout $\scriptstyle B\, \cup_A\, C$, and the natural map $\scriptstyle C\, \rightarrowtail\, B\,\cup_A\, C$ should be cofibration:

## Relations with other notions {#Relations_with_other_notions}

In [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) there are several notions of categories equipped with some specified classes of morphisms. If *C* has a structure of an [exact category](wikipedia://en/10987985), then by defining we(*C*) to be isomorphisms, co(*C*) to be admissible monomorphisms, one obtains a structure of a Waldhausen category on *C*. Both kinds of structure may be used to define [K-theory](wikipedia://en/42083967) of *C*, using the [Q-construction](wikipedia://en/41403415) for an exact structure and [S-construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-construction) for a Waldhausen structure. An important fact is that the resulting K-theory spaces are homotopy equivalent.

If *C* is a [model category](wikipedia://en/2895304) with a zero object, then the full subcategory of cofibrant objects in *C* may be given a Waldhausen structure.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="S-construction"}

## S-construction {#S-construction}

The **Waldhausen S-construction** produces from a Waldhausen category *C* a sequence of [Kan complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan_complex) $S_n(C)$, which forms a [spectrum](wikipedia://en/1033697). Let $K(C)$ denote the loop space of the geometric realization $|S_*(C)|$ of $S_*(C)$. Then the [group](wikipedia://en/19447)

$$\pi_n K(C) = \pi_{n+1} |S_*(C)|$$

is the *n*-th *K*-group of *C*. Thus, it gives a way to define higher *K*-groups. Another approach for higher *K*-theory is [Quillen's Q-construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quillen's_Q-construction).

The construction is due to [Friedhelm Waldhausen](wikipedia://en/20163961).

:::

## biWaldhausen categories {#biWaldhausen_categories}

A category *C* is equipped with bifibrations if it has cofibrations and its [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) *C*<sup>OP</sup> has so also. In that case, we denote the fibrations of *C*<sup>OP</sup> by quot(*C*). In that case, *C* is a **biWaldhausen category** if *C* has [bifibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifibration) and weak equivalences such that both (*C*, co(*C*), we) and (*C*<sup>OP</sup>, quot(*C*), we<sup>OP</sup>) are Waldhausen categories.

Waldhausen and biWaldhausen categories are linked with [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500). There, many interesting categories are complicial biWaldhausen categories. For example: The category $\scriptstyle C^b(\mathcal{A})$ of bounded chain complexes on an exact category $\scriptstyle \mathcal{A}$. The category $\scriptstyle S_n \mathcal{C}$ of functors $\scriptstyle \operatorname{Ar}(\Delta ^n)\, \to\, \mathcal{C}$ when $\scriptstyle\mathcal{C}$ is so. And given a diagram $\scriptstyle I$, then $\scriptstyle \mathcal{C}^I$ is a nice complicial biWaldhausen category when $\scriptstyle \mathcal{C}$ is.
