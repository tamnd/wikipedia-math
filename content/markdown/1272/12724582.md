# Modulus and characteristic of convexity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Modulus and characteristic of convexity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **modulus of convexity** and the **characteristic of convexity** are measures of "how [convex](wikipedia://en/6292)" the [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is. In some sense, the modulus of convexity has the same relationship to the *ε*-*δ* definition of [uniform convexity](wikipedia://en/9133418) as the [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) does to the *ε*-*δ* definition of [continuity](wikipedia://en/6122).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The **modulus of convexity** of a Banach space (*X*, ||⋅||) is the function *δ* : \[0, 2\] → \[0, 1\] defined by

$$\delta (\varepsilon) = \inf \left\{ 1 - \left\| \frac{x + y}{2} \right\| \,:\, x, y \in S, \| x - y \| \geq \varepsilon \right\},$$

where *S* denotes the unit sphere of (*X*, || ||). In the definition of *δ*(*ε*), one can as well take the infimum over all vectors *x*, *y* in *X* such that ǁ*x*ǁ, ǁ*y*ǁ ≤ 1 and ǁ*x* − *y*ǁ ≥ *ε*.[^o4]

The **characteristic of convexity** of the space (*X*, || ||) is the number *ε*<sub>0</sub> defined by

$$\varepsilon_{0} = \sup \{ \varepsilon \,:\, \delta(\varepsilon) = 0 \}.$$

These notions are implicit in the general study of uniform convexity by J. A. Clarkson ((Clarkson 1936); this is the same paper containing the statements of [Clarkson's inequalities](wikipedia://en/13409455)). The term "modulus of convexity" appears to be due to M. M. Day.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The modulus of convexity, *δ*(*ε*), is a [non-decreasing](wikipedia://en/48260) function of *ε*, and the quotient *δ*(*ε*) / *ε* is also non-decreasing on (0, 2\].[^o9] The modulus of convexity need not itself be a [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) of *ε*.[^o10] However, the modulus of convexity is equivalent to a convex function in the following sense:[^o11] there exists a convex function *δ*<sub>1</sub>(*ε*) such that

$$\delta(\varepsilon / 2) \le \delta_1(\varepsilon) \le \delta(\varepsilon), \quad \varepsilon \in [0, 2].$$

- The normed space (*X*, ǁ ⋅ ǁ) is [uniformly convex](wikipedia://en/9133418) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *δ*(*ε*) > 0 for every *ε* > 0.
- The Banach space (*X*, ǁ ⋅ ǁ) is a [strictly convex space](wikipedia://en/12740746) (i.e., the boundary of the unit ball *B* contains no line segments) if and only if *δ*(2) = 1, *i.e.*, if only [antipodal points](wikipedia://en/367577) (of the form *x* and *y* = −*x*) of the unit sphere can have distance equal to 2.
- When *X* is uniformly convex, it admits an equivalent norm with power type modulus of convexity.[^o12] Namely, there exists *q* ≥ 2 and a constant *c* > 0 such that

$$\delta(\varepsilon) \ge c \, \varepsilon^q, \quad \varepsilon \in [0, 2].$$

:::

## Modulus of convexity of the *L*<sup>*P*</sup> spaces {#Modulus_of_convexity_of_the_LP_spaces}

The modulus of convexity is known for the *L*<sup>*P*</sup> spaces.[^o14] If $1<p\le2$, then it satisfies the following implicit equation:

$$\left(1-\delta_p(\varepsilon)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^p+\left(1-\delta_p(\varepsilon)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^p=2.$$

Knowing that $\delta_p(\varepsilon+)=0,$ one can suppose that $\delta_p(\varepsilon)=a_0\varepsilon+a_1\varepsilon^2+\cdots$. Substituting this into the above, and expanding the left-hand-side as a [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) around $\varepsilon=0$, one can calculate the $a_i$ coefficients:

$$\delta_p(\varepsilon)=\frac{p-1}{8}\varepsilon^2+\frac{1}{384}(3-10p+9p^2-2p^3)\varepsilon^4+\cdots.$$

For $2<p<\infty$, one has the explicit expression

$$\delta_p(\varepsilon)=1-\left(1-\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^p\right)^{\frac1p}.$$

Therefore, $\delta_p(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{p2^p}\varepsilon^p+\cdots$.
