# Peter Constantin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Constantin"}

**Peter S. Constantin** (born 29 August 1951) is a Romanian-American mathematician known for his work on [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034). His research focuses on mathematical aspects of hydrodynamics, including the [Euler equations](wikipedia://en/396022), the [Navier–Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395), and the theory of [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664). He is the John von Neumann Professor of Mathematics and Applied and Computational Mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o3] He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2021.[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Constantin studied mathematics at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), receiving his B.A. in 1974 and an M.A. *summa cum laude* in 1975. He later emigrated to Israel and received his Ph.D. from the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) under the supervision of [Shmuel Agmon](wikipedia://en/32542224). His doctoral dissertation was titled *Spectral Properties of Schrödinger Operators in Domains with Infinite Boundaries*.[^o6]

In 1985 he joined the faculty of the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) as an assistant professor. He became professor of mathematics in 1988 and later held the titles of Louis Block Professor and Louis Block Distinguished Service Professor. From 2007 to 2011 he served as chair of the department of mathematics.[^o7]

Constantin joined [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2011 as the William R. Kenan Jr. Professor of Mathematics and Applied and Computational Mathematics and later became John von Neumann Professor.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Constantin’s research concerns nonlinear partial differential equations arising in fluid dynamics and mathematical physics. His work addresses turbulence theory, scaling laws in hydrodynamics, intermittency, turbulent transport, and the mathematical analysis of the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations.[^o3]

He has contributed to the study of active scalar equations, nonlocal models in fluid mechanics, and problems related to the existence and regularity of solutions of fluid-dynamics equations. Constantin also introduced mathematical tools such as local smoothing estimates for dispersive equations and developed approaches to the analysis of generalized Lyapunov exponents and attractor dimensions for the Navier–Stokes equations.[^o9]

In 1994, Constantin, together with [Weinan E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weinan_E) and [Edriss Titi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edriss_Titi), proved that weak solutions of the Euler equations with Hölder regularity greater than one third conserve energy, establishing the energy-conservation side of Onsager's conjecture.[^o10] The complementary part of the conjecture, concerning non-conservation below this regularity threshold, was proved by [Philip Isett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Isett) in 2018.[^o11]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- He was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1994 in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich).[^o7]
- He is a Fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) and an inaugural Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]
- He was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2010 and to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2021.[^o9][^o4]
