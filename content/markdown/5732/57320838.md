# Henry Felix Kaiser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Felix Kaiser"}

**Henry Felix Kaiser** (June 7, 1927 – January 14, 1992) was an American psychologist and educator who worked in the fields of psychometrics and statistical psychology. He developed the [Varimax rotation](wikipedia://en/15714607) method and the [Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin test](wikipedia://en/67689502) for [factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492) in the late 1950s.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

He was born in [Morristown, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morristown,_New_Jersey). Kaiser studied psychology at the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California), with a break in military service, where in 1956 he graduated with a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Psychological and Educational Statistics. In 1957 he was appointed assistant professor at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois), where he became a professor in 1962. In 1965 he went to the University of Wisconsin as a professor of educational psychology. In 1968, he received an invitation to the University of California, Berkeley, which he accepted and where he retired in 1984.[^o5]

Kaiser provided fundamental contributions to psychometrics and statistical psychology.[^o6] His contributions to [factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492) were central. Kaiser was president of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) and the [Society of Multivariate Experimental Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Multivariate_Experimental_Psychology) and publisher of the journal *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)*.[^o7]

Kaiser was married and had two sons and a daughter.[^o5] He died in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).
