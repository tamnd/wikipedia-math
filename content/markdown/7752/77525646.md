# Laurence Patrick Lee

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Laurence Patrick Lee"}

**Laurence Patrick "Laurie" Lee** (1913 – 28 January 1985) was a New Zealand mathematician, [geodesist](wikipedia://en/12608), and [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) who was the Chief [Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) for the [Department of Lands and Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Lands_and_Survey) and one of the foremost experts on (especially [conformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_map_projection)) [map projections](wikipedia://en/51784).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Lee was born in England in 1913, but moved with his family to [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland), New Zealand at a young age. After earning a Bachelor of Science degree from the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), he took a job in 1934 in the [Department of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Department_of_Public_Works) in [Whangārei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whangārei), then transferred in 1936 to the Department of Lands and Survey in Auckland as a [draughting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_drawing) cadet. Because of his mathematical talents, in 1941 he was sent to [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington) as a [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29), where he remained until his retirement in 1974, serving as the Chief Computer for the department from 1964 to 1974. After retirement he continued consulting for the department.

Lee had a [stammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stammer) since childhood. In 1950, after reading about research psychologist William Kerr of [Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey), who claimed to have discovered a cure, Lee took a leave of absence from the Department of Lands and Survey and worked as an engineer's steward in return for passage to England on the [*Trojan Star*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Star_Line). Kerr's method involved pronouncing each syllable separately with a slight pause between, in a regular rhythm, with a result "described as mechanical, stilted, and artificial". According to a newspaper report, after staying with Kerr for two weeks Lee considered himself effectively cured, with only a slight occasional stammer remaining.

Lee was a lifelong [bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor).

## Work {#Work}

*Image: Lee Conformal World in a Tetrahedron projection.png.* Lee's [Conformal Tetrahedric Projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_conformal_world_in_a_tetrahedron) can be computed using [Dixon elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/51402829).

At the Department of Lands and Survey, Lee was involved with completing the First Order Geodetic [Triangulation](wikipedia://en/50691950) of New Zealand, and establishing the [Geodetic Datum](wikipedia://en/995417) 1949; the change to [metric units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units); and computations for the [latitude and longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude_and_longitude) program of the [International Geophysical Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Geophysical_Year), 1957–1959.

Lee was a specialist in [map projections](wikipedia://en/51784) – especially [conformal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_map_projection), which preserve angles and local shapes – and wrote many papers on the subject. Notably, he developed improved methods for calculating the [transverse Mercator projection](wikipedia://en/639245); developed a conformal projection of the Pacific Ocean minimising scale errors; and computed new conformal [polyhedral map projections](wikipedia://en/69388599) using [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939), building on the work of [Oscar S. Adams](wikipedia://en/77543256). His 1976 monograph *Conformal Projections Based on Elliptic Functions* is still a definitive survey. His 1944 proposal for classifying map projections has been widely adopted and built upon.

Lee joined New Zealand's [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society_of_New_Zealand) in 1948 and was Director of the Society's Computing Section from 1954 to 1972. From 1974 to 1977 he was an editor for the Society's quarterly journal, *[Southern Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Stars_%28journal%29)*. He was a founding member of the New Zealand Institute of Draughtsmen and edited its journal from 1947 to 1950, a founding member of the New Zealand Cartographic Society, and was made an honorary member of the New Zealand Institute of Surveyors in 1971.
