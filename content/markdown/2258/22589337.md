# Fritz Joachim Weyl

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fritz Joachim Weyl"}

*Image: WeylFamily1932 MFO.jpg.* 1932 near Göttingen, with his mother Helene (left) and father [Hermann](wikipedia://en/187544) (mid)

**Fritz Joachim Weyl** (February 19, 1915 – July 20, 1977) was a mathematician born in [Zurich, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich,_Switzerland).[^o2] He contributed to mathematics research and taught at several universities, most notably at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Fritz Joachim Weyl was the son of mathematician [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and the author and translator [Helene Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helene_Weyl). Fritz received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) in 1935. At [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Fritz received a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1937 and a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1939. His Ph.D. [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) at Princeton was entitled *Analytic Curves*. [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847) served as his thesis advisor and oversaw his research while Fritz was studying at Princeton.

## Career {#Career}

Weyl taught at a number of different universities during his life. These include the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois); the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park); [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University); and [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University). In addition to teaching, Weyl was employed as a research analyst by the U.S. government. He went on to serve as the dean of science and mathematics at [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City).

Weyl served as the president of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) from 1960 to 1961.[^o6]

## Other work {#Other_work}

Both Fritz Joachim Weyl and his father Hermann Weyl published mathematical research.[^o8] In the 1970s, [Constance Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_Reid) wrote books about [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), where Hermann Weyl is mentioned many times while Fritz is mentioned only once, on page 381 in the volume about Courant. In 1949, Hermann published a book entitled *Philosophy of Mathematics and Natural Science*. This work was originally published by Princeton University Press. Fritz and Hermann published a book together called *Meromorphic Functions and Analytic Curves*.[^o9][^o10] This work is believed to be based on notes from a course given at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) during the first term of 1942–1943. While Fritz was working for the U.S. government, he compiled what is known as *Research in the service of national purpose; proceedings of the Office of Naval Research Vicennial Convocation*, in 1966. This work is available at the National Museum of American History located at the [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian). Also, many researchers have cited Weyl's work when conducting their own. For example, his work is cited as a reference in the publication *The Philosophical Review*.

When Fritz died on July 20, 1977, the mathematics community considered the loss devastating. On October 8, 1977, the board of trustees for the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) issued a memoriam. In part, it read: "...in warm recognition of his enduring vision for both the beauties and the practicalities of applied mathematics...the members and officers of SIAM and SIMS offer their heartfelt tribute".
