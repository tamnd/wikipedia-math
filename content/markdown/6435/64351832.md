# Leifur Ásgeirsson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leifur Ásgeirsson"}

**Leifur Ásgeirsson** (25 May 1903 – 19 August 1990) was the first Icelandic mathematician to gain major international recognition.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Leifur Ásgeirsson graduated in 1927 from [Reykjavik Junior College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reykjavik_Junior_College) and received his doctorate in 1933 from the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). His doctoral advisor was [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238). From 1931 to 1943 Leifur Ásgeirsson was the head of a district school in [Laugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Þingeyjarsveit), located in southwestern Iceland's Reykjadalur Valley east of [Reykjavík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reykjavík).[^o2] In 1936 he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Oslo.[^o4] At the [University of Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iceland) he was appointed in 1943 a lecturer in mathematics and in 1945 a full professor of mathematics.[^o2]

The journal *Mathematica Scandinavica* was founded in 1953 with Ásgeirsson as one of the founding editors.[^o5]

... *Mathematica Scandinavica* ... editors were appointed. From Denmark: [Fenchel](wikipedia://en/4784468), from Finland: [Gustav Elfving](wikipedia://en/31600984), from Iceland: Leifur Ásgeirsson, from Norway: [Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765), and from Sweden: [Pleijel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Åke_Pleijel). Immediately after the decisions had been taken Fenchel, in agreement with Pleijel, wrote to the other editors and suggested Bundgaard as co-ordinating editor of *Math. Scand.*[^o5]
