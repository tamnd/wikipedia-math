# Anna Erschler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anna Erschler"}

**Anna Gennadievna Erschler**, née Dyubina (Анна Геннадьевна Эршлер; born 14 February 1977), is a Russian mathematician working in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France). She specializes in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), in particular, [random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) on groups.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Beginning in 1994, Erschler studied mathematics at [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), receiving her [M.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.Sc.) in 1999 and then studying in the academic year 1999–2000 at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University). In 2001, she received her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from Saint Petersburg State University under the direction of [Anatoly Vershik](wikipedia://en/9036441), with a thesis titled *Geometric und probabilistic properties of wreath products*. In October 2012, she received her habilitation (Thèse d'État) from the [University of Paris 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_11).[^o2]

She was a postdoc in the academic year 2001–2002 at the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute) in Saint Petersburg and in the academic year 2002–2003 at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) in Paris and at IRMAR in [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes). From October 2003 to December 2005, she was *Chargée de recherche* at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique), [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille). At CNRS, University Paris 11, she was from January 2006 to September 2013 *Chargée de recherché*, and then from October 2013 to April 2013, *Directrice de recherche*. From May 2014, she has been *Directrice de recherche* at CNRS, DMA/ENS, Orsay.[^o2]

She is a co-editor of the journal *Groups, Geometry and Dynamics*.

## Work {#Work}

In an early work, Erschler proved that all [asymptotic cones](wikipedia://en/18609360#Asymptotic_cones) of a [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) are [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914), and that this property characterises hyperbolic spaces.[^o6] She also did groundbreaking work on the *drift* of a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) in [finitely generated groups](wikipedia://en/963042), namely estimating at what distance from the identity will a particule walking randomly on the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of a group be after $n$ steps, giving new examples with unexpected behaviors.[^o7]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Erschler received in 2001 the Möbius Prize of the [Independent University of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_University_of_Moscow) and in 2002 the Annual Prize of the Saint Petersburg Mathematical Society. In 2010, she was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad), where she gave a talk on *Poisson–Furstenberg boundaries, large-scale geometry and growth of groups*. In the summer of 2010 at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) she was the ninth [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) visiting professor, lecturing on random walks and Poisson–Furstenberg boundaries.[^o9] In 2015, she received the [Élie Cartan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Cartan_Prize) of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). In 2020, she won the [CNRS Silver Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Silver_Medal).[^o10]
