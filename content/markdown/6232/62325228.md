# Milnor–Wood inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milnor–Wood inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **Milnor–Wood inequality** is an obstruction to endow circle bundles over surfaces with a flat structure. It is named after [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) and John W. Wood.

:::

## Flat bundles {#Flat_bundles}

For [linear bundles](wikipedia://en/276524), [flatness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_vector_bundle) is defined as the vanishing of the curvature form of an associated [connection](wikipedia://en/391816). An arbitrary smooth (or topological) *d*-dimensional [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) is flat if it can be endowed with a [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) of codimension d that is transverse to the fibers.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="The inequality"}

## The inequality {#The_inequality}

The Milnor–Wood inequality is named after two separate results that were proven by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) and John W. Wood. Both of them deal with orientable circle bundles over a [closed oriented surface](wikipedia://en/27865) $\Sigma_g$ of positive genus *g*.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Milnor, 1958)"}

**Theorem (Milnor, 1958)[^o6]** Let $\pi\colon E \to \Sigma_g$ be a flat oriented linear circle bundle. Then the [Euler number](wikipedia://en/2932393) of the bundle satisfies $|e(\pi)| \leq g -1$.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Wood, 1971)"}

**Theorem (Wood, 1971)[^o8]** Let $\pi\colon E \to \Sigma_g$ be a flat oriented topological circle bundle. Then the [Euler number](wikipedia://en/2932393) of the bundle satisfies $|e(\pi)| \leq 2g -2$.

Wood's theorem implies Milnor's older result, as the homomorphism $\pi_1:\Sigma\to SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ classifying the linear flat circle bundle gives rise to a topological circle bundle via the 2-fold [covering map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_map) $SL(2,\mathbb{R})\to PSL(2,\mathbb{R}) \subset \operatorname{Homeo}^+(S^1)$, doubling the Euler number.

Either of these two statements can be meant by referring to the Milnor–Wood inequality.

:::
