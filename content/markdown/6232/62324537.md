# William S. Burnside

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William S. Burnside"}

**William Snow Burnside** (20 December 1839 – 11 March 1920) was an Irish mathematician whose entire career was spent at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) (TCD). He is chiefly remembered for the book *The Theory of Equations: With an Introduction to the Theory of Binary Algebraic Forms* (1881)[^o3] and his long tenure as [Erasmus Smith's Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Smith's_Professor_of_Mathematics) at TCD. He is sometimes confused with his rough contemporary, the English mathematician [William Burnside](wikipedia://en/251883).[^o4]

:::

William Snow Burnside was born at Corcreevy House, near [Fivemiletown, Tyrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fivemiletown,_Tyrone), to William Smyth Burnside (1810–1884, Chancellor of [Clogher Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clogher_Cathedral)) and Anne Henderson (1808–1881).[^o5] He studied mathematics under [George Salmon](wikipedia://en/589203) at TCD (BA 1861, MA 1866, Fellowship 1871), and taught there until his retirement in 1917. He served as Erasmus Smiths's Professor of Mathematics for many decades (1879–1913), and co-authored the influential 1881 book *The Theory of Equations: With an Introduction to the Theory of Binary Algebraic Forms* with his TCD colleague Arthur William Panton (1843–1906). It ran to at least 7 editions, and was reissued by [Dover Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_Books) in 1960. TCD awarded him a DSc in 1891. He lived one and a half miles away from campus, on [Raglan Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raglan_Road,_Dublin), and was allegedly "the last man to regularly arrive in College on horseback".[^o6]
