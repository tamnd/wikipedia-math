# Walther graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walther graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Walther graph**, also called the **Tutte fragment**, is a planar bipartite graph with 25 vertices and 31 edges named after [Hansjoachim Walther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansjoachim_Walther). It has [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, girth 3 and diameter 8.

:::

If the single vertex of degree 1 whose neighbour has degree 3 is removed, the resulting graph has no [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437). This property was used by Tutte when combining three Walther graphs to produce the [Tutte graph](wikipedia://en/24511700),[^o4] the first known counterexample to [Tait's conjecture](wikipedia://en/52435) that every 3-regular polyhedron has a Hamiltonian cycle.[^o5]

By integrating these subgraphs into a complex 46-vertex structure, Tutte demonstrated that certain 3-regular planar graphs lack the symmetry required for a Hamiltonian cycle. This construction effectively bridged the gap between local vertex connectivity and the global properties of polyhedral graphs.

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The Walther graph is an identity graph; its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059).

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Walther graph is :

$$\begin{aligned} x^3 \left(x^{22} \right. & {} -31 x^{20}+411 x^{18}-3069 x^{16}+14305 x^{14}-43594 x^{12} \\ & \left. {} +88418 x^{10}-119039 x^8+103929 x^6-55829 x^4+16539 x^2-2040\right) \end{aligned}$$

:::
