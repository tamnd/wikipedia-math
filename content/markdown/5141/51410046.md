# Folded-t and half-t distributions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Folded-t and half-t distributions"}

In statistics, the **folded-*t*** and **half-*t* distributions** are derived from [Student's *t*-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375) by taking the [absolute values](wikipedia://en/991) of variates. This is analogous to the [folded-normal](wikipedia://en/6260959) and the [half-normal](wikipedia://en/15906455) [statistical distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_distribution) being derived from the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The **folded non-standardized *t* distribution** is the distribution of the absolute value of the non-standardized *t* distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom; its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is given by:

$$g\left(x\right)\;=\;\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\sqrt{\nu\pi\sigma^2}}\left\lbrace \left[1+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\left(x-\mu\right)^2}{\sigma^2}\right]^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}}+\left[1+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{\left(x+\mu\right)^2}{\sigma^2}\right]^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} \right\rbrace \qquad(\mbox{for}\quad x \geq 0).$$

The **half-*t* distribution** results as the special case of $\mu=0$, and the standardized version as the special case of $\sigma=1$.

If $\mu=0$, the folded-*t* distribution reduces to the special case of the half-*t* distribution. Its [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) then simplifies to

$$g\left(x\right)\;=\;\frac{2\;\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\sqrt{\nu\pi\sigma^2}} \left(1+\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{x^2}{\sigma^2}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}} \qquad(\mbox{for}\quad x \geq 0).$$

The half-*t* distribution's first two [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) ([expectation](wikipedia://en/9653) and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344)) are given by:[^o4]

$$\operatorname{E}[X]\;=\;2\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\nu}{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\nu+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2})\,(\nu-1)} \qquad\mbox{for}\quad \nu > 1,$$

and

$$\operatorname{Var}(X)\;=\;\sigma^2\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu-2}-\frac{4\nu}{\pi(\nu-1)^2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\nu+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2})}\right)^2\right) \qquad\mbox{for}\quad \nu > 2.$$

:::

## Relation to other distributions {#Relation_to_other_distributions}

Folded-*t* and half-*t* generalize the *[folded normal](wikipedia://en/6260959)* and *[half-normal distributions](wikipedia://en/15906455)* by allowing for finite [degrees-of-freedom](wikipedia://en/2321606) (the [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) analogues constitute the limiting cases of infinite degrees-of-freedom). Since the [Cauchy distribution](wikipedia://en/7003) constitutes the special case of a [Student-*t* distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student-t_distribution) with one degree of freedom, the families of folded and half-*t* distributions include the **folded Cauchy distribution** and **half-Cauchy distributions** for $\nu=1$.
