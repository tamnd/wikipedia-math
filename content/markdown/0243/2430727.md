# Subsolar point

*Image: Lahaina\_noon\_subsolar\_point.svg.* The subsolar point at Honolulu during [Lahaina Noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina_Noon) with the range of possible subsolar points shaded in pink – the angle between the Sun and the local horizontal level is exactly 90° at the subsolar point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subsolar point"}

The **subsolar point** on a [planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) or a [moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_satellite) is the point at which its [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) is perceived to be directly overhead (at the [zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith));[^o2] that is, where the Sun's [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28optics%29) strike the planet exactly [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to its surface. The subsolar point occurs at the location on a planet or a moon where the Sun [culminates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culminate) at the location's [zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith). This occurs at [solar noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_noon). At this point, the Sun's rays will fall exactly vertical relative to an object on the ground and thus cast no observable [shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow).[^o3]

:::

To an observer on a planet with an [orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) and [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_rotation) similar to those of [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228), the subsolar point will appear to move westward with a speed of 1600 km/h, completing one circuit around the [globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe) each day, approximately moving along the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). However, it will also move [north and south](wikipedia://en/8612) between the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) over the course of a year, so will appear to spiral like a [helix](wikipedia://en/179924).

The term subsolar point can also mean the point closest to the Sun on an [astronomical object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object), even though the Sun might not be visible.

## On Earth {#On_Earth}

*Image: Qibla\_observation\_by\_shadows\_map.svg.* On this [azimuthal equidistant projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azimuthal_equidistant_projection) centred on Mecca, when the subsolar point is on the Ka'bah, shadows of vertical poles on the sunlit hemisphere point away from it

On Earth, the subsolar point only occurs within the [tropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics). The subsolar point contacts the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer) on the [June solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_solstice) and the [Tropic of Capricorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Capricorn) on the [December solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_solstice). The subsolar point crosses the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) on the [March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_equinox) and [September equinoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_equinox).

### Coordinates of the subsolar point {#Coordinates_of_the_subsolar_point}

The subsolar point moves constantly on the surface of the Earth, but for any given time, its coordinates, or [latitude](wikipedia://en/17616) and [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude), can be calculated as follows:[^o6]

$$\phi_{s} = \delta,$$

$$\lambda_{s} = -15(T_\mathrm{GMT}-12+E_\mathrm{min}/60).$$

where

- $\phi_{s}$ is the latitude of the subsolar point in degrees,
- $\lambda_{s}$ is the longitude of the subsolar point in degrees,
- $\delta$ is the [declination of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declination_of_the_Sun) in degrees,
- $T_\mathrm{GMT}$ is the [Greenwich Mean Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Mean_Time) or [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC), in decimal hours since 00:00:00 UTC on the relevant date
- $E_\mathrm{min}$ is the [equation of time](wikipedia://en/438948) in minutes.

### Specific observations {#Specific_observations}

- [Qibla observation by shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qibla_observation_by_shadows), when the subsolar point passes through the [Ka'bah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka'bah) in [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia), allowing the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) sacred direction to be found by observing shadows.
- [Lahaina Noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaina_Noon), when the point passes through [Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii), which is the only [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in which this happens.[^o9]
