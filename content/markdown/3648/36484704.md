# Bulk queue

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bulk queue"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), a **bulk queue**[^o2] (sometimes **batch queue**[^o3]) is a general [queueing model](wikipedia://en/50578) where jobs arrive in and/or are served in groups of random size.[^o4] Batch arrivals have been used to describe large deliveries[^o5] and batch services to model a hospital out-patient department holding a clinic once a week,[^o15] a transport link with fixed capacity[^o6][^o7] and an elevator.[^o8]

:::

Networks of such queues are known to have a [product form stationary distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_form_stationary_distribution) under certain conditions.[^o9] Under heavy traffic conditions a bulk queue is known to behave like a [reflected Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/35046943).[^o10][^o11]

## Kendall's notation {#Kendall's_notation}

In [Kendall's notation](wikipedia://en/7345405) for single queueing nodes, the random variable denoting bulk arrivals or service is denoted with a superscript, for example M<sup>*X*</sup>/M<sup>*Y*</sup>/1 denotes an [M/M/1 queue](wikipedia://en/14032611) where the arrivals are in batches determined by the random variable *X* and the services in bulk determined by the random variable *Y*. In a similar way, the [GI/G/1 queue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GI/G/1_queue) is extended to GI<sup>*X*</sup>/G<sup>*Y*</sup>/1.[^o2]

## Bulk service {#Bulk_service}

Customers arrive at random instants according to a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) and form a single queue, from the front of which batches of customers (typically with a fixed maximum size[^o14]) are served at a rate with independent distribution.[^o15] The equilibrium distribution, mean and variance of queue length are known for this model.[^o15]

The optimal maximum size of batch, subject to operating cost constraints, can be modelled as a [Markov decision process](wikipedia://en/1125883).[^o16]
