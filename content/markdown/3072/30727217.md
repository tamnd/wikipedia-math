# Sub-Stonean space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sub-Stonean space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **sub-Stonean space** is a [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) such that any two open [σ-compact](wikipedia://en/346681) disjoint subsets have disjoint compact closures. Related, an **F-space**, introduced by (Gillman Henriksen 1956), is a [completely regular](wikipedia://en/30554) Hausdorff space for which every finitely generated [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of the ring of real-valued continuous functions is [principal](wikipedia://en/145126), or equivalently every real-valued continuous function $f$ can be written as $f=g|f|$ for some real-valued continuous function $g$. When dealing with [compact spaces](wikipedia://en/6042), the two concepts are the same, but in general, the concepts are different. The relationship between the sub-Stonean spaces and F-spaces is studied in Henriksen and Woods, 1989.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

[Rickart spaces](wikipedia://en/30727109) and the [corona sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corona_set) of locally compact σ-compact Hausdorff spaces are sub-Stonean spaces.
