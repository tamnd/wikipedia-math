# Cramér's decomposition theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cramér's decomposition theorem"}

**Cramér's decomposition theorem** is a result in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). It is well known that if [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ are [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) and [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462), then their sum is also normally distributed. It turns out that the converse statement is also true. This result was anticipated by [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462) and proved by [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129).[^o2] As a consequence, a new direction in probability theory emerged—the theory of decompositions of random variables into independent summands (the arithmetic of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543)[^o3]).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let a random variable $\xi$ have a normal distribution and admit a representation as a sum of two independent random variables, $\xi=\xi_1+\xi_2$. Then the random variables $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ are also normally distributed.

The proof of the theorem is based on the theory of [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372). It follows from the Cramér decomposition theorem that the normal distribution belongs to the [Linnik class $I_0$](wikipedia://en/83308793).

:::

## Generalizations to locally compact Abelian groups {#Generalizations_to_locally_compact_Abelian_groups}

The following theorem gives a complete description of locally compact Abelian groups on which Cramér's decomposition theorem holds.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem**,[^o8] see also.[^o9] Let $\xi$, $\xi_1$, and $\xi_2$ be random variables taking values in a second countable locally compact Abelian group $X$. Suppose that $\xi$ has a [Gaussian distribution](wikipedia://en/66573106) and that $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ are independent. Then the equality $\xi=\xi_1+\xi_2$ implies that $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$ also have Gaussian distributions if and only if the group $X$ contains no subgroups topologically isomorphic to the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177).

:::
