# Free choice inference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free choice inference"}

paradox of free choice **Free choice** is a phenomenon in natural language where a linguistic [disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction) appears to receive a logical [conjunctive](wikipedia://en/18152) interpretation when it interacts with a [modal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_modality) operator. For example, the following English sentences can be interpreted to mean that the addressee can watch a movie *and* that they can also play video games, depending on their preference:[^o2]

:::

1. You can watch a movie or play video games.
1. You can watch a movie or you can play video games.

Free choice inferences are a major topic of research in [formal semantics](wikipedia://en/31395652) and [philosophical logic](wikipedia://en/304939) because they are not [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) in classical systems of [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365). If they were valid, then the semantics of natural language would validate the *Free Choice Principle*.

1. *Free Choice Principle*: $\Diamond( P \lor Q) \rightarrow (\Diamond P \land \Diamond Q)$

This symbolic logic formula above is not valid in classical [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365): Adding this principle as an axiom to standard modal logics would allow one to conclude $\Diamond Q$ from $\Diamond P$, for any $P$ and $Q$. This observation is known as the *Paradox of Free Choice*.[^o2][^o3] To resolve this paradox, some researchers have proposed analyses of free choice within nonclassical frameworks such as [dynamic semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_semantics), [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675), [alternative semantics](wikipedia://en/66422858), and [inquisitive semantics](wikipedia://en/58208186).[^o2][^o4][^o5] Others have proposed ways of deriving free choice inferences as [scalar implicatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_implicature) which arise on the basis of [classical](wikipedia://en/55868) lexical entries for disjunction and modality.[^o2][^o6][^o7][^o8]

Free choice inferences are most widely studied for [deontic modals](wikipedia://en/1625633), but also arise with other flavors of modality as well as [imperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperative_mood), [conditionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_sentence), and other kinds of operators.[^o2][^o9][^o10][^o5] [Indefinite noun phrases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_article) give rise to a similar inference which is also referred to as "free choice" though researchers disagree as to whether it forms a [natural class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_class) with disjunctive free choice.[^o10][^o11]
