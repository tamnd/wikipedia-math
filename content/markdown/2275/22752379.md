# Stanislav Smirnov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stanislav Smirnov"}

**Stanislav Konstantinovich Smirnov** (Станисла́в Константи́нович Cмирно́в; born 3 September 1970) is a Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) currently working as a professor at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2010. His research involves [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Stanislav Smirnov graduated [Saint Petersburg Lyceum 239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Lyceum_239), a highly selective high school, which is alma-mater for many well-known mathematicians. During his high-school years, he won two perfect scores in [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) 1986 and 1987. He received his undergraduate and master degree in mathematics from the [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University), one of the top universities in Russia, in 1992. In 1996, he defended his Ph.D. thesis, *Spectral Analysis of Julia Sets*, at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech), with [Nikolai Makarov](wikipedia://en/56789183) as his primary thesis advisor.[^o6]

After spending time at Yale University and Princeton's [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) as a postdoc, Smirnov joined the faculty of the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) in 1998. In 2003, he become a professor in the Analysis, Mathematical Physics and Probability group at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva).[^o13][^o7]

In 2010, Smirnov became the founding director of the Chebyshev Laboratory in [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) in Russia.

## Research {#Research}

Smirnov has worked on [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805), where he proved [Cardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cardy)'s formula for critical site percolation on the [triangular lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_lattice), and deduced [conformal invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformally_invariant).[^o9] The conjecture was proved in the special case of site percolation on the triangular lattice.[^o10] Smirnov's theorem has led to a fairly complete theory for percolation on the triangular lattice, and to its relationship to the [Schramm–Loewner evolution](wikipedia://en/6706108) introduced by [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580). He also established conformality for the [two-dimensional critical Ising model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional_critical_Ising_model).[^o11]

## Awards {#Awards}

Smirnov was awarded the [Saint Petersburg Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9103238) Prize (1997), the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) (2001), the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) (joint with [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580), 2001), the [Göran Gustafsson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göran_Gustafsson_Prize) (2001), the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize) (2002), and the Prize of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) (2004).[^o13] In 2010 Smirnov was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for his work on the mathematical foundations of [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), particularly finite lattice models.[^o14] His citation reads "for the proof of [conformal invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformally_invariant) of [percolation](wikipedia://en/187805) and the planar [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) in statistical physics".[^o15]
