# Octant projection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octant projection"}

*Image: Leonardo-Octant-projection.JPG.* Outline of the octant projection in [Codex Atlanticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Atlanticus)

The **octant projection**[^o2][^o3][^o4] or **octants projection**, is a type of [map projection](wikipedia://en/51784)[^o5] proposed the first time, in 1508, by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) in his [Codex Atlanticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Atlanticus).[^o7] Leonardo's authorship would be demonstrated by [Christopher Tyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Tyler), who stated "For those projections dated later than 1508,[^o6] his drawings should be effectively considered the original precursors."[^o7][^o8]

:::

The same page of the Codex contains sketches of eight other projections of the globe (those known in the late fifteenth century) studied by Leonardo, including [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s conical planisphere projection and [Roselli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Rosselli)'s pseudocylindric projection.[^o8]

## Description {#Description}

*Image: Cahill butterfly projection3.jpg.* Development of Cahill-butterfly's quasi-octant projection circa 1915

The octant projection is the first known [polyhedral map projection](wikipedia://en/69388599). It is neither [conformal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_projection) nor [equal-area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal-area_projection).[^o10] In it, the spherical surface of the earth is divided into eight octants, each flattened into the shape of a [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252) bound by circular arcs. If transferred to an elastic support, it would be possible to cover with them the surface of a model of the earth's globe.[^o11][^o12]

The eight triangles are oriented in a similar way as per two [four-leaf clovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-leaf_clover)[^o13] side by side (fleurons),[^o14] being the earth poles in the center of each clove. One of the sides of the eight triangles, (the one opposite to the center of the pseudo clover), is one fourth of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), the remaining two (those that converge to the center of the pseudo clover), are part of the two [meridians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_%28geography%29) that with the equator dissect the globe in the eight octants.[^o15]

## History of authorship research {#History_of_authorship_research}

Although Leonardo's first description of the octant projection has been proved by Tyler,[^o7] who decided to treat separately Leonardo's projection authorship (1508) from Leonardo's map authorship (1514), the other authors before him treat together the authorship of both map and [projection](wikipedia://en/51784), for they speak about "the eighth of a supposed globe represented in a plane" or about "globe sections" (Harrisse) or others about "[gores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gore_%28segment%29)", which are in fact a projection of the globe.

So, bearing in mind the fact that Tyler was the first scholar to mention the sketch of this projection in [Codex Atlanticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Atlanticus) in 2017, the authorship of the map it is not universally accepted, with some authors being completely against any minimal contribution from Leonardo, such as [Henry Harrisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Harrisse) (1892),[^o18] or [Eugène Müntz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugène_Müntz) (1899 – citing Harrisse authority from 1892, although none of them talks about the projection's sketch in [Codex Atlanticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Atlanticus)).[^o19]

Other scholars accept explicitly both (map and [projection](wikipedia://en/51784): "the eight of a supposed globe represented in a plane"), completely as a Leonardo's work, describing the projection as the first of this type, among them, [R. H. Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._H._Major) (1865) in his work *Memoir on a mappemonde by Leonardo da Vinci, being the earliest map hitherto known containing the name of America*,[^o20] Grothe,[^o21] the *Enciclopedia universal ilustrada europeo-americana* (1934),[^o11] Snyder in his book *Flattening the Earth* (1993),[^o12] Christopher Tyler in his paper (2014) "Leonardo da Vinci's World Map",[^o7] [José Luis Espejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Luis_Espejo_Pérez) in his book (2012) *Los mensajes ocultos de Leonardo Da Vinci*,[^o22] or David Bower in his work (2012) "The unusual projection for one of John Dee's maps of 1580".[^o23]

Others also accept explicitly both (map and [projection](wikipedia://en/51784)) as authentic, although leaving in the air Leonardo's direct hand, giving the authorship of the work to one of his disciples as [Nordenskjold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Erik_Nordenskjöld) states in his book *Facsimile-Atlas* (1889) confirmed by Dutton (1995) and many others: "on account of the remarkable projection..not by Leonardo himself, but by some ignorant clerk",[^o24] or Keunig (1955) being more precise: "by one of his followers at his direction".[^o25]
