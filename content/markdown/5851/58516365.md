# F. Michael Christ

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="F. Michael Christ"}

**Francis Michael Christ** (born 7 June 1955) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), specializing in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), and [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).[^o3][^o4] He is known for the [Christ–Kiselev maximal inequality](wikipedia://en/42730692).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He received in 1977 from [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College) his bachelor's degree[^o3] and in 1982 from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) his PhD under the supervision of [Alberto Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón) with thesis *Restrictions of the Fourier transform to submanifolds of low codimension*. At [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Christ worked with [Elias M. Stein](wikipedia://en/418073) from 1982 to 1984 as an instructor and from 1984 to 1986 as an assistant professor. He was at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA) from 1986 to 1988 an associate professor and from 1988 to 1996 a full professor. In 1996, he became a full professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o4]

Christ was a Sloan Fellow for the academic year 1986–1987. He was an invited speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1990 in Kyoto[^o7] and in 1998 in Berlin.[^o8] In 1997 he shared with [David E. Barrett](wikipedia://en/47548927) the [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize).[^o4] He was named to the 2021 class of fellows of the American Mathematical Society "for contributions to harmonic and complex analysis, and linear partial differential equations".[^o9]
