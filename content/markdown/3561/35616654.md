# Andrew Mackay (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Mackay"}

**Andrew Mackay** (1760–1809) was a Scottish mathematician and astronomer, known as a teacher of [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He lived in Aberdeen, where he was in October 1781, appointed unsalaried keeper of the observatory on the Castle hill. There he made calculated the [latitude](wikipedia://en/17616) and [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) of his native town (see infra). He was awarded an honorary doctorate (LLD) by [Aberdeen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_University) in 1786. In 1793, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) (FRSE). His proposers were [Dugald Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugald_Stewart), [James Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gregory_%28physician%29), and [John Playfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Playfair).[^o3]

He was also an honorary member of the [Literary and Philosophical Society of Newcastle-on-Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_and_Philosophical_Society_of_Newcastle-on-Tyne), and mathematical examiner to the corporation of [Trinity House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_House) (1805–9) and to the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company).[^n1]

In his later years Mackay took pupils in London at his house in George Street, [Trinity Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Square,_Tower_Hamlets); he taught mathematics and natural philosophy, navigation, architecture, and engineering.

He died in London on 3 August 1809, leaving a widow and children, and was buried in [Allhallows Churchyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allhallows_Barking) in Barking.[^n1] Mackay's library was sold in London by auction by Leigh & Sotheby on 9 February 1810 (and following day); a copy of the catalogue is held at Cambridge University Library (shelfmark Munby.c.167\[1\]).

## Works {#Works}

Mackay contributed to the theory of navigation, and was a calculator of mathematical tables. His main works are:

- *The Theory and Practice of finding the Longitude at Sea or on Land: to which are added various Methods of Determining the Latitude of a Place by Variation of the Compass: with new Tables*, published by subscription, 1793, 2 vols.; 2nd edit., with author's portrait, 1801; 3rd edit. 1810. In this work is given an account of a new method of finding the longitude and latitude of a ship at sea, together with the apparent time, from the same set of observations; for which the author had received the thanks of the boards of longitude of England and France.
- *A Collection of Mathematical Tables*, 1804.
- *The Complete Navigator*, 1804; 2nd edit. 1810. The preface contains criticisms of the books on navigation then in current use.[^n1]

Other works are:

- *A Comparison of different Methods of Solving Halley's Problem*.
- *Description and Use of the Sliding Rule in Arithmetic and in the Mensuration of Surfaces and Solids*, &c., 1799; 2nd edit. 1806.
- *The Commencement of the Nineteenth Century determined upon unerring Principles*, Aberdeen, 1800. The object of this tract was to explain that the century began on 1 January 1800 and not on 1 January 1801.
- *Description and Use of the Sliding Gunter in Navigation*, Aberdeen, 1802; 2nd edit. Leith, 1812, edited by Alexander Ingram, with portrait of author prefixed.[^n1]

He also contributed articles to the *[Encyclopædia Britannica, third edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica,_third_edition)* (1797), on "Navigation", "Parallax", "Pendulum", "Projection of the Sphere", "Shipbuilding", and (naval) "Tactics"; and he was a contributor to *[Rees's Cyclopædia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rees's_Cyclopædia)*. He published a paper on the latitude and longitude of Aberdeen in the *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh*, vol. iv. Examples of Mackay as a [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computer) are in the *Scriptores Logarithmici* of [Francis Maseres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Maseres), vol. vi.[^n1]
