# Feedback with Carry Shift Registers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Feedback with Carry Shift Registers"}

In sequence design, a **Feedback with Carry Shift Register** (or FCSR) is the arithmetic or with carry analog of a [linear-feedback shift register](wikipedia://en/58992) (LFSR). If $N >1$ is an integer, then an *N*-ary FCSR of length $r$ is a finite state device with a state $(a;z) = (a_0,a_1,\dots,a_{r-1};z)$ consisting of a vector of elements $a_i$ in $\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}=S$ and an integer $z$.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o11] The state change operation is determined by a set of coefficients $q_1,\dots,q_n$ and is defined as follows: compute $s = q_r a_0+q_{r-1} a_1+\dots+q_1 a_{r-1} + z$. Express s as $s = a_r + N z'$ with $a_r$ in $S$. Then the new state is $(a_1,a_2,\dots,a_r; z')$. By iterating the state change an FCSR generates an infinite, eventually periodic sequence of numbers in $S$.

:::

FCSRs have been used in the design of [stream ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_ciphers) (such as the [F-FCSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-FCSR) generator), in the [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) of the [summation combiner](wikipedia://en/13128066) stream cipher (the reason Goresky and Klapper invented them[^o2]), and in generating [pseudorandom numbers](wikipedia://en/23210) for [quasi-Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/487599) (under the name [Multiply With Carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiply-with-carry) (MWC) generator - invented by Couture and L'Ecuyer,[^o3]) generalizing work of [Marsaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marsaglia) and [Zaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arif_Zaman).[^o5]

FCSRs are analyzed using [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Associated with the FCSR is a connection integer $q = q_r N^r + \dots + q_1 N^1 - 1$. Associated with the output sequence is the [N-adic number](wikipedia://en/51423) $a = a_0 + a_1 N + a_2N^2+\dots$ The fundamental theorem of FCSRs says that there is an integer $u$ so that $a = u/q$, a rational number. The output sequence is strictly periodic if and only if $u$ is between $-q$ and $0$. It is possible to express u as a simple quadratic polynomial involving the initial state and the q<sub>i</sub>.[^o2]

There is also an exponential representation of FCSRs: if $g$ is the inverse of $N \pmod q$, and the output sequence is strictly periodic, then $a_i = (A g_i \bmod q) \bmod N$, where $A$ is an integer. It follows that the period is at most the order of $N$ in the multiplicative group of units modulo $q$. This is maximized when $q$ is prime and $N$ is a [primitive element](wikipedia://en/186864) modulo $q$. In this case, the period is $q-1$. In this case the output sequence is called an l-sequence (for "long sequence").[^o2]

l-sequences have many excellent statistical properties that make them candidates for use in applications,[^o6] including near uniform distribution of sub-blocks, ideal arithmetic autocorrelations, and the arithmetic shift and add property. They are the with-carry analog of m-sequences or [maximum length sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_length_sequence).

There are efficient [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms) for FCSR synthesis. This is the problem: given a prefix of a sequence, construct a minimal length FCSR that outputs the sequence. This can be solved with a variant of [Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496)[^o7] and De Weger's[^o8] lattice based analysis of N-adic numbers when $N=2$;[^o2] by a variant of the Euclidean algorithm when $N$ is prime; and in general by Xu's adaptation of the Berlekamp-Massey algorithm.[^o9] If L is the size of the smallest FCSR that outputs the sequence (called the N-adic complexity of the sequence), then all these algorithms require a prefix of length about $2L$ to be successful and have quadratic time complexity. It follows that, as with LFSRs and linear complexity, any stream cipher whose *N*-adic complexity is low should not be used for cryptography.

FCSRs and LFSRs are special cases of a very general algebraic construction of sequence generators called Algebraic Feedback Shift Registers (AFSRs) in which the integers are replaced by an arbitrary ring $R$ and $N$ is replaced by an arbitrary non-unit in $R$.[^o10] A general reference on the subject of LFSRs, FCSRs, and AFSRs is the book.[^o11]
