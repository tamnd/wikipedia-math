# Peter M. Neumann

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Peter M. Neumann"}

**Peter Michael Neumann** [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) (28 December 1940 – 18 December 2020)[^o4] was a British mathematician. His fields of interest included the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in December 1940, Neumann was a son of the German-born mathematicians [Bernhard Neumann](wikipedia://en/5032588) and [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308). He gained a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree from [The Queen's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Queen's_College,_Oxford) in 1963, and a [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1966.[^o4] On completing his doctorate, Neumann was named a [Tutorial Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutorial_Fellow) at the Queen's College, Oxford, and in 1967 he became a lecturer at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o6] His research work was in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). In 1987, Neumann won the [Lester R. Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford) Award of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for his review of [Harold Edwards'](wikipedia://en/26012311) book *Galois Theory*.[^o7][^o8]

He was the first Chairman of the [United Kingdom Mathematics Trust](wikipedia://en/552624), from October 1996 to April 2004, succeeded by [Bernard Silverman](wikipedia://en/7785395).[^o9]

Neumann showed in 1997 that [Alhazen's problem](wikipedia://en/26855522) (reflecting a light ray off a spherical mirror to hit a target) cannot be solved with a [straightedge and compass construction](wikipedia://en/61229). Although the solution is a straightforward application of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) it settles the constructibility of one of the last remaining geometric construction problems posed in antiquity.[^o10]

In 2003, the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) awarded him the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize).[^o11] He was appointed [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (OBE) in the [2008 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year_Honours_2008).[^o12]

After retiring in 2008, he became an Emeritus Fellow at the Queen's College.

Neumann's work in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) includes his 2011 publication *The Mathematical Writings of Évariste Galois*, an English language book on the work of French mathematician [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) (1811–1832).[^o13] Neumann was a long-standing supporter of the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793), whose [Neumann Prize](wikipedia://en/7795793#Neumann_Prize) is named in his honour.[^o14]

Neumann was the president of the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403) from 2015 to 2016.[^o4]

Neumann died from [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19) on 18 December 2020, ten days before his 80th birthday, during the [COVID-19 pandemic in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^o15][^o16][^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Neumann married Sylvia Bull in 1962. She was a fellow mathematics undergraduate at Oxford, where they met.[^o16]
