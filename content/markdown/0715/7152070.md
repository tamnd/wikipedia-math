# Dagger category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dagger category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **dagger category** (also called **involutive category** or **category with involution**[^o12][^o13]) is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) equipped with a certain structure called *dagger* or *involution*. The name dagger category was coined by Peter Selinger.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A **dagger category** is a category $\mathcal{C}$ equipped with an [involutive](wikipedia://en/293450) [contravariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) [endofunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) $\dagger$ which is the identity on [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29).[^o4]

In detail, this means that:

- for all [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) $f: A \to B$, there exists its [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) $f^\dagger: B \to A$
- for all morphisms $f$, $(f^\dagger)^\dagger = f$
- for all objects $A$, $\mathrm{id}_A^\dagger = \mathrm{id}_A$
- for all $f: A \to B$ and $g: B \to C$, $(g \circ f)^\dagger = f^\dagger \circ g^\dagger: C \to A$

Note that in the previous definition, the term "adjoint" is used in a way analogous to (and inspired by) the [linear-algebraic](wikipedia://en/18422) sense, not in [the category-theoretic sense](wikipedia://en/53991).

Some sources[^o14] define a **category with involution** to be a dagger category with the additional property that its [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of morphisms is [partially ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) and that the order of morphisms is compatible with the composition of morphisms, that is $a < b$ implies $a\circ c<b\circ c$ for morphisms $a$, $b$, $c$ whenever their sources and targets are compatible.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The category **Rel** of [sets and relations](wikipedia://en/5930730) possesses a dagger structure: for a given [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558) $R:X \rightarrow Y$ in **Rel**, the relation $R^\dagger:Y \rightarrow X$ is the [relational converse](wikipedia://en/3941020) of $R$. In this example, a self-adjoint morphism is a [symmetric relation](wikipedia://en/200459).
- The category **Cob** of [cobordisms](wikipedia://en/391832#Categorical_aspects) is a [dagger compact category](wikipedia://en/7155145), in particular it possesses a dagger structure.
- The category **Hilb** of [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) also possesses a dagger structure: Given a [bounded linear map](wikipedia://en/20598932#Operators_on_Hilbert_spaces) $f:A \rightarrow B$, the map $f^\dagger:B \rightarrow A$ is just its [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) in the usual sense.
- Any [monoid with involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_with_involution) is a dagger category with only one object. In fact, every [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) [hom-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-set) in a dagger category is not simply a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), but a monoid with involution, because of the dagger.
- A [discrete category](wikipedia://en/808519) is trivially a dagger category.
- A [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) (and as trivial corollary, a [group](wikipedia://en/19447)) also has a dagger structure with the adjoint of a morphism being its inverse. In this case, all morphisms are unitary (definition below).

:::

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Self-adjoint morphism"}

## Remarkable morphisms {#Remarkable_morphisms}

In a dagger category $\mathcal{C}$, a morphism $f$ is called

- **unitary** if $f^\dagger = f^{-1},$
- **self-adjoint** if $f^\dagger = f.$

The latter is only possible for an [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) $f\colon A \to A$. The terms *unitary* and *self-adjoint* in the previous definition are taken from the category of Hilbert spaces, where the morphisms satisfying those properties are then [unitary](wikipedia://en/236007) and [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) in the usual sense.
