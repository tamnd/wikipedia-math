# Evans Harrell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Evans Harrell"}

**Evans Malott Harrell II** (born 1950) is an American mathematician known for his research in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operators), particularly related to the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874).[^o12] From 2005 to 2014 he was Professor and Associate Dean in the School of Mathematics at the [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Institute_of_Technology).[^o20]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Harrell was born in 1950 in [Indianapolis, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis,_Indiana). He earned his undergraduate degree from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and completed his Ph.D. at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1976,[^o18] where his doctoral dissertation was titled "Schrödinger Operators with Singular Perturbation Potentials" under the advisor [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon).[^o10] He held visiting and postdoctoral posts at [Haverford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College), the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), and [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) before his first tenure-track role at [Johns Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). He joined Georgia Tech in 1983 and remained there until his retirement.[^o13]

## Research and Academic Contributions {#Research_and_Academic_Contributions}

Harrell’s research focuses on the analysis of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), particularly in the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137) and semiclassical [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). He is recognized for his work on the influence of geometry on the [eigenvalues and eigenvectors](wikipedia://en/2161429) of differential equations, as well as for studying the behavior of [quantum systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_systems) at atomic scales.[^o18]

## Education reform {#Education_reform}

Harrell has been an active voice in science education reform[^o19] and has contributed to international mathematics education. He participated in scientific exchanges and delivered lectures in countries that included [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin), [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal), and [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali).[^o16][^o13] Additionally, he serves on the board of the educational foundation [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) (G4G).[^o11]

## Science Communication through the Arts {#Science_Communication_through_the_Arts}

Harrell integrates dance, music, circus arts, and drama into his science communication efforts. He founded Mathematics in Motion, a production company devoted to blending mathematics with the lively arts.[^o15] Through this initiative, Harrell has produced and directed science-themed performances at various events, including the Atlanta Science Festival[^o13] and the [Julia Robinson Mathematics Festival](wikipedia://en/57295923).[^o20]

## Honors {#Honors}

Harrell won a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) in 1983.[^o14] He is a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (AAAS).[^o17] [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) awarded him an Outstanding Service Award in 1996 and the Eichholtz Teaching Award in 2006.[^o20]
