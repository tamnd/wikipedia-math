# Ruth Margaret Williams

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Margaret Williams"}

**Ruth Margaret Williams** (born 1945) is a British mathematician at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). Her research focuses on discrete gravity.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Williams was born in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) and attended primary schools in [Lozells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozells) and Banners Gate. Her secondary education was at Sutton Coldfield High School for Girls. The first member of her family to go to university, she read mathematics at [Girton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge), then moved to [Imperial College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College,_London), for her PhD, which was in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) and focused on current algebras. She obtained a postdoctoral research position at the [Center for Theoretical Studies at the University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Theoretical_Studies,_University_of_Miami) (1968-1970). She held a research position at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College) (1970-1973) and a temporary lectureship at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) (1973-1974), before moving back to a [Hertha Ayrton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertha_Ayrton) Research Fellowship at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College) in 1974.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1976, Williams became an Official Fellow, Lecturer and Director of Studies in Mathematics at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College),[^o5] positions she held until she retired in 2012. At the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics, [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), she was a temporary lecturer (1991-1994),[^o5] Assistant Director of Research (1994-2002)[^o5] and Reader in Mathematical Physics[^o5] from 2002 until retiring in 2012. She is now a Life Fellow at [Girton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College).[^o7] Throughout her time in Cambridge, she lectured on and supervised most of the courses in theoretical physics.

## Research {#Research}

Williams’ early research was in [elementary particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle_physics), then during her second postdoctoral position she started working in classical [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Eventually, she combined these two interests by working in [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) in an attempt to find a unified theory of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) and [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Her particular approach, called [Regge calculus](wikipedia://en/1012545), is a version of discrete gravity where curved space-times are approximated by collections of flat simplices. This may be thought of as a generalisation of [geodesic domes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_domes) to higher dimensions. Williams’ work on Regge calculus includes the classical evolution of model universes,[^o9] and numerical simulations of discrete quantum gravity,[^o10] together with investigations of the relationship between Regge calculus and the [continuum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_theory).[^o11] She has written around 100 papers on her research.[^o12]

## Knowledge transfer {#Knowledge_transfer}

Williams has given talks on her research to student societies, sixth formers and younger pupils. She has been Treasurer of the Cambridgeshire branch of the [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution) Mathematics Workshops, which runs Saturday morning sessions to foster the mathematical interest of local twelve- and thirteen-year-olds.[^o14]

## Women in STEM {#Women_in_STEM}

Williams has always been especially concerned that women should not be deterred from reaching their potential as mathematicians. To this end, and encouraged by [Dusa McDuff](wikipedia://en/2838322), she started the British Women in Mathematics Day, which is now an annual event.[^o22]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Williams is the widow of Czech-born British mathematician, [Jan Saxl](wikipedia://en/63906865), Professor of Algebra at the University of Cambridge, and they have one daughter, Miriam.[^o24]
