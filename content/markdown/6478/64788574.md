# Toshiki Mabuchi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toshiki Mabuchi"}

**Toshiki Mabuchi** ([kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji): 満渕俊樹, [hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana): マブチ トシキ, Mabuchi Toshiki, born in 1950) is a Japanese mathematician, specializing in [complex differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o2] In 2006 in Madrid he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o3] Mabuchi is known for introducing the [Mabuchi functional](wikipedia://en/68495772).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1972 Mabuchi graduated from the University of Tokyo Faculty of Science[^o2] and became a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o5] There he graduated with a Ph.D. in 1977 with thesis *C3-Actions and Algebraic Threefolds with Ample Tangent Bundle* and advisor [Shoshichi Kobayashi](wikipedia://en/20695112) As a postdoc Mabuchi was from 1977 to 1978 a guest researcher at the University of Bonn. Since 1978 he is a faculty member of the Department of Mathematics of [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University). His research deals with complex differential geometry, extremal [Kähler metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric), [stability of algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/62719320), and the [Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchin–Kobayashi_correspondence).[^o2]

In 2006 Toshiki Mabuchi and Takashi Shioya received the [Geometry Prize of the Mathematical Society of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometry_prize).

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Mabuchi is well-known for his introduction, in 1986, of the *[Mabuchi energy](wikipedia://en/68495772)*, which gives a variational interpretation to the problem of [Kähler metrics of constant scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/37860980). In particular, the Mabuchi energy is a real-valued function on a Kähler class whose [Euler-Lagrange equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler-Lagrange_equation) is the constant scalar curvature equation. In the case that the Kähler class represents the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of the complex manifold, one has a relation to the [Kähler-Einstein problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler-Einstein_metric), due to the fact that constant scalar curvature metrics in such a Kähler class must be Kähler-Einstein.

Owing to the second variation formulas for the Mabuchi energy, every critical point is stable. Furthermore, if one integrates a holomorphic vector field and pulls back a given Kähler metric by the corresponding one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms, then the corresponding restriction of the Mabuchi energy is a linear function of one real variable; its derivative is the *Futaki invariant* discovered a few years earlier by Akito Futaki.[^o8] The Futaki invariant and Mabuchi energy are fundamental in understanding obstructions to the existence of Kähler metrics which are Einstein or which have constant scalar curvature.

A year later, by use of the $\partial\overline{\partial}$-lemma, Mabuchi considered a natural [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on a Kähler class, which allowed him to define length, [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096), and [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770); the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) of Mabuchi's metric is nonpositive. Along geodesics in the Kähler class, the Mabuchi energy is convex. So the Mabuchi energy has strong variational properties.
