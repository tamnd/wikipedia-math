# Affine involution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine involution"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), an **affine involution** is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) which is a [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) or [affine](wikipedia://en/38449) transformation over the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$. Such involutions are easy to characterize and they can be described geometrically.[^o2]

:::

## Linear involutions {#Linear_involutions}

To give a linear involution is the same as giving an [involutory matrix](wikipedia://en/7917643), a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) $\mathbf{A}$ such that

$$\mathbf A^2 = \mathbf I \quad\quad\quad\quad (1)$$

where $\mathbf{I}$ is the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718).[^o4]

It is a quick check that a square matrix $\mathbf{D}$ whose elements are all zero off the main diagonal and ±1 on the diagonal, that is, a [signature matrix](wikipedia://en/7887913) of the form

$$\mathbf D = \begin{pmatrix} \pm 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \pm 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \pm 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & \pm 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

satisfies (1), i.e. is the matrix of a linear involution. It turns out that all the matrices satisfying (1) are of the form

$$\mathbf A = \mathbf U^{-1} \mathbf{DU},$$

where $\mathbf{U}$ is invertible and $\mathbf{D}$ is as above. That is to say, the matrix of any linear involution is of the form $\mathbf{D}$ [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a [matrix similarity](wikipedia://en/341149). Geometrically this means that any linear involution can be obtained by taking [oblique reflections](wikipedia://en/2864280) against any number from 0 through $n$ [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) going through the origin. (The term *oblique reflection* as used here includes ordinary reflections.)

One can easily verify that $\mathbf{A}$ represents a linear involution if and only if $\mathbf{A}$ has the form

$$\mathbf A = \pm (2 \mathbf P - \mathbf I)$$

for a linear [projection](wikipedia://en/519182) $\mathbf{P}$.

## Affine involutions {#Affine_involutions}

If *A* represents a linear involution, then *x*→*A*(*x*−*b*)+*b* is an [affine](wikipedia://en/38449) involution. One can check that any affine involution in fact has this form. Geometrically, this means that any affine involution can be obtained by taking oblique reflections against any number from 0 through *n* hyperplanes going through a point *b*.[^o6]

Affine involutions can be categorized by the dimension of the [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) of [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738); this corresponds to the number of values 1 on the diagonal of the similar matrix *D* (see above), i.e., the dimension of the [eigenspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace) for [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) 1.

The affine involutions in $}\mathbb{R^3$ are:[^o7]

- the [identity](wikipedia://en/15069)
- the [reflection](wikipedia://en/12487536) in respect to a point
- the [oblique reflection](wikipedia://en/2864280) in respect to a line
- the [oblique reflection](wikipedia://en/2864280) in respect to a plane

## Isometric involutions {#Isometric_involutions}

In the case that the eigenspace for eigenvalue 1 is the [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) of that for eigenvalue −1, i.e., every eigenvector with eigenvalue 1 is [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) to every eigenvector with eigenvalue −1, such an affine involution is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777). The two extreme cases for which this always applies are the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) and [inversion in a point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_in_a_point).

The other involutive isometries are inversion in a line (in 2D, 3D, and up; this is in 2D a [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829), and in 3D a [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789) about the line by 180°), inversion in a plane (in 3D and up; in 3D this is a reflection in a plane), inversion in a 3D space (in 3D: the identity), etc.
