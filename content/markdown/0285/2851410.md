# Five-dimensional space

*Image: 5-cube t0.svg.* A 2D [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of a [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Five-dimensional space"}

A **five-dimensional (5D) space** is a [mathematical](wikipedia://en/5308894) or [physical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_%28physics%29) space that has five independent [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398). In physics and geometry, such a space extends the familiar three spatial dimensions plus time (4D spacetime) by introducing an additional degree of freedom, which is often used to model advanced theories such as higher-dimensional gravity, extra spatial directions, or connections between different points in spacetime.

:::

## Concepts {#Concepts}

Concepts related to five-dimensional spaces include super-dimensional or [hyper-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperspace) spaces, which generally refer to any space with more than four dimensions. These ideas appear in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), and [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) to explore phenomena beyond ordinary perception.

Important related topics include:

- [5-manifold](wikipedia://en/7763362) — a generalization of a surface or volume to five dimensions.
- [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215) — also called a penteract, a specific five-dimensional hypercube.
- [Hypersphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere) — the generalization of a sphere to higher dimensions, including five-dimensional space.
- [List of regular 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#5_dimensions) — regular geometric shapes that exist in five-dimensional space.
- [Four-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/1364622) — a foundational step to understanding five-dimensional extensions.

## Five-dimensional Euclidean geometry {#Five-dimensional_Euclidean_geometry}

5D [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), designated *E*<sup>5</sup>,[^o4] is dimensions beyond two ([planar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry)) and three ([solid](wikipedia://en/507960)). Shapes studied in five dimensions include counterparts of [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299) and of the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859).

### Polytopes {#Polytopes}

In five or more dimensions, only three [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) exist. In five dimensions, they are:

- The **[5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex)** of the [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) family, {3,3,3,3}, with 6 vertices, 15 edges, 20 faces (each an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285)), 15 cells (each a regular [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606)), and 6 hypercells (each a [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821)).
- The **[5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215)** of the [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) family, {4,3,3,3}, with 32 vertices, 80 edges, 80 faces (each a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29)), 40 cells (each a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)), and 10 hypercells (each a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112)).
- The **[5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615)** of the [cross polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_polytope) family, {3,3,3,4}, with 10 vertices, 40 edges, 80 faces (each a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654)), 80 cells (each a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606)), and 32 hypercells (each a [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821)).

An important uniform 5-polytope is the [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232), h{4,3,3,3} has half the vertices of the 5-cube (16), bounded by alternating [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821) and [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) hypercells. The [expanded](wikipedia://en/6341070) or [stericated 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stericated_5-simplex) is the vertex figure of the [A<sub>5</sub> lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_lattice), . It and has a doubled symmetry from its symmetric Coxeter diagram. The kissing number of the lattice, 30, is represented in its vertices.[^o6] The [rectified 5-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_5-orthoplex) is the vertex figure of the [D<sub>5</sub> lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D5_lattice), . Its 40 vertices represent the [kissing number](wikipedia://en/408555) of the lattice and the highest for dimension 5.[^o7]

## Other five-dimensional geometries {#Other_five-dimensional_geometries}

The theory of [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) makes use of [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488), a type of geometry that locates events in both space and time. The time dimension is mathematically distinguished from the spatial dimensions by a modification in the formula for computing the "distance" between events. Ordinary Minkowski spacetime has four dimensions in all, three of space and one of time. However, higher-dimensional generalizations of the concept have been employed in various proposals. [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070), a speculative attempt to develop a unified theory of [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) and [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism), relied upon a spacetime with four dimensions of space and one of time.[^o11]

Geometries can also be constructed in which the coordinates are something other than real numbers. For example, one can define a space in which the points are labeled by [tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) of 5 [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). This is often denoted $\mathbb{C}^5$. In [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory), quantum systems described by [quantum states](wikipedia://en/30876419) belonging to $\mathbb{C}^5$ are sometimes called *ququints.*[^o12][^o13]
