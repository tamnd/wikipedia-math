# Hadamard space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadamard space"}

*Image: End of universe.jpg.* In an Hadamard space, a triangle is [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1283865); that is, the middle one in the picture. In fact, any complete metric space where a triangle is hyperbolic is an Hadamard space.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **Hadamard space**, named after [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), is a non-linear generalization of a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). In the literature they are also equivalently defined as complete [CAT(0) spaces](wikipedia://en/12941912).

:::

An Hadamard space is defined to be a nonempty[^o2] complete [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) such that, given any points $x$ and $y,$ there exists a point $m$ such that for every point $z,$

$$d(z, m)^2 + {d(x, y)^2 \over 4} \leq {d(z, x)^2 + d(z, y)^2 \over 2}.$$

The point $m$ is then the midpoint of $x$ and $y:$ $d(x, m) = d(y, m) = d(x, y)/2.$

In a Hilbert space, the above inequality is equality (with $m = (x+y)/2$), and in general an Hadamard space is said to be flat if the above inequality is equality. A flat Hadamard space is isomorphic to a closed convex subset of a Hilbert space. In particular, a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) is an Hadamard space if and only if it is a Hilbert space.

The geometry of Hadamard spaces resembles that of Hilbert spaces, making it a natural setting for the study of [rigidity theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigidity_theorem). In a Hadamard space, any two points can be joined by a unique [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) between them; in particular, it is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414). Quite generally, if $B$ is a bounded subset of a metric space, then the center of the closed ball of the minimum radius containing it is called the *[circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter_%28metric_space%29)* of $B.$[^o3] Every bounded subset of a Hadamard space is contained in the smallest closed ball (which is the same as the closure of its convex hull). If $\Gamma$ is the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) of a Hadamard space leaving invariant $B,$ then $\Gamma$ fixes the circumcenter of $B$ (**Bruhat–Tits fixed point theorem**).

The basic result for a non-positively curved manifold is the [Cartan–Hadamard theorem](wikipedia://en/11939373). The analog holds for a Hadamard space: a complete, connected metric space which is locally isometric to a Hadamard space has an Hadamard space as its [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover). Its variant applies for non-positively curved [orbifolds](wikipedia://en/293504). (cf. Lurie.)

Examples of Hadamard spaces are [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932), the [Poincaré disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_disc), complete [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914) (for example, complete [Bruhat–Tits building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat–Tits_building)), [$(p, q)$-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28p,_q%29-space) with $p, q \geq 3$ and $2 p q \geq p + q,$ and [Hadamard manifolds](wikipedia://en/12471936), that is, complete simply-connected [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) of nonpositive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618). Important examples of Hadamard manifolds are simply connected nonpositively curved [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877).

Applications of Hadamard spaces are not restricted to geometry. In 1998, [Dmitri Burago](wikipedia://en/57995304) and [Serge Ferleger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Ferleger)[^o4] used [CAT(0) geometry](wikipedia://en/12941912) to solve a problem in [dynamical billiards](wikipedia://en/2160429): in a gas of hard balls, is there a uniform bound on the number of collisions? The solution begins by constructing a configuration space for the [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), obtained by joining together copies of corresponding billiard table, which turns out to be a Hadamard space.
