# List of uniform polyhedra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of uniform polyhedra"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform polyhedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) which has [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) as [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) and is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) ([transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_group_action) on its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), isogonal, i.e. there is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) mapping any vertex onto any other). It follows that all vertices are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330), and the polyhedron has a high degree of [reflectional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflectional_symmetry) and [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096).

:::

Uniform polyhedra can be divided between [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) forms with convex [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) faces and star forms. Star forms have either regular [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) or [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) or both.

This list includes these:

::: {#o2 .entry cue=list-entry name="uniform"}

- all 75 nonprismatic [uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223) [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470);

:::

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="prisms"}

- a few representatives of the infinite sets of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851);

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Skilling's figure"}

- one degenerate polyhedron, [Skilling's figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skilling's_figure) with overlapping edges.

:::

It was proven in Sopov that there are only 75 [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) other than the infinite families of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851). John Skilling discovered an overlooked degenerate example, by relaxing the condition that only two faces may meet at an edge. This is a degenerate uniform polyhedron rather than a uniform polyhedron, because some pairs of edges coincide.

Not included are:

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Uniform polyhedron compound"}

- The [uniform polyhedron compounds](wikipedia://en/8606809).

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="uniform polyhedra with degenerate"}

- 40 potential [uniform polyhedra with degenerate](wikipedia://en/35367651) [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) which have overlapping edges (not counted by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter));

:::

- The uniform tilings (infinite polyhedra)
  - 11 Euclidean [convex uniform tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_tilings_of_regular_polygons#Archimedean.2C_uniform_or_semiregular_tilings);
  - 28 Euclidean [nonconvex or apeirogonal uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635#Expanded_lists_of_uniform_tilings);
  - Infinite number of [uniform tilings in hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/18640974).

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Polygons"}

- Any [polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygons) or [4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-polytopes)

:::

## Indexing {#Indexing}

Four numbering schemes for the uniform polyhedra are in common use, distinguished by letters:

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="convex"}

- \[**C**\] Coxeter et al., 1954, showed the [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) forms as figures 15 through 32; three prismatic forms, figures 33–35; and the nonconvex forms, figures 36–92.

:::

- \[**W**\] Wenninger, 1974, has 119 figures: 1–5 for the Platonic solids, 6–18 for the Archimedean solids, 19–66 for stellated forms including the 4 regular nonconvex polyhedra, and ended with 67–119 for the nonconvex uniform polyhedra.

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="dihedral symmetry"}

- \[**K**\] Kaleido, 1993: The 80 figures were grouped by symmetry: 1–5 as representatives of the infinite families of prismatic forms with [dihedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2922089), 6–9 with [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472), 10–26 with [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805), 27–80 with [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844).

:::

- \[**U**\] Mathematica, 1993, follows the Kaleido series with the 5 prismatic forms moved to last, so that the nonprismatic forms become 1–75.

## Names of polyhedra by number of sides {#Names_of_polyhedra_by_number_of_sides}

There are generic [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) names for the most common [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470). The 5 [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) are called a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), [hexahedron](wikipedia://en/237209), [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) and [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) with 4, 6, 8, 12, and 20 sides respectively. The regular hexahedron is a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285).

## Table of polyhedra {#Table_of_polyhedra}

The convex forms are listed in order of degree of [vertex configurations](wikipedia://en/3603745) from 3 faces/vertex and up, and in increasing sides per face. This ordering allows topological similarities to be shown.

There are infinitely many prisms and antiprisms, one for each regular polygon; the ones up to the 12-gonal cases are listed.

## Column key {#Column_key}

- Uniform indexing: U01–U80 (Tetrahedron first, Prisms at 76+)
- Kaleido software indexing: K01–K80 (K<sub>*n*</sub> = U<sub>*n*–5</sub> for *n* = 6 to 80) (prisms 1–5, Tetrahedron etc. 6+)

::: {#o119 .entry cue=list-entry name="Magnus Wenninger"}

- [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/2915428) Polyhedron Models: W001-W119

:::

  - 1–18: 5 convex regular and 13 convex semiregular
  - 20–22, 41: 4 non-convex regular
  - 19–66: Special 48 stellations/compounds (Nonregulars not given on this list)
  - 67–109: 43 non-convex non-snub uniform
  - 110–119: 10 non-convex snub uniform

::: {#o120 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler characteristic"}

- Chi: the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420), $\chi$. Uniform tilings on the plane correspond to a torus topology, with Euler characteristic of zero.

:::

::: {#o121 .entry cue=list-entry name="Density (polytope)"}

- Density: the [Density (polytope)](wikipedia://en/34257549) represents the number of windings of a polyhedron around its center. This is left blank for non-[orientable](wikipedia://en/187446) polyhedra and [hemipolyhedra](wikipedia://en/24084383) (polyhedra with faces passing through their centers), for which the density is not well-defined.
