# Ambient space (mathematics)

Ambient space

*Image: Euclidian and non euclidian geometry.png.* Three examples of different geometries: [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417), [elliptical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_geometry), and [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ambient space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), an **ambient space** is the space surrounding a [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961) along with the object itself.

:::

For example, a [1-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_dimension) [line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29) $(l)$ may be studied in isolation —in which case the ambient space of $l$ is the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line), or it may be studied as an object embedded in [2-dimensional](wikipedia://en/19469852) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $(\mathbb{R}^2)$—in which case the ambient space of $l$ is $\mathbb{R}^2$, or as an object embedded in 2-dimensional [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) $(\mathbb{H}^2)$—in which case the ambient space of $l$ is $\mathbb{H}^2$. To see why this makes a difference, consider the statement "[Parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) lines never intersect." This is true if the ambient space is $\mathbb{R}^2$, but false if the ambient space is $\mathbb{H}^2$, because the geometric properties (in particular, [curvature](wikipedia://en/651361)) of $\mathbb{R}^2$ are different from the geometric properties of $\mathbb{H}^2$. All spaces are [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of their ambient space.
