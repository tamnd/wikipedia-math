# Hexagonal prism

*Image: Prisma hexagonal 3D.stl.* 3D model of a uniform hexagonal prism

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal prism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **hexagonal prism** is a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) with [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) base. this polyhedron has 8 [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), 18 [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), and 12 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A hexagonal prism has twelve vertices, eighteen edges, and eight faces. Every prism has two faces known as its bases, and the bases of a hexagonal prism are hexagons. The hexagons has six vertices, each of which pairs with another hexagon's vertex, forming six edges. These edges form three parallelograms as other faces.[^o5] A prism is said to be right if the edges are of the same length and perpendicular to the base.

If faces are all regular, the hexagonal prism is a [semiregular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1548894)—more generally, a [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223)—and the fourth in an infinite set of prisms formed by square sides and two regular polygon caps. It can be seen as a **[truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [hexagonal hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192)**, represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) t{2,6}. Alternately it can be seen as the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of a regular hexagon and a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860), and represented by the product {6}×{}. The [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of a right hexagonal prism is [prismatic symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prismatic_symmetry) $D_{6 \mathrm{h}}$ of order 24, consisting of rotation around an axis passing through the regular hexagon bases' center, and reflection across a horizontal plane.[^o6] The [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of a hexagonal prism is a [hexagonal bipyramid](wikipedia://en/2081012), both of which have the same three-dimensional symmetry group.

As in most prisms, the volume is found by taking the area of the base, with a side length of $a$, and multiplying it by the height $h$, giving the formula:[^o7]

$$V = \frac{3 \sqrt{3}}{2}a^2h,$$

and its surface area is by summing the area of two regular hexagonal bases and the lateral faces of six squares:

$$S = 3a(\sqrt{3}a+2h).$$

:::

## Honeycombs {#Honeycombs}

*Image: Hexagonal prismatic honeycomb.png.* Hexagonal prismatic honeycomb

The hexagonal prism is one of the [parallelohedra](wikipedia://en/25207334), a polyhedral class that can be translated without rotations in Euclidean space, producing [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654); this class was discovered by [Evgraf Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgraf_Fedorov) in accordance with his studies of crystallography systems. The hexagonal prism is generated from four line segments, three of them parallel to a common plane and the fourth not.[^o9] Its most symmetric form is the right prism over a regular hexagon, forming the [hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb).[^o10]
