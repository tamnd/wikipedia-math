# Triangular prism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triangular prism"}

A **triangular prism** or **trigonal prism**[^n1] is a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) with two triangular bases in geometry. If the edges pair with each triangle's vertex and if they are perpendicular to the base, the triangular prism is a right prism. A right triangular prism may be both [semiregular](wikipedia://en/1548894) and [uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223).

:::

The triangular prism can be used as the core of constructing other polyhedra, examples are some of the [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034) and [Schönhardt polyhedron](wikipedia://en/20346715). It has a relationship with the [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654) and [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471). It can be found in many real-life applications as in architecture and natural science.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

A triangular prism has six vertices, nine edges, and five faces. Every prism has two congruent faces known as its bases, and the bases of a triangular prism are [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654). The triangle has three vertices, each of which pairs with another triangle's vertex, forming three edges. These edges form three [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496) as other faces.[^o6] It is also considered a special case of a [wedge](wikipedia://en/2951640), a polyhedron with two triangular and three trapezoidal faces.[^n2] A triangular prism can have the form of a right prism, meaning that its edges are perpendicular to the base, forming [rectangular](wikipedia://en/70048)-shaped lateral faces.[^n3] Like any-sided prism, it has all vertices lying on the parallel bases, a [prismatoid](wikipedia://en/23917).[^n4]

The vertices and edges of a triangular prism can give rise to a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). This is due to [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817), stating that any convex polyhedron can be drawn into a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) that is [3-connected](wikipedia://en/7566175), meaning the edges of a graph do not cross each other, and the vertices are impossible to disconnect whenever picking any two vertices to be removed. Classifying into a family, the graph of a triangular prism is the [prism graph](wikipedia://en/45012199) $\Pi_{3}$, where the symbol $\Pi_{n}$ represents the graph of an $n$-sided prism.[^n5] The graph of a triangular prism is a type of planar graph formed from a tree with no degree-two vertices by adding a cycle connecting its leaves, an example of [Halin graph](wikipedia://en/19658548).[^n6]

### As a uniform prism {#As_a_uniform_prism}

*Image: Triangular prism.stl.* 3D model of a uniform triangular prism

When all edges are equal in length, its bases and its lateral faces are all [equilaterals](wikipedia://en/173285) and [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), respectively. Hence, the triangular prism is [semiregular](wikipedia://en/1548894). A semiregular prism means that the number of its polygonal base's edges equals the number of its square faces.[^n7] More generally, the triangular prism is [uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223). This means that a triangular prism has [regular faces](wikipedia://en/333306) and has a symmetry of mapping any two vertices known as [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560).[^o14] The dihedral angle between two adjacent square faces is the [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of an equilateral triangle $\pi/3 = 60^{\circ}$, and that between a square and a triangle is $\pi/2 = 90^{\circ}$.[^n8]

The volume of any prism is the product of the area of the base and the distance between the two bases.[^n9] In the case of a triangular prism, its base is a triangle. The area of a triangle is the half product of its base $b$ and its height $h$, formulated as $\tfrac{1}{2}bh$. Since the triangular prism has a distance $l$ between two triangular bases, the general formula for its volume is:[^o17]

$$V = \frac{1}{2}bh \cdot l.$$

In the case of a right triangular prism, where all its edges are equal in length $l$, its volume can be calculated as the product of the equilateral triangle's area and the distance between bases:[^n10]

$$V_\text{uniform} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l^2 \cdot l = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}l^3 \approx 0.433\,l^3$$

The [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525) of a triangular prism is [prismatic symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prismatic_symmetry) $D_{3h}$ of order 12: the appearance is unchanged if the triangular prism is rotated one- and two- thirds of a full angle around its [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) passing through the center's base, and reflecting across a horizontal plane.[^n8] The [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) of any prism is a [bipyramid](wikipedia://en/4153), a polyhedron formed by fusing two pyramids base-to-base. In the case of a triangular prism, its dual is a [triangular bipyramid](wikipedia://en/646933), both of which have a common three-dimensional symmetry group.[^n1]

## Applications {#Applications}

A triangular prism is commonly designed as a [transparent optical object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersive_prism) to [disperse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispersion_%28optics%29) light.[^n11] The [cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_%28chemistry%29) of a chemical compound known as [trigonal prismatic molecular geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonal_prismatic_molecular_geometry) resembles the structure of a triangular prism, with an example compound being [hexamethyltungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexamethyltungsten).[^n12] In architecture, an example of a building with a triangular prism shape is [Flatiron Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatiron_Building) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City).[^n13] An instance of food that resembles the shape of a triangular prism is a [Swiss chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_chocolate) brand of [Toblerone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toblerone).[^n14]

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

### Honeycomb and tiling {#Honeycomb_and_tiling}

The uniform triangular prism is a [plesiohedron](wikipedia://en/53396858), a [space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583) that can be defined as the [Voronoi cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_cell) of a symmetric [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512) in order to create a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654). The [triangular prismatic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/8802963) is an example where triangular prisms are the only [prototiles](wikipedia://en/39372).[^n15] The other honeycombs with a cell being triangular prism and other polyhedra are [gyroelongated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb) (with regular octahedra and tetrahedra), [elongated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb) (with regular octahedra and tetrahedra), [snub square prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snub_square_prismatic_honeycomb), [triangular-hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular-hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb), [truncated hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb), [rhombitriangular-hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombitriangular-hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb), [snub triangular-hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snub_triangular-hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb), [elongated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb).[^n16]

[John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Charles Radin](wikipedia://en/39630926) in 1998 introduced the [quaquaversal tiling](wikipedia://en/9401706), a three-dimensional [non-periodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145). The prototiles are the [30-60-90 right triangular](wikipedia://en/2278116) prisms, some of which are rotated $\pi/3$ and the others are rotated $\pi/2$ in a perpendicular direction.[^n17]

### In the construction of polyhedra {#In_the_construction_of_polyhedra}

*Image: Triaugmented triangular prism (symmetric view).svg.* The [triaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/728019)

Beyond the triangular bipyramid as its dual polyhedron, many other polyhedra are related to the triangular prism. A [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) is a convex polyhedron with regular faces, and this definition sometimes omits uniform polyhedra such as [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393), prisms, and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851).[^o33] There are six Johnson solids with their construction involving the triangular prism:

- The [elongated triangular pyramid](wikipedia://en/1177937) and the [elongated triangular bipyramid](wikipedia://en/1177950) are made by attaching [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) onto the base of a triangular prism.[^o34]
- The [augmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/1177970), the [biaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/1177972), and the [triaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/728019) are constructed by attaching [equilateral square pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilateral_square_pyramid) onto the square face of the prism.[^o34]
- The [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963) is constructed by attaching a triangular prism quarter-turn along to any square face of another one.[^o34] It can tile a space of [gyrated triangular prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_triangular_prismatic_honeycomb).[^n18] Unlike the triangular prism, which is a plesiohedron, the gyrobifastigium is a [stereohedron](wikipedia://en/53413889), because the points at the centers of the cells of its face-to-face tiling (where they are forced to go by symmetry) have differently-shaped Voronoi cells. If the gyrobifastigium has [isosceles right triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosceles_right_triangle) and [silver rectangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_rectangle), then it is a plesiohedron.

*Image: TruncatedTriangularPrism.svg.* A right triangular prism with cutting off its part at an oblique angle

One can cut the part of a triangular prism at an oblique angle. The result has two bases that are not parallel, such that every distance between those bases has a different edge length. If the edges connecting bases are perpendicular to one of its bases, this prism can have a version of a right triangular prism. Given that $A$ is the area of the triangular prism's base, and the three heights $h_{1}$, $h_{2}$, and $h_{3}$, its volume can be determined in the following formula:[^n19]

$$V = \frac{A (h_1 + h_2 + h_3)}{3}.$$

*Image: Schönhardt polyhedron.svg.* [Schönhardt polyhedron](wikipedia://en/20346715)

[Schönhardt polyhedron](wikipedia://en/20346715) is another polyhedron constructed from a triangular prism with equilateral triangle bases. This way, one of its bases rotates around the prism's centerline and breaks the square faces into [skew polygons](wikipedia://en/6545461). Each square face can be re-triangulated with two triangles to form a non-convex dihedral angle.[^o37] As a result, the Schönhardt polyhedron cannot be [triangulated](wikipedia://en/1440970) by a partition into tetrahedra. It is also that the Schönhardt polyhedron has no internal diagonals.[^n20] It is named after German mathematician [Erich Schönhardt](wikipedia://en/25330786), who described it in 1928, although artist [Karlis Johansons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlis_Johansons) exhibited the related structure in 1921.[^o39]

The triangular prism can be [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) from the convex hull of its midpoints' edges. The resulting construction yields three square faces on the same planes as the square faces of the prism, two equilateral triangle faces on the planes of the triangular ends of the prism, and six more isosceles triangle faces. By giving rise to a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), its [dual](wikipedia://en/2536864) forms another convex polyhedron called [Herschel enneahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_enneahedron).[^n21]

The number of triangular prisms can be composed by sharing a common center. Four uniform compounds of triangular prisms, they are [compound of four triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/14836112), [compound of eight triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/14836141), [compound of ten triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/14826806), [compound of twenty triangular prisms](wikipedia://en/14826884).[^n22]

### Polytopes {#Polytopes}

The triangular prism is first in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope). Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the previous polytope. [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) identified this series in 1900 as containing all [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) facets, containing all [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex) ([equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) in the case of the triangular prism). In [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter)'s notation the triangular prism is given the symbol −1<sub>21</sub>.[^n23]
