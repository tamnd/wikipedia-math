# Riemannian submersion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemannian submersion"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Riemannian submersion** is a [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413) from one [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) to another that respects the metrics, meaning that it is an [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) on tangent spaces.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let (*M*, *g*) and (*N*, *h*) be two Riemannian manifolds and $f:M\to N$ a (surjective) submersion, i.e., a [fibered manifold](wikipedia://en/3247635). The horizontal distribution $\mathrm{ker}(df)^{\perp}$ is a [sub-bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of $TM$ which depends both on the projection $f$ and on the metric $g$. The expression $\mathrm{ker}(df)^{\perp}$ denotes the subbundle of $TM$ that is the orthogonal complement of $\mathrm{ker}(df_x) \subset T_{x}M$ at each point x of *M*.

Then, *f* is called a Riemannian submersion if and only if, for all $x\in M$, the vector space isomorphism $(df)_x : \mathrm{ker}(df_x)^{\perp} \rightarrow T_{f(x)}N$ is an isometry, or in other words it carries each vector to one of the same length.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An example of a Riemannian submersion arises when a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ acts isometrically, [freely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) and [properly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action) on a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$. The projection $\pi: M \rightarrow N$ to the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $N = M /G$ equipped with the quotient metric is a Riemannian submersion. For example, component-wise multiplication on $S^3 \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ by the group of unit complex numbers yields the [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384).

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The sectional curvature of the target space of a Riemannian submersion can be calculated from the curvature of the total space by **O'Neill's formula**, named for [Barrett O'Neill](wikipedia://en/53631967):

$$K_N(X,Y)=K_M(\tilde X, \tilde Y)+\tfrac34|[\tilde X,\tilde Y]^V|^2$$

where $X,Y$ are orthonormal vector fields on $N$, $\tilde X, \tilde Y$ their horizontal lifts to $M$, $[*,*]$ is the [Lie bracket of vector fields](wikipedia://en/10282799) and $Z^V$ is the projection of the vector field $Z$ to the [vertical distribution](wikipedia://en/4067918).

In particular the lower bound for the sectional curvature of $N$ is at least as big as the lower bound for the sectional curvature of $M$.

:::
