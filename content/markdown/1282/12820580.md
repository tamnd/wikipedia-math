# Ali Akansu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ali Akansu"}

**Ali Naci Akansu** (born May 6, 1958) is a [Turkish-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish-American) professor of electrical & computer engineering and scientist in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).

:::

He is best known for his contributions to the theory and applications of [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) methods including [sub-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-band_coding) and [wavelet transforms](wikipedia://en/3352292), particularly the [binomial QMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_QMF)[^o3][^o4] (also known as [Daubechies wavelet](wikipedia://en/554121)) and the multivariate framework to design statistically optimized [filter bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_bank) (eigen filter bank).[^o5][^o6]

## Biography {#Biography}

Akansu received his B.S. degree from the [Istanbul Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Technical_University), Turkey, in 1980, his M.S. and PhD degrees from the [Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_University_of_New_York) (now [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University)), Brooklyn, New York, in 1983 and 1987, respectively, all in Electrical Engineering. Since 1987, he has been with the [New Jersey Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Institute_of_Technology) where he is a Professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering. He was a visiting professor at [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) of the [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), 2009–2010.

In 1990, he showed that the [binomial quadrature mirror filter bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_QMF) (binomial QMF) is identical to the [Daubechies wavelet](wikipedia://en/554121) filter, and interpreted and evaluated its performance from a discrete-time signal processing perspective.[^o8] It was an extension of his prior work on [Binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) and [Hermite polynomials](wikipedia://en/195984) that he developed the Modified Hermite Transformation (MHT) in 1987.[^o9][^o10] The magnitude square functions of [Binomial-QMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial-QMF) filters were shown to be the unique maximally flat functions in a two-band PR-QMF design formulation.[^o11][^o12] He organized the first [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903) conference in the United States at NJIT in April 1990,[^o13] and, then in 1992[^o14] and 1994.[^o15] Together with Richard A. Haddad, he published the first [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903)-related engineering book in the literature entitled *Multiresolution Signal Decomposition: Transforms, Subbands and Wavelets* in 1992.[^o16]

He made contributions in the areas of optimal filter banks,[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22] nonlinear phase extensions of discrete Walsh-[Hadamard transform](wikipedia://en/634765)[^o23] and [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811),[^o24] [principal component analysis](wikipedia://en/76340) of first-order autoregressive process,[^o25] [sparse approximation](wikipedia://en/15951862),[^o26] [digital watermarking](wikipedia://en/1061511),[^o27] [^o28] [^o29] [^o30] [^o31] [^o32] [financial signal processing](wikipedia://en/36501659) and [quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_finance).[^o33][^o34][^o35][^o36]

Akansu was a founding director of the New Jersey Center for Multimedia Research (NJCMR), 1996–2000, and NSF Industry-University Cooperative Research Center (IUCRC) for Digital Video, 1998–2000. He was the vice president for research and development of the [IDT Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDT_Corporation), 2000–2001, the founding president and CEO of PixWave, Inc. (an [IDT Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDT_Entertainment) subsidiary) that has built the technology for secure [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer) video distribution over the Internet. He was an academic visitor at David Sarnoff Research Center ([Sarnoff Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnoff_Corporation)), at IBM's [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center), and at [Marconi Electronic Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marconi_Electronic_Systems).

He is an [IEEE Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Fellow) (since 2008) with the citation *for contributions to optimal design of transforms and filter banks for communications and multimedia security.*[^o37]

According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), as of October 2023, Akansu had a total of 25 doctorate students.[^o38]
