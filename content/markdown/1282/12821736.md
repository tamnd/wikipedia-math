# Presentation complex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Presentation complex"}

In [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), a **presentation complex** is a 2-dimensional [cell complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_complex) associated to any [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G*. The complex has a single vertex, and one loop at the vertex for each [generator](wikipedia://en/99945) of *G*. There is one 2-cell for each relation in the presentation, with the boundary of the 2-cell attached along the appropriate [word](wikipedia://en/12792326).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of the presentation complex is the group *G* itself.
- The [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of the presentation complex is a **Cayley complex** for *G*, whose 1-skeleton is the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of *G*.
- Any presentation complex for *G* is the 2-skeleton of an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(G,1)$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let $G= \mathbb{Z}^2$ be the two-dimensional integer [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246), with presentation

$$G=\langle x,y|xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\rangle.$$

Then the presentation complex for *G* is a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800), obtained by gluing the opposite sides of a square, the 2-cell, which are labelled *x* and *y*. All four corners of the square are glued into a single vertex, the 0-cell of the presentation complex, while a pair consisting of a longtitudal and meridian circles on the torus, intersecting at the vertex, constitutes its 1-skeleton.

The associated Cayley complex is a regular tiling of the [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) by unit squares. The 1-skeleton of this complex is a Cayley graph for $\mathbb{Z}^2$.

Let $G = \mathbb{Z}_2 *\mathbb{Z}_2$ be the [Infinite dihedral group](wikipedia://en/4296050), with presentation $\langle a,b \mid a^2,b^2 \rangle$. The presentation complex for $G$ is $\mathbb{RP}^2 \vee \mathbb{RP}^2$, the [wedge sum](wikipedia://en/684143) of [projective planes](wikipedia://en/24350). For each path, there is one 2-cell glued to each loop, which provides the standard [cell structure](wikipedia://en/353022#Examples) for each projective plane. The Cayley complex is an infinite string of spheres.[^o6]

:::
