# Charles L. Bouton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles L. Bouton"}

**Charles Leonard Bouton** (April 25, 1869 – February 20, 1922) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Charles L. Bouton was born in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis), Missouri, where his father was an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).[^o4] He studied in the public schools of St. Louis. He later received a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree from [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis).[^o4] In 1898 he received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University). His Ph.D. advisor was [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010).

He married Mary G. Spencer in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore) on June 15, 1907.[^o6]

## Teaching {#Teaching}

He taught at the Smith Academy, Washington University and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o4] From 1900 to 1902 Bouton was an editor of the *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)*.[^o4]

He died at his home in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) on February 20, 1922.[^o8]

## Publications {#Publications}

In 1902 Bouton published a solution of the game [Nim](wikipedia://en/21885).[^o10] This result is today viewed as the birth of [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231).
