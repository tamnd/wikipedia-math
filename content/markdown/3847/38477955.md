# Kelly's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kelly's lemma"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Kelly's lemma** states that for a stationary [continuous-time Markov chain](wikipedia://en/1125342), a process defined as the time-reversed process has the same stationary distribution as the forward-time process.[^o2] The theorem is named after [Frank Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kelly_%28mathematician%29).[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

For a continuous time Markov chain with an at most countable state space $S$ and [transition-rate matrix](wikipedia://en/72460002) $Q$ (with elements $q_{ij}$), if we can find a set of non-negative numbers $q_{ij}'$ and a positive measure $\pi$ that satisfy the following conditions:[^o2]

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{j \in S} q_{ij} &= \sum_{j \in S} q'_{ij} \quad \forall i\in S\\ \pi_i q_{ij} &= \pi_jq_{ji}' \quad \forall i,j \in S, \end{aligned}$$

then $q_{ij}'$ are the rates for the reversed process and $\pi$ is proportional to the stationary distribution for both processes.

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Given the assumptions made on the $q_{ij}$ and $\pi$ we have

$$\sum_{i \in S} \pi_i q_{ij} = \sum_{i \in S} \pi_j q'_{ji} = \pi_j \sum_{i \in S} q'_{ji} = \pi_j \sum_{i \in S} q_{ji} =\pi_j,$$

so the [global balance equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_balance_equation) are satisfied and the measure $\pi$ is proportional to the stationary distribution of the original process. By symmetry, the same argument shows that $\pi$ is also proportional to the stationary distribution of the reversed process.

:::

:::
