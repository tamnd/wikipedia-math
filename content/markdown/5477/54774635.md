# Gillian Thornley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gillian Thornley"}

**Gillian Thornley** is a retired New Zealander mathematician.

:::

Gillian Brown Thornley was born in 1940 in [Murchison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murchison,_New_Zealand), New Zealand. Living on the family dairy farm,[^o3] she won a scholarship to board at [Nelson College for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_College_for_Girls) and in 1958 enrolled at [Canterbury University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury) where she graduated with a masters with first class honours in mathematics in 1963. At Canterbury she was a contemporary of [Beatrice Tinsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Tinsley).[^o4]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Thornley received her PhD in metric differential geometry from the University of Toronto in 1963. She returned first to Canterbury then took up a two-year lectureship in Trinidad at the University of The West Indies. She then moved back to New Zealand (Nelson and Wellington), combining part-time positions in both academia and the public service (where she worked on economic modelling) with caring for her two young children.[^o3] She joined the mathematics institute at [Massey University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey_University), Palmerston North, remaining there from 1980 to her retirement in 2006.[^o6] In 1989 she was elected first woman President of the [New Zealand Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Mathematical_Society). Thornley presented at the 1990 Conference of the International Mathematics Organisation on the experience of women mathematicians in academia.[^o7] She also co-authored an article in 2001 on the experience of mathematics doctoral students in New Zealand.[^o8]

The Gillian Thornley Award was inaugurated by the New Zealand Mathematical Society in her honour in 2020.[^o9]
