# Ralph A. Bradley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph A. Bradley"}

**Ralph Allan Bradley** (November 28, 1923 – October 30, 2001) was a Canadian-American statistician and statistics educator, whose research lay in the fields of [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530), and [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Analysis). He is known for the [Bradley–Terry model](wikipedia://en/44439173) in [pairwise comparison](wikipedia://en/4395832)[^o2] and for founding the Department of Statistics at [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bradley was born in [Smiths Falls, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Falls,_Ontario), Canada and grew up in [Wellington, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington,_Ontario). He studied mathematics and physics at [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston), receiving an honors degree in 1944. After a stint in the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army), he returned to Queen's University and obtained an MA in 1946. He received his PhD in theoretical statistics in 1949 at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) under the supervision of [Harold Hotelling](wikipedia://en/935621).

Bradley was employed at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) from 1949 to 1950 and as a faculty member at [Virginia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Tech) from 1950 to 1958. In 1959, he moved to [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) to found the Department of Statistics there and stayed on until 1978 as the head of the department. In 1982, he moved to the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) as Research Professor of Statistics. Bradley retired from the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) in 1992. He continued to participate in research activities afterward and was named professor emeritus at both [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University) and [the University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia).[^o3][^o6]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Bradley was editor of the journal [Biometrics](wikipedia://en/11885692) from 1957 to 1962. He was Vice-President from 1975 to 1978 and President in 1981 of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). Bradley was a Fellow of the American Statistical Association and a Fellow of the Institute of Mathematical Statistics.[^o4] The statistics department of the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) organizes an annual lecture in his name.[^o8] In 1992 Bradley was awarded the [American Statistical Association's Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).[^o9]
