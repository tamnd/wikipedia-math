# Phase-field models on graphs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Phase-field models on graphs"}

**Phase-field models on graphs** are a discrete analogue to [phase-field models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-field_models), defined on a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806). They are used in [image analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_analysis) (for feature identification) and for the segmentation of [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network).

:::

## Graph Ginzburg–Landau functional {#Graph_Ginzburg–Landau_functional}

For a graph with vertices *V* and edge weights $\omega_{i,j}$, the graph Ginzburg–Landau functional of a map $u:V\to \mathbb{R}$ is given by

$$F_\varepsilon(u) = \frac\varepsilon2 \sum_{i,j\in V} \omega_{ij} (u_i-u_j)^2 + \frac1\varepsilon \sum_{i \in V} W(u_i),$$

where *W* is a double well potential, for example the quartic potential *W*(*x*) = *x*<sup>2</sup>(1 − *x*<sup>2</sup>). The graph Ginzburg–Landau functional was introduced by Bertozzi and Flenner.[^o3] In analogy to continuum phase-field models, where regions with *u* close to 0 or 1 are models for two phases of the material, vertices can be classified into those with *u*<sub>*j*</sub> close to 0 or close to 1, and for small $\varepsilon$, minimisers of $F_\varepsilon$ will satisfy that *u*<sub>*j*</sub> is close to 0 or 1 for most nodes, splitting the nodes into two classes.

## Graph Allen–Cahn equation {#Graph_Allen–Cahn_equation}

To effectively minimise $F_\varepsilon$, a natural approach is by [gradient flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient_flow) ([steepest descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steepest_descent)). This means to introduce an artificial time parameter and to solve the graph version of the [Allen–Cahn equation](wikipedia://en/37054750),

$$\frac{d}{dt} u_j = -\varepsilon (\Delta u)_j-\frac1\varepsilon W'(u_j),$$

where $\Delta$ is the [graph Laplacian](wikipedia://en/1448472). The ordinary continuum Allen–Cahn equation and the graph Allen–Cahn equation are natural counterparts, just replacing ordinary calculus by [calculus on graphs](wikipedia://en/61556353). A convergence result for a numerical graph Allen–Cahn scheme has been established by Luo and Bertozzi.[^o5]

It is also possible to adapt other computational schemes for [mean curvature flow](wikipedia://en/13110176), for example schemes involving [thresholding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thresholding_%28image_processing%29) like the Merriman–Bence–Osher scheme, to a graph setting, with analogous results.[^o6]
