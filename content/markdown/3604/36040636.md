# Duopyramid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Duopyramid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of 4 dimensions or higher, a **double pyramid**, **duopyramid**, or **fusil** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) constructed by 2 [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) polytopes with [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) connecting all pairs of [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) between the two. The term [fusil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusil_%28heraldry%29) is used by [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) as a [rhombic](wikipedia://en/183884)-shape.[^o2] The term *duopyramid* was used by George Olshevsky, as the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of a [duoprism](wikipedia://en/2114155).

:::

The lowest dimensional forms are 4 dimensional and connect two polygons. A ***p*-*q* duopyramid** or ***p*-*q* fusil**, represented by a composite [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p} + {q}, and [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) . The regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) can be seen as a 4-4 duopyramid or 4-4 fusil, , symmetry 4,2,4, order 128.

A *p-q duopyramid* or *p-q* fusil has [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) symmetry \[*p*,2,*q*\], order 4pq. When *p* and *q* are identical, the symmetry in [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) is doubled as *p*,2,*p* or \[2*p*,2<sup>\+</sup>,2*q*\], order 8*p*<sup>2</sup>.

Edges exist on all pairs of vertices between the *p*-gon and *q*-gon. The [1-skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of a *p*-*q* duopyramid represents edges of each *p* and *q* polygon and *pq* [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) between them.

### Geometry {#Geometry}

A *p*-*q* duopyramid can be seen as two regular planar polygons of *p* and *q* sides with the same center and orthogonal orientations in 4 dimensions. Along with the *p* and *q* edges of the two polygons, all permutations of vertices in one polygon to vertices in the other form edges. All faces are triangular, with one edge of one polygon connected to one vertex of the other polygon. The *p* and *q* sided polygons are *hollow*, passing through the polytope center and not defining faces. Cells are tetrahedra constructed as all permutations of edge pairs between each polygon.

It can be understood by analogy to the relation of the 3D [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) and their dual [bipyramids](wikipedia://en/4153) with Schläfli symbol { } + {*p*}, and a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) in 2D as { } + { }. A bipyramid can be seen as a 3D degenerated duopyramid, by adding an edge across the [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) { } on the inner axis, and adding intersecting interior triangles and tetrahedra connecting that new edge to p-gon vertices and edges.

Other nonuniform polychora can be called duopyramids by the same construction, as two orthogonal and co-centered polygons, connected with edges with all combinations of vertex pairs between the polygons. The symmetry will be the product of the symmetry of the two polygons. So a *rectangle-rectangle duopyramid* would be topologically identical to the uniform *4-4 duopyramid*, but a lower symmetry \[2,2,2\], order 16, possibly doubled to 32 if the two rectangles are identical.

### Coordinates {#Coordinates}

The coordinates of a p-q duopyramid (on a unit [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792)) can be given as:

$$(\cos\frac{2\pi i}{p},\sin\frac{2\pi i}{p},0,0), \quad i = 1 \dots p$$

$$(0,0,\cos\frac{2\pi j}{q},\sin\frac{2\pi j}{q}), \quad j = 1 \dots q$$

All pairs of vertices are connected by edges.

### Orthogonal projections {#Orthogonal_projections}

The 2n vertices of a *n-n duopyramid* can be orthogonally projected into two regular n-gons with edges between all vertices of each n-gon.

The regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) can be seen as a **4-4 duopyramid**, being dual to the **4-4 duoprism**, which is the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112). As a 4-4 duopyramid, the 16-cell's symmetry is \[4,2,4\], order 64, and doubled to 4,2,4, order 128 with the 2 central squares interchangeable. The regular 16-cell has a higher symmetry \[3,3,4\], order 384.
