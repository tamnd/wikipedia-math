# Basis pursuit denoising

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Basis pursuit denoising"}

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **basis pursuit denoising** (BPDN) refers to a [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) problem of the form

:::

$$\min_x \left(\frac{1}{2} \|y - Ax\|^2_2 + \lambda \|x\|_1\right),$$

where $\lambda$ is a parameter that controls the trade-off between [sparsity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_vector) and reconstruction fidelity, $x$ is an $N \times 1$ solution vector, $y$ is an $M \times 1$ vector of observations, $A$ is an $M \times N$ transform matrix and $M < N$. This is an instance of [convex optimization](wikipedia://en/1674411).

Some authors refer to basis pursuit denoising as the following closely related problem:

$$\min_x \|x\|_1 \text{ subject to } \|y - Ax\|^2_2 \le \delta,$$

which, for any given $\lambda$, is equivalent to the unconstrained formulation for some (usually unknown *a priori*) value of $\delta$. The two problems are quite similar. In practice, the unconstrained formulation, for which most specialized and efficient computational algorithms are developed, is usually preferred. The unconstrained formulation is NP-hard.[^o2]

Either types of basis pursuit denoising solve a [regularization](wikipedia://en/2009061) problem with a trade-off between having a small residual (making $y$ close to $Ax$ in terms of the squared error) and making $x$ simple in the [$\ell_1$-norm](wikipedia://en/45194) sense. It can be thought of as a mathematical statement of [Occam's razor](wikipedia://en/36797), finding the simplest possible explanation (i.e. one that yields $\min_x \|x\|_1$) capable of accounting for the observations $y$.

Exact solutions to basis pursuit denoising are often the best computationally tractable approximation of an underdetermined system of equations. Basis pursuit denoising has potential applications in statistics (see the [LASSO](wikipedia://en/22349350#Lasso_method) method of [regularization](wikipedia://en/2009061)), [image compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_compression) and [compressed sensing](wikipedia://en/11403316).

When $\delta = 0$, this problem becomes [basis pursuit](wikipedia://en/29276125).

Basis pursuit denoising was introduced by Chen and [Donoho](wikipedia://en/26137723) in 1994,[^o3] in the field of signal processing. In statistics, it is well known under the name [LASSO](wikipedia://en/22349350), after being introduced by [Tibshirani](wikipedia://en/31404681) in 1996.

## Solving basis pursuit denoising {#Solving_basis_pursuit_denoising}

The problem is a convex quadratic problem, so it can be solved by many general solvers, such as [interior-point methods](wikipedia://en/1622862). For very large problems, many specialized methods that are faster than interior-point methods have been proposed.

Several popular methods for solving basis pursuit denoising include the [in-crowd algorithm](wikipedia://en/33668408) (a fast solver for large, sparse problems[^o5]), [homotopy continuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_continuation), [fixed-point continuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-point_continuation) (a special case of the forward–backward algorithm[^o6]) and [spectral projected gradient for L1 minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_projected_gradient_for_L1_minimization) (which actually solves [LASSO](wikipedia://en/22349350#Lasso_method), a related problem).
