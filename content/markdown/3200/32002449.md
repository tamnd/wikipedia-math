# Ockham algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ockham algebra"}

In mathematics, an **Ockham algebra** is a [bounded](wikipedia://en/310878) [distributive lattice](wikipedia://en/310883) $L$ with a [dual endomorphism](wikipedia://en/600618), that is, an operation $\sim\colon L \to L$ satisfying

:::

- $$\sim (x \wedge y) ={} \sim x \vee {} \sim y,$$
- $$\sim(x \vee y) = {} \sim x \wedge {}\sim y,$$
- $$\sim 0 = 1,$$
- $$\sim 1 = 0.$$

They were introduced by Berman,[^o2] and were named after [William of Ockham](wikipedia://en/33617) by Urquhart.[^o3] Ockham algebras form a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753).

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of Ockham algebras include [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/54476844), [De Morgan algebras](wikipedia://en/8495580), [Kleene algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleene_algebra_%28with_involution%29), and [Stone algebras](wikipedia://en/32022697).

:::
