# Leo Zippin

*Image: Leo Zippin.jpeg.* Leo Zippin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Zippin"}

**Leo Zippin** (January 25, 1905 – May 11, 1995) was an American mathematician.[^o2] He is best known for solving [Hilbert's Fifth Problem](wikipedia://en/152760) with [Deane Montgomery](wikipedia://en/5280983) and [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871) in 1952.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Leo Zippin was born in 1905 to Bella Salwen and Max Zippin, who had emigrated to New York City from [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) in 1903. He did his [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate) and [graduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_degree) work at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1929. His doctoral adviser was [John Robert Kline](wikipedia://en/21005350).

Leo Zippin is the author of *The Uses Of Infinity*, and together with Deane Montgomery, the monograph *Topological Transformation Groups*. In 1952, he, along with [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871) and [Deane Montgomery](wikipedia://en/5280983) solved [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760).[^o4][^o5]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He married Frances Levinson in 1932. They had two children, [Nina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Baym), a prominent literary critic and literary historian, and Vivian.[^o7] He taught at [Queens College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College) in [Flushing, NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing,_NY).
