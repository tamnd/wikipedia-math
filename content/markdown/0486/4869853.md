# Richard Leibler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Leibler"}

**Richard A. Leibler** (;[^o3] March 18, 1914 – October 25, 2003) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [cryptanalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalyst). Richard Leibler was born in March 1914. He received his A.M. in mathematics from [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) and his Ph.D. from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) in 1939. While working at the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939), he and [Solomon Kullback](wikipedia://en/506420) formulated the [Kullback–Leibler divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052),[^o4] a measure of similarity between probability distributions which has found important applications in [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773) and [cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptology). Leibler is also credited by the NSA as having opened up "new methods of attack" in the celebrated [VENONA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venona_project) code-breaking project during 1949-1950;[^o5] this may be a reference to his joint paper with Kullback, which was published in the open literature in 1951 and was immediately noted by Soviet cryptologists.[^o6]

:::

He was director of the [Communications Research Division at the Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses#Center_for_Communications_and_Computing) from 1962 to 1977, during which he was the boss of [Jim Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844).[^o7] He was inducted into the [NSA Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Hall_of_Honor) for his efforts against the VENONA code.[^o8]
