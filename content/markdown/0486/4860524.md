# L

L

l

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="L"}

**L** ([minuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case): **l**) is the twelfth [letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_%28alphabet%29) of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), used in the [modern English alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet), the alphabets of other Western [European languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Europe) and others worldwide. Its name in English is [*el*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet#Letter_names) (pronounced ), plural *els*.[^o3]

:::

Lamedh may have come from a pictogram of an ox [goad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goad) or [cattle prod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_prod). Some have suggested that it represents a shepherd's staff.[^o6]

### Typographic variants {#Typographic_variants}

In most [sans-serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sans-serif) typefaces, the lowercase letter *ell* $\langle l\rangle$, written as the [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) l, may be difficult to distinguish from the uppercase letter "eye" $\langle I\rangle$ (written as the glyph I); in some [serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serif) typefaces, the glyph l may be confused with the glyph 1, the digit *[one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29)*. To avoid such confusion, some newer [computer fonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_font) (such as [Trebuchet MS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebuchet_MS)) have a [finial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finial_%28typography%29), a curve to the right at the bottom of the lowercase letter *ell*. Other style variants are provided in [script typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Script_typeface) and [display typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_typeface). All these variants of the letter are encoded in Unicode as 004C or 006C, allowing presentation to be chosen according to each context. For specialist mathematical and scientific use, there are a number of dedicated [codepoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codepoint) in the [Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Alphanumeric_Symbols_block).

In the *[Romain du Roi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romain_du_Roi)*, where the ascenders of lowercase letters have symmetrical serifs at the top, l has an extra serif to the left at the [mean line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_line) to distinguish it from capital I.[^o8]

Another means of reducing such confusion is to use symbol ℓ, which is a [cursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursive), handwriting-style lowercase form of the letter "ell". In Japan and Korea, for example, this is the symbol for the [liter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litre). (The [International Committee for Weights and Measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_for_Weights_and_Measures) recommends using L or l for the liter,[^o24] without specifying a typeface.) In [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode), the cursive form is encoded as 2113 from the "[letter-like symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterlike_Symbols_%28Unicode_block%29)" block. Unicode encodes an explicit symbol as 1D4C1.[^o9] The [TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX) syntax `<nowiki><math>\ell</math></nowiki>` renders it as $\ell$. In mathematical formulas, an italic form (*ℓ*) of the script ℓ is the norm.

### English {#English}

In [English orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_orthography), $\langle l\rangle$ usually represents the phoneme , which can have several sound values, depending on the speaker's accent, and whether it occurs before or after a vowel. In [Received Pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Received_Pronunciation), the [alveolar lateral approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alveolar_lateral_approximant) (the sound represented in [IPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet) by lowercase ) occurs before a vowel, as in *lip* or *blend*, while the [velarized alveolar lateral approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velarized_alveolar_lateral_approximant) (IPA ) occurs in *bell* and *milk*. This velarization does not occur in many European languages that use $\langle l\rangle$; it is also a factor making the pronunciation of $\langle l\rangle$ difficult for users of languages that lack $\langle l\rangle$ or have different values for it, such as [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language) or some southern dialects of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language). A medical condition or speech impediment restricting the pronunciation of $\langle l\rangle$ is known as [lambdacism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambdacism).

In English orthography, $\langle l\rangle$ is often silent in such words as *walk* or *could* (though its presence can modify the preceding vowel letter's value), and it is usually silent in such words as *palm* and *psalm*; however, there is some regional variation. L is the [eleventh most frequently used letter](wikipedia://en/6767165) in the English language.

### Other languages {#Other_languages}

$\langle l\rangle$ usually represents the sound or some other [lateral consonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_consonant). Common digraphs include $\langle \operatorname{ll}\rangle$, which has a value identical to $\langle l\rangle$ in English, but has the separate value [voiceless alveolar lateral fricative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_alveolar_lateral_fricative) (IPA ) in [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language), where it can appear in an initial position. In [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language), $\langle \operatorname{ll}\rangle$ represents (, , , , , or , depending on dialect).

A [palatal lateral approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatal_lateral_approximant) or palatal $\langle l\rangle$ (IPA ) occurs in many languages, and is represented by $\langle \operatorname{gli}\rangle$ in [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), $\langle \operatorname{ll}\rangle$ in [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language) and [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language), $\langle \operatorname{lh}\rangle$ in [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), and $\langle \text{ļ}\rangle$ in [Latvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvian_language).

In [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language), $\langle l\rangle$ generally represents l, but represents ɫ before $\langle a\rangle$, $\langle \imath\rangle$, $\langle o\rangle$, or $\langle u\rangle$.

In [Washo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washo_language), lower-case $\langle l\rangle$ represents a typical \[l\] sound, while upper-case $\langle L\rangle$ represents a [voiceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voicelessness) \[l̥\] sound, a bit like double $\langle \operatorname{ll}\rangle$ in [Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language).

### Other systems {#Other_systems}

The [International Phonetic Alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet) uses l to represent the [voiced alveolar lateral approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_lateral_approximant) and a [small cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_caps) ʟ to represent the [voiced velar lateral approximant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_velar_lateral_approximant).

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

- The capital letter L is used as the [currency sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_sign) for the [Albanian lek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_lek) and the [Honduran lempira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_lempira). It was often used, especially in handwriting, as the currency sign for the [Italian lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_lira). Historically, it was commonly used as a currency sign for the British [pound sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sterling) (to abbreviate the Latin [libra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_pound), a pound, see [£sd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/£sd)); in modern usage, it has been overtaken by the [pound sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_sign) (£), which is based on $\mathfrak{L}$, the [blackletter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackletter) form of the letter. In running text, its lower-case form (usually [italicised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italics)), *l*, was more often seen.
- The [Roman numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral) L represents the number [50](wikipedia://en/308628).[^o23]
- In the [International system of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_system_of_units), the [liter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liter) (or litre) is abbreviated using a lower-case l or ℓ, or an upper-case L. The latter form is used to avoid the risk of confusion between the letter l (el) and the numeral 1 (one) [^o24] (For ℓ, see above.)
- In watchmaking, the [ligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligne) (a traditional [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) measure of length still used in the industry) is abbreviated using an upper-case L.[^o25]
- In chemistry, *L* is used as a symbol (as is *N*${}_{A}$) for the [Avogadro constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avogadro_constant).

## Other representations {#Other_representations}

### Computing {#Computing}

The Latin letters $\langle L\rangle$ and $\langle l\rangle$ have [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) encodings 004C and 006C. These are the same [code points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) as those used in [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) and [ISO 8859](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_8859). There are also [precomposed character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precomposed_character) encodings for $\langle L\rangle$ and $\langle l\rangle$ with diacritics, for most of those listed above; the remainder are produced using [combining diacritics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combining_diacritic).

Variant forms of the letter have unique code points for specialist use: the [alphanumeric symbols set](wikipedia://en/971549) in mathematics and science, and [halfwidth and fullwidth forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfwidth_and_fullwidth_forms) for legacy [CJK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJK_characters) font compatibility.
