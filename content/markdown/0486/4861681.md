# Binomial number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binomial number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **binomial number** is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) which can be obtained by evaluating a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) containing two terms. It is a generalization of a [Cunningham number](wikipedia://en/15364270).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **binomial number** is an integer obtained by evaluating a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) containing two terms, also called a [binomial](wikipedia://en/542399). The form of this binomial is $x^n \!\pm y^n$, with $x > y$ and $n > 1$. However, since $x^n \!- y^n$ is always divisible by $x - y$, when studying the numbers generated from the version with the negative sign, they are usually divided by $x - y$ first. Binomial numbers formed this way form [Lucas sequences](wikipedia://en/734635). Specifically:

$U_n(a+b,ab) = \frac{a^n - b^n}{a-b},$ and $V_n(a+b,ab) = a^n \!+ b^n$

Binomial numbers are a generalization of [Cunningham numbers](wikipedia://en/15364270), and it will be seen that the Cunningham numbers are binomial numbers where $y = 1$. Other subsets of the binomial numbers are the [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_numbers) and the [repunits](wikipedia://en/215056).

:::

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Factorization of binomial numbers"}

## Factorization {#Factorization}

The main reason for studying these numbers is to obtain their [factorizations](wikipedia://en/15491). Aside from algebraic [factors](wikipedia://en/49492), which are obtained by factoring the underlying [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) (binomial) that was used to define the number, such as [difference of two squares](wikipedia://en/1102297) and [sum of two cubes](wikipedia://en/72077175), there are other [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) factors (called primitive prime factors, because for a given $x^n \!\pm y^n$ they do not factor $x^m \!\pm y^m$ with $m < n$, except for a small number of exceptions as stated in [Zsigmondy's theorem](wikipedia://en/5767229)) which occur seemingly at random, and it is these which the number theorist is looking for.

Some binomial numbers' underlying binomials have [Aurifeuillean factorizations](wikipedia://en/23683488), which can assist in finding prime factors. [Cyclotomic polynomials](wikipedia://en/171992) are also helpful in finding factorizations.

The amount of work required in searching for a factor is considerably reduced by applying Legendre's theorem. This theorem states that all factors of a binomial number are of the form $kn + 1$ if $n$ is [even](wikipedia://en/143135) or $2kn + 1$ if it is [odd](wikipedia://en/143135).

:::

## Observation {#Observation}

Some people write "binomial number" when they mean [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668), but this usage is not standard and is deprecated.
