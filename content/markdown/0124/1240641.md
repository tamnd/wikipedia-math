# Lickorish–Wallace theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lickorish–Wallace theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Lickorish–Wallace theorem** in the theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) states that any [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable), connected 3-manifold may be obtained by performing [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) on a [framed link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_link) in the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) with ±1 surgery coefficients. Furthermore, each component of the link can be assumed to be unknotted.

:::

The theorem was proved in the early 1960s by [W. B. R. Lickorish](wikipedia://en/11177096) and [Andrew H. Wallace](wikipedia://en/47781281), independently and by different methods. Lickorish's proof rested on the [Lickorish twist theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lickorish_twist_theorem), which states that any orientable [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) of a closed orientable [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) is generated by [Dehn twists](wikipedia://en/765970) along 3*g* − 1 specific simple closed curves in the surface, where *g* denotes the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of the surface. Wallace's proof was more general and involved adding handles to the boundary of a higher-dimensional ball.

A corollary of the theorem is that every closed, orientable 3-manifold bounds a [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected) compact [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795).

By using his work on automorphisms of non-orientable surfaces, Lickorish also showed that every closed, non-orientable, connected 3-manifold is obtained by Dehn surgery on a link in the non-orientable 2-sphere bundle over the circle. Similar to the orientable case, the surgery can be done in a special way which allows the conclusion that every closed, non-orientable 3-manifold bounds a compact 4-manifold.
