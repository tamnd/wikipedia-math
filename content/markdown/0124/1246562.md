# IPUMS

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="IPUMS"}

**IPUMS**, originally the *Integrated Public Use Microdata Series*,[^o2] is the world's largest individual-level population database. IPUMS consists of [microdata](wikipedia://en/21725173) samples from [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) *(**IPUMS-USA**)* and international *(**IPUMS-International**)* [census](wikipedia://en/6889) records, as well as data from U.S. and international surveys. The records are converted into a consistent format and made available to researchers through a web-based data dissemination and analysis system.[^o3]

:::

IPUMS is housed at the Institute for Social Research and Data Innovation (ISRDI), an interdisciplinary research center at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), under the direction of Professor [Steven Ruggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Ruggles).[^o4]

## Description {#Description}

IPUMS includes all persons enumerated in the [United States censuses](wikipedia://en/447689) from [1850](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1850_United_States_census) to [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_United_States_census) (though, the [1890 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890_United_States_census) is missing because it was destroyed in a fire) and from the [American Community Survey](wikipedia://en/592043) since 2000 and the [Current Population Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_Population_Survey) since 1962. IPUMS includes household-level data for United States Censuses from [1790](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1790_United_States_census) to [1840](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1840_United_States_census), due to the first six censuses only including the name of the head of household, with tallied household totals following.[^o6] IPUMS provides consistent variable names, coding schemes, and documentation across all the samples, facilitating the analysis of long-term change.[^o7]

IPUMS-International includes countries from Africa, Asia, Europe, and Latin America for 1960 forward. The database currently includes more than a billion individuals enumerated in 365 censuses from 94 countries around the world.[^o8] IPUMS-International converts census microdata for multiple countries into a consistent format, allowing for comparisons across countries and time periods. Special efforts are made to simplify use of the data while losing no meaningful information. Comprehensive documentation is provided in a coherent form to facilitate comparative analyses of social and economic change.[^o9]

Additional databases in the IPUMS family include the:

- [North Atlantic Population Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Population_Project) (NAPP)[^o10]
- IPUMS [National Historical Geographic Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Geographic_Information_System) (NHGIS)[^o11]
- IPUMS Health Surveys [^o12]
- IPUMS Global Health [^o13]
- IPUMS Time Use [^o14]

The *Journal of American History* described the effort as "One of the great archival projects of the past two decades."[^o15] Liens Socio, the French portal for the social sciences, gave IPUMS the only “best site” designation that has gone to any non-French website, writing “IPUMS est un projet absolument extraordinaire...époustouflante \[mind-blowing\]!” [^o16]

The official motto of IPUMS is "use it for good, never for evil."[^o17] All public IPUMS data and documentation are available online free of charge.
