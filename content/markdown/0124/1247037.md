# Marcel Grossmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marcel Grossmann"}

Grossmann Marcell

**Marcel Grossmann** (Grossmann Marcell; April 9, 1878 – September 7, 1936)[^o4] was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who was a friend and classmate of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). Grossmann came from an old Swiss family in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). His father managed a textile factory. He became a [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of Mathematics at the Federal Polytechnic School in Zürich, today the [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich), specializing in [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891).

:::

Grossmann was born to a Jewish family in Budapest.

## Career {#Career}

In 1900 Grossmann graduated from the [Federal Polytechnic School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Polytechnic_School) (ETH) and became an assistant to the geometer [Wilhelm Fiedler](wikipedia://en/42762029).[^o6] He continued to do research on [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610) and taught in high schools for the next seven years. In 1902, he earned his doctorate from the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) with the thesis *Ueber die metrischen Eigenschaften kollinearer Gebilde* (translated *On the Metrical Properties of Collinear Structures*) with Fiedler as advisor. In 1907, he was appointed full professor of descriptive geometry at the Federal Polytechnic School.[^o7]

As a professor of geometry, Grossmann organized summer courses for high school teachers. In 1910, he became one of the founders of the [Swiss Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Mathematical_Society). He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1912 at [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge)[^o8] and in 1920 at [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg).

### Collaborations with Albert Einstein {#Collaborations_with_Albert_Einstein}

[Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s friendship with Grossmann began with their school days in Zürich. Grossmann's careful and complete lecture notes at the Federal Polytechnic School proved to be a salvation for Einstein, who missed many lectures.[^o10] Grossmann's father helped Einstein get his job at the Swiss Patent Office in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern),[^o11] and it was Grossmann who helped to conduct the negotiations to bring Einstein back from Prague as a professor of physics at the Zurich Polytechnic. Grossmann was an expert in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus), the mathematical tools which would provide a proper mathematical framework for Einstein's work on gravity. Thus, it was natural that Einstein would enter into a scientific collaboration with Grossmann.[^o12]

It was Grossmann who emphasized the importance of a non-Euclidean geometry called [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) (also [elliptic geometry](wikipedia://en/236020)) to Einstein, which was a necessary step in the development of Einstein's [general theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_theory_of_relativity). [Abraham Pais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Pais)'s book[^o13] on Einstein suggests that Grossmann mentored Einstein in [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) theory as well. Grossmann introduced Einstein to the [absolute differential calculus](wikipedia://en/35456546), started by [Elwin Bruno Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Bruno_Christoffel)[^o14] and fully developed by [Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223) and [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180).[^o15] Grossmann facilitated Einstein's unique synthesis of mathematical and theoretical physics in what is still today considered the most elegant and powerful theory of gravity: the general theory of relativity. The collaboration of Einstein and Grossmann led to a ground-breaking paper, "Outline of a Generalized Theory of Relativity and of a Theory of Gravitation", which was published in 1913 and was one of the two fundamental papers which established Einstein's theory of gravity.[^o16]

## Death {#Death}

Grossmann died of [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis) in 1936.[^o18] The community of relativists celebrates Grossmann's contributions to physics by organizing [Marcel Grossmann meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICRANet#Marcel_Grossmann_meetings) every three years.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The [International Center for Relativistic Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Center_for_Relativistic_Astrophysics) presents the [Marcel Grossmann Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Center_for_Relativistic_Astrophysics). Each recipient receives a silver casting of the T. E. S. T. sculpture [^o20] by the artist A. Pierelli. Each year, an institution is selected and between two and six individual scientists are selected. Past institutional winners include the [Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_%28spacecraft%29) Scientific Collaboration ([ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Space_Agency)), AlbaNova University Center, [Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) ([IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHES)) and others. Past individual winners include [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137), [Tsung-Dao Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsung-Dao_Lee), [Christine Jones Forman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Jones_Forman) and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148).
