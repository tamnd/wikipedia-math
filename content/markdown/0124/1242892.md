# Hodge index theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hodge index theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hodge index theorem** for an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) *V* determines the [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_%28quadratic_form%29) of the [intersection pairing](wikipedia://en/3148602) on the [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) *C* on *V*. It says, roughly speaking, that the space spanned by such curves (up to [linear equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence)) has a one-dimensional subspace on which it is [positive definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_definite) (not uniquely determined), and decomposes as a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899) of some such one-dimensional subspace, and a complementary subspace on which it is [negative definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_definite).

:::

In a more formal statement, specify that *V* is a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [projective surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_surface), and let *H* be the [divisor class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class) on *V* of a [hyperplane section](wikipedia://en/3459767) of *V* in a given [projective embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_embedding). Then the intersection

$$H \cdot H = d\$$

where *d* is the [degree](wikipedia://en/2776558) of *V* (in that embedding). Let *D* be the vector space of rational divisor classes on *V*, up to [algebraic equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equivalence). The dimension of *D* is finite and is usually denoted by ρ(*V*). The Hodge index theorem says that the subspace spanned by *H* in *D* has a complementary subspace on which the intersection pairing is negative definite. Therefore, the signature (often also called *index*) is (1,ρ(*V*)-1).

The abelian group of divisor classes up to algebraic equivalence is now called the [Néron-Severi group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Néron-Severi_group); it is known to be a [finitely-generated abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_abelian_group), and the result is about its [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) with the rational number field. Therefore, ρ(*V*) is equally the rank of the Néron-Severi group (which can have a non-trivial [torsion subgroup](wikipedia://en/144052), on occasion).

This result was proved in the 1930s by [W. V. D. Hodge](wikipedia://en/384430), for varieties over the complex numbers, after it had been a conjecture for some time of the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424) (in particular, [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716), who in this case showed that ρ \< ∞). Hodge's methods were the [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) ones brought in by [Lefschetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz). The result holds over general ([algebraically closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed)) fields.
