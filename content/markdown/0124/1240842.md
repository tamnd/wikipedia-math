# Adjunction space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adjunction space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **adjunction space** (or **attaching space**) is a common construction in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) where one [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) is attached or "glued" onto another. Specifically, let $X$ and $Y$ be topological spaces, and let $A$ be a [subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) of $Y$. Let $f : A \rightarrow X$ be a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28topology%29) (called the **attaching map**). One forms the adjunction space $X \cup_f Y$ (sometimes also written as $X +_f Y$) by taking the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/1564380) of $X$ and $Y$ and identifying $a$ with $f(a)$ for all $a$ in $A$. Formally,

:::

$$X\cup_f Y = (X\sqcup Y) / \sim$$

where the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) $\sim$ is generated by $a\sim f(a)$ for all $a$ in $A$, and the quotient is given the [quotient topology](wikipedia://en/237213). As a set, $X \cup_f Y$ consists of the disjoint union of $X$ and ($Y-A$). The topology, however, is specified by the quotient construction.

Intuitively, one may think of $Y$ as being glued onto $X$ via the map $f$.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A common example of an adjunction space is given when *Y* is a closed *n*-[ball](wikipedia://en/160556) (or *cell*) and *A* is the boundary of the ball, the (*n*−1)-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859). Inductively attaching cells along their spherical boundaries to this space results in an example of a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022).
- Adjunction spaces are also used to define [connected sums](wikipedia://en/490134) of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). Here, one first removes open balls from *X* and *Y* before attaching the boundaries of the removed balls along an attaching map.
- If *A* is a space with one point then the adjunction is the [wedge sum](wikipedia://en/684143) of *X* and *Y*.
- If *X* is a space with one point then the adjunction is the quotient *Y*/*A*.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The continuous maps *h* : *X* ∪<sub>*f*</sub> *Y* → *Z* are in 1-1 correspondence with the pairs of continuous maps *h*<sub>*X*</sub> : *X* → *Z* and *h*<sub>*Y*</sub> : *Y* → *Z* that satisfy *h*<sub>*X*</sub>(*f*(*a*))=*h*<sub>*Y*</sub>(*a*) for all *a* in *A*.

In the case where *A* is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) subspace of *Y* one can show that the map *X* → *X* ∪<sub>*f*</sub> *Y* is a closed [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) and (*Y* − *A*) → *X* ∪<sub>*f*</sub> *Y* is an open embedding.

:::

## Categorical description {#Categorical_description}

The attaching construction is an example of a [pushout](wikipedia://en/582024) in the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051). That is to say, the adjunction space is [universal](wikipedia://en/32245) with respect to the following [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: AdjunctionSpace-01.svg.*

Here *i* is the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) and *Φ*<sub>*X*</sub>, *Φ*<sub>*Y*</sub> are the maps obtained by composing the quotient map with the canonical injections into the disjoint union of *X* and *Y*. One can form a more general pushout by replacing *i* with an arbitrary continuous map *g*—the construction is similar. Conversely, if *f* is also an inclusion the attaching construction is to simply glue *X* and *Y* together along their common subspace.
