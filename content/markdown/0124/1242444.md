# Pointed set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pointed set"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **pointed set**[^n1][^o3] (also **based set**[^n1] or **rooted set**[^o21]) is an [ordered pair](wikipedia://en/22362) $(X, x_0)$ where $X$ is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) and $x_0$ is an element of $X$ called the **base point** [^o3] (also spelled **basepoint**).[^o5]

:::

A [map](wikipedia://en/516931) between pointed a sets $(X, x_0)$ and $(Y, y_0)$—called a **based map**,[^o6] **pointed map**,[^o5] or **point-preserving map**[^n2]—is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) from $X$ to $Y$ that maps one basepoint to another, i.e. a map $f : X \to Y$ such that $f(x_0) = y_0$. A based map is usually denoted $f : (X, x_0) \to (Y, y_0)$.

Pointed sets are very simple [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364). In the sense of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200), a pointed set is a set $X$ together with a single [nullary operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullary_operation) $*: X^0 \to X$, which picks out the basepoint.[^o8] Pointed maps are the [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) of these algebraic structures.

The [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all pointed sets together with the class of all based maps forms a [category](wikipedia://en/5869). Every pointed set can be converted to an ordinary set by forgetting the basepoint (the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) is [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor)), but the reverse is not true.[^o9] In particular, the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) cannot be pointed, because it has no element that can be chosen as the basepoint.[^n3]

## Categorical properties {#Categorical_properties}

The category of pointed sets and based maps is equivalent to the category of sets and [partial functions](wikipedia://en/23577).[^n2] The base point serves as a "default value" for those arguments for which the partial function is not defined. One textbook notes that "This formal completion of sets and partial maps by adding 'improper', 'infinite' elements was reinvented many times, in particular, in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) ([one-point compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-point_compactification)) and in [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392)."[^o13] This category is also isomorphic to the [coslice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coslice_category) ($\mathbf{1} \downarrow \mathbf{Set}$), where $\mathbf{1}$ is (a functor that selects) a singleton set, and $\scriptstyle {\mathbf{Set}}$ (the identity functor of) the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829).[^o9][^o14] This coincides with the algebraic characterization, since the unique map $\mathbf{1} \to \mathbf{1}$ extends the [commutative triangles](wikipedia://en/138484) defining arrows of the coslice category to form the [commutative squares](wikipedia://en/138484) defining homomorphisms of the algebras.

There is a [faithful functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor) from pointed sets to usual sets, but it is not full and these categories are not [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051).[^o9]

The category of pointed sets is a [pointed category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointed_category). The pointed [singleton sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_set) $(\{a\}, a)$ are both [initial objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) and [terminal objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object),[^n1] i.e. they are [zero objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object).[^o5] The category of pointed sets and pointed maps has both [products](wikipedia://en/237868) and [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889), but it is not a [distributive category](wikipedia://en/6053993). It is also an example of a category where $0 \times A$ is not isomorphic to $0$.[^n3]

## Applications {#Applications}

Many [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/1021753) rely on a distinguished point. For example, [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) are pointed sets by choosing the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) as the basepoint, so that [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396) are point-preserving maps.[^o19] This observation can be restated in category theoretic terms as the existence of a [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) from groups to pointed sets.[^o19]

A pointed set may be seen as a [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666) under the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology) or as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [field with one element](wikipedia://en/13798040).[^o20]

As "rooted set" the notion naturally appears in the study of [antimatroids](wikipedia://en/667063)[^o21] and transportation polytopes.[^o22]
