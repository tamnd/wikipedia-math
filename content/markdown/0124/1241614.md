# Atoroidal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atoroidal"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **atoroidal** [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) is one that does not contain an essential [torus](wikipedia://en/74800). There are two major variations in this terminology: an essential torus may be defined geometrically, as an [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119), non-[boundary parallel](wikipedia://en/1241623), [incompressible](wikipedia://en/896174) [torus](wikipedia://en/74800), or it may be defined algebraically, as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$ of its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) that is not [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176#Conjugacy_of_subgroups_and_general_subsets) to a [peripheral subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_subgroup) (i.e., the image of the map on fundamental group induced by an inclusion of a boundary component). The terminology is not standardized, and different authors require atoroidal 3-manifolds to satisfy certain additional restrictions. For instance:

:::

- (txt) gives a definition of atoroidality that combines both geometric and algebraic aspects, in terms of maps from a torus to the manifold and the induced maps on the fundamental group. He then notes that for [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29) [boundary-incompressible](wikipedia://en/25257756) 3-manifolds this gives the algebraic definition.[^o3]
- (txt) uses the algebraic definition without additional restrictions.[^o5]
- (txt) uses the geometric definition, restricted to irreducible manifolds.[^o7]
- (txt) requires the algebraic variant of atoroidal manifolds (which he calls simply atoroidal) to avoid being one of three kinds of [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526). He makes the same restriction on geometrically atoroidal manifolds (which he calls topologically atoroidal) and in addition requires them to avoid incompressible boundary-parallel embedded [Klein bottles](wikipedia://en/17412). With these definitions, the two kinds of atoroidality are equivalent except on certain [Seifert manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert_manifold).[^o9]

A 3-manifold that is not atoroidal is called **toroidal**.
