# Moment problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moment problem"}

*Image: Standard\_deviation\_diagram.svg.* Example: Given the mean and variance $\sigma^2$ (as well as all further [cumulants](wikipedia://en/359684) equal 0) the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) is the distribution solving the moment problem.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **moment problem** arises as the result of trying to invert the mapping that takes a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ to the sequence of [moments](wikipedia://en/368684)

:::

$$m_n = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x^n \,d\mu(x)\,.$$

More generally, one may consider

$$m_n = \int_{-\infty}^\infty M_n(x) \,d\mu(x)\,.$$

for an arbitrary sequence of functions $M_n$.

## Introduction {#Introduction}

In the classical setting, $\mu$ is a measure on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line), and $M$ is the sequence $\{x^n : n=1,2,\dotsc\}$. In this form the question appears in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), asking whether there is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) having specified [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean), [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) and so on, and whether it is unique.

There are three named classical moment problems: the [Hamburger moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216491) in which the [support](wikipedia://en/381013) of $\mu$ is allowed to be the whole real line; the [Stieltjes moment problem](wikipedia://en/3242790), for $[0,\infty)$; and the [Hausdorff moment problem](wikipedia://en/3216387) for a bounded interval, which [without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000) may be taken as $[0,1]$.

The moment problem also extends to [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) as the [trigonometric moment problem](wikipedia://en/11034989) in which the Hankel matrices are replaced by [Toeplitz matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toeplitz_matrices) and the support of $\mu$ is the [complex unit circle](wikipedia://en/508177) instead of the real line.[^n1]

## Existence {#Existence}

A sequence of numbers $m_n$ is the sequence of moments of a measure $\mu$ if and only if a certain positivity condition is fulfilled; namely, the [Hankel matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hankel_matrices) $H_n$,

$$(H_n)_{ij} = m_{i+j}\,,$$

should be [positive semi-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix). This is because a positive-semidefinite Hankel matrix corresponds to a linear functional $\Lambda$ such that $\Lambda(x^n) = m_n$ and $\Lambda(f^2) \geq 0$ (non-negative for sum of squares of polynomials). Assume $\Lambda$ can be extended to $\mathbb{R}[x]^*$. In the univariate case, a non-negative polynomial can always be written as a sum of squares. So the linear functional $\Lambda$ is positive for all the non-negative polynomials in the univariate case. By Haviland's theorem, the linear functional has a measure form, that is $\Lambda(x^n) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^n d \mu$. A condition of similar form is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a measure $\mu$ supported on a given interval $[a,b]$.

One way to prove these results is to consider the linear functional $\varphi$ that sends a polynomial

$$P(x) = \sum_k a_k x^k$$

to

$$\sum_k a_k m_k.$$

If $m_k$ are the moments of some measure $\mu$ supported on $[a,b]$, then evidently

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

\varphi(P) \ge 0$ for any polynomial $P$ that is non-negative on $[a,b]$. (1)

:::

Vice versa, if ((1)) holds, one can apply the [M. Riesz extension theorem](wikipedia://en/9179665) and extend $\varphi$ to a functional on the [space of continuous functions with compact support](wikipedia://en/340630#Functional_analysis) $C_c([a,b])$), so that

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

\varphi(f) \ge 0$ for any $f \in C_c([a,b]),\;f\ge 0. (2)

:::

By the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452#The_representation_theorem_for_linear_functionals_on_Cc.28X.29), ((2)) holds iff there exists a measure $\mu$ supported on $[a,b]$, such that

$$\varphi(f) = \int f \, d\mu$$

for every $f \in C_c([a,b])$.

Thus the existence of the measure $\mu$ is equivalent to ((1)). Using a representation theorem for positive polynomials on $[a,b]$, one can reformulate ((1)) as a condition on Hankel matrices.[^n2][^n3]

## Uniqueness (or determinacy) {#Uniqueness_(or_determinacy)}

The uniqueness of $\mu$ in the Hausdorff moment problem follows from the [Weierstrass approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_approximation_theorem), which states that [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) are [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) under the [uniform norm](wikipedia://en/349755) in the space of [continuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_functions) on $[0,1]$. For the problem on an infinite interval, uniqueness is a more delicate question.[^n4] There are distributions, such as [log-normal distributions](wikipedia://en/102476), which have finite moments for all the positive integers but where other distributions have the same moments.

## Formal solution {#Formal_solution}

When the solution exists, it can be formally written using derivatives of the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021) as

$$d\mu(x) = \rho(x) dx, \quad \rho(x) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{n!}\delta^{(n)}(x)m_n.$$

The expression can be derived by taking the inverse Fourier transform of its [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401).

## Variations {#Variations}

An important variation is the [truncated moment problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_moment_problem), which studies the properties of measures with fixed first $k$ moments (for a finite $k$). Results on the truncated moment problem have numerous applications to [extremal problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_problem), optimisation and limit theorems in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^n3]

:::

## Probability {#Probability}

The moment problem has applications to probability theory. The following is commonly used:[^o16]

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem (Fréchet-Shohat)"}

**Theorem (Fréchet-Shohat).**

If $\mu$ is a determinate measure (i.e. its moments determine it uniquely), and the measures $\mu_n$ are such that

$$\forall k \geq 0 \quad \lim _{n \rightarrow \infty} m_k\left[\mu_n\right]=m_k[\mu],$$

then $\mu_n \rightarrow \mu$ in distribution.

:::

By checking [Carleman's condition](wikipedia://en/9442947), we know that the standard normal distribution is a determinate measure, thus we have the following form of the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406):

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=math-theorem name="Corollary"}

**Corollary.**

If a sequence of probability distributions $\nu_n$ satisfy

$$m_{2k}[\nu_n] \to \frac{(2k)!}{2^k k!}; \quad m_{2k+1}[\nu_n] \to 0$$

then $\nu_n$ converges to $N(0, 1)$ in distribution.

:::
