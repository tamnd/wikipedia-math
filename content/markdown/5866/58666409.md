# Nonlinear algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nonlinear algebra"}

**Nonlinear algebra** is the nonlinear analogue to [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), generalizing notions of spaces and transformations coming from the linear setting.[^o2] [Algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) is one of the main areas of mathematical research supporting nonlinear algebra, while major components coming from [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833) support the development of the area into maturity.

:::

The topological setting for nonlinear algebra is typically the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744), where closed sets are the algebraic sets. Related areas in mathematics are [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041), [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), and [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization).

## Algebraic geometry {#Algebraic_geometry}

Nonlinear algebra is closely related to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where the main objects of study include [algebraic equations](wikipedia://en/404001), [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808), and [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754).

## Computational nonlinear algebra {#Computational_nonlinear_algebra}

Current methods in computational nonlinear algebra can be broadly broken into two domains: symbolic and numerical. Symbolic methods often rely on the computation of [Gröbner bases](wikipedia://en/357328) and [resultants](wikipedia://en/2195020).[^o5] On the other hand, [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/52802478) typically use algebraically founded [homotopy continuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_continuation), with a base field of the complex numbers.[^o6]
