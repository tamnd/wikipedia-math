# Hans-Joachim Bremermann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans-Joachim Bremermann"}

**Hans-Joachim Bremermann** (1926 – 1996) was a German-American mathematician and [biophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysicist). He worked on [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution), introducing ideas of how mating generates new gene combinations. [Bremermann's limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremermann's_limit), named after him, is the maximum computational speed of a self-contained system in the material universe.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Bremermann was born in 1926, in [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), to Bernard Bremermann and Berta Wicke.[^o7]

Bremermann undertook doctoral studies at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster), completing his *[Staatsexamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsexamen)* in mathematics and physics in 1951. In the same year, his doctoral dissertation *Die Charakterisierung von Regularitätsgebieten durch pseudokonvexe Funktionen* was published, in which he solved a special case of the [Levi problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_problem). He was to become a specialist in complex analysis.

## Career {#Career}

Bremermann came to the United States in 1952 and held a research associate position at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). In 1953, he was appointed a research fellow at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). He returned to Münster for 1954–55.[^o7]

After returning to the United States, he was a mathematics researcher at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (1955–57), and then appointed assistant professor at the [University of Washington, Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington,_Seattle) (1957–58). He then spent another year researching at Princeton (1958–59), this time in physics.[^o7]

In 1959, he became an associate professor of mathematics at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he remained for the rest of his career, being promoted to full professor in 1966. He held chairs at Berkeley in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics).[^o7] By the 1960s, his work had turned towards the theory of computation and evolutionary biology, in which he studied [complexity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity_theory_%28computation%29), [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_programming) [search algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_algorithm), and [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706).[^o7]

In 1978, he gave the "What Physicists Do" series of lectures at [Sonoma State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_State_University), discussing physical limitations to mathematical understanding of physical and biological systems.[^o7] He continued work in mathematical biology through the 1980s, developing mathematical models of parasites and disease, neural networks, and [AIDS epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS_epidemiology) and pathology. He retired from the University of California in 1991.[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On 16 May 1954, Bremermann married Maria Isabel Lopez Perez-Ojeda, a scholar of romance language and literature.[^o7] He died in 1996, aged 69 or 70.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

R.W. Anderson writes:

> \[Bremermann\] continued to develop mathematical modelling as a tool to understanding complex (especially biological) systems for the rest of his life. His intellectual journey was marked by brilliant insight and foresight. \[...\] Hans Bremermann is not only remembered for his genius but also for his warmth, generosity, courage, integrity, humility, and love.[^o7]

In 1995, a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) was published with a brief biography and 13 scientific papers of his former students and colleagues in a special issue of *[BioSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BioSystems)*.[^o8]
