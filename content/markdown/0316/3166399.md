# Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn"}

De BruijnDe Bruijn

*Image: Prof. dr. N. G. de Bruyn, 1947.jpg.* Prof. dr. N. G. de Bruyn, 1947

**Nicolaas Govert** "**Dick**" **de Bruijn** (; 9 July 1918 – 17 February 2012) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), noted for his many contributions in the fields of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

De Bruijn was born in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague) where he attended elementary school between 1924 and 1930 and secondary school until 1934. He started studies in mathematics at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) in 1936 but his studies were interrupted by the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) in 1939. He became a full-time Assistant in the Department of Mathematics of the [Technological University of Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology) in September 1939 while continuing his studies.[^o5] He completed his undergraduate studies at the University of Leiden in 1941. He received his PhD in 1943 from the *[Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam)* with a thesis entitled "*Over modulaire vormen van meer veranderlijken*" advised by [Jurjen Ferdinand Koksma](wikipedia://en/13283214).

From June 1944 he was a Scientific Associate working in [Philips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips) Research Laboratories in [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven).

He married Elizabeth de Groot on 30 August 1944. The couple had four children: Jorina Aleida (born 19 January 1947), Frans Willem (born 13 April 1948), Elisabeth (born 24 November 1950), and Judith Elizabeth (born 31 March 1963).[^o5]

De Bruijn started his academic career at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), where he was Professor of Mathematics from 1952 to 1960. In 1960 he moved to the [Technical University Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_Eindhoven) where he was Professor of Mathematics until his retirement in 1984.[^o3] Among his graduate students were [Johannes Runnenburg](wikipedia://en/40668679) (1960), Antonius Levelt (1961), S. Ackermans (1964), Jozef Beenakker (1966), W. van der Meiden (1967), Matheus Hautus (1970), Robert Nederpelt Lazarom (1973), Lambert van Benthem Jutting (1977), A. Janssen (1979), Diederik van Daalen (1980), and Harmannus Balsters (1986).[^o6]

In 1957 he was appointed member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o7] He was Knighted with the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).

## Work {#Work}

De Bruijn covered many areas of mathematics. He is especially noted for:

- the discovery of the [De Bruijn sequence](wikipedia://en/1565267),
- discovering an [algebraic theory](wikipedia://en/24497685) of the [Penrose tiling](wikipedia://en/26611936) and, more generally, discovering the "projection" and "multigrid" methods for constructing quasi-periodic tilings,[^o9][^o10]
- the [De Bruijn–Newman constant](wikipedia://en/45265), related to the Riemann hypothesis
- the *[De Bruijn–Erdős theorem](wikipedia://en/22728378)*, in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/22728378), on coloring an infinite graph
- a different theorem of the same name: the *De Bruijn–Erdős theorem* in [incidence geometry](wikipedia://en/26825148), on the number of lines determined by n points in a plane
- the [BEST theorem](wikipedia://en/1455603) in graph theory, on Eulerian circuits, and
- [De Bruijn indices](wikipedia://en/10314482) in the lambda calculus.

He wrote one of the standard books in advanced [asymptotic analysis](wikipedia://en/641995) (De Bruijn, 1958).

In the late sixties, he designed the [Automath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automath) language for representing mathematical proofs, so that they could be verified automatically (see [automated theorem checking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_theorem_checking)). Shortly before his death, he had been working on models for the [human brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_brain).
