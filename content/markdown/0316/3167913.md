# Smoluchowski coagulation equation

*Image: Smoluchowski Aggregation Kinetics.svg.* This diagram describes the aggregation kinetics of discrete particles according to the Smoluchowski aggregation equation.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smoluchowski coagulation equation"}

In [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), the **Smoluchowski coagulation equation** is a [population balance equation](wikipedia://en/19875582) introduced by [Marian Smoluchowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Smoluchowski) in a seminal 1916 publication,[^o2] describing the [time evolution](wikipedia://en/937739) of the [number density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_density) of particles as they coagulate (in this context "clumping together") to size *x* at time *t*.

:::

Simultaneous coagulation (or aggregation) is encountered in processes involving [polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization),[^o3] [coalescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalescence_%28chemistry%29) of [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosols),[^o4] [emulsication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emulsion),[^o5] [flocculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flocculation).[^o6]

## Equation {#Equation}

The distribution of particle size changes in time according to the interrelation of all particles of the system. Therefore, the Smoluchowski coagulation equation is an [integrodifferential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrodifferential_equation) of the particle-size distribution. In the case when the sizes of the coagulated particles are [continuous variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_variable), the equation involves an [integral](wikipedia://en/15532):

$$\frac{\partial n(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\int^x_0K(x-y,y)n(x-y,t)n(y,t)\,dy - \int^\infty_0K(x,y)n(x,t)n(y,t)\,dy.$$

If *dy* is interpreted as a [counting measure](wikipedia://en/191786), i.e. when particles join in [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_variable) sizes, then the discrete form of the equation is a [summation](wikipedia://en/246160):

$$\frac{\partial n(x_i,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum^{i-1}_{j=1} K(x_i-x_j,x_j)n(x_i-x_j,t)n(x_j,t) - \sum^\infty_{j=1}K(x_i,x_j)n(x_i,t)n(x_j,t).$$

There exists a unique solution for a chosen [kernel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_function).[^o8]

## Coagulation kernel {#Coagulation_kernel}

The [operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator), *K*, is known as the coagulation [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_kernel) and describes the rate at which particles of size $x_1$ coagulate with particles of size $x_2$. [Analytic solutions](wikipedia://en/585143) to the equation exist when the kernel takes one of three simple forms:

$$K = 1,\quad K = x_1 + x_2, \quad K = x_1x_2,$$

known as the [constant](wikipedia://en/24758132), [additive](wikipedia://en/22473529), and [multiplicative](wikipedia://en/20059) kernels respectively.[^o10] For the case $K = 1$ it could be mathematically proven that the solution of Smoluchowski coagulation equations have asymptotically the [dynamic scaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_scaling) property.[^o11] This self-similar behaviour is closely related to [scale invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_invariance) which can be a characteristic feature of a [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423).

However, in most practical applications the kernel takes on a significantly more complex form. For example, the free-molecular kernel which describes [collisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision) in a dilute [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas)-[phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28matter%29) system,

$$K = \sqrt{\frac{\pi k_B T}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{m(x_1)}+\frac{1}{m(x_2)}\right)^{1/2}\left(d(x_1)+d(x_2)\right)^2.$$

Some coagulation kernels account for a specific [fractal dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) of the clusters, as in the [diffusion-limited aggregation](wikipedia://en/1312515):

$$K = \frac{2}{3} \frac{ k_B T} {\eta} \left(x_1^{1/y_1} +x_2^{1/y_2}\right)\left(x_1^{-1/y_1} +x_2^{-1/y_2}\right),$$

or Reaction-limited aggregation:

$$K = \frac{2}{3} \frac{ k_B T} {\eta} \frac{(x_1x_2)^\gamma}{W}\left(x_1^{1/y_1} +x_2^{1/y_2}\right)\left(x_1^{-1/y_1} +x_2^{-1/y_2}\right),$$

where $y_1,y_2$ are [fractal dimensions](wikipedia://en/285907) of the clusters, $k_B$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the temperature, $W$ is the Fuchs stability ratio, $\eta$ is the continuous phase viscosity, and $\gamma$ is the exponent of the product kernel, usually considered a fitting parameter.[^o12] For cloud, the kernel for coagulation of cloud particles are usually expressed as:

$$K = \pi [r(x_1)+r(x_2)]^2 |v(x_1)-v(x_2)| E_{coll}(x_1,x_2),$$

where $r(x)$ and $v(x)$ are the radius and fall speed of the cloud particles usually expressed using power law.

Generally the coagulation equations that result from such physically realistic kernels are not solvable, and as such, it is necessary to appeal to [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/21506). Most of [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic) methods can be used when there is only one particle property (*x*) of interest, the two principal ones being the [method of moments](wikipedia://en/5456797)[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17] and sectional methods.[^o18] In the [multi-variate](wikipedia://en/615222) case, however, when two or more properties (such as size, shape, composition, etc.) are introduced, one has to seek special approximation methods that suffer less from [curse of dimensionality](wikipedia://en/787776). Approximation based on Gaussian [radial basis functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radial_basis_functions) has been successfully applied to the coagulation equation in more than one dimension.[^o19][^o20]

When the accuracy of the solution is not of primary importance, [stochastic particle (Monte Carlo) methods](wikipedia://en/56098) are an attractive alternative. Through this method, to compute the coagulation rates for different coagulation events, the simulation entries are virtualized to be equally weighted. The accuracy of this transformation can be adjusted such that just those coagulation events are considered while keeping the number of simulation entries constant.[^o21]
