# Robert C. Prim

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert C. Prim"}

**Robert Clay Prim III** (September 25, 1921 – November 18, 2021) was an American mathematician and computer scientist.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Robert Clay Prim III was born in [Sweetwater, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweetwater,_Texas) on September 25, 1921.[^o3] In 1941, Prim received his B.S. in [Electrical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_Engineering) from [The University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Texas_at_Austin),[^o4] where he also met his wife Alice (Hutter) Prim (1921–2009), whom he married in 1942. Later in 1949, he received his Ph.D. in [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he also worked as a research associate from 1948 until 1949.

During the climax of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) (1941–1944), Prim worked as an engineer for [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric). From 1944 until 1949, he was hired by the [United States Naval Ordnance Lab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Ordnance_Lab) as an engineer and later a mathematician. At [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), he served as director of mathematics research from 1958 to 1961. There, Prim developed [Prim's algorithm](wikipedia://en/53783). Also during his tenure at Bell Labs, Robert Prim assisted the Weapons Reliability Committee at Sandia National Laboratory chaired by [Walter McNair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_McNair) in 1951.[^o5] After Bell Laboratories, Prim became vice president of research at [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories).

During his career at Bell Laboratories, Robert Prim along with coworker [Joseph Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kruskal) developed two different algorithms (see [greedy algorithm](wikipedia://en/89247)) for finding a [minimum spanning tree](wikipedia://en/41795) in a weighted [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), a basic stumbling block in [computer network design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network). His self-named algorithm, [Prim's algorithm](wikipedia://en/53783), was originally discovered in 1930 by mathematician [Vojtěch Jarník](wikipedia://en/2175894) and later independently by Prim in 1957. It was later rediscovered by [Edsger Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_Dijkstra) in 1959. It is sometimes referred to as the *DJP algorithm* or the *Jarník algorithm*.

Prim was also the co-developer of [Prim–Read theory](wikipedia://en/61113330), a [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) concept that calculates the best possible [anti-ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ballistic_missile) (ABM) defensive layout. This demonstrated that the cost of the defence was always dramatically more than the offensive missiles needed to overwhelm it, and was one of the major reasons the US did not deploy an ABM system at the time.

Robert C. Prim died in [San Clemente, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente,_California) on November 18, 2021, at the age of 100.[^o6]
