# Bishop–Cannings theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bishop–Cannings theorem"}

The **Bishop–Cannings theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [evolutionary game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_game_theory). It states that (i) all members of a mixed [evolutionarily stable strategy](wikipedia://en/9616) (ESS) have the same payoff (Theorem 2), and (ii) that none of these can also be a pure ESS[^o2] (from their Theorem 3). The usefulness of the results comes from the fact that they can be used to directly find [ESSs](wikipedia://en/9616) [algebraically](wikipedia://en/18716923), rather than [simulating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulating) the game and solving it by [iteration](wikipedia://en/68833).

:::

The theorem was formulated by Tim Bishop and Chris Cannings at [Sheffield University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_University), who published it in 1978.

A review is given by [John Maynard Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Smith) in his book *[Evolution and the Theory of Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_and_the_Theory_of_Games)*, with proof in the appendix.[^o3]
