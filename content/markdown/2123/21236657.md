# Cylindrical σ-algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cylindrical σ-algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) — the **cylindrical σ-algebra**[^o2] or **product σ-algebra**[^o3][^o4] is a type of [σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/29586) which is often used when studying [product measures](wikipedia://en/2986564) or [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) on [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).

:::

For a product space, the cylinder σ-algebra is the one that is [generated](wikipedia://en/29586#σ-algebra_generated_by_an_arbitrary_family) by [cylinder sets](wikipedia://en/2613658).

In the context of a Banach space $X$ and its [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of [continuous linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) $X',$ the cylindrical σ-algebra $\mathfrak{A}(X,X')$ is defined to be the coarsest σ-algebra (that is, the one with the fewest measurable sets) such that every [continuous linear function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_function) on $X$ is a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055). In general, $\mathfrak{A}(X,X')$ is *not* the same as the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_σ-algebra) on $X,$ which is the coarsest σ-algebra that contains all open subsets of $X.$

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider two [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752) $N$ and $M$ with [dual pairing](wikipedia://en/63735167) $\langle,\rangle:=\langle,\rangle_{N,M}$, then we can define the so called [Borel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_algebra) cylinder sets

$$C_{f_1,\dots,f_m,B}=\{x\in N\colon (\langle x,f_1\rangle,\dots,\langle x,f_m\rangle)\in B\}$$

for some $f_1,\dots,f_m\in M$ and $B\in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^m)$. The family of all these sets is denoted as $\mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$. Then

$$\operatorname{Cyl}(N,M)=\bigotimes_n \mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$$

is called the **cylindrical algebra**. Equivalently one can also look at the open cylinder sets and get the same algebra.

The **cylindrical σ-algebra** $\mathfrak{A}(N,M)=\sigma(\operatorname{Cyl}(N,M))$ is the σ-algebra generated by the cylindrical algebra.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Let $X$ a Hausdorff locally convex space which is also a hereditarily [Lindelöf space](wikipedia://en/310480), then

$\mathfrak{A}(X,X')=\mathcal{B}(X).$[^o10]

:::
