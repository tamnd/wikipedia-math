# John Chambers (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Chambers"}

**John McKinley Chambers** (born April 28, 1941) is the creator of the [S programming language](wikipedia://en/919313), and core member of the [R programming language](wikipedia://en/376707) project. He was awarded the 1998 [ACM Software System Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Software_System_Award) for developing S.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

John McKinley Chambers was born on April 28, 1941, in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), Ontario.[^o6] He received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1963. He received a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in 1965 and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree in 1966, both in statistics, from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o3][^o7][^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Chambers started at [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories) in 1966 as a member of its technical staff.[^o3][^o8] From 1981 to 1983, he was the head of its Advanced Software Department and from 1983 to 1989 he was the head of its Statistics and Data Analysis Research Department.[^o3][^o8] In 1989, he moved back to full-time research and in 1995, he became a distinguished member of the technical staff.[^o3][^o8] In 1997, he was made the first [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of Bell Labs and was cited for "pioneering contributions to the field of statistical computing".[^o3] He remained a distinguished member of the technical staff and a Fellow until his retirement from Bell Labs in 2005.[^o8]

After retiring from Bell Labs, Chambers became a visiting professor at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) and [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o8][^o10] Since 2008, he has been active at Stanford, currently serving as Senior Advisor of its [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904) program and an adjunct professor in Stanford's Department of Statistics.[^o8]

Chambers is a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) and the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o8][^o7]

## Awards and accomplishments {#Awards_and_accomplishments}

Chambers has received the following awards:

- 1998, awarded the [ACM Software System Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Software_System_Award) for developing the [S programming language](wikipedia://en/919313). The award was presented on May 15, 1999.[^o3]
- 2004, awarded an [honorary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) Doctor of Mathematics degree from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo)[^o8]

### John M. Chambers Statistical Software Award {#John_M._Chambers_Statistical_Software_Award}

Following his 1998 ACM Software System Award, Chambers donated his prize money (US\$10,000) to the American Statistical Association to endow an award for novel statistical software, the *John M. Chambers Statistical Software Award*.[^o13]
