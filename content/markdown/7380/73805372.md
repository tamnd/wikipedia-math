# Brunner Munzel Test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brunner Munzel Test"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Brunner Munzel test**[^o2][^o3][^o4] (also called the **generalized Wilcoxon test**) is a [nonparametric](wikipedia://en/223369) [test](wikipedia://en/30284) of the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) that, for randomly selected values *X* and *Y* from two populations, the probability of *X* being greater than *Y* is equal to the probability of *Y* being greater than *X*.

:::

It is thus highly similar to the well-known [Mann–Whitney U test](wikipedia://en/433164). The core difference is that the Mann-Whitney U test assumes equal distributions under the null hypothesis (see [Mann–Whitney U test#Assumptions and formal statement of hypotheses](wikipedia://en/433164#Assumptions_and_formal_statement_of_hypotheses)), while the Brunner Munzel test does not require that assumption, making it more robust and applicable to a wider range of conditions. As a result, multiple authors recommend using the Brunner Munzel instead of the Mann-Whitney U test by default.[^o5][^o6]

## Assumptions and formal statement of hypotheses {#Assumptions_and_formal_statement_of_hypotheses}

1. All the observations from both groups are [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) of each other,
1. The responses are at least [ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_measurement) (i.e., one can at least say, of any two observations, which is the greater),
1. Under the null hypothesis *H*<sub>0</sub>, is that the probability of an observation from population *X* exceeding an observation from population *Y* is the same than the probability of an observation from *Y* exceeding an observation from *X*; i.e., $P(X > Y) = P(Y > X)$ or $P(X > Y) + 0.5 \cdot P(X = Y) = 0.5$.
1. The alternative hypothesis *H*<sub>1</sub> is that $P(X > Y) \ne P(Y > X)$ or $P(X > Y) + 0.5 \cdot P(X = Y) \ne 0.5$

Under these assumptions, the test is consistent and approximately [exact](wikipedia://en/2485378).[^o2] The crucial difference compared to the [Mann–Whitney U test](wikipedia://en/433164) is that the latter is not approximately exact under these assumptions. Both tests are exact when additionally assuming equal distributions under the null hypothesis.

## Software implementations {#Software_implementations}

The Brunner Munzel test is available in the following packages

- [R](wikipedia://en/376707): [brunnermunzel](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/brunnermunzel/index.html), [lawstat](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/lawstat/index.html), [rankFD](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/rankFD/index.html) (function rank.two.samples())
- [Python (programming language)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29): [scipy.stats.brunnermunzel](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.brunnermunzel.html)
- [jamovi](wikipedia://en/70595459): [bmtest](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-8611/5/2/26)
