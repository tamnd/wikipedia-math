# Kathryn Chaloner

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kathryn Chaloner"}

**Kathryn Mary Chaloner** (August 24, 1954 – October 19, 2014) was a British-born American [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685).

:::

Chaloner was a statistics researcher who developed methods in [Bayesian experimental design](wikipedia://en/1706860), and well known for her work on [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV)/[AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS), infectious diseases, and [women's health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_health).[^o3] She was a board member of the National Alliance for Doctoral Studies in the Mathematical Sciences, a group of faculty working towards inclusion and diversity in the doctoral-level mathematical sciences. She led an initiative in statistical sciences to broaden participation in doctoral-level studies in statistics and biostatistics.

## Biography {#Biography}

She earned a bachelor's degree in Mathematics from [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) where she studied at [Somerville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College,_Oxford), and a master's degree in Statistics from the [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) before moving to the United States to study at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), where received a PhD in Statistics.

She was a faculty member of the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) School of Statistics from 1982–2002. In 2002, she was appointed Professor and Chair of the Department of Biostatistics at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- Fellow (posthumous) of the Society for Clinical Trials
- Elected Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988)
- Elected Fellow of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute)
- Elected Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)
- [COPSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPSS) [Elizabeth L. Scott Award](wikipedia://en/60371939) for contributions to service and leadership, mentoring junior faculty and graduate students, contributions to statistical methodology, and application of cutting edge methods to medicine and public health.[^o6]
