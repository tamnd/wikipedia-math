# Thomas Farrer, 1st Baron Farrer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Farrer, 1st Baron Farrer"}

**Thomas Henry Farrer, 1st Baron Farrer** (24 June 1819 – 11 October 1899) was an English [civil servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_servant), [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and member of the [House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords).

:::

## Background and early life {#Background_and_early_life}

Farrer was the son of Thomas Farrer, a solicitor in [Lincoln's Inn Fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_Inn_Fields). Born in London, he was educated at [Eton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eton_College) and [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford), where he graduated in 1840. He was called to the bar at [Lincoln's Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln's_Inn) in 1844, but retired from practice in the course of a few years.

## Career in the civil service {#Career_in_the_civil_service}

He entered the public service in 1850 as secretary to the naval department (renamed the marine department in 1853) of the [Board of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Trade). In 1865 he was promoted to be one of the joint secretaries of the Board of Trade, and in 1867 became permanent secretary.[^o4]

His tenure of the office of permanent secretary, which he held for upwards of twenty years, was marked by many reforms and an energetic administration. Not only was he an advanced [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_party) in politics, but an uncompromising advocate of [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire) of the strictest school. He was created a [baronet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baronet) for his services at the Board of Trade in 1883, and in 1886 he retired from office. During the same year he published a work entitled *Free Trade versus Fair Trade*, in which he dealt with an economic controversy then greatly agitating the public mind. He had already, in 1883, written a volume on *The State in its Relation to Trade*.[^o4]

In 1889 he was co-opted by the Progressives as an alderman of the [London County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_County_Council), of which he became vice-chairman in 1890. His efficiency and ability in this capacity were warmly recognized; but in the course of time divergences arose between his personal views and those of many of his colleagues. The tendency towards socialistic legislation which became apparent was quite at variance with his principles of individual enterprise and responsibility. He consequently resigned his position.[^o4]

In the [1893 Birthday Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Birthday_Honours), he was raised to the peerage as **Baron Farrer**, *of Abinger, in the County of Surrey*. From this time forward he devoted much of his energy and leisure to advocating his views at the [Cobden Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobden_Club), the [Political Economy Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_Economy_Club), on the platform, and in the press. His efforts were especially directed against the opinions of the [Fair Trade League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Trade_League), and upon this and other economic controversies he wrote able, clear, and uncompromising letters, which left no doubt that he still adhered to the doctrines of free trade as advocated by its earliest exponents. In 1898 he published his *Studies in Currency*.[^o4]

He was [President of the Royal Statistical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Royal_Statistical_Society) from 1894 to 1896.[^o8]

## Marriage and issue {#Marriage_and_issue}

Farrer married twice, first in 1854 to Frances Erskine (1825–1870), daughter of the historian and orientalist [William Erskine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Erskine_%28historian%29) (1773–1852) and his wife, Maitland Mackintosh, daughter of [Sir James Mackintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mackintosh) by his first wife. They bore the following children:

- Hon. [Emma Cecilia "Ida" Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Darwin) (1854–1946), married [Horace Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Darwin), son of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)
- Hon. [Thomas Cecil Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Farrer,_2nd_Baron_Farrer) (1859–1940), married Evelyn Spring Rice, daughter of Hon. Charles Spring Rice
- [Claude Erskine Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Farrer) (1862–1890)
- Hon. [Noel Maitland Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Farrer) (1867–1929), father of [Anthony Farrer, 5th Baron Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Farrer,_5th_Baron_Farrer).

Frances died on 15 May 1870. Farrer remarried in 1873 to his former wife's half-cousin, Katherine Euphemia Wedgwood (1839–1931), daughter of [Hensleigh Wedgwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hensleigh_Wedgwood) of the [Wedgwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedgwood) pottery family and his wife Fanny Mackintosh.

## Death {#Death}

Farrer died at [Abinger Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abinger_Hall), [Dorking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorking), in 1899 and was buried at [Brookwood Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookwood_Cemetery). He was succeeded in the title by his eldest son [Thomas Cecil Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Farrer,_2nd_Baron_Farrer) (1859–1940).[^o4]
