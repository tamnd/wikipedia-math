# Lidstone series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lidstone series"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Lidstone series**, named after [George James Lidstone](wikipedia://en/25446803), is a kind of polynomial expansion that can express certain types of [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372).

:::

Let *ƒ*(*z*) be an entire function of [exponential type](wikipedia://en/4081509) less than (*N* + 1)*π*, as defined below. Then *ƒ*(*z*) can be expanded in terms of polynomials *A*<sub>*n*</sub> as follows:

$$f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \left[ A_n(1-z) f^{(2n)}(0) + A_n(z) f^{(2n)}(1) \right] + \sum_{k=1}^N C_k \sin (k\pi z).$$

Here *A*<sub>*n*</sub>(*z*) is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in *z* of degree *n*, *C*<sub>*k*</sub> a constant, and *ƒ*<sup>(*n*)</sup>(*a*) the *n*th [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of *ƒ* at *a*.

A function is said to be of **[exponential type](wikipedia://en/4081509) of less than *t*** if the function

$$h(\theta; f) = \underset{r\to\infty}{\limsup}\, \frac{1}{r} \log |f(r e^{i\theta})|$$

is bounded above by *t*. Thus, the constant *N* used in the summation above is given by

$$t= \sup_{\theta\in [0,2\pi)} h(\theta; f)$$

with

$$N\pi \leq t < (N+1)\pi.$$
