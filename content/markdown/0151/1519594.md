# Lebesgue point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lebesgue point"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), given a locally [Lebesgue integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integrable) function $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^k$, a point $x$ in the domain of $f$ is a **Lebesgue point** if[^o2]

:::

$$\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^+}\frac{1}{\lambda (B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)} \!|f(y)-f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}y=0.$$

Here, $B(x,r)$ is a ball centered at $x$ with radius $r > 0$, and $\lambda (B(x,r))$ is its [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198). The Lebesgue points of $f$ are thus points where $f$ does not oscillate too much, in an average sense.[^o3]

The [Lebesgue differentiation theorem](wikipedia://en/5766547) states that, given any $f\in L^1(\mathbb{R}^k)$, [almost every](wikipedia://en/44688) $x$ is a Lebesgue point of $f$.[^o4]
