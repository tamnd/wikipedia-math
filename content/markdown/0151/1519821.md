# Michael Artin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Artin"}

**Michael Artin** (; born 28 June 1934) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a professor emeritus in the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology Mathematics Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Mathematics_Department), known for his contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Artin was born in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), Germany, and brought up in [Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana). His parents were [Natalia Naumovna Jasny](wikipedia://en/46472280) (Natascha) and [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351), preeminent algebraist of the 20th century of [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_people) descent. Artin's parents left Germany in 1937, because his mother's father was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_people). His elder sister is Karin Tate, who was married to mathematician [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) until the late 1980s.

Artin did his undergraduate studies at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), receiving an A.B. in 1955. He then moved to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he received a Ph.D. in 1960 under the supervision of [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352), defending a thesis about [Enriques surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enriques_surfaces).[^o3]

In the early 1960s, Artin spent time at the [IHÉS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHÉS) in France, contributing to the [SGA4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SGA4) volumes of the [Séminaire de géométrie algébrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique), on [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory) and [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231), jointly with [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). He also collaborated with [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) to define [étale homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/39583234) which has become an important tool in algebraic geometry, and applied ideas from algebraic geometry (such as the [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337)) to the study of [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) of [compact manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_manifold).

His work on the problem of characterising the [representable functors](wikipedia://en/844461) in the [category of schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_schemes) has led to the [Artin approximation theorem](wikipedia://en/2388724) in [local algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_algebra) as well as the "Existence theorem". This work also gave rise to the ideas of an [algebraic space](wikipedia://en/2394027) and [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377), and has proved very influential in [moduli theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_theory).

He also has made important contributions to the [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of algebraic varieties, serving as the basis for all future work in this area of algebraic geometry. With [Peter Swinnerton-Dyer](wikipedia://en/2105540), he provided a resolution of the Shafarevich-Tate conjecture for elliptic [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) and the pencil of elliptic curves over finite fields.

He contributed to the theory of surface singularities which are both fundamental and seminal. The [rational singularity](wikipedia://en/2629851) and fundamental cycles, which are used in matroid theory, are such examples of his sheer originality and thinking.

He began to turn his interest from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) to [noncommutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_algebra) ([noncommutative ring](wikipedia://en/24062516) theory), especially geometric aspects, after a talk by [Shimshon Amitsur](wikipedia://en/19937535) and an encounter in [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) with [Claudio Procesi](wikipedia://en/35219117) and Lance W. Small, "which prompted \[his\] first foray into ring theory".[^o8] Today, he is a recognized world authority in [noncommutative algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/25493347).

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2002, Artin won the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s annual [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_P._Steele_Prizes) for Lifetime Achievement.

In 2005, he was awarded the [Harvard Centennial Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Centennial_Medal).

In 2013, he won the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278), and in 2015 was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) from President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama).

He is also a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) and a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1969),[^o10] the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376),[^o3] and the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]

He is a Foreign Member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and Honorary Fellow of the [Moscow Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/9098251), and was awarded honorary doctorates from the universities of Hamburg and [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium). He was invited to give a talk on the topic "The Étale Topology of Schemes" at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1966 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR).
