# Prime constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime constant"}

The **prime constant** is the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\rho$ whose $n$th [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) digit is 1 if $n$ is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) and 0 if $n$ is [composite](wikipedia://en/82289) or 1.[^o2]

:::

In other words, $\rho$ is the number whose [binary expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) corresponds to the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790) of the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). That is,

$$\rho = \sum_{p} \frac{1}{2^p} = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi_{\mathbb{P}}(n)}{2^n}$$

where $p$ indicates a prime and $\chi_{\mathbb{P}}$ is the [characteristic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function) of the set $\mathbb{P}$ of prime numbers.

The beginning of the decimal expansion of *ρ* is: $\rho = 0.414682509851111660248109622\ldots$ [^o2]

The beginning of the binary expansion is: $\rho = 0.011010100010100010100010000\ldots_2$

## Irrationality {#Irrationality}

The number $\rho$ is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof by contradiction"}

### Proof by contradiction {#Proof_by_contradiction}

Suppose $\rho$ were [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024).

Denote the $k$th digit of the binary expansion of $\rho$ by $r_k$. Then since $\rho$ is assumed rational, its binary expansion is eventually periodic, and so there exist positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $N$ and $k$ such that $r_n = r_{n+ik}$ for all $n > N$ and all $i \in \mathbb{N}$.

Since there are [an infinite number of](wikipedia://en/3143591) primes, we may choose a prime $p > N$. By definition we see that $r_p=1$. As noted, we have $r_p=r_{p+ik}$ for all $i \in \mathbb{N}$. Now consider the case $i=p$. We have $r_{p+i \cdot k}=r_{p+p \cdot k}=r_{p(k+1)}=0$, since $p(k+1)$ is composite because $k+1 \geq 2$. Since $r_p \neq r_{p(k+1)}$ we see that $\rho$ is irrational.

:::
