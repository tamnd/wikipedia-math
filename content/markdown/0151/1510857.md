# Witt algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Witt algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the complex **Witt algebra**, named after [Ernst Witt](wikipedia://en/2619911), is the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of meromorphic vector fields defined on the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) that are holomorphic except at two fixed points. It is also the [complexification](wikipedia://en/755604) of the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on a circle, and the Lie algebra of derivations of the ring **C**\[*z*,*z*<sup>−1</sup>\].

:::

There are some related Lie algebras defined over finite fields, that are also called Witt algebras.

The complex Witt algebra was first defined by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) (1909), and its analogues over finite fields were studied by Witt in the 1930s.

## Basis {#Basis}

A basis for the Witt algebra is given by the [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) $L_n=-z^{n+1} \frac{\partial}{\partial z}$, for *n* in *$\mathbb Z$*.

The [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/294437) of two [basis vector](wikipedia://en/18420) fields is given by

$$[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}.$$

This algebra has a [central extension](wikipedia://en/345807#Central_extension) called the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029) that is important in [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) and [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

Note that by restricting *n* to 1,0,-1, one gets a [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239). Taken over the field of complex numbers, this is just the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ of the [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489) $\mathrm{SO}(3,1)$. Over the reals, it is the algebra [***sl***(2,R)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SL%282,R%29) = ***su***(1,1). Conversely, ***su***(1,1) suffices to reconstruct the original algebra in a presentation.[^o3]

## Over finite fields {#Over_finite_fields}

Over a field *k* of characteristic *p*>0, the Witt algebra is defined to be the Lie algebra of derivations of the ring

*k*\[*z*\]/*z*<sup>*p*</sup>

The Witt algebra is spanned by *L*<sub>*m*</sub> for −1≤ *m* ≤ *p*−2.
