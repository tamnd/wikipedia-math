# Diocles (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diocles"}

**Diocles** (Διοκλῆς; c. 240 BC – c. 180 BC) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_Greece) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Although little is known about the life of Diocles, it is known that he was a contemporary of [Apollonius](wikipedia://en/257242) and that he flourished sometime around the end of the 3rd century BC and the beginning of the 2nd century BC.[^o3]

Diocles is thought to be the first person to prove the focal property of the [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231). His name is associated with the geometric [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) called the [Cissoid of Diocles](wikipedia://en/820041), which was used by Diocles to solve the problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110). The curve was alluded to by [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus) in his commentary on [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) and attributed to Diocles by [Geminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geminus) as early as the beginning of the 1st century.[^o4]

Fragments of a work by Diocles entitled *On burning mirrors* were preserved by [Eutocius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutocius) in his commentary of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844)' *On the Sphere and the Cylinder* and also survived in an [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) translation of the lost Greek original titled *Kitāb Dhiyūqlīs fī l-marāyā l-muḥriqa* (lit. “The book of Diocles on burning mirrors”).[^o5] Historically, *On burning mirrors* had a large influence on Arabic mathematicians, particularly on [al-Haytham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhazen), the 11th-century polymath of Cairo whom Europeans knew as "Alhazen". The treatise contains sixteen propositions that are proved by [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_sections). One of the fragments contains propositions seven and eight, which is a solution to the problem of dividing a sphere by a plane so that the resulting two volumes are in a given ratio. Proposition ten gives a solution to the problem of doubling the cube. This is equivalent to solving a certain [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787). Another fragment contains propositions eleven and twelve, which use the cissoid to solve the problem of finding two mean proportionals in between two magnitudes. Since this treatise covers more topics than just [burning mirrors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_glass), it may be the case that *On burning mirrors* is the aggregate of three shorter works by Diocles.[^o6] In the same work, Diocles, just after demonstrating that the parabolic mirror could focus the rays in a single point, he mentioned that It is possible to obtain a lens with the same property.[^o7]
