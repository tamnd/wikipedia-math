# Helly–Bray theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helly–Bray theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Helly–Bray theorem** relates the [weak convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) of [cumulative distribution functions](wikipedia://en/5793) to the convergence of [expectations](wikipedia://en/9653) of certain [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055). It is named after [Eduard Helly](wikipedia://en/2202570) and [Hubert Evelyn Bray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Evelyn_Bray).

:::

Let *F* and *F*<sub>1</sub>, *F*<sub>2</sub>, ... be cumulative distribution functions on the [real line](wikipedia://en/20646438). The Helly–Bray theorem states that if *F*<sub>*n*</sub> converges weakly to *F*, then

$$\int_\mathbb{R} g(x)\,dF_n(x) \quad\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\quad \int_\mathbb{R} g(x)\,dF(x)$$

for each [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509), [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *g*: **R** → **R**, where the integrals involved are [Riemann&ndash;Stieltjes integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann&ndash;Stieltjes_integral).

Note that if *X* and *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>, ... are [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) corresponding to these distribution functions, then the Helly–Bray theorem does not imply that E(*X*<sub>*n*</sub>) → E(*X*), since *g*(*x*) = *x* is not a bounded function.

In fact, a stronger and more general theorem holds. Let *P* and *P*<sub>1</sub>, *P*<sub>2</sub>, ... be [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) on some [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *S*. Then *P*<sub>*n*</sub> converges weakly to *P* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922)

$$\int_S g \,dP_n \quad\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\quad \int_S g \,dP,$$

for all bounded, continuous and [real-valued](wikipedia://en/20646438) functions on *S*. (The integrals in this version of the theorem are [Lebesgue–Stieltjes integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue–Stieltjes_integral).)

The more general theorem above is sometimes taken as *defining* [weak convergence of measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_probability_measures) (see Billingsley, 1999, p. 3).
