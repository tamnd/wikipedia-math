# Codensity monad

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Codensity monad"}

In mathematics, especially in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the **codensity monad** is a fundamental construction associating a [monad](wikipedia://en/355140) to a wide class of [functors](wikipedia://en/10987).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The codensity monad of a functor $G: D \to C$ is defined to be the [right Kan extension](wikipedia://en/2502518) of $G$ along itself, provided that this Kan extension exists. Thus, by definition it is in particular a functor

$$T^G : C \to C.$$

The monad structure on $T^G$ stems from the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of the right Kan extension.

The codensity monad exists whenever $D$ is a small category (has only a set, as opposed to a [proper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class), of morphisms) and $C$ possesses all (small, i.e., set-indexed) [limits](wikipedia://en/39248). It also exists whenever $G$ has a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint).

By the general formula computing right Kan extensions in terms of [ends](wikipedia://en/3865669), the codensity monad is given by the following formula:

$$T^G(c) = \int_{d \in D} G(d)^{C(c, G(d))},$$

where $C(c, G(d))$ denotes the set of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) in $C$ between the indicated objects and the integral denotes the end. The codensity monad therefore amounts to considering maps from $c$ to an object in the image of $G,$ and maps from the set of such morphisms to $G(d),$ compatible for all the possible $d.$ Thus, as is noted by Avery, codensity monads share some kinship with the concept of [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_%28mathematics%29) and double dualization.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Codensity monads of right adjoints {#Codensity_monads_of_right_adjoints}

If the functor $G$ admits a left adjoint $F,$ the codensity monad is given by the composite $G \circ F,$ together with the standard unit and multiplication maps.

### Concrete examples for functors not admitting a left adjoint {#Concrete_examples_for_functors_not_admitting_a_left_adjoint}

In several interesting cases, the functor $G$ is an inclusion of a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) not admitting a left adjoint. For example, the codensity monad of the inclusion of **[FinSet](wikipedia://en/25044975)** into **[Set](wikipedia://en/26829)** is the [ultrafilter monad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_monad) associating to any set $M$ the set of [ultrafilters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_%28set_theory%29) on $M.$ This was proven by Kennison and Gildenhuys, though without using the term "codensity". In this formulation, the statement is reviewed by [Leinster](wikipedia://en/78103499).[^n1]

A related example is discussed by Leinster:[^n2] the codensity monad of the inclusion of [finite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-dimensional) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) (over a fixed [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$) into all vector spaces is the [double dualization monad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_dualization_monad) given by sending a vector space $V$ to its [double dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_dual)

$$V^{**} = \operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}(V, k), k).$$

Thus, in this example, the end formula mentioned above simplifies to considering (in the notation above) only one object $d,$ namely a one-dimensional vector space, as opposed to considering all objects in $D.$ Adámek and Sousa show that, in a number of situations, the codensity monad of the inclusion

$$D := C^{fp} \subseteq C$$

of finitely presented objects (also known as [compact objects](wikipedia://en/57633087)) is a double dualization monad with respect to a sufficiently nice [cogenerating object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_cogenerator). This recovers both the inclusion of finite sets in sets (where a cogenerator is the set of two elements), and also the inclusion of finite-dimensional vector spaces in vector spaces (where the cogenerator is the ground field).

Sipoş showed that the [algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_monad) over the codensity monad of the inclusion of finite sets (regarded as [discrete topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topological_space)) into topological spaces are equivalent to [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219). Avery shows that the [Giry monad](wikipedia://en/75998118) arises as the codensity monad of natural [forgetful functors](wikipedia://en/844783) between certain categories of [convex vector spaces](wikipedia://en/33677222) to [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333).

:::

## Relation to Isbell duality {#Relation_to_Isbell_duality}

Di Liberti[^n3] shows that the codensity monad is closely related to [Isbell duality](wikipedia://en/22851039): for a given small category $C,$ Isbell duality refers to the adjunction

$$\mathcal O : Set^{C^{op}} \rightleftarrows (Set^C)^{op} : Spec$$

between the category of [presheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf) on $C$ (that is, functors from the opposite category of $C$ to sets) and the opposite category of copresheaves on $C.$ The monad

$$Spec \circ \mathcal O$$

induced by this adjunction is shown to be the codensity monad of the [Yoneda embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_embedding)

$$y: C \to Set^{C^{op}}.$$

Conversely, the codensity monad of a full small dense subcategory $K$ in a [cocomplete category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocomplete_category) $C$ is shown to be induced by Isbell duality.[^n4]
