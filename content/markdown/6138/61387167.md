# Ind-completion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ind-completion"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **ind-completion** or **ind-construction** is the process of freely adding [filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit) to a given [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *C*. The objects in this ind-completed category, denoted Ind(*C*), are known as **direct systems**, they are [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) from a small [filtered category](wikipedia://en/4322449) *I* to *C*.

:::

The [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) concept is the pro-completion, Pro(*C*).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Filtered categories {#Filtered_categories}

Direct systems depend on the notion of *filtered categories*. For example, the category **N**, whose objects are [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), and with exactly one morphism from *n* to *m* whenever $n \le m$, is a filtered category.

### Direct systems {#Direct_systems}

A *direct system* or an *ind-object* in a category *C* is defined to be a functor

$$F : I \to C$$

from a small filtered category *I* to *C*. For example, if *I* is the category **N** mentioned above, this datum is equivalent to a sequence

$$X_0 \to X_1 \to \cdots$$

of objects in *C* together with morphisms as displayed.

### The ind-completion {#The_ind-completion}

Ind-objects in *C* form a category ind-*C*.

Two ind-objects

$$F:I\to C$$

and

$G:J\to C$ determine a functor

*I*<sup>op</sup> x *J* $\to$ *Sets*,

namely the functor

$$\operatorname{Hom}_C(F(i),G(j)).$$

The set of morphisms between *F* and *G* in Ind(*C*) is defined to be the colimit of this functor in the second variable, followed by the limit in the first variable:

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Ind}\text{-}C}(F,G) = \lim_i \operatorname{colim}_j \operatorname{Hom}_C(F(i), G(j)).$$

More colloquially, this means that a morphism consists of a collection of maps $F(i) \to G(j_i)$ for each *i*, where $j_i$ is (depending on *i*) large enough.

### Relation between *C* and Ind(*C*) {#Relation_between_C_and_Ind(C)}

The [final category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_category) I = {\*} consisting of a single object \* and only its [identity morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism) is an example of a filtered category. In particular, any object *X* in *C* gives rise to a functor

$$\{*\} \to C, * \mapsto X$$

and therefore to a functor

$$C \to \operatorname{Ind}(C), X \mapsto (* \mapsto X).$$

This functor is, as a direct consequence of the definitions, fully faithful. Therefore Ind(*C*) can be regarded as a larger category than *C*.

Conversely, there need not in general be a natural functor

$$\operatorname{Ind}(C) \to C.$$

However, if *C* possesses all [filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit) (also known as direct limits), then sending an ind-object $F: I \to C$ (for some filtered category *I*) to its colimit

$$\operatorname {colim}_I F(i)$$

does give such a functor, which however is not in general an equivalence. Thus, even if *C* already has all filtered colimits, Ind(*C*) is a strictly larger category than *C*.

Objects in Ind(*C*) can be thought of as formal direct limits, so that some authors also denote such objects by

$$\text{“}\varinjlim_{i \in I} \text{'' } F(i).$$

This notation is due to [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544).[^o8]

:::

## Universal property of the ind-completion {#Universal_property_of_the_ind-completion}

The passage from a category *C* to Ind(*C*) amounts to freely adding filtered colimits to the category. This is why the construction is also referred to as the *ind-completion* of *C*. This is made precise by the following assertion: any functor $F: C \to D$ taking values in a category *D* that has all filtered colimits extends to a functor $Ind(C) \to D$ that is uniquely determined by the requirements that its value on *C* is the original functor *F* and such that it preserves all filtered colimits.

## Basic properties of ind-categories {#Basic_properties_of_ind-categories}

### Compact objects {#Compact_objects}

Essentially by design of the morphisms in Ind(*C*), any object *X* of *C* is [compact](wikipedia://en/57633087) when regarded as an object of Ind(*C*), i.e., the [corepresentable functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corepresentable_functor)

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Ind}(C)}(X, -)$$

preserves filtered colimits. This holds true no matter what *C* or the object *X* is, in contrast to the fact that *X* need not be compact in *C*. Conversely, any compact object in Ind(*C*) arises as the image of an object in *X*.

A category *C* is called compactly generated, if it is equivalent to $\operatorname{Ind}(C_0)$ for some small category $C_0$. The ind-completion of the category [FinSet](wikipedia://en/25044975) of *finite* sets is the [category of *all* sets](wikipedia://en/26829). Similarly, if *C* is the category of finitely generated groups, *ind-C* is equivalent to the category of all groups.

### Recognizing ind-completions {#Recognizing_ind-completions}

These identifications rely on the following facts: as was mentioned above, any functor $F: C \to D$ taking values in a category *D* that has all filtered colimits, has an extension

$$\tilde F: \operatorname{Ind}(C) \to D,$$

that preserves filtered colimits. This extension is unique up to equivalence. First, this functor $\tilde F$ is [essentially surjective](wikipedia://en/4557668) if any object in *D* can be expressed as a filtered colimits of objects of the form $F(c)$ for appropriate objects *c* in *C*. Second, $\tilde F$ is [fully faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fully_faithful_functor) if and only if the original functor *F* is fully faithful and if *F* sends arbitrary objects in *C* to *compact* objects in *D*.

Applying these facts to, say, the inclusion functor

$$F: \operatorname{FinSet} \subset \operatorname{Set},$$

the equivalence

$$\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{FinSet}) \cong \operatorname{Set}$$

expresses the fact that any set is the filtered colimit of finite sets (for example, any set is the union of its finite subsets, which is a filtered system) and moreover, that any finite set is compact when regarded as an object of *Set*.

## The pro-completion {#The_pro-completion}

Like other categorical notions and constructions, the ind-completion admits a dual known as the pro-completion: the pro-completion Pro(*C*) can defined in terms of the ind-completion as

$$\operatorname{Pro}(C) := \operatorname{Ind}(C^{op})^{op}.$$

(The original definition of pro-*C* is due to (Grothendieck 1960).[^o15])

Therefore, the objects of Pro(*C*) are inverse systems or pro-objects in *C*. By definition, these are direct system in the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) $C^{op}$ or, equivalently, functors

$$F: I \to C$$

from a small cofiltered category *I*.

### Examples of pro-categories {#Examples_of_pro-categories}

While Pro(*C*) exists for any category *C*, several special cases are noteworthy because of connections to other mathematical notions.

- If *C* is the category of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), then *pro-C* is equivalent to the category of [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266) and continuous homomorphisms between them.
- The process of endowing a [preordered set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preordered_set) with its [Alexandrov topology](wikipedia://en/542198) yields an equivalence of the pro-category of the category of finite preordered sets, $\operatorname{Pro}(\operatorname{FinPreord})$, with the category of [spectral topological spaces](wikipedia://en/544273) and quasi-compact morphisms.
- [Stone duality](wikipedia://en/379619) asserts that the pro-category $\operatorname{Pro}(\operatorname{FinSet})$ of the [category of finite sets](wikipedia://en/25044975) is equivalent to the category of [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219).[^o17]

The appearance of topological notions in these pro-categories can be traced to the equivalence, which is itself a special case of Stone duality,

$$\operatorname{FinSet}^{\text{op}} = \operatorname{FinBool}$$

which sends a finite set to the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) (regarded as a finite Boolean algebra). The duality between pro- and ind-objects and known description of ind-completions also give rise to descriptions of certain opposite categories. For example, such considerations can be used to show that the opposite category of the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) (over a fixed field) is equivalent to the category of linearly compact vector spaces and continuous linear maps between them.[^o18]

::: {#o20 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

Pro-completions are less prominent than ind-completions, but applications include [shape theory](wikipedia://en/18767352). Pro-objects also arise via their connection to [pro-representable functors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-representable_functor), for example in [Grothendieck's Galois theory](wikipedia://en/372470), and also in [Schlessinger's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlessinger's_criterion) in [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory).

:::

### Related notions {#Related_notions}

[Tate objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_object) are a mixture of ind- and pro-objects.

## Infinity-categorical variants {#Infinity-categorical_variants}

The ind-completion (and, dually, the pro-completion) has been extended to [∞-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_category) by (Lurie 2009).
