# Population balance equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Population balance equation"}

**Population balance equations (PBEs)** have been introduced in several branches of modern science, mainly in [Chemical Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Engineering),[^o2] to describe the evolution of a population of particles. This includes topics like [crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization),[^o3] [leaching (metallurgy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaching_%28metallurgy%29),[^o4][^o5] [liquid–liquid extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid–liquid_extraction), gas-liquid dispersions like water [electrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolysis),[^o6] liquid-liquid reactions, comminution, [aerosol engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol_engineering), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) (where the separate entities are cells based on their size or intracellular proteins[^o7]), [polymerization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerization), etc. Population balance equations can be said to be derived as an extension of the [Smoluchowski coagulation equation](wikipedia://en/3167913) which describes only the coalescence of particles. PBEs, more generally, define how populations of separate entities develop in specific properties over time. They are a set of [Integro-partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integro-partial_differential_equations) which gives the mean-field behavior of a population of particles from the analysis of behavior of single particle in local conditions.[^o8] Particulate systems are characterized by the birth and death of particles. For example, consider [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_%28chemistry%29) process (formation of solid from liquid solution) which has the subprocesses [nucleation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleation), [agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_aggregation), breakage, etc., that result in the increase or decrease of the number of particles of a particular [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) (assuming formation of spherical particles). Population balance is nothing but a *balance on the number of particles of a particular state* (in this example, *size*).

:::

## Formulation of PBE {#Formulation_of_PBE}

Consider the average number of particles with particle properties denoted by a particle state vector (**x**,**r**) (where **x** corresponds to particle properties like size, density, etc. also known as internal coordinates and, **r** corresponds to spatial position or external coordinates) dispersed in a continuous phase defined by a phase vector Y(**r**,t) (which again is a function of all such vectors which denote the phase properties at various locations) is denoted by f(**x**,**r**,t). Hence it gives the particle characteristics in property and space domains. Let h(**x**,**r**,**Y**,t) denote the birth rate of particles per unit volume of particle state space, so the number conservation can be written as[^o8]

$$\frac{d}{dt} \int_{\Omega_x(t)} dV_x \int_{\Omega_r(t)} dV_r\,f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r},t) = \int_{\Omega_x(t)} dV_x \int_{\Omega_r(t)} dV_r\,h(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{Y},t)$$

This is a generalized form of PBE.[^o8]

## Solution to PBE {#Solution_to_PBE}

[Monte Carlo methods](wikipedia://en/56098) [^o11] ,[^o12] [discretization](wikipedia://en/330017) methods,[^o13] numerical techniques like Finite Volume Method [^o14] and moment methods [^o11][^o12][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] are mainly used to solve these [equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations). The choice depends on the application and computing infrastructure.[^o2]
