# Theodor Kaluza

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theodor Kaluza"}

**Theodor Franz Eduard Kaluza** (; 9 November 1885 – 19 January 1954) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) known for the [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070), involving [field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_equations) in five-dimensional space-time. His idea that fundamental forces can be unified by introducing additional dimensions was reused much later for [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kaluza was born to a [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic) family from the town of [Ratibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racibórz) (present-day Racibórz in Poland) in the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire)'s [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) [Province of Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Silesia). Kaluza himself was born in Wilhelmsthal (a village that was incorporated into Oppeln (presently [Opole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opole)) in 1899). He spent his youth in [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg), where his father, [Maximilian "Max" Kaluza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Kaluza), was a professor of the English language. He entered the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) to study mathematics and gained his doctorate with a thesis on [Tschirnhaus transformations](wikipedia://en/595824). Kaluza was primarily a mathematician but began studying [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001). In April 1919 Kaluza noticed that when he solved [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s equations for [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) using five dimensions, then [Maxwellian](wikipedia://en/28989696) equations for [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) resulted spontaneously.[^o4][^o5][^o6] Kaluza wrote to Einstein who, in turn, encouraged him to publish. Kaluza's theory was published in 1921 in a paper "Zum Unitätsproblem der Physik" with Einstein's support in *Sitzungsberichte Preußische Akademie der Wissenschaften* 966–972 (1921).[^o5]

Kaluza's insight is remembered as the [Kaluza–Klein theory](wikipedia://en/17070) (named also after physicist [Oskar Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Klein)). However, the work was neglected for many years, as attention was directed towards [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). His idea that fundamental forces can be explained by additional dimensions was not reused until [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) was developed. It is, however, also notable that many of the aspects of this body of work were already published in 1914 by [Gunnar Nordström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnar_Nordström), but his work also went unnoticed and was not recognized when the ideas were reused.

Among his PhD students was [Shmuel Sambursky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_Sambursky), Professor of Physics at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), and winner of the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize).

For the rest of his career Kaluza continued to produce ideas about relativity and about models of the [atomic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_nucleus). Despite Einstein's encouragement, Kaluza remained only a ([Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent)) at [Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königsberg) until 1929, when he was appointed as professor at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel). In 1935, he became a full professor at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he remained until his death in 1954. Perhaps his finest mathematical work is the textbook *Höhere Mathematik für den Praktiker*, which was written jointly with [Georg Joos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Joos).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Kaluza was extraordinarily versatile. He spoke or wrote 17 languages. He also had an unusually modest personality. He refused the [Nazi ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism), and his appointment to the Göttingen professorship was possible only with difficulties and by assistance of his colleague [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118). Strange stories were told of his private life, for example, that he taught himself to swim during his thirties by reading a book about it and succeeded at his first attempt in the water.

Kaluza had a son, also named Theodor Kaluza (mathematician) (1910–1994), who was a notable [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).
