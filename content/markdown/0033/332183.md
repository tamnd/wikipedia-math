# Ian Stewart (mathematician)

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Ian Stewart"}

**Ian Nicholas Stewart** (born 24 September 1945)[^o5] is a British mathematician and a [popular-science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular-science), textbooks, and science-fiction books writer. He is emeritus professor of mathematics at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), England.

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Stewart was born in 1945 in [Folkestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkestone), England. While in the [sixth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form) at [Harvey Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Harvey_Grammar_School) in Folkestone he came to the attention of the mathematics teacher. The teacher had Stewart sit mock [A-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Level_%28UK%29) examinations without any preparation along with the upper-sixth students; Stewart was placed first in the examination. He was awarded a scholarship to study at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) as an undergraduate student of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge), where he studied the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) and obtained a first-class [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in mathematics in 1966. Stewart then went to the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) where his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) on [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) was supervised by [Brian Hartley](wikipedia://en/11275293) and completed in 1969.[^o8]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After his PhD, Stewart was offered an academic position at Warwick. He is well known for his popular expositions of mathematics and his contributions to [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288).[^o10]

While at Warwick, Stewart edited the mathematical magazine *[Manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_%28magazine%29)*.[^o11] He also wrote a column called "Mathematical Recreations" for *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* magazine from 1991 to 2001. This followed the work of past columnists like [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758), and [A. K. Dewdney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._K._Dewdney). Altogether, he wrote 96 columns for *Scientific American*, which were later reprinted in the books "Math Hysteria", "How to Cut a Cake: And Other Mathematical Conundrums" and "Cows in the Maze".

Stewart has held visiting academic positions in Germany (1974), New Zealand (1976), and the US ([University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) 1977–78, [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston) 1983–84).

Stewart has published more than 140 [scientific papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_publishing), including a series of influential papers co-authored with [Jim Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Collins_%28bioengineer%29) coupled oscillators and the symmetry of animal gaits.[^o6][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17]

Stewart has collaborated with [Jack Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Cohen_%28biologist%29) and [Terry Pratchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett) on four [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) books based on Pratchett's *[Discworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discworld)*. In 1999 [Terry Pratchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett) made both [Jack Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Cohen_%28scientist%29) and Stewart "Honorary Wizards of the Unseen University" at the same ceremony at which the University of Warwick gave Terry Pratchett an honorary degree.

In March 2014 Ian Stewart's iPad app, Incredible Numbers by Professor Ian Stewart, launched in the App Store. The app was produced in partnership with [Profile Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profile_Books) and [Touch Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_Press).[^o18]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1995 Stewart received the [Michael Faraday Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Faraday_Medal) and in 1997 he gave the [Royal Institution Christmas Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution_Christmas_Lectures) on *The Magical Maze*. He was elected as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2001.[^o2] Stewart was the first recipient in 2008 of the [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415) Medal, awarded jointly by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (LMS) and the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) (IMA) for his work on promoting mathematics.[^o37]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Stewart married Avril, in 1970.[^o2] They met at a party at a house that Avril was renting while she trained as a nurse. They have two sons.[^o2] He lists his recreations as science fiction, painting, guitar, keeping fish, geology, Egyptology and snorkelling.[^o2]
