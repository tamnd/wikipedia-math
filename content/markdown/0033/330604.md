# Monoidal category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoidal category"}

In mathematics, a **monoidal category** (or **tensor category**) is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $\mathbf C$ equipped with a [bifunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifunctor)

:::

$$\otimes : \mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{C} \to \mathbf{C}$$

that is [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism), and an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $I$ that is both a [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_identity) and [right identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_identity) for $\otimes$, again up to a natural isomorphism. The associated natural isomorphisms are subject to certain [coherence conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_condition), which ensure that all the relevant [diagrams](wikipedia://en/7651625) [commute](wikipedia://en/138484).

The ordinary [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) makes [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), [*R*-modules](wikipedia://en/276410), or [*R*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) into monoidal categories. Monoidal categories can be seen as a generalization of these and other examples. Every ([small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category)) monoidal category may also be viewed as a "[categorification](wikipedia://en/1596969)" of an underlying [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652), namely the monoid whose elements are the isomorphism classes of the category's objects and whose binary operation is given by the category's tensor product.

A rather different application, for which monoidal categories can be considered an abstraction, is a system of [data types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_type) closed under a [type constructor](wikipedia://en/24047271) that takes two types and builds an aggregate type. The types serve as the objects, and ⊗ is the aggregate constructor. The associativity up to isomorphism is then a way of expressing that different ways of aggregating the same data—such as $((a,b),c)$ and $(a,(b,c))$—store the same information even though the aggregate values need not be the same. The aggregate type may be analogous to the operation of addition ([type sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_type)) or of multiplication ([type product](wikipedia://en/14892710)). For type product, the identity object is the unit $()$, so there is only one inhabitant of the type, and that is why a product with it is always isomorphic to the other operand. For type sum, the identity object is the [void type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Void_type), which stores no information, and it is impossible to address an inhabitant. The concept of monoidal category does not presume that values of such aggregate types can be taken apart; on the contrary, it provides a framework that unifies classical and [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274) theory.[^o2]

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), monoidal categories can be used to define the concept of a [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) and an associated action on the objects of the category. They are also used in the definition of an [enriched category](wikipedia://en/142622).

Monoidal categories have numerous applications outside category theory proper. They are used to define models for the multiplicative fragment of [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262) [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675). They also form the mathematical foundation for the [topological order](wikipedia://en/3087602) in [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics). [Braided monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/1470466) have applications in [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274), [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), and [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A **monoidal category** is a category $\mathbf C$ equipped with a monoidal structure. A monoidal structure consists of the following:

- a [bifunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifunctor) $\otimes \colon \mathbf C\times\mathbf C\to\mathbf C$ called the *monoidal product*,[^o5] or *[tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844)*,
- an object $I$ called the *monoidal unit*,[^o5] *unit object*, or *identity object*,
- three [natural isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) subject to certain [coherence conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_condition) expressing the fact that the tensor operation:
  - is associative: there is a natural (in each of three arguments $A$, $B$, $C$) isomorphism $\alpha$, called the *associator*, with components $\alpha_{A,B,C} \colon A\otimes (B\otimes C) \cong (A\otimes B)\otimes C$,
  - has $I$ as left and right identity: there are two natural isomorphisms $\lambda$ and $\rho$, respectively called the *left unitor* and *right unitor*, with components $\lambda_A \colon I\otimes A\cong A$ and $\rho_A \colon A\otimes I\cong A$.

Note that a good way to remember how $\lambda$ and $\rho$ act is by alliteration; *Lambda*, $\lambda$, cancels the identity on the *left*, while *Rho*, $\rho$, cancels the identity on the *right*.

The coherence conditions for these natural transformations are:

- for all $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ in $\mathbf C$, the pentagon [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625)

*Image: Pentagonal diagram for monoidal categories.svg.* This is one of the main diagrams used to define a monoidal category; it is perhaps the most important one.

[commutes](wikipedia://en/138484);

- for all $A$ and $B$ in $\mathbf C$, the triangle diagram

*Image: Monoidal2.svg.* This is one of the diagrams used in the definition of a monoidal cateogory. It takes care of the case for when there is an instance of an identity between two objects.

commutes.

A **strict monoidal category** is one for which the natural isomorphisms *α*, *λ* and *ρ* are identities. Every monoidal category is monoidally [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to a strict monoidal category (see [Mac Lane's coherence theorem](wikipedia://en/61970353)).

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any category with finite [products](wikipedia://en/237868) can be regarded as monoidal with the product as the monoidal product and the [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) as the unit. Such a category is sometimes called a [cartesian monoidal category](wikipedia://en/43676277). For example:
  - **Set**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) with the Cartesian product, any particular one-element set serving as the unit.
  - **Cat**, the [category of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741) with the [product category](wikipedia://en/4587226), where the category with one object and only its identity map is the unit.
- Dually, any category with finite [coproducts](wikipedia://en/310889) is monoidal with the coproduct as the monoidal product and the [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) as the unit. Such a monoidal category is called **cocartesian monoidal**
- ***R*-Mod**, the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*, is a monoidal category with the [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> serving as the monoidal product and the ring *R* (thought of as a module over itself) serving as the unit. As special cases one has:
  - ***K*-Vect**, the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*, with the one-dimensional vector space *K* serving as the unit. ***K*-FdVect** (the [category of finite-dimensional vector spaces](wikipedia://en/72243857)) by extension fits under this.
  - **Ab**, the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748), with the group of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) **Z** serving as the unit.
- For any commutative ring *R*, the category of [*R*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-algebra) is monoidal with the [tensor product of algebras](wikipedia://en/372395) as the product and *R* as the unit.
- The [category of pointed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_pointed_spaces) (restricted to [compactly generated spaces](wikipedia://en/899382) for example) is monoidal with the [smash product](wikipedia://en/698010) serving as the product and the pointed [0-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-sphere) (a two-point discrete space) serving as the unit.
- The category of all [endofunctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) on a category **C** is a *strict* monoidal category with the composition of functors as the product and the identity functor as the unit.
- Just like for any category **E**, the [full subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959#Embeddings) spanned by any given object is a monoid, it is the case that for any [2-category](wikipedia://en/2740708) **E**, and any object **C** in Ob(**E**), the full 2-subcategory of **E** spanned by {**C**} is a monoidal category. In the case **E** = **Cat**, we get the [endofunctors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) example above.
- [Bounded-above meet semilattices](wikipedia://en/755647) are strict [symmetric monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/3865249): the product is meet and the identity is the top element.
- Any ordinary monoid $(M,\cdot,1)$ is a small monoidal category with object set $M$, only identities for [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891), $\cdot$ as tensor product and $1$ as its identity object. Conversely, the set of isomorphism classes (if such a thing makes sense) of a monoidal category is a monoid w.r.t. the tensor product.
- Any commutative monoid $(M, \cdot, 1)$ can be realized as a monoidal category with a single object. Recall that a category with a single object is the same thing as an ordinary monoid. By an [Eckmann-Hilton argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckmann-Hilton_argument), adding another monoidal product on $M$ requires the product to be commutative.

:::

## Properties and associated notions {#Properties_and_associated_notions}

It follows from the three defining coherence conditions that *a large class* of diagrams (i.e. diagrams whose morphisms are built using $\alpha$, $\lambda$, $\rho$, identities and tensor product) commute: this is [Mac Lane's](wikipedia://en/336574) "[coherence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherence_theorem)". It is sometimes inaccurately stated that *all* such diagrams commute.

There is a general notion of [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) in a monoidal category, which generalizes the ordinary notion of [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) from [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). Ordinary monoids are precisely the monoid objects in the cartesian monoidal category **Set**. Further, any (small) strict monoidal category can be seen as a monoid object in the category of categories **Cat** (equipped with the monoidal structure induced by the cartesian product).

[Monoidal functors](wikipedia://en/4278182) are the functors between monoidal categories that preserve the tensor product and [monoidal natural transformations](wikipedia://en/4585487) are the natural transformations, between those functors, which are "compatible" with the tensor product.

Every monoidal category can be seen as the category **B**(∗, ∗) of a [bicategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory) **B** with only one object, denoted ∗.

The concept of a category **C** [enriched](wikipedia://en/142622) in a monoidal category **M** replaces the notion of a set of morphisms between pairs of objects in **C** with the notion of an **M**-object of morphisms between every two objects in **C**.

### Free strict monoidal category {#Free_strict_monoidal_category}

For every category **C**, the [free](wikipedia://en/36769284) strict monoidal category Σ(**C**) can be constructed as follows:

- its objects are lists (finite sequences) *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>*n*</sub> of objects of **C**;
- there are arrows between two objects *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>*m*</sub> and *B*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *B*<sub>*n*</sub> only if *m* = *n*, and then the arrows are lists (finite sequences) of arrows *f*<sub>1</sub>: *A*<sub>1</sub> → *B*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *f*<sub>*n*</sub>: *A*<sub>*n*</sub> → *B*<sub>*n*</sub> of **C**;
- the tensor product of two objects *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>*n*</sub> and *B*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *B*<sub>*m*</sub> is the concatenation *A*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *A*<sub>*n*</sub>, *B*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *B*<sub>*m*</sub> of the two lists, and, similarly, the tensor product of two morphisms is given by the concatenation of lists. The identity object is the empty list.

This operation Σ mapping category **C** to Σ(**C**) can be extended to a strict 2-[monad](wikipedia://en/355140) on **Cat**.

## Specializations {#Specializations}

- If, in a monoidal category, $A\otimes B$ and $B\otimes A$ are naturally isomorphic in a manner compatible with the coherence conditions, we speak of a [braided monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1470466). If, moreover, this natural isomorphism is its own inverse, we have a [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249).
- A [closed monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1396924) is a monoidal category where the functor $X \mapsto X \otimes A$ has a [right adjoint](wikipedia://en/53991), which is called the "internal Hom-functor" $X \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf C}(A , X)$. Examples include [cartesian closed categories](wikipedia://en/237037) such as **Set**, the category of sets, and [compact closed categories](wikipedia://en/5557623) such as **FdVect**, the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces.
- [Autonomous categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_category) (or [compact closed categories](wikipedia://en/5557623) or [rigid categories](wikipedia://en/25383437)) are monoidal categories in which duals with nice properties exist; they abstract the idea of **FdVect**.
- [Dagger symmetric monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/7156065), equipped with an extra dagger functor, abstracting the idea of **FdHilb**, finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. These include the [dagger compact categories](wikipedia://en/7155145).
- [Tannakian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannakian_category) are monoidal categories enriched over a field, which are very similar to representation categories of [linear algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/395885).

### Preordered monoids {#Preordered_monoids}

A [preordered](wikipedia://en/23582) monoid is a monoidal category in which for every two objects $c, c'\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{C})$, there exists *at most one* morphism $c\to c'$ in **C**. In the context of preorders, a morphism $c\to c'$ is sometimes notated $c \leq c'$. The [reflexivity](wikipedia://en/200458) and [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463) properties of an order, defined in the traditional sense, are incorporated into the categorical structure by the identity morphism and the composition formula in **C**, respectively. If $c\leq c'$ and $c'\leq c$, then the objects $c, c'$ are isomorphic which is notated $c\cong c'$.

Introducing a monoidal structure to the preorder **C** involves constructing

- an object $I\in\mathbf{C}$, called the *monoidal unit*, and
- a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}$, denoted by "$\;\cdot\;$", called the *monoidal multiplication*.

$I$ and $\cdot$ must be unital and associative, up to isomorphism, meaning:

$(c_1\cdot c_2)\cdot c_3 \cong c_1\cdot (c_2\cdot c_3)$ and $I\cdot c \cong c\cong c\cdot I$.

As · is a functor,

if $c_1\to c_1'$ and $c_2\to c_2'$ then $(c_1\cdot c_2)\to (c_1'\cdot c_2')$.

The other coherence conditions of monoidal categories are fulfilled through the preorder structure as every diagram commutes in a preorder.

The [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers) are an example of a monoidal preorder: having both a [monoid structure](wikipedia://en/19652#Examples) (using + and 0) and a [preorder structure](wikipedia://en/23582#Examples) (using ≤) forms a monoidal preorder as $m\leq n$ and $m'\leq n'$ implies $m+m'\leq n+n'$.

The free monoid on some generating set produces a monoidal preorder, producing the [semi-Thue system](wikipedia://en/2452154).
