# Saharon Shelah

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Saharon Shelah"}

**Saharon Shelah** (שַׂהֲרֹן שֶׁלַח; he, ; born July 3, 1945) is an Israeli mathematician. He is a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) and [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Shelah was born in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem) on July 3, 1945. He is the son of the [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishuv) poet and [Canaanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanism) political activist [Yonatan Ratosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonatan_Ratosh).[^o4] He attended [Tichon Hadash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tichon_Hadash) high school in Tel Aviv.[^o5] He received his PhD for his work on [stable theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_theories) in 1969 from the Hebrew University.

Shelah is married to Yael,[^o4] and has three children.[^o7] His brother, magistrate judge Hamman Shelah was murdered along with his wife and daughter by an Egyptian soldier in the [Ras Burqa massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_Burqa_massacre) in 1985.

Shelah planned to be a scientist while at primary school, but initially was attracted to physics and biology, not mathematics.[^o8] Later he found [mathematical beauty](wikipedia://en/18974136) in studying geometry: He said, "But when I reached the ninth grade I began studying geometry and my eyes opened to that beauty—a system of demonstration and theorems based on a very small number of axioms which impressed me and captivated me." At the age of 15, he decided to become a mathematician, a choice cemented after reading [Abraham Halevy Fraenkel](wikipedia://en/528451)'s book *An Introduction to Mathematics*.[^o8]

He received a B.Sc. from [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in 1964, served in the Israel Defense Forces Army between 1964 and 1967, and obtained a M.Sc. from the Hebrew University (under the direction of Haim Gaifman) in 1967.[^o9] He then worked as a teaching assistant at the Institute of Mathematics of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem while completing a Ph.D. there under the supervision of [Michael Oser Rabin](wikipedia://en/298404),[^o9] on a study of stable theories.

Shelah was a lecturer at Princeton University during 1969–70, and then worked as an assistant professor at the University of California, Los Angeles during 1970–71.[^o9] He became a professor at Hebrew University in 1974, a position he continues to hold.[^o9]

He has been a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at the following universities:[^o9] the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) (1977–78), the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) (1978 and 1982), the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (1984–85), at [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University), Burnaby, British Columbia (1985), and [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), New Jersey (1985). He has been a distinguished visiting professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) since 1986.[^o9]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Shelah's main interests lie in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) in particular, and in [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory).[^o11] He is a prolific author, with more than 1100 peer-reviewed research papers since his first in 1969.[^o12]

In [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), he developed *[classification theory](wikipedia://en/15828771)*, which led him to a solution of [Morley's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morley's_problem).[^o13] In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), he discovered the notion of [proper forcing](wikipedia://en/6250799), an important tool in iterated [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) arguments. With [PCF theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCF_theory), he showed that in spite of the undecidability of the most basic questions of cardinal arithmetic (such as the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705)), there are still highly nontrivial [ZFC](wikipedia://en/152214) theorems about [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) exponentiation. Shelah constructed a [Jónsson group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jónsson_group),[^o14] an uncountable group for which every proper subgroup is countable. He showed that [Whitehead's problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead's_problem)[^o15] is [independent of ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_statements_undecidable_in_ZFC). He gave the first [primitive recursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_recursive) upper bound to [van der Waerden's numbers V(C,N)](wikipedia://en/157178).[^o16] He extended [Arrow's impossibility theorem](wikipedia://en/89425) on voting systems.[^o17]

[Maryanthe Malliaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryanthe_Malliaris) and Shelah[^o18] studied [Keisler's order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keisler's_order), a construction from model theory, and in the process proved equality between two [cardinal characteristics of the continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_characteristics_of_the_continuum), 𝖕 and 𝖙, resolving a problem that had been open for fifty years. This work earned them the 2017 [Hausdorff Medal](wikipedia://en/62036856) of the European Set Theory Society.

Shelah's work has had a deep impact on model theory and set theory. The tools he developed for his classification theory have been applied to a wide number of topics and problems in model theory and have led to great advances in stability theory and its uses in algebra and algebraic geometry as shown for example by [Ehud Hrushovski](wikipedia://en/3132981) and many others. Classification theory involves deep work developed in many dozens of papers to completely solve the spectrum problem on classification of first order theories in terms of structure and number of nonisomorphic models, a huge tour de force. Following that he has extended the work far beyond first order theories, for example for [abstract elementary classes](wikipedia://en/27344508). This work also has had important applications to algebra by works of [Boris Zilber](wikipedia://en/47992829).[^o19]

## Awards {#Awards}

- Three times speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (1974 invited, 1983 plenary, 1986 plenary)
- The first recipient of the [Erdős Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdős_Prize), in 1977[^o21]
- The [Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize) of the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438) in 1983[^o22]
- The [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize), for mathematics, in 1998[^o23]
- The [Bolyai Prize](wikipedia://en/18706230) in 2000[^o24]
- The [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2001[^o25]
- [The EMET Prize for Art, Science and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_EMET_Prize_for_Art,_Science_and_Culture) in 2011[^o26]
- The [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550), for Seminal Contribution to Research, in 2013[^o27]
- Honorary member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences), in 2013[^o28]
- Advanced grant of the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) (2013)[^o29]
- [Hausdorff Medal](wikipedia://en/62036856) of the European Set Theory Society, joint with [Maryanthe Malliaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryanthe_Malliaris), 2017[^o30]
- [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize) in Logic and Philosophy of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences), 2018[^o31]
- [Honorary doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from the [Technische Universität Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Wien), 2019[^o32]
