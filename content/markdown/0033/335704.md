# Mathematics and God

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics and God"}

Connections between **mathematics and God** include the use of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in arguments about the [existence of God](wikipedia://en/621169) and about whether belief in God is beneficial.

:::

## Mathematical arguments for God's existence {#Mathematical_arguments_for_God's_existence}

In the 1070s, [Anselm of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury), an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) medieval [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology), created an [ontological argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontological_argument) which sought to use [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) to prove the existence of God.[^o3] A more elaborate version was given by [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz) in the early eighteenth century.[^o3] [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) created a formalization of Leibniz' version, known as [Gödel's ontological proof](wikipedia://en/12420).[^o3]

A more recent argument was made by [Stephen D. Unwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_D._Unwin) in 2003, who suggested the use of [Bayesian probability](wikipedia://en/4890) to estimate the probability of God's existence, and was also criticized.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

## Mathematical arguments for belief {#Mathematical_arguments_for_belief}

A common application of [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216) to the belief in God is [Pascal's wager](wikipedia://en/215539), published by [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068) in his 1669 work *[Pensées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensées)*. The application is a defense of [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) stating that "If God does not exist, the Atheist loses little by believing in him and gains little by not believing. If God does exist, the Atheist gains eternal life by believing and loses an infinite good by not believing".[^o9] The [atheist's wager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist's_wager) has been proposed as a counterargument to Pascal's Wager.[^o10] The [Pascal's wager](wikipedia://en/215539) itself has faced numerous criticisms,[^o11] and some have reformulated the argument by proposing an [evil God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_God_challenge) that sends [people who believe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief) in him to [Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell).[^o12][^o13]
