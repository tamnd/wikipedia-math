# Raoul Bott

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raoul Bott"}

**Raoul Bott** (September 24, 1923 – December 20, 2005)[^o3] was a Hungarian-American mathematician known for numerous foundational contributions to [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) in its broad sense. He is best known for his [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099), the [Morse–Bott functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse–Bott_function) which he used in this context, and the [Borel–Bott–Weil theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Bott–Weil_theorem).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Bott was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_%281920–1946%29), the son of Margit Kovács and Rudolph Bott.[^o5] His father was of Austrian descent, and his mother was of Hungarian Jewish descent; Bott was raised a Catholic by his mother and stepfather in Bratislava, Czechoslovakia, now the capital of Slovakia.[^o6][^o7] Bott grew up in [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) and spent his working life in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). His family emigrated to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) in 1938, and subsequently he served in the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces) in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

## Career {#Career}

Bott later went to college at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) in [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), where he studied [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). He then earned a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics from [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) in [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh) in 1949. His thesis, titled *Electrical Network Theory*, was written under the direction of [Richard Duffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Duffin). Afterward, he began teaching at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in [Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Arbor). Bott continued his study at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton.[^o9] He was a professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1959 to 1999. In 2005 Bott died of [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego).

With [Richard Duffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Duffin) at Carnegie Mellon, Bott studied existence of [electronic filters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_filter) corresponding to given [positive-real functions](wikipedia://en/23437839). In 1949 they proved[^o10] a fundamental theorem of [filter synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_synthesis_filters). Duffin and Bott extended earlier work by [Otto Brune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Brune) that requisite functions of [complex frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_frequency) *s* could be realized by a [passive network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_filter) of [inductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor) and [capacitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor). The proof relied on [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on the sum of the [degrees of the polynomials](wikipedia://en/5930652) in the numerator and denominator of the rational function.[^o11] In his 2000 interview[^o12] with Allyn Jackson of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), he explained that he sees "networks as discrete versions of harmonic theory", so his experience with [network synthesis](wikipedia://en/59441761) and [electronic filter topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_filter_topology) introduced him to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

Bott met [Arnold S. Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_S._Shapiro) at the IAS and they worked together. He studied the [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), using methods from [Morse theory](wikipedia://en/348560), leading to the [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099) (1957). In the course of this work, he introduced [Morse–Bott functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse–Bott_function), an important generalization of [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function).

This led to his role as collaborator over many years with [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), initially via the part played by periodicity in [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748). Bott made important contributions towards the [index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752), especially in formulating related [fixed-point theorems](wikipedia://en/1011848), in particular the so-called '[Woods Hole fixed-point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole_fixed-point_theorem)', a combination of the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261) and [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530) (it is named after [Woods Hole, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole,_Massachusetts), the site of a conference at which collective discussion formulated it).[^o13] The major Atiyah–Bott papers on what is now the [Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/3636103) were written in the years up to 1968; they collaborated further in recovering in contemporary language [Ivan Petrovsky](wikipedia://en/4598657) on [Petrovsky lacunas](wikipedia://en/18898515) of [hyperbolic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/1575825), prompted by [Lars Gårding](wikipedia://en/8000370). In the 1980s, Atiyah and Bott investigated [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155), using the [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/672202) on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) to obtain topological information about the [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) of stable bundles on Riemann surfaces. In 1983 he spoke to the Canadian Mathematical Society in a talk he called "A topologist marvels at Physics".[^o14]

He is also well known in connection with the [Borel–Bott–Weil theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel–Bott–Weil_theorem) on representation theory of Lie groups via holomorphic [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) and their cohomology groups; and for work on [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227). With [Chern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern) he worked on [Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135), studied [holomorphic vector bundles](wikipedia://en/4794668) over [complex analytic manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_analytic_manifold) and introduced the Bott-Chern classes, useful in the theory of [Arakelov geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakelov_geometry) and also to [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

He introduced [Bott–Samelson varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott–Samelson_varieties) and the [Bott residue formula](wikipedia://en/35025787) for complex manifolds and the [Bott cannibalistic class](wikipedia://en/35048260).

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1964, he was awarded the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075) by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). In 1983, he was awarded the [Jeffery–Williams Prize](wikipedia://en/18739069)[^o16] by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055). In 1987, he was awarded the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science).[^o17]

In 2000, he received the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize). In 2005, he was elected an Overseas Fellow of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London).

## Students {#Students}

Bott had 35 PhD students, including [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294), [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519), [Peter Landweber](wikipedia://en/41079632), [Robert MacPherson](wikipedia://en/10238121), [Robert W. Brooks](wikipedia://en/34336906), [Susan Tolman](wikipedia://en/63929834), and [Eric Weinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Weinstein). Smale and Quillen won [Fields Medals](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1966 and 1978 respectively.
