# Johann Radon

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Radon"}

**Johann Karl August Radon** (; 16 December 1887 – 25 May 1956) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His doctoral dissertation was on the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) (in 1910, at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna)).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Radon[^o4] was born in Tetschen, [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), now [Děčín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Děčín), [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic). He received his doctoral degree at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 1910. He spent the winter semester 1910/11 at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), then he was an assistant at the [German Technical University in Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Technical_University_in_Brno), and from 1912 to 1919 at the [Technical University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Vienna). In 1913/14, he passed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the University of Vienna. Due to his near-sightedness, he was exempt from the draft during wartime.

In 1919, he was called to become [Professor extraordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany#Main_positions) at the newly founded [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg); in 1922, he became *[Professor ordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany#Main_positions)* at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald), and in 1925 at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen). Then he was *[Ordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany#Main_positions)* at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau) from 1928 to 1945.

After a short stay at the [University of Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Innsbruck) he became *Ordinarius* at the Institute of Mathematics of the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) on 1 October 1946. In 1954/55, he was rector of the University of Vienna.

In 1939, Radon became corresponding member of the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences), and in 1947, he became a member. From 1952 to 1956, he was Secretary of the Class of Mathematics and Science of this Academy. From 1948 to 1950, he was president of the [Austrian Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Mathematical_Society).

Johann Radon married Maria Rigele, a secondary school teacher, in 1916. They had three sons who died young or very young. Their daughter Brigitte (1924–2020) obtained a Ph.D. in mathematics at the [University of Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Innsbruck) and married the Austrian mathematician Erich Bukovics in 1950.

Radon, as Curt C. Christian described him in 1987 at the occasion of the unveiling of his brass bust at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), was a friendly, good-natured man, highly esteemed by students and colleagues alike, a noble personality. He did make the impression of a quiet scholar, but he was also sociable and willing to celebrate. He loved music, and he played music with friends at home, being an excellent violinist himself, and a good singer. His love for classical literature lasted through all his life.

In 2003, the [Austrian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Academy_of_Sciences) founded an Institute for Computational and Applied Mathematics and named it after Johann Radon (see the external link below).

## Achievements {#Achievements}

Radon is known for a number of lasting contributions, including:

- his part in the [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746);
- the [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) concept of [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) as [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional);
- the [Radon transform](wikipedia://en/503378), in [integral geometry](wikipedia://en/349336), based on integration over [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862)—with application to [tomography](wikipedia://en/385523) for [scanners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_scanner) (see [tomographic reconstruction](wikipedia://en/995908));
- [Radon's theorem](wikipedia://en/1406385), that *d* + 2 points in *d* dimensions may always be partitioned into two subsets with intersecting [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634);
- the [Radon–Hurwitz numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Hurwitz_number).
- He is possibly the first to make use of the so-called [Radon–Riesz property](wikipedia://en/20894986).
