# Semiprime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semiprime"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **semiprime** is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) that is the [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of exactly two [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). The two primes in the product may equal each other, so the semiprimes include the [squares](wikipedia://en/213919) of prime numbers. Because there are infinitely many prime numbers, there are also infinitely many semiprime numbers. Semiprimes are also called **biprimes**, since they include two primes, or **second numbers**,[^o3] by analogy with how "prime" means "first". Alternatively, semiprimes are called **almost-prime** numbers, specifically the "2-almost-prime" **biprime** and "3-almost-prime" **triprime.**[^o4]

:::

## Formula for number of semiprimes {#Formula_for_number_of_semiprimes}

Let $\pi_2(n)$ denote the number of semiprimes less than or equal to *n*. Then

$$\pi_2(n) = \sum_{k=1}^{\pi \left(\sqrt n\right) } \left[\pi\left(\frac{n}{p_k}\right) - k + 1 \right]$$

where $\pi(x)$ is the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) and $p_k$ denotes the *k*th prime.[^o9]

To see this, take $p_k$ to be the smaller prime factor. Then $p_k \le \sqrt n$, and the larger factor may be any prime $q$ satisfying $p_k \le q \le n/p_k$. The number of such primes is $\pi(n/p_k)-\pi(p_k-1)$. Since $p_k$ is the *k*th prime, $\pi(p_k-1)=k-1$, giving the summand $\pi(n/p_k)-k+1$.

## Properties {#Properties}

Semiprime numbers have no [composite numbers](wikipedia://en/82289) as factors other than themselves.[^o12] For example, the number 26 is semiprime and its only factors are 1, 2, 13, and 26, of which only 26 is composite.

For a squarefree semiprime $n=pq$ (with $p\ne q$) the value of [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452) $\varphi(n)$ (the number of positive integers less than or equal to $n$ that are [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime) to $n$) takes the simple form

$$\varphi(n)=(p-1)(q-1)=n-(p+q)+1.$$

This calculation is an important part of the application of semiprimes in the [RSA cryptosystem](wikipedia://en/25385).[^o13] For a square semiprime $n=p^2$, the formula is again simple:[^o13]

$$\varphi(n)=p(p-1)=n-p.$$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

*Image: Arecibo message bw.svg.* The [Arecibo message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecibo_message)

Semiprimes are highly useful in the area of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), most notably in [public key cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key_cryptography), where they are used by [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) and [pseudorandom number generators](wikipedia://en/45524) such as [Blum Blum Shub](wikipedia://en/39599). These methods rely on the fact that finding two large primes and multiplying them together (resulting in a semiprime) is computationally simple, whereas [finding the original factors](wikipedia://en/15491) appears to be difficult. In the [RSA Factoring Challenge](wikipedia://en/498851), [RSA Security](wikipedia://en/301240) offered prizes for the factoring of specific large semiprimes and several prizes were awarded. The original RSA Factoring Challenge was issued in 1991, and was replaced in 2001 by the New RSA Factoring Challenge, which was later withdrawn in 2007.[^o16]

In 1974 the [Arecibo message](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecibo_message) was sent with a radio signal aimed at a [star cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_cluster). It consisted of $1679$ binary digits intended to be interpreted as a $23 \times 73$ [bitmap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitmap) image. The number $1679=23\cdot 73$ was chosen because it is a semiprime and therefore can be arranged into a rectangular image in only two distinct ways (23 rows and 73 columns, or 73 rows and 23 columns).[^o17]
