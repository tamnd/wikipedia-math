# Hopkins statistic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hopkins statistic"}

The **Hopkins statistic** (introduced by Brian Hopkins and [John Gordon Skellam](wikipedia://en/31693107)) is a way of measuring the [cluster tendency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_tendency) of a data set.[^o2] It belongs to the family of sparse sampling tests. It acts as a [statistical hypothesis test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing) where the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) is that the data is generated by a [Poisson point process](wikipedia://en/41597450) and are thus uniformly randomly distributed.[^o3] If individuals are aggregated, then its value approaches 1, and if they are randomly distributed along the value tends to 0.5.[^o4]

:::

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

A typical formulation of the Hopkins statistic follows.[^o3]

Let $X$ be the set of $n$ data points.

Generate a random sample $\overset{\sim}{X}$ of $m \ll n$ data points sampled without replacement from $X$.

Generate a set $Y$ of $m$ uniformly randomly distributed data points.

Define two distance measures,

$u_i,$ the minimum distance (given some suitable metric) of $y_i \in Y$ to its nearest neighbour in $X$, and

$w_i,$ the minimum distance of $\overset{\sim}{x}_i \in \overset{\sim}{X}\subseteq X$ to its nearest neighbour $x_j \in X,\, \overset{\sim}{x_i}\ne x_j.$

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

With the above notation, if the data is $d$ dimensional, then the Hopkins statistic is defined as:[^o8]

$$H=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^m{u_i^d}}{\sum_{i=1}^m{u_i^d}+\sum_{i=1}^m{w_i^d}} \,$$

Under the null hypotheses, this statistic has a Beta(m,m) distribution.

:::
