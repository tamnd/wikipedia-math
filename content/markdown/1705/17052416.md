# Quantificational variability effect

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantificational variability effect"}

**Quantificational variability effect** (**QVE**) is the intuitive equivalence of certain sentences with quantificational adverbs (Q-adverbs) and sentences without these, but with quantificational determiner phrases (DP) in argument position instead.

:::

- \1. (a) A cat is **usually** smart. (Q-adverb)
- \1. (b) **Most cats** are smart. (DP)
- \2. (a) A dog is **always** smart. (Q-adverb)
- \2. (b) **All dogs** are smart. (DP)[^o2]

Analysis of QVE is widely cited as entering the literature with [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kellogg_Lewis)' "Adverbs of Quantification" (1975), where he proposes QVE as a solution to [Peter Geach](wikipedia://en/302884)'s [donkey sentence](wikipedia://en/16183619) (1962). Terminology, and comprehensive analysis, is normally attributed to Stephen Berman's "Situation-Based Semantics for Adverbs of Quantification" (1987).
