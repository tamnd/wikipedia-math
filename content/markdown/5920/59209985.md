# Jack Thorne (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jack Thorne"}

**Jack A. Thorne** (born 13 June 1987) is a British mathematician working in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and arithmetic aspects of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). He specialises in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Thorne read mathematics at [Trinity Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge). He completed his PhD with [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863) and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 2012.

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Thorne was a Clay Research Fellow.[^o5] Currently, he is a Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge),[^o6] where he has been since 2015, and is also a fellow at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).

Thorne's paper on adequate representations[^o7] significantly extended the applicability of the Taylor–Wiles method. His paper on deformations of reducible representations[^o8] generalized previous results of [Chris Skinner](wikipedia://en/59264582) and [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) from two-dimensional representations to *n*-dimensional representations. With Gebhard Böckle, [Michael Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452), and [Chandrashekhar Khare](wikipedia://en/11350853), he has applied techniques from modularity lifting to the Langlands conjectures over function fields. With Kai-Wen Lan, Harris, and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931), Thorne constructed [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) associated to non-self dual regular algebraic cuspidal [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) for GL(*n*) over CM fields.[^o9] Thorne's 2015 joint work with Khare on potential automorphy and [Leopoldt's conjecture](wikipedia://en/21829200)[^o10] has led to a proof of a potential version of the [modularity conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modularity_conjecture)[^o11] for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over imaginary [quadratic fields](wikipedia://en/454781).[^o12]

In joint work with James Newton, Thorne has established symmetric power functoriality for all [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms).[^o13][^o14]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Thorne was awarded the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) in 2017.[^o16] In 2018, Thorne was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Rio de Janeiro.[^o17][^o18] He was awarded the 2018 [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize) for his contributions to the field of mathematics. He shared the prize with [Yifeng Liu](wikipedia://en/59846401).[^o19][^o20][^o21] In April 2020 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o22] In 2020 he received the EMS Prize of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470),[^o23] in 2021 he was awarded a [New Horizons in Mathematics Prize](wikipedia://en/43125933#2022) and in 2022 he was awarded the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110).[^o24] For 2023 he received the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Number Theory of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o25] In 2024 he received the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) jointly with James Newton.[^o26]
