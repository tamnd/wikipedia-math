# Noise (spectral phenomenon)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noise"}

**Noise** is any type of random, troublesome, problematic, or unwanted [signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28electrical_engineering%29).

:::

Acoustic [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise) may mar aesthetic experience, such as attending a concert hall. It may also be a medical issue inherent in the biology of hearing.

In technology, noise is unwanted signals in a device or apparatus, commonly of an electrical nature. The nature of noise is much studied in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and is a prominent topic in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).

This article provides a survey of specific topics linked to their primary articles.

## Acoustic noise {#Acoustic_noise}

### In transportation {#In_transportation}

- [Aircraft noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_noise)
- [Jet noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_noise), caused by high-velocity jets and turbulent eddies
- [Noise and vibration on maritime vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_and_vibration_on_maritime_vessels)
- [Noise, vibration, and harshness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise,_vibration,_and_harshness), quality criteria for vehicles
- Traffic noise, including [roadway noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadway_noise) and [train noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_noise)

### Other acoustic noise {#Other_acoustic_noise}

- [Artificial noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_noise), in spectator sports
- [Background noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_noise), in acoustics, any sound other than the monitored one
- [Comfort noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfort_noise), used in telecommunications to fill silent gaps
- [Grey noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_noise), random noise with a psychoacoustic adjusted spectrum
- [Industrial noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_noise), relevant to hearing damage and industrial hygiene
- [Noise pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution), that affects negatively the quality of life

## Noise in biology {#Noise_in_biology}

- [Cellular noise](wikipedia://en/32104707), in biology, random variability between cells
- [Developmental noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_noise), variations among living beings with the same genome
- [Neuronal noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuronal_noise), in neuroscience
- [Synaptic noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptic_noise), in neuroscience
- [Transcriptional noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcriptional_noise), in biochemistry, errors in genetic transcription

## Noise in computer graphics {#Noise_in_computer_graphics}

*Noise* in computer graphics refers to various pseudo-random functions used to create textures, including:

- [Gradient noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient_noise), created by interpolation of a lattice of pseudorandom gradients
  - [Perlin noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlin_noise), a type of gradient noise developed in 1983
- [Simplex noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplex_noise), a method for constructing an *n*-dimensional noise function comparable to Perlin noise
- [Simulation noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation_noise), a function that creates a divergence-free field
- [Value noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_noise), created by interpolation of a lattice of pseudorandom values; differs from gradient noise
- [Wavelet noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelet_noise), an alternative to Perlin noise which reduces problems of aliasing and detail loss
- [Worley noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worley_noise), a noise function introduced by Steven Worley in 1996

## Noise in mathematics {#Noise_in_mathematics}

- Any one of many statistical types or [colors of noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colors_of_noise), such as
  - [White noise](wikipedia://en/46182), which has constant power spectral density
  - [Gaussian noise](wikipedia://en/1387827), with a probability density function equal to that of the normal distribution
  - [Pink noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_noise), with spectral density inversely proportional to frequency
  - [Brownian noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_noise) or "brown" noise, with spectral density inversely proportional to the square of frequency
- [Pseudorandom noise](wikipedia://en/41593), in cryptography, artificial signal that can pass for random
- [Statistical noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_noise), a colloquialism for recognized amounts of unexplained variation in a sample
- [Shot noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_noise), noise which can be modeled by a Poisson process
- [Noise-based logic](wikipedia://en/31730014), where logic values are different stochastic processes
- [Noise print](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_print), a statistical signature of ambient noise, used in its suppression

## Other types of noise {#Other_types_of_noise}

- [Electrochemical noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical_noise), electrical fluctuations in electrolysis, corrosion, etc.
- [Phonon noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonon_noise), in materials science
- [Seismic noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismic_noise), random tremors of the ground

## Measures of noise intensity {#Measures_of_noise_intensity}

- [Noise figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_figure), the ratio of the output noise power to attributable thermal noise
- [Ambient noise level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_noise_level), the background sound pressure level at a given location
- [Noise power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_power), with several related meanings
- [Noise spectral density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_spectral_density), *N*<sub>o</sub> measured in Watt/Hertz
- [Noise temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_temperature), temperature that would produce equivalent semiconductor noise
