# Andrew Booker (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Booker"}

**Andrew Richard Booker** (born 1976)[^o3] is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is currently Professor of Pure Mathematics at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol). He is an [analytic number theorist](wikipedia://en/251513) known for his work on [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) and his contributions to the [sums of three cubes problem](wikipedia://en/60187666).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Booker graduated from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia) in 1998, earning the E. J. McShane Prize as the top undergraduate in mathematics. He completed his doctoral degree at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 2003, under the supervision of [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In the spring of 2019 Booker gained international attention by showing that 33 can be expressed as the sum of three [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_number). At that time 33 and 42 were the only numbers less than 100 for which this problem was open. Later that year, in joint work with [Andrew Sutherland](wikipedia://en/63111112) of [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), he settled the case of 42, as well as answering a 65-year-old question of [Mordell](wikipedia://en/991484) by finding a third representation for 3 as the sum of three cubes. [Popular Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mechanics) cited the result for 42 as one of the top two mathematical breakthroughs of 2019.
