# School Mathematics Project

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="School Mathematics Project"}

The **School Mathematics Project** arose in the United Kingdom as part of the [new mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_mathematics) educational movement of the 1960s.[^o2] It is a developer of mathematics textbooks for [secondary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school), formerly based in [Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton) in the UK.

:::

Now generally known as SMP, it began as a research project inspired by a 1961 conference chaired by [Bryan Thwaites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Thwaites) at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton), which itself was precipitated by calls to reform mathematics teaching in the wake of the [Sputnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik) launch by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the same circumstances that prompted the wider [New Math](wikipedia://en/404988) movement. It maintained close ties with the former Collaborative Group for Research in Mathematics Education at the university.

Instead of dwelling on 'traditional' areas such as [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), SMP dwelt on subjects such as [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [logic](wikipedia://en/19636), non-cartesian co-ordinate systems, [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) mathematics, [affine transforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_transform), [Euclidean vectors](wikipedia://en/32533), and non-decimal number systems.

### SMP, Book 1 {#SMP,_Book_1}

This was published in 1965. It was aimed at entry level pupils at [secondary school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_school), and was the first book in a series of 4 preparing pupils for Elementary Mathematics Examination at 'O' level.[^o5]

## Simpol programming language {#Simpol_programming_language}

The Simpol language was devised by The School Mathematics Project [^o8] in the 1960s so as to introduce secondary pupils (typically aged 13) to what was then the novel concept of computer programming. It runs on the fictitious Simon computer.

An interpreter for the Simpol language (that will run on a present-day PC) can be downloaded from the University of Southampton, at their SMP 2.0 website.[^n1]

## Joint Schools Project (JSP) {#Joint_Schools_Project_(JSP)}

The Joint Schools Project in West Africa was one of the offshoots of SMP. Its originators were Michael Mitchelmore and Brian Radnor. Starting at [Achimota College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achimota_College), [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana) in 1966, it aimed to introduce SMP ideas within an African curriculum. Later, when Mitchelmore moved to Jamaica, a West Indian version of JSP was developed.
