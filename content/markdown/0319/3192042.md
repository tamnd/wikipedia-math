# Tubular neighborhood

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tubular neighborhood"}

*Image: Tubular neighborhood.png.* A curve, in blue, and some lines perpendicular to it, in green. Small portions of those lines around the curve are in red.

*Image: Voisinage tubulaire.svg.* A close up of the figure above. The curve is in blue, and its tubular neighborhood *T* is in red. With the notation in the article, the curve is *S*, the space containing the curve is *M*, and $T = j(N).$

*Image: Tubular neighborhood3.png.* A schematic illustration of the normal bundle *N*, with the zero section $N_0$ in blue. The transformation *j* maps *N*<sub>0</sub> to the curve *S* in the figure above, and *N* to the tubular neighbourhood of *S*.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **tubular neighborhood** of a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) is an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) around it resembling the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200). The tubular neighbourhood deformation retracts onto the submanifold.

:::

The idea behind a tubular neighborhood can be explained in a simple example. Consider a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) curve in the plane without self-intersections. On each point on the [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) draw a line [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the curve. Unless the curve is straight, these lines will intersect among themselves in a rather complicated fashion. However, if one looks only in a narrow band around the curve, the portions of the lines in that band will not intersect, and will cover the entire band without gaps. This band is a tubular neighborhood.

In general, let *S* be a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M*, and let *N* be the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) of *S* in *M*. Here *S* plays the role of the curve and *M* the role of the plane containing the curve. Consider the natural map

$$i : N_0 \to S$$

which establishes a [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) correspondence between the [zero section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_section) $N_0$ of *N* and the submanifold *S* of *M*. An extension *j* of this map to the entire normal bundle *N* with values in *M* such that $j(N)$ is an open set in *M* and *j* is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) between *N* and $j(N)$ is called a tubular neighbourhood.

Often one calls the open set $T = j(N),$ rather than *j* itself, a tubular neighbourhood of *S*, it is assumed implicitly that the homeomorphism *j* mapping *N* to *T* exists.

## Normal tube {#Normal_tube}

A **normal tube** to a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) curve is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) defined as the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of all discs such that

- all the discs have the same fixed radius;
- the center of each disc lies on the [curve](wikipedia://en/89246); and
- each disc lies in a plane [normal](wikipedia://en/102221) to the curve where the curve passes through that disc's center.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $S \subseteq M$ be smooth manifolds. A tubular neighborhood of $S$ in $M$ is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) $\pi: E \to S$ together with a smooth map $J : E \to M$ such that

- $J \circ 0_E = i$ where $i$ is the embedding $S \hookrightarrow M$ and $0_E$ the zero section
- there exists some open sets $U \subseteq E$ and $V \subseteq M$ with $0_E[S] \subseteq U$ and $S \subseteq V$ such that $J\vert_U : U \to V$ is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564).

The normal bundle is a tubular neighborhood and because of the diffeomorphism condition in the second point, all tubular neighborhood have the same dimension, namely (the dimension of the vector bundle considered as a manifold is) that of $M.$

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Generalizations of smooth manifolds yield generalizations of tubular neighborhoods, such as regular neighborhoods, or [spherical fibrations](wikipedia://en/48880896#Spherical_fibration) for [Poincaré spaces](wikipedia://en/17094227).

These generalizations are used to produce analogs to the normal bundle, or rather to the [stable normal bundle](wikipedia://en/9901365), which are replacements for the tangent bundle (which does not admit a direct description for these spaces).
