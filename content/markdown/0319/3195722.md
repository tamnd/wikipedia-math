# Fatou–Lebesgue theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fatou–Lebesgue theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Fatou–Lebesgue theorem** establishes a chain of [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) relating the [integrals](wikipedia://en/15532) (in the sense of [Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration)) of the [limit inferior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior) and the [limit superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_superior_and_limit_inferior) of a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) to the limit inferior and the limit superior of integrals of these functions. The theorem is named after [Pierre Fatou](wikipedia://en/629100) and [Henri Léon Lebesgue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Léon_Lebesgue).[^o2]

:::

If the sequence of functions converges [pointwise](wikipedia://en/340198), the inequalities turn into [equalities](wikipedia://en/90446) and the theorem reduces to Lebesgue's [dominated convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/351853).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *f*<sub>1</sub>, *f*<sub>2</sub>, ... denote a sequence of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) functions defined on a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) (*S*,*Σ*,*μ*). If there exists a Lebesgue-integrable function *g* on *S* which dominates the sequence in absolute value, meaning that |*f*<sub>*n*</sub>| ≤ *g* for all [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) *n*, then all *f*<sub>*n*</sub> as well as the limit inferior and the limit superior of the *f*<sub>*n*</sub> are integrable and

$$\int_S \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n\,d\mu \le \liminf_{n\to\infty} \int_S f_n\,d\mu \le \limsup_{n\to\infty} \int_S f_n\,d\mu \le \int_S \limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n\,d\mu\,.$$

Here the limit inferior and the limit superior of the *f*<sub>*n*</sub> are taken pointwise. The integral of the absolute value of these limiting functions is bounded above by the integral of *g*.

Since the middle inequality (for sequences of real numbers) is always true, the directions of the other inequalities are easy to remember.

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

All *f*<sub>*n*</sub> as well as the limit inferior and the limit superior of the *f*<sub>*n*</sub> are measurable and dominated in absolute value by *g*, hence integrable.

Using [linearity of the Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288#Basic_theorems_of_the_Lebesgue_integral) and applying [Fatou's lemma](wikipedia://en/291614) to the non-negative functions $f_n + g$ we get

$$\int_X \liminf_{n \to\infty} f_n \,d\mu + \int_X g \,d\mu = \int_X \liminf_{n \to \infty} (f_n + g) \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X (f_n + g) \, d\mu =\liminf_{n \to \infty} \int_X f_n \,d\mu + \int_X g \, d\mu.$$

Cancelling the finite(!) $\int_X g \,d\mu$ term we get the first inequality. The second inequality is the elementary inequality between $\liminf$ and $\limsup$. The last inequality follows by applying [reverse Fatou lemma](wikipedia://en/291614#Reverse_Fatou_lemma), i.e. applying the Fatou lemma to the non-negative functions $g-f_n$, and again (up to sign) cancelling the finite $\int_X g \,d\mu$ term.

Finally, since $\limsup_n |f_n| \le g$,

$$\max\left(\left|\int_S \liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n\,d\mu\right| , \left|\int_S \limsup_{n\to\infty} f_n\,d\mu\right| \right) \le\int_S \max\left(\left|\liminf_{n\to\infty} f_n\right|, \left|\limsup_{n\to \infty} f_n\right|\right)\, d\mu \le\int_S \limsup_{n\to\infty} |f_n|\,d\mu \le\int_S g\,d\mu$$

by the [monotonicity of the Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288#Basic_theorems_of_the_Lebesgue_integral).
