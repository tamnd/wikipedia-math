# Higgs prime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Higgs prime"}

A **Higgs prime**, named after [Denis Higgs](wikipedia://en/37948324), is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) with a totient (one less than the prime) that evenly divides the square of the product of the smaller Higgs primes. (This can be generalized to cubes, fourth powers, etc.) To put it algebraically, given an exponent *a*, a Higgs prime *Hp*<sub>*n*</sub> satisfies

:::

$$\phi(Hp_n)|\prod_{i = 1}^{n - 1} {Hp_i}^a\mbox{ and }Hp_n > Hp_{n - 1}$$

where Φ(*x*) is [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452).

For squares, the first few Higgs primes are [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_%28number%29), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_%28number%29), [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_%28number%29), [11](wikipedia://en/208156), [13](wikipedia://en/37231), [19](wikipedia://en/208292), [23](wikipedia://en/362203), [29](wikipedia://en/362212), [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_%28number%29), [37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_%28number%29), [43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43_%28number%29), [47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47_%28number%29), ... . So, for example, 13 is a Higgs prime because the square of the product of the smaller Higgs primes is 5336100, and divided by 12 this is 444675. But 17 is not a Higgs prime because the square of the product of the smaller primes is 901800900, which leaves a remainder of 4 when divided by 16.

Observation further reveals that a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) $2^{2^n} + 1$can't be a Higgs prime for the *a*th power if *a* is less than 2<sup>*n*</sup>.

It's not known if there are infinitely many Higgs primes for any exponent *a* greater than 1. The situation is quite different for *a* = 1. There are only four of them: 2, 3, 7 and 43 (a sequence [suspiciously](wikipedia://en/18093481) similar to [Sylvester's sequence](wikipedia://en/4535485)). (Burris Lee 1993) found that about a fifth of the primes below a million are Higgs prime, and they concluded that even if the sequence of Higgs primes for squares is finite, "a computer enumeration is not feasible."
