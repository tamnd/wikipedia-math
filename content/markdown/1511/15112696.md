# Roy C. Geary

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roy C. Geary"}

**Robert (Roy) Charles Geary** (April 11, 1896 – February 8, 1983)[^o3] was an Irish mathematician, [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and founder of both the [Central Statistics Office](wikipedia://en/546187) and the [Economic and Social Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Social_Research_Institute). Geary is known for his contributions to the estimation of [errors-in-variables models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors-in-variables_models),[^o4] [Geary's C](wikipedia://en/15300799), the [Geary–Khamis dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geary–Khamis_dollar), the [Stone–Geary utility function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone–Geary_utility_function), and Geary's theorem,[^o5] which has that if the sample mean is distributed independently of the sample variance, then the population is [distributed normally](wikipedia://en/21462).[^o6][^o7][^o8]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Geary was born in [Dublin, Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin) and received his secondary education at the [O'Connell School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Connell_School). He went on to study mathematics and mathematical physics at the [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin), where he obtained his B.Sc. and M.Sc. degrees in 1916 and 1918, respectively. He was awarded a scholarship to continue his study at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in Paris, where he attended lectures by [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161), [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274), [Édouard Goursat](wikipedia://en/1674255), [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514), and [Paul Langevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Langevin). Geary returned to Ireland in 1921, and was offered a lecturer position in mathematics at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton) (1922–23) and in applied economics at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) (1946–47). He was a statistician in the Department of Industry and Commerce between 1923 and 1957. The [National University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Ireland) conferred a Doctorate of Science on him in 1938.[^o3]

Geary was the founding director of the [Central Statistics Office (Ireland)](wikipedia://en/546187) (in 1949). He was head of the [National Accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Accounts) Branch of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) from 1957 to 1960. He was the founding director of the [Economic and Social Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Social_Research_Institute) (ESRI) in 1960 where he stayed till his retirement in 1966. He was an honorary fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). In 1981, he won the [Boyle Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyle_Medal).[^o10] To honour his contributions to social sciences, the UCD Geary Institute for Public Policy was named after him in 2005.[^o11]
