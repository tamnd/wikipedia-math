# Six exponentials theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Six exponentials theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), the **six exponentials theorem** is a result that, given the right conditions on the exponents, guarantees the [transcendence](wikipedia://en/30325) of at least one of a set of six exponentials.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If $x_1, x_2, x_3$ are three [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) that are [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) over the [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers), and $y_1, y_2$ are two complex numbers that are also linearly independent over the rational numbers, then at least one of the following numbers is [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325):

$$e^{x_1y_1}, e^{x_1y_2}, e^{x_2y_1}, e^{x_2y_2}, e^{x_3y_1}, e^{x_3y_2}.$$

The theorem can be stated in terms of logarithms by introducing the set *L* of logarithms of [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158):

$$\mathcal{L}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,e^\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\}.$$

The theorem then says that if $\lambda_{ij}$ are elements of *L* for $i = 1,2, j = 1,2,3$ such that $\lambda_{11}, \lambda_{12}, \lambda_{13}$ are linearly independent over the rational numbers, and λ<sub>11</sub> and λ<sub>21</sub> are also linearly independent over the rational numbers, then the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}&\lambda_{12}&\lambda_{13} \\ \lambda_{21}&\lambda_{22}&\lambda_{23}\end{pmatrix}$$

has [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) 2.

:::

## History {#History}

A special case of the result where *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, and *x*<sub>3</sub> are logarithms of positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), *y*<sub>1</sub> = 1, and *y*<sub>2</sub> is [real](wikipedia://en/20646438), was first mentioned in a paper by [Leonidas Alaoglu](wikipedia://en/7689104) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) from 1944 in which they try to prove that the ratio of consecutive [colossally abundant numbers](wikipedia://en/1759217) is always [prime](wikipedia://en/23666). They claimed that [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) knew of a proof of this special case, but it is not recorded.[^o5] Using the special case they manage to prove that the ratio of consecutive colossally abundant numbers is always either a prime or a [semiprime](wikipedia://en/339542).

The theorem was first explicitly stated and proved in its complete form independently by [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991)[^o6] and [Kanakanahalli Ramachandra](wikipedia://en/32512780)[^o7] in the 1960s.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Five exponentials theorem"}

## Five exponentials theorem {#Five_exponentials_theorem}

A stronger, related result is the **five exponentials theorem**,[^o10] which is as follows. Let *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub> and *y*<sub>1</sub>, *y*<sub>2</sub> be two pairs of complex numbers, with each pair being linearly independent over the rational numbers, and let γ be a non-zero algebraic number. Then at least one of the following five numbers is transcendental:

$$e^{x_1 y_1}, e^{x_1 y_2}, e^{x_2 y_1}, e^{x_2 y_2}, e^{\gamma x_2/x_1}.$$

This theorem implies the six exponentials theorem and in turn is implied by the as yet unproven four exponentials conjecture, which says that in fact one of the first four numbers on this list must be transcendental.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Sharp six exponentials theorem"}

## Sharp six exponentials theorem {#Sharp_six_exponentials_theorem}

Another related result that implies both the six exponentials theorem and the five exponentials theorem is the **sharp six exponentials theorem**.[^o13] This theorem is as follows. Let *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, and *x*<sub>3</sub> be complex numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers, and let *y*<sub>1</sub> and *y*<sub>2</sub> be a pair of complex numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers, and suppose that β<sub>*ij*</sub> are six algebraic numbers for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ 3 and 1 ≤ *j* ≤ 2 such that the following six numbers are algebraic:

$$e^{x_1 y_1-\beta_{11}}, e^{x_1 y_2-\beta_{12}}, e^{x_2 y_1-\beta_{21}}, e^{x_2 y_2-\beta_{22}}, e^{x_3 y_1-\beta_{31}}, e^{x_3 y_2-\beta_{32}}.$$

Then *x*<sub>*i*</sub> *y*<sub>*j*</sub> = β<sub>*ij*</sub> for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ 3 and 1 ≤ *j* ≤ 2. The six exponentials theorem then follows by setting β<sub>*ij*</sub> = 0 for every *i* and *j*, while the five exponentials theorem follows by setting *x*<sub>3</sub> = γ/*x*<sub>1</sub> and using [Baker's theorem](wikipedia://en/28751817) to ensure that the *x*<sub>*i*</sub> are linearly independent.

There is a sharp version of the five exponentials theorem as well, although it as yet unproven so is known as the **sharp five exponentials conjecture**.[^o14] This conjecture implies both the sharp six exponentials theorem and the five exponentials theorem, and is stated as follows. Let *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub> and *y*<sub>1</sub>, *y*<sub>2</sub> be two pairs of complex numbers, with each pair being linearly independent over the rational numbers, and let α, β<sub>11</sub>, β<sub>12</sub>, β<sub>21</sub>, β<sub>22</sub>, and γ be six algebraic numbers with γ ≠ 0 such that the following five numbers are algebraic:

$$e^{x_1 y_1-\beta_{11}}, e^{x_1 y_2-\beta_{12}}, e^{x_2 y_1-\beta_{21}}, e^{x_2 y_2-\beta_{22}}, e^{(\gamma x_2/x_1)-\alpha}.$$

Then *x*<sub>*i*</sub> *y*<sub>*j*</sub> = β<sub>*ij*</sub> for 1 ≤ *i*, *j* ≤ 2 and γ*x*<sub>2</sub> = α*x*<sub>1</sub>.

A consequence of this conjecture that isn't currently known would be the transcendence of *e*<sup>π²</sup>, by setting *x*<sub>1</sub> = *y*<sub>1</sub> = β<sub>11</sub> = 1, *x*<sub>2</sub> = *y*<sub>2</sub> = *i*π, and all the other values in the statement to be zero.

:::

## Strong six exponentials theorem {#Strong_six_exponentials_theorem}

*Image: N-Exponentials Conjecture.png.* The logical implications between the various problems in this circle. Those in red are as yet unproven while those in blue are known results. The top most result refers to that discussed at [Baker's theorem](wikipedia://en/28751817#Extensions), while the four exponentials conjectures are detailed at the [four exponentials conjecture](wikipedia://en/15114628) article.

A further strengthening of the theorems and conjectures in this area are the strong versions. The **strong six exponentials theorem** is a result proved by Damien Roy that implies the sharp six exponentials theorem.[^o17] This result concerns the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the algebraic numbers generated by 1 and all logarithms of algebraic numbers, denoted here as *L*<sup>∗</sup>. So *L*<sup>∗</sup> is the set of all complex numbers of the form

$$\beta_0+\sum_{i=1}^n \beta_i\log\alpha_i,$$

for some *n* ≥ 0, where all the β<sub>*i*</sub> and α<sub>*i*</sub> are algebraic and every [branch of the logarithm](wikipedia://en/4870290#Branches_of_the_complex_logarithm) is considered. The strong six exponentials theorem then says that if *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, and *x*<sub>3</sub> are complex numbers that are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers, and if *y*<sub>1</sub> and *y*<sub>2</sub> are a pair of complex numbers that are also linearly independent over the algebraic numbers then at least one of the six numbers *x*<sub>*i*</sub> *y*<sub>*j*</sub> for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ 3 and 1 ≤ *j* ≤ 2 is not in *L*<sup>∗</sup>. This is stronger than the standard six exponentials theorem which says that one of these six numbers is not simply the logarithm of an algebraic number.

There is also a **strong five exponentials conjecture** formulated by [Michel Waldschmidt](wikipedia://en/35216482).[^o18] It would imply both the strong six exponentials theorem and the sharp five exponentials conjecture. This conjecture claims that if *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub> and *y*<sub>1</sub>, *y*<sub>2</sub> are two pairs of complex numbers, with each pair being linearly independent over the algebraic numbers, then at least one of the following five numbers is not in *L*<sup>∗</sup>:

$$x_1y_1,\,x_1y_2,\,x_2y_1,\,x_2y_2,\,x_1/x_2.$$

All the above conjectures and theorems are consequences of the unproven extension of [Baker's theorem](wikipedia://en/28751817), that logarithms of algebraic numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers are automatically algebraically independent too. ## Generalization to commutative group varieties {#Generalization_to_commutative_group_varieties}

The exponential function $e^{z}$ uniformizes the exponential map of the multiplicative group $\mathbf{G}_{m}$. Therefore, we can reformulate the six exponential theorem more abstractly as follows:

Let $G = \mathbf{G}_{m} \times \mathbf{G}_{m}$ and take $u : \mathbf{C} \to G(\mathbf{C})$ to be a non-zero complex-analytic group [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564). Define $L$ to be the set of complex numbers $l$ for which $u(l)$ is an algebraic point of $G$. If a minimal generating set of $L$ over $\mathbf{Q}$ has more than two elements then the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) $u(\mathbf{C})$ is an algebraic subgroup of $G(\mathbf{C})$.

(In order to derive the classical statement, set $u(z) = (e^{y_{1}z}; e^{y_{2}z})$ and note that $\mathbf{Q}x_{1} + \mathbf{Q}x_{2} + \mathbf{Q}x_{3}$ is a subset of $L$).

In this way, the statement of the six exponentials theorem can be generalized to an arbitrary commutative [group variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_variety) $G$ over the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of algebraic numbers. This **generalized six exponential conjecture**, however, seems out of scope at the current state of [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698).

For the special but interesting cases $G = \mathbf{G}_{m} \times E$ and $G = E \times E'$, where $E, E'$ are [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) over the field of algebraic numbers, results towards the generalized six exponential conjecture were proven by Aleksander Momot.[^o20] These results involve the exponential function $e^{z}$ and a Weierstrass function $\wp$ resp. two Weierstrass functions $\wp, \wp'$ with algebraic invariants $g_2, g_3, g_2', g_3'$, instead of the two exponential functions $e^{y_1z}, e^{y_2z}$ in the classical statement.

Let $G = \mathbf{G}_{m} \times E$ and suppose $E$ is not isogenous to a curve over a real field and that $u(\mathbf{C})$ is not an algebraic subgroup of $G(\mathbf{C})$. Then $L$ is generated over $\mathbf{Q}$ either by two elements $x_{1}, x_{2}$, or three elements $x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}$ which are not all contained in a real line $\mathbf{R}c$, where $c$ is a non-zero complex number. A similar result is shown for $G = E \times E'$.[^o21]
