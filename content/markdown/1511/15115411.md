# Formation rule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formation rule"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **formation rules** are rules for describing [well-formed words](wikipedia://en/10939#Definition) over the [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_%28computer_science%29) of a [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939).[^o2] These rules only address the location and manipulation of the strings of the language. It does not describe anything else about a language, such as its [semantics](wikipedia://en/29107) (i.e. what the strings mean). (See also [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716)).

:::

## Formal language {#Formal_language}

A *formal language* is an organized [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673) the essential feature being that it can be precisely defined in terms of just the shapes and locations of those symbols. Such a language can be defined, then, without any [reference](wikipedia://en/20110874) to any [meanings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaning_%28linguistics%29) of any of its expressions; it can exist before any [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) is assigned to it—that is, before it has any meaning. A [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716) determines which symbols and sets of symbols are [formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28mathematical_logic%29) in a formal language.

## Formal systems {#Formal_systems}

A *formal system* (also called a *logical calculus*, or a *logical system*) consists of a formal language together with a [deductive apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_apparatus) (also called a *deductive system*). The deductive apparatus may consist of a set of [transformation rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_rule) (also called *inference rules*) or a set of [axioms](wikipedia://en/928), or have both. A formal system is used to [derive](wikipedia://en/183478) one expression from one or more other expressions. Propositional and predicate calculi are examples of formal systems.

## Propositional and predicate logic {#Propositional_and_predicate_logic}

The formation rules of a [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) may, for instance, take a form such that;

- if we take Φ to be a propositional formula we can also take Φ to be a formula;
- if we take Φ and Ψ to be a propositional formulas we can also take (Φ Ψ), (Φ Ψ), (Φ Ψ) and (Φ Ψ) to also be formulas.

A [predicate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_calculus) will usually include all the same rules as a propositional calculus, with the addition of [quantifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantifiers_%28logic%29) such that if we take Φ to be a formula of propositional logic and α as a [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) then we can take (α)Φ and (α)Φ each to be formulas of our predicate calculus.
