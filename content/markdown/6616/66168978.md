# Straight line mission

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Straight line mission"}

A **straight line mission** is an outdoor recreational activity where participants attempt to cross an area in a completely straight line, typically on foot. Straight line missions have been popularised by British [YouTuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTuber) Tom Davies ([GeoWizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoWizard)) who has attempted a number of ambitious straight line missions and documented them on his YouTube channel.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] These videos have inspired others to attempt similar missions.[^o6]

:::

Another similar challenge is [Beeline Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land's_End_to_John_o'_Groats#In_a_straight_line), where participants travel (using kayaks, bikes and on foot) from [Land's End to John o' Groats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land's_End_to_John_o'_Groats) in a straight line. This was first completed in 2014 by a four person team.[^o7]

Straight line missions also have some similarity to [parkour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkour), where practitioners attempt to get from one point to another in the fastest and most efficient way possible, though are usually attempted over much longer distances across non-urban environments. Well known parkour practitioners [Storror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storror) have attempted straight line missions.[^o8] Walking in a straight line following a specified compass bearing is also a commonly required skill in wilderness navigation.[^o9] The revolutionary organisation the [Situationist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situationist_International) suggested walking in a straight line as a potentially revolutionary act[^o10] though most straight line missions are attempted for recreational or entertainment purposes. The artist [Richard Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Long_%28artist%29) completed a straight line mission across [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor)[^o11] the map of which is held in the [MoMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoMA) collection.[^o12]

## History {#History}

Between 800BCE and 100BCE, [Paracas culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracas_culture) in [Palpa province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpa_province) created straight line [geoglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoglyph) in what is now Peru.[^o14] Also in Peru, between 500BCE and 500CE some eight hundred straight [Nazca lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_lines) were later created for purposes including astronomy, the longest was around 9 miles.[^o15]

## Criteria {#Criteria}

Straight line missions can be planned using software such as [Google Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Earth) and tracked using a [GPS device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS_device).[^o17] Mapping the line on software such as Google Earth ensures the line is a [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) and does not depend on the [map projection](wikipedia://en/51784).[^o18]

Straight line missions are scored according to a number of criteria based on the largest deviation from the planned route. With max deviations less than a certain amount achieving the following grades

- 25m - Platinum
- 50m - Gold
- 75m - Silver
- 100m - Bronze

A total score (Burdell score) can also be computed for the entire line.[^o19]

## Notable Missions {#Notable_Missions}

### Straight line across England {#Straight_line_across_England}

At the end of the 20th century, a north-south crossing of England in a straight line was achieved by geographer [Nicholas Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Crane)[^o22], though using a much less stringent boundary of 1km either side of the line.

*Image: England location map-straight-line.png.* Mouth of the River Esk near Gretna (left) to Bamburgh, Northumbria

The first east-west crossing of England in a straight line was accomplished in 2024 by [GeoWizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoWizard)[^o2][^o4] on a route going from near [Gretna, Dumfries and Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretna,_Dumfries_and_Galloway) to [Bamburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamburgh), Northumbria.

### Straight line across Scotland {#Straight_line_across_Scotland}

The first crossing of Scotland in a straight line was achieved in 2022 by Adam and Archie Fieldhouse.[^o6] The longest straight line mission at the time, 49 miles, was completed in the [Cairngorms National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairngorms_National_Park) from the Pass of Drumochter to Corgarff.[^o24].

### Straight line across Wales {#Straight_line_across_Wales}

The first crossing of Wales in a straight line was accomplished in 2023 by [GeoWizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoWizard) on the fourth attempt[^o3] a feat which received national media coverage.[^o26] The line was 42-miles long from Craignant, [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire), to Tal-y-bont, near Barmouth, in [Gwynedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd).

### Straight line across Latvia {#Straight_line_across_Latvia}

The first crossing of [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia) in a straight line was completed in October 2024 by an Estonian couple, Iti-Jantra Erikso and Toomas Erikso [^o28]. The mission received national media coverage in Latvia. [^o29] The route began at the Lithuania-Latvia border and ended at the [Gulf of Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Riga), totalling 75.4 kilometres.

### Straight line across Estonia {#Straight_line_across_Estonia}

The first crossing of [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia) in a straight line was completed in April 2025 also by Iti-Jantra Erikso and Toomas Erikso.[^o31] The line began at the Estonia-Latvia border and ended in [Vainupea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vainupea) beach, on the north coast of Estonia, totalling 176 kilometres. The mission received media coverage in The Baltic Guide tourism newspaper. [^o32]

### Longest possible straight line {#Longest_possible_straight_line}

The longest possible overland straight line mission goes from [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) to [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China) over a distance of 11,241 km.[^o34]

## Popularity {#Popularity}

Due to the popularisation of the idea by GeoWizard a number of others have attempted straight line missions across different countries such as Scotland[^o36] and New Zealand.[^o37] Other attempts focus on lines of the longest possible distance,[^o38] use different modes of travel[^o39] or are simply recreational.[^o40]
