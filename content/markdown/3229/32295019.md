# Lafforgue's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lafforgue's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Lafforgue's theorem**, due to [Laurent Lafforgue](wikipedia://en/104485), completes the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) for [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564) over [algebraic function fields](wikipedia://en/447645), by giving a correspondence between [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) on these groups and representations of [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673).

:::

The Langlands conjectures were introduced by (txt) and describe a correspondence between representations of the [Weil group](wikipedia://en/6375618) of an [algebraic function field](wikipedia://en/447645) and representations of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924) over the function field, generalizing [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) of function fields from abelian Galois groups to non-abelian Galois groups.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Langlands conjectures for GL1"}

## Langlands conjectures for GL<sub>1</sub> {#Langlands_conjectures_for_GL1}

The Langlands conjectures for GL<sub>1</sub>(*K*) follow from (and are essentially equivalent to) [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695). More precisely the [Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_map) gives a map from the idele class group to the abelianization of the Weil group.

:::

## Automorphic representations of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) {#Automorphic_representations_of_GLn(F)}

The representations of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) appearing in the Langlands correspondence are automorphic representations.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Lafforgue's theorem for GLn(F)"}

## Lafforgue's theorem for GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) {#Lafforgue's_theorem_for_GLn(F)}

Here *F* is a global field of some positive characteristic *p*, and ℓ is some prime not equal to *p*.

Lafforgue's theorem states that there is a bijection σ between:

- Equivalence classes of cuspidal representations π of GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*), and
- Equivalence classes of irreducible ℓ-adic representations σ(π) of dimension *n* of the absolute Galois group of *F*

that preserves the *L*-function at every place of *F*.

The proof of Lafforgue's theorem involves constructing a representation σ(π) of the absolute Galois group for each cuspidal representation π. The idea of doing this is to look in the [ℓ-adic cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ℓ-adic_cohomology) of the moduli stack of [shtukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shtuka) of rank *n* that have compatible [level *N* structures](wikipedia://en/47016783) for all *N*. The cohomology contains subquotients of the form

π⊗σ(π)⊗σ(π)<sup>∨</sup>

which can be used to construct σ(π) from π. A major problem is that the moduli stack is not of finite type, which means that there are formidable technical difficulties in studying its cohomology.

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Lafforgue's theorem implies the [Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture](wikipedia://en/782173) that if an automorphic form for GL<sub>*n*</sub>(*F*) has central character of finite order, then the corresponding Hecke eigenvalues at every unramified place have absolute value 1.

Lafforgue's theorem implies the conjecture of (Deligne 1980, 1.2.10) that an irreducible finite-dimensional *l*-adic representation of the absolute Galois group with determinant character of finite order is pure of weight 0.

:::
