# Theil–Sen estimator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theil–Sen estimator"}

Robust regression

*Image: Thiel-Sen estimator.svg.* The Theil–Sen estimator of a set of sample points with outliers (black line) compared to the non-robust [ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) line for the same set (blue). The dashed green line represents the [ground truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_truth) from which the samples were generated.

In [non-parametric statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics), the **Theil–Sen estimator** is a method for [robustly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robust_estimator) [fitting a line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitting_a_line) to sample points in the plane (a form of [simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/2593771)) by choosing the [median](wikipedia://en/18837) of the [slopes](wikipedia://en/29368) of all lines through pairs of points. It has also been called **Sen's slope estimator**,[^o2][^o8] **slope selection**,[^o32][^o33] the **single median method**,[^o3] the **Kendall robust line-fit method**,[^o4] and the **Kendall–Theil robust line**.[^o5] It is named after [Henri Theil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Theil) and [Pranab K. Sen](wikipedia://en/32302045), who published papers on this method in 1950 and 1968 respectively,[^o6] and after [Maurice Kendall](wikipedia://en/589136) because of its relation to the [Kendall tau rank correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_tau_rank_correlation_coefficient).[^o13]

:::

Theil–Sen regression has several advantages over [Ordinary least squares](wikipedia://en/1651906) regression. It is insensitive to [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951). It can be used for significance tests even when residuals are not normally distributed.[^n1] It can be significantly more accurate than [non-robust simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/1651906) (least squares) for [skewed](wikipedia://en/28212) and [heteroskedastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroskedastic) data, and competes well against least squares even for [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) data in terms of [statistical power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power).[^o15] It has been called "the most popular nonparametric technique for estimating a linear trend".[^o8] There are fast algorithms for efficiently computing the parameters.

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

As defined by (Theil 1950), the Theil–Sen estimator of a set of two-dimensional points $(x_{i}, y_{i})$ is the median $m$ of the slopes $(y_{j} - y_{i})/(x_{j} - x_{i})$ determined by all pairs of sample points. (Sen 1968) extended this definition to handle the case in which two data points have the same $x$ coordinate. In Sen's definition, one takes the median of the slopes defined only from pairs of points having distinct $x$ coordinates.[^o6]

Once the slope $m$ has been determined, one may determine a line from the sample points by setting the [$y$-intercept](wikipedia://en/464331) $b$ to be the median of the values $y_{i} - mx_{i}$. The fit line is then the line $y = mx + b$ with coefficients $m$ and $b$ in [slope–intercept form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope–intercept_form).[^o26] As Sen observed, this choice of slope makes the [Kendall tau rank correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_tau_rank_correlation_coefficient) become approximately zero, when it is used to compare the values $x_{i}$ with their associated [residuals](wikipedia://en/461509) $y_{i} - mx_{i} - b$. Intuitively, this suggests that how far the fit line passes above or below a data point is not correlated with whether that point is on the left or right side of the data set. The choice of $b$ does not affect the Kendall coefficient, but causes the median residual to become approximately zero; that is, the fit line passes above and below equal numbers of points.[^o13]

A [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911) for the slope estimate may be determined as the interval containing the middle 95% of the slopes of lines determined by pairs of points[^o14] and may be estimated quickly by sampling pairs of points and determining the 95% interval of the sampled slopes. According to simulations, approximately 600 sample pairs are sufficient to determine an accurate confidence interval.[^o15]

:::

## Variations {#Variations}

A variation of the Theil–Sen estimator, the [repeated median regression](wikipedia://en/56638029) of (Siegel 1982), determines for each sample point $(x_{i}, y_{i})$, the median $m_{i}$ of the slopes $(y_{j} - y_{i})/(x_{j} - x_{i})$ of lines through that point, and then determines the overall estimator as the median of these medians. It can tolerate a greater number of outliers than the Theil–Sen estimator, but known algorithms for computing it efficiently are more complicated and less practical.[^o19]

A different variant pairs up sample points by the rank of their $x$-coordinates: the point with the smallest coordinate is paired with the first point above the median coordinate, the second-smallest point is paired with the next point above the median, and so on. It then computes the median of the slopes of the lines determined by these pairs of points, gaining speed by examining significantly fewer pairs than the Theil–Sen estimator.[^o20]

Variations of the Theil–Sen estimator based on [weighted medians](wikipedia://en/38492648) have also been studied, based on the principle that pairs of samples whose $x$-coordinates differ more greatly are more likely to have an accurate slope and therefore should receive a higher weight.[^o21]

For seasonal data, it may be appropriate to smooth out seasonal variations in the data by considering only pairs of sample points that both belong to the same month or the same season of the year, and finding the median of the slopes of the lines determined by this more restrictive set of pairs.[^o22]

:::

## Statistical properties {#Statistical_properties}

The Theil–Sen estimator is an [unbiased estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbiased_estimator) of the true slope in [simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/2593771).[^o24] For many distributions of the [response error](wikipedia://en/461509), this estimator has high [asymptotic efficiency](wikipedia://en/27250866) relative to [least-squares](wikipedia://en/82359) estimation.[^o25] Estimators with low efficiency require more independent observations to attain the same sample variance of efficient unbiased estimators.

The Theil–Sen estimator is more [robust](wikipedia://en/2885691) than the least-squares estimator because it is much less sensitive to [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951). It has a [breakdown point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakdown_point) of

$$1-\frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\approx 29.3\%,$$

meaning that it can tolerate arbitrary corruption of up to 29.3% of the input data-points without degradation of its accuracy.[^o26] However, the breakdown point decreases for higher-dimensional generalizations of the method.[^o28] A higher breakdown point, 50%, holds for a different robust line-fitting algorithm, the [repeated median estimator](wikipedia://en/56638029) of Siegel.[^o26]

The Theil–Sen estimator is [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) under every [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of its response variable, meaning that transforming the data first and then fitting a line, or fitting a line first and then transforming it in the same way, both produce the same result.[^o27] However, it is not equivariant under [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) of both the predictor and response variables.[^o28]

::: {#o30 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

The median slope of a set of $n$ sample points may be computed exactly by computing all $O(n^{2})$ lines through pairs of points, and then applying a linear time [median finding algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_algorithm). Alternatively, it may be estimated by sampling pairs of points. This problem is equivalent, under [projective duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_duality), to the problem of finding the crossing point in an [arrangement of lines](wikipedia://en/864149) that has the median $x$-coordinate among all such crossing points.[^n2]

The problem of performing slope selection exactly but more efficiently than the brute force quadratic time algorithm has been extensively studied in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927). Several different methods are known for computing the Theil–Sen estimator exactly in $O(n \log n)$ time, either deterministically[^o32] or using [randomized algorithms](wikipedia://en/495383).[^o33] Siegel's repeated median estimator can also be constructed in the same time bound.[^n3] In models of computation in which the input coordinates are integers and in which [bitwise operations](wikipedia://en/264399) on integers take constant time, the Theil–Sen estimator can be constructed even more quickly, in randomized expected time $O(n\sqrt{\log n})$.[^n4]

An estimator for the slope with approximately median rank, having the same breakdown point as the Theil–Sen estimator, may be maintained in the [data stream model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_algorithm) (in which the sample points are processed one by one by an algorithm that does not have enough persistent storage to represent the entire data set) using an algorithm based on [ε-nets](wikipedia://en/28807941).[^n5]

### Implementations {#Implementations}

In the [R](wikipedia://en/376707) statistics package, both the Theil–Sen estimator and Siegel's repeated median estimator are available through the `mblm` library.[^o39] A free standalone [Visual Basic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_Basic) application for Theil–Sen estimation, `KTRLine`, has been made available by the [US Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Geological_Survey).[^o40] The Theil–Sen estimator has also been implemented in [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) as part of the [SciPy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SciPy) and [scikit-learn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scikit-learn) libraries.[^o41]

## Applications {#Applications}

Theil–Sen estimation has been applied to [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) due to its ability to handle [censored regression models](wikipedia://en/4907628).[^n6] In [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics), (Fernandes Leblanc 2005) suggest its use for [remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) applications such as the estimation of leaf area from reflectance data due to its "simplicity in computation, analytical estimates of confidence intervals, robustness to outliers, testable assumptions regarding residuals and ... limited a priori information regarding measurement errors".[^n7] For measuring seasonal environmental data such as [water quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_quality), a seasonally adjusted variant of the Theil–Sen estimator has been proposed as preferable to least squares estimation due to its high precision in the presence of skewed data.[^o22] In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), the Theil–Sen method has been used to estimate trends in [software aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_aging).[^n8] In [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) and [climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatology), it has been used to estimate the long-term trends of wind occurrence and speed.[^n9]
