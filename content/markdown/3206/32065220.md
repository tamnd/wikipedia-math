# Springer resolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Springer resolution"}

In mathematics, the **Springer resolution** is a [resolution](wikipedia://en/9914115) of the variety of [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235) elements in a [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944),[^o2][^o3] or the [unipotent](wikipedia://en/1724478) elements of a reductive algebraic group, introduced by [Tonny Albert Springer](wikipedia://en/10836474) in 1969.[^o4] The fibers of this resolution are called **Springer fibers**.[^o5]

:::

If *U* is the variety of unipotent elements in a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G*, and *X* the variety of [Borel subgroups](wikipedia://en/588356) *B*, then the Springer resolution of *U* is the variety of pairs (*u*,*B*) of *U*×*X* such that *u* is in the Borel subgroup *B*. The map to *U* is the projection to the first factor. The Springer resolution for Lie algebras is similar, except that *U* is replaced by the nilpotent elements of the Lie algebra of *G* and *X* replaced by the variety of Borel subalgebras.[^o6]

The **Grothendieck–Springer resolution** is defined similarly, except that *U* is replaced by the whole group *G* (or the whole Lie algebra of *G*). When restricted to the unipotent elements of *G* it becomes the Springer resolution.[^o7][^o8]

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

When *G=SL(2)*, the Lie algebra Springer resolution is *T<sup>\*</sup>**P**<sup>1</sup> → n*, where *n* are the nilpotent elements of *sl(2)*. In this example, *n* are the matrices *x* with *tr(x<sup>2</sup>)=0*, which is a two dimensional conical subvariety of *sl(2)*. *n* has a unique singular point *0*, the fibre above which in the Springer resolution is the zero section ***P****<sup>1</sup>*.

:::
