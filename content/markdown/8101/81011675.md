# Straightedge-only construction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Straightedge-only construction"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **Steiner construction** – also known as **straightedge-only construction** – is the construction of lengths, angles, and other geometric figures using only an [idealized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealization_%28science_philosophy%29) [ruler](wikipedia://en/65593). It is similar to [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge) constructions except the [compass](wikipedia://en/492445) is not available for the construction.

:::

The idealized ruler, known as a [straightedge](wikipedia://en/147854), is assumed to be infinite in length, have only one edge, and no markings on it. It can be used to draw a [line](wikipedia://en/946975) between any two marked points on a plane and construct the intersection points between any two lines.

The straightedge is strictly weaker than a straightedge and compass together; a straightedge can only produce [projective invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_invariant) of the initial configuration. For example, one cannot mark the midpoint of a segment using a straightedge alone, since a [projective transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) can send the midpoint elsewhere. This is in contrast to the [Mohr–Mascheroni theorem](wikipedia://en/1196909), which states that every straightedge and compass construction can be made with just a compass alone.

A set of points or lines is said to be **Steiner-constructible** if it can be constructed using only a straightedge. Compare this with the term *[constructible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructible_point)*, which includes constructions involving a compass.

It turns out, if a given single circle and its center is initially given, every straightedge and compass construction becomes possible using a straightedge alone, making it just as powerful as a compass. This result is known as the [Poncelet–Steiner theorem](wikipedia://en/652660).

## History {#History}

The term **Steiner construction** is named after [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445), who first proved the [Poncelet–Steiner theorem](wikipedia://en/652660) in 1833, an important theorem about straightedge-only constructions. Since then, the term is used to refer to geometric constructions which only involve the straightedge.[^o2]

## Constructions {#Constructions}

In the book *The Ruler In Geometrical Constructions* (1961), A. S. Smogorzhevskii provides multiple constructions that are possible using only a straightedge. The book also contains elementary proofs for these constructions.[^o6]

### Harmonic conjugate {#Harmonic_conjugate}

Given three points $A$, $B$, $C$ on a straight line, the [harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/16094600) $D$ of point $C$ with respect to $A$ and $B$ can be constructed. This is done by taking advantage of [Ceva's theorem](wikipedia://en/152428) and [Menelaus's theorem](wikipedia://en/1480484).

As a corollary, if there are two points $A$ and $B$ with their [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) $M$ given, one can construct a line [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) to line $AB$ passing through any given points. The converse is also true.

The [dual](wikipedia://en/539925) version is as follows: Given three lines $a$, $b$, $c$ all passing through a common point, one can construct the line $d$ which, together with line $c$, divide lines $a$ and $b$ harmonically.

### Pole and polar {#Pole_and_polar}

Given a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673) $q$ and a point $P$ not on the conic section, it is possible to construct the [polar line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_line) $\pi$ of point $P$. Conversely, if a straight line $\pi$ is given, it is possible to construct its [pole](wikipedia://en/19451201) $P$ on the conic section.

### Tangent to a conic {#Tangent_to_a_conic}

Given a conic section $q$ and a point $P$, it is possible to construct tangents to $q$ through point $P$. This construction is possible regardless of whether $P$ is on the conic section or not.

Smogorzhevskii (2021) provides an elementary construction of this result in their book, but it is also possible give a proof for the circle case using analytical geometry and trigonometry.[^o10]

### Determining whether six points lie on a conic {#Determining_whether_six_points_lie_on_a_conic}

Given 6 points $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $E$, $F$, it can be determined using only a straightedge whether they all lie on the same conic section.

The dual version is as follows: Given any 6 lines $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$, $f$, it can be determined whether they inscribe a single conic section.

[Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068) proved the first one in 1639 by making use of [Pascal's theorem](wikipedia://en/699966), which reduces testing whether six points lie on a conic to checking whether three points are collinear. The dual version may be proved using [Brianchon's theorem](wikipedia://en/1554065).[^o12]

In 2021, Traves and Wehlau found a straightedge-only method to check whether 10 points lie on the same [cubic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_curve).[^o12]

### Constructions with two given parallel lines {#Constructions_with_two_given_parallel_lines}

If two parallel lines $l$ and $m$ are initially given, the following are possible:

- [Bisect](wikipedia://en/152547) a given [segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) $AB$ on line $l$.
- Draw a line passing through a given point $P$ parallel to the given lines $l$ and $m$.
- Given segment $AB$ and point $C$ on line $l$, construct segment $CD$ on line $l$ such that $CD = n \cdot AB$ ($n$ is a whole number).
- Given segment $AB$ on line $l$, divide $AB$ into $n$ equal parts ($n$ is a whole number).

### Constructions with a given parallelogram or square {#Constructions_with_a_given_parallelogram_or_square}

If any [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) is initially given, the following is possible:

- Draw a line passing through a given point $P$ and parallel to any given line $l$.

If any [square](wikipedia://en/659939) is initially given, the following are possible:

- Draw a line passing through a given point $P$ and [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to any given line $l$. This construction works regardless of whether $P$ is on line $l$ or not.
- [Bisect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) any given [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956).

## Poncelet–Steiner theorem {#Poncelet–Steiner_theorem}

*Image: Steiner construction of a parallel to a diameter.gif.* Construction of a parallel (*h*) to a diameter *g* through any given point *P*, using only a straightedge and a given fixed circle

The **Poncelet–Steiner theorem** states that only a single [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) and its [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_%28geometry%29) is needed for a straightedge to be able to perform any [straightedge-and-compass construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straightedge-and-compass_construction). The theorem was first suggested by [Jean Victor Poncelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Victor_Poncelet) in 1822 and proven by Jakob Steiner in 1833.

This shows that, while a compass can make constructions easier, it is no longer needed once the first circle has been drawn. All constructions thereafter can be performed using only the straightedge, although the [arcs](wikipedia://en/5578920) of circles themselves cannot be drawn without the compass.

Other variations of the theorem also give the same result, such as when two intersecting circles (without their centers) are given, or two concentric circles are given.

:::

## Limitations {#Limitations}

The straightedge is strictly weaker than a straightedge and compass together. This is because a straightedge can only produce [projective invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_invariant) of the initial configuration. Because lengths are not preserved under [projective transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation), any geometric construction involving lengths is not possible using a straightedge alone.

For example, without any additional information, it is impossible to construct midpoints, because there is a projective transformation that sends the midpoint elsewhere. Similarly, parallel lines are impossible to construct with only a straightedge.

Another example is, given a circle on the plane, it is impossible to construct its center using a straightedge alone. This was first proven by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) by constructing a projective transformation of the plane to itself such that the given circle is fixed, but the center of the circle is not preserved.[^o18] This also demonstrates the importance of the circle center in the Poncelet–Steiner theorem.

This shows that a straightedge can only achieve results that projective geometry allows, which is far fewer than the full set of straightedge-and-compass constructions described in *[Euclid's Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)*.
