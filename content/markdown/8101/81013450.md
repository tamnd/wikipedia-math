# Qutb al-Din Razi

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Qutb al-Din Razi"}

**Quṭb al-Dīn al-Rāzī al-Taḥtānī** (d. 766 AH / 1365 CE) was a 14th-century Islamic [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), jurist, and commentator in the post-[Avicennan](wikipedia://en/1130) philosophical tradition.[^o4] He played a significant role in refining logical and philosophical discourse, particularly in relation to issues such as universals, and was associated with the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) legal school.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Philosophical and logical contribution {#Philosophical_and_logical_contribution}

His philosophical corpus includes major works in logic such as *Taḥrīr al-qawāʿid al-manṭiqiyya and Lawāmiʿ al-asrār*, as well as philosophical commentaries like *al-Muḥākamāt bayna sharḥay al-Ishārāt*.[^o9]

He contributed significantly to the study of universals, particularly through critical engagement with [Avicenna](wikipedia://en/1130)’s theory of the natural universal (*al-kullī al-ṭabīʿī*) the view that universal quiddity exists within particulars as a part of their essence.[^o9][^o10]

His treatises such as *Risāla fī taḥqīq al-kulliyāt* and *Risālat al-maʿmūla fī al-taṣawwur wa-l-taṣdīq* shaped later debates in predication, definition, and [universal concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_concept).[^o9]
