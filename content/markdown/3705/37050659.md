# Heinrich Jung

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Heinrich Jung"}

**Heinrich Wilhelm Ewald Jung** (4 May 1876, [Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen) – 12 March 1953, [Halle (Saale)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_%28Saale%29)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who specialized in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Heinrich Jung was born as the son of a *Bergrat* (a mining officer of high rank) in Essen and studied from 1895 to 1899 mathematics, physics, and chemistry in [Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg)/Lahn and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) under outstanding professors including [Friedrich Schottky](wikipedia://en/5872505), [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565), [Lazarus Immanuel Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Immanuel_Fuchs), [Hermann Amandus Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Amandus_Schwarz), [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048), and [Max Planck](wikipedia://en/19848). In his 1899 doctoral dissertation *Über die kleinste Kugel, die eine räumliche Figur einschließt* (About the smallest sphere enclosing a spatial figure) under Schottky he proved the eponymous [Jung's Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung's_Theorem). In 1902 he completed his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) thesis in Marburg and remained there until 1908 as a [privatdocent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdocent). Afterwards he was a [Studienrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studienrat) (teacher at a secondary school, *i.e.*, [*Gymnasium*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29)) in Hamburg, before he became in 1913 a professor ordinarius in Kiel. After brief military service in World War I he became in 1918 a professor in Dorpat and in 1920 the successor to [Albert Wangerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Wangerin) (1844–1933) at the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), where he remained until his retirement as professor emeritus in 1948. In Halle he was not only a professor but also one of the directors of mathematical seminars and dean of the mathematical and sciences faculty and until 1951 he continued to give lectures. He was a member of the [Deutsche Akademie der Naturforscher Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Akademie_der_Naturforscher_Leopoldina).

Jung developed with his teacher Schottky a general theory of [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745). Jung's fame derives mainly from his arithmetic theory of algebraic functions in two variables. His original research in this theory is gathered together in his book *Einführung in die Theorie der algebraischen Funktionen zweier Veränderlicher*. He also applied his theory to [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311) (with a presentation of this research in his book *Algebraische Flächen*) and worked on birational transformations in the plane ([Cremona transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona_transformation)).

During the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), Jung was a member of the anti-republican [Alldeutschen Verband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alldeutscher_Verband) and also *[Der Stahlhelm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Stahlhelm,_Bund_der_Frontsoldaten)*. In the Nazi era, Jung was a member of the [Nationalsozialistischen Volkswohlfahrt (NSV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalsozialistische_Volkswohlfahrt), the [Nationalsozialistischer Deutscher Dozentenbund (NSDDB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_German_Lecturers_League), and the Nationalsozialistischer Altherrenbund. In 1945 he represented the [CDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_%28Germany%29).[^o3]
