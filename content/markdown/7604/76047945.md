# David W. Lewis (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David W. Lewis"}

**David W. Lewis** (21 February 1944 in [Douglas, Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas,_Isle_of_Man)—20 August 2021 in Dublin)[^o3] was a [Manx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man) mathematician known for his contributions to [quadratic forms theory](wikipedia://en/251478). He spent his entire career at [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) (UCD), where he was head of the Department of Mathematics (now the School of Mathematics and Statistics) from 1999 until 2002. After his retirement in 2009 he remained research active for many years.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Lewis attended Douglas High School where he developed an interest in physics and astronomy, and ultimately mathematics. He attended the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool), and after completing his BSc degree in 1965 commenced doctoral studies in topology under the guidance of [CTC (Terry) Wall](wikipedia://en/2613478). When his PhD funding ended in 1968, he started as assistant lecturer at the UCD Mathematics Department, while continuing the work on his doctoral thesis, shifting from topology to algebra and specifically to the area of quadratic and [hermitian forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form).

During his first decade of lecturing at UCD, he completed his PhD thesis, *Hermitian Forms over Algebras with Involution*, under the supervision of Professor Wall and was awarded a doctorate by the [National University of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Ireland) in 1979. He received a DSc from NUI in 1992, and served as head of the Department of Mathematics there from 1999 until 2002.[^o4][^o3][^o5]

He supervised 4 PhDs[^o7] and authored one monograph.
