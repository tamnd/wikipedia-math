# Julian Sahasrabudhe

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Julian Sahasrabudhe"}

**Julian Sahasrabudhe** (born May 8, 1988) is a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) of mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), in their [Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Pure_Mathematics_and_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o3] His research interests are in [extremal](wikipedia://en/2675917) and [probabilistic combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_combinatorics), [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), random polynomials and matrices, and [combinatorial number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_number_theory).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Sahasrabudhe grew up on [Bowen Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowen_Island), [British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia), Canada. He studied music at [Capilano College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capilano_University) and later moved to study at [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) where he completed his undergraduate degree in mathematics.[^o5] After graduating in 2012, Julian received his Ph.D. in 2017 under the supervision of [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956) at the [University of Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Memphis).[^o3]

Following his Ph.D., Sahasrabudhe was a Junior Research Fellow at [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge) from 2017 to 2021.[^o3][^o6] He currently holds a position as an assistant professor in the Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics (DPMMS) at the University of Cambridge.[^o3]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Sahasrabudhe's work covers many topics such as Littlewood problems on polynomials, probability and geometry of polynomials, arithmetic Ramsey theory, Erdős covering systems, random matrices and polynomials, etc.[^o3][^o6] In one of his more recent works in [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), he published a paper on *Exponential Patterns in Arithmetic Ramsey Theory* in 2018 by building on an observation made by Alessandro Sisto[^o8] in 2011.[^o9] He proved that for every finite colouring of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) there exists $a, b > 1$ such that the triple $a,b,a^b$ is monochromatic, demonstrating the partition regularity of complex exponential patterns. This work marks a crucial development in understanding the structure of numbers under partitioning.

In 2023, Sahasrabudhe submitted a paper titled *An exponential improvement for diagonal Ramsey* along with Marcelo Campos,[^o12] Simon Griffiths,[^o10] and [Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_%28mathematician%29). In this paper, they proved that the [Ramsey number](wikipedia://en/184898)

$R(k) \leq (4-\epsilon)^k$ for some constant $\epsilon > 0$

This is the first exponential improvement over the upper bound of [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Szekeres](wikipedia://en/2563194), proved in 1935.[^o11]

Sahasrabudhe has also worked with Marcelo Campos,[^o12] Matthew Jenssen,[^o13] and Marcus Michelen[^o14] on [random matrix theory](wikipedia://en/1648765) with the paper *The singularity probability of a random symmetric matrix is exponentially small*.[^o15] The paper addresses a long-standing conjecture concerning symmetric matrix with entries in $\{-1, 1\}$. They proved that the probability of such a matrix being [singular](wikipedia://en/217122) is exponentially small. The research quantifies this probability as $\mathbb{P}(\det(A)=0)\leq e^{-cn}$ where $A$ is drawn uniformly at random from the set of all $n \times n$ symmetric matrices and $c$ is an absolute constant.

In 2020, Sahasrabudhe published a paper named *Flat Littlewood Polynomial exists*,[^o16] which he co-authored with Paul Ballister,[^o17] [Bela Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956), [Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_%28mathematician%29), and Marius Tiba.[^o18] This work confirms the Littlewood conjecture by demonstrating the existence of Littlewood polynomials with coefficients of $\pm 1$ that are flat, meaning their magnitudes remain bounded within a specific range on the complex unit circle. This achievement not only validates a hypothesis made by Littlewood in 1966 but also contributes significantly to the field of mathematics, particularly in combinatorics and polynomial analysis.

In 2022, the authors worked on [Erdős covering systems](wikipedia://en/2297820) with the paper *On the Erdős Covering Problem: The Density of the Uncovered Set*.[^o19] They confirmed and provided a stronger proof of a conjecture proposed by Micheal Filaseta,[^o20] [Kevin Ford](wikipedia://en/55700462), [Sergei Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447), [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159), and Gang Yu,[^o21][^o19][^o22] which states that for distinct moduli within the interval $[n, Cn]$, the density of uncovered integers is bounded below by a constant. Furthermore, the authors establish a condition on the moduli that provides an optimal lower bound for the density of the uncovered set.[^o19]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In August 2021, Julian Sahasrabudhe was awarded the [European Prize in Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Prize_in_Combinatorics)[^o24] for his contribution to applying combinatorial methods to problems in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [combinatorial number theory](wikipedia://en/18243381), [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o3] In particular, Sahasrabudhe proved theorems on the Littlewood problems, on geometry of polynomials (Pemantle's conjecture), and on problems of Erdős, Schinzel, and Selfridge.

In October 2023, Julian Sahasrabudhe was awarded with the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize)[^o25] for his contribution to [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). More specifically, Sahasrabudhe improved the bound on the singularity probability of random symmetric matrices and obtained a new upper bound for diagonal [Ramsey numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_numbers).[^o3][^o24]

Sahasrabudhe is a 2024 recipient of the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913), given "for his outstanding contributions to Ramsey theory, his solutions to famous problems in complex analysis and random matrix theory, and his remarkable progress on sphere packings".[^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Sahasrabudhe's parents are television producers Deepak Sahasrabudhe and Susan Miller.[^o28][^o29] His grandfather, Madhu Sahasrabudhe, was the acting director of the Food Research Institute of [Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_and_Agri-Food_Canada).[^o30]
