# School of Chios

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="School of Chios"}

**The School of Chios** (Σχολή της Χίου) was a mathematical school in [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) that flourished from around 480 BC to 450 BC.

:::

## Main Representatives of the School {#Main_Representatives_of_the_School}

The School of Chios was a mathematical school established in the early 5th century, succeeding the [Pythagorean School](wikipedia://en/291170). Its most prominent representative was [Hippocrates of Chios](wikipedia://en/3632522) (~470-400 BC), who flourished around 440 BC. His teacher was [Oenopides of Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oenopides), who was a generation older. Also, [Anaxagoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaxagoras) (500-428 BC) was likely a member of the School. [^o3]

## The Mathematical Work of the School {#The_Mathematical_Work_of_the_School}

There is no information on whether the School of Chios was based in [Chios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chios) or if its representatives hailed from there. However, it was the brief period between the Pythagorean School and the [Platonic Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_Academy). The Academy declined, and its traces were lost shortly after the mid-5th century, during a flourishing geometric tradition, where the central focus was on the three famous "unsolvable" problems of antiquity: the [Doubling of the cube](wikipedia://en/91110) or Delian problem, the Trisection of the angle, and the [Squaring of the circle](wikipedia://en/201359). [^o5]

Anaxagoras is mentioned as the first to deal with the problem of squaring the circle. [^o6]

According to [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus), Oenopides was involved in mathematics and gained fame from it; he also introduced the use of the rule and compass for geometric constructions. [^o7]

Hippocrates is mentioned as the first to reduce the problem of doubling the cube to its equivalent, the interpolation of two mean proportionals between two straight line segments a and b=2a, so that the continuous proportions a/x=x/y=y/2a hold. Hippocrates also squared lunules, according to Simplicius (4th century AD), in a text that is preserved to us. [^o8]

The peak of the School of Chios did not last long, and the scientific center soon after the mid-5th century BC shifted to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens). The three above-mentioned main representatives of the School of Chios settled in Athens and continued their mathematical work.
