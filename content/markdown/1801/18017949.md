# Atiyah–Segal completion theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atiyah–Segal completion theorem"}

The **Atiyah–Segal completion theorem** is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) about [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). Let *G* be a [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and let *X* be a *G*-[CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex). The theorem then states that the projection map

:::

$$\pi\colon X\times EG\to X$$

induces an isomorphism of [prorings](wikipedia://en/24266)

$$\pi^*\colon K_G^*(X)_{\widehat{I\,}} \to K^*((X\times EG)/G).$$

Here, the induced map has as [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) the [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) of the *G*-equivariant K-theory of *X* with respect to *I*, where *I* denotes the [augmentation ideal](wikipedia://en/2224774) of the [representation ring](wikipedia://en/16673816) of *G*.

In the special case of *X* being a point, the theorem specializes to give an isomorphism $K^*(BG)\cong R(G)_{\widehat{I\,}}$ between the K-theory of the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of *G* and the completion of the representation ring.

The theorem can be interpreted as giving a comparison between the geometrical process of taking the homotopy quotient of a *G*-space, by making the [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) free before passing to the quotient, and the algebraic process of completing with respect to an ideal.[^o2]

The theorem was first proved for [finite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_groups) by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) in 1961,[^o3] and a proof of the general case was published by Atiyah together with [Graeme Segal](wikipedia://en/6713316) in 1969.[^o4] Different proofs have since appeared generalizing the theorem to completion with respect to families of subgroups.[^o5][^o6] The corresponding statement for algebraic K-theory was proven by [Alexander Merkurjev](wikipedia://en/31902178), holding in the case that the group is algebraic over the complex numbers.
