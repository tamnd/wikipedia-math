# Selma Fine Goldsmith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Selma Fine Goldsmith"}

**Selma Evelyn Fine Goldsmith** (1912–1962) was an American [economic statistician](wikipedia://en/340363) who accurately estimated the personal income distribution of Americans.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Selma Fine was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on January 17, 1912,[^o4] and attended [Morris High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_High_School_%28Bronx%29) in [The Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx). She graduated in 1932 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and completed her doctorate in 1936 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with a dissertation on 17th- and 18th-century British business cycles.[^o2]

Fine began working for the [United States Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture) and then for the National Resources Planning Board, where she began working on income tax data.[^o4] During this time she married Yale economist [Raymond W. Goldsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_W._Goldsmith). They had three children. Her major publications on income data were produced later, in the 1950s.[^o2]

She died of cancer on April 15, 1962.[^o2][^o5]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

Fine was married to Raymond W. Goldsmith, Professor of Economics at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Goldsmith won the Distinguished Service Award of the [Department of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Commerce) in 1955, and a Rockefeller public service award in 1956.[^o2] In 1962, she was elected as a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) for "numerous definitive studies clarifying the complex relationships among the statistics relating to the distribution of family income, family expenditures, and the national income accounts".[^o8]

## Published works {#Published_works}

- \1939. "The Use of Income Tax Data in the National Resources Committee Estimate of the Distribution of Income by Size," (with Enid Baird), *Studies in Income and Wealth 3*.
- \1950. "Statistical Information on the Distribution of Income by Size," *American Economic Review* 40.
- \1951. "Appraisal of Basic Data Available for Constructing Income Size Distributions," *Studies in Income and Wealth 13*.
- \1953. "Income Distribution in the United States by Size, 1944–50," (with George Jaszi), Supplement to the *Survey of Current Business*.
- \1954. "Size Distribution of Income Since the Mid-Thirties," (with George Jaszi, Hyman Kaitz, and Maurice Liebenberg), *Review of Economics and Statistics* 34.
- \1954. "Inequality of Income Distribution—Discussion," *American Economic Review* 44.
- \1956. "Reply to Dr. Clyman," *Review of Economics and Statistics* 38.
- \1957. "Changes in the Size Distribution of Income," *American Economic Review* 47.
- \1958. "Size Distribution of Personal Income," *Survey of Current Business* 38.
- \1958. "The Relation of [Census](wikipedia://en/6889) Income Distribution Statistics to Other Income Data, An Appraisal of the 1950 Census Income Data," *Studies in Income and Wealth* 23.
- \1960. "Size Distribution of Income and Wealth in the United States," in Arndt, Helmut (ed.), *Die Konzentration in der Wirtschaft*.
- \1962. "Low-Income Families and Measures of Income Inequality," *Review of Social Economy* 20.[^o4]
