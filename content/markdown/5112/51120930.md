# Andrea Razmadze

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Andrea Razmadze"}

**Andrea Mikhailovich Razmadze** (sometimes spelled Andria/Andrei Razmadze, 12 August 1889 – 2 October 1929[^o2]) was a Georgian mathematician, and one of the founders of [Tbilisi State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tbilisi_State_University), whose Mathematics Institute was renamed in his honor in 1944.[^o4] The department's scientific journal, published continuously since 1937, was also renamed as the [Proceedings of A. Razmadze Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_A._Razmadze_Mathematical_Institute) in his honor.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Andrea Razmadze was the son of Mikhail Gavrilovich Razmadze, a railway worker, and Nino Georgievna Nodia.[^o6] He graduated from [Kutaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutaisi) nonclassical secondary school in 1906 (where Public School #41 has been renamed for him[^o7]), then studied at [Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), earning a Diploma in 1910, and then a Masters in 1917 while teaching at local classical and secondary schools.[^o8] At the invitation of the university, he briefly stayed in Moscow University to teach mathematics in 1917,[^o9] but soon left to become one of the founders of Tbilisi University.[^o10] Though he died just 11 years later, during his time there he greatly expanded Georgian mathematical terminology by publishing three textbooks in that language,[^o6] and insisting that all courses be taught in Georgian, an approach that attracted renowned mathematician [Nikoloz Muskhelishvili](wikipedia://en/977804) to the school.[^o11] He also founded the [Georgian Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/77639699) on 21 February 1923 and was its first president; this institution lapsed on his death, but was reorganized from 1962 to the present.[^o12] He is most famous for his work in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), where he discovered an efficient method for finding the extrema of integral functions, and a comprehensive theory for finding the extrema of discontinuous ("angular") functions that can be represented by a finite number of curves.[^o6] He presented this last result at the 1924 International Congress of Mathematicians in Toronto,[^o13] for which he was awarded a Sc.D. by the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris).[^o8] He also delivered lectures in [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675)'s famous seminar series in Paris, along with such notables as [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462), [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485), and [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) laureates [Louis de Broglie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Broglie) and [Max Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Born).[^o14]
