# Landweber exact functor theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landweber exact functor theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Landweber exact functor theorem**, named after [Peter Landweber](wikipedia://en/41079632), is a theorem in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). It is known that a [complex orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_orientation) of a [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) leads to a [formal group law](wikipedia://en/480335). The Landweber exact functor theorem (or LEFT for short) can be seen as a method to reverse this process: it constructs a homology theory out of a formal group law.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The coefficient ring of [complex cobordism](wikipedia://en/1033666) is $MU_*(*) = MU_* \cong \mathbb{Z}[x_1,x_2,\dots]$, where the degree of $x_i$ is $2i$. This is isomorphic to the graded [Lazard ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazard_ring) $\mathcal{}L_*$. This means that giving a formal group law F (of degree $-2$) over a graded ring $R_*$ is equivalent to giving a graded ring morphism $L_*\to R_*$. Multiplication by an integer $n>0$ is defined inductively as a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958), by

$[n+1]^F x = F(x, [n]^F x)$ and $[1]^F x = x.$

Let now F be a formal group law over a ring $\mathcal{}R_*$. Define for a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X*

$$E_*(X) = MU_*(X)\otimes_{MU_*}R_*$$

Here $R_*$ gets its $MU_*$-algebra structure via F. The question is: is E a homology theory? It is obviously a homotopy invariant functor, which fulfills excision. The problem is that tensoring in general does not preserve exact sequences. One could demand that $R_*$ be [flat](wikipedia://en/492616) over $MU_*$, but that would be too strong in practice. Peter Landweber found another criterion:

**Theorem** (Landweber exact functor theorem)

For every prime p, there are elements $v_1,v_2,\dots \in MU_*$ such that we have the following: Suppose that $M_*$ is a graded $MU_*$-module and the sequence $(p,v_1,v_2,\dots, v_n)$ is [regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_sequence_%28algebra%29) for $M$, for every *p* and *n*. Then

$$E_*(X) = MU_*(X)\otimes_{MU_*}M_*$$

is a homology theory on [CW-complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex).

In particular, every formal group law F over a ring $R$ yields a module over $\mathcal{}MU_*$ since we get via F a ring morphism $MU_*\to R$.

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

- There is also a version for [Brown–Peterson cohomology](wikipedia://en/13698492) BP. The [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29) BP is a direct summand of $MU_{(p)}$ with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[v_1,v_2,\dots]$. The statement of the LEFT stays true if one fixes a prime p and substitutes BP for MU.
- The classical proof of the LEFT uses the Landweber–Morava invariant ideal theorem: the only prime ideals of $BP_*$ which are invariant under coaction of $BP_*BP$ are the $I_n = (p,v_1,\dots, v_n)$. This allows to check flatness only against the $BP_*/I_n$ (see Landweber, 1976).
- The LEFT can be strengthened as follows: let $\mathcal{E}_*$ be the (homotopy) category of Landweber exact $MU_*$-modules and $\mathcal{E}$ the category of MU-module spectra M such that $\pi_*M$ is Landweber exact. Then the functor $\pi_*\colon\mathcal{E}\to \mathcal{E}_*$ is an equivalence of categories. The inverse functor (given by the LEFT) takes $\mathcal{}MU_*$-algebras to (homotopy) MU-algebra spectra (see Hovey, Strickland, 1999, Thm 2.7).

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The archetypical and first known (non-trivial) example is [complex K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) K. Complex K-theory is [complex oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_orientation) and has as formal group law $x+y+xy$. The corresponding morphism $MU_*\to K_*$ is also known as the [Todd genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_genus). We have then an isomorphism

$$K_*(X) = MU_*(X)\otimes_{MU_*}K_*,$$

called the *Conner–Floyd isomorphism*.

While complex K-theory was constructed before by geometric means, many homology theories were first constructed via the Landweber exact functor theorem. This includes [elliptic homology](wikipedia://en/10295395), the [Johnson–Wilson theories](wikipedia://en/31507401) $E(n)$ and the [Lubin&ndash;Tate spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubin&ndash;Tate_spectra) $E_n$.

While homology with rational coefficients $H\mathbb{Q}$ is Landweber exact, homology with integer coefficients $H\mathbb{Z}$ is not Landweber exact. Furthermore, [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664) K(n) is not Landweber exact.

:::

## Modern reformulation {#Modern_reformulation}

A module M over $\mathcal{}MU_*$ is the same as a [quasi-coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) $\mathcal{F}$ over $\text{Spec }L$, where L is the Lazard ring. If $M = \mathcal{}MU_*(X)$, then M has the extra datum of a $\mathcal{}MU_*MU$ coaction. A coaction on the ring level corresponds to that $\mathcal{F}$ is an equivariant sheaf with respect to an action of an affine group scheme G. It is a theorem of [Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) that $G \cong \mathbb{Z}[b_1, b_2,\dots]$ and assigns to every ring R the group of power series

$$g(t) = t+b_1t^2+b_2t^3+\cdots\in R[[t]].$$

It acts on the set of formal group laws $\text{Spec }L(R)$ via

$$F(x,y) \mapsto gF(g^{-1}x, g^{-1}y).$$

These are just the coordinate changes of formal group laws. Therefore, one can identify the [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) quotient $\text{Spec }L // G$ with the *stack of (1-dimensional) [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group)* $\mathcal{M}_{fg}$ and $M = MU_*(X)$ defines a quasi-coherent sheaf over this stack. Now it is quite easy to see that it suffices that M defines a quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ which is flat over $\mathcal{M}_{fg}$ in order that $MU_*(X)\otimes_{MU_*}M$ is a homology theory. The Landweber exactness theorem can then be interpreted as a flatness criterion for $\mathcal{M}_{fg}$ (see Lurie 2010).

## Refinements to $E_\infty$-ring spectra {#Refinements_to_E_\infty-ring_spectra}

While the LEFT is known to produce (homotopy) ring spectra out of $\mathcal{}MU_*$, it is a much more delicate question to understand when these spectra are actually [$E_\infty$-ring spectra](wikipedia://en/27342975). As of 2010, the best progress was made by [Jacob Lurie](wikipedia://en/26663863). If X is an [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377) and $X\to \mathcal{M}_{fg}$ a flat map of stacks, the discussion above shows that we get a presheaf of (homotopy) ring spectra on X. If this map factors over $M_p(n)$ (the stack of 1-dimensional [p-divisible groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-divisible_group) of height n) and the map $X\to M_p(n)$ is [etale](wikipedia://en/5707590), then this presheaf can be refined to a sheaf of $E_\infty$-ring spectra (see Goerss). This theorem is important for the construction of [topological modular forms](wikipedia://en/10274436) and topological automorphic forms.
