# J-homomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J-homomorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the ***J*-homomorphism** is a mapping from the [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of the [special orthogonal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) to the [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697). It was defined by (txt), extending a construction of (txt).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Whitehead's original [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) is defined geometrically, and gives a homomorphism

$$J \colon \pi_r (\mathrm{SO}(q)) \to \pi_{r+q}(S^q)$$

of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) for [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) *q*, and $r \ge 2$. (Hopf defined this for the special case $q = r+1$.)

The *J*-homomorphism can be defined as follows. An element of the special orthogonal group SO(*q*) can be regarded as a map

$$S^{q-1}\rightarrow S^{q-1}$$

and the homotopy group $\pi_r(\operatorname{SO}(q))$) consists of [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) classes of maps from the [*r*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) to SO(*q*). Thus an element of $\pi_r(\operatorname{SO}(q))$ can be represented by a map

$$S^r\times S^{q-1}\rightarrow S^{q-1}$$

Applying the [Hopf construction](wikipedia://en/43975548) to this gives a map

$$S^{r+q}= S^r*S^{q-1}\rightarrow S( S^{q-1}) =S^q$$

in $\pi_{r+q}(S^q)$, which Whitehead defined as the image of the element of $\pi_r(\operatorname{SO}(q))$ under the J-homomorphism.

Taking a limit as *q* tends to infinity gives the stable *J*-homomorphism in [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655):

$$J \colon \pi_r(\mathrm{SO}) \to \pi_r^S ,$$

where $\mathrm{SO}$ is the infinite special orthogonal group, and the right-hand side is the *r*-th [stable stem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_stem) of the [stable homotopy groups of spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_groups_of_spheres).

:::

## Image of the J-homomorphism {#Image_of_the_J-homomorphism}

The [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of the *J*-homomorphism was described by (txt), assuming the **Adams conjecture** of (Adams 1963) which was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by (txt), as follows. The [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $\pi_r(\operatorname{SO})$ is given by [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity). It is always [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327); and if *r* is positive, it is of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) 2 if *r* is 0 or 1 [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 8, infinite if *r* is 3 or 7 modulo 8, and order 1 otherwise (Switzer 1975, 488). In particular the image of the stable *J*-homomorphism is cyclic. The stable homotopy groups $\pi_r^S$ are the direct sum of the (cyclic) image of the *J*-homomorphism, and the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of the Adams e-invariant (Adams 1966), a homomorphism from the stable homotopy groups to $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$. If *r* is 0 or 1 mod 8 and positive, the order of the image is 2 (so in this case the *J*-homomorphism is [injective](wikipedia://en/45196)). If *r* is 3 or 7 mod 8, the image is a cyclic group of order equal to the denominator of $B_{2n}/4n$, where $B_{2n}$ is a [Bernoulli number](wikipedia://en/4964). In the remaining cases where *r* is 2, 4, 5, or 6 mod 8 the image is [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059) because $\pi_r(\operatorname{SO})$ is trivial.

## Applications {#Applications}

(txt) introduced the group *J*(*X*) of a space *X*, which for *X* a sphere is the image of the *J*-homomorphism in a suitable dimension.

The [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715) of the *J*-homomorphism $J \colon \pi_n(\mathrm{SO}) \to \pi_n^S$ appears in the group [Θ<sub>*n*</sub>](wikipedia://en/1114931) of [*h*-cobordism](wikipedia://en/681049) classes of oriented [homotopy *n*-spheres](wikipedia://en/1202074) ((Kosinski 1992)).

:::
