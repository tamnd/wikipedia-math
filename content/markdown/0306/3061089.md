# Afrikan Spir

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Afrikan Spir"}

**Afrikan Alexandrovich Spir**, also spelled **African Spir** (1837–1890), was a Russian-born [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) of [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans)-[Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) descent who wrote primarily in German, but also French.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

His book *Denken und Wirklichkeit* (*Thought and Reality*, 1873) had a significant influence on several eminent philosophers, scholars and writers such as [Hans Vaihinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Vaihinger), [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche), [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James), [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) and [Rudolf Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Steiner).

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and family {#Early_life_and_family}

Afrikan Spir was born on 10 November 1837 in his father's estates of Spirovska, near the city of Elizabethgrad, [Kherson Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson_Governorate), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (now [Kropyvnytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kropyvnytskyi), Ukraine).[^o3]

His father, Alexander Alexandrovich Spir, of German-Jewish descent, was a Russian Empire surgeon—Chief Physician of the military Hospital of [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa) specifically—and former professor of mathematics in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow). In 1812, he received the [Order of St. Vladimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Vladimir), was knighted, and became councillor and member of Kherson's Governorate hereditary nobility. His mother, Helena Constantinovna Spir, daughter of the major Poulevich, was on her mother's side the granddaughter of the Greek painter Logino, who arrived in Russian Empire under the reign of [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great).[^o10]

Alexander and Helena had four children—three boys and one girl— Aristarque, Kharitine, Alexandre and African.[^o11] The names were chosen, on the whim (*un caprice*) of their father, from an old [synaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaxis) of the [Orthodox calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_liturgical_calendar), which is the source of the curious name "Afrikan" (a very uncommon name of a minor orthodox saint, the father of [Šimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šimon)).[^o12] Spir disliked his Christian name, simply signing his letters and books "A. Spir". His modesty impelled him not to use either the German "von" or the French "de"—denoting his noble status—before his family name.

### Education {#Education}

He described his education as follows: "I spent my childhood in the countryside and later I studied for a while in Odessa, first in a Private boarding-school and after in a Gymnasium, more or less equivalent, if I do not mistake, to a French high-school".[^o14]

During this period he developed an interest in philosophy and read (in the French translation of Tissot) [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant)'s *[Critique of Pure Reason](wikipedia://en/347543)*, which gave him the basis of his speculative thought. He later followed the readings of [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes), [David Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hume), and [Stuart Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Mill).

Later he went to [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) where he attended the lectures of [Moritz Wilhelm Drobisch](wikipedia://en/31322407) (1802–1896), a [Herbartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbartian) philosopher and one of the forerunners of the [neo-Kantian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Kantianism) revival of the 1860s. He was there at the same time that [Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nietzsche) was a student, although it does not appear that they met.

### Military service {#Military_service}

After [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29), Spir entered the Midshipmen's School in Nikolayev (now [Mykolaiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv)), not far from the [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea).[^o14] In 1855, at the age of 18, he participated as Sub-lieutenant of the Russian navy in the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), during which he was twice decorated ([Order of St. Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Andrew) and [Order of St. George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_George_%28Russia%29)). Spir defended the same bastion (N. 4 at [Malakoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Malakoff)) as [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) during the [siege of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_%281854–55%29).

### Later life, marriage and Swiss citizenship {#Later_life,_marriage_and_Swiss_citizenship}

After his father's death in 1852, he inherited his father's estates (his last remaining brother, the poet Aristarch, having died in 1841) whereupon he emancipated his [serfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom_in_Russia) and gave them land, goods and money, presaging the [reform of 1861](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861).

In 1862, he left Elizabethgrad for a tour in Germany, where he spent two years "to know better the mind's matter".[^o14] His sister Charitis died soon after his return to Russia in 1864. After the death of his mother, in 1867, he sold his estates at a ridiculously low price, distributed almost all of his possessions and left Russian Empire permanently.[^o10]

In 1869, he moved to [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen) and to [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) in 1871. Here, at the orthodox Church of the Court, he married on 30 January 1872, Elisabeth (Elise) Gatternich.[^o17] The couple had a daughter, Hélène.

In 1884, Spir asked the Russian Emperor for an allowance to forsake Russian citizenship and to obtain [Swiss citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_citizenship). In the same year, he received the imperial authorization and applied for a certificate of registry at [Belmont-sur-Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont-sur-Lausanne), where he lived with his family. In 1886, to enjoy the facilities of a bigger library (the "Société de Lecture", a private reading society), he moved to [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva). On 17 September 1889, he received authorization for his wife, his daughter, and himself to become Swiss citizens from the Swiss Federal Government.[^o18]

### Death {#Death}

In 1878, having suffered from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia), in order to treat the consequences of his illness, a chronic cough, Spir moved to [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), where he spent five years.

He died of [influenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1889–1890_flu_pandemic) on 26 March 1890 in Geneva, at 6 rue Petitot. He was buried in the Saint-Georges cemetery. He was survived by his wife Elisabeth and his daughter, Hélène.[^o10]

The surname and lineage are reported to persist in modern times, with some pointing to [Mikhailo Fyodorovich Spir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykhailo_Shpir) (b. 1986), Deputy Minister in the Kherson region, as a possible descendant.

### Writings {#Writings}

In Leipzig, Spir befriended the publisher and fellow [Freemason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemason) [Joseph Gabriel Findel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gabriel_Findel), who published most of Spir's works. His most important book, *Denken und Wirklichkeit: Versuch einer Erneuerung der kritischen Philosophie* (*Thought and Reality: Attempt at a Renewal of Critical Philosophy*) was published in 1873. A second edition, which was the one owned by Nietzsche, was published in 1877. In an attempt to reach a broader readership, Spir wrote directly in French his *Esquisses de philosophie critique* (*Outlines of critical philosophy*), published for the first time in 1877. A new edition was published forty years after his death, in 1930, with an introduction by the French philosopher and professor at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) [Léon Brunschvicg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Brunschvicg).

Manuscripts, personal papers, photographs, books by or on African Spir were donated in March 1940 by his daughter Hélène Claparède-Spir (who was married to the Swiss neurologist [Édouard Claparède](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Claparède)) to the Library of Geneva ([Bibliothèque de Genève](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliothèque_de_Genève), formerly Bibliothèque Publique et Universitaire de Genève), where they compose the "Fonds African Spir" and can be consulted.[^o21][^o22]

To celebrate the centenary of his birth, a selection of Spir's writings, *Paroles d'un sage* ("Words of a [sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sage_%28philosophy%29)"), was collated and published by his daughter, Hélène Claparède-Spir in 1937.[^o23] Some of these thoughts were later reproduced in a collection of Spir's letters.[^o24]

Other papers concerning Spir, his daughter Hélène Claparède-Spir and her family can be consulted at [Harvard University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University_Library).

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

### Epistemology {#Epistemology}

Spir referred to his philosophy as "[critical philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_philosophy)". He sought to establish philosophy as the science of first principles, he held that the task of philosophy was to investigate immediate [knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge), show the delusion of [empiricism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism), and present the true nature of things by strict statements of facts and logically controlled inference. This method led Spir to proclaim the [principle of identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_identity) (or [law of identity](wikipedia://en/1403190), *A* ≡ *A*) as the fundamental law of knowledge, which is opposed to the changing appearance of empirical reality.[^o29]

### Ontology {#Ontology}

For Spir the [principle of identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_identity) is not only the fundamental law of knowledge, it is also an [ontological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontological) principle, expression of the unconditioned essence of reality (Realität = Identität mit sich), which is opposed to the empirical reality (Wirklichkeit), which in turn is evolution (Geschehen).[^o31] The principle of identity displays the essence of reality: only that which is identical to itself is real, the empirical world is ever-changing, therefore it is not real. Thus the empirical world has an illusory character, because phenomena are ever-changing, and empirical reality is unknowable.

### Religion and morality {#Religion_and_morality}

Religion, [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality) and philosophy, have for Spir the same theoretical foundation: the [principle of identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_identity), which is the characteristic of the supreme being, of the absolute, of God. God is not the [creator deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity) of the universe and mankind, but man's true nature and the norm of all things, in general. The moral and religious conscience live in the consciousness of the contrast between this norm (*Realität*) and empirical reality (*Wirklichkeit*). "There is a radical [dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualism_%28philosophy_of_mind%29) between the empirical nature of man and his moral nature"[^o33] and the awareness of this dualism is the sole true foundation of moral [judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgment).[^o34]

### Social justice {#Social_justice}

Socially, Spir was not favourable to inherited wealth's accumulation in private hands and demanded just distribution of material goods, but disapproved of [collectivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivism_and_individualism).[^o36] He set the example, redistributing his personal inherited land properties to his former serfs.[^o10]

## Critical reception {#Critical_reception}

Although he spent most of his life as a philosopher, Spir never held a university appointment and his writings remained relatively unknown and unrecognized throughout his life. Indeed, Spir complained of a wall or conspiracy of silence (*das Totschweigen*).[^o38] Yet, he had a significant influence on several eminent philosophers, scholars and writers such as [Hans Vaihinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Vaihinger), [Friedrich Nietzsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche), [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James), [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) and [Rudolf Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Steiner). Vaihinger read Spir's *Thought and Reality* as soon as it was published (1873) and recalls that it made a "great impression". Nietzsche described him as "a distinguished logician" (*eines ausgezeichneten logikers*) and "the logician I value" (*Der von mir geschätzte Logiker heißt: A. Spir*).[^o39][^o40] James makes several references to Spir in his *[Principles of Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principles_of_Psychology)*, for example when debating a certain Kantian issue he wrote, "On the whole, the best recent treatment of the question known to me is in one of A. Spir's works, his *Denken und Wirklichkeit*".[^o41]

*Image: Leo Tolstoy 1897, black and white, 37767u.jpg.* "*I am reading Spir all the time, and the reading provokes a mass of thoughts*". [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy), Journal, May 1896

In 1896 [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy) read *Thought and Reality* and was also deeply impressed, as he mentioned in a letter to Hélène Claparède-Spir: "reading *Thought and Reality* has been a great joy for me. I do not know a philosopher so profound and at the same time so precise, I mean scientific, accepting only what is strictly necessary and clear for everybody. I am sure that his doctrine will be understood and appreciated as it deserves and that the destiny of his work will be similar to that of Schopenhauer, who became known and admired only after his death".[^o42]

In his Journal (2 May 1896) Tolstoy wrote: "Still another important event the work \[*Thought and Reality*\] of African Spir. I just read through what I wrote in the beginning of this notebook. At bottom, it is nothing else than a short summary of all of Spir's philosophy which I not only had not read at that time, but about which I had not the slightest idea. This work clarified my ideas on the meaning of life remarkably, and in some ways strengthened them. The essence of his doctrine is that things do not exist, but only our impressions which appear to us in our conception as objects. Conception (Vorstellung) has the quality of believing in the existence of objects. This comes from the fact that the quality of thinking consists in attributing an objectivity to impressions, a substance, and a projecting of them into space". The next day (3 May 1896) he added, "I am reading Spir all the time, and the reading provokes a mass of thoughts".

The most important works on Spir's philosophy were published between 1900 and 1914 ([Theodor Lessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Lessing), Andreas Zacharoff, Joseph Segond, Gabriel Huan, [Piero Martinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Martinetti)).

In a lecture given in 1917, [Rudolf Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Steiner) called Spir "extraordinarily fascinating" and an "original thinker", and someone possessed of a "subtlety" not found in his 19th century contemporaries. As a consequence, Spir was unfortunately not understood and "suffered all the distress that a thinker can experience from being entirely ignored; killed by silence as the saying goes".[^o43]

After the First World War, the interpretation of Spir's thought by the Italian philosopher [Martinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Martinetti) gave it a second life for a short while, in the form of a "religious idealism".[^o44][^o45] In 1937, for the centennial of Spir's birth, Martinetti published in Italy a monographic edition on Spir of the *Rivista di Filosofia* (*Philosophical Review*).[^o46]

Before the Second World War, Hélène Claparède-Spir published some new editions of her father's books in French and had an extensive exchange of letters to promote her father's thought.[^o47]

After the Second World War, African Spir fell into oblivion. In 1990, for the centennial of Spir's death in Geneva, the Geneva Public Library organized an exhibition of African Spir's corpus[^o48] and published the analytical catalogue.[^o22] Many of Spir's books have not been entirely sold and are still available in their first or second edition (in German, French, Italian, English, or Spanish translations). Presumably, due to the increasing interest in the argument at the beginning of the twenty-first century, a reprint of the Italian translation by Odoardo Campa in 1911 of Spir's *Moralität und Religion* (1874) has been published in 2008.[^o49]
