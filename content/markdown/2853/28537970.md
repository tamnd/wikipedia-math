# Stratifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stratifold"}

In [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **stratifold** is a generalization of a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) where certain kinds of [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763) are allowed. More specifically a stratifold is stratified into differentiable manifolds of (possibly) different dimensions. Stratifolds can be used to construct new [homology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theories). For example, they provide a new geometric model for ordinary homology. The concept of stratifolds was invented by [Matthias Kreck](wikipedia://en/33582824). The basic idea is similar to that of a [topologically stratified space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_stratified_space), but adapted to differential topology.

:::

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Before we come to stratifolds, we define a preliminary notion, which captures the minimal notion for a smooth structure on a space: A *differential space* (in the sense of Sikorski) is a pair $(X, C),$ where *X* is a topological space and *C* is a subalgebra of the continuous functions $X \to \mathbb{R}$ such that a function is in *C* if it is locally in *C* and $g \circ \left(f_1, \ldots, f_n\right) : X \to \mathbb{R}$ is in C for $g : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ smooth and $f_i \in C.$ A simple example takes for *X* a smooth manifold and for *C* just the smooth functions.

For a general differential space $(X, C)$ and a point *x* in *X* we can define as in the case of manifolds a [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) $T_x X$ as the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of all [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) of function [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) at *x*. Define strata $X_i = \{x\in X : T_x X$ has dimension i$\}.$ For an *n*-dimensional manifold *M* we have that $M_n = M$ and all other strata are empty. We are now ready for the definition of a stratifold, where more than one stratum may be non-empty:

A *k*-dimensional *stratifold* is a differential space $(S, C),$ where *S* is a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) with [countable base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable_base) of topology. All skeleta should be closed. In addition we assume:

*Image: suspension.svg.* The suspension

1. The $\left(S_i, C|_{S_i}\right)$ are *i*-dimensional smooth manifolds.
1. For all *x* in *S*, restriction defines an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) of [stalks](wikipedia://en/15879621) $C_x \to C^{\infty}(S_i)_x.$
1. All tangent spaces have dimension ≤ *k*.
1. For each *x* in *S* and every neighbourhood *U* of *x*, there exists a function $\rho : U \to \mathbb{R}$ with $\rho(x) \neq 0$ and $\text{supp}(\rho) \subset U$ (a bump function).

A *n*-dimensional stratifold is called *oriented* if its (*n* − 1)-stratum is empty and its top stratum is oriented. One can also define stratifolds with boundary, the so-called *c-stratifolds*. One defines them as a pair $(T,\partial T)$ of topological spaces such that $T-\partial T$ is an *n*-dimensional stratifold and $\partial T$ is an (*n* − 1)-dimensional stratifold, together with an equivalence class of [collars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collar_%28topology%29).

An important subclass of stratifolds are the *regular* stratifolds, which can be roughly characterized as looking locally around a point in the *i*-stratum like the *i*-stratum times a (*n* − *i*)-dimensional stratifold. This is a condition which is fulfilled in most stratifold one usually encounters.

## Examples {#Examples}

There are plenty of examples of stratifolds. The first example to consider is the open [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) over a manifold *M*. We define a continuous function from *S* to the reals to be in *C* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is smooth on $M \times (0, 1)$ and it is locally constant around the cone point. The last condition is automatic by point 2 in the definition of a stratifold. We can substitute *M* by a stratifold *S* in this construction. The cone is oriented if and only if *S* is oriented and not zero-dimensional. If we consider the (closed) cone with bottom, we get a stratifold with boundary *S*.

Other examples for stratifolds are [one-point compactifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-point_compactification) and [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) of manifolds, (real) algebraic varieties with only isolated singularities and (finite) simplicial complexes.

:::

## Bordism theories {#Bordism_theories}

*Image: Pair of pants cobordism (pantslike).svg.* An example of a bordism relation

In this section, we will assume all stratifolds to be regular. We call two maps $S, S' \to X$ from two oriented compact *k*-dimensional stratifolds into a space *X* *[bordant](wikipedia://en/391832)* if there exists an oriented (*k* + 1)-dimensional compact stratifold *T* with boundary *S* + (−*S*') such that the map to *X* extends to *T*. The set of equivalence classes of such maps $S \to X$ is denoted by $SH_k X.$ The sets have actually the structure of abelian groups with disjoint union as addition. One can develop enough differential topology of stratifolds to show that these define a [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory). Clearly, $SH_k(\text{point}) = 0$ for $k > 0$ since every oriented stratifold *S* is the boundary of its cone, which is oriented if $\dim(S) > 0.$ One can show that $SH_0(\text{point})\cong\mathbb{Z}.$ Hence, by the [Eilenberg–Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg&ndash;Steenrod_axioms) uniqueness theorem, $SH_k(X) \cong H_k(X)$ for every space *X* homotopy-equivalent to a [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex), where *H* denotes [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041). For other spaces these two homology theories need not be isomorphic (an example is the one-point compactification of the surface of infinite genus).

There is also a simple way to define [equivariant homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_homology_theory) with the help of stratifolds. Let *G* be a compact [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945). We can then define a bordism theory of stratifolds mapping into a space *X* with a *G*-action just as above, only that we require all stratifolds to be equipped with an orientation-preserving free *G*-action and all maps to be G-equivariant. Denote by $SH_k^G(X)$ the bordism classes. One can prove $SH_k^G(X)\cong H_{k-\dim(G)}^G(X)$ for every X homotopy equivalent to a CW-complex.

## Connection to the theory of genera {#Connection_to_the_theory_of_genera}

A [genus](wikipedia://en/5574857) is a ring homomorphism from a bordism ring into another ring. For example, the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) defines a ring homomorphism $\Omega^O(\text{point}) \to \mathbb{Z}/2[t]$ from the [unoriented bordism ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordism#Unoriented_cobordism) and the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) defines a ring homomorphism $\Omega^{SO}(\text{point}) \to \mathbb{Z}[t]$ from the [oriented bordism ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordism#Oriented_cobordism). Here *t* has in the first case degree *1* and in the second case degree *4*, since only manifolds in dimensions divisible by *4* can have non-zero signature. The left hand sides of these homomorphisms are homology theories evaluated at a point. With the help of stratifolds it is possible to construct homology theories such that the right hand sides are these homology theories evaluated at a point, the Euler homology and the Hirzebruch homology respectively.

## Umkehr maps {#Umkehr_maps}

Suppose, one has a closed embedding $i : N\hookrightarrow M$ of manifolds with oriented normal bundle. Then one can define an [umkehr map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umkehr_map) $H_k(M) \to H_{k+\dim(N)-\dim(M)}(N).$ One possibility is to use stratifolds: represent a class $x \in H_k(M)$ by a stratifold $f : S \to M.$ Then make *ƒ* transversal to *N*. The intersection of *S* and *N* defines a new stratifold *S*' with a map to *N*, which represents a class in $H_{k+\dim(N)-\dim(M)}(N).$ It is possible to repeat this construction in the context of an embedding of [Hilbert manifolds](wikipedia://en/2504412) of finite codimension, which can be used in [string topology](wikipedia://en/28725806).
