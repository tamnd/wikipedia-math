# Jacobi's theorem (geometry)

*Image: GeneralisationOfNapoleonPoint.svg.* Adjacent colored angles are equal in measure. The point $N$ is the Jacobi point for triangle $\triangle ABC$ and these angles.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobi's theorem"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), a **Jacobi point** is a point in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) determined by a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) $\triangle ABC$ and a triple of angles $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$. This information is sufficient to determine three points $X, Y, Z$ such that

$$\begin{aligned} \angle ZAB &= \angle YAC &= \alpha, \\ \angle XBC &= \angle ZBA &= \beta, \\ \angle YCA &= \angle XCB &= \gamma. \end{aligned}$$

Then, by a theorem of Karl Friedrich Andreas Jacobi, the lines $AX, BY, CZ$ are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157),[^o2][^o3][^o4] at a point $N$ called the Jacobi point.[^o4]

:::

The Jacobi point is a generalization of the [Fermat point](wikipedia://en/2666205), which is obtained by letting $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 60^{\circ}$ and $\triangle ABC$ having no angle being greater or equal to 120°.

If the three angles above are equal, then $N$ lies on the [rectangular hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectangular_hyperbola) given in [areal coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areal_coordinates) by

$$yz(\cot B - \cot C) + zx(\cot C - \cot A) + xy(\cot A - \cot B) = 0,$$

which is [Kiepert's hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert's_hyperbola). Each choice of three equal angles determines a [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488).

The Jacobi point can be further generalized as follows: If points *K*, *L*, *M*, *N*, *O* and *P* are constructed on the sides of triangle *ABC* so that *BK/KC = CL/LB = CM/MA = AN/NC = AO/OB = BP/PA*, triangles *OPD*, *KLE* and *MNF* are constructed so that ∠*DOP* = ∠*FNM*, ∠*DPO* = ∠*EKL*, ∠*ELK* = ∠*FMN* and triangles *LMY*, *NOZ* and *PKX* are respectively similar to triangles *OPD*, *KLE* and *MNF*, then *DY*, *EZ* and *FX* are concurrent.[^o5]
