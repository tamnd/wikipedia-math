# Jeffrey Yi-Lin Forrest

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeffrey Yi-Lin Forrest"}

**Yi Lin** (; born 1959), also known as **Jeffrey Forrest** and **Jeffrey Yi-Lin Forrest**, is a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [systems science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_science), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), and [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) at [Pennsylvania State System of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_System_of_Higher_Education) ([Slippery Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slippery_Rock,_Pennsylvania) campus) (SSHE) and at several major universities in China. Lin has been an active researcher in the field of [systems science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_science) since mid-1980s and serves as the founder and president of the [International Institute for General Systems Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_General_Systems_Studies) (IIGSS).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Yi Lin was born in [Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuzhou), [Fujian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian), [People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_China), in 1959. He earned his BS degree in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) and an MS degree in general topology from [Northwest University, Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_University_%28China%29), respectively in 1982 and 1984. During 1985–1988, he pursued his PhD degree study under the supervision of Ben Fitzpatrick of [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University), [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama), and earned his PhD degree in 1988. In 1990–1991, he did one year post-doctoral study in statistics under the guidance of [Stephen Fienberg](wikipedia://en/2660488) at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh).

From 1988 to 1992, Lin was an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor), from 1992 to 2002, [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor), from 2002 on, a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in mathematics at SSHE (Slippery Rock campus). He was a visiting professor of the GMD Institute for Computer Architecture and Software Technology of the German National Research Center for Information Technology during the summer of 1997. Lin served as a guest professor at the Institute of Systems Engineering and Management Science, [Henan Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan_Agricultural_University), [Zhengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou), from 1997 to 2002; an adjunct professor at Hebei University of Economics and Trade since 1998, [Shijiazhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijiazhuang); visiting professor at [Huazhong University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huazhong_University_of_Science_and_Technology), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), from 2001 to 2004; honorary advisor of Chongqing Contemporary Research Institute of Systems Sciences, from 2004–2006; guest professor and a PhD program supervisor at College of Economics and Management, 2000–present, and executive president of the Institute for Grey Systems Studies, 2008–present, [Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics_University); specially appointed professor at Department of Mathematics and Systems Science, [National University of Defense Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Defense_Technology), [Changsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changsha), 2009–2011. Since 2009, Lin has served on the board of directors of the World Organization of Cybernetics and Systems.

Lin has lectured globally and organized various large scale projects involving scholars from over 80 [countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries) and geographic [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions) representing over 50 different [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific) disciplines. He was a founder of the [International Institute for General Systems Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_General_Systems_Studies) (IIGSS), and has served as its president since its inception in 1993. Since 2008, Lin has been editor of the book series, entitled "[Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems) Evaluation, [Prediction](wikipedia://en/246066) and [Decision-Making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-Making)," published by the [CRC Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRC_Press) of Taylor and Francis Group. In 1999, Lin was recognized with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) Award for the most outstanding paper in the 1998 volume of [Kybernetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybernetics_Society). In 2002, Lin's joint work was selected by Literati Club as one highly commended paper for the year of 2001. In 2002, Lin's joint paper was judged by the Award Committee of the World Organization of Cybernetics and Systems as a best paper at the 12th International Congress of the WOSC. In that same year, Lin was inducted into the Honorary Fellowship of the WOCS.[^o3]

Lin is married with three children. His family resides in [Grove City, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grove_City,_Pennsylvania).

## Work {#Work}

Lin's research interests cover a wide range of topics from [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954)[^o5] to [laws of conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_conservation), from the elementary structure of the universe to mysteries of the family, from the laws underlying [civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) to the natural endowments of man. Even so, most of his works appeared in the areas of [systems theory](wikipedia://en/29238) and applications, [mathematical](wikipedia://en/20590) and [systems modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_modeling), and [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358).

### Systems research {#Systems_research}

Since mid 1980s, Lin studied the general theory of multi-relational systems[^o7][^o8] under the influence of [George Jiří Klir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jiří_Klir), [Mihajlo D. Mesarovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihajlo_D._Mesarovic), and Yasuhiko Takahara, and applied the theory to a diverse array of scientific areas, including [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), [materials science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science), and others. His work in this area was treated as representative of mathematical systems theory.[^o9] Since 1995, in the name of the IIGSS, Lin has organized regular international and [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) conferences on [systems research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_research) and applications.

### Blown-up theory {#Blown-up_theory}

Continuing his works on the chaos of general [systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems), starting in 1995, Lin joined hands with Shoucheng OuYang in establishing a theory about transitional changes naturally existing in evolutions of materials and events in the hope of producing more accurate [forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_forecast) for disastrous [weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather) conditions.[^o11] When Lin's edited volume "Mystery of Nonlinearity and Lorenz's Chaos" was published in 1998,[^o11] over one thousand correspondences from various scholars were received within the following year.[^o12] Works along this line have been practically employed in day-to-day [weather forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_forecasts) of several stations in China.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

### Grey systems theory {#Grey_systems_theory}

Starting in early 1997, Lin collaborated with [Sifeng Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sifeng_Liu) on their works of a new theory of [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954) for partially known and partially unknown systems.[^o17] This theory was initially founded by [Julong Deng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julong_Deng) of [Huazhong University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huazhong_University_of_Science_and_Technology), Wuhan, in 1982 with the publication of his paper "Control problems of grey systems," in the international journal Systems and Control Letter, edited at the time by [Roger W. Brockett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_W._Brockett). In 2007, they founded the [Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute) for Grey Systems Studies at [Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_University_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics) and started an [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) conference series, named "IEEE International Conference on Grey Systems and intelligent Services," in 2007.[^o18]

### Crises in foundations of mathematics {#Crises_in_foundations_of_mathematics}

With an invitation from [Ronald Mickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Mickens) in 1987, Lin, along with Wendell Holladay ([Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University)), [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) ([University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago)), [John Polkinghorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Polkinghorne) (Cambridge, UK), and others, expressed his opinions[^o20] from the angle of systems research on [Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) laureate [Eugene P. Wigner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_P._Wigner)'s assertion about "the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics." Continuing this work, Lin[^o21] (with a colleague) addressed the problem of knowability of the physical world in 1997. Then in 2008 Lin guest edited a special volume of the international journal Kybernetes on the fourth crisis in the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) along with technical explanations on why the 2nd and the 3rd crises were not resolved as believed in history.[^o22][^o23]

### The systemic yoyo model {#The_systemic_yoyo_model}

Continuing his joint works of 1998 and 2002, in 2008 Lin officially proposed a new [model](wikipedia://en/2381958), the systemic yoyo, for the [general system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Systems), hoping that this model could play a role in systems research as that of the points of [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) in modern science. It is found that this model can be readily applied to study structures and organizations from diverse areas.[^o25]

### Evolution science {#Evolution_science}

Based on his works in blown-up [theory](wikipedia://en/30746), [predictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictions) of disastrous weather conditions, and systems modeling, Lin, Shoucheng OuYang, and other members of their research center on blown-ups theory, studied why [modern science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_science) did not appear in China, why such theories as [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) have become extremely successful, while predicting (near) zero probability disastrous weathers is still practically impossible. After pointing out the limitations of [modern science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_science), Lin, along with colleagues, proposed how [post-modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-modern) science would look like after first addressing what [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) is.[^o25]
