# Moyal product

the product on functions on phase space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moyal product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Moyal product** (after [José Enrique Moyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Enrique_Moyal); also called the **star product** or **Weyl–Groenewold product**, after [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [Hilbrand J. Groenewold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbrand_J._Groenewold)) is an example of a **phase-space star product**. It is an associative, non-commutative product, ★, on the functions on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$, equipped with its [Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340) (with a generalization to [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356), described below). It is a special case of the ★-product of the "algebra of symbols" of a [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551).

:::

## Historical comments {#Historical_comments}

The Moyal product is named after [José Enrique Moyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Enrique_Moyal), but is also sometimes called the [Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)–Groenewold product as it was introduced by [H. J. Groenewold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbrand_J._Groenewold) in his 1946 doctoral dissertation, in a trenchant appreciation[^o3] of the [Weyl correspondence](wikipedia://en/4277086). Moyal actually appears not to know about the product in his celebrated article[^o4] and was crucially lacking it in his legendary correspondence with Dirac, as illustrated in his biography.[^o5] The popular naming after Moyal appears to have emerged only in the 1970s, in homage to his flat [phase-space quantization](wikipedia://en/36053570) picture.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The product for [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) $f$ and $g$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ takes the form

$$f \star g = fg + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \hbar^n C_n(f,g),$$

where each $C_{n}$ is a certain bi[differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) of order $n$, characterized by the following properties (see below for an explicit formula):

- $f \star g = fg + \mathcal O(\hbar),$ Deformation of the pointwise product — implicit in the formula above.
- $f \star g - g \star f = i\hbar\{f,g\} + \mathcal O(\hbar^3) \equiv i\hbar \{\{f,g\}\},$ Deformation of the Poisson bracket, called [Moyal bracket](wikipedia://en/8875186).
- $f \star 1 = 1 \star f = f,$ The 1 of the undeformed algebra is also the identity in the new algebra.
- $\overline{f \star g} = \overline{g} \star \overline{f},$ The [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) is an [antilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilinear) [antiautomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiautomorphism).

Note that, if one wishes to take functions valued in the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers), then an alternative version eliminates the $i$ in the second condition and eliminates the fourth condition.

If one restricts to polynomial functions, the above algebra is isomorphic to the [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) $A_{n}$, and the two offer alternative realizations of the [Weyl map](wikipedia://en/4277086) of the space of polynomials in $n$ variables (or the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) of a vector space of dimension $2n$).

To provide an explicit formula, consider a constant [Poisson bivector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_bivector) $\Pi$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$:

$$\Pi = \sum_{i,j} \Pi^{ij} \partial_i \wedge \partial_j,$$

where $\Pi^{ij}$ is a real number for each $i, j$. The star product of two functions $f$ and $g$ can then be defined as the [pseudo-differential operator](wikipedia://en/973479) acting on both of them,

$$f \star g = fg + \frac{i\hbar}{2} \sum_{i,j} \Pi^{ij} (\partial_i f) (\partial_j g) - \frac{\hbar^2}{8} \sum_{i,j,k,m} \Pi^{ij} \Pi^{km} (\partial_i \partial_k f) (\partial_j \partial_m g) + \ldots,$$

where $\text{ħ}$ is the [reduced Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_Planck_constant), treated as a formal parameter here.

This is a special case of what is known as the [Berezin formula](wikipedia://en/363551)[^o9] on the algebra of symbols and can be given a closed form[^o10] (which follows from the [Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula](wikipedia://en/285422)). The closed form can be obtained by using the [exponential](wikipedia://en/672731):

$$f \star g = m \circ e^{\frac{i\hbar}{2} \Pi}(f \otimes g),$$

where $m$ is the multiplication map, $m(a \otimes b) = ab$, and the exponential is treated as a power series,

$$e^A = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{1}{n!} A^n.$$

That is, the formula for $C_{n}$ is

$$C_n = \frac{i^n}{2^n n!} m \circ \Pi^n.$$

As indicated, often one eliminates all occurrences of $i$ above, and the formulas then restrict naturally to real numbers.

Note that if the functions $f$ and $g$ are polynomials, the above infinite sums become finite (reducing to the ordinary Weyl-algebra case).

The relationship of the Moyal product to the generalized ★-product used in the definition of the "algebra of symbols" of a [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) follows from the fact that the [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) is the universal enveloping algebra of the [Heisenberg algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_algebra) (modulo that the center equals the unit).

:::

## On manifolds {#On_manifolds}

On any symplectic manifold, one can, at least locally, choose coordinates so as to make the symplectic structure *constant*, by [Darboux's theorem](wikipedia://en/1543358); and, using the associated Poisson bivector, one may consider the above formula. For it to work globally, as a function on the whole manifold (and not just a local formula), one must equip the symplectic manifold with a torsion-free symplectic [connection](wikipedia://en/308815). This makes it a [Fedosov manifold](wikipedia://en/24864800).

More general results for *arbitrary Poisson manifolds* (where the Darboux theorem does not apply) are given by the [Kontsevich quantization formula](wikipedia://en/26049975).

## Examples {#Examples}

A simple explicit example of the construction and utility of the ★-product (for the simplest case of a two-dimensional euclidean [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101)) is given in the article on the [Wigner–Weyl transform](wikipedia://en/4277086): two Gaussians compose with this ★-product according to a hyperbolic tangent law:[^o14]

$$\exp\left[-a\left(q^2 + p^2\right)\right] \star \exp\left[-b\left(q^2 + p^2\right)\right] = \frac{1}{1 + \hbar^2 ab} \exp\left[-\frac{a + b}{1 + \hbar^2 ab} \left(q^2 + p^2\right)\right].$$

Equivalently,

$$e^{-\tanh (a) \frac{q^2 + p^2}{\hbar }} \star e^{-\tanh (b) \frac{q^2 + p^2}{\hbar }} = \frac{\tanh (a + b)}{\tanh (a) + \tanh (b)} e^{-\tanh (a+b) \frac{q^2 + p^2}{\hbar }}$$

The [classical limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_limit) at $\hbar \to 0, a/\hbar \to \alpha, b / \hbar \to \beta$ is $e^{-\alpha(q^2+p^2)} e^{-\beta(q^2+p^2)} = e^{-(\alpha + \beta)(q^2+p^2)}$, as expected.

*Every correspondence prescription* between phase space and Hilbert space, however, induces its own [proper](wikipedia://en/25848944) ★-product.[^o15][^o16]

Similar results are seen in the [Segal–Bargmann space](wikipedia://en/40731477) and in the [theta representation](wikipedia://en/4433311) of the [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518), where the creation and annihilation operators $a^{\ast} = z$ and $a = \partial/\partial z$ are understood to act on the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) (respectively, the [upper half-plane](wikipedia://en/287826) for the Heisenberg group), so that the position and momenta operators are given by $q =\frac{a + a^*}{2}$ and $p = \frac{a - a^*}{2i}$. This situation is clearly different from the case where the positions are taken to be real-valued, but does offer insights into the overall algebraic structure of the Heisenberg algebra and its envelope, the Weyl algebra.

:::

## Inside phase-space integrals {#Inside_phase-space_integrals}

Inside a phase-space integral, just one star product of the Moyal type may be dropped,[^o18] resulting in plain multiplication, as evident by [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252),

$$\int dx\,dp\;f\star g= \int dx\,dp ~f ~g,$$

making the cyclicity of the phase-space trace manifest. This is a unique property of the above specific Moyal product, and does not hold for other correspondence rules' star products, such as Husimi's, etc.
