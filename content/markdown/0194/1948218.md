# Hjalmar Mellin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hjalmar Mellin"}

**Robert Hjalmar Mellin** (19 June 1854 – 5 April 1933) was a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and function theorist.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mellin was born on June 19, 1854 to priest and a former teacher Gustaf Robert Mellin (1826-1880) and Sofia Augusta Thérmen (1821-1888) in [Liminka, Northern Ostrobothnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liminka), [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland). He was the oldest among his four siblings, He worked as a translator of his father's religious and literary works after his father's expulsion from the priesthood in 1866 because of his heavy drinking, although he was suspended in 1864 because of the same reason. Hjarmar's mother Sofia was the sister of the former Councilor of State, Karl Otto Themén (1818-1893).[^o6] Mellin studied at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki) and later in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) under [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459).[^o7][^o4] He is chiefly remembered for his use of the [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) known as the *[Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297)*. He studied related [gamma functions](wikipedia://en/12316), [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223), [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258) and the [Riemann ζ function](wikipedia://en/25809). He was appointed professor at the Polytechnic Institute in Helsinki, which later became [Helsinki University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_University_of_Technology) with Mellin as first rector.

Later in his career Mellin also became known for his critical opposition to the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001); he published several papers in which he argued against the theory from a chiefly philosophical standpoint. In his private life he was known as an outspoken [fennoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennoman): a proponent of adopting [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) as the language of state and culture in the [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland), in preference to [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language), which had predominantly been used hitherto.
