# Michael Minovitch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Minovitch"}

**Michael Andrew Minovitch** (c. 1936 - 16 September 2022)[^o3] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who developed [gravity assist](wikipedia://en/155758) technique when he was a [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) [graduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_education) and working summers at [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)'s [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory).[^o4][^o5] He claimed that he invented the technique, even though multiple publications preceded his own. He later sued several people, including [Richard Battin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Battin) who published a paper on gravity assists in 1958, for stealing his ideas, but lost.[^o6]

:::

In 1961 Minovitch began using the fastest available computer at the time, the [IBM 7090](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_7090), to find solutions of the [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420). He ran simulations and developed his own solution by 1962.[^o3]

The first mission to use a gravity assist was [Pioneer 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_10), which increased its velocity from 52,000 km/h to 132,000 km/h as it passed by Jupiter in December, 1973.[^o7][^o8]

Minovitch patented a vehicle for space travel under the patent title *Magnetic propulsion system and operating method*, US Patent 6193194 B1.
