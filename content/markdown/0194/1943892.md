# Greek letters used in mathematics, science, and engineering

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Greek letters used in mathematics, science, and engineering"}

[Greek letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_letters) are used in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), and other areas where mathematical notation is used as symbols for [constants](wikipedia://en/48779551), [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function), and also conventionally for [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) representing certain quantities. In these contexts, the capital letters and the small letters represent distinct and unrelated entities. Those Greek letters which have the same form as [Latin letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_letters) are rarely used: capital Α, Β, Ε, Ζ, Η, Ι, Κ, Μ, Ν, Ο, Ρ, Τ, Υ, and Χ. Small ι, ο and υ are also rarely used, since they closely resemble the Latin letters i, o and u. Sometimes, font variants of Greek letters are used as distinct symbols in mathematics, in particular for ε/ϵ and π/ϖ. The archaic letter [digamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digamma) (Ϝ/ϝ/ϛ) is sometimes used.

:::

The [Bayer designation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_designation) naming scheme for [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star) typically uses the first Greek letter, α, for the brightest star in each constellation, and runs through the alphabet before switching to Latin letters.

In [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680), [the Greeks](wikipedia://en/250251) are the variables denoted by Greek letters used to describe the risk of certain investments.

## Typography {#Typography}

Some common conventions:

- [Intensive quantities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_quantity) in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) are usually denoted with [minuscules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_case) while [extensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensive_quantity) are denoted with [capital letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_letter).
- Most symbols are written in [italics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italic_type).
- [Vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometry%29) can be denoted in [boldface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boldface).
- Sets of [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) are typically bold or [blackboard bold](wikipedia://en/40276).

The Greek letter forms used in mathematics are often different from those used in [Greek-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) text: they are designed to be used in isolation, not connected to other letters, and some use variant forms which are not normally used in current Greek typography.

The [OpenType](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenType) font format has the feature tag "mgrk" ("Mathematical Greek") to identify a [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) as representing a Greek letter to be used in mathematical (as opposed to Greek language) contexts.

The table below shows a comparison of Greek letters rendered in [TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX) and HTML. The lowercase Greek letters used in the TeX rendering is an italic style. This is in line with the convention that variables should be italicized. As Greek letters are more often than not used as variables in mathematical formulas, a Greek letter appearing similar to the TeX rendering is more likely to be encountered in works involving mathematics. Unlike Unicode and HTML, TeX does not have special symbols for Greek capital letters that look identical to their Latin counterparts. Instead the Latin capital letters A, E, Z, H, I, K, M, N, O, P, T, X are used for capital Alpha, Epsilon, Zeta, Eta, Iota, Kappa, Mu, Nu, Omicron, Rho, Tau, and Chi respectively.[^o3]

## Concepts represented by a Greek letter {#Concepts_represented_by_a_Greek_letter}

### Αα (alpha) {#Αα_(alpha)}

- $\alpha$ represents:
  - the first [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), opposite the side *a*
  - the [statistical significance](wikipedia://en/160995) of a result
  - the [false positive rate](wikipedia://en/5657877) in statistics ("Type I" error)[^o31]
  - the [fine-structure constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine-structure_constant) in physics[^o32][^o33]
  - the [angle of attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_attack) of an aircraft
  - an [alpha particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_particle) (He<sup>2+</sup>)
  - [angular acceleration](wikipedia://en/65929) in physics[^o34]
  - the [linear thermal expansion coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coefficient_of_thermal_expansion)
  - the [thermal diffusivity](wikipedia://en/58282)[^o35]
  - In [organic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_chemistry) the α-carbon is the backbone carbon next to the carbonyl carbon, most often for [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acids)
  - [right ascension](wikipedia://en/26073) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy)[^o36]
  - the brightest star in a constellation[^o37]
  - [Iron ferrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropes_of_iron) and numerous phases within materials science
  - the [return in excess of the compensation for the risk borne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_%28investment%29) in investment
  - the [α-conversion](wikipedia://en/18203#α-conversion) in [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203)
  - the [independence number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_number) of a graph
  - a placeholder for [ordinal numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_numbers) in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636)
  - a type of receptor for the neurotransmitter [noradrenaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noradrenaline) in [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience)

### Ββ (beta) {#Ββ_(beta)}

- $\mathrm{B}$ represents the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660)
- $\beta$ represents:
  - the [thermodynamic beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic_beta), equal to (*k*<sub>B</sub>*T*)<sup>−1</sup>, where *k*<sub>B</sub> is the Boltzmann constant and *T* is the absolute temperature.
  - the second angle in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), opposite the side *b*
  - the [standardized regression coefficient](wikipedia://en/7338849) for predictor or independent variables in [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386) (unstandardized regression coefficients are represented with the lower-case Latin b, but are often called "betas" as well)
  - the ratio of collector current to base current in a bipolar junction [transistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transistor) (BJT) in electronics (current gain)
  - the [false negative rate](wikipedia://en/5657877) in statistics ("Type II" error)[^o39]
  - the [beta coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_coefficient), the non-diversifiable risk, of an asset in [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)
  - the [sideslip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sideslip) angle of an airplane
  - a [beta particle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_particle) (e<sup>−</sup> or e<sup>\+</sup>)
  - the beta brain wave in [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain) or [cognitive sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_sciences)[^o40]
  - [ecliptic latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecliptic_latitude) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy)[^o41]
  - the ratio of [plasma pressure to magnetic pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_%28plasma_physics%29) in [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics)[^o42]
  - [β-reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Β-reduction) in [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203)
  - the ratio of the velocity of an object to the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light) as used in the [Lorentz factor](wikipedia://en/732446)[^o43]
  - a type of receptor for the [noradrenaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noradrenaline) neurotransmitter in [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience)

### Γγ (gamma) {#Γγ_(gamma)}

- $\Gamma$ represents:
  - the [circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulation_%28fluid_dynamics%29) in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034)
  - the [reflection coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_coefficient) of a transmission or telecommunication line.[^o45]
  - the confinement factor of an optical mode in a [waveguide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveguide)
  - the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), a generalization of the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)[^o46]
  - the [upper incomplete gamma function](wikipedia://en/456050)
  - the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019), the group of fractional linear transformations
  - the [gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/207079), a continuous probability distribution defined using the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316)[^o47]
  - [second-order sensitivity to price](wikipedia://en/250251#Gamma) in [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)
  - the [Christoffel symbols](wikipedia://en/1401020) that describe components of a metric connection[^o48]
  - the stack alphabet in the formal definition of a [pushdown automaton](wikipedia://en/24510), or the tape-alphabet in the formal definition of a [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403)
  - the [Feferman–Schütte ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084455) Γ<sub>0</sub>[^o49]
  - atmospheric [lapse rates](wikipedia://en/224312) in [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904)
- $\gamma$ represents:
  - the [specific weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_weight) of substances
  - the [lower incomplete gamma function](wikipedia://en/456050)
  - the third angle in a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), opposite the side *c*
  - the [Euler–Mascheroni constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Mascheroni_constant) in mathematics[^o50]
  - [gamma rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_ray) and the [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon)
  - the [heat capacity ratio](wikipedia://en/1949009) in [thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/29952)
  - the [Lorentz factor](wikipedia://en/732446) in special relativity[^o51]
  - the [flight path angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_flight_dynamics) of an airplane

### Δδ (delta) {#Δδ_(delta)}

- $\Delta$ represents:
  - a [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147)
  - a [difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator)
  - a [symmetric difference](wikipedia://en/262288)
  - the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706)
  - giving heat in a [chemical reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction)
  - the angle that subtends the arc of a circular curve in [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying)
  - the maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of any vertex in a given [graph](wikipedia://en/325806)
  - [sensitivity to price](wikipedia://en/250251) in [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)
  - the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of a polynomial (in a quadratic polynomial determines the nature of the roots)
- $\delta$ represents:
  - [percent error](wikipedia://en/640422)
  - a variation in the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882)
  - the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890) function[^o53]
  - the [Feigenbaum constants](wikipedia://en/79150)[^o54]
  - the [force of interest](wikipedia://en/434428) in [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680)
  - the [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021)[^o55]
  - the [receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_%28biochemistry%29) which [enkephalins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enkephalins) have the highest affinity for in [pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology)[^o56]
  - the [Skorokhod integral](wikipedia://en/18420736) in [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), a subfield of stochastic analysis
  - the minimum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of any vertex in a given [graph](wikipedia://en/325806)
  - a [partial charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_charge). δ− represents a negative partial charge, and δ+ represents a positive partial charge in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry)(See also: [Solvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvation))
  - the [chemical shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_shift) of an atomic nucleus in [NMR spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMR_spectroscopy). For protons, this is relative to [tetramethylsilane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetramethylsilane) = 0
  - [stable isotope compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotope_analysis#Stable_isotope_analysis_in_aquatic_ecosystems)
  - [declination](wikipedia://en/8612) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy)[^o36]
  - noncentrality measure in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685)[^o57]
  - The transition function in the formal definition of a [finite automaton](wikipedia://en/10931), [pushdown automaton](wikipedia://en/24510), or [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403)
  - [Infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) - see Limit of a function
- Not to be confused with [∂](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∂) which is based on the Latin letter d but often called a "script delta"

### Εε (epsilon) {#Εε_(epsilon)}

- $\epsilon$ represents:
  - a small positive quantity; see [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602)
  - a random error in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997)
  - the absolute value of an error[^o59]
  - in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the limit [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932) of the sequence $\omega,\omega^{\omega},\omega^{\omega^{\omega}},\dots$
  - in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), the [empty string](wikipedia://en/27701)
  - the [Levi-Civita symbol](wikipedia://en/195982)
  - in [electromagnetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetics), [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric) [permittivity](wikipedia://en/53933)[^o60]
  - [emissivity](wikipedia://en/902820)
  - [strain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_%28mechanics%29) in [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918)
  - [permittivity](wikipedia://en/53933)[^o61]
  - the Earth's [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy)[^o62]
  - [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28economics%29) in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics)[^o63]
  - [electromotive force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromotive_force)
  - in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), the [molar extinction coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molar_extinction_coefficient) of a [chromophore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromophore)
  - in mathematics, a [surreal number](wikipedia://en/51432) that is bigger than zero, but smaller than all positive real numbers.
- [set](wikipedia://en/26691) membership symbol ∈ is based on ε

### Ϝϝ (digamma) {#(digamma)}

- Ϝ is sometimes used to represent the [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935), though the Latin letter F (which is nearly identical) is usually substituted.
- A hypothetical particle Ϝ speculated to be implicated in the [750&nbsp;GeV diphoton excess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/750&nbsp;GeV_diphoton_excess), now known to be simply a statistical anomaly

### Ζζ (zeta) {#Ζζ_(zeta)}

### Ψψ (psi) {#Ψψ_(psi)}

- $\Psi$ represents:
  - a quaternary combinator in [combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848)
  - a symbol for [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology)
  - the [wave function](wikipedia://en/145343) in the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202)[^o81]
- $\psi$ represents:
  - the [J/psi mesons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J/psi_meson) in particle physics
  - the [stream function](wikipedia://en/218091) in fluid dynamics
  - the [reciprocal Fibonacci constant](wikipedia://en/11827553)[^o114]
  - the second [Chebyshev function](wikipedia://en/5793598) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527)[^o115]
  - the [polygamma function](wikipedia://en/406932) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831)[^o116]
  - [the supergolden ratio](wikipedia://en/58128086)[^o117]
  - In [circadian physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circadian_physiology), ψ represents the phase relationship between a [zeitgeber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitgeber) and a [biological rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_rhythm).[^o118]
  - In [virology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virology) the ψ site is a viral packaging signal.[^o119]
  - [Water potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_potential) in movement of water between plant cells.[^o120]

### Ωω (omega) {#Ωω_(omega)}

- $\Omega$ represents:
  - [Absolute infinite](wikipedia://en/3231)
  - the [SI unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_unit) measure of [electrical resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance), the [ohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm_%28unit%29)[^o122][^o81]
  - the [right ascension of the ascending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_ascension_of_the_ascending_node) (RAAN) or [Longitude of the ascending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude_of_the_ascending_node) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [orbital mechanics](wikipedia://en/182146)
  - the [omega constant](wikipedia://en/408863) 0.5671432904097838729999686622...[^o123]
  - an asymptotic lower bound notation related to [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)
  - in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), the [support](wikipedia://en/381013)
  - a [solid angle](wikipedia://en/94102)[^o124][^o60]
  - the [omega baryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_baryon)
  - the [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170#Ω%28n%29,_ω%28n%29,_νp%28n%29_–_prime_power_decomposition) counting a number's prime factors counted with multiplicity
  - the [density parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_parameter) in [cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378)[^o125]
  - the [first uncountable ordinal](wikipedia://en/20860540) (also written as ω<sub>1</sub>)[^o126]
  - [Chaitin's constant](wikipedia://en/6205) for a given computer program
  - the [vacuum state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_vacuum_state) in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267)
- $\omega$ represents:
  - [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) / [radian frequency](wikipedia://en/65927) (rad/sec)[^o60]
  - the [argument of periapsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_of_periapsis) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [orbital mechanics](wikipedia://en/182146)
  - a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [cube](wikipedia://en/292506) [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) — the other is $\omega^2$ — (used to describe various ways of calculating the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811))
  - the differentiability class (*i.e.* $C^\omega$) for functions that are infinitely differentiable because they are [complex analytic](wikipedia://en/61478)
  - the first [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932)
  - the [omega meson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_meson)
  - the set of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) (although $\mathbb{N}$ or **N** is more common in other areas of mathematics)
  - an asymptotic dominant notation related to [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)
  - in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a possible outcome of an experiment
  - the [arithmetic function](wikipedia://en/3170#Ω%28n%29,_ω%28n%29,_νp%28n%29_–_prime_power_decomposition) counting a number's distinct prime factors[^o127]
  - the symbol ϖ, a graphic variant of π, is sometimes construed as omega with a bar over it; see π
  - the [unsaturated fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsaturated_fat) nomenclature in biochemistry (e.g. [ω−3 fatty acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega-3_fatty_acid))
  - the first uncountable ordinal $\omega_1$ (also written as Ω)
  - the [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) number (number of vertices in a maximum clique) of a graph in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401)[^o128]
