# Serre's inequality on height

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Serre's inequality on height"}

In algebra, specifically in the theory of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), **Serre's inequality on height** states: given a (Noetherian) [regular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring) *A* and a pair of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) $\mathfrak{p}, \mathfrak{q}$ in it, for each prime ideal $\mathfrak r$ that is a [minimal prime ideal](wikipedia://en/16143832) over the sum $\mathfrak p + \mathfrak q$, the following inequality on [heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_of_an_ideal) holds:[^o2][^o3]

:::

$$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak r) \le \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p) + \operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q).$$

Without the assumption on regularity, the inequality can fail; see [scheme-theoretic intersection#Proper intersection](wikipedia://en/52580781#Proper_intersection).

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Sketch of Proof"}

## Sketch of Proof {#Sketch_of_Proof}

Serre gives the following proof of the inequality, based on the validity of [Serre's multiplicity conjectures](wikipedia://en/472700) for [formal power series ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_power_series_ring) over a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28ring_theory%29) [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633).[^o6]

By replacing $A$ by the localization at $\mathfrak r$, we assume $(A, \mathfrak r)$ is a local ring. Then the inequality is equivalent to the following inequality: for finite $A$-modules $M, N$ such that $M \otimes_A N$ has finite length,

$$\dim_A M + \dim_A N \le \dim A$$

where $\dim_A M = \dim(A/\operatorname{Ann}_A(M))$ = the dimension of the support of $M$ and similar for $\dim_A N$. To show the above inequality, we can assume $A$ is complete. Then by [Cohen's structure theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen's_structure_theorem), we can write $A = A_1/a_1 A_1$ where $A_1$ is a formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) ring over a complete discrete valuation ring and $a_1$ is a nonzero element in $A_1$. Now, an argument with the [Tor spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_spectral_sequence) shows that $\chi^{A_1}(M, N) = 0$. Then one of Serre's conjectures says $\dim_{A_1} M + \dim_{A_1} N < \dim A_1$, which in turn gives the asserted inequality. $\square$

:::
