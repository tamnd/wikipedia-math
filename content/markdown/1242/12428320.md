# Arthur T. Benjamin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur T. Benjamin"}

**Arthur T. Benjamin** (born March 19, 1961) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who specializes in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). Since 1989, he has been a professor of mathematics at [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College), where he is the Smallwood Family Professor of Mathematics.[^o3]

:::

He is known for [mental math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_math) capabilities and "[Mathemagics](wikipedia://en/2164767)" performances in front of live audiences. His mathematical abilities have been highlighted in newspaper and magazine articles, at [TED Talks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_Talks) and on the [Colbert Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colbert_Report).

## Education {#Education}

Benjamin earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) with highest honors in applied mathematics at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) in 1983. He then went on to receive a [Master of Science in Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science_in_Engineering) in 1985 and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1989 in mathematical sciences at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). His PhD dissertation was titled "Turnpike Structures for Optimal Maneuvers", and was supervised by [Alan J. Goldman](wikipedia://en/50339482).[^o5]

During his freshman year at CMU, he wrote the lyrics and created the magic effects for the musical comedy, *Kije!*, in collaboration with author Scott McGregor and composer Arthur Darrell Turner. This musical was the winner of an annual competition and was first performed as the CMU's Spring Musical in 1980.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

### Academic {#Academic}

*Image: Arthur Benjamin Washington 2009.jpg.* Arthur Benjamin at the Joint Mathematics Meetings in [Washington, DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_DC), January 2009.

Benjamin held several mathematics positions while attending university, including stints with the [National Bureau of Standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Standards), the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939), and the [Institute for Defense Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses). Upon receipt of his PhD, he was hired as an assistant professor of mathematics at Harvey Mudd College. He is currently a full professor at Harvey Mudd and was [chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman) of the mathematics department from 2002 to 2004. He has published over 90 academic papers and five books.[^o5] He has also filmed several sets of lectures on mathematical topics for [The Great Courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Courses) series from [The Teaching Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Teaching_Company), including a course on The Joy of Mathematics, Discrete Mathematics, Mental Math, The Mathematics of Games and Puzzles, Math and Magic, and Fibonacci Numbers and the Golden Ratio. He served as co-editor of *[Math Horizons](wikipedia://en/21340642)* magazine for five years.[^o9]

### Mathemagics {#Mathemagics}

*Image: LightningFastCalculatorBeatsElectronicCalculator.jpg.* Arthur Benjamin performs at the 1983 [CSICOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSICOP) Conference in [Buffalo, NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_NY).

Benjamin has long had an interest in [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_%28illusion%29). While in college, he honed his skills as a magician and attended magic conferences. At one of these conferences, he met well-known magician and [skeptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeptic) [James Randi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Randi), who greatly influenced Benjamin's decision to perform Mathemagics shows for live audiences. Randi invited him to perform his mathematical tricks on a television program called *Exploring Psychic Powers Live*, co-hosted by [Uri Geller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uri_Geller). Randi also encouraged Benjamin to become involved in the growing skeptical movement. He attended early meetings of the Southern California Skeptics in the 1990s, which later evolved into the [Skeptics Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeptics_Society). It was at these meetings that he met Skeptics Society President [Michael Shermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Shermer), who would later become a co-author on three of Benjamin's books.[^o11]

Benjamin regularly performs his Mathemagics program for live audiences at schools, colleges, conferences, and even at [The Magic Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Castle) in [Hollywood, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_California).[^o12] These shows feature Benjamin performing mathematical feats like rapidly [squaring](wikipedia://en/659942) numbers with up to five digits and correctly identifying the day of the week on which audience members were born based on their birth dates.[^o13]

He was also featured in *Mathemagics*, a multimedia disc released for the [3DO Interactive Multiplayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3DO_Interactive_Multiplayer) in 1994, which consists largely of short demonstrations and lessons by Benjamin in mental math and Mathemagics.[^o14]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- American Backgammon Tour Player of the Year, 1997 and 2020 (online)[^o16]
- Fellow of the [Institute of Combinatorics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/17545919), 2000[^o5]
- [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics),[^o17] [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), 2000[^o5]
- CHOICE Award, Outstanding Academic Title, for *Proofs that Really Count: The Art of Combinatorial Proof*, [American Library Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Library_Association), 2004[^o5]
- Designated "America's Best Math Whiz" by *[Reader's Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader's_Digest)*, May 2005[^o5]
- Beckenbach Book Prize, for *Proofs that Really Count*, Mathematical Association of America, 2006[^o5]
- George Pólya Lecturer, Mathematical Association of America, 2006-2008[^o5]
- Selected by *[The Princeton Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princeton_Review)* as one of The Best 300 Professors, 2012[^o5]
- American Backgammon Hall of Fame, 2022 [^o18]

## Media {#Media}

Benjamin has appeared in three TED Talks. The first, in 2005, was a demonstration of his Mathemagics show. The second, in 2009, was a plea for improved math education in schools. The third, in 2013, was about the way the [Fibonacci series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_series) of numbers provides an excellent example of the three most important reasons for studying mathematics: Calculation, Application, and Inspiration.[^o20]

Benjamin hosted lectures in [The Great Courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Courses) titled *The Joy of Mathematics* and *Secrets of Mental Math*.[^o21]

He has appeared on numerous television programs throughout the years, including a notable performance on the Colbert Report in 2010. He has been profiled in over 100 articles in periodicals such as *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*, *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_%28magazine%29)*, *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*, and *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*.[^o22]
