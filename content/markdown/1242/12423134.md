# François Proth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="François Proth"}

**François Proth** (22 March 1852 – 21 January 1879) was a self-taught French mathematician and farmer who lived in [Vaux-devant-Damloup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaux-devant-Damloup) near [Verdun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdun), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France).[^o2]

:::

He stated four primality-related theorems.[^o3] The most famous of these, [Proth's theorem](wikipedia://en/3225985), can be used to test whether a [Proth number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proth_number) (a number of the form *k* × 2<sup>*n*</sup> + 1 with *k* odd and *k* \< 2<sup>*n*</sup>) is prime. The numbers passing this test are called [Proth primes](wikipedia://en/62508931); they continue to be of importance in the computational search for large prime numbers.[^o4]

Proth also formulated [Gilbreath's conjecture](wikipedia://en/1010141) on successive differences of primes, 80 years prior to Gilbreath, but his proof of the conjecture turned out to be erroneous.[^o5]

The cause of Proth's death is not known.[^o6]
