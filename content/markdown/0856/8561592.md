# Geometry of interaction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometry of interaction"}

In [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), the **Geometry of Interaction** (GoI) was introduced by [Jean-Yves Girard](wikipedia://en/579673) shortly after his work on [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675). In linear logic, [proofs](wikipedia://en/1197184) can be seen as various kinds of networks as opposed to the flat tree structures of [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329). To distinguish the real [proof nets](wikipedia://en/921927) from all the possible networks, Girard devised a criterion involving trips in the network. Trips can in fact be seen as some kind of [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) acting on the proof. Drawing from this observation, Girard[^o2] described directly this operator from the proof and has given a formula, the so-called *execution formula*, encoding the process of [cut elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_elimination) at the level of operators. Subsequent constructions by Girard proposed variants in which proofs are represented as flows,[^o3] or operators in [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185).[^o4]

:::

One of the first significant applications of GoI was a better analysis[^o5] of Lamping's algorithm[^o6] for optimal reduction for the [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203). GoI had a strong influence on [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121) for [linear logic](wikipedia://en/579675) and [PCF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language_for_Computable_Functions).

GoI has been applied to deep [compiler optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler_optimisation) for [lambda calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_calculi).[^o7] A bounded version of GoI dubbed **the Geometry of Synthesis** has been used to compile [higher-order programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-order_programming) directly into static circuits.[^o8]
