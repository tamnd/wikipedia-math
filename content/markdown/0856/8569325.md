# Mishnat ha-Middot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mishnat ha-Middot"}

The **Mishnat ha-Middot** (מִשְׁנַת הַמִּדּוֹת, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Treatise of Measures') is the earliest known [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) [treatise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise) on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), composed of 49 *[mishnayot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah#Six_orders)* in six chapters. Scholars have dated the work to either the [Mishnaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnaic_period) or the [early Islamic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age).

:::

## History {#History}

### Date of composition {#Date_of_composition}

[Moritz Steinschneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Steinschneider) dated the *Mishnat ha-Middot* to between 800 and 1200 CE.[^o4] Sarfatti and Langermann have advanced Steinschneider's claim of [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) influence on the work's [terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_terminology), and date the text to the early ninth century.[^o5][^o6]

On the other hand, [Hermann Schapira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Schapira) argued that the treatise dates from an earlier era, most likely the [Mishnaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnaic_period), as its mathematical terminology differs from that of the [Hebrew mathematicians](wikipedia://en/11353703) of the [Arab period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age). [Solomon Gandz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Gandz) conjectured that the text was compiled no later than 150 (possibly by [Rabbi Nehemiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi_Nehemiah)) and intended to be a part of the [Mishnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishnah), but was excluded from its final canonical edition because the work was regarded as too [secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular).[^o15] The content resembles both the work of [Hero of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/68274) (c. 100) and that of [al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khwārizmī) (c. 800) and the proponents of the earlier dating therefore see the *Mishnat ha-Middot* linking [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics) and [Islamic mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam).[^o7]

### Modern history {#Modern_history}

The *Mishnat ha-Middot* was discovered in MS 36 of the [Munich Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Library) by Moritz Steinschneider in 1862. The manuscript, copied in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) in 1480, goes as far as the end of Chapter V. According to the [colophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colophon_%28publishing%29), the copyist believed the text to be complete.[^o9] Steinschneider published the work in 1864, in honour of the seventieth birthday of [Leopold Zunz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Zunz). The text was edited and published again by mathematician Hermann Schapira in 1880.[^o10]

After the discovery by [Otto Neugebauer](wikipedia://en/737813) of a [genizah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genizah)-fragment in the [Bodleian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library) containing Chapter VI, [Solomon Gandz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Gandz) published a complete version of the *Mishnat ha-Middot* in 1932, accompanied by a thorough [philological analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology). A third manuscript of the work was found among uncatalogued material in the Archives of the [Jewish Museum of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_in_Prague) in 1965.

## Contents {#Contents}

Although primarily a practical work, the *Mishnat ha-Middot* attempts to define terms and explain both geometric application and theory.[^o12] The book begins with a discussion that defines "aspects" for the different kinds of [plane figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_figure) ([quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278), [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), and [segment of a circle](wikipedia://en/404049)) in Chapter I (§1–5), and with the basic principles of measurement of [areas](wikipedia://en/1209) (§6–9). In Chapter II, the work introduces concise rules for the measurement of plane figures (§1–4), as well as a few problems in the calculation of [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) (§5–12). In Chapters III–V, the *Mishnat ha-Middot* explains again in detail the measurement of the four types of plane figures, with reference to numerical examples.[^o13] The text concludes with a discussion of the proportions of the [Tabernacle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabernacle) in Chapter VI.[^o14]

The treatise argues against the common belief that the [Tanakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) defines the [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) ratio [π](wikipedia://en/23601) as being exactly equal to 3 and defines it as [$\tfrac{22}{7}$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximations_of_π) instead.[^o15] The book arrives at this approximation by calculating the [area of a circle](wikipedia://en/1896782) according to the formulae

$A=d^2-\tfrac{d^2}{7}-\tfrac{d^2}{14}$ and $A=\tfrac{c}{2}\cdot\tfrac{d}{2}$.p. II§3, V§3
