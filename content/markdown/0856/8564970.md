# Landau–Kolmogorov inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landau–Kolmogorov inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Landau–Kolmogorov inequality**, named after [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) and [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), is the following family of [interpolation inequalities](wikipedia://en/38508663) between different derivatives of a function *f* defined on a subset *T* of the real numbers:[^o2]

:::

$$\|f^{(k)}\|_{L_\infty(T)} \le C(n, k, T) {\|f\|_{L_\infty(T)}}^{1-k/n} {\|f^{(n)}\|_{L_\infty(T)}}^{k/n} \text{ for } 1\le k < n.$$

## On the real line {#On_the_real_line}

For *k* = 1, *n* = 2 and *T* = \[*c*,∞) or *T* = **R**, the inequality was first proved by Edmund Landau[^o4] with the sharp constants *C*(2, 1, \[*c*,∞)) = 2 and *C*(2, 1, **R**) = √2. Following contributions by [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) and [Georgiy Shilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiy_Shilov), Andrey Kolmogorov found the sharp constants and arbitrary *n*, *k*:[^o5]

$$C(n, k, \mathbb R) = a_{n-k} a_n^{-1+k/n}~,$$

where *a*<sub>*n*</sub> are the [Favard constants](wikipedia://en/8531319).

## On the half-line {#On_the_half-line}

Following work by Matorin and others, the extremising functions were found by [Isaac Jacob Schoenberg](wikipedia://en/977176),[^o7] explicit forms for the sharp constants are however still unknown.

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

There are many generalisations, which are of the form

$$\|f^{(k)}\|_{L_q(T)} \le K \cdot {\|f\|^\alpha_{L_p(T)}} \cdot {\|f^{(n)}\|^{1-\alpha}_{L_r(T)}}\text{ for }1\le k < n.$$

Here all three norms can be different from each other (from *L<sub>1</sub>* to *L<sub>∞</sub>*, with *p*=*q*=*r*=∞ in the classical case) and *T* may be the real axis, semiaxis or a closed segment.

The [Kallman–Rota inequality](wikipedia://en/29633061) generalizes the Landau–Kolmogorov inequalities from the derivative operator to more general [contractions](wikipedia://en/10907456) on [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).[^o9]
