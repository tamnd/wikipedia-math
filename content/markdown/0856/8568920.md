# Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption"}

The **Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption** holds that [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) flows horizontally in an [unconfined aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconfined_aquifer) and that the [groundwater discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater_discharge) is proportional to the saturated [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) thickness. It was formulated by [Jules Dupuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Dupuit) and [Philipp Forchheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Forchheimer) in the late 1800s to simplify [groundwater flow equations](wikipedia://en/1863666) for [analytical solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_solution).

:::

The Dupuit–Forchheimer assumption requires that the [water table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_table) be relatively flat and that the groundwater be [hydrostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrostatic) (that is, that the [equipotential](wikipedia://en/2866340) lines are vertical):

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} &= -\gamma = -\rho \, g \\[0.5em] \frac{\partial h}{\partial z} &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

where $\partial P/\partial z$ is the vertical pressure gradient, $\gamma$ is the [specific weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_weight), $\rho$ is the density of water, $g$ is the [standard gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gravity), and $\partial h/\partial z$ is the vertical [hydraulic gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_gradient).
