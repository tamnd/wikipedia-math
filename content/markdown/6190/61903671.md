# Patricia Hiddleston

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Patricia Hiddleston"}

**Patricia Hiddleston** (9 May 1933 – 8 December 2017), also known as **Pat Hiddleston**, was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who spent most of her professional career in [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rhodesia), central [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Patricia Wallace was born 9 May 1933 to hotel proprietors Harry and Jessie Wallace of [Troon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troon). She achieved good grades in school and went on to study mathematics and [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). She graduated in 1956 with a double first and was awarded the Napier medal for mathematics.[^o4][^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In July 1956 she graduated and, the same month, married George Hiddleston and became Pat Hiddleston.[^o7] She and her husband applied to the [Colonial Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Office) for a posting abroad and were allocated to [Northern Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rhodesia).[^o5][^o8]

Hiddleston worked at [Munali Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munali_Secondary_School), then a teacher training college near [Lusaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusaka).[^o9] She studied for a doctorate at the [University of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Africa) and was subsequently made the first member of the mathematics department of the [University of Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zambia), where she lectured from 1965–1970.[^o5]

After 14 years, Hiddleston and her family returned to Scotland so that the four children could have higher education there.[^o7] Hiddleston was the Headmistress of [St Margaret's School, Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Margaret's_School,_Edinburgh).

In 1984, Hiddleston relocated to South Africa to become the Principal of [Durban Girls' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durban_Girls'_College), where she oversaw the school's move to admit all races.[^o5]

Hiddleston then went to teach mathematics education[^o5] at the [University of Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malawi) in 1988. She continued this work until her husband's death in 1996, after which she moved into a consulting career involving advising governments on encouraging women into STEM studies and teacher training and development.[^o5][^o9]

Hiddleston also co-wrote and edited a number of textbooks.[^o5]

## Later life, death, and commemoration {#Later_life,_death,_and_commemoration}

Hiddleston volunteered at the 2014 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow and was honoured by the University of Edinburgh Institute of Mathematics for life services to maths education in 2015.[^o9]

Hiddleston continued to work until age 82, when she became ill whilst on a trip to Bangladesh and was diagnosed with ovarian cancer.[^o5]

In 2018, the University of Edinburgh began offering a Patricia Hiddleston Summer Scholarship.[^o4]
