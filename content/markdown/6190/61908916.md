# David Shale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Shale"}

**David Winston Howard Shale** (22 March 1932, [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) – 7 January 2016) was a New Zealand-American mathematician, specializing in the mathematical foundations of quantum physics.[^o2] He is known as one of the namesakes of the [Segal–Shale-Weil representation](wikipedia://en/32313477).[^o3]

:::

After secondary and undergraduate education in New Zealand, Shale became a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) and received his Ph.D. there in 1960.[^o2] His thesis *On certain groups of operators on Hilbert space* was written under the supervision of [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal). Shale became an assistant professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and then became in 1964 a professor at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), where he continued teaching until his retirement.[^o2]

He was an expert in the mathematical foundations of Quantum Physics with many very original ideas on the subject. In addition, he discovered what is now called the Shale-Weil Representation in operator theory. He was also an expert in Bayesian Probability Theory, especially as it applied to Physics.[^o2]

According to Irving Segal: ... although contrary to common intuitive belief, Lorentz-invariance in itself is materially insufficient to characterize the vacuum for any [free field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_field) (this remarkable fact is due to David Shale; it should perhaps be emphasized that this lack of uniqueness holds even in such a simple case as the conventional scalar meson field ...), none of the Lorentz-invariant states other than the conventional vacuum is consistent with the postulate of the positivity of the energy, when suitably and simply formulated.[^o5]
