# Robert P. Dilworth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert P. Dilworth"}

**Robert Palmer Dilworth** (December 2, 1914 – October 29, 1993) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His primary research area was [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory); his biography at the [MacTutor History of Mathematics archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacTutor_History_of_Mathematics_archive) states "it would not be an exaggeration to say that he was one of the main factors in the subject moving from being merely a tool of other disciplines to an important subject in its own right". He is best known for [Dilworth's theorem](wikipedia://en/749033) (Dilworth 1950) relating [chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_%28order_theory%29) and antichains in [partial orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order); he was also the first to study [antimatroids](wikipedia://en/667063) (Dilworth 1940).

:::

Dilworth was born in 1914 in [Hemet, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemet,_California), at that time a remote desert ranching town. He went to college at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), receiving his baccalaureate in 1936 and continuing there for his graduate studies. Dilworth's graduate advisor was [Morgan Ward](wikipedia://en/28805642), a student of [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236), who was also on the Caltech faculty at the time. On receiving his Ph.D. in 1939, Dilworth took an instructorship at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). While at Yale, he met and married his wife, Miriam White, with whom he eventually had two sons. He returned to Caltech as a faculty member in 1943, and spent the remainder of his academic career there. Dilworth advised 17 Ph.D. students and has 635 academic descendants listed at the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), many through his student [Juris Hartmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Hartmanis), a noted [complexity theorist](wikipedia://en/7543). Other notable mathematicians advised by Dilworth include [Curtis Greene](wikipedia://en/34818925) and [Alfred W. Hales](wikipedia://en/38295597).
