# Donaldson invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donaldson invariant"}

In [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) in mathematics, **Donaldson invariants** are [invariants](wikipedia://en/1126638) of the smooth structure of four-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifolds) (short [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795)) based on the methods behind [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754). In particular, key informations are obtained from the [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) of the [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/35544664) on the 4-manifold, which as [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) depend on the smooth structure. Donaldson invariants can distinguish these, hence are able to detect exotic smooth structures, meaning explicitly that [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) smooth 4-manifolds, hence identical from a topological perspective, can nonetheless have different Donaldson invariants. Donaldson invariants are named after [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), who first described Donaldson's theorem in 1983[^o2] (with improvement in 1987[^o3]) and the resulting invariants in 1986. He was also awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for his contributions in 1986.[^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Simon Donaldson.jpg.* Simon Donaldson was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1986[^o4] for the development of Donaldson theory

*Image: Witten at Chalmers2.jpg.* Edward Witten was awarded the Fields Medal in 1990[^o6] for the development of topological quantum field theories (TQFT)[^o7]

In 1983[^o2] (with later improvement in 1987[^o3]), [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305) proved [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754) with the unexpected strategy of using the (anti) self-dual [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) ((A)SDYM moduli space) of [principal SU(2)-bundles](wikipedia://en/80364907), hence with the [special unitary group](wikipedia://en/173997) $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ as [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group). In 1984, [Ronald Fintushel](wikipedia://en/62176391) und [Ronald Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Stern) adapted his work to [principal SO(3)-bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_SO%283%29-bundle),[^o8] hence with the special orthogonal group $\operatorname{SO}(3)$ as structure group. Both groups are connected by a double cover $\operatorname{SU}(2)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{SO}(3)$, exactly the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188), alternatively phrased one has the third [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) $\operatorname{Spin}(3)\cong\operatorname{SU}(2)$ and the second [projective unitary group](wikipedia://en/4402359) $\operatorname{PU}(2)\cong\operatorname{SO}(3)$. In 1986, Simon Donaldson expanded his work into [invariants](wikipedia://en/1126638) about the smooth structure of smooth manifolds, which work with both structure groups $\operatorname{SU}(2)$ and $\operatorname{SO}(3)$. In 1988, [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) opened up the field of [topological quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/443757) (TQFT)[^o7] and showed, that Donaldson invariants can be expressed as the [expectation values](wikipedia://en/11371560) of a topologically twisted [N = 2 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_2_supersymmetric_Yang-Mills_theory) (TT D = 4 N = 2 SYM).

In early 1994, [Peter Kronheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Kronheimer) and [Tomasz Mrowka](wikipedia://en/20652006) achieved a strong simplification of Donaldson invariants with the introduction of [Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes](wikipedia://en/35131387),[^o9][^o10] which reduced its [infinite sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_sum) to finite sums. In late 1994, [Nathan Seiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Seiberg) and Edward Witten constructed the dual TT N = 2 SYM.[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14] Both this gauge theory as well as its application to four-dimensional geometry are often referred to as [Seiberg–Witten theory](wikipedia://en/3526032), but strictly speaking are two different theories. [Seiberg–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/14407762) brought multiple improvements over Donaldson invariants, mainly due to the easier and furthermore [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) $\operatorname{U}(1)$ as structure group or the always [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Seiberg–Witten moduli space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_moduli_space), with made laborious [compactifications](wikipedia://en/6617) using [Uhlenbeck's compactness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhlenbeck's_compactness_theorem) required for Donaldson invariants obsolete. Despite that, it could be shown in the following years that Seiberg–Witten invariants still contain more information than Donaldson invariants, so the latter are not used as actively any more. In particular, [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920) described a connection to [Gromov–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/2301291) and Edward Witten concluded from purely physical arguments the Witten conjecture, that Kronheimer–Mrowka basic classes and therefore Donaldson invariants can be fully obtained from Seiberg–Witten theory.[^o15]

By now, also topologically twisted four-dimensional [N = 4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theories](wikipedia://en/36625367) (TT D = 4 N = 4 SYM) are considered, including [Donaldson–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Witten_theory), [Vafa–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vafa–Witten_theory) and [Kapustin–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapustin–Witten_theory), with other variants like three-dimensional [Rozansky–Witten theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozansky–Witten_theory). From these, further invariants like [Vafa–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vafa–Witten_invariants) and [Rozansky–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozansky–Witten_invariants) arise, with current research trying to determine how they expand and improve the original idea of Donaldson invariants.

## SU(2) Donaldson invariants {#SU(2)_Donaldson_invariants}

Let $X$ be an [oriented](wikipedia://en/187446) [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) [4-manifold](wikipedia://en/1362795) and $P\twoheadrightarrow X$ be a [principal SU(2)-bundle](wikipedia://en/80364907), then there exists a unique [homotopy class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class) represented by a classifying map $\tau\colon X\rightarrow\operatorname{BSU}(2)\cong\mathbb{H}P^\infty$ into the [classifying space for SU(2)](wikipedia://en/76359866) giving a pullback $P=\tau^*\operatorname{ESU}(2)=\tau^*S^\infty$. With the [Kronecker pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronecker_pairing) and the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) $[X]\in H_4(X,\mathbb{Z})$ from the orientation, the second [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) concretely gives a [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397):

$$\langle c_2(-),[X]\rangle\colon \operatorname{Prin}_{\operatorname{SU}(2)}(X)\xrightarrow\cong\mathbb{Z}.$$

Let $\mathcal{A}:=\Omega_{\operatorname{Ad}}^1(P,\mathfrak{su}(2))$ be the configuration space of connections on $P$, which is an [affine vector space](wikipedia://en/298834) not canonically isomorphic to $\Omega^1(X,\operatorname{Ad}(P))$. Let $\mathcal{G}:=\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{SU}(2)}(P)$ be the [gauge group](wikipedia://en/39240530) of [gauge transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformations) of $P$, which acts on $\mathcal{A}$. Let $\mathcal{B}:=\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$ be the [orbit space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_space),[^o17][^o18] then there is a [weak homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_homotopy_equivalence) $\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\operatorname{Map}_P(X,\operatorname{BSU}(2))$ in the homotopy class of the classifying map of $P$.[^o19][^o20] [Currying](wikipedia://en/6600) it corresponds to a map $\mathcal{B}\times X\rightarrow\operatorname{BSU}(2)$ and through pullback a principal $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-bundle $\widetilde{P}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{B}\times X$.[^o21] For every gauge class $[A]\in\mathcal{B}$, one has $\widetilde{P}\vert_{\{[A]\}\times X}\cong P\twoheadrightarrow X$. With the second Chern class $c_2(\widetilde{P})\in H^4(\mathcal{B}\times X,\mathbb{Z})$, the [slant product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slant_product) yields a map:[^o22][^o23][^o24][^o18]

$$\mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{Z}), \mu(\alpha):=c_2(\widetilde{P})/\alpha.$$

*Image: Donaldson's Theorem cobordism.png.* Visualization[^o25][^o26] of the five-dimensional self-dual Yang–Mills moduli space from the proof of Donaldson's theorem

With the subset $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{B}$ of an (anti) self-dual [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) there is an induced map $H^2(\mathcal{B},\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})$, which through postcomposition yields a map $\mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})$. With the interpretation of the homology classes as embedded surfaces $i\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow M$ with $i_*[\Sigma]\in H_2(M,\mathbb{Z})$ and the interpretation of the cohomology classes as [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) of second order using [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) $H^2(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})\cong H_\mathrm{dR}^2(\mathcal{M})$, one can see the map $\mu$ as mapping embedded surfaces in $M$ to differential forms of second order in $\mathcal{M}$. Donaldson invariants are defined using the integration of these differential forms, which is disrupted by the missing compatness and the singularities of the moduli space. A compactification $\mathcal{M}\subset\overline\mathcal{M}$ can be constructed using [Uhlenbeck's compactness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhlenbeck's_compactness_theorem) and leads to a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear map $\mu\colon H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^2(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})$.[^o27]

Let $P_k\twoheadrightarrow X$ be the unique principal $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-bundle with $\langle c_2(P_k),[X]\rangle=k$ according to the above group isomorphism, then $\langle p_1\operatorname{Ad}(P_k),[X]\rangle=-4k$ for the first Pontrjagin class of the [adjoint vector bundle](wikipedia://en/9943932) and the virtual dimension of its anti self-dual Yang–Mills (ASDYM) moduli space is given with the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752) by:[^o28][^o29][^o30]

$$\dim\mathcal{M}_k =-8k-3(1-b_1(M)+b_2^+(M)).$$

If $-b_1(M)+b_2^+(M)$ is odd, then the virtual dimension is an even number $\dim\mathcal{M}_k =2d_k$,[^o30] potentially negative given the choice of $k$. With a fundamental class $\left[\mathcal{M}_k\right]\in H_{2d_k}(\mathcal{M}_k,\mathbb{Z}) \cong\mathbb{Z}^{b_0\left(\mathcal{M}_k\right)}$ since the ASDYM moduli space is orientable, the Donaldson invariant is given with the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) and the Kronecker pairing:[^o30][^o29]

$$\mathcal{D}_{X,k}\colon \operatorname{Sym}^{d_k}H_2(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}, \mathcal{D}_{X,k}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d_k}):=\left\langle\mu(\alpha_1)\smile\ldots\smile\mu(\alpha_{d_k}), \left[\mathcal{M}_k\right]\right\rangle.$$

Considering the second homology classes in a symmetric setting only up to [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) comes from the symmetry of the cup product, hence the irrelevance of any order. It can furthermore be shown, that the Donaldson invariants also don't depend on the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) and therefore only on the smooth structure.

## Change of instanton number {#Change_of_instanton_number}

According to the above formula, $\dim\mathcal{M}_{k+1}=\dim\mathcal{M}_k+8$ holds under a change of the instanton number, expanding the input of the Donaldson invariant by four new second homology classes. More generally, the slant product yields a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear map $\mu\colon H_n(X,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{4-n}(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})$,[^o24][^o32][^o33] so for $X$ connected with $H_0(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ a map $\mu\colon\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow H^4(\overline\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Z})$. Hence one can also expand the Donaldson invariant by two images of a generator.

## SO(3) Donaldson invariants {#SO(3)_Donaldson_invariants}

Let $P\twoheadrightarrow X$ be a [principal SO(3)-bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_SO%283%29-bundle), then there exists a unique homotopy class represented by a classifying map $\tau\colon X\rightarrow\operatorname{BSO}(3)$ into the [classifying space for SO(3)](wikipedia://en/76359810) giving a pullback $P=\tau^*\operatorname{ESO}(3)$. Now the second [Stiefel–Whitney class](wikipedia://en/877761) and the first [Pontrjagin class](wikipedia://en/385944) give a group isomorphism:

$$(w_2,\langle p_1(-),[X]\rangle)\colon \operatorname{Prin}_{\operatorname{SO}(3)}(X)\xrightarrow\cong\{(v,n)\in H^2(X,\mathbb{Z}_2)\times\mathbb{Z}|\langle v^2,[X]\rangle\equiv n\operatorname{mod}4\}.$$

In this case, the first Pontrjagin class replaces the second Chern class in the slant product.

## Donaldson invariants of compactification {#Donaldson_invariants_of_compactification}

### Compactification to two dimensions {#Compactification_to_two_dimensions}

According to the classification of surfaces, every oriented closed 2-manifold (surface) is homeomorphic to a Sigma surface $\Sigma_g=\# gT^2$, given as a connected sum of 2-tori $T^2=S^1\times S^1$ with the convention $\Sigma_0=S^2$. Now the Donaldson invariants can be considered on the 4-manifold $\Sigma_g\times S^2$.

### Compactification to three dimensions {#Compactification_to_three_dimensions}

For an oriented closed 3-manifold $Y$, one can consider the Donaldson invariants on the 4-manifold $Y\times S^1$. For general structure groups, they relate to the [Rozansky–Witten invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozansky–Witten_invariant) of $Y$ and for the structure group $\operatorname{SU}(2)$, they relate to the [Casson–Walker–Lescop invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casson–Walker–Lescop_invariant) of $Y$.
