# Transition-rate matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transition-rate matrix"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **transition-rate matrix** (also known as a **Q-matrix**,[^n1] **intensity matrix**,[^o3] or **[infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437) matrix**[^o4]) is an array of numbers describing the instantaneous rate at which a [continuous-time Markov chain](wikipedia://en/1125342) transitions between states.

:::

In a transition-rate matrix $Q$ (sometimes written $A$[^o5]), element $q_{ij}$ (for $i \neq j$) denotes the rate departing from $i$ and arriving in state $j$. The rates $q_{ij} \geq 0$, and the diagonal elements $q_{ii}$ are defined such that

$$q_{ii} = -\sum_{j\neq i} q_{ij},$$

and therefore the rows of the matrix sum to zero.

Up to a global sign, a large class of examples of such matrices is provided by the [Laplacian of a directed, weighted graph](wikipedia://en/1448472). The vertices of the graph correspond to the Markov chain's states.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The transition-rate matrix has following properties:[^o8]

- There is at least one eigenvector with a vanishing eigenvalue, exactly one if the graph of $Q$ is strongly connected.
- All other eigenvalues $\lambda$ fulfill $0 > \mathrm{Re}\{\lambda\} \geq 2 \min_i q_{ii}$.
- All eigenvectors $v$ with a non-zero eigenvalue fulfill $\sum_{i}v_{i} = 0$.
- The Transition-rate matrix satisfies the relation $Q=P'(0)$ where P(t) is the continuous [stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/217313).

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

An [M/M/1 queue](wikipedia://en/14032611), a model which counts the number of jobs in a queueing system with arrivals at rate λ and services at rate μ, has transition-rate matrix

$$Q=\begin{pmatrix} -\lambda & \lambda \\ \mu & -(\mu+\lambda) & \lambda \\ &\mu & -(\mu+\lambda) & \lambda \\ &&\mu & -(\mu+\lambda) & \ddots &\\ &&&\ddots&\ddots \end{pmatrix}.$$

:::
