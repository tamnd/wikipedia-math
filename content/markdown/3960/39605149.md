# Fractional-order system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fractional-order system"}

In the fields of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), a **fractional-order system** is a dynamical system that can be modeled by a [fractional differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_differential_equation) containing [derivatives of non-integer order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_derivative).[^o2] Such systems are said to have *fractional dynamics*. [Derivatives and integrals of fractional orders](wikipedia://en/237864) are used to describe objects that can be characterized by [power-law](wikipedia://en/24522) nonlocality,[^o3] power-law [long-range dependence](wikipedia://en/1516694) or [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) properties. Fractional-order systems are useful in studying the anomalous behavior of dynamical systems in physics, [electrochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemistry), biology, [viscoelasticity](wikipedia://en/711288) and [chaotic systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaotic_system).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A general dynamical system of fractional order can be written in the form[^o6]

$$H(D^{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_m})(y_1,y_2,\ldots,y_l) = G(D^{\beta_1,\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_n})(u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_k)$$

where $H$ and $G$ are functions of the [fractional derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_derivative) operator $D$ of orders $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_m$ and $\beta_1,\beta_2,\ldots,\beta_n$ and $y_i$ and $u_j$ are functions of time. A common special case of this is the [linear time-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time-invariant) (LTI) system in one variable:

$$\left(\sum_{k=0}^m a_k D^{\alpha_k}\right) y(t) = \left(\sum_{k=0}^n b_k D^{\beta_k}\right) u(t)$$

::: {#o7 .definition cue=dfn name="commensurate"}

::: {#o8 .definition cue=dfn name="rational"}

The orders $\alpha_k$ and $\beta_k$ are in general complex quantities, but two interesting cases are when the orders are ***commensurate***,

$$\alpha_k, \beta_k = k \delta, \quad \delta \in R^+,$$

and when they are also ***rational***:

$$\alpha_k, \beta_k = k \delta, \quad \delta = \frac{1}{q}, q \in Z^+.$$

:::

:::

When $q=1$, the derivatives are of integer order and the system becomes an [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753). Thus by increasing specialization, LTI systems can be of general order, commensurate order, rational order or integer order.

### Transfer function {#Transfer_function}

By applying a [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) to the LTI system above, the [transfer function](wikipedia://en/31146) becomes

$$G(s) = \frac{Y(s)}{U(s)} = \frac{\sum_{k=0}^n b_k s^{\beta_k}}{\sum_{k=0}^m a_k s^{\alpha_k}}$$

For general orders $\alpha_k$ and $\beta_k$ this is a non-rational transfer function. Non-rational transfer functions cannot be written as an expansion in a finite number of terms (e.g., a [binomial expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_expansion) would have an infinite number of terms) and in this sense fractional orders systems can be said to have the potential for unlimited memory.[^o6]

:::

## Motivation to study fractional-order systems {#Motivation_to_study_fractional-order_systems}

Exponential laws are a classical approach to study dynamics of population densities, but there are many systems where dynamics undergo faster or slower-than-exponential laws. In such case the anomalous changes in dynamics may be best described by [Mittag-Leffler functions](wikipedia://en/1603459).[^o11]

[Anomalous diffusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalous_diffusion) is one more dynamic system where fractional-order systems play significant role to describe the anomalous flow in the diffusion process.

[Viscoelasticity](wikipedia://en/711288) is the property of material in which the material exhibits its nature between purely elastic and pure fluid. In case of real materials the relationship between stress and strain given by [Hooke's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooke's_law) and [Newton's law](wikipedia://en/18963754) both have obvious disadvances. So [G. W. Scott Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._W._Scott_Blair) introduced a new relationship between stress and strain given by

$$\sigma (t)=E{D_t^\alpha} \varepsilon(t), \quad 0<\alpha<1.$$

In [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295), it has been observed that chaos occurs in [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) of order 3 or more. With the introduction of fractional-order systems, some researchers study chaos in the system of total order less than 3.[^o12]

In [neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience), it has been found that single rat neocortical [pyramidal neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_neurons) adapt with a time scale that depends on the time scale of changes in stimulus statistics. This multiple time scale adaptation is consistent with fractional order differentiation, such that the neuron's firing rate is a fractional derivative of slowly varying stimulus parameters.[^o13]

## Analysis of fractional differential equations {#Analysis_of_fractional_differential_equations}

Consider a fractional-order [initial value problem](wikipedia://en/498383):

$${_0^C D_t^\alpha} x(t)=f(t,x(t)) , \quad t\in [0,T], \quad x(0)=x_0, \quad 0<\alpha<1.$$

::: {#o16 .statement cue=heading name="Existence and uniqueness"}

### Existence and uniqueness {#Existence_and_uniqueness}

Here, under the continuity condition on function f, one can convert the above equation into corresponding integral equation.

$$x(t)=x_0 + {_0^C D_t^{-\alpha}}f(t,x(t)) = x_0 + \frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t \frac{f(s,x(s))\,ds}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}},$$

One can construct a solution space and define, by that equation, a continuous self-map on the solution space, then apply a [fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/1011848), to get a [fixed-point](wikipedia://en/449738), which is the solution of above equation.

:::

### Numerical simulation {#Numerical_simulation}

For numerical simulation of solution of the above equations, Kai Diethelm has suggested fractional linear multistep [Adams–Bashforth method](wikipedia://en/2090865#Families_of_multistep_methods) or [quadrature methods](wikipedia://en/170089#Quadrature_rules_based_on_interpolating_functions).[^o18]
