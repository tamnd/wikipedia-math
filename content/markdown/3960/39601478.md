# Alexey Stakhov

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexey Stakhov"}

**Alexey Petrovich Stakhov** (Алексей Петрович Стахов Олексій Петрович Стахов; 7 May 1939 – 25 January 2021) was a Ukrainian-Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer), who has made contributions to the theory of [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers) and the "[Golden Section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Section)" and their applications in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022) theory and technology. [Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_science) of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) (1972), [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) (1974). Author of over 500 publications, 14 books and 65 international patents.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born May 7, 1939, in Partizany, [Kherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kherson) region, Ukraine, [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR). In 1956 graduated with honours from [Rivne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivne) village high school. Same year became a student of the [Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) Faculty of the [Kyiv Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Polytechnic_Institute) (now the [National Technical University of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Ukraine) “Kyiv Polytechnic Institute”). In 1959, transferred to the [Radio Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Engineering) Faculty of Kharkiv Aviation Institute (now the [National Aerospace University of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aerospace_University_–_Kharkiv_Aviation_Institute)). After graduation, worked for two years as an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) in the Kharkiv Electrical Instrument Design Bureau (now the space technology company “[Khartron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartron)”). "[Khartron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartron)" was one of the top secret space companies of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). It was engaged in the research, development and manufacture of automatic control systems for [missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missiles) and [space craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_craft) on board systems. Through working there Stakhov obtained thorough practical engineering experience and published his first scientific papers. Later he worked at the universities of Russia and Ukraine (Kharkiv Institute of [Radio Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Electronics), [Taganrog Radio Engineering Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_University), [Vinnytsia Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Technical_University), [Vinnytsia Agricultural University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Agricultural_University), Vinnytsia Pedagogic University). He was a visiting professor at many universities abroad (Austria, Germany, [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya), [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique)). Since 2004, he lived and worked in Canada.

Stakhov died on 25 January 2021, at the age of 81.[^o5]

## Teaching, research, and work {#Teaching,_research,_and_work}

- Dean of the Faculty of [Computer Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Engineering) of the Kharkiv Institute of Radio Electronics (now, [Kharkiv National University of Radio Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_National_University_of_Radioelectronics)), 1968–1970
- Head of the Department of Informational and Measuring [Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [Taganrog Radio Engineering Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_University), 1971–1977
- Head of the Department of [Computer Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Engineering), Vinnytsia Polytechnic Institute (now – Vinnytsia National Technical University), 1977–1988
- Director of the Special Design and Technological Bureau "Module" of the Vinnytsia Polytechnic Institute, 1986–1989
- Head of the Department of [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) and [Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing) Systems, Vinnytsia Technical University, 1989–1996
- Head of the Department of Computer Science, Vinnytsia State Agricultural University (now Vinnytsia National Agrarian University), 1997–2004
- Member of the [Shevchenko Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shevchenko_Scientific_Society) in Canada (2005)
- Professor Emeritus of [Taganrog University of Radio Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_University)
- Since 2003, president of the International Club of the Golden Section, and from 2005, Director of the Golden Section Institute, Academy of Trinitarizm (Russia).
- Initiated the creation of the so-called "Slavic Golden Group" (Kyiv, 1992) and was Scientific Director of the International Congress on the Mathematics of Harmony ([Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa), 2010).
- In 2009 he was awarded the Medal "Knight of Arts and Sciences" (Russian Academy of Natural Sciences)

## Brief description of research and scientific achievements {#Brief_description_of_research_and_scientific_achievements}

*Image: STAKHOV INT PATENTS.png.* Prof. **Stakhov's international patents**

*Image: STAKHOV MINIMALFORM PATENT.png.* US Patent for the **Fibonacci *p*-code minimal form**

- Created a new direction in the measurement theory, the [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) [measurement](wikipedia://en/19022) [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) described in his book – A.P. Stakhov. "Introduction into Algorithmic Measurement Theory" ([Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow): Soviet Radio, 1977).
- Created the theory of the redundant [number systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_system) based on the generalised [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_numbers) ([Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) *p*-codes) and the generalised [golden proportions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_section) (codes of the golden *p*-proportion) The principals of the [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) *p*-codes and the new computer arithmetic based on them are described in his book – A.P. Stakhov. "Introduction into Algorithmic Measurement Theory" (Moscow: Soviet Radio, 1977). The fundamentals of the theory of the codes of the [golden](wikipedia://en/12386) *p*-proportion are stated in the book – AP Stakhov. The Codes of the Golden Proportion ([Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), Radio and Communication, 1984).
- Put forward the concept of "Fibonacci computers". The international priority of this invention was protected by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in an unprecedented patenting operation by Soviet standards, resulting in 65 international patents, covering the territories of the United States, Japan, United Kingdom, France, Germany, Canada and other countries.
- Elaborated and evolved the concept "[Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of [Harmony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmony)",[^o8] which goes back in its foundations to [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s “[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)” (III c. BC), "[Divina Proportione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divina_Proportione)" (Divine proportion) by [Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473) (1509), [Harmonice Mundi](wikipedia://en/1264842) (Harmony of the World) by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) (1619). For the first time the concept of "Mathematics of Harmony" in relation to the achievements of modern science was presented by Prof. Stakhov in a speech "The Golden Section and Modern Harmony Mathematics," made at the seventh International Conference "[Fibonacci Numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_Numbers) and Their Applications" (Austria, [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz), 1996). The book "The Mathematics of Harmony. From Euclid to Contemporary Mathematics and Computer Science" – 748 pages ([World Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scientific), 2009) is Stakhov's main scientific achievement in this field.[^o9]
- Published over 500 scientific works, among them 14 books, 65 international patents, 130 [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) invention certificates. During his work in Canada (2004–2012) published over 30 articles in international journals ([Chaos, Solitons & Fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos,_Solitons_&_Fractals), Applied Mathematics, [Arc Combinatoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Combinatoria_%28journal%29), Visual Mathematics, etc.).
- Alexey Stakhov prepared 30 [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) students, five of Stakhov's PhD students, became [Doctors of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences).
