# Compact embedding

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact embedding"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the notion of being **compactly embedded** expresses the idea that one set or space is "well contained" inside another. There are versions of this concept appropriate to general [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). The notation for "$X$ is compactly embedded in $Y$" is $X \subset\subset Y$, or $X \Subset Y$.

:::

When used in functional analysis, compact embedding is usually about [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces) of functions.

Several of the [Sobolev embedding theorems](wikipedia://en/4665038) are compact embedding theorems.

When an embedding is not compact, it may possess a related, but weaker, property of [cocompactness](wikipedia://en/49395621).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Topological spaces {#Topological_spaces}

Let $X$ be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), and let $V$ and $W$ be [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of $X$. We say that $V$ is **compactly embedded** in $W$ if

- $V \subseteq \operatorname{Cl}(V) \subseteq \operatorname{Int}(W)$, where $\operatorname{Cl}(V)$ denotes the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of $V$, and $\operatorname{Int}(W)$ denotes the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $W$; and
- $\operatorname{Cl}(V)$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

For [Hausdorff spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_spaces), this is equivalent to there existing some compact set $K$ such that $V \subseteq K \subseteq \operatorname{Int}(W)$.

### Normed spaces {#Normed_spaces}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) with norms $\| \cdot \|_X$ and $\| \cdot \|_Y$ respectively, and suppose that $X \subseteq Y$. We say that $X$ is **compactly embedded** in $Y$, if

- $X$ is [continuously embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_embedded) in $Y$; i.e., there is a constant $C$ such that $\|x\|_Y \le C \|x\|_X$ for all $x$ in $X$; and
- The embedding of $X$ into $Y$ is a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441): any [bounded set](wikipedia://en/48258) in $X$ is [totally bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded) in $Y$, i.e. every [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) in such a bounded set has a [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) that is [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/6085) in the norm $\| \cdot \|_Y$.

### Banach spaces {#Banach_spaces}

If $Y$ is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), an equivalent definition is that the embedding operator (the identity) $i \colon X \to Y$ is a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441).
