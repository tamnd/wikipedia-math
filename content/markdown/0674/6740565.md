# Helly's selection theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helly's selection theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Helly's selection theorem** (also called the *Helly selection principle*) states that a uniformly bounded sequence of monotone real functions admits a [convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence) [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098). In other words, it is a sequential compactness theorem for the space of uniformly bounded monotone functions. It is named for the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Eduard Helly](wikipedia://en/2202570). A more general version of the theorem asserts compactness of the space BV<sub>loc</sub> of functions locally of [bounded total variation](wikipedia://en/330673) that are [uniformly bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_bounded) at a point.

:::

The theorem has applications throughout [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the result implies compactness of a [tight family of measures](wikipedia://en/6553354).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>)<sub>*n* ∈ **N**</sub> be a sequence of increasing functions mapping a real interval **I** into the real line **R**, and suppose that it is uniformly bounded: there are *a,b* ∈ **R** such that *a* ≤ *f*<sub>*n*</sub> ≤ *b* for every *n* ∈ **N**. Then the sequence (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>)<sub>*n* ∈ **N**</sub> admits a pointwise convergent subsequence.

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The proof requires the basic facts about [monotonic functions](wikipedia://en/48260#Some_basic_applications_and_results): An increasing function *f* on an interval I has at most countably many points of discontinuity.

### Step 1. Inductive Construction of a subsequence converging at discontinuities and rationals (diagonal process). {#Step_1._Inductive_Construction_of_a_subsequence_converging_at_discontinuities_and_rationals_(diagonal_process).}

Let $A_n = \{ x \in I; f_n(y) \not\rightarrow f_n(x) \text{ as } y \to x \}$ be the set of discontinuities of $f_n$; each of these sets are countable by the above basic fact. The set $A := \left( \textstyle\bigcup_{n\in \mathbb{N}} A_n \right) \cup (I \cap \mathbb{Q})$ is countable, and it can be denoted as $\{a_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$.

By the uniform boundedness of $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ and the [Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem](wikipedia://en/31211), there is a subsequence $\{f^{(1)}_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ such that $\{f^{(1)}_n(a_1)\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges. Suppose $\{f^{(k)}_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ has been chosen such that $\{f^{(k)}_n(a_i)\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges for $i = 1,\dots,k$, then by uniform boundedness and Bolzano–Weierstrass, there is a subsequence $\{f^{(k+1)}_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ of $\{f^{(k)}_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ such that $\{f^{(k)}_n(a_{k+1})\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges, thus $\{f^{(k+1)}_n(a_i)\}_{n=1}^\infty$ converges for $i = 1,\dots,k+1$.

Let $g_k=f^{(k)}_k$, then $\{g_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ is a subsequence of $\{f_n\}_{n=1}^\infty$ that converges pointwise everywhere in $A$.

### Step 2. *g<sub>k</sub>* converges in I except possibly in an at most countable set. {#Step_2._gk_converges_in_I_except_possibly_in_an_at_most_countable_set.}

Let $h_k(x)=\sup_{a \leq x , a \in A} g_k(a)$, then, *h<sub>k</sub>(a)=g<sub>k</sub>(a)* for *a*∈**A**, *h<sub>k</sub>* is increasing, let $h(x)=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}h_k(x)$, then h is increasing, since supremes and limits of increasing functions are increasing, and $h(a)=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}g_k(a)$ for *a*∈ **A** by **Step 1**. Moreover, *h* has at most countably many discontinuities.

We will show that *g<sub>k</sub>* converges at all continuities of *h*. Let *x* be a continuity of *h*, *q,r*∈ A, *q\<x\<r*, then $g_k(q)-h(r)\leq g_k(x)-h(x)\leq g_k(r)-h(q)$,hence

$$\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(x)-h(x)\bigr)\leq \limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(r)-h(q)\bigr)=h(r)-h(q)$$

$$h(q)-h(r)=\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(q)-h(r)\bigr)\leq \liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(x)-h(x)\bigr)$$

Thus,

$$h(q)-h(r)\leq\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(x)-h(x)\bigr)\leq \limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(g_k(x)-h(x)\bigr)\leq h(r)-h(q)$$

Since *h* is continuous at *x*, by taking the limits $q\uparrow x, r\downarrow x$, we have $h(q),h(r)\rightarrow h(x)$, thus $\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}g_k(x)=h(x)$

### Step 3. Choosing a subsequence of *g<sub>k</sub>* that converges pointwise in I {#Step_3._Choosing_a_subsequence_of_gk_that_converges_pointwise_in_I}

This can be done with a diagonal process similar to **Step 1**.

With the above steps we have constructed a subsequence of (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>)<sub>*n* ∈ **N**</sub> that converges pointwise in **I.**

:::

## Generalisation to BV<sub>loc</sub> {#Generalisation_to_BVloc}

Let *U* be an [open subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_subset) of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) and let *f*<sub>*n*</sub> : *U* → **R**, *n* ∈ **N**, be a sequence of functions. Suppose that (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>) has uniformly [bounded](wikipedia://en/330673) [total variation](wikipedia://en/683561) on any *W* that is [compactly embedded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactly_embedded) in *U*. That is, for all sets *W* ⊆ *U* with [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) *W̄* ⊆ *U*,

$$\sup_{n \in \mathbf{N}} \left( \| f_{n} \|_{L^{1} (W)} + \| \frac{\mathrm{d} f_{n}}{\mathrm{d} t} \|_{L^{1} (W)} \right) < + \infty,$$

where the derivative is taken in the sense of [tempered distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29).

Then, there exists a [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) *f*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub>, *k* ∈ **N**, of *f*<sub>*n*</sub> and a function *f* : *U* → **R**, locally of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673), such that

- *f*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub> converges to *f* pointwise [almost everywhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_everywhere_convergence);
- and *f*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub> converges to *f* locally in *L*<sup>1</sup> (see [locally integrable function](wikipedia://en/4258398)), i.e., for all *W* compactly embedded in *U*,

$\lim_{k \to \infty} \int_{W} \big| f_{n_{k}} (x) - f(x) \big| \, \mathrm{d} x = 0;$ [^o10]

- and, for *W* compactly embedded in *U*,

$\left\| \frac{\mathrm{d} f}{\mathrm{d} t} \right\|_{L^{1} (W)} \leq \liminf_{k \to \infty} \left\| \frac{\mathrm{d} f_{n_{k}}}{\mathrm{d} t} \right\|_{L^{1} (W)}.$[^o10]

## Further generalizations {#Further_generalizations}

There are many generalizations and refinements of [Helly's theorem](wikipedia://en/1409506). The following theorem, for BV functions taking values in [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), is due to Barbu and Precupanu:

Let *X* be a [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091), [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and let *E* be a closed, [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) subset of *X*. Let Δ : *X* → \[0, +∞) be [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite) and [homogeneous of degree one](wikipedia://en/622844). Suppose that *z*<sub>*n*</sub> is a uniformly bounded sequence in BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) with *z*<sub>*n*</sub>(*t*) ∈ *E* for all *n* ∈ **N** and *t* ∈ \[0, *T*\]. Then there exists a subsequence *z*<sub>*n*<sub>*k*</sub></sub> and functions *δ*, *z* ∈ BV(\[0, *T*\]; *X*) such that

- for all *t* ∈ \[0, *T*\],

$$\int_{[0, t)} \Delta (\mathrm{d} z_{n_{k}}) \to \delta(t);$$

- and, for all *t* ∈ \[0, *T*\],

$$z_{n_{k}} (t) \rightharpoonup z(t) \in E;$$

- and, for all 0 ≤ *s* \< *t* ≤ *T*,

$$\int_{[s, t)} \Delta(\mathrm{d} z) \leq \delta(t) - \delta(s).$$
