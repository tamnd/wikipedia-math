# Ronald C. Read

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ronald C. Read"}

**Ronald Cedric Read** (19 December 1924 – 7 January 2019) was a British mathematician, latterly a professor emeritus of mathematics at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), Canada. He published many books[^o3] and papers, primarily on enumeration of [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), [graph isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577), [chromatic polynomials](wikipedia://en/1460126), and particularly, the use of computers in graph-theoretical research. [Read's conjecture](wikipedia://en/71238169) was proved after more than 40 years by [June Huh](wikipedia://en/54434626) in 2009. A majority of Read's later work was done in Waterloo. Read received his Ph.D. (1959) in graph theory from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).[^o4]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Ronald Read served in the Royal Navy during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), then completed a degree in mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) before joining the University College of the West Indies (later [the University of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_the_West_Indies)) in Jamaica as the second founding member of the Mathematics Department there. In 1970 he moved his family to Canada to take up a post as Professor of Mathematics at the University of Waterloo, Ontario, Canada.

While in Jamaica he became interested in cave exploration, and in 1957 he founded the Jamaica Caving Club.

He had a lifelong interest in the making of string figures and is the inventor of the Olympic Flag String Figure.[^o6]

He was an accomplished musician and played many instruments including violin, viola, cello, double bass, piano, guitar, lute, and many early music instruments, some of which he also built. He had diplomas in Theory and in Composition from the Royal Conservatory of Music in Toronto, Canada, and composed four works for orchestra and several pieces for smaller groups.[^o7] Read died in January 2019 at the age of 94.[^o8]
