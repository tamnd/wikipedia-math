# Richard S. Ellis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard S. Ellis"}

**Richard Steven Ellis** (May 15, 1947 – July 2, 2018) was an American mathematician.

:::

He was born on May 15, 1947, in [Brookline, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookline,_Massachusetts), to parents Murray and Helen. Ellis graduated from [Boston Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Latin_School) and attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he studied German literature and mathematics. He pursued graduate study at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences). In 1972, he received his PhD from the [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) for his thesis *Chapman-Eskog-Hilbert Expansion for Models of the Boltzmann Equation* under the supervision of [Henry McKean](wikipedia://en/35051081).[^o3] He began teaching at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) and left for the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) in 1975.[^o4] In 1984, he improved a key result in [large deviations theory](wikipedia://en/3772909) originally due to [Jürgen Gärtner](wikipedia://en/62362388), which is now known as Gärtner-Ellis Theorem. He was named fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1999.[^o5][^o6] Ellis died of bile duct cancer in New York City on July 2, 2018, aged 71.[^o7]
