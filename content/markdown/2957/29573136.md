# Brauer–Fowler theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer–Fowler theorem"}

In [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/41890#Finite_group_theory), the **Brauer–Fowler theorem**, [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by (Brauer Fowler 1955), states that if a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) *G* has even [order](wikipedia://en/493527) *g* > 2 then it has a proper [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of order greater than *g*<sup>1/3</sup>. The technique of the proof is to count [involutions](wikipedia://en/293450#Group_theory) (elements of order 2) in *G*. Perhaps more important is another result that the authors derive from the same count of involutions, namely that up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) there are only a finite number of [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) with a given [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of an involution. This suggested that finite simple groups could be classified by studying their centralizers of involutions, and it led to the discovery of several [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806). Later it motivated a part of the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).
