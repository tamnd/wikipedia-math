# Jean-François Callet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-François Callet"}

**Jean-François Callet** (25 October 1744 – 14 November 1798) was a French professor of mathematics who wrote an influential compilation of [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860) tables and taught [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405) and navigation.

:::

Callet was born in Versailles and became a professor of hydrographic engineering.[^o2] His most influential work was *Tables Portatives de Logarithmes*, published in 1795: a compilation of logarithmic tables, based on the work of [William Gardiner](wikipedia://en/49461780), which remained in print until 1906. Its roughly 800 pages give logarithms of the sine, cosine and tangent, as well as natural sines and cosines.[^o3][^o4] It was influential, leading to other improved tables such as those of [Edward Sang](wikipedia://en/30059562).[^o5] Another of Callet's works was a spherical trigonometry text for use in navigation, published in 1798 and written in collaboration with instrument maker Jean François Richer, who was attempting to create a navigation instrument called the trigonometric compass in his application for the Abbé Raynal Prize. The prize was then being offered for a reliable and accurate method to reduce the apparent distance between two stars to the true distance, which could be used to determine the longitude of a ship's position. Richer's application won the 1791 prize.[^o6]
