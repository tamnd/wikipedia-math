# Cayley's formula

*Image: Cayley's formula 2-4.svg.* The complete list of all trees on 2,3,4 labeled vertices: $2^{2-2}=1$ tree with 2 vertices, $3^{3-2}=3$ trees with 3 vertices and $4^{4-2}=16$ trees with 4 vertices.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cayley's formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Cayley's formula** is a result in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) named after [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034). It states that for every [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) $n$, the number of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) on $n$ labeled [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) is $n^{n-2}$.

:::

The formula equivalently counts the [spanning trees](wikipedia://en/455770) of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) with labeled vertices .

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Many proofs of Cayley's tree formula are known.[^o4] One classical proof of the formula uses [Kirchhoff's matrix tree theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchhoff's_matrix_tree_theorem), a formula for the number of spanning trees in an arbitrary graph involving the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859). [Prüfer sequences](wikipedia://en/1650168) yield a [bijective proof](wikipedia://en/917006) of Cayley's formula. Another bijective proof, by [André Joyal](wikipedia://en/9157736), finds a one-to-one transformation between *n*-node trees with two distinguished nodes and maximal directed [pseudoforests](wikipedia://en/13511542). A proof by [double counting](wikipedia://en/296942) due to Jim Pitman counts in two different ways the number of different sequences of directed edges that can be added to an [empty graph](wikipedia://en/1520758) on n vertices to form from it a rooted tree; see Double counting (proof technique).

:::

## History {#History}

The formula was first discovered by [Carl Wilhelm Borchardt](wikipedia://en/482522) in 1860, and proved via a [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468).[^o6] In a short 1889 note, Cayley extended the formula in several directions, by taking into account the degrees of the vertices.[^o7] Although he referred to Borchardt's original paper, the name "Cayley's formula" became standard in the field.

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

Cayley's formula immediately gives the number of labelled rooted [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_%28graph_theory%29) on *n* vertices, namely $(n + 1)^{n - 1}$. Each labelled rooted forest can be turned into a labelled tree with one extra vertex, by adding a vertex with label $n + 1$ and connecting it to all roots of the trees in the forest.

There is a close connection with rooted forests and [parking functions](wikipedia://en/76711561), since the number of parking functions on *n* cars is also $(n + 1)^{n - 1}$. A [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between rooted forests and parking functions was given by [M. P. Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337) in 1968.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The following generalizes Cayley's formula to labelled forests: Let *T*<sub>*n*,*k*</sub> be the number of labelled forests on *n* vertices with *k* connected components, such that vertices 1, 2, ..., *k* all belong to different connected components. Then $T_{n,k} = k n^{n - k - 1}$.[^o12]

:::
