# Legendre chi function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Legendre chi function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Legendre chi function** (named after [Adrien-Marie Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695)) is a [special function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) whose [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) is also a [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258), given by

$$\chi_\nu(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)^\nu}.$$

*Image: Legendre chi function.svg.* Legendre chi function

:::

As such, it resembles the Dirichlet series for the [polylogarithm](wikipedia://en/482471), and, indeed, is trivially expressible as the [odd part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_part_of_a_function) of the polylogarithm

$$\chi_\nu(z) = \frac{1}{2}\left[\operatorname{Li}_\nu(z) - \operatorname{Li}_\nu(-z)\right].$$

The Legendre chi function appears as the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811), with respect to the order *ν*, of the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952), and also of the [Euler polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_polynomial), with the explicit relationships given in those articles.

The Legendre chi function is a special case of the [Lerch transcendent](wikipedia://en/1342978), and is given by

$$\chi_\nu(z)=2^{-\nu}z\,\Phi (z^2,\nu,1/2).$$

## Identities {#Identities}

$$\chi_{-1}\left(x\right)=\frac{x\left(1+x^{2}\right)}{\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{2}}$$

$$\chi_{0}\left(x\right)=\frac{x}{1-x^{2}}$$

$$\chi_1(x) =\operatorname{arctanh}(x) =\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)$$

$$\chi_2(x) = \int_{0}^{x}\frac{\operatorname{arctanh}(t)}t \mathrm dt$$

$$\chi_\infty(x) = x$$

$$\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\chi_\nu(x) = \frac{\chi_{\nu-1}(x)}{x}$$

$$\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\chi_2(x) = \frac{\operatorname{arctanh}(x)}{x}$$

$$\chi_2(x) + \chi_2(1/x)=\left\{\begin{array}{rcl} \frac{1}{4}\bigl(\ln^2(x)-\ln^2(-x)\bigr) & \text{for} & |x|>1\\[4pt] \frac{1}{4}\bigl(\ln^2(1/x)-\ln^2(-1/x)\bigr) & \text{for} & |x|\le1\setminus\{0\} \end{array}\right.$$

$$\chi_2(x)+\chi_2\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)=\frac{\pi^2}{8}+\ln(x)\operatorname{arctanh}(x), \quad x\in(0,1)$$

## Special Values {#Special_Values}

It takes the special values:

$$\chi_2(1) = \frac{\pi^2}{8}$$

$$\chi_2(-1) = -\frac{\pi^2}{8}$$

$$\chi_2(\sqrt{2}-1) = \frac{\pi^2}{16}-\frac14\ln^2(\sqrt{2}+1)$$

$$\chi_2\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi^2}{12}-\frac{3}{4}\ln^2\left(\varphi\right), ~ \text{where} ~ \varphi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}, \text{ which is the golden ratio.}$$

$$\chi_2(\sqrt{5}-2) = \frac{\pi^2}{24}-\frac{3}{4}\ln^2\left(\varphi\right)$$

$$\chi_2(i) = iK,$$

where $i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) and *K* is [Catalan's constant](wikipedia://en/46860).[^o4] Other special values include:

$$\chi_n(1) = \lambda(n)$$

$$\chi_n(i) = i\beta(n),$$

where $\lambda(n)$ is the [Dirichlet lambda function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_lambda_function) and $\beta(n)$ is the [Dirichlet beta function](wikipedia://en/2216061).[^o4]

## Integral relations {#Integral_relations}

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \arcsin (r \sin \theta)\, \mathrm d\theta = \chi_2(r), \qquad\int_0^{\pi/2} \arccos (r \cos \theta)\, \mathrm d\theta = \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^2-\chi_2(r)\qquad {\rm if}~~|r|\leq 1$$

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \arctan (r \sin \theta) \,\mathrm d\theta = -\frac{1}{2}\int_0^{\pi} \frac{ r \theta \cos \theta}{1+ r^2 \sin^2 \theta} \,\mathrm d\theta = 2 \chi_2\!\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+r^2}- 1}{r}\right),\quad r\ne0$$

$$\int_0^{\pi/2} \arctan (p \sin \theta)\arctan (q \sin \theta) \,\mathrm d\theta = \pi \chi_2\!\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{1+p^2}- 1}{p}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1+q^2}- 1}{q}\right), \quad p,q\ne0$$

$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\operatorname{arctanh}(p\sin\theta)\operatorname{arctanh}(q\sin\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta=\pi\chi_2\!\!\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-p^2}}{p}\cdot\frac{1-\sqrt{1-q^2}}{q}\right),\quad |p|,|q|\le1\setminus\{0\}$$

$$\int_0^{\alpha}\!\!\int_0^{\beta} \frac{\mathrm dx\, \mathrm dy}{1-x^2 y^2} = \chi_2(\alpha\beta)\qquad {\rm if}~~|\alpha\beta|\leq 1$$
