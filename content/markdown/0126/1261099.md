# Alexander McAulay

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander McAulay"}

**Alexander McAulay** (9 November 1863 – 5 July 1931) was a mathematician and physicist who was the first professor of mathematics and physics at the [University of Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tasmania), [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart), Australia.[^o3] He was also a proponent of [dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/8730871), which he termed "octonions" or "Clifford biquaternions".

:::

## Life {#Life}

McAulay was born on 9 November 1863 and attended [Kingswood School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingswood_School) in Bath. He proceeded to [Caius College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caius_College,_Cambridge), there taking up a study of the [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) algebra. In 1883 he published an article "Some general theorems in quaternion integration".[^o5] McAulay took his degree in 1886, and began to reflect on the instruction of students in quaternion theory. In an article "Establishment of the fundamental properties of quaternions",[^o7] he suggested improvements to the texts then in use. He also wrote a technical article[^o8] on [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_%28mathematics%29).

Departing for [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), he became a lecturer and tutor in mathematics at [Ormond College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormond_College,_Melbourne), University of Melbourne, from 1888 to 1895, being appointed first lecturer of mathematics in 1893.[^o3] As a distant correspondent, he participated in a vigorous debate about the place of quaternions in [physics education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_education).[^o9] In 1893 his book *Utility of Quaternions in Physics* was published. [A. S. Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._S._Hathaway) contributed a positive review[^o10] and [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait) praised it in these terms:

In 1896, McAulay took up the position of first professor of mathematics and physics at the newly-founded [University of Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tasmania). He ceased teaching in 1924 due to incipient blindness and was selected as research professor until his retirement in 1929.[^o3]

Following [William Kingdon Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820), who had extended quaternions to [dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/8730871), McAulay made a special study of this [hypercomplex number](wikipedia://en/51438) system. In 1898 McAulay published, through [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press), his *Octonions: a Development of Clifford's Biquaternions*.[^o12]

McAulay died from cerebral haemorrhage on 5 July 1931, in his home at [Sandy Bay, Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Bay,_Tasmania), and was buried in Hobart.[^o3]

His brother [Francis Sowerby Macaulay](wikipedia://en/3592252), who stayed in England, also contributed to [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424). The University of Tasmania has commemorated the McAulays' contributions in *Winter Public Lectures*.[^o13]
