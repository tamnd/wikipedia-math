# Georg Kreisel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Kreisel"}

**Georg Kreisel** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (September 15, 1923 – March 1, 2015)[^o3] was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria)-born [mathematical logician](wikipedia://en/19636) who studied and worked in the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) and [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kreisel was born in [Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz) and came from a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) background; his family sent him to the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) before the [Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) in 1938. He studied mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), and then, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), worked on [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) subjects. Kreisel never took a Ph.D., though much later, in 1962, he was awarded the Cambridge degree of [Sc.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science#United_Kingdom,_Ireland,_India,_Pakistan_and_the_Commonwealth), a 'higher doctorate' given on the basis of published research.[^o5]

He taught at the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) from 1949 until 1954 and then worked at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1955 to 1957. He returned to Reading in 1957, but then taught at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) from 1958 to 1959. Then back at Reading for the year 1959–1960, and then the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) 1960–1962. Kreisel was appointed a professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1962 and remained on the faculty there until he retired in 1985.[^o6]

Kreisel worked in various areas of logic,[^o8] and especially in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), where he is known for his so-called "[unwinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unwinding_%28proof_theory%29)" program, whose aim was to extract [constructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29) content from superficially non-constructive [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285).[^o9]

Kreisel was elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1966;[^o7] Kreisel remained a close friend of [Francis Crick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Crick)[^o10][^o11] whom he had met in the Royal Navy during WWII.[^o12]

While a student at Cambridge, Kreisel's philosophical abilities were highly respected by [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741). [Ray Monk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Monk) writes, "In 1944—when Kreisel was still only twenty-one—Wittgenstein shocked [Rush Rhees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_Rhees) by declaring Kreisel to be the most able philosopher he had ever met who was also a mathematician."[^o13]

Kreisel was also a close friend of the Anglo-Irish philosopher and novelist [Iris Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_Murdoch). They met at Cambridge in 1947 during Murdoch's year of study there. [Peter Conradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_J._Conradi) reports that Murdoch transcribed Kreisel's letters into her journals over the next fifty years. According to Conradi, "For half a century she nonetheless records variously Kreisel's brilliance, wit and sheer 'dotty' solipsistic strangeness, his amoralism, cruelty, ambiguous vanity and obscenity." Murdoch dedicated her 1971 novel *[An Accidental Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Accidental_Man)* to Kreisel and he became a (partial) model for several characters in other novels, including Marcus Vallar in *[The Message to the Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Message_to_the_Planet)* and Guy Openshaw in *[Nuns and Soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuns_and_Soldiers)*.[^o14]

After retirement Kreisel lived in [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), Austria. He wrote several biographies of mathematicians including [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736),[^o15] [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)[^o16] and [Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer).[^o17] He died in Salzburg, aged 91.

## Anecdotes {#Anecdotes}

When Kreisel was teaching at the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) he would frequently take the train into London. There was a particularly fast train that was timed just right for the shows in London, and he would go to the station at that time. He checked the timetable one day, and that train was canceled. But out of habit one day he showed up at the station at the usual time and the train was there. It seems that it was going from [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol) to London, stopping at Reading only to get water. He got on the train anyway, and from then on took that train regularly. One day he was accosted by the conductor after he got on. "The train doesn't stop here sir!" "In that case I didn't get on here."[^o19]
