# Pitman–Yor process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pitman–Yor process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **Pitman–Yor process**[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] denoted PY(*d*, *θ*, *G*<sub>0</sub>), is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) whose sample path is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543). A random sample from this process is an infinite discrete probability distribution, consisting of an infinite set of atoms drawn from *G*<sub>0</sub>, with weights drawn from a two-parameter [Poisson-Dirichlet distribution](wikipedia://en/71307878). The process is named after [Jim Pitman](wikipedia://en/73269493) and [Marc Yor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Yor).

:::

The parameters governing the Pitman–Yor process are: 0 ≤ *d* \< 1 a discount parameter, a strength parameter *θ* > −*d* and a base distribution *G*<sub>0</sub> over a probability space *X*. When *d* = 0, it becomes the [Dirichlet process](wikipedia://en/8330403). The discount parameter gives the Pitman–Yor process more flexibility over tail behavior than the Dirichlet process, which has exponential tails. This makes Pitman–Yor process useful for modeling data with [power-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-law) tails (e.g., word frequencies in natural language).

The exchangeable random partition induced by the Pitman–Yor process is an example of a [Chinese restaurant process](wikipedia://en/4482900#Two-parameter_generalization), a [Poisson–Kingman partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson–Kingman_partition), and of a [Gibbs type random partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbs_type_random_partition).

The Pitman-Yor process is used to model the observation process of words, or species, etc. It is useful because it can generate phenomena with [heavy-tailed distributions](wikipedia://en/8092200).

## Naming conventions {#Naming_conventions}

The name "Pitman–Yor process" was coined by Ishwaran and James[^o7] after Pitman and Yor's review on the subject.[^o3] However the process was originally studied in Perman et al.[^o8][^o9]

It is also sometimes referred to as the two-parameter Poisson–Dirichlet process, after the two-parameter generalization of the Poisson–Dirichlet distribution which describes the joint distribution of the sizes of the atoms in the [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846), sorted by strictly decreasing order.
