# Joel Hass

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Joel Hass"}

**Joel Hass** (born January 3, 1956) is an American mathematician and professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis). His research focuses on low-dimensional geometry and topology, including [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257), [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734), [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), and computational complexity questions in topology.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hass was born in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv) on January 3, 1956, to Julian and Aliza Hass, and attended public schools in Queens, New York, after his family emigrated to the United States. He spent parts of his childhood in England, and later entered [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) before transferring to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o2] Hass received a B.A. from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1976, an M.A. from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1978, and a Ph.D. from Berkeley in 1981.[^o7] His doctoral thesis, *Minimal surfaces in low dimensional manifolds*, was supervised by [Robion Kirby](wikipedia://en/2932424).

After completing his doctorate, Hass held postdoctoral and visiting positions at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). He joined the faculty of the University of California, Davis in 1988, became professor in 1994, and served as chair of the UC Davis Department of Mathematics from 2010 to 2014.[^o7][^o9]

Hass was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1990-1991, 2000-2001, and 2015-2016, and a research professor at MSRI in 2006. He also held visiting positions at the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_-_Israel_Institute_of_Technology), the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), and the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem).[^o2][^o7] From 2017 to 2020 he directed the UC Davis branch of [COSMOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COSMOS), a California summer program for high school students in mathematics and science.[^o9] In 2021 he was one of the founders of the [Association for Mathematical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Mathematical_Research) and served as its first president.[^o9]

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Hass's work centers on low-dimensional topology and geometry. With [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) and [Peter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Scott_%28mathematician%29), he proved foundational results on least-area incompressible surfaces in 3-manifolds.[^o11]

Hass also constructed examples of bounded 3-manifolds admitting negatively curved metrics with concave boundary, a result highlighted in Robion Kirby's biography as an unexpected example in 3-dimensional geometry.[^o2]

With Roger Schlafly, Hass proved the equal-volume case of the [double bubble conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bubble_conjecture), showing that the standard double bubble minimizes surface area among enclosures of two equal volumes in three-dimensional Euclidean space.[^o12]

Hass has also worked on algorithmic problems in knot theory and 3-manifold topology. With [Jeffrey Lagarias](wikipedia://en/4327781) and [Nicholas Pippenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Pippenger), he proved that the [unknotting problem](wikipedia://en/4956355) is in [NP](wikipedia://en/21562).[^o13] With Lagarias, he gave an exponential upper bound on the number of [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374) needed to transform a diagram of the unknot into a standard circle.[^o14] With [Ian Agol](wikipedia://en/32084718) and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190), he proved that knot genus in 3-manifolds is NP-complete.[^o15]

His later work includes applications of geometry and topology to biological shape analysis, including work with Patrice Koehl on conformal methods for comparing genus-zero surfaces and shapes arising from bones, proteins, and teeth.[^o5][^o2]
