# James Milne (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Milne"}

**James S. Milne** (born 10 October 1942) is a New Zealand mathematician working in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Milne attended high school in [Invercargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invercargill) in [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand) until 1959, and then studied at the [University of Otago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Otago) in [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) (B.A. 1964) and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (Masters 1966, Ph.D. 1967 under [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933)). From then to 1969 he was a lecturer at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). After that he was at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), as Assistant Professor (1969–1972), Associate Professor (1972–1977), Professor (1977–2000), and Professor Emeritus (since 2000). He has also been a visiting professor at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London), at the [Institut des hautes études scientifiques](wikipedia://en/14527) in Paris (1975, 1978), at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) (1986–87), and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) (1976–77, 1982, 1988).

In his dissertation, entitled "The conjectures of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer for constant abelian varieties over function fields," he proved the [conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer](wikipedia://en/435161) for constant [abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_varieties) over [function fields in one variable over a finite field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_function_field).[^o3] He also gave the first examples of nonzero abelian varieties with finite [Tate–Shafarevich group](wikipedia://en/25711736). He went on to study [Shimura varieties](wikipedia://en/10389861) (certain [hermitian symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/4127357), low-dimensional examples being [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552)) and [motives](wikipedia://en/430680).

For 2025 Milne was awarded the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Mathematical Exposition of the American Mathematical Society.[^o4]

His students include Piotr Blass, Michael Bester, Matthew DeLong, Pierre Giguere, William Hawkins Jr, Matthias Pfau, Victor Scharaschkin, Stefan Treatman, Anthony Vazzana, and Wafa Wei.

Milne is also an avid mountain climber.
