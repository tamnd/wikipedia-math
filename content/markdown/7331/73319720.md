# Quasi-free algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-free algebra"}

In abstract algebra, a **quasi-free algebra** is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) that satisfies the lifting property similar to that of a [formally smooth algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formally_smooth_algebra) in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). The notion was introduced by [Cuntz](wikipedia://en/38543091) and [Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) for the applications to [cyclic homology](wikipedia://en/1460172).[^o2] A quasi-free algebra generalizes a [free algebra](wikipedia://en/494669), as well as the [coordinate ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_ring) of a smooth affine [complex curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_curve). Because of the latter generalization, a quasi-free algebra can be thought of as signifying smoothness on a [noncommutative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_space).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* be an associative algebra over the complex numbers. Then *A* is said to be *quasi-free* if the following equivalent conditions are met:[^o6][^o7][^o8]

- Given a [square-zero extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-zero_extension) $R \to R/I$, each homomorphism $A \to R/I$ lifts to $A \to R$.
- The cohomological dimension of *A* with respect to [Hochschild cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochschild_cohomology) is at most one.

Let $(\Omega A, d)$ denotes the [differential envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_envelope) of *A*; i.e., the universal [differential-graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863) generated by *A*.[^o9][^o10] Then *A* is quasi-free if and only if $\Omega^1 A$ is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) as a [bimodule](wikipedia://en/492612) over *A*.[^o6]

There is also a characterization in terms of a connection. Given an *A*-bimodule *E*, a [right connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_connection) on *E* is a linear map

$$\nabla_r : E \to E \otimes_A \Omega^1 A$$

that satisfies $\nabla_r(as) = a \nabla_r(s)$ and $\nabla_r(sa) = \nabla_r(s) a + s \otimes da$.[^o11] A left connection is defined in the similar way. Then *A* is quasi-free if and only if $\Omega^1 A$ admits a right connection.[^o12]

:::

## Properties and examples {#Properties_and_examples}

One of basic properties of a quasi-free algebra is that the algebra is left and right [hereditary](wikipedia://en/5123176) (i.e., a [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of a projective left or right module is projective or equivalently the left or right global dimension is at most one).[^o14] This puts a strong restriction for algebras to be quasi-free. For example, a hereditary (commutative) [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is precisely a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132). In particular, a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a field is quasi-free if and only if the number of variables is at most one.

An analog of the [tubular neighborhood theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubular_neighborhood_theorem), called the *formal tubular neighborhood theorem*, holds for quasi-free algebras.[^o15]
