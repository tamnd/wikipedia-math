# Strong duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strong duality"}

**Strong duality** is a condition in [mathematical optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization) in which the primal optimal objective and the [dual](wikipedia://en/3372717) optimal objective are equal. By definition, strong duality holds if and only if the [duality gap](wikipedia://en/33328430) is equal to 0. This is opposed to [weak duality](wikipedia://en/34390896) (the primal problem has optimal value greater than or equal to the dual problem, in other words the [duality gap](wikipedia://en/33328430) is greater than or equal to zero).

:::

## Sufficient conditions {#Sufficient_conditions}

Each of the following conditions is sufficient for strong duality to hold:

- $F = F^{**}$ where $F$ is the [perturbation function](wikipedia://en/33430345) relating the primal and dual problems and $F^{**}$ is the [biconjugate](wikipedia://en/1001490) of $F$ (follows by construction of the [duality gap](wikipedia://en/33328430))
- $F$ is convex and lower [semi-continuous](wikipedia://en/57824) (equivalent to the first point by the [Fenchel–Moreau theorem](wikipedia://en/35099255))
- the primal problem is a [linear optimization problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_optimization)
- [Slater's condition](wikipedia://en/33295435) for a [convex optimization problem](wikipedia://en/1674411).[^o3][^o4]

## Strong duality and computational complexity {#Strong_duality_and_computational_complexity}

Under certain conditions (called "constraint qualification"), if a problem is polynomial-time solvable, then it has strong duality (in the sense of [Lagrangian duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_duality)). It is an open question whether the opposite direction also holds, that is, if strong duality implies polynomial-time solvability.[^o6]
