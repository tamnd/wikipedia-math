# Emanuel Lodewijk Elte

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Emanuel Lodewijk Elte"}

**Emanuel Lodewijk Elte** (16 March 1881 in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) – 9 April 1943 in [Sobibór](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobibor_extermination_camp))[^o2] was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is noted for discovering and classifying semiregular [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) in dimensions four and higher.

:::

Elte's father Hartog Elte was headmaster of a school in Amsterdam. Emanuel Elte married Rebecca Stork in 1912 in Amsterdam, when he was a teacher at a high school in that city. By 1943 the family lived in [Haarlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haarlem). When on January 30 of that year a German officer was shot in that town, in reprisal a hundred inhabitants of Haarlem were transported to the [Camp Vught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzogenbusch_concentration_camp), including Elte and his family. As Jews, he and his wife were further deported to Sobibór, where they were murdered; his two children were murdered at [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz).[^o2]

## Elte's semiregular polytopes of the first kind {#Elte's_semiregular_polytopes_of_the_first_kind}

His work rediscovered the finite [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875) of [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset), and further allowing not only regular [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647), but recursively also allowing one or two semiregular ones. These were enumerated in his 1912 book, *The Semiregular Polytopes of the Hyperspaces*.[^o4] He called them *semiregular polytopes of the first kind*, limiting his search to one or two types of regular or semiregular *k*-faces. These polytopes and more were rediscovered again by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter), and renamed as a part of a larger class of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).[^o5] In the process he discovered all the main representatives of the exceptional E<sub>*n*</sub> family of polytopes, save only [1<sub>42</sub>](wikipedia://en/19091290) which did not satisfy his definition of semiregularity.
