# Trisectrix of Maclaurin

*Image: Maclaurin animated2.gif.* Maclaurin's trisectrix as the locus of the intersection of two rotating lines

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trisectrix of Maclaurin"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **trisectrix of Maclaurin** is a [cubic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) notable for its [trisectrix](wikipedia://en/2909893) property, meaning it can be used to [trisect an angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisect_an_angle). It can be defined as [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of the point of [intersection of two lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_of_two_lines), each rotating at a uniform rate about separate points, so that the ratio of the rates of rotation is 1:3 and the lines initially coincide with the line between the two points. A generalization of this construction is called a [sectrix of Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/18635842). The curve is named after [Colin Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/7824) who investigated the curve in 1742.

:::

## Equations {#Equations}

Let two lines rotate about the points $P = (0,0)$ and $P_1 = (a, 0)$ so that when the line rotating about $P$ has angle $\theta$ with the *x* axis, the rotating about $P_1$ has angle $3\theta$. Let $Q$ be the point of intersection, then the angle formed by the lines at $Q$ is $2\theta$. By the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222),

$${r \over \sin 3\theta} = {a \over \sin 2\theta}\!$$

so the equation in [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinate) is (up to translation and rotation)

$$r= a \frac{\sin 3\theta}{\sin 2\theta} = {a \over 2} \frac{4 \cos^2 \theta - 1} {\cos \theta} = {a \over 2} (4 \cos \theta - \sec \theta).\!$$

The curve is therefore a member of the [conchoid of de Sluze](wikipedia://en/898316) family.

In [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) the equation of this curve is

$$2x(x^2+y^2)=a(3x^2-y^2).\!$$

Solving for x, we get

$$x=\frac{\frac{1}{2}a(3x^2-y^2) }{x^2+y^2}$$

Solving for y, we get

$$y=\sqrt{\frac{a(3x^2-y^2) }{2x}-x^2}$$

If the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) is moved to (*a*, 0) then a derivation similar to that given above shows that the equation of the curve in polar coordinates becomes

$$r = 2a \cos{\theta \over 3},\!$$

making it an example of a limacon with a loop.

## The trisection property {#The_trisection_property}

*Image: MaclaurinTrisectrix.SVG.* The trisectrix of Maclaurin showing the angle trisection property

Given an angle $\phi$, draw a ray from $(a, 0)$ whose angle with the $x$-axis is $\phi$. Draw a ray from the origin to the point where the first ray intersects the curve. Then, by the construction of the curve, the angle between the second ray and the $x$-axis is $\phi/ 3$.

## Notable points and features {#Notable_points_and_features}

The curve has an [x-intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) at $3a \over 2$ and a [double point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point) at the origin. The vertical line $x={-{a \over 2}}$ is an [asymptote](wikipedia://en/3107). The curve intersects the line x = a, or the point corresponding to the trisection of a right angle, at $(a,{\pm {1 \over \sqrt{3}} a})$. As a [nodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Node_%28singularity%29) cubic, it is of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) zero.

## Relationship to other curves {#Relationship_to_other_curves}

The trisectrix of Maclaurin can be defined from [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_sections) in three ways. Specifically:

- It is the [inverse](wikipedia://en/15040455) with respect to the unit circle of the [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052)

$$2x=a(3x^2-y^2).$$

- It is [cissoid](wikipedia://en/899085) of the circle

$$(x+a)^2+y^2 = a^2$$

and the line $x={a \over 2}$ relative to the origin.

- It is the [pedal](wikipedia://en/845864) with respect to the origin of the [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231)

$$y^2=2a(x-\tfrac{3}{2}a).$$

In addition:

- The inverse with respect to the point $(a, 0)$ is the [Limaçon trisectrix](wikipedia://en/18635261).
- The trisectrix of Maclaurin is related to the [folium of Descartes](wikipedia://en/2737940) by [affine transformation](wikipedia://en/38449).
