# Kampyle of Eudoxus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kampyle of Eudoxus"}

*Image: Kampyle Eudoxus.png.* Graph of Kampyle of Eudoxus with *a* = 1

The **kampyle of Eudoxus** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek): καμπύλη \[γραμμή\], meaning simply "curved \[line\], curve") is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) with a [Cartesian equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_equation) of

:::

$$x^4 = a^2(x^2+y^2),$$

from which the solution *x* = *y* = 0 is excluded.

## Alternative parameterizations {#Alternative_parameterizations}

In [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates), the Kampyle has the equation

$$r = a\sec^2\theta.$$

Equivalently, it has a parametric representation as

$$x=a\sec(t), \quad y=a\tan(t)\sec(t).$$

## History {#History}

This [quartic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_curve) was studied by the Greek astronomer and mathematician [Eudoxus of Cnidus](wikipedia://en/48388) (c. 408 BC – c.347 BC) in relation to the classical problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Kampyle is symmetric about both the *x*- and *y*-axes. It crosses the *x*-axis at (±*a*,0). It has [inflection points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflection_points) at

$$\left(\pm a\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2},\pm a\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$$

(four inflections, one in each quadrant). The top half of the curve is asymptotic to $x^2/a-a/2$ as $x \to \infty$, and in fact can be written as

$$y = \frac{x^2}{a}\sqrt{1-\frac{a^2}{x^2}} = \frac{x^2}{a} - \frac{a}{2} \sum_{n=0}^\infty C_n\left(\frac{a}{2x}\right)^{2n},$$

where

$$C_n = \frac1{n+1} \binom{2n}{n}$$

is the $n$th [Catalan number](wikipedia://en/216488).

:::
