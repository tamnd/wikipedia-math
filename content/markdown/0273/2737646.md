# Bicorn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bicorn"}

*Image: Bicorn.svg.* Bicorn

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **bicorn**, also known as a **cocked hat curve** due to its resemblance to a [bicorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicorne), is a [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve) [quartic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve) defined by the equation[^o2]

$$y^2 \left(a^2 - x^2\right) = \left(x^2 + 2ay - a^2\right)^2.$$

It has two [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758) and is symmetric about the y-axis.[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

In 1864, [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) studied the curve

$$y^4 - xy^3 - 8xy^2 + 36x^2y+ 16x^2 -27x^3 = 0$$

in connection with the classification of [quintic equations](wikipedia://en/303368); he named the curve a bicorn because it has two cusps. This curve was further studied by [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) in 1867.[^o5]

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Bicorn-inf.jpg.* A transformed bicorn with *a* = 1

The bicorn is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of degree four and [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) zero. It has two cusp singularities in the real plane, and a double point in the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) at $(x=0, z=0)$. If we move $x=0$ and $z=0$ to the origin and perform an [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) rotation on $x$ by substituting $ix/z$ for $x$ and $1/z$ for $y$ in the bicorn curve, we obtain

$$\left(x^2 - 2az + a^2 z^2\right)^2 = x^2 + a^2 z^2.$$

This curve, a [limaçon](wikipedia://en/641715), has an ordinary double point at the origin, and two nodes in the complex plane, at $x= \pm i$ and $z=1$.[^o8]

The [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108) of a bicorn curve are

$$\begin{aligned} x &= a \sin\theta \\ y &= a \, \frac{(2 + \cos\theta) \cos^2\theta}{3 + \sin^2\theta} \end{aligned}$$

with $-\pi \le \theta \le \pi.$
