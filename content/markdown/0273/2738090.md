# Trident curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trident curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **trident curve** (also **trident of Newton** or **parabola of Descartes**) is any member of the family of [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) that have the [formula](wikipedia://en/164040):

:::

$$xy+ax^3+bx^2+cx=d.$$

*Image: Newton\_trident.svg.* Trident curve with $a=b=c=d=1$.

Trident curves are [cubic plane curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_plane_curve) with an ordinary double point in the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) at $x=0$, $y=1$, $z=0$. If we substitute $x=x/z$ and $y=1/z$ into the equation of the trident curve, we get

$$ax^3+bx^2z+cxz^2+xz = dz^3,$$

*Image: Newton trident y=inf.svg.* Trident curve at $y=\infty$ with $a=b=c=d=1$. This curve partially resembles the [folium of Descartes](wikipedia://en/2737940).

which has an ordinary double point at the origin. Trident curves are therefore [rational plane algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) of [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) zero.

Solving for $y$, we get

$$y=\frac{d}{x}-ax^2-bx-c.$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$x=\frac{d-ax^3-bx^2-cx}{y}.$$
