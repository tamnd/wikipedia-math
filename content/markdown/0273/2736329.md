# Port-Royal Logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Port-Royal Logic"}

*Image: 17380000-Port Royal-Logic-Title.jpg.* Seventh, 1738, edition of Port-Royal Logic

***Port-Royal Logic***, or *Logique de Port-Royal*, is the common name of *La logique, ou l'art de penser*, an important [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) on logic first published anonymously in 1662 by [Antoine Arnauld](wikipedia://en/228442) and [Pierre Nicole](wikipedia://en/228444), two prominent members of the [Jansenist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jansenism) movement, centered on [Port-Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-Royal-des-Champs). [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068) likely contributed considerable portions of the text. Its linguistic companion piece is the *[Port-Royal Grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port-Royal_Grammar)* (1660) by Arnauld and Lancelot.

:::

The book helped popularize the concept that a definition with more qualifications or features (the intension) denotes a class with fewer members (the extension), and vice versa. It was based on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s ideas about [*genus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_%28philosophy%29) and *species*. The same concept would later become a fundamental element in the works of [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281).

## Use as a textbook on Cartesian metaphysics and epistemology {#Use_as_a_textbook_on_Cartesian_metaphysics_and_epistemology}

Written in French, it became quite popular and was in use up to the twentieth century, introducing the reader to logic, and exhibiting strong [Cartesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesianism) elements in its [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) (Arnauld having been one of the main philosophers whose objections were published, with replies, in [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes)' *[Meditations on First Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditations_on_First_Philosophy)*). The *Port-Royal Logic* is sometimes cited as a paradigmatic example of traditional [term logic](wikipedia://en/321652).

## Impact {#Impact}

According to [Ian Hacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Hacking), the book was the "most influential logic book after Aristotle and before the end of the nineteenth century".[^o4]

The philosopher [Louis Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Marin_%28philosopher%29) particularly studied it in the 20th century (*La Critique du discours*, Éditions de Minuit, 1975), while [Michel Foucault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Foucault) considered it, in *[The Order of Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Order_of_Things)*, as one of the bases of the classical *[épistémè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Épistémè)*.

Among the contributions of the *Port-Royal Logic* is the popularization of the distinction between comprehension and [extension](wikipedia://en/39095), which would later become a more refined distinction between [intension](wikipedia://en/14570) and [extension](wikipedia://en/3934566).[^o5] Roughly speaking: a definition with more qualifications or features (the intension) denotes a class with fewer members (the extension), and vice versa. The main idea traces back through the [scholastic philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism) to [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s ideas about [*genus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_%28philosophy%29) and *species*,[^o6] and is fundamental in the philosophy of [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281).[^o7]

More recently, it has been related to mathematical [lattice theory](wikipedia://en/469304) in [formal concept analysis](wikipedia://en/313845), and independently formalized similarly by [Yu. Schreider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Anatolyevich_Schrader)'s group in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Russia),[^o8] [Jon Barwise](wikipedia://en/912858) & Jerry Seligman in *Information Flow*,[^o9] and others.
