# Hippopede

*Image: PedalCurve1.gif.* Hippopede (red) given as the [pedal curve](wikipedia://en/845864) of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) (black). The equation of this hippopede is: $4x^2 + y^2 = (x^2 + y^2)^2$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hippopede"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hippopede** (grc) is a [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) determined by an equation of the form

:::

$$(x^2+y^2)^2=cx^2+dy^2,$$

where it is assumed that $c > 0$ and $c > d$ since the remaining cases either reduce to a single point or can be put into the given form with a rotation. Hippopedes are [bicircular](wikipedia://en/24215692), [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024), [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) 4 and symmetric with respect to both the $x$ and $y$ axes.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

When *d* > 0 the curve has an oval form and is often known as an **oval of Booth**, and when *d* \< 0 the curve resembles a sideways figure eight, or [lemniscate](wikipedia://en/5210590), and is often known as a **lemniscate of Booth**, after 19th-century mathematician [James Booth](wikipedia://en/44180309) who studied them. Hippopedes were also investigated by [Proclus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclus) (for whom they are sometimes called **Hippopedes of Proclus**) and [Eudoxus](wikipedia://en/48388). For *d* = −*c*, the hippopede corresponds to the [lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908).

:::

## Definition as spiric sections {#Definition_as_spiric_sections}

*Image: Hippopede02.svg.* Hippopedes with *a* = 1, *b* = 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0.

*Image: Hippopede01.svg.* Hippopedes with *b* = 1, *a* = 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, and 2.0.

Hippopedes can be defined as the curve formed by the intersection of a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) and a plane, where the plane is parallel to the axis of the torus and tangent to it on the interior circle. Thus it is a [spiric section](wikipedia://en/5300614) which in turn is a type of [toric section](wikipedia://en/5350369).

If a circle with radius *a* is rotated about an axis at distance *b* from its center, then the equation of the resulting hippopede in [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinate)

$$r^2 = 4 b (a - b \sin^{2}\! \theta)$$

or in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate)

$$(x^2+y^2)^2+4b(b-a)(x^2+y^2)=4b^2x^2.$$

Note that when *a* > *b* the torus intersects itself, so it does not resemble the usual picture of a torus.
