# George Gibson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Gibson"}

*Image: George Gibson mathematician.jpg.* George Alexander Gibson

**George Alexander Gibson** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) LLD (19 April 1858 – 1 April 1930) was a Scottish mathematician and academic writer.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born on 19 April 1858 in [Greenlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlaw) in [Berwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwickshire) the third son of Robert Gibson JP (1830–1903). He attended the free church school in the parish, and showing great promise, went to the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) where he graduated with an MA in 1882 and immediately joined the university staff.

In 1889, aged 29, he was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [William Thomson, Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thomson,_Lord_Kelvin), [Prof William Jack](wikipedia://en/52790828), [Sir Thomas Muir](wikipedia://en/491426) and [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803). He served as the society's vice president from 1917 to 1920. He also served as president of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940).[^o3] In 1895 he became a professor of mathematics at the Glasgow and West of Scotland Technical College. In 1909 he returned to the University of Glasgow as a professor, replacing his mentor Prof William Jack.

Gibson lived on campus at 10 The university, Glasgow.[^o4] The [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) awarded him an honorary doctorate (LLD) in 1905 and the University of Glasgow did likewise in 1927, the year of his retiral.

## Death {#Death}

He died at [Scotstounhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotstounhill) in Glasgow on 1 April 1930.[^o6] On his death the Gibson Memorial Lecture was founded. The first lecturer in this series was [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736). Other lecturers include [Charles Coulson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Coulson) and [Edward Collingwood](wikipedia://en/9990753).[^o7]

## Family {#Family}

In 1890, he married Nellie Stenhouse Hunter, daughter of James D. Hunter, while still a student.[^o7] They had three children.
