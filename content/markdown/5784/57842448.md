# Vincent Calvez

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vincent Calvez"}

**Vincent Calvez** (born 24 August 1981) is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is currently a [directeur de recherche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_France) (senior researcher) at the Institute Camille Jordan at the [Claude Bernard University Lyon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard_University_Lyon_1). He is known for his work in mathematical modeling in biology, especially in the movement of bacteria.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Saint-Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Malo), he attended [lycee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycee) in [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France), he eventually gained admission to and attended [École normale supérieure (Paris)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29).

He obtained his [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation) in 2005 and did his thesis in mathematics under [Bernoit Perthame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoit_Perthame) at the [University of Paris 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_6), finishing in 2007. In 2008, he took a position as a chargé de recherche at the [École normale supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Lyon) in the Unité de mathématiques pures et appliquées (UMPA) in 2008. He defended his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 2015 and obtained the position of Directeur de Recherche at [Claude Bernard University Lyon 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard_University_Lyon_1) in 2016.

Since 2014, he has been an associate editor of the *[Journal of Mathematical Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Mathematical_Biology)*, and, since 2018, he has been an associate editor of the *SIAM Journal of Mathematical Analysis*.

Aside from research, Calvez is also involved in the popularization of mathematics by coordinating the travelling exhibition “Mathàlyon,” intended for junior high and high school students.

## Research {#Research}

Calvez's early research involved the development of models for the collective movements of bacteria via [chemotaxis](wikipedia://en/7403) using kinetic models. This model describes both the individual movements of bacteria and the transport of colonies as a whole, and satisfactorily explains the specific characters of this movement. Later, he worked in collaboration with biophysicists to find a new explanation for [cell polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_polarity) and in collaboration with ecologists on models for invasion fronts.
