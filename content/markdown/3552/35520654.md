# Tangential polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangential polygon"}

*Image: Tangential trapezoid.svg.* A tangential [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826)

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **tangential polygon**, also known as a **circumscribed polygon**, is a [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) that contains an [inscribed circle](wikipedia://en/4470203) (also called an *incircle*). This is a circle that is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to each of the polygon's sides. The [dual polygon](wikipedia://en/19865549) of a tangential polygon is a [cyclic polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon), which has a [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262) passing through each of its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

:::

All [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) are tangential, as are all [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) with any number of sides. A well-studied group of tangential polygons are the [tangential quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/9176412), which include the [rhombi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombi) and [kites](wikipedia://en/84840).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A convex polygon has an incircle [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) all of its internal [angle bisectors](wikipedia://en/152547#Angle_bisector) are [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157). This common point is the *incenter* (the center of the incircle).[^o4]

There exists a tangential polygon of *n* sequential sides of lengths *a*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *a*<sub>*n*</sub> if and only if the [system of equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_equations)

$$x_1+x_2=a_1,\quad x_2+x_3=a_2,\quad \ldots,\quad x_n+x_1=a_n$$

has a solution (*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>) in positive [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438).[^o13] If such a solution exists, then *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub> are the *tangent lengths* of the polygon (the lengths from the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) to the points where the incircle is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the sides).

:::

## Uniqueness and non-uniqueness {#Uniqueness_and_non-uniqueness}

If the number of sides *n* is odd, then for any given set of sidelengths $a_1, \dots , a_n$ satisfying the existence criterion above there is only one tangential polygon. But if *n* is even there are an infinitude of them.[^o6] For example, in the quadrilateral case where all sides are equal we can have a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) with any value of the acute angles, and all rhombi are tangential to an incircle.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Semiperimeter theorem"}

## Semiperimeter theorem {#Semiperimeter_theorem}

*Image: visual\_proof\_tangential\_polygon\_area\_is\_inradius\_times\_semiperimeter.svg.* [Proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) that the area of a tangential quadrilateral equals the product of its inradius and its semiperimeter – the dashed edges also show that the total length of even-numbered edges equal that of odd-numbered ones

In any tangential polygon (a polygon with an incircle), the area is given by:

$$K = pr$$

where $K$ is the area of the polygon, $p$ is its semiperimeter, and $r$ is the inradius.

Equivalently, the area can be expressed as:

$$K = \frac{1}{2} r \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i$$

where $a_1, \dots, a_n$ are the side lengths of the polygon.[^o9]

:::

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

- For a tangential polygon with an odd number of sides, all sides are equal if and only if all angles are equal (so the polygon is regular). A tangential polygon with an even number of sides has all sides equal if and only if the alternate angles are equal (that is, angles *A*, *C*, *E*, ... are equal, and angles *B*, *D*, *F*, ... are equal).[^o12]
- In a tangential polygon with an even number of sides, the sum of the odd numbered sides' lengths is equal to the sum of the even numbered sides' lengths.[^o13]
- A tangential polygon has a larger area than any other polygon with the same perimeter and the same interior angles in the same sequence.[^o14][^o15]
- The [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of any tangential polygon, the centroid of its boundary points, and the center of the inscribed circle are [collinear](wikipedia://en/3189581), with the polygon's centroid between the others and twice as far from the incenter as from the boundary's centroid.[^o14]

## Tangential triangle {#Tangential_triangle}

While all triangles are tangential to some circle, a triangle is called the [tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle) of a reference triangle if the tangencies of the tangential triangle with the circle are also the vertices of the reference triangle.
