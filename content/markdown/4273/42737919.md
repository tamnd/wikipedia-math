# Vitali–Carathéodory theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vitali–Carathéodory theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Vitali–Carathéodory theorem** is a result in [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478) that shows that, under the conditions stated below, [integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable) functions can be approximated in L<sup>1</sup> from above and below by lower- and [upper-semicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper-semicontinuous) functions, respectively. It is named after [Giuseppe Vitali](wikipedia://en/634990) and [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *X* be a [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) equipped with a [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322), μ, that is finite on every [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set), [outer regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_regular), and [tight](wikipedia://en/6553354) when restricted to any Borel set that is open or of finite mass. If *f* is an element of L<sup>1</sup>(μ) then, for every *ε* > 0, there are functions *u* and *v* on *X* such that *u* ≤ *f* ≤ *v*, *u* is upper-semicontinuous and bounded above, *v* is lower-semicontinuous and bounded below, and

$$\int_X (v - u) \,\mathrm{d}\mu < \varepsilon.$$
