# GNRS conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="GNRS conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Do minor-closed graph families have $\ell_1$ embeddings with bounded distortion?

:::

*Image: ℓ₁ embedding.svg.* An [isometric](wikipedia://en/254777) embedding of the weighted graph on the left into [$\ell_{1}$ space](wikipedia://en/45194). The [taxicab distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab_distance) between each vertex in the embedding exactly matches the [graph distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) between vertices in the weighted graph, meaning the *stretch factor* is 1 (and therefore it is isometric).

In [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) and [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), the **GNRS conjecture** connects the theory of [graph minors](wikipedia://en/353042), the [stretch factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor) of [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119), and the [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio) of [multi-commodity flow problems](wikipedia://en/6809151). It is named after Anupam Gupta, Ilan Newman, Yuri Rabinovich, and [Alistair Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alistair_Sinclair), who formulated it in 2004.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

One formulation of the conjecture involves embeddings of the [shortest path distances](wikipedia://en/41985) of weighted [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) into [$\ell_{1}$ spaces](wikipedia://en/45194), real [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) in which the distance between two vectors is the sum of their coordinate differences. If an embedding maps all pairs of vertices with distance $d$ to pairs of vectors with distance in the range $[cd,Cd]$ then its [stretch factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretch_factor) or distortion is the ratio $C/c$; an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) has stretch factor one, and all other embeddings have greater stretch factor.

The graphs that have an embedding with at most a given distortion are closed under [graph minor](wikipedia://en/353042) operations, operations that delete vertices or edges from a graph or contract some of its edges. The GNRS conjecture states that, conversely, every minor-closed family of graphs, other than the family of all graphs, can be embedded into an $\ell_1$ space with bounded distortion. That is, the distortion of graphs in the family is bounded by a constant that depends on the family but not on the individual graphs. For instance, the [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) are closed under minors. Therefore, it would follow from the GNRS conjecture that the planar graphs have bounded distortion.

An alternative formulation involves analogues of the [max-flow min-cut theorem](wikipedia://en/78130) for undirected [multi-commodity flow problems](wikipedia://en/6809151). The ratio of the maximum flow to the minimum cut, in such problems, is known as the *flow-cut gap*. The largest flow-cut gap that a flow problem can have on a given graph equals the distortion of the optimal $\ell_1$ embedding of the graph. Therefore, the GNRS conjecture can be rephrased as stating that the minor-closed families of graphs have bounded flow-cut gap.

## Related results {#Related_results}

Arbitrary $n$-vertex graphs (indeed, arbitrary $n$-point [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018)) have $\ell_1$ embeddings with distortion $O(\log n)$. Some graphs have logarithmic flow-cut gap, and in particular this is true for a multicommodity flow with every pair of vertices having equal demand on a bounded-degree [expander graph](wikipedia://en/9313). Therefore, this logarithmic bound on the distortion of arbitrary graphs is tight. [Planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) can be embedded with smaller distortion, $O(\sqrt{\log n})$.

Although the GNRS conjecture remains unsolved, it has been proven for some minor-closed graph families that bounded-distortion embeddings exist. These include the [series–parallel graphs](wikipedia://en/9938559) and the graphs of bounded [circuit rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_rank), the graphs of bounded [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142), the [2-clique-sums](wikipedia://en/17598460) of graphs of bounded size, and the [$k$-outerplanar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-outerplanar_graph).

In contrast to the behavior of metric embeddings into $\ell_1$ spaces, every finite metric space has embeddings into $\ell_2$ with stretch arbitrarily close to one by the [Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma](wikipedia://en/14855952), and into $\ell_\infty$ spaces with stretch exactly one by the [tight span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_span) construction.
