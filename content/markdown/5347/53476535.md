# Tangent–secant theorem

*Image: Sekanten tangenten.svg.* Beginning with the [alternate segment theorem](wikipedia://en/462730#Corollary), $\begin{array}{cl} \implies & \angle PG_2T = \angle PTG_1 \\[4pt] \implies & \triangle PTG_2 \sim \triangle PG_1T \\[4pt] \implies & \frac{|PT|}{|PG_2|}=\frac{|PG_1|}{|PT|} \\[2pt] \implies & |PT|^2=|PG_1|\cdot|PG_2| \end{array}$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangent–secant theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **tangent-secant theorem** describes the relation of [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) created by a [secant](wikipedia://en/241886) and a [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) line with the associated [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). This result is found as Proposition 36 in Book 3 of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107).

:::

Given a secant $g$ intersecting the circle at points $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ and a tangent $t$ intersecting the circle at point $T$ and given that $g$ and $t$ intersect at point $P$, the following equation holds:

$$|PT|^2=|PG_1|\cdot|PG_2|$$

The tangent-secant theorem can be proven using similar triangles (see graphic).

Like the [intersecting chords theorem](wikipedia://en/19359050) and the [intersecting secants theorem](wikipedia://en/19359084), the tangent-secant theorem represents one of the three basic cases of a more general theorem about two intersecting lines and a circle, namely, the [power of point theorem](wikipedia://en/2094044).
