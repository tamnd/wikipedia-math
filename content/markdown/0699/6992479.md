# Arf invariant of a knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arf invariant of a knot"}

In the mathematical field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Arf invariant** of a knot, named after [Cahit Arf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahit_Arf), is a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) obtained from a quadratic form associated to a [Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951). If *F* is a Seifert surface of a knot, then the [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) H<sub>1</sub>(*F*, **Z**/2**Z**) has a quadratic form whose value is the number of full twists mod 2 in a neighborhood of an embedded circle representing an element of the homology group. The [Arf invariant](wikipedia://en/6950659) of this quadratic form is the Arf invariant of the knot.

:::

## Definition by Seifert matrix {#Definition_by_Seifert_matrix}

Let $V = v_{i,j}$ be a [Seifert matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert_matrix) of the knot, constructed from a set of curves on a [Seifert surface](wikipedia://en/1343951) of genus *g* which represent a basis for the first [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of the surface. This means that *V* is a 2*g* × 2*g* matrix with the property that *V* − *V*<sup>T</sup> is a [symplectic matrix](wikipedia://en/173983). The *Arf invariant* of the knot is the residue of

$$\sum\limits^g_{i=1} v_{2i-1,2i-1} v_{2i,2i} \pmod 2.$$

Specifically, if $\{a_i, b_i\}, i = 1 \ldots g$, is a symplectic basis for the intersection form on the Seifert surface, then

$$\operatorname{Arf}(K) = \sum\limits^g_{i=1}\operatorname{lk}\left(a_i, a_i^+\right)\operatorname{lk}\left(b_i, b_i^+\right) \pmod 2.$$

where lk is the [link number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_number) and $a^+$ denotes the positive pushoff of *a*.

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Pass equivalence"}

## Definition by pass equivalence {#Definition_by_pass_equivalence}

This approach to the Arf invariant is due to [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman).

We define two knots to be **pass equivalent** if they are related by a finite sequence of pass-moves.[^o5]

Every knot is pass-equivalent to either the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) or the [trefoil](wikipedia://en/404773); these two knots are not pass-equivalent and additionally, the right- and left-handed trefoils are pass-equivalent.[^o6]

Now we can define the Arf invariant of a knot to be 0 if it is pass-equivalent to the unknot, or 1 if it is pass-equivalent to the trefoil. This definition is equivalent to the one above.

:::

## Definition by partition function {#Definition_by_partition_function}

[Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628) showed that the Arf invariant can be obtained by taking the partition function of an [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) on a [knot diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram).[^o8]

## Definition by Alexander polynomial {#Definition_by_Alexander_polynomial}

This approach to the Arf invariant is by Raymond Robertello.[^o10] Let

$$\Delta(t) = c_0 + c_1 t + \cdots + c_n t^n + \cdots + c_0 t^{2n}$$

be the [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) of the knot. Then the Arf invariant is the residue of

$$c_{n-1} + c_{n-3} + \cdots + c_r$$

modulo 2, where *r* = 0 for *n* odd, and *r* = 1 for *n* even.

Kunio Murasugi[^o11] proved that the Arf invariant is zero if and only if Δ(−1) ≡ ±1 modulo 8.

## Arf as knot concordance invariant {#Arf_as_knot_concordance_invariant}

From the Fox-Milnor criterion, which tells us that the Alexander polynomial of a [slice knot](wikipedia://en/4158126) $K \subset \mathbb{S}^3$ factors as $\Delta(t) = p(t) p\left(t^{-1}\right)$ for some polynomial $p(t)$ with integer coefficients, we know that the determinant $\left| \Delta(-1) \right|$ of a slice knot is a square integer. As $\left| \Delta(-1) \right|$ is an odd integer, it has to be congruent to 1 modulo 8. Combined with Murasugi's result, this shows that the Arf invariant of a slice knot vanishes.
