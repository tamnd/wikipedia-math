# Dysnomia (deity)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dysnomia"}

In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), **Dysnomia** (Δυσνομία)[^o2] is the [personification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personification) of [lawlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawlessness). According to Hesiod's *[Theogony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theogony)*, Dysnomia was the offspring of [Eris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eris_%28mythology%29) (Strife), with no father mentioned.[^o3] Like all of the children of Eris given by Hesiod, Dysnomia is a personified abstraction, allegorizing the meaning of their name, and representing one of the many harmful things which might be thought to result from discord and strife, with no other identity.[^o4]

:::

Hesiod associates Dysnomia with [Ate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ate_%28mythology%29) (Recklessness). He names both as offspring of Eris, on the same line (230) of his *Theogony*, and says that the two are "much like one another".[^o5]

## Solon {#Solon}

The [Athenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athenian) statesman [Solon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon) contrasted Dysnomia with [Eunomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eunomia), the personification of the ideal government:[^o7] This is what my heart bids me teach the Athenians, that Lawlessness \[Dysnomia\] brings the city countless ills, but Lawfulness \[Eunomia\] reveals all that is orderly and fitting, and often places fetters round the unjust.[^o8] Solon makes Dysnomia the cause of the "countless" evils besetting Athens: greed, the injustice of the city's leaders, the slavery of the poor, and civil war.[^o9]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

In 2005, Dysnomia was chosen as [the name for the moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysnomia_%28moon%29) of the dwarf planet [Eris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eris_%28dwarf_planet%29).[^o11]
