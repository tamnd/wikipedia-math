# Hypercomplex manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypercomplex manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **hypercomplex manifold** is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) with the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) equipped with an [action](wikipedia://en/276410) by [the algebra of quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternions) in such a way that the quaternions $I, J, K$ define integrable [almost complex structures](wikipedia://en/514258).

:::

If the almost complex structures are instead not assumed to be integrable, the manifold is called quaternionic, or almost hypercomplex.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [hyperkähler manifold](wikipedia://en/646120) is also hypercomplex. The converse is not true. The [Hopf surface](wikipedia://en/7016769)

$$\bigg({\mathbb H}\backslash 0\bigg)/{\mathbb Z}$$

(with ${\mathbb Z}$ acting as a multiplication by a quaternion $q$, $|q|>1$) is hypercomplex, but not [Kähler](wikipedia://en/390538), hence not [hyperkähler](wikipedia://en/646120) either. To see that the Hopf surface is not Kähler, notice that it is [diffeomorphic](wikipedia://en/8564) to a product $S^1\times S^3,$ hence its odd cohomology group is odd-dimensional. By [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition), odd cohomology of a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) are always even-dimensional. In fact Hidekiyo Wakakuwa proved [^o5] that on a compact [hyperkähler manifold](wikipedia://en/646120) $\ b_{2p+1}\equiv 0 \ mod \ 4$. [Misha Verbitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misha_Verbitsky) has shown that any compact hypercomplex manifold admitting a Kähler structure is also hyperkähler.[^o6]

In 1988, left-invariant hypercomplex structures on some compact [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) were constructed by the physicists Philippe Spindel, Alexander Sevrin, Walter Troost, and Antoine Van Proeyen. In 1992, [Dominic Joyce](wikipedia://en/1120202) rediscovered this construction, and gave a complete classification of left-invariant hypercomplex structures on compact Lie groups. Here is the complete list.

$$T^4, SU(2l+1), T^1 \times SU(2l), T^l \times SO(2l+1),$$

$$T^{2l}\times SO(4l), T^l \times Sp(l), T^2 \times E_6,$$

$$T^7\times E^7, T^8\times E^8, T^4\times F_4, T^2\times G_2$$

where $T^i$ denotes an $i$-dimensional compact torus.

It is remarkable that any [compact Lie group](wikipedia://en/750326) becomes hypercomplex after it is multiplied by a sufficiently big torus.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

Hypercomplex manifolds as such were studied by Charles Boyer in 1988. He also proved that in real dimension 4, the only compact hypercomplex manifolds are the complex torus $T^4$, the [Hopf surface](wikipedia://en/7016769) and the [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116).

Much earlier (in 1955) [Morio Obata](wikipedia://en/80481508) studied [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690) associated with *almost hypercomplex structures* (under the former terminology of [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108)[^o9] of *almost quaternionic structures*). His construction leads to what [Edmond Bonan](wikipedia://en/39597334) called the *Obata connection*[^o10][^o11] which is *torsion free*, if and only if, "two" of the almost complex structures $I, J, K$ are integrable and in this case the manifold is hypercomplex.

:::

## Twistor spaces {#Twistor_spaces}

There is a 2-dimensional sphere of quaternions $L\in{\mathbb H}$ satisfying $L^2=-1$. Each of these quaternions gives a complex structure on a hypercomplex manifold *M*. This defines an almost complex structure on the manifold $M\times S^2$, which is fibered over ${\mathbb C}P^1=S^2$ with fibers identified with $(M, L)$. This complex structure is integrable, as follows from Obata's theorem (this was first explicitly proved by [Dmitry Kaledin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Kaledin)[^o13]). This complex manifold is called the [twistor space](wikipedia://en/2688710) of $M$. If *M* is ${\mathbb H}$, then its twistor space is isomorphic to ${\mathbb C}P^3\backslash {\mathbb C}P^1$.
