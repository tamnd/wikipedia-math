# Meronomy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Meronomy"}

A **meronomy** is a hierarchical [taxonomy](wikipedia://en/36675611) that deals with part–whole relationships. For example, a car has parts that include engine, body and wheels; and the body has parts that include doors and windows.

:::

These conceptual structures are used in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), with applications in [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology). The part–whole relationship is sometimes referred to as *HAS-A*, and corresponds to object composition in [object-oriented programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object-oriented_programming).[^o2]

The study of meronomy is known as *[mereology](wikipedia://en/375380)*, and in linguistics a *meronym* is the name given to a constituent part of, a substance of, or a member of something. "X" is a meronym of "Y" if an X is a part of a Y.[^o3] The unit of organisation that corresponds to the taxonomical **taxon** is the **meron**.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

- Cars have parts: engine, headlight, wheel
  - Engines have parts: crankcase, carburetor
  - Headlights have parts: headlight bulb, reflector
  - Wheels have parts: rim, spokes, tires

:::

## In knowledge representation {#In_knowledge_representation}

In formal terms, in the context of [knowledge representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_representation) and [ontologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontologies), a meronomy is a [partial order](wikipedia://en/23572) of concept types by the part–whole [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558).[^o7]

In the classic study of parts and wholes, [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380), the three defining properties of a partial order serve as [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms).[^o8] They are, respectively, that the part-of relation is:

- [Transitive](wikipedia://en/200463) – "Parts of parts are parts of the whole" (If *A* is part of *B* and *B* is part of *C*, then *A* is part of *C*);
- [Reflexive](wikipedia://en/200458) – "Everything is part of itself" (*A* is part of *A*); and
- [Antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176) – "Nothing is a part of its parts" (If *A* is part of *B* and *A* ≠ *B*, then *B* is not part of *A*).

Meronomies may be represented in [semantic web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_web) languages such as [OWL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Ontology_Language) and [SKOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKOS). In [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173) they are represented by [meronyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meronymy) and [holonyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holonymy).
