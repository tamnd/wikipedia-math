# Wally Smith (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wally Smith"}

**Walter Laws Smith** (November 12, 1926 – March 6, 2023) was a British-born American mathematician, known for his contributions to applied [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Smith was born in London on November 12, 1926.[^o5]

Smith received a B.A. in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) (1947) from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), having gained First Class in the Mathematical Tripos Part 1 and Part 2. He then received an M.A. (1951) and Ph.D. (1953) from Cambridge. His dissertation was entitled *Stochastic Sequences of Events* advised by [Henry Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Daniels_%28statistician%29) and [D. R. Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771), with whom he published the book *Queues* (1961) and also published with in his early years. He became a professor of statistics at The [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) Chapel Hill (1954–56 and 1958–), and he was an [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) professor in the Department of Statistics and Operations Research.[^o7]

Smith was a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (1966), a winner of the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) at the University of Cambridge (1960), a Sir Winston Churchill overseas fellow and a recipient of a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) (see [List of Guggenheim Fellowships awarded in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1974))

Smith died in [Chapel Hill, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_Hill,_North_Carolina), on March 6, 2023, at the age of 96.[^o8]
