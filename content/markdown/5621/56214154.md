# Intensity measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Intensity measure"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), an **intensity measure** is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) that is derived from a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846). The intensity measure is a non-random measure and is defined as the [expectation value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectation_value) of the random measure of a set, hence it corresponds to the average volume the random measure assigns to a set. The intensity measure contains important information about the properties of the random measure. A [Poisson point process](wikipedia://en/41597450), interpreted as a random measure, is for example uniquely determined by its intensity measure.[^o10]

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\zeta$ be a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846) on the [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(S, \mathcal A)$ and denote the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of a random element $Y$ with $\operatorname E [Y]$.

The intensity measure

$$\operatorname E \zeta \colon \mathcal A \to [0,\infty]$$

of $\zeta$ is defined as

$$\operatorname E \zeta(A)= \operatorname E[\zeta(A)]$$

for all $A \in \mathcal A$.[^o9][^o8]

Note the difference in notation between the expectation value of a random element $Y$, denoted by $\operatorname E [Y]$ and the intensity measure of the random measure $\zeta$, denoted by $\operatorname E\zeta$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The intensity measure $\operatorname E\zeta$ is always [s-finite](wikipedia://en/16629890) and satisfies

$$\operatorname E \left[ \int f(x) \; \zeta(\mathrm dx)\right]= \int f(x) \operatorname E\zeta(dx)$$

for every positive [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$ on $(S, \mathcal A)$.[^o8]
