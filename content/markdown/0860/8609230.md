# Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy"}

**The "Simion Stoilow" Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy** is a [research institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_institute) in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania). It is affiliated with the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy), and it is named after [Simion Stoilow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Stoilow), one of its founders.

:::

## History {#History}

On December 29, 1945, a group of twenty Romanian mathematicians from various institutions in Bucharest led by [Dimitrie Pompeiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Pompeiu) held a meeting at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) to establish the *Institute of Mathematical Sciences* with the aim of "promoting scientific research in mathematical sciences, through communications, talks, publications, congresses, and other means proper to this aim". This group also included [Dan Barbilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Barbu), [Alexandru Froda](wikipedia://en/22289393), [Alexandru Ghica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ghica), [Gheorghe Mihoc](wikipedia://en/3421581), [Grigore Moisil](wikipedia://en/4000394), [Miron Nicolescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miron_Nicolescu), [Octav Onicescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octav_Onicescu), Stoilow, [Gabriel Sudan](wikipedia://en/6749249), [Victor Vâlcovici](wikipedia://en/45710371), and [Gheorghe Vrănceanu](wikipedia://en/6063405). In January 1946 they registered the Institute as a [legal person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_person), specifically an [NGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-governmental_organization), with the [Ilfov County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilfov_County) Court.[^o4]

On June 9, 1948 the new [Communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) revamped the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) to an institution modeled on the [Academy of Sciences of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences#The_Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_USSR), increasing by 1966 the number of its member research centers and institutes from 7 to 56. Among the newly created institutes was the *Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy*, established in 1949 on the basis of the previous NGO with the contribution of [Simion Stoilow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Stoilow), one of the twenty founding members from 1945.

In 1974, [Zoia Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoia_Ceaușescu), a graduate of the Faculty of Mathematics of the University of Bucharest and the daughter of [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceaușescu), the communist head of state, was hired by the institute. Her parents were not happy with her choice in studying Mathematics. Provoked by a verbal disagreement with [Miron Nicolescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miron_Nicolescu), in April 1975, Ceaușescu issued a decree to close down the Institute. The ensuing disruption of scientific life led to the eventual departure from Romania of a number of leading mathematicians, including [Ciprian Foias](wikipedia://en/28465086) and [Dan-Virgil Voiculescu](wikipedia://en/7656821). In 1978, with help from Zoia Ceaușescu, some of the former members of the Institute were hired into a newly established Mathematical section of the National Institute Scientific and Technical Creation (*Institutul Național pentru Creație Științifică și Tehnică*, INCREST), previously known as the Institute of Fluid Mechanics and Aerospace Research (*Institutul de Mecanică a Fluidelor și Cercetări Aerospațiale*, IMFCA), currently a private company (*INAV S.A.*), owned by Grupul S.C.R.

After the [Romanian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) of 1989, the Institute of Mathematics of the Romanian Academy (abbreviated IMAR) was re-established on March 8, 1990 by a decree of the post-Communist [Romanian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Roman_cabinet_%28Romania%29). It was placed under the aegis of the Romanian Academy, itself partially reorganized by a decree of the same government on January 5, 1990.

## Current situation {#Current_situation}

Currently, IMAR is the leading Romanian institution in Mathematics research, with about 100 full- and part-time researchers. In 2000–2004, the Institute was a Centre of Excellence in Research of the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission). Since 2008, IMAR has a *Laboratoire Européen Associé* collaboration with [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique). The Institute publishes two scientific journals (*Mathematical Reports* and *[Revue roumaine de Mathématiques pures et appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revue_roumaine_de_Mathématiques_pures_et_appliquées)*), and organizes many specialized conferences and congresses. Some of its members are well-known mathematicians.

IMAR also hosts the Theta Foundation, a not-for-profit independent publisher of scientific mathematical literature, and SNS-B, an independent postgraduate-level teaching initiative to guide towards research the best Romanian students.

The Institute is located on [Calea Griviței Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grivița), in the [Sector 1 of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_1_%28Bucharest%29); it occupies the same downtown building it had before 1975.
