In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great grand stellated 120-cell** or **great grand stellated polydodecahedron** is a [regular star 4-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_star_4-polytope) with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {5/2,3,3}, one of 10 regular Schläfli-Hess 4-polytopes. It is unique among the 10 for having 600 vertices, and has the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) as the regular convex [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358).

:::

It is one of four *regular star polychora* discovered by [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408). It is named by [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), extending the naming system by [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) for the [Kepler-Poinsot solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot_solid), and the only one containing all three modifiers in the name.

## As a stellation {#As_a_stellation}

The **great grand stellated 120-cell** is the final regular [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358), and is the only Schläfli-Hess polychoron to have the 120-cell for its convex hull. In this sense it is analogous to the three-dimensional [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878), which is the final stellation of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) and the only Kepler-Poinsot polyhedron to have the dodecahedron for its convex hull. Indeed, the great grand stellated 120-cell is dual to the [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538), which could be taken as a 4D analogue of the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898), dual of the great stellated dodecahedron.

The edges of the great grand stellated 120-cell are [τ](wikipedia://en/12386)<sup>6</sup> as long as those of the 120-cell core deep inside the polychoron, and they are τ<sup>3</sup> as long as those of the [small stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604577) deep within the polychoron.
