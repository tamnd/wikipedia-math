# Uniform polyhedron compound

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform polyhedron compound"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform polyhedron compound** is a [polyhedral compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound) whose constituents are identical (although possibly [enantiomorphous](wikipedia://en/32703814)) [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223), in an arrangement that is also uniform, i.e. the [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of the compound acts [transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) on the compound's [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

:::

The uniform polyhedron compounds were first enumerated by John Skilling in 1976, with a proof that the enumeration is complete. The following table lists them according to his numbering.

The prismatic compounds of $\{p/q\}-$gonal [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) ([UC<sub>20</sub>](wikipedia://en/14890508) and [UC<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/14854954)) exist only when $\tfrac{p}{q} > 2$, and when $p$ and $q$ are [coprime](wikipedia://en/6556). The uniform prismatic compounds of $\{p/q\}-$gonal [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) ([UC<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/14890505), [UC<sub>23</sub>](wikipedia://en/14855183), [UC<sub>24</sub>](wikipedia://en/14890505) and [UC<sub>25</sub>](wikipedia://en/14855183)) exist only when $\tfrac{p}{q} > \tfrac{3}{2}$, and when $p$ and $q$ are coprime. Furthermore, when $\tfrac{p}{q} = 2$, the antiprisms [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) into [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra) with [digonal](wikipedia://en/5442728) bases.
