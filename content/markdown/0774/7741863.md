# Commutative magma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Commutative magma"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), there exist **[magmas](wikipedia://en/141916) that are [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) but not [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative)**. A simple example of such a magma may be derived from the children's game of [rock, paper, scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock,_paper,_scissors). Such magmas give rise to [non-associative algebras](wikipedia://en/6533841).

:::

A magma which is both commutative and associative is a commutative [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799).

## Example: rock, paper, scissors {#Example:_rock,_paper,_scissors}

In the game of [rock paper scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_paper_scissors), let $M := \{ r, p, s \}$ , standing for the "rock", "paper" and "scissors" gestures respectively, and consider the [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) $\cdot : M \times M \to M$ derived from the rules of the game as follows:[^o3]

For all $x, y \in M$:

- If $x \neq y$ and $x$ beats $y$ in the game, then $x \cdot y = y \cdot x = x$
- $x \cdot x = x$ I.e. every $x$ is [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent).

So that for example:

- $r \cdot p = p \cdot r = p$ "paper beats rock";
- $s \cdot s = s$ "scissors tie with scissors".

This results in the [Cayley table](wikipedia://en/1802723):[^o3]

$$\begin{array}{c|ccc} \cdot & r & p & s\\ \hline r & r & p & r\\ p & p & p & s\\ s & r & s & s \end{array}$$

By definition, the magma $(M, \cdot)$ is commutative, but it is also non-associative,[^o4] as shown by:

$$r \cdot (p \cdot s) = r \cdot s = r$$

but

$$(r \cdot p) \cdot s = p \cdot s = s$$

i.e.

$$r \cdot (p \cdot s) \neq (r \cdot p) \cdot s$$

It is the simplest non-associative magma that is *conservative*, in the sense that the result of any magma operation is one of the two values given as arguments to the operation.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612), and [generalized means](wikipedia://en/13143) of numbers or of higher-dimensional quantities, such as [Frechet means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frechet_mean), are often commutative but non-associative.[^o7]

Commutative but non-associative magmas may be used to analyze [genetic recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_recombination).[^o8]
