# KMS state

*Image: Warszawa Centrum Nowych Technologii UW-6.jpg.* Kubo–Martin–Schwinger condition as featured on a monument in front of [Warsaw University's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) Centre of New Technologies

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="KMS state"}

In the [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) of [quantum mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) systems and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), the properties of a system in thermal equilibrium can be described by a mathematical object called a **Kubo–Martin–Schwinger** (**KMS**) **state**: a state satisfying the **KMS condition**.

:::

[Ryogo Kubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryogo_Kubo) introduced the condition in 1957,[^o2] Paul C. Martin and [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980) used it in 1959 to define [thermodynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamic) [Green's functions](wikipedia://en/311001),[^o3] and [Rudolf Haag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Haag), Marinus Winnink and [Nico Hugenholtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaas_Marinus_Hugenholtz) used the condition in 1967 to define equilibrium states and called it the KMS condition.[^o4]

## Overview {#Overview}

The simplest case to study is that of a finite-dimensional [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), in which one does not encounter complications like [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423) or [spontaneous symmetry breaking](wikipedia://en/297466). The [density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844) of a **thermal state** is given by

$$\rho_{\beta,\mu}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta \left(H - \mu N\right)}}{\mathrm{Tr}\left[ \mathrm{e}^{-\beta \left(H - \mu N\right)} \right]}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\beta \left(H - \mu N\right)}}{Z(\beta,\mu)}$$

where *H* is the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) [operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28physics%29) and *N* is the [particle number operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_number_operator) (or [charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_%28physics%29) operator, if we wish to be more general) and

$$Z(\beta,\mu)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \mathrm{Tr}\left[ \mathrm{e}^{-\beta \left(H-\mu N\right)} \right]$$

is the [partition function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29). We assume that *N* commutes with *H,* or in other words, that particle number is [conserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29).

In the [Heisenberg picture](wikipedia://en/306706), the density matrix does not change with time, but the operators are time-dependent. In particular, translating an operator *A* by τ into the future gives the operator

$$\alpha_\tau(A)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \mathrm{e}^{iH\tau}A \mathrm{e}^{-iH\tau}.$$

A combination of [time translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_translation) with an [internal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_symmetry) "rotation" gives the more general

$$\alpha^{\mu}_{\tau}(A)\ \stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\ \mathrm{e}^{i\left(H-\mu N\right)\tau} A \mathrm{e}^{-i\left(H - \mu N\right)\tau}$$

A bit of algebraic manipulation shows that the [expected values](wikipedia://en/9653)

$$\left\langle\alpha^\mu_\tau(A)B\right\rangle_{\beta,\mu} = \mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho \alpha^\mu_\tau(A)B\right] = \mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho B \alpha^\mu_{\tau+i\beta}(A)\right] = \left\langle B\alpha^\mu_{\tau+i\beta}(A)\right\rangle_{\beta,\mu}$$

for any two operators *A* and *B* and any real τ (we are working with finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces after all). We used the fact that the density matrix commutes with any function of (*H* − μ*N*) and that the [trace](wikipedia://en/192266) is cyclic.

As hinted at earlier, with infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces, we run into a lot of problems like phase transitions, spontaneous symmetry breaking, operators that are not [trace class](wikipedia://en/192266), divergent partition functions, etc..

The [complex functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_function) of *z*, $\left\langle\alpha^\mu_z(A)B\right\rangle$ converges in the complex strip $-\beta < \Im{z} < 0$ whereas $\left\langle B\alpha^\mu_z(A)\right\rangle$ converges in the complex strip $0 < \Im{z} < \beta$ if we make certain technical assumptions like the [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_of_an_operator) of *H* − μ*N* is bounded from below and its density does not increase exponentially (see [Hagedorn temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagedorn_temperature)). If the functions converge, then they have to be [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) within the strip they are defined over as their derivatives,

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left\langle\alpha^\mu_z(A)B\right\rangle = i\left\langle\alpha^\mu_z\left(\left[H - \mu N, A\right]\right)B\right\rangle$$

and

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\left\langle B\alpha^\mu_z(A)\right\rangle = i\left\langle B\alpha^\mu_z\left(\left[H - \mu N, A\right]\right)\right\rangle$$

exist.

However, we can still define a **KMS state** as any state satisfying

$$\left\langle \alpha^\mu_\tau(A)B\right\rangle = \left\langle B\alpha^\mu_{\tau+i\beta}(A)\right\rangle$$

with $\left\langle\alpha^\mu_z(A)B\right\rangle$ and $\left\langle B\alpha^\mu_z(A)\right\rangle$ being analytic functions of *z* within their domain strips.

$\left\langle\alpha^\mu_\tau(A)B\right\rangle$ and $\left\langle B\alpha^\mu_{\tau+i\beta}(A)\right\rangle$ are the boundary [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) values of the analytic functions in question.

This gives the right large volume, large particle number thermodynamic limit. If there is a phase transition or spontaneous symmetry breaking, the KMS state is not unique.

The density matrix of a KMS state is related to [unitary transformations](wikipedia://en/236007) involving time translations (or time translations and an [internal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_symmetry) transformation for nonzero chemical potentials) via the [Tomita–Takesaki theory](wikipedia://en/2898625).
