# Richard Askey

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Askey"}

**Richard Allen Askey** (June 4, 1933 – October 9, 2019)[^o12] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his expertise in the area of [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function). The [Askey–Wilson polynomials](wikipedia://en/19582010) (introduced by him in 1984 together with [James A. Wilson](wikipedia://en/32810784)) are on the top level of the ($q$-)[Askey scheme](wikipedia://en/32649081), which organizes [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) of ($q$-)hypergeometric type into a hierarchy. The [Askey–Gasper inequality](wikipedia://en/19291343) for [Jacobi polynomials](wikipedia://en/30863587) is essential in [de Brange's](wikipedia://en/79126) famous proof of the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Askey earned a B.A. at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis) in 1955, an M.A. at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1956, and a Ph.D. at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1961.[^o13] After working as an instructor at Washington University (1958–1961) and [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1961–1963), he joined the faculty of the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1963 as an Assistant Professor of Mathematics. He became a full professor at Wisconsin in 1968, and since 2003 was a professor emeritus.[^o14] Askey was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow), 1969–1970, which academic year he spent at the *[Mathematisch Centrum](wikipedia://en/506063)* in Amsterdam. In 1983, he gave an [invited lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) (ICM)[^o15] in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw). He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1993.[^o11] In 1999, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o16] In 2009, he became a fellow of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).[^o17] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o18] In December 2012, he received an honorary doctorate[^o19] from [SASTRA University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_University) in [Kumbakonam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbakonam), India.

*Image: Grave of Richard Allen Askey (1933–2019) at Forest Hill Cemetery, Madison, WI.jpg.* Askey's grave at Forest Hill Cemetery

Askey explained why [hypergeometric functions](wikipedia://en/2055223) appear so frequently in mathematical applications: "[Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) showed that the requirement that a differential equation have regular singular points at three given points and every other complex point is a regular point is so strong a restriction that [(Riemann's) differential equation](wikipedia://en/2071666) is the [hypergeometric equation](wikipedia://en/2055223#The_hypergeometric_differential_equation) with the three singularities moved to the three given points. Differential equations with four or more singular points only infrequently have a solution which can be given explicitly as a series whose coefficients are known, or have an explicit integral representation. This partly explains why the classical hypergeometric function arises in many settings that seem to have nothing to do with each other. The differential equation they satisfy is the most general one of its kind that has solutions with many nice properties".[^o20]

Askey was also very much involved with commenting and writing on mathematical education at American schools. A well-known article by him on this topic is "Good Intentions are not Enough".[^o21]

He died in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin) on October 9, 2019, and was buried at [Forest Hill Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Hill_Cemetery_%28Madison,_Wisconsin%29).[^o4]
