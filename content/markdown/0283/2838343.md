# Calculus on Manifolds (book)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Calculus on Manifolds"}

***Calculus on Manifolds: A Modern Approach to Classical Theorems of Advanced Calculus*** (1965) by [Michael Spivak](wikipedia://en/333393) is a brief, rigorous, and modern textbook on multivariable calculus, differential forms, and integration on manifolds for advanced undergraduates.

:::

## Description {#Description}

*Calculus on Manifolds* is a brief [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph) on the [theory](wikipedia://en/26478) of vector-valued functions of [several real variables](wikipedia://en/615222) ($f: \mathbb{R}^n \mapsto \mathbb{R}^m$) and [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) in Euclidean space. In addition to extending the concepts of [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiation_%28calculus%29) (including the [inverse](wikipedia://en/287229) and [implicit function theorems](wikipedia://en/581005)) and [Riemann integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_integration) (including [Fubini's theorem](wikipedia://en/255245)) to functions of several variables, the book treats the classical theorems of vector calculus, including those of [Green](wikipedia://en/243334), [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss'_theorem), and [Stokes](wikipedia://en/25441497), in the language of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) embedded in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), and as [corollaries](wikipedia://en/168865) of the [generalized Stokes theorem](wikipedia://en/29040) on [manifolds with boundaries](wikipedia://en/2073470#Manifold_with_boundary). The book culminates with the statement and proof of this vast and abstract modern generalization of several classical results:

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Stokes' Theorem for Manifolds with Boundaries."}

**Stokes' Theorem for Manifolds with Boundaries..**

*If* $M$ *is a compact oriented* $k$*\-dimensional manifold-with-boundary,* $\partial M$ *is the boundary given the induced orientation, and* $\omega$ *is a* ($k-1$)*\-form on* $M$*, then* $\int_M d\omega =\int_{\partial M} \omega$.

:::

The cover of *Calculus on Manifolds* features snippets of a July 2, 1850 letter from [Lord Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Kelvin) to Sir [George Stokes](wikipedia://en/212838) containing the first disclosure of the classical Stokes' theorem.[^o5]

## Reception {#Reception}

*Calculus on Manifolds* aims to present the topics of [multivariable](wikipedia://en/615222) and [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) in the manner in which they are seen by a modern working mathematician, yet simply and selectively enough to be understood by undergraduate students whose previous coursework in mathematics comprises only single-variable calculus and introductory linear algebra. While Spivak's elementary treatment of modern mathematical tools is broadly successful—and this approach has made *Calculus on Manifolds* a standard introduction to the rigorous theory of multivariable calculus—the text is also well known for its laconic style, lack of motivating examples, and frequent omission of non-obvious steps and arguments.[^o7][^o8] For example, in order to state and prove the generalized Stokes' theorem on chains, a profusion of unfamiliar concepts and constructions (e.g., [tensor products](wikipedia://en/832212), differential forms, [tangent spaces](wikipedia://en/30807), [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/494995), [exterior derivatives](wikipedia://en/220782), [cube and chains](wikipedia://en/224765)) are introduced in quick succession within the span of 25 pages. Moreover, careful readers have noted a number of nontrivial oversights throughout the text, including missing hypotheses in theorems, inaccurately stated theorems, and proofs that fail to handle all cases.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Other textbooks {#Other_textbooks}

A more recent textbook which also covers these topics at an undergraduate level is the text *Analysis on Manifolds* (1991) by [James Munkres](wikipedia://en/7615776).[^o13] At 366 pages or more than twice the length of *Calculus on Manifolds*, Munkres's work presents a more careful and detailed treatment of the subject matter at a leisurely pace. Nevertheless, Munkres acknowledges the influence of Spivak's earlier text in the preface of his text.[^o14]

Spivak's five-volume textbook *A Comprehensive Introduction to Differential Geometry* states in its preface that *Calculus on Manifolds* serves as a prerequisite for a course based on this text. In fact, several of the concepts introduced in *Calculus on Manifolds* reappear in the first volume of this classic work in more sophisticated settings.[^o15]
