# Director circle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Director circle"}

*Image: Fermat-Apollonius circle.svg.* An ellipse, its minimum bounding box, and its director circle.

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **director circle** of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) or [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) (also called the **[orthoptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoptic) circle** or **Fermat–Apollonius circle**) is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) consisting of all points where two [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) [tangent lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) to the ellipse or hyperbola cross each other.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The director circle of an ellipse [circumscribes](wikipedia://en/161262) the [minimum bounding box](wikipedia://en/12087300) of the ellipse. It has the same center as the ellipse, with radius $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, where $a$ and $b$ are the [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) and [semi-minor axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-minor_axis) of the ellipse. Additionally, it has the property that, when viewed from any point on the circle, the ellipse spans a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956).[^o4]

The director circle of a hyperbola has radius $\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$, and so, may not exist in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), but could be a circle with imaginary radius in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628).

The director circle of a circle is a concentric circle having radius $\sqrt{2}$ times the radius of the original circle.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

More generally, for any collection of points $P_{i}$, weights $w_{i}$, and constant $C$, one can define a circle as the locus of points $X$ such that

$$\sum_i w_i \, d(X,P_i)^2 = C.$$

The director circle of an ellipse is a special case of this more general construction with two points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ at the foci of the ellipse, weights $w_{1} = w_{2} = 1$, and $C$ equal to the square of the major axis of the ellipse. The [Apollonius circle](wikipedia://en/6220#Circle_of_Apollonius), the locus of points $X$ such that the ratio of distances of $X$ to two foci $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ is a fixed constant $r$, is another special case, with $w_{1} = 1$, $w_{2} = -r^{2}$, and $C = 0$.

:::

## Related constructions {#Related_constructions}

In the case of a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) the director circle degenerates to a straight line, the [directrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directrix_%28conic_section%29) of the parabola.[^o8]
