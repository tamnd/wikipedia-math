# Sinc numerical methods

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sinc numerical methods"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), **sinc numerical methods** are numerical techniques[^o2] for finding approximate solutions of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and [integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_equations) based on the translates of [sinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinc) function and Cardinal function C(f,h) which is an expansion of f defined by

:::

$$C(f,h)(x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty f(kh) \, \textrm{sinc} \left(\dfrac{x}{h}-k \right)$$

where the step size h>0 and where the sinc function is defined by

$$\textrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}$$

Sinc approximation methods excel for problems whose solutions may have singularities, or infinite domains, or boundary layers.

The truncated Sinc expansion of f is defined by the following series:

$C_{M,N}(f,h)(x) = \displaystyle \sum_{k=-M}^{N} f(kh) \, \textrm{sinc} \left(\dfrac{x}{h}-k \right)$ .

## Sinc numerical methods cover {#Sinc_numerical_methods_cover}

- function approximation,
- approximation of [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921),
- approximate definite and indefinite [integration](wikipedia://en/15532),
- approximate solution of initial and boundary value ordinary [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) (ODE) problems,
- approximation and inversion of [Fourier](wikipedia://en/52247) and [Laplace](wikipedia://en/18610) transforms,
- approximation of [Hilbert transforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_transforms),
- approximation of definite and indefinite [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519),
- approximate solution of partial differential equations,
- approximate solution of [integral equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_equations),
- construction of conformal maps.

In the standard setup of the sinc numerical methods, the errors (in [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)) are known to be $O\left(e^{-c\sqrt{n}}\right)$ with some c>0, where n is the number of nodes or bases used in the methods. However, Sugihara[^o4] has recently found that the errors in the Sinc numerical methods based on double exponential transformation are $O\left(e^{-\frac{k n}{\ln n}}\right)$ with some k>0, in a setup that is also meaningful both theoretically and practically and are found to be best possible in a certain mathematical sense.
