# Karl F. Sundman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karl F. Sundman"}

*Image: Karl Sundman.jpg.* Karl F. Sundman.

**Karl Frithiof Sundman** (28 October 1873, in [Kaskinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskinen) – 28 September 1949, in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki)) was a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902)[^o2] who used analytic methods to prove the existence of a convergent [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) solution to the [three-body problem](wikipedia://en/23859945#Three-body_problem) in two papers published in 1907[^o3] and 1909.[^o4] His results gained fame when they were reproduced in *[Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708)* in 1912.[^o5] He also published a paper on [regularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularization_%28physics%29) methods in [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics) in 1912.

:::

## Awards, recognition {#Awards,_recognition}

Sundman was awarded the Pontécoulant prize by the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1913 for his work on the 3-body problem.[^o2] In 1908, Sundman was elected member of the [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters)[^o7] and in 1947 foreign member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o8] The crater [Sundman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundman_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him, as is the asteroid [1424 Sundmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1424_Sundmania).
