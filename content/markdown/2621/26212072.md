# Neighborly polytope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neighborly polytope"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519), a **$k$-neighborly polytope** is a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) in which every set of $k$ or fewer [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) forms a [face](wikipedia://en/149926). For instance, a 2-neighborly polytope is a polytope in which every pair of [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) is connected by an [edge](wikipedia://en/12158034), forming a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816). 2-neighborly polytopes with more than four vertices may exist only in spaces of four or more dimensions, and in general a $k$-neighborly polytope (other than a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781)) requires a dimension of $2k$ or more. A $d$-simplex is $d$-neighborly. A polytope is said to be **neighborly**, without specifying $k$, if it is $k$-neighborly for $k = \lfloor\tfrac{d}{2}\rfloor$. If we exclude simplices, this is the maximum possible $k$: in fact, every polytope that is $k$-neighborly for some $k \ge 1 + \lfloor\tfrac{d}{2}\rfloor$ is a simplex.[^o2]

:::

In a $k$-neighborly polytope with $k \ge 3$, every 2-face must be a triangle, and in a $k$-neighborly polytope with $k \ge 4$, every 3-face must be a tetrahedron. More generally, in any $k$-neighborly polytope, all faces of dimension less than $k$ are [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781).

The [cyclic polytopes](wikipedia://en/26652374) formed as the convex hulls of finite sets of points on the [moment curve](wikipedia://en/29536414) $(t, t^{2}, \ldots, t^{d})$ in $d$-dimensional space are automatically neighborly. [Theodore Motzkin](wikipedia://en/13554055) conjectured that all neighborly polytopes are combinatorially equivalent to cyclic polytopes.[^o3] However, contrary to this conjecture, there are many neighborly polytopes that are not cyclic: the number of combinatorially distinct neighborly polytopes grows superexponentially, both in the number of vertices of the polytope and in the dimension.[^o4]

The [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of a set of random points, drawn from a [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution) with the number of points proportional to the dimension, is with high probability $k$-neighborly for a value $k$ that is also proportional to the dimension.[^o5]

The number of faces of all dimensions of a neighborly polytope in an even number of dimensions is determined solely from its dimension and its number of vertices by the [Dehn–Sommerville equations](wikipedia://en/8276156): the number of $k$-dimensional faces, $f_{k}$, satisfies the inequality

$$f_{k-1} \le \sum_{i=0}^{d/2} {}^* \left( \binom{d-i}{k-i}+\binom{i}{k-d+i} \right) \binom{n-d-1+i}{i},$$

where the asterisk means that the sums ends at $i = \lfloor\tfrac{d}{2}\rfloor$ and final term of the sum should be halved if $d$ is even.[^o6] According to the [upper bound theorem](wikipedia://en/40964551) of (McMullen 1970),[^o8] neighborly polytopes achieve the maximum possible number of faces of any $n$-vertex $d$-dimensional convex polytope.

A generalized version of the [happy ending problem](wikipedia://en/2576185) applies to higher-dimensional point sets, and implies that for every dimension $d$ and every $n > d$ there exists a number $m(d,n)$ with the property that every $m$ points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) in $d$-dimensional space contain a subset of $n$ points that form the vertices of a neighborly polytope.[^o9]
