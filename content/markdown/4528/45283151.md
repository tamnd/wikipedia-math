# Harmonic morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harmonic morphism"}

In mathematics, a **harmonic morphism** is a (smooth) map $\phi:(M^m,g)\to (N^n,h)$ between [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) that pulls back real-valued [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762) on the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) to harmonic functions on the domain. Harmonic morphisms form a special class of [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484), namely those that are horizontally (weakly) conformal.[^o2]

:::

In local coordinates, $x$ on $M$ and $y$ on $N$, the **harmonicity** of $\phi$ is expressed by the [non-linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear) system

$$\tau(\phi )=\sum_{i,j=1}^m g^{ij}\left(\frac{\partial^2\phi^{\gamma}}{\partial x_i\partial x_j} -\sum_{k=1}^m\hat\Gamma^k_{ij}\frac{\partial\phi^{\gamma}}{\partial x_k} +\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^n\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\circ\phi \frac{\partial\phi^{\alpha}}{\partial x_i} \frac{\partial\phi^{\beta }}{\partial x_j} \right)=0,$$

where $\phi^\alpha=y_\alpha\circ\phi$ and $\hat\Gamma,\Gamma$ are the [Christoffel symbols](wikipedia://en/1401020) on $M$ and $N$, respectively. The **horizontal conformality** is given by

$$\sum_{i,j=1}^mg^{ij}(x)\frac{\partial\phi^\alpha}{\partial x_i}(x)\frac{\partial\phi^\beta }{\partial x_j}(x)=\lambda^2(x)h^{\alpha\beta}(\phi(x)),$$

where the conformal factor $\lambda:M\to\mathbb R_0^+$ is a continuous function called the [dilation](wikipedia://en/1591770). Harmonic morphisms are therefore solutions to [non-linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear) [over-determined systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-determined_system) of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), determined by the geometric data of the [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) involved. For this reason, they are difficult to find and have no general existence theory, not even locally.

## Complex analysis {#Complex_analysis}

When the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) of $\phi:(M,g)\to (N^2,h)$ is a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865), the system of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) that we are dealing with, is invariant under conformal changes of the metric $h$. This means that, at least for local studies, the [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) can be chosen to be the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) with its standard flat metric. In this situation a complex-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $\phi=u+iv:(M,g)\to\mathbb C$ is a harmonic morphisms if and only if

$$\Delta_M(\phi)=\Delta_M (u)+i\Delta_M (v)=0$$

and

$$g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi )=\|\nabla u\|^2-\|\nabla v\|^2+2ig(\nabla u,\nabla v)=0.$$

This means that we look for two real-valued [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762) $u,v:(M,g)\to\mathbb R$ with [gradients](wikipedia://en/12461) $\nabla u,\nabla v$ that are orthogonal and of the same norm at each point. This shows that complex-valued harmonic morphisms $\phi:(M,g)\to\mathbb C$ from [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) generalise [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) $f:(M,g,J)\to\mathbb C$ from [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) and possess many of their highly interesting properties. The theory of harmonic morphisms can therefore be seen as a generalisation of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).[^o2]

## Minimal surfaces {#Minimal_surfaces}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), one is interested in constructing minimal [submanifolds](wikipedia://en/525095) of a given ambient space $(M,g)$. Harmonic morphisms are useful tools for this purpose. This is due to the fact that every regular fibre $\phi^{-1}(\{z_0\})$ of such a map $\phi:(M,g)\to (N^2,h)$ with values in a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) is a minimal submanifold of the domain with codimension 2.[^o2] This gives an attractive method for manufacturing whole families of [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) in 4-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) $(M^4,g)$, in particular, [homogeneous spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_spaces), such as [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and [symmetric spaces](wikipedia://en/1642877).

## Examples {#Examples}

- Identity and constant maps are harmonic morphisms.
- [Holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) are harmonic morphisms.
- [Holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) in the [complex vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_vector_space) $\mathbb C^n$ are harmonic morphisms.
- [Holomorphic maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) from [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) with values in a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) are harmonic morphisms.
- The [Hopf maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_map) $\phi:S^3\to S^2$, $\phi:S^7\to S^4$ and $\phi:S^{15}\to S^8$ are harmonic morphisms.
- For [compact Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) $K\subset H\subset G$ the standard Riemannian [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) $\phi:G/H\to G/K$ is a harmonic morphism.
- [Riemannian submersions](wikipedia://en/2850614) with minimal fibres are harmonic morphisms.
