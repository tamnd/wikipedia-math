# David W. McLaughlin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David W. McLaughlin"}

**David Warren McLaughlin** (born October 11, 1944) is an applied mathematician and academic administrator whose work concerns [nonlinear systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_systems), nonlinear waves, and mathematical models of [visual neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_neuroscience). McLaughlin served as the provost of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) from 2002 to 2016.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

McLaughlin grew up in [Woodbine, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbine,_Iowa), where his father sold gas to farmers.[^o4] He attended [Creighton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_University) in [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska), majoring in physics and mathematics and graduating [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) in 1966.[^o4] McLaughlin went on to graduate study at [Indiana University (Bloomington)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_%28Bloomington%29) where he studied [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) with [Larry Schulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Schulman), completing a Ph.D. in physics in 1971.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

McLaughlin moved several times within a few years at the beginning of his career. He first joined New York University in 1971 as an assistant professor in mathematics, recruited by [Joseph Keller](wikipedia://en/4454126), but had to leave in 1972 when NYU's University Heights campus in the Bronx was sold due to financial problems.[^o4] He next moved to [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 1972, again as an assistant professor of mathematics, and then joined the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) as associate professor in 1974. [^o4] He served on the faculty at the University of Arizona for 15 years, during which time he was the chair of the applied mathematics program.[^o4]

In 1989, McLaughlin joined [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as professor of mathematics, where he directed the applied and computational mathematics program before returning to NYU in 1994 as director of the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences).[^o4] At Courant, he led efforts in mathematics, applied mathematics, computational science, and computer science, recruiting faculty in these areas and developing programs in atmosphere-ocean studies, financial mathematics, multimedia technology, and computational biology.[^o4] He was appointed the provost of NYU in 2002, serving in that role for 14 years.[^o4]. He is currently the Silver Professor of Mathematics and Neural Science at the Courant Institute.[^o13]

## Research {#Research}

McLaughlin's work was applied mathematics focused on large-scale nonlinear systems best described by [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), with a long interest in mathematical models of complex systems.[^o11] His research areas included nonlinear waves, laser beams, and in recent years, neuronal networks for visual neural science. [^o11] His early research used [asymptotic methods](wikipedia://en/641995) applied to [path integrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) and the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748).[^o4][^o7] His major work was on nonlinear dispersive waves, including systems where wave dissipation is weak, and his work addressed coherent behavior, chaotic behavior, and dispersive wave turbulence.[^o4][^o8]

After returning to the Courant, he developed an interest in neuroscience; he collaborated with [Robert Shapley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Shapley) at NYU’s Center for Neural Science, working at the intersection of mathematics and neuroscience.[^o4][^o9] With a focus on visual neuroscience, McLaughlin developed large-scale computational models of the front end of the cortical visual system, particularly the [primary visual cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_visual_cortex).[^o11]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- Member, [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (2002).[^o4][^o11]
- Fellow, [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (2000).[^o12]
- Fellow, [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (2003). [^o13]
- Fellow, [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376)[^o13]
