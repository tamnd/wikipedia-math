# Locally profinite group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally profinite group"}

In mathematics, a **locally profinite group** is a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) in which every [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) of the identity element contains a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) open subgroup. Equivalently, a locally profinite group is a topological group that is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637), [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632), and [totally disconnected](wikipedia://en/4947490). Moreover, a locally profinite group is compact if and only if it is [profinite](wikipedia://en/24266); this explains the terminology. Basic examples of locally profinite groups are [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536) and the [*p*-adic Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Lie_group). Non-examples are real [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), which have the [no small subgroup property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_small_subgroup_property).

:::

In a locally profinite group, a closed subgroup is locally profinite, and every compact subgroup is contained in an open compact subgroup.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Important examples of locally profinite groups come from [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). Let *F* be a [non-archimedean](wikipedia://en/31046646) [local field](wikipedia://en/145375). Then both *F* and $F^\times$ are locally profinite. More generally, the matrix ring $\operatorname{M}_n(F)$ and the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) $\operatorname{GL}_n(F)$ are locally profinite. Another example of a locally profinite group is the absolute [Weil group](wikipedia://en/6375618) of a non-archimedean local field: this is in contrast to the fact that the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of such is profinite (in particular compact).

:::

## Representations of a locally profinite group {#Representations_of_a_locally_profinite_group}

Let *G* be a locally profinite group. Then a group homomorphism $\psi: G \to \mathbb{C}^\times$ is continuous if and only if it has open kernel.

Let $(\rho, V)$ be a complex representation of *G*.[^o6] $\rho$ is said to be [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_representation) if *V* is a union of $V^K$ where *K* runs over all open compact subgroups *K*. $\rho$ is said to be [admissible](wikipedia://en/11055227) if it is smooth and $V^K$ is finite-dimensional for any open compact subgroup *K*.

We now make a blanket assumption that $G/K$ is at most countable for all open compact subgroups *K*.

The dual space $V^*$ carries the [action](wikipedia://en/12781) $\rho^*$ of *G* given by $\left\langle \rho^*(g) \alpha, v \right\rangle = \left\langle \alpha, \rho^*(g^{-1}) v \right\rangle$. In general, $\rho^*$ is not smooth. Thus, we set $\widetilde{V} = \bigcup_K (V^*)^K$ where $K$ is acting through $\rho^*$ and set $\widetilde{\rho} = \rho^*$. The smooth representation $(\widetilde{\rho}, \widetilde{V})$ is then called the [contragredient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contragredient_representation) or smooth dual of $(\rho, V)$.

The contravariant [functor](wikipedia://en/10987)

$$(\rho, V) \mapsto (\widetilde{\rho}, \widetilde{V})$$

from the category of smooth representations of *G* to itself is exact. Moreover, the following are equivalent.

- $\rho$ is admissible.
- $\widetilde{\rho}$ is admissible.[^o7]
- The canonical *G*-module map $\rho \to \widetilde{\widetilde{\rho}}$ is an isomorphism.

When $\rho$ is admissible, $\rho$ is irreducible if and only if $\widetilde{\rho}$ is irreducible.

The countability assumption at the beginning is really necessary, for there exists a locally profinite group that admits an irreducible smooth representation $\rho$ such that $\widetilde{\rho}$ is not irreducible.

:::

## Hecke algebra of a locally profinite group {#Hecke_algebra_of_a_locally_profinite_group}

Let $G$ be a unimodular locally profinite group such that $G/K$ is at most countable for all open compact subgroups *K*, and $\mu$ a left Haar measure on $G$. Let $C^\infty_c(G)$ denote the space of locally constant functions on $G$ with compact support. With the multiplicative structure given by

$$(f * h)(x) = \int_G f(g) h(g^{-1} x) d \mu(g)$$

$C^\infty_c(G)$ becomes not necessarily unital associative $\mathbb{C}$-algebra. It is called the Hecke algebra of *G* and is denoted by $\mathfrak{H}(G)$. The algebra plays an important role in the study of smooth representations of locally profinite groups. Indeed, one has the following: given a smooth representation $(\rho, V)$ of *G*, we define a new action on *V*:

$$\rho(f) = \int_G f(g) \rho(g) d\mu(g).$$

Thus, we have the functor $\rho \mapsto \rho$ from the category of smooth representations of $G$ to the category of non-degenerate $\mathfrak{H}(G)$-modules. Here, "non-degenerate" means $\rho(\mathfrak{H}(G))V=V$. Then the fact is that the functor is an [equivalence](wikipedia://en/379051).[^o9]
