# Pamela Gorkin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pamela Gorkin"}

*Image: Grivaux Bayart Mortini Gorkin 2006 MFO8218.jpg.* *From left:* Raymond Mortini, Sophie Grivaux, Frederic Bayart, and Pamela Gorkin, 2006 at the [MFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach)

**Pamela Gorkin** is an American mathematician specializing in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675). She is a professor of mathematics at [Bucknell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucknell_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Gorkin earned bachelor's and master's degrees in statistics from [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) in 1976. She then shifted to [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) for her doctoral studies, completing her Ph.D. at Michigan State in 1982, the same year she joined the Bucknell Faculty. Her dissertation, *Decompositions of the Maximal Ideal Space of L*, was supervised by [Sheldon Axler](wikipedia://en/30456092).

At Bucknell, she was Presidential Professor from 2001 to 2004.

## Books {#Books}

With Ulrich Daepp, Gorkin is the author of the undergraduate textbook *Reading, Writing, and Proving: A Closer Look at Mathematics* (Springer, 2003; 2nd ed., 2011).

With Daepp, Andrew Shaffer, and Karl Voss, she is the author of *[Finding Ellipses: What Blaschke Products, Poncelet’s Theorem, and the Numerical Range Know about Each Other](wikipedia://en/68609370)* ([Carus Mathematical Monographs](wikipedia://en/2941952), MAA Press, 2018). The book studies a connection between [Blaschke products](wikipedia://en/981855), [Poncelet's closure theorem](wikipedia://en/2412120), and the [numerical range](wikipedia://en/15581094) of [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859). A Blaschke product is a certain kind of mapping of the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) to itself, and the ones considered in the first part of the book have order three (they map the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) three-to-one to itself, so that each point on the unit circle has three preimages). These triples of preimages form triangles that are all inscribed in the unit circle, and (it turns out) they all circumscribe an ellipse. Thus, they form an infinite system of polygons inscribed in and circumscribing two conics, as Poncelet's theorem describes. The ellipse is the boundary of the numerical range of a certain matrix derived from the Blaschke product, a region within which the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the matrix can be found, and in this case the eigenvalues are at the [foci](wikipedia://en/1032607) of the ellipse. The book tells "a story of discovery" outlining these connections, extends similar results to Blaschke products of higher order, and outlines a plan for further research in this area.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Gorkin was the 2018 [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer). Her lecture was on "Finding Ellipses", the topic of one of her books. She is also the recipient of Bucknell's Lindback Award for Distinguished Teaching, and the Crawford Distinguished Teaching Award of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).
