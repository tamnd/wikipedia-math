# Symbolic power of an ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Symbolic power of an ideal"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), given a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) $R$ and an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) $I$ in it, the ***n*-th symbolic power** of $I$ is the ideal

:::

$$I^{(n)} = \bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in \operatorname{Ass}(R/I)} \varphi_{\mathfrak p}^{-1}(I^n R_{\mathfrak p})$$

where $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of $R$ at $\mathfrak p$, we set $\varphi_{\mathfrak p}\colon R \to R_{\mathfrak p}$ is the canonical map from a ring to its localization, and the intersection runs through all of the [associated primes](wikipedia://en/17286442) of $R/I$.

Though this definition does not require $I$ to be [prime](wikipedia://en/24928), this assumption is often worked with because in the case of a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928), the symbolic power can be equivalently defined as the $I$-[primary component](wikipedia://en/1208391) of $I^n$. Very roughly, it consists of functions with zeros of order $n$ along the variety defined by $I$. We have: $I^{(1)} = I$ and if $I$ is a [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164), then $I^{(n)} = I^n$.

Symbolic powers induce the following chain of ideals:

$$I^{(0)}=R\supset I=I^{(1)}\supset I^{(2)}\supset I^{(3)}\supset I^{(4)}\supset \cdots$$

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The study and use of symbolic powers has a long history in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). [Krull's](wikipedia://en/964328) famous proof of his [principal ideal theorem](wikipedia://en/542421) uses them in an essential way. They first arose after [primary decompositions](wikipedia://en/1208391) were proved for [Noetherian rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_rings). [Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) used symbolic powers in his study of the analytic [normality](wikipedia://en/48629) of [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties). [Chevalley's](wikipedia://en/366040) famous lemma comparing [topologies](wikipedia://en/29954) states that in a [complete local domain](wikipedia://en/153391) the symbolic powers [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of any [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) is [finer](wikipedia://en/393069) than the [*m*-adic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28algebra%29). A crucial step in the vanishing theorem on [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091) of Hartshorne and Lichtenbaum uses that for a prime $I$ defining a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in a [complete local domain](wikipedia://en/153391), the powers of $I$ are [cofinal](wikipedia://en/1633290) with the symbolic powers of $I$. This important property of being [cofinal](wikipedia://en/6693) was further developed by Schenzel in the 1970s.[^o8]

:::

## In algebraic geometry {#In_algebraic_geometry}

Though [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371) for *ordinary powers* of $I$ are well understood when $I$ is given in terms of its generators as $I = (f_1, \ldots, f_k)$, it is still very difficult in many cases to determine the generators of symbolic powers of $I$. But in the [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) setting, there is a clear geometric interpretation in the case when $I$ is a [radical ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_ideal) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero).

If $X$ is an [irreducible](wikipedia://en/2652476) [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) whose ideal of vanishing is $I$, then the *[differential power](wikipedia://en/8056148)* of $I$ consists of all the [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) in $R$ that vanish to order ≥ *n* on $X$, i.e.

$$I^{\langle n\rangle} := \{ f \in R \mid f \text{ vanishes to order} \geq n \text{ on all of }X \}.$$

Or equivalently, if $\mathbf{m}_p$ is the [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) for a point $p\in X$, $I^{\langle n\rangle}=\bigcap _{p \in X} \mathbf{m}_p^n$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Nagata, Zariski)"}

**Theorem (Nagata, Zariski)**[^o6] Let $I$ be a prime ideal in a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $K[x_1, \ldots , x_N ]$ over an algebraically closed field. Then

$$I^{(m)}=I^{\langle m\rangle}$$

This result can be extended to any [radical ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_ideal).[^o7] This formulation is very useful because, in [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero), we can compute the differential powers in terms of generators as:

$$I^{\langle m\rangle}=\left\langle f \mid \frac{\partial^\mathbf{a}f}{\partial x^\mathbf{a}}\in I \text{ for all }\mathbf{a}\in \mathbb{N} ^N \text{ where }|\mathbf{a}|=\sum_{i=1}^N a_i\leq m-1 \right\rangle$$

For another formulation, we can consider the case when the base [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603). In this case, we can interpret the *n*-th symbolic power as the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of all function [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) over $X = \operatorname{Spec}(R) \text{ vanishing to order} \geq n \text{ at } Z=V(I)$ In fact, if $X$ is a [smooth variety](wikipedia://en/35033819) over a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088), then

$I^ {(n)} = \{f \in R \mid f \in \mathbf{m}^ n \text{ for every closed point }\mathbf{m} \in Z\}$[^o8]

:::

## Containments {#Containments}

It is natural to consider whether or not symbolic powers agree with ordinary powers, i.e. does $I^n=I^{(n)}$ hold? In general this is not the case. One example of this is the prime ideal $P =(x^4-yz,\, y^2-xz, \,x^3y-z^2) \subseteq K[x,y,z]$. Here we have that $P^2\neq P^{(2)}$.[^o8] However, $P^2\subset P^{(2)}$ does hold and the generalization of this [inclusion](wikipedia://en/11728075) is well understood. Indeed, the containment $I^n\subseteq I^{(n)}$follows from the definition. Further, it is known that $I^r\subseteq I^{(m)}$ if and only if $m\leq r$. The proof follows from [Nakayama's lemma](wikipedia://en/1835001).[^o10]

There has been extensive study into the other containment, when symbolic powers are contained in ordinary powers of ideals, referred to as the Containment Problem. Once again this has an easily stated answer summarized in the following theorem. It was developed by Ein, Lazarfeld, and Smith in characteristic zero [^o11] and was expanded to [positive characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) by Hochster and Huneke.[^o12] Their papers both build upon the results of [Irena Swanson](wikipedia://en/59004974) in *Linear Equivalence of Ideal Topologies* (2000).[^o13]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Ein, Lazarfeld, Smith; Hochster, Huneke)"}

**Theorem (Ein, Lazarfeld, Smith; Hochster, Huneke)** Let $I\subset K[x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_N]$ be a [homogeneous ideal](wikipedia://en/103094). Then the inclusion

$I^{(m)}\subset I^r$ holds for all $m\geq Nr.$

It was later verified that the [bound](wikipedia://en/42693) of $N$ in the theorem cannot be tightened for general ideals.[^o15] However, following a question posed[^o15] by Bocci, Harbourne, and Huneke, it was discovered that a better bound exists in some cases.

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem** The inclusion $I^{(m)}\subseteq I^r$ for all $m\geq Nr-N+1$ holds

1. for arbitrary ideals in characteristic 2;[^o17]
1. for [monomial ideals](wikipedia://en/43023424) in arbitrary characteristic[^o10]
1. for ideals of [d-stars](wikipedia://en/313303)[^o15]
1. for ideals of general points in [$\mathbb{P}^ 2 \text{ and }\mathbb{P}^ 3$](wikipedia://en/242135)[^o18][^o19]

:::
