# Rectified truncated icosahedron

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Rectified truncated icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **rectified truncated icosahedron** is a convex [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It has 92 faces: 60 [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428), 12 [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon), and 20 [regular hexagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon). It is constructed as a [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366), [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282), rectification truncating vertices down to mid-edges.

:::

As a [near-miss Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/3881930), under [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844), the pentagons are always regular, although the hexagons, while having equal edge lengths, do not have the same edge lengths with the pentagons, having slightly different but alternating angles, causing the triangles to be [isosceles](wikipedia://en/315428) instead. The shape is a [symmetrohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrohedron) with notation *I(1,2,\*,\[2\])*

## Dual {#Dual}

By [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705), the dual polyhedron can be called a *joined truncated icosahedron*, **jtI**, but it is topologically equivalent to the [rhombic enneacontahedron](wikipedia://en/1707682) with all rhombic faces.
