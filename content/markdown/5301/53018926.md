# Mary de Lellis Gough

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mary de Lellis Gough"}

Sister **Mary de Lellis Gough** (15 February 1892 – 7 April 1983) was an Irish nun who spent most of her life in the USA. She is notable for being the earliest known Irish woman to earn a doctorate in mathematics.

:::

## Life {#Life}

She was born in [Kilmore, County Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmore,_County_Wexford), Ireland. Her parents were Ellen Dunne and Walter Gough. She attended the local St John of God's primary school.[^o8] She emigrated to Texas in 1909 with a group of young Irish women, and joined the [Sisters of Charity of the Incarnate Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_the_Incarnate_Word), taking vows as Mary de Lellis in 1911.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

While working as a high school teacher, she studied at the [Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America). She graduated in 1920, earned a master's degree in 1923, and completed her PhD from the same university in 1931.[^o4] Her PhD dissertation was entitled *On the condition for the existence of triangles in and circumscribed to certain types of the rational quartic curve and having a common side* and supervised by [Aubrey E. Landry](wikipedia://en/74358250).[^o4]

She taught mathematics at [Incarnate Word College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Incarnate_Word) from 1920 to 1943.[^o4] In 1953, she coined the term "[mathephobia](wikipedia://en/8111444)" based on her observations of her students.[^o7]

In 1944, she left teaching due to health issues and returned to Texas where she worked as an accountant at a local hospital in Fort Worth. She retired in 1964.[^o8]
