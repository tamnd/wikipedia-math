# Josephine Chanler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Josephine Chanler"}

**Josephine Hughes Chanler** (7 April 1906 – 23 December 1992) was an American mathematician specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

She was born in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri). She was the only child of Louisa Castle and James Chanler. Her parents separated soon after her birth, and she grew up with her mother, who lived in [Bowling Green, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green,_Kentucky) and worked as a schoolteacher. As a girl she became ill with [polio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polio), and had to undergo treatment for it at intervals later in her life. When she was a high school student, she and her mother moved to [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida), but they moved back to Bowling Green so that Chanler could attend the [Western Kentucky State Normal School and Teacher's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Kentucky_University). She completed a degree there in 1927, and became a high school teacher.[^o4]

In 1929, Chanler and her mother moved again, to [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois), so that Chanler could begin graduate studies at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). She earned a master's degree under the mentorship of [Bessie Irving Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessie_Irving_Miller), but Miller died soon afterward.[^o4] She completed her doctorate there in 1933.[^o5][^o6] Her dissertation, *Poristic Double Binary Forms*, was [supervised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) by [Arthur Byron Coble](wikipedia://en/17821492).

## Career {#Career}

After completing her PhD, she joined the faculty at Illinois,[^o9] and became an associate professor in 1944.[^o10] Despite an offer to move to a college in [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), Chanler remained at Illinois for the rest of her career.[^o4] She retired as an associate professor in 1971.[^o4] After her retirement, she continued to tutor students.[^o9]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Chanler lived with her mother until her mother's death in 1948.[^o4]

Chanler was a [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_%28United_States%29), a member of [Toastmasters International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toastmasters_International), and a member of St. Mary's Catholic Church.[^o4] She listed her religion in about 1940 as [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism), but she described herself in a 1983 conversation as "a Southern Presbyterian–Calvinist–Catholic with leanings toward Judaism and Islam and a very high regard for Buddhism, Mormons, and some Hindu gods".[^o4]

Chanler died in [Urbana, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbana,_Illinois) in 1992, having been in a health care facility for five years prior; she is buried there at Mount Hope Cemetery.[^o4] She was remembered as "an excellent teacher and a person that everyone seemed to admire" by Annette Sinclair, who once took a summer course in classical algebraic geometry with Chanler.[^o12]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The Josephine Chanler Scholarship for undergraduate students at UI was created in 2020 by Padmini Joshi.[^o14]
