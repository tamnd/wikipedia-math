# Josephine Burns Glasgow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Josephine Burns Glasgow"}

**Josephine Elizabeth Burns Glasgow** (July 22, 1887 – January 22, 1969) was an American mathematician whose Ph.D. thesis, "The abstract definitions of the [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of degree 8" was published in the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)*.[^o3][^o6] She was the second woman to receive a PhD from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Glasgow was born in [Greenville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_Illinois). She was the third of four children. Her parents were Ida Jane and James Clinton Burns. Her father was a professor of history at [Western Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Illinois_University).[^o5]

She married Robert D. Glasgow in 1916.[^o6]

In 1928 Glasgow's husband took a post as state [entomologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomologist) of [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State) and the couple moved to [Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York). Josephine became active in the [American Association of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Women), rising through various roles to be the vice president of the national board of directors in 1952. In 1951 the Albany section named a fellowship in her honour.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Glasgow received her A.B. degree in 1909 and her master's degree in mathematics in 1911.[^o6] She received her PhD from the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) in 1913, being the second woman to do so at that university. Her doctoral supervisor was [George Abram Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Abram_Miller).[^o5]

She was an instructor at the University of Illinois.[^o5]
