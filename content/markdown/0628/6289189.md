# Isotoxal figure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isotoxal figure"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) (for example, a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470)) or a [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) is **isotoxal** (el) or **edge-transitive** if its [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741) act [transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_group_action) on its [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034). Informally, this means that there is only one type of edge to the object: given two edges, there is a [translation](wikipedia://en/204682), [rotation](wikipedia://en/410009), and/or [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829) that will move one edge to the other while leaving the region occupied by the object unchanged.

:::

## Isotoxal polygons {#Isotoxal_polygons}

An isotoxal polygon is an even-sided i.e. [equilateral polygon](wikipedia://en/214547), but not all equilateral polygons are isotoxal. The [duals](wikipedia://en/609737#Dimension-reversing_dualities) of isotoxal polygons are [isogonal polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isogonal_polygon). Isotoxal $4n$-gons are [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry), thus are also [zonogons](wikipedia://en/55312103).

In general, a (non-regular) isotoxal $2n$-gon has $\mathrm{D}_n, (^*nn)$ [dihedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry). For example, a (non-square) [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884) is an isotoxal "$2$×$2$-gon" (quadrilateral) with $\mathrm{D}_2, (^*22)$ symmetry. All [regular ${\color{royalblue}n}$-gons](wikipedia://en/333306) (also with odd $n$) are isotoxal, having double the minimum symmetry order: a regular $n$-gon has $\mathrm{D}_n, (^*nn)$ dihedral symmetry.

An isotoxal $\mathbf{2}n$-gon with outer internal angle $\alpha$ can be denoted by $\{n_\alpha\}.$ The inner internal angle $(\beta)$ may be less or greater than $180$[${\color{royalblue}^\mathsf{o}},$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_%28geometry%29) making convex or concave polygons respectively.

A [star ${\color{royalblue}\mathbf{2}n}$-gon](wikipedia://en/313303) can also be isotoxal, denoted by $\{(n/q)_\alpha\},$ with $q \le n - 1$ and with the [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354) $\gcd(n,q) = 1,$ where $q$ is the [turning number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_number) or [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_%28polygon%29).[^o3] Concave inner vertices can be defined for $q < n/2.$ If $D = \gcd(n,q) \ge 2,$ then $\{(n/q)_\alpha\} = \{(Dm/Dp)_\alpha\}$ is "reduced" to a compound $D \{(m/p)_\alpha\}$ of $D$ rotated copies of $\{(m/p)_\alpha\}.$

Caution:

The vertices of $\{(n/q)_\alpha\}$ are not always placed like those of $\{n_\alpha\},$ whereas the vertices of the regular $\{n/q\}$ are placed like those of the regular $\{n\}.$

A set of ["uniform" tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635#Uniform_tilings_using_star_polygons), actually [isogonal tilings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isogonal_tiling) using isotoxal polygons as less symmetric faces than regular ones, can be defined.

## Isotoxal polyhedra and tilings {#Isotoxal_polyhedra_and_tilings}

[Regular polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedra) are isohedral (face-transitive), isogonal (vertex-transitive), and isotoxal (edge-transitive).

[Quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651) polyhedra, like the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) and the [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023), are isogonal and isotoxal, but not isohedral. Their duals, including the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) and the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025), are isohedral and isotoxal, but not isogonal.

Not every [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or 2-dimensional [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) constructed from [regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polygons) is isotoxal. For instance, the [truncated icosahedron](wikipedia://en/31282) (the familiar soccerball) is not isotoxal, as it has two edge types: hexagon-hexagon and hexagon-pentagon, and it is not possible for a symmetry of the solid to move a hexagon-hexagon edge onto a hexagon-pentagon edge.

An isotoxal polyhedron has the same [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/7297972) for all edges.

The dual of a convex polyhedron is also a convex polyhedron.[^o9]

The dual of a non-convex polyhedron is also a non-convex polyhedron.[^o9] (By contraposition.)

The dual of an isotoxal polyhedron is also an isotoxal polyhedron. (See the [Dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) article.)

There are nine [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) isotoxal polyhedra: the five ([regular](wikipedia://en/333299)) [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), the two ([quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651)) common cores of dual Platonic solids, and their two duals.

There are thirty-eight non-convex isotoxal polyhedra:

- the four regular [Kepler-Poinsot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot) polyhedra
- their two rectifications and their duals
- the three ditrigonary uniforms and their duals
- nine out of the ten uniform hemipolyhedra (all except the [great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030))
- four isotoxal polyhedra constructed as the duals of dyadic interpretations of degenerate polyhedral compounds
- eleven isotoxal polyhedra which are neither isohedral nor isogonal[^o10]

There are at least five isotoxal polyhedral compounds: the five [regular polyhedral compounds](wikipedia://en/23901); their five duals are also the five regular polyhedral compounds (or one chiral twin).

There are at least five isotoxal polygonal tilings of the Euclidean plane, and infinitely many isotoxal polygonal tilings of the hyperbolic plane, including the Wythoff constructions from the [regular hyperbolic tilings](wikipedia://en/505253#Hyperbolic_tilings) {*p*,*q*}, and non-right (*p q r*) groups.
