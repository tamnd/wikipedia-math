# Isohedral figure

*Image: Dice Set.jpg.* A set of isohedral dice

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isohedral figure"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) of dimension $2$ (a plane tiling) or higher, or a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) of dimension $3$ (a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470)) or higher, is **isohedral** or **face-transitive** if all its [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) are the same. More specifically, all faces must be not merely [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) but must be *transitive*, i.e. must lie within the same *[symmetry orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_orbit)*. In other words, for any two faces $A$ and $B$, there must be a symmetry of the *entire* figure by [translations](wikipedia://en/204682), [rotations](wikipedia://en/410009), and/or [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829) that maps $A$ onto $B$. For this reason, [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) isohedral polyhedra are the shapes that will make [fair dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_dice).[^o2]

:::

Isohedral polyhedra are called **isohedra**. They can be described by their [face configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_configuration). An isohedron has an [even](wikipedia://en/143135) number of faces.

The [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) of an isohedral polyhedron is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), i.e. isogonal. The [Catalan solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_solids), the Platonic Solids, the [bipyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipyramids), and the [trapezohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezohedra) are all isohedral. They are the duals of the (isogonal) [Archimedean solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_solids), [Platonic Solids](wikipedia://en/23905), [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), and [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851), respectively. The [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids), which are either self-dual or dual with another Platonic solid, are vertex-, edge-, and face-transitive (i.e. isogonal, isotoxal, and isohedral).

A form that is isohedral, has regular vertices, and is also [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive) (i.e. isotoxal) is said to be a [quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651) dual. Some theorists regard these figures as truly quasiregular because they share the same symmetries, but this is not generally accepted.

A polyhedron which is isohedral and isogonal is said to be [noble](wikipedia://en/9352244).

Not all isozonohedra[^o3] are isohedral.[^o4] For example, a [rhombic icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3691443) is an isozonohedron but not an isohedron.[^o5]

## *k*-isohedral figure {#k-isohedral_figure}

A polyhedron (or polytope in general) is ***k*-isohedral** if it contains *k* [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) within its symmetry fundamental domains.[^o13] Similarly, a ***k*-isohedral tiling** has *k* separate symmetry orbits (it may contain *m* different face shapes, for *m* = *k*, or only for some *m* \< *k*).[^o14] ("1-isohedral" is the same as "isohedral".)

A **monohedral** polyhedron or [monohedral tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monohedral_tiling) (*m* = 1) has congruent faces, either directly or reflectively, which occur in one or more symmetry positions. An ***m*-hedral** polyhedron or tiling has *m* different face shapes ("*dihedral*", "*trihedral*"... are the same as "2-hedral", "3-hedral"... respectively).[^o15]

## Related terms {#Related_terms}

A **cell-transitive** or **isochoric** figure is an *n*-[polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) (*n* ≥ 4) or *n*-[honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) (*n* ≥ 3) that has its [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) congruent and transitive with each others. In 3 dimensions, the [catoptric honeycombs](wikipedia://en/38829912), duals to the uniform honeycombs, are isochoric. In 4 dimensions, isochoric polytopes have been enumerated up to 20 cells.[^o22]

A **facet-transitive** or **isotopic** figure is an *n*-dimensional polytope or honeycomb with its [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) ((*n*−1)-[faces](wikipedia://en/149926)) congruent and transitive. The [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope) of an *isotope* is an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) polytope. By definition, this isotopic property is common to the duals of the [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).

- An isotopic 2-dimensional figure is isotoxal, i.e. edge-transitive.
- An isotopic 3-dimensional figure is isohedral, i.e. face-transitive.
- An isotopic 4-dimensional figure is isochoric, i.e. cell-transitive.
