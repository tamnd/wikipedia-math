# Bs space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bs space"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the space **bs** consists of all infinite [sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28mathematics%29) (*x*<sub>*i*</sub>) of [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) $\mathbb{R}$ or [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) $\mathbb{C}$ such that

$$\sup_n \left|\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right|$$

is finite. The set of such sequences forms a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) with the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) operations defined [componentwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Componentwise_operation), and the norm given by

$$\|x\|_{bs} = \sup_n \left|\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right|.$$

:::

Furthermore, with respect to [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) induced by this norm, *bs* is [complete](wikipedia://en/7832): it is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989).

The space of all sequences $\left(x_i\right)$ such that the [series](wikipedia://en/15287)

$$\sum_{i=1}^\infty x_i$$

is [convergent](wikipedia://en/1743431) (possibly [conditionally](wikipedia://en/1198956)) is denoted by *cs*. This is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [vector subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_subspace) of *bs*, and so is also a Banach space with the same norm.

The space *bs* is [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to the [Space of bounded sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_of_bounded_sequences) $\ell^{\infty}$ via the mapping

$$T(x_1, x_2, \ldots) = (x_1, x_1+x_2, x_1+x_2+x_3, \ldots).$$

Furthermore, the [space of convergent sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_of_convergent_sequences) [*c*](wikipedia://en/17543372) is the image of *cs* under $T.$
