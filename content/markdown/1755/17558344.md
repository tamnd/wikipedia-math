# Mikko Kaasalainen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mikko Kaasalainen"}

**Mikko K.J. Kaasalainen** (1965 – 12 April 2020) was a [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finns) [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist). He was professor of mathematics at the department of mathematics at [Tampere University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampere_University_of_Technology). Kaasalainen mostly worked on [inverse problems](wikipedia://en/203956) and their applications especially in astrophysics, as well as on [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o3][^o24]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Kaasalainen received an MSc in theoretical physics at the [University of Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helsinki) in 1990, moving shortly afterwards to [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford) where he completed his DPhil in theoretical physics in 1994, supervised by [James Binney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Binney). After a series of post-doctoral and senior positions in Europe, he moved to the University of Helsinki and to his present institute in 2009. He led a research group in the Finnish Centre of Excellence in Inverse Problems Research.

Kaasalainen was awarded the first [Pertti Lindfors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertti_Lindfors) prize of the Finnish Inverse Problems Society in 2001. The asteroid [16007&nbsp;Kaasalainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16007&nbsp;Kaasalainen), discovered by [ODAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCA–DLR_Asteroid_Survey) in 1999, was named in his honour.[^o24] The official 16007 was published by the [Minor Planet Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Center) on 7 January 2004 ([M.P.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Planet_Circulars) 50463).[^o25]

## Research {#Research}

Kaasalainen's research interests mostly focused on mathematical modelling in various fields ranging from [remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) and space research to planetary and galactic dynamics. Typically, the models and mathematical methods Kaasalainen developed with his colleagues are connected with inverse problems. Two such topics featured prominently in Kaasalainen's research:

- Asteroid [lightcurve inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightcurve_inversion),[^o6] i.e., the reconstruction of the shapes and spin states of [asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) from their brightness measurements ([lightcurves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_curve)), based on mathematical results and uniqueness and stability theorems[^o7][^o8][^o9] that have been transformed into modelling algorithms with which a multitude of otherwise unresolvable asteroids can now be mapped.[^o10] This method has also been used in the direct verification of the [Yarkovsky–O'Keefe–Radzievskii–Paddack effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkovsky–O'Keefe–Radzievskii–Paddack_effect) in the [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System).[^o11][^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]
- Analysis of large dynamical systems, where [torus construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torus_construction) methods[^o21][^o22] in phase space allow a compact representation or approximation of the dynamics of the observed system (such as a [galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy)).
