# Hopf–Whitney theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hopf–Whitney theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), the **Hopf–Whitney theorem** is a result relating the [homotopy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_classes) between a [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) and a multiply connected space with [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) classes of the former with coefficients in the first nontrivial [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) of the latter. It can for example be used to calculate [cohomotopy](wikipedia://en/682693) as [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859) are multiply connected.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

For a $n$-dimensional [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022) $X$ and a $n-1$-[connected space](wikipedia://en/6233) $Y$, the well-defined map:

$$[X,Y]\rightarrow H^n(X,\pi_n(Y)), [f]\mapsto f^*\iota$$

with a certain [cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_class) $\iota\in H^n(Y,\pi_n(Y))$ is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828).

The [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587) claims that the well-defined map $\pi_n(Y)\rightarrow H_n(Y,\mathbb{Z}),[f]\mapsto f_*[S^n]$ with a [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) $[S^n]\in H_n(S^n,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ is an isomorphism and that $H_{n-1}(Y,\mathbb{Z})\cong 1$, which implies $\operatorname{Ext}_\mathbb{Z}^1(H_{n-1}(Y,\mathbb{Z}),\pi_n(Y))\cong 1$ for the [Ext functor](wikipedia://en/840758). The [Universal coefficient theorem](wikipedia://en/996107) then simplifies and claims:

$$H^n(Y,\pi_n(Y)) \cong\operatorname{Hom}_\mathbb{Z}(H_n(Y,\mathbb{Z}),\pi_n(Y)) \cong\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{Z}(\pi_n(Y)).$$

$\iota\in H^n(Y,\pi_n(Y))$ is then the cohomology class corresponding to the [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_map) $\operatorname{id} \in\operatorname{End}_\mathbb{Z}(\pi_n(Y))$.

In the [Postnikov tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postnikov_tower) removing [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) from above, the space $Y_n$ only has a single nontrivial homotopy group $\pi_n(Y_n)\cong\pi_n(Y)$ and hence is an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(\pi_n(Y),n)$ (up to [weak homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_homotopy_equivalence)), which classifies [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology). Combined with the canonical map $Y\rightarrow Y_n\simeq K(\pi_n(Y),n)$, the map from the Hopf–Whitney theorem can alternatively be expressed as a postcomposition:

$$[X,Y]\rightarrow[X,K(\pi_n(Y),n)]\cong H^n(X,\pi_n(Y)).$$

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For homotopy groups, cohomotopy sets or cohomology, the Hopf–Whitney theorem reproduces known results but weaker:

- For every $n-1$-connected space $Y$ one has:

$$[S^n,Y] \cong H^n(S^n,\pi_n(Y)) \cong\pi_n(Y).$$

In general, this holds for every topological space by definition.

- For a $n$-dimensional CW complex $X$ one has:

$$[X,S^n] \cong H^n(X,\pi_n(S^n)) \cong H^n(X,\mathbb{Z}).$$

For $n=1$, this also follows from $S^1\simeq K(\mathbb{Z},1)$.

- For a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) $G$ and a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$, the Eilenberg–MacLane space $K(G,n)$ is $n-1$-connected by construction, hence for every $n-1$-dimensional CW-complex $X$ one has:

$$[X,K(G,n)] \cong H^n\left(X,\pi_nK(G,n)\right) \cong H^n(X,G)$$

In general, this holds for every [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). The Hopf–Whitney theorem produces a weaker result because the fact that the higher homotopy groups of an Eilenberg–MacLane space also vanish does not enter.
