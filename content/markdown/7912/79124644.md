# Gromoll–Meyer sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gromoll–Meyer sphere"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), the **Gromoll–Meyer sphere** is a special seven-dimensional [exotic sphere](wikipedia://en/1114931) with several unique properties. It is named after [Detlef Gromoll](wikipedia://en/18026868) and Wolfgang Meyer, who first described it in detail in 1974, although it was already found by [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) in 1956.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Brieskorn sphere {#Brieskorn_sphere}

In $\mathbb{C}^5$ consider the [complex variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variety) ([Brieskorn manifold](wikipedia://en/34748487)):

$$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^3+e^5=0.$$

A description of the Gromoll–Meyer sphere is the intersection of the above variety with a small sphere around the origin.

### Lie group biquotient {#Lie_group_biquotient}

The first [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) $\operatorname{Sp}(1)$ (isomorphic to $\operatorname{SU}(2)$) acts on the second symplectic group $\operatorname{Sp}(2)$ (isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spin}(5)$) with the embedding $\operatorname{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(2), q\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(q,q)$ and multiplication from the left as well as the embedding $\operatorname{Sp}(1)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(2), q\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(q,1)$ and multiplication from the right. A description of the Gromoll–Meyer sphere is the biquotient space:

$$\operatorname{Sp}(1)\backslash\operatorname{Sp}(2)/\operatorname{Sp}(1).$$

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- It is the only seven-dimensional exotic sphere which can be expressed as a biquotient of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945).
- It can be expressed as a $S^3$-[fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) over $S^4$ and hence is a [Milnor sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_sphere). Such bundles also include the quaternionic [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384), whose total space is the ordinary $S^7$.
- It generates the seventh [Kervaire–Milnor group](wikipedia://en/79124653) $\Theta_7\cong\mathbb{Z}_{28}$.

:::
