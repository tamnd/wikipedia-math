# Coherent set of characters

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coherent set of characters"}

In mathematical [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), **coherence** is a property of sets of [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) that allows one to extend an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) from the degree-zero subspace of a space of characters to the whole space. The general notion of coherence was developed by (txt), as a generalization of the proof by Frobenius of the existence of a Frobenius kernel of a [Frobenius group](wikipedia://en/1716123) and of the work of Brauer and Suzuki on [exceptional characters](wikipedia://en/29780460). (Feit Thompson 1963, Chapter 3) developed coherence further in the proof of the [Feit–Thompson theorem](wikipedia://en/1656473) that all [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of odd order are solvable.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose that *H* is a subgroup of a finite group *G*, and *S* a set of [irreducible characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_character) of *H*. Write *I*(*S*) for the set of integral linear combinations of *S*, and *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) for the subset of degree 0 elements of *I*(*S*). Suppose that τ is an isometry from *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) to the degree 0 virtual characters of *G*. Then τ is called **coherent** if it can be extended to an isometry from *I*(*S*) to characters of *G* and *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) is non-zero. Although strictly speaking coherence is really a property of the isometry τ, it is common to say that the set *S* is coherent instead of saying that τ is coherent.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Feit's theorem"}

## Feit's theorem {#Feit's_theorem}

Feit proved several theorems giving conditions under which a set of characters is coherent. A typical one is as follows. Suppose that *H* is a subgroup of a group *G* with [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) *N*, such that *N* is a Frobenius group with kernel *H*, and let *S* be the irreducible characters of *N* that do not have *H* in their kernel. Suppose that τ is a linear isometry from *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) into the degree 0 characters of *G*. Then τ is coherent unless

- either *H* is an elementary abelian group and *N*/*H* acts simply transitively on its non-identity elements (in which case *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) is zero)
- or *H* is a non-abelian *p*-group for some prime *p* whose abelianization has order at most 4|*N*/*H*|<sup>2</sup>+1.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

If *G* is the simple group SL<sub>2</sub>(**F**<sub>2<sup>*n*</sup></sub>) for *n*>1 and *H* is a Sylow 2-subgroup, with τ induction, then coherence fails for the first reason: *H* is [elementary abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_abelian) and *N*/*H* has order 2<sup>*n*</sup>–1 and acts simply transitively on it.

If *G* is the simple Suzuki group of order (2<sup>*n*</sup>–1) 2<sup>2*n*</sup>( 2<sup>2*n*</sup>+1) with *n* odd and *n*>1 and *H* is the Sylow 2-subgroup and τ is induction, then coherence fails for the second reason. The abelianization of *H* has order 2<sup>*n*</sup>, while the group *N*/*H* has order 2<sup>*n*</sup>–1.

:::

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In the proof of the Frobenius theory about the existence of a kernel of a Frobenius group *G* where the subgroup *H* is the subgroup fixing a point and *S* is the set of all irreducible characters of *H*, the isometry τ on *I*<sub>0</sub>(*S*) is just induction, although its extension to *I*(*S*) is not induction.

Similarly in the theory of [exceptional characters](wikipedia://en/29780460) the isometry τ is again induction.

In more complicated cases the isometry τ is no longer induction. For example, in the [Feit–Thompson theorem](wikipedia://en/1656473) the isometry τ is the [Dade isometry](wikipedia://en/29775880).

:::
