# Bagplot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bagplot"}

*Image: Bagplot recent.png.* Example of a bagplot.

A **bagplot** [^o2] is a method in [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691) for visualizing [two-](wikipedia://en/22615608) or three-dimensional statistical data, analogous to the one-dimensional [box plot](wikipedia://en/160960). Introduced in 1999 by [Rousseeuw](wikipedia://en/30760578) et al., the bagplot allows one to visualize the location, spread, [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212), and [outliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outliers) of a data set.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The bagplot consists of three nested [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621), called the "bag", the "fence", and the "loop".

- The inner polygon, called the *bag*, is constructed on the basis of [Tukey depth](wikipedia://en/34627868), the smallest number of observations that can be contained by a [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane) that also contains a given point.[^o6] It contains 50% of the data points. Inside the bag is a cross or asterisk indicating the point with highest depth, called the depth median.
- The outermost of the three polygons, called the *fence* is not drawn as part of the bagplot, but is used to construct it. It is formed by inflating the bag around the depth median by a certain factor (usually 3). Observations outside the fence are flagged as [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951).[^o7]
- The observations that are not marked as outliers are surrounded by a *loop*, the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the observations within the fence.[^o8]

There is an option to connect the points between the bag and fence to the bag, on a line to the depth median [^o9].

The three-dimensional version of the bagplot[^o10] draws the loop in transparent colors, so the bag remains visible.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The bagplot is equivariant under [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) of the plane, and robust against outliers.[^o13]

:::
