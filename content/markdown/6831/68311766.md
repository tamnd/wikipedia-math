# Charles Joel Stone

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Joel Stone"}

**Charles "Chuck" Joel Stone** (July 13, 1936 – April 16, 2019) was an American statistician and mathematician.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Charles Joel Stone was born and raised in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles).[^o4] After secondary school at [North Hollywood High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood_High_School), Stone graduated with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in science from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1958.[^o5][^o6] He then matriculated at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where in 1961 he received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in statistics. His PhD thesis *Limit Theorems for Birth and Death Processes and Diffusion Processes* was supervised by [Samuel Karlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Karlin).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

From 1962 to 1964 Stone was an assistant professor in the mathematics department of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). From 1964 1981 to he was a faculty member of the mathematics department of UCLA ([University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles)), where he worked extensively with [Leo Breiman](wikipedia://en/4909283) (who moved to Berkeley in 1980) and Sidney Charles Port (born 1935).[^o4] In the statistics department of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), Stone was from 1981 a full professor, a position which he held until his retirement as professor emeritus.[^o6]

When they were professors at UCLA, Stone and Breiman consulted for Technology Services Corporation in Santa Monica. Based on their work, they co-authored a 1978 technical report, *Parsimonious Binary Classification Trees*, Elaborating on the report, in 1984 they published, with two more co-authors, [Jerome H. Friedman](wikipedia://en/40671069) and Richard Allen Oshlen (born 1942), a greatly expended version entitled *Classification and Regression Trees*.[^o6]

In addition to research on statistical algorithms, Stone did research on potential theory, "local limit theorems, weak convergence of stochastic processes, and renewal theory."[^o6]

Stone was a Guggenheim Fellow for the academic year 1980–1981.[^o10] In 1986 he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).[^o11] He was elected a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1970 [^o12] and a Fellow of the Class of 2013 (announced in 2012) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). He was elected a Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1993.[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In June 1966 he married Barbara L. Cohn in Los Angeles. They had two sons.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

Stone died on April 16, 2019.[^o6]
