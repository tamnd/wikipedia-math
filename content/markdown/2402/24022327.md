# S. Ramanan

Ramanan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="S. Ramanan"}

**Sundararaman Ramanan** (born 20 July 1937) is an Indian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works in the area of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [moduli spaces](wikipedia://en/361609) and [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). He is one of India's leading mathematicians and recognised as an expert in algebraic geometry, especially in the area of moduli problems. He has also worked in differential geometry: his joint paper with MS Narasimhan on universal connections has been influential. It enabled SS Chern and B Simons to introduce what is known as the Chern-Simons invariant, which has proved useful in theoretical physics.[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He is an alumnus of the [Ramakrishna Mission School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Mission_School) in Chennai and the [Vivekananda College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramakrishna_Mission_Vivekananda_College) in Chennai, where he completed a BA Honours in mathematics. He completed his PhD at the [Tata Institute of Fundamental Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TIFR), under the direction of [MS Narasimhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudumbai_Seshachalu_Narasimhan). He did his post-doctoral studies at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich).

He later pursued a career at TIFR. He picked up the methods of modern differential geometry from the French mathematician [Jean-Louis Koszul](wikipedia://en/5147910),[^o5] and later successfully applied it for his research centred on algebraic geometry. He has also made contributions to the topics of [Abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) and [vector bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_bundles).

## Collaborations and influences {#Collaborations_and_influences}

He collaborated with [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457), who was at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). He has been a visiting professor at Harvard University, [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley), the Institute of Advanced Study in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA), [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), the Max Planck Institute and [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). In 1978, he gave one of the invited talks at the International Congress of Mathematicians in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki). In 1999, he spoke about some aspects of the work of [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) on the occasion of his being awarded the Inamouri Prize.

Ramanan discovered and encouraged [Vijay Kumar Patodi](wikipedia://en/1163110), who proved part of the [Atiyah-Singer index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem), Patodi did his PhD under the combined direction of Narasimhan and Ramanan.[^o7] Ramanan was [MS Raghunathan's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M_S_Raghunathan) senior colleague and influenced him considerably.[^o8]

Ramanan wrote the book *Moduli of Abelian Varieties* with Allan Adler, published by Springer-Verlag, and a graduate-level book on algebraic geometry called *Global Calculus*, published by the American Mathematical Society.[^o9] He continues his contributions via teaching and mentoring at the [Chennai Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/3124675),[^o10] where he is an adjunct professor, and at the [Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Chennai](wikipedia://en/2718996).

## Awards {#Awards}

Ramanan received the [Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Prize), India's highest science prize,[^o12] in 1979; the [TWAS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TWAS_Prize) for Mathematics in 2001[^o13] and the Ramanujan Medal in 2008.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He is married to Anuradha Ramanan, a translator and former librarian. They have two daughters, Sumana Ramanan, a journalist, and [Kavita Ramanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavita_Ramanan),[^o15] a noted mathematician who is a professor of applied mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island).
