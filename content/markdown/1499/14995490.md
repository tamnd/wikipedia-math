# Victor Ginzburg

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Victor Ginzburg"}

**Victor Ginzburg** (born 1957) is a [Russian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and in [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917). He is known for his contributions to geometric representation theory, especially, for his works on representations of [quantum groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_groups) and [Hecke algebras](wikipedia://en/521808), and on the geometric [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) (Satake [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051)). He is currently a Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Ginzburg received his Ph.D. at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1985, under the direction of [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515) and [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643).

Ginzburg wrote a textbook *Representation theory and complex geometry* with [Neil Chriss](wikipedia://en/14663994) on geometric representation theory.

A paper by [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), Ginzburg, and Wolfgang Soergel introduced the concept of [Koszul duality](wikipedia://en/42478011) (cf. [Koszul algebra](wikipedia://en/7987653)) and the technique of "mixed categories" to representation theory. Furthermore, Ginzburg and Mikhail Kapranov developed Koszul duality theory for [operads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operads).

In [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917), Ginzburg defined, following earlier ideas of [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509), the notion of [Calabi–Yau](wikipedia://en/188932) algebra. An important role in the theory of motivic [Donaldson–Thomas invariants](wikipedia://en/32335603) is played by the so-called "Ginzburg dg algebra", a Calabi-Yau (dg)-algebra of dimension 3 associated with any cyclic potential on the [path algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_algebra) of a [quiver](wikipedia://en/355100).

More recently, Ginzburg applied techniques of mixed l-adic sheaves of [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383), [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) to Symplectic duality, a subject closely related to 3-dimensional [Mirror symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_symmetry) and Relative [Langlands duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_duality).
