# Sl2-triple

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sl2-triple"}

In the theory of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), an ***sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple** is a triple of elements of a Lie algebra that satisfy the commutation relations between the standard generators of the [special linear Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/19571780) *sl*<sub>2</sub>. This notion plays an important role in the theory of [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587), especially in regard to their [nilpotent orbits](wikipedia://en/10198492).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Elements {*e*,*h*,*f*} of a Lie algebra *g* form an ***sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple** if

$$[h,e] = 2e, \quad [h,f] = -2f, \quad [e,f] = h.$$

These commutation relations are satisfied by the generators

$$h = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0\\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad e = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1\\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad f = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0\\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

of the Lie algebra *sl*<sub>2</sub> of 2 by 2 matrices with zero [trace](wikipedia://en/43270). It follows that *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triples in *g* are in a bijective correspondence with the Lie algebra [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) from *sl*<sub>2</sub> into *g*.

The alternative notation for the elements of an *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple is {*H*, *X*, *Y*}, with *H* corresponding to *h*, *X* corresponding to *e*, and *Y* corresponding to *f*. H is called a neutral, X is called a nilpositive, and Y is called a nilnegative.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Assume that *g* is a finite dimensional Lie algebra over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero). From the representation theory of the Lie algebra *sl*<sub>2</sub>, one concludes that the Lie algebra *g* decomposes into a direct sum of finite-dimensional subspaces, each of which is isomorphic to *V*<sub>j</sub>, the (*j* + 1)-dimensional simple *sl*<sub>2</sub>-module with [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) *j*. The element *h* of the *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple is semisimple, with the simple [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) *j*, *j* − 2, ..., −*j* on a submodule of *g* isomorphic to *V*<sub>j</sub> . The elements *e* and *f* move between different eigenspaces of *h*, increasing the eigenvalue by 2 in case of *e* and decreasing it by 2 in case of *f*. In particular, *e* and *f* are [nilpotent elements](wikipedia://en/10198492) of the Lie algebra *g*.

Conversely, the *[Jacobson–Morozov theorem](wikipedia://en/34733019)* states that any nilpotent element *e* of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) *g* can be included into an *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple {*e*,*h*,*f*}, and all such triples are conjugate under the action of the group *Z*<sub>*G*</sub>(*e*), the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of *e* in the adjoint [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) *G* corresponding to the Lie algebra *g*.

The semisimple element *h* of any *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple containing a given nilpotent element *e* of *g* is called a **characteristic** of *e*.

An *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple defines a grading on *g* according to the eigenvalues of *h*:

$$g = \bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} g_j,\quad [h, a]= ja {\ \ }\textrm{ for } {\ \ } a\in g_j.$$

The *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple is called **even** if only even *j* occur in this decomposition, and **odd** otherwise.

If *g* is a semisimple Lie algebra, then *g*<sub>0</sub> is a [reductive](wikipedia://en/1337587) Lie subalgebra of *g* (it is not semisimple in general). Moreover, the direct sum of the eigenspaces of *h* with non-negative eigenvalues is a [parabolic subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_subalgebra) of *g* with the Levi component *g*<sub>0</sub>.

If the elements of an *sl*<sub>2</sub>-triple are [regular](wikipedia://en/31861096), then their span is called a [principal subalgebra](wikipedia://en/31864145).
