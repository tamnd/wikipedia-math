# Apeirogonal hosohedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirogonal hosohedron"}

Reg tiling stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apeirogonal hosohedron** or **infinite hosohedron**[^o2] is a tiling of the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) consisting of two [vertices at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139). It may be considered an improper [regular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tiling) of the [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417) plane, with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{2,\infty\}.$

:::

## Related tilings and polyhedra {#Related_tilings_and_polyhedra}

The apeirogonal hosohedron is the arithmetic limit of the family of [hosohedra](wikipedia://en/2316192) {2,*p*}, as *p* tends to [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), thereby turning the hosohedron into a Euclidean tiling. All the vertices have then receded to infinity and the digonal faces are no longer defined by closed circuits of finite edges.

Similarly to the [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) and the [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635), eight uniform tilings may be based from the regular apeirogonal tiling. The [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) and [cantellated](wikipedia://en/6317514) forms are duplicated, and as two times infinity is also infinity, the [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) and [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) forms are also duplicated, therefore reducing the number of unique forms to four: the [apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/35870958), the apeirogonal hosohedron, the [apeirogonal prism](wikipedia://en/18119040), and the [apeirogonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/18119626).
