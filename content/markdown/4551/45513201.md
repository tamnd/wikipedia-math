# Alexander's theorem

*Image: An Element of the Braid Group.svg.* This is a typical element of the braid group, which is used in the mathematical field of knot theory.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander's theorem"}

In mathematics **Alexander's theorem** states that every [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) or [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) can be represented as a [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_theory#Closed_braids) [braid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braid_theory); that is, a braid in which the corresponding ends of the strings are connected in pairs. The theorem is named after [James Waddell Alexander II](wikipedia://en/9848523), who published a proof in 1923.[^o2]

:::

Braids were first considered as a tool of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) by Alexander. His theorem gives a positive answer to the question *Is it always possible to transform a given knot into a closed braid?* A good construction example is found in [Colin Adams](wikipedia://en/3499637)'s book.[^o3]

However, the correspondence between knots and braids is clearly not [one-to-one](wikipedia://en/3942): a knot may have many braid representations. For example, conjugate braids yield equivalent knots. This leads to a second fundamental question: *Which closed braids represent the same knot type?* This question is addressed in [Markov's theorem](wikipedia://en/56349373), which gives ‘moves’ relating any two closed braids that represent the same knot.
