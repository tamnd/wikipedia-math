# Matching (statistics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matching"}

**Matching** is a statistical technique that evaluates the effect of a treatment by comparing the treated and the non-treated units in an [observational study](wikipedia://en/6326483) or [quasi-experiment](wikipedia://en/11864322) (i.e. when the treatment is not randomly assigned). The goal of matching is to reduce bias for the estimated treatment effect in an observational-data study, by finding, for every treated unit, one (or more) non-treated unit(s) with similar observable characteristics against which the covariates are balanced out (similar to the [K-nearest neighbors algorithm](wikipedia://en/1775388)). By matching treated units to similar non-treated units, matching enables a comparison of outcomes among treated and non-treated units to estimate the effect of the treatment reducing bias due to [confounding](wikipedia://en/3105999).[^o2][^o3][^o4] [Propensity score matching](wikipedia://en/16722927), an early matching technique, was developed as part of the [Rubin causal model](wikipedia://en/5700418),[^o5] but has been shown to increase model dependence, bias, inefficiency, and power and is no longer recommended compared to other matching methods.[^o6] A simple, easy-to-understand, and statistically powerful method of matching is known as Coarsened Exact Matching or CEM.[^o7]

:::

Matching has been promoted by [Donald Rubin](wikipedia://en/7790659).[^o5] It was prominently criticized in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) by [Robert LaLonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_LaLonde) (1986),[^o8] who compared estimates of treatment effects from an [experiment](wikipedia://en/59861) to comparable estimates produced with matching methods and showed that matching methods are [biased](wikipedia://en/184099). [Rajeev Dehejia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajeev_Dehejia) and [Sadek Wahba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadek_Wahba) (1999) reevaluated LaLonde's critique and showed that matching is a good solution.[^o9] Similar critiques have been raised in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science)[^o10] and [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology)[^o11] journals.

## Analysis {#Analysis}

When the outcome of interest is binary, the most general tool for the analysis of matched data is [conditional logistic regression](wikipedia://en/45447625) as it handles strata of arbitrary size and continuous or binary treatments (predictors) and can control for covariates. In particular cases, simpler tests like [paired difference test](wikipedia://en/23004551), [McNemar test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNemar_test) and [Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel_test) are available.

When the outcome of interest is continuous, estimation of the [average treatment effect](wikipedia://en/14527587) is performed.

Matching can also be used to "pre-process" a sample before analysis via another technique, such as [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997).[^o13]

::: {#o15 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Over-matching"}

## Overmatching {#Overmatching}

*Overmatching,* or *post-treatment bias,* is matching for an apparent mediator that actually is a result of the exposure.[^o16] If the mediator itself is stratified, an obscured relation of the exposure to the disease would highly be likely to be induced.[^o17] Overmatching thus causes [statistical bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_bias).[^o17]

For example, matching the control group by gestation length and/or the number of [multiple births](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_birth) when estimating [perinatal mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perinatal_mortality) and birthweight after [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization) (IVF) is overmatching, since IVF itself increases the risk of premature birth and multiple birth.[^o18]

It may be regarded as a [sampling bias](wikipedia://en/17692) in decreasing the [external validity](wikipedia://en/1900609) of a study, because the controls become more similar to the cases in regard to exposure than the general population.
