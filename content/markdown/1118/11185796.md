# Beverton–Holt model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beverton–Holt model"}

The **Beverton–Holt model** is a classic [discrete-time](wikipedia://en/40240263) [population model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_modeling) which gives the [expected](wikipedia://en/9653) number *n*<sub>*t*+1</sub> (or [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density)) of individuals in generation *t* + 1 as a function of the number of individuals in the previous generation,

:::

$$n_{t+1} = \frac{R_0 n_t}{1+ n_t/M}.$$

Here *R*<sub>0</sub> is interpreted as the proliferation rate per generation and *K* = (*R*<sub>0</sub> − 1) *M* is the [carrying capacity](wikipedia://en/47544) of the environment. The Beverton–Holt model was introduced in the context of [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheries_management) by [Beverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Beverton) & [Holt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Holt) (1957). Subsequent work has derived the model under other assumptions such as [contest competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_%28biology%29) (Brännström & Sumpter 2005), within-year resource limited competition (Geritz & Kisdi 2004) or even as the outcome of a source-sink Malthusian patches linked by density-dependent dispersal (Bravo de la Parra et al. 2013). The Beverton–Holt model can be generalized to include [scramble competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_competition) (see the [Ricker model](wikipedia://en/5813188), the [Hassell model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassell_model) and the [Maynard Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maynard_Smith)–Slatkin model). It is also possible to include a parameter reflecting the spatial clustering of individuals (see Brännström & Sumpter 2005).

Despite being [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear), the model can be solved explicitly, since it is in fact an inhomogeneous linear equation in 1/*n*. The solution is

$$n_t = \frac{K n_0}{n_0 + (K - n_0) R_0^{-t}}.$$

Because of this structure, the model can be considered as the discrete-time analogue of the continuous-time [logistic equation](wikipedia://en/84563#Logistic_differential_equation) for [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) introduced by [Verhulst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Verhulst); for comparison, the logistic equation is

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = rN \left( 1 - \frac{N}{K} \right),$$

and its solution is

$$N(t) = \frac{K N(0)}{N(0) + (K - N(0)) e^{-rt}}.$$
