# Hydrophobic-polar protein folding model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hydrophobic-polar protein folding model"}

The **hydrophobic-polar protein folding model** is a highly simplified model for examining [protein folds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding) in space. First proposed by [Ken Dill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_A._Dill) in 1985, it is the most known type of [lattice protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_protein): it stems from the observation that [hydrophobic interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic_interactions) between [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid) residues are the driving force for proteins folding into their [native state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_state).[^o2] All amino acid types are classified as either [hydrophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobic) (H) or [polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_polarity) (P), and the folding of a protein sequence is defined as a [self-avoiding walk](wikipedia://en/11186496) in a 2D or 3D [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246). The HP model imitates the hydrophobic effect by assigning a negative (favorable) weight to interactions between adjacent, non-covalently bound H residues. Proteins that have minimum energy are assumed to be in their native state.

:::

The HP model can be expressed in both two and three dimensions, generally with [square lattices](wikipedia://en/2874853), although triangular lattices have been used as well. It has also been studied on general regular lattices.[^o3]

Randomized search algorithms are often used to tackle the HP folding problem. This includes [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222), [evolutionary algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_algorithm) like the [Monte Carlo method](wikipedia://en/56098), [genetic algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_algorithms), and [ant colony optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant_colony_optimization). While no method has been able to calculate the experimentally determined minimum energetic state for long protein sequences, the most advanced methods today are able to come close.[^o4][^o5] For some model variants/lattices, it is possible to compute optimal structures (with maximal number of H-H contacts) using [constraint programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constraint_programming) techniques[^o6][^o7] as e.g. implemented within the [CPSP-tools webserver](http://cpsp.informatik.uni-freiburg.de).[^o8]

Even though the HP model abstracts away many of the details of protein folding, it is still an [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) problem on both 2D and 3D square lattices.[^o9]

A Monte Carlo method, named FRESS, was developed and appears to perform well on HP models.[^o10]
