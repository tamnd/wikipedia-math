# Correlation coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Correlation coefficient"}

A **correlation coefficient** is a [numerical measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_measure) of some type of linear [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057), meaning a [linear function](wikipedia://en/39712387) between two [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109). The variables may be two columns of a given [data set](wikipedia://en/8495) of observations, often called a [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29), or two components of a [multivariate random variable](wikipedia://en/49821) with a known [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28statistics%29).

:::

Several types of correlation coefficient exist, each with their own definition and range of usability and characteristics. They all assume values in the range from −1 to +1, where ±1 indicates the strongest possible correlation and 0 indicates no correlation.[^o2] As tools of analysis, correlation coefficients present certain problems, including the propensity of some types to be distorted by [outliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outliers) and the possibility of incorrectly being used to infer a [causal relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_relationship) between the variables (for more, see [Correlation does not imply causation](wikipedia://en/39834)).[^o3]

## Types {#Types}

There are several different measures for the degree of correlation in data, depending on the kind of data: principally, whether the data is a measurement, [ordinal](wikipedia://en/52904244), or [categorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_data).

### Pearson {#Pearson}

The [Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_product-moment_correlation_coefficient), also known as $r$, $R$, or *Pearson's* $r$, is a measure of the strength and direction of the *linear* relationship between two variables that is defined as the [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) of the variables divided by the product of their standard deviations.[^o6] This is the best-known and most commonly used type of correlation coefficient. When the term "correlation coefficient" is used without further qualification, it usually refers to the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient.

$r = \frac{\operatorname{cov}(X,Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y}$[^o2]

where **r** is the Pearson correlation coefficient; **cov(X,Y)** denotes the covariance between variables **X** and **Y**; and $\sigma_X$ and $\sigma_Y$ denote the standard deviations of **X** and **Y**, respectively.

$$r = \frac{n\sum xy - (\sum x)(\sum y)}{\sqrt{[n\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2][n\sum y^2 - (\sum y)^2]}}$$

where **n** is the number of data pairs; $\sum xy$ is the sum of the product of paired scores;$\sum x$ and $\sum y$ are the sums of x-scores and y-scores;$\sum x^2$and $\sum y^2$ are the sums of squared x-scores and squared y-scores.

### Intra-class {#Intra-class}

[Intraclass correlation](wikipedia://en/11391832) (ICC) is a descriptive statistic that can be used when quantitative measurements are made on units that are organized into groups; it describes how strongly units in the same group resemble each other.

### Rank {#Rank}

[Rank correlation](wikipedia://en/3316627) is a measure of the relationship between the rankings of two variables, or two rankings of the same variable:

- [Spearman's rank correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/235623) is a measure of how well the relationship between two variables can be described by a monotonic function.
- The [Kendall tau rank correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_tau_rank_correlation_coefficient) is a measure of the portion of ranks that match between two data sets.
- [Goodman and Kruskal's gamma](wikipedia://en/8925986) is a measure of the strength of association of the cross tabulated data when both variables are measured at the ordinal level.

### Tetrachoric and polychoric {#Tetrachoric_and_polychoric}

The [polychoric correlation](wikipedia://en/13792245) coefficient measures the association between two ordered-categorical variables. It's technically defined as the estimate of the Pearson correlation coefficient one would obtain if:

1. The two variables were measured on a continuous scale, instead of as ordered-category variables.
1. The two continuous variables followed a [bivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347).

When both variables are [dichotomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichotomous_variable) instead of ordered-categorical, the [polychoric correlation](wikipedia://en/13792245) coefficient is called the tetrachoric correlation coefficient.

### Interpreting correlation coefficient values {#Interpreting_correlation_coefficient_values}

The correlation between two variables have different associations that are measured in values such as $r$ or $R$. Correlation values range from −1 to +1, where ±1 indicates the strongest possible correlation and 0 indicates no correlation between variables.[^o11]

::: {#o12 .table cue=table}

| $r$ or $R$ | $r$ or $R$ | Strength or weakness of association between variables[^o13] |
|---|---|---|
| \+0.8 to +1.0 | \-1.0 to -0.8 | Perfect or very strong association |
| \+0.6 to +0.8 | \-0.8 to -0.6 | Strong association |
| \+0.4 to +0.6 | \-0.6 to -0.4 | Moderate association |
| \+0.2 to +0.4 | \-0.4 to -0.2 | Weak association |
| \+0.0 to 0.2 | \-0.2 to 0.0 | Very weak or no association |

:::
