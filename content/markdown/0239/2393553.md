# Daniel Goldston

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Goldston"}

**Daniel Alan Goldston** (born January 4, 1954, in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California)) is an American mathematician who specializes in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is currently a professor of mathematics at [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Daniel Alan Goldston was born on January 4, 1954, in Oakland, California. In 1972, he matriculated to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he earned his bachelor's degree and, in 1981, a Ph.D. in mathematics. His doctoral advisor at Berkeley was Russell Sherman Lehman; his dissertation was entitled "Large Differences between Consecutive Prime Numbers".[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

After earning his doctorate, Goldston worked at the [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth) and then spent the next academic year (1982–83) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in Princeton. He has worked at [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University) since 1983, save for stints at the IAS (1990), the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) (1994), and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in Berkeley (1999).

## Research {#Research}

In 2009, Goldston, [János Pintz](wikipedia://en/22191053), and [Cem Yıldırım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_Yıldırım) proved: $\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}-p_n}{\log p_n}=0$

where $p_n$ denotes the n<sup>th</sup> [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). In other words, for every $c>0\$, there exist infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes $p_n\$ and that $p_{n+1}\$ are closer to each other than the average distance between consecutive primes by a factor of $c\$, i.e., $p_{n+1}-p_n<c\log p_n\$.[^o7] This result was originally reported in 2003 by Goldston and Yıldırım but was later retracted.[^o8][^o9] Then Pintz joined the team and they completed the proof with the [GPY sieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPY_sieve).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2014, Goldston won the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177), shared with [Yitang Zhang](wikipedia://en/39441844) and colleagues [Cem Yıldırım](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_Yıldırım) and [János Pintz](wikipedia://en/22191053), for his contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o4] Also, Goldston was named to the 2021 class of fellows of the American Mathematical Society "for contributions to [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513)".[^o11]
