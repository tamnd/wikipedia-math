# Magnetic scalar potential

Magnetostatics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magnetic scalar potential"}

**Magnetic scalar potential**, *ψ*, is a [physical quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantity) in [classical electromagnetism](wikipedia://en/426219) analogous to [electric potential](wikipedia://en/59615). It is used to specify the [magnetic **H**-field](wikipedia://en/36563#The_H-field) in cases when there are no [free currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_current), in a manner analogous to using the electric potential to determine the electric field in [electrostatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatics). One important use of *ψ* is to determine the magnetic field due to [permanent magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet) when their [magnetization](wikipedia://en/2404348) is known. The potential is valid in any [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) region with zero [current density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_density), thus if currents are confined to wires or surfaces, piecemeal solutions can be stitched together to provide a description of the magnetic field at all points in space.

:::

## Magnetic scalar potential {#Magnetic_scalar_potential}

*Image: VFPt flat magnets gap potential+contour.svg.* Magnetic scalar potential of flat cylinder magnets encoded as color from positive (magenta) through zero (yellow) to negative (cyan).

The [scalar potential](wikipedia://en/461654) is a useful quantity in describing the magnetic field, especially for [permanent magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_magnet).

Where there is no free current and no [displacement current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_current),

$$\nabla\times\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0},$$

so if this holds in [simply connected domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected_domain) we can define a magnetic scalar potential, $\psi$, as[^o3]

$$\mathbf{H} = -\nabla\psi.$$

The dimension of $\psi$ in [SI base units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_base_units) is $\mathsf{A}$, which can be expressed in SI units as [amperes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere).

Using the definition of $\mathbf{H}$:

$$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = \mu_{0}\nabla\cdot\left(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}\right) = 0,$$

it follows that

$$\nabla^2 \psi = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{H} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf{M}.$$

Here, $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{M}$ acts as the source for magnetic field, much like $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{P}$ acts as the source for electric field. So analogously to [bound electric charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_charge), the quantity

$$\rho_m = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{M}$$

is called the *bound magnetic charge* density. Magnetic charges $q_m = \int \rho_m \, dV$ never occur isolated as [magnetic monopoles](wikipedia://en/175470), but only within dipoles and in magnets with a total magnetic charge sum of zero. The energy of a localized magnetic charge $q_{m}$ in a magnetic scalar potential is

$$Q = \mu_0\,q_m\psi,$$

and of a magnetic charge density distribution $\rho_{m}$ in space

$$Q = \mu_0 \int \rho_m \psi \, dV ,$$

where $\mu_{0}$ is the [vacuum permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_permeability). This is analog to the energy $Q = q V_E$ of an electric charge $q$ in an electric potential $V_E$.

If there is free current, one may subtract the contributions of free current per [Biot–Savart law](wikipedia://en/75110) from total magnetic field and solve the remainder with the scalar potential method.
