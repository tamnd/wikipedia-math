# Dilogarithm

*Image: Mplwp dilogarithm.svg.* The dilogarithm along the real axis

*Image: Complex polylog2.jpg.* The [principal value](wikipedia://en/408225) of the dilogarithm plotted in the complex plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dilogarithm"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **dilogarithm** (or **Spence's function**), denoted as $\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)$, is a particular case of the [polylogarithm](wikipedia://en/482471). Two related [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) are referred to as Spence's function, the dilogarithm itself:

:::

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(z) = -\int_0^z{\ln(1-u) \over u}\, du \text{, }z \in \mathbb{C}$$

and its reflection. For $\lvert z\rvert \le 1$, an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) also applies (the integral definition constitutes its analytical extension to the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628)):

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(z) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty {z^k \over k^2}.$$

Alternatively, the dilogarithm function is sometimes defined as

$$\int_{1}^{v} \frac{ \ln t }{ 1 -t } dt = \operatorname{Li}_2(1-v).$$

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) the dilogarithm can be used to compute the volume of an [ideal simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_simplex). Specifically, a simplex whose vertices have [cross ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_ratio) $z$ has [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628)

$$D(z) = \operatorname{Im} \operatorname{Li}_2(z) + \arg(1-z) \log|z|.$$

The function $D(z)$ is sometimes called the Bloch-Wigner function.[^o2] [Lobachevsky's function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky's_function) and [Clausen's function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausen's_function) are closely related functions.

[William Spence](wikipedia://en/71138035), after whom the function was named by early writers in the field, was a Scottish mathematician working in the early nineteenth century.[^o3] He was at school with [John Galt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Galt_%28novelist%29),[^o4] who later wrote a biographical essay on Spence.

## Analytic structure {#Analytic_structure}

Using the former definition above, the dilogarithm function is analytic everywhere on the complex plane except at $z = 1$, where it has a logarithmic branch point. The standard choice of branch cut is along the positive real axis $(1, \infty)$. However, the function is continuous at the branch point and takes on the value $\operatorname{Li}_2(1) = \pi^2/6$.

## Identities {#Identities}

$\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\operatorname{Li}_2(-z)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Li}_2(z^2).$[^o7]

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(1-z)+\operatorname{Li}_2\left(1-\frac{1}{z}\right)=-\frac{(\ln z)^2}{2}.$$

$\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\operatorname{Li}_2(1-z)=\frac{{\pi}^2}{6}-\ln z \cdot\ln(1-z).$[^o7] The [reflection formula](wikipedia://en/4025531).

$\operatorname{Li}_2(-z)-\operatorname{Li}_2(1-z)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Li}_2(1-z^2)=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{12}-\ln z \cdot \ln(z+1).$[^o8]

$\operatorname{Li}_2(z) +\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{z}\right) = - \frac{\pi^2}{6} - \frac{(\ln(-z))^2}{2}.$[^o7]

$\operatorname{L}(x)+\operatorname{L}(y)=\operatorname{L}(xy)+\operatorname{L}\left(\frac{x(1-y)}{1-xy}\right)+\operatorname{L}\left(\frac{y(1-x)}{1-xy}\right)$.[^o9][^o10] Abel's functional equation or five-term relation where $\operatorname{L}(z)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}[\operatorname{Li}_2(z)+\frac12\ln(z)\ln(1-z)]$ is the [Rogers](wikipedia://en/9024921) [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283) (an analogous relation is satisfied also by the [quantum dilogarithm](wikipedia://en/31601344))

## Particular value identities {#Particular_value_identities}

$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)-\frac{1}{6}\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)=\frac{{\pi}^2}{18}-\frac{(\ln 3)^2}{6}.$[^o8]

$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{18}+\frac{(\ln 3)^2}{6}.$[^o8]

$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{18}+\ln2\cdot \ln3-\frac{(\ln 2)^2}{2}-\frac{(\ln 3)^2}{3}.$[^o8]

$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)=\frac{{\pi}^2}{18}+2\ln2\cdot\ln3-2(\ln 2)^2-\frac{2}{3}(\ln 3)^2.$ [^o8]

$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(-\frac{1}{8}\right)+\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln{\frac{9}{8}}\right)^2.$[^o8]

$$36\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-36\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-12\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)+6\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{64}\right)={\pi}^2.$$

## Special values {#Special_values}

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(-1)=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{12}.$$

$\operatorname{Li}_2(0)=0.$ Its slope = 1.

$$\operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{{\pi}^2}{12}-\frac{(\ln 2)^2}{2}.$$

$\operatorname{Li}_2(1) = \zeta(2) = \frac{{\pi}^2}{6},$ where $\zeta(s)$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

$$\operatorname{Li}_2(2)=\frac{{\pi}^2}{4}-i\pi\ln2.$$

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Li}_2\left(-\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right) &=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{15}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}\right)^2 \\ &=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{15}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{arcsch}^2 2. \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Li}_2\left(-\frac{\sqrt5+1}{2}\right) &=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{10}-\ln^2 \frac{\sqrt5+1}{2} \\ &=-\frac{{\pi}^2}{10}-\operatorname{arcsch}^2 2. \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\right) &=\frac{{\pi}^2}{15}-\ln^2 \frac{\sqrt5+1}{2} \\ &=\frac{{\pi}^2}{15}-\operatorname{arcsch}^2 2. \end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Li}_2\left(\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\right) &=\frac{{\pi}^2}{10}-\ln^2 \frac{\sqrt5+1}{2} \\ &=\frac{{\pi}^2}{10}-\operatorname{arcsch}^2 2. \end{aligned}$$

## In particle physics {#In_particle_physics}

Spence's Function is commonly encountered in [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics) while calculating [radiative corrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiative_correction). In this context, the function is often defined with an [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) inside the logarithm:

$$\operatorname{\Phi}(x) = -\int_0^x \frac{\ln|1-u|}{u} \, du = \begin{cases} \operatorname{Li}_2(x), & x \leq 1; \\ \frac{\pi^2}{3} - \frac{1}{2}(\ln x)^2 - \operatorname{Li}_2(\frac{1}{x}), & x > 1. \end{cases}$$
