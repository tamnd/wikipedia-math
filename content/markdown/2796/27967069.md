# Uniform matroid

*Image: Graphic matroid of C4.svg.* The [graphic matroid](wikipedia://en/745714) of the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) $C_{4}$, which is the **uniform matroid** $U{}^3_4$. More generally, the graphic matroid of $C_{n}$ is $U{}^{n-1}_{n}$.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform matroid"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **uniform matroid** is a [matroid](wikipedia://en/244321) in which the *independent sets* are exactly the sets containing at most *r* elements, for some fixed integer *r*. An alternative definition is that every [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of the elements is a [symmetry](wikipedia://en/2714149).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ is defined over a set of $n$ elements. A subset of the elements is independent if and only if it contains at most $r$ elements. A subset is a basis if it has exactly $r$ elements, and it is a circuit if it has exactly $r+1$ elements. The [rank](wikipedia://en/37123010) of a subset $S$ is $\min(|S|,r)$ and the rank of the matroid is $r$.[^o5][^o6]

A matroid of rank $r$ is uniform if and only if all of its circuits have exactly $r+1$ elements.[^o7]

The matroid $U{}^2_n$ is called the **$n$-point line**.

:::

## Duality and minors {#Duality_and_minors}

The [dual matroid](wikipedia://en/23515837) of the uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ is another uniform matroid $U{}^{n-r}_n$. A uniform matroid is self-dual if and only if $r=n/2$.[^o9]

Every [minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) of a uniform matroid is uniform. Restricting a uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ by one element (as long as $r < n$) produces the matroid $U{}^r_{n-1}$ and contracting it by one element (as long as $r > 0$) produces the matroid $U{}^{r-1}_{n-1}$.[^o10]

## Realization {#Realization}

The uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ may be [represented](wikipedia://en/37165960) as the matroid of affinely independent subsets of $n$ points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) in $r$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), or as the matroid of linearly independent subsets of $n$ vectors in general position in an $(r+1)$-dimensional real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370).

Every uniform matroid may also be realized in [projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135) and vector spaces over all sufficiently large [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615).[^o22] However, the field must be large enough to include enough independent vectors. For instance, the $n$-point line $U{}^2_n$ can be realized only over finite fields of $n-1$ or more elements (because otherwise the projective line over that field would have fewer than $n$ points): $U{}^2_4$ is not a [binary matroid](wikipedia://en/36848165), $U{}^2_5$ is not a ternary matroid, etc. For this reason, uniform matroids play an important role in [Rota's conjecture](wikipedia://en/36760081) concerning the [forbidden minor](wikipedia://en/36749660) characterization of the matroids that can be realized over finite fields.[^o12]

::: {#o14 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

The problem of finding the minimum-weight basis of a [weighted](wikipedia://en/9401560) uniform matroid is well-studied in computer science as the [selection problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selection_algorithm). It may be solved in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time).[^o15]

Any algorithm that tests whether a given matroid is uniform, given access to the matroid via an [independence oracle](wikipedia://en/36878315), must perform an exponential number of oracle queries, and therefore cannot take polynomial time.[^o16]

:::

## Related matroids {#Related_matroids}

The [free matroid](wikipedia://en/65782984) over a given ground-set $E$ is the matroid in which the independent sets are all subsets of $E$. It is a special case of a uniform matroid; specifically, when $E$ has cardinality $n$, it is the uniform matroid $U{}^{n}_{n}$.[^o18]

Unless $r\in\{0,n\}$, a uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ is connected: it is not the direct sum of two smaller matroids.[^o19] The direct sum of a family of uniform matroids (not necessarily all with the same parameters) is called a [partition matroid](wikipedia://en/36780298).

Every uniform matroid is a [paving matroid](wikipedia://en/37729421),[^o20] a [transversal matroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversal_matroid)[^o21] and a [strict gammoid](wikipedia://en/26952253).[^o22]

Not every uniform matroid is [graphic](wikipedia://en/745714), and the uniform matroids provide the smallest example of a non-graphic matroid, $U{}^2_4$. The uniform matroid $U{}^1_n$ is the graphic matroid of an $n$-edge [dipole graph](wikipedia://en/6412575), and the dual uniform matroid $U{}^{n-1}_n$ is the graphic matroid of its [dual graph](wikipedia://en/2536864), the $n$-edge [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962). $U{}^0_n$ is the graphic matroid of a graph with $n$ self-loops, and $U{}^n_n$ is the graphic matroid of an $n$-edge [forest](wikipedia://en/48560). Other than these examples, every uniform matroid $U{}^r_n$ with $1 < r < n-1$ contains $U{}^2_4$ as a minor and therefore is not graphic.[^o1]

The $n$-point line provides an example of a [Sylvester matroid](wikipedia://en/36827387), a matroid in which every line contains three or more points.[^o23]
