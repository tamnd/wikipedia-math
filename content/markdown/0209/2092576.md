# Dym equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dym equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and in particular in the theory of [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654), the **Dym equation** (**HD**) is the third-order [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564)

:::

$$u_t = u^3u_{xxx}.\,$$

It is often written in the equivalent form for some function v of one space variable and time

$$v_t=(v^{-1/2})_{xxx}.\,$$

The Dym equation first appeared in Kruskal [^o2] and is attributed to an unpublished paper by [Harry Dym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dym).

The Dym equation represents a system in which [dispersion](wikipedia://en/853778) and [nonlinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity) are coupled together. HD is a [completely integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_integrable) [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear) [evolution equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_equation) that may be solved by means of the [inverse scattering transform](wikipedia://en/4916481). It obeys an [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) number of [conservation laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29); it does not possess the [Painlevé property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painlevé_property).

The Dym equation has strong links to the [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation). C.S. Gardner, J.M. Greene, Kruskal and R.M. Miura applied \[Dym equation\] to the solution of corresponding problem in [Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg–de_Vries_equation). The [Lax pair](wikipedia://en/5527029) of the Harry Dym equation is associated with the [Sturm–Liouville operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm–Liouville_operator). The Liouville transformation transforms this operator [isospectrally](wikipedia://en/386031) into the [Schrödinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger) operator.[^o3] Thus by the inverse Liouville transformation solutions of the Korteweg–de Vries equation are transformed into solutions of the Dym equation. An explicit solution of the Dym equation, valid in a finite interval, is found by an auto-[Bäcklund transform](wikipedia://en/701077)[^o3]

$$u(t,x) = \left[ - 3 \alpha \left( x + 4 \alpha^2 t \right) \right]^{2/3} .$$
