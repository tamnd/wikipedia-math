# Yves Meyer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yves Meyer"}

**Yves F. Meyer** (; born 19 July 1939) is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is among the progenitors of [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903) theory, having proposed the [Meyer wavelet](wikipedia://en/38833005). Meyer was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2017.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in Paris into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew)[^o4] family, Yves Meyer studied at the Lycée Carnot in [Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunis);[^o5] he won the French General Student Competition ([Concours Général](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concours_général)) in Mathematics, and was placed first in the entrance examination for the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in 1957.[^o6] He obtained his Ph.D. in 1966, under the supervision of [Jean-Pierre Kahane](wikipedia://en/6949961).[^o8] The Mexican historian [Jean Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Meyer) is his cousin.

Yves Meyer taught at the [Prytanée national militaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prytanée_national_militaire) during his military service (1960–1963), then was a teaching assistant at the [Université de Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Strasbourg) (1963–1966), a professor at [Université Paris-Sud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris-Sud) (1966–1980), a professor at [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) (1980–1986), a professor at [Université Paris-Dauphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Paris-Dauphine) (1985–1995), a senior researcher at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) (CNRS) (1995–1999), an invited professor at the [Conservatoire National des Arts et Métiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatoire_National_des_Arts_et_Métiers) (2000), a professor at [École Normale Supérieure de Cachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_Paris-Saclay) (1999–2003), and has been a professor emeritus at Ecole Normale Supérieure de Cachan since 2004.

He was awarded the 2010 Gauss Prize for fundamental contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and his pivotal role in the development of wavelets and [multiresolution analysis](wikipedia://en/1963076).[^o8] He also received the 2017 [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) "for his pivotal role in the development of the mathematical theory of wavelets."[^o9][^o10]

## Awards and recognitions {#Awards_and_recognitions}

- He is a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) since 1993.[^o18]
- Meyer was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1970 in Nice, in 1983 in Warsaw,[^o19] and in 1990 in Kyoto.[^o20]
- In 2010, Yves Meyer was awarded the [Carl Friedrich Gauss Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss_Prize).[^o8]
- In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o21]
- In 2017 he was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) for his pivotal role in developing the mathematical theory of wavelets.[^o22]
- In 2020 he received the [Princess of Asturias Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_of_Asturias_Awards) for Technical and Scientific Research.[^o23]
