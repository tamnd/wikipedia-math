# Faceting

Facet (disambiguation)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Faceting"}

*Image: CubeAndStel.svg.*

Stella octangula as a faceting of the cube

:::

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **faceting** (also spelled **facetting**) is the process of removing parts of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), without creating any new [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).

New edges of a faceted polyhedron may be created along [face diagonals](wikipedia://en/10830506) or internal [space diagonals](wikipedia://en/1337505). A *faceted polyhedron* will have two faces on each edge and creates new polyhedra or compounds of polyhedra.

Faceting is the reciprocal or [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) process to *[stellation](wikipedia://en/180253)*.[^n1] For every stellation of some [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885), there exists a dual faceting of the [dual polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope).

## Faceted polygons {#Faceted_polygons}

For example, a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014) has one symmetry faceting, the [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544), and the regular [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) has two symmetric facetings, one as a polygon, and one as a compound of two triangles.

## Faceted polyhedra {#Faceted_polyhedra}

The [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) can be faceted into three regular [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](wikipedia://en/16814): small stellated dodecahedron, great dodecahedron, and great icosahedron. They all have 30 edges.

The regular [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) can be faceted into one regular [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron](wikipedia://en/16814), three [uniform star polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_star_polyhedra), and three [regular polyhedral compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polyhedral_compound). The uniform stars and [compound of five cubes](wikipedia://en/8010502) are constructed by [face diagonals](wikipedia://en/10830506). The [excavated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/32917096) is a facetting with star hexagon faces.

The [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula) is the sole faceting of the cube, and the tetrahedron has no facetings.

## History {#History}

Faceting has not been studied as extensively as [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253).

- In 1568 [Wenzel Jamnitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzel_Jamnitzer) published his book *[Perspectiva Corporum Regularium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspectiva_Corporum_Regularium)*, showing many stellations and facetings of polyhedra.[^o10]
- In 1619, [Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) described a [regular compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound#Regular_compounds) of two [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) which fits inside a cube, and which he called the **[Stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula)**.
- In 1858, [Bertrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_François_Bertrand) derived the regular [star polyhedra](wikipedia://en/7087334) ([Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](wikipedia://en/16814)) by faceting the [regular](wikipedia://en/333299) [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) and [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407).
- In 1974, Bridge enumerated the more straightforward facetings of the [regular](wikipedia://en/333299) polyhedra, including those of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407).
- In 2006, Inchbald described the basic theory of faceting diagrams for polyhedra. For a given vertex, the diagram shows all the possible edges and facets (new faces) which may be used to form facetings of the original hull. It is [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28mathematics%29) to the [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815)'s stellation diagram, which shows all the possible edges and vertices for some face plane of the original core.
