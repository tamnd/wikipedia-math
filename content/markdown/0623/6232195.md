# Theoretical philosophy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theoretical philosophy"}

The [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_philosophy) division of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) into **theoretical philosophy** and [practical philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_philosophy)[^o2][^o3] has its origin in [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s categories of [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy) and [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_philosophy).[^o4] The one has [theory](wikipedia://en/17645570) for its object, and the other [practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_reason).[^o2] \_\_forcetoc\_\_

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

In [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark),[^o6] [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland),[^o7] [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany),[^o8] the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands),[^o9] [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden),[^o10] and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States),[^o11] courses in theoretical and practical philosophy are taught separately, and are separate degrees. Other countries may use a similar scheme—some [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) universities, for example, divide philosophy into [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), and [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics)—but in most universities around the world philosophy is taught as a single subject. There is also a unified philosophy subject in some Swedish universities, such as [Södertörns Högskola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Södertörn_University_College).

Theoretical philosophy is sometimes confused with [analytic philosophy](wikipedia://en/159211), but the latter is a philosophical movement, embracing certain ideas and methods but dealing with all philosophical subject matters, while the former is a way of sorting philosophical questions into two different categories in the context of a [curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum).
