# Kurt Otto Friedrichs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Otto Friedrichs"}

**Kurt Otto Friedrichs** (September 28, 1901 – December 31, 1982) was a German-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was the co-founder of the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), and a recipient of the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Friedrichs was born in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel), [Schleswig-Holstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Schleswig-Holstein) on September 28, 1901. His family soon moved to [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Düsseldorf), where he grew up. He attended several different universities in Germany studying the philosophical works of [Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heidegger) and [Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl), but finally decided that mathematics was his real calling. During the 1920s, Friedrichs pursued this field in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), which had a renowned Mathematical Institute under the direction of [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238). Courant became a close colleague and lifelong friend of Friedrichs.

In 1931, Friedrichs became a full professor of mathematics at the [Technische Hochschule in Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Braunschweig). In early February 1933, a few days after [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) [became](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) the [Chancellor of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany), Friedrichs met and immediately fell in love with a young Jewish student, Nellie Bruell. Their relationship became increasingly challenging and difficult because of the antisemitic [Nuremberg Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws) of Hitler's government. In 1937, both Friedrichs and Nellie Bruell managed to emigrate separately to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) where they finally married. Their long and very happy marriage produced five children.[^o5]

Courant had left Germany in 1933 and had founded an institute for graduate studies in mathematics at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). Friedrichs joined him when he arrived in 1937 and remained there for forty years. He was instrumental in the development of the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences), which eventually became one of the most distinguished research institutes for applied mathematics in the world. Friedrichs died in [New Rochelle, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York) on December 31, 1982.

Friedrichs' greatest contribution to applied mathematics was his work on [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He also did major research and wrote many books and papers on existence theory, [numerical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_methods), [differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_operators) in [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), non-linear buckling of plates, flows past wings, solitary waves, shock waves, combustion, magneto-fluid dynamical shock waves, relativistic flows, [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), perturbation of the continuous spectrum, scattering theory, and symmetric hyperbolic equations.[^o5] With [Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274),[^o6][^o7] Friedrichs[^o8] gave a "geometrized" formulation of Newtonian gravitation theory—also known as "[Newton–Cartan theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton–Cartan_theory)"—and later developed by Dautcourt, Dixon, Dombrowski and Horneffer, Ehlers, Havas, Künzle, Lottermoser, [Trautman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Trautman), and others.

A member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) since 1959, Friedrichs received many honorary degrees and awards for his work. There is a student prize named after Friedrichs at [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU). The [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) selected him as the Josiah Willards Gibbs lecturer for 1954.[^o9][^o10] In November 1977, Friedrichs received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) from President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) "for bringing the powers of modern mathematics to bear on problems in physics, fluid dynamics, and elasticity."
