# Fundamental lemma (Langlands program)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental lemma"}

In the mathematical theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), the **fundamental lemma** relates orbital integrals on a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) to stable orbital integrals on its [endoscopic groups](wikipedia://en/20546343). It was conjectured by (txt) in the course of developing the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905). The fundamental lemma was proved by [Gérard Laumon](wikipedia://en/22565856) and [Ngô Bảo Châu](wikipedia://en/11133659) in the case of [unitary groups](wikipedia://en/173993) and then by (Ngô 2010) for general reductive groups, building on a series of important reductions made by [Jean-Loup Waldspurger](wikipedia://en/31862139) to the case of [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebras). *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_%28magazine%29)* magazine placed Ngô's proof on the list of the "Top 10 scientific discoveries of 2009".[^o4] In 2010, Ngô was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) for this proof.

:::

## Motivation and history {#Motivation_and_history}

Langlands outlined a strategy for proving local and global [Langlands conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_conjectures) using the [Arthur–Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621), but in order for this approach to work, the geometric sides of the trace formula for different groups must be related in a particular way. This relationship takes the form of identities between [orbital integrals](wikipedia://en/23462941) on [reductive groups](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* and *H* over a nonarchimedean [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *F*, where the group *H*, called an [endoscopic group](wikipedia://en/20546343) of *G*, is constructed from *G* and some additional data.

The first case considered was $G = {\rm SL}_2$ (Labesse Langlands 1979). (txt) then developed the general framework for the theory of endoscopic transfer and formulated specific conjectures. However, during the next two decades only partial progress was made towards proving the fundamental lemma.[^o8][^o9] Harris called it a "bottleneck limiting progress on a host of arithmetic questions".[^o10] Langlands himself, writing on the origins of endoscopy, commented:

... it is not the fundamental lemma as such that is critical for the analytic theory of automorphic forms and for the arithmetic of [Shimura varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimura_varieties); it is the stabilized (or stable) trace formula, the reduction of the trace formula itself to the stable trace formula for a group and its endoscopic groups, and the stabilization of the [Grothendieck–Lefschetz formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck–Lefschetz_formula). None of these are possible without the fundamental lemma and its absence rendered progress almost impossible for more than twenty years.[^o11]

::: {#o13 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The fundamental lemma states that an orbital integral *O* for a group *G* is equal to a stable orbital integral *SO* for an endoscopic group *H*, up to a transfer factor Δ (Nadler 2012):

$$SO_{\gamma_H}(1_{K_H}) = \Delta(\gamma_H,\gamma_G)O^\kappa_{\gamma_G}(1_{K_G})$$

where

- *F* is a local field,
- *G* is an unramified group defined over *F*, in other words a quasi-split reductive group defined over *F* that splits over an unramified extension of *F*,
- *H* is an unramified endoscopic group of *G* associated to κ,
- *K*<sub>*G*</sub> and *K*<sub>*H*</sub> are hyperspecial maximal compact subgroups of *G* and *H*, which means roughly that they are the subgroups of points with coefficients in the ring of integers of *F*,
- 1<sub>*K*<sub>*G*</sub></sub> and 1<sub>*K*<sub>*H*</sub></sub> are the characteristic functions of *K*<sub>*G*</sub> and *K*<sub>*H*</sub>,
- Δ(γ<sub>*H*</sub>,γ<sub>*G*</sub>) is a transfer factor, a certain elementary expression depending on γ<sub>*H*</sub> and γ<sub>*G*</sub>,
- γ<sub>*H*</sub> and γ<sub>*G*</sub> are elements of *G* and *H* representing stable conjugacy classes, such that the stable [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) of *G* is the transfer of the stable conjugacy class of *H*,
- κ is a character of the group of conjugacy classes in the stable conjugacy class of γ<sub>*G*</sub>,
- *SO* and *O* are stable orbital integrals and orbital integrals depending on their parameters.

:::

## Approaches {#Approaches}

(Shelstad 1982) proved the fundamental lemma for Archimedean fields.

(Waldspurger 1991) verified the fundamental lemma for general linear groups.

(Kottwitz 1992) and (Blasius Rogawski 1992) verified some cases of the fundamental lemma for 3-dimensional unitary groups.

(Hales 1997) and (Weissauer 2009) verified the fundamental lemma for the symplectic and general symplectic groups Sp<sub>4</sub>, GSp<sub>4</sub>.

A paper of [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig) and [David Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833) pointed out that orbital integrals could be interpreted as counting points on certain algebraic varieties over finite fields. Further, the integrals in question can be computed in a way that depends only on the residue field of *F*; and the issue can be reduced to the Lie algebra version of the orbital integrals. Then the problem was restated in terms of the [Springer fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_fiber) of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924).[^o22] The circle of ideas was connected to a [purity conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purity_conjecture); Laumon gave a [conditional proof](wikipedia://en/6562) based on such a conjecture, for unitary groups. (txt) then proved the fundamental lemma for unitary groups, using [Hitchin fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchin_fibration) introduced by (txt), which is an abstract geometric analogue of the [Hitchin system](wikipedia://en/23652892) of complex algebraic geometry. (Waldspurger 2006) showed for Lie algebras that the function field case implies the fundamental lemma over all local fields, and (Waldspurger 2008) showed that the fundamental lemma for Lie algebras implies the fundamental lemma for groups.
