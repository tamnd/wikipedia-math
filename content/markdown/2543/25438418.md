# Dushnik–Miller theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dushnik–Miller theorem"}

In mathematics, the **Dushnik–Miller theorem** is a result in [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924) stating that every [countably infinite](wikipedia://en/6026) [linear order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_order) has a non-identity [order embedding](wikipedia://en/762203) into itself. It is named for Ben Dushnik and E. W. Miller, who proved this result in a paper of 1940; in the same paper, they showed that the statement does not always hold for [uncountable](wikipedia://en/32248) linear orders, using the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) to build a suborder of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) of [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6173) [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758677) with no non-identity order embeddings into itself.

:::

In [reverse mathematics](wikipedia://en/326365), the Dushnik–Miller theorem for countable linear orders has the same strength as the [arithmetical comprehension axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetical_comprehension_axiom) (ACA<sub>0</sub>), one of the "big five" subsystems of [second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454). This result is closely related to the fact that (as [Louise Hay](wikipedia://en/31132826) and Joseph Rosenstein proved) there exist [computable](wikipedia://en/332264) linear orders with no computable non-identity self-embedding.
