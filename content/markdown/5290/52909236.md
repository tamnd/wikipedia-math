# Biryukov equation

*Image: Sine oscillations.jpg.* [Sine](wikipedia://en/5010838) oscillations $F = 0.01$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biryukov equation"}

In the study of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), the **Biryukov equation** (or **Biryukov oscillator**), named after Vadim Biryukov (1946), is a [non-linear](wikipedia://en/146103) second-order [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) used to model damped [oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation).[^o2]

:::

The equation is given by

$$\frac{d^2 y}{dt^2}+f(y)\frac{dy}{dt}+y=0, \qquad\qquad (1)$$

where $\text{ƒ}(y)$ is a piecewise [constant function](wikipedia://en/320861) which is positive, except for small $y$ as

$$\begin{aligned} & f(y) = \begin{cases} -F, & |y|\le Y_0; \\[4pt] F, & |y|>Y_0. \end{cases} \\[6pt] & F = \text{const.} > 0, \quad Y_0 = \text{const.} > 0. \end{aligned}$$

Eq. (1) is a special case of the [Lienard equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lienard_equation); it describes the auto-oscillations.

Solution (1) at separate time intervals when f(y) is constant is given by[^o3]

$$y_k(t) = A_{1,k}\exp(s_{1,k}t) + A_{2,k}\exp(s_{2,k}t) \qquad\qquad (2)$$

where $\exp$ denotes the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678). Here

$$s_k = \begin{cases} \displaystyle \frac{F}{2}\mp\sqrt{ \left(\frac{F}{2}\right)^2-1}, & |y|<Y_0; \\[2pt] \displaystyle -\frac{F}{2}\mp\sqrt{ \left(\frac{F}{2}\right)^2-1} & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$

Expression (2) can be used for real and complex values of $s_{k}$.

The first half-period’s solution at $y(0)=\pm Y_0$ is

*Image: Fig 2 Relaxation oscillations F = 4.png.* Relaxation oscillations $F = 4$

$$\begin{aligned} y(t) &= \begin{cases} y_1(t), & 0\le t<T_0; \\[4pt] y_2(t), & \displaystyle T_0\le t< \frac{T}{2}. \end{cases} \\[4pt] y_1(t) &= A_{1,k}\cdot \exp (s_{1,k}t)+A_{2,k}\cdot \exp (s_{2,k}t), \\[2pt] y_2(t) &= A_{3,k}\cdot \exp(s_{3,k}t)+A_{4,k}\cdot \exp (s_{4,k}t). \end{aligned}$$

The second half-period’s solution is

$$y(t)= \begin{cases} \displaystyle -y_1\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right), & \displaystyle \frac{T}{2} \le t < \frac{T}{2} + T_0; \\[4pt] \displaystyle -y_2\left(t-\frac{T}{2}\right), & \displaystyle \frac{T}{2} + T_0 \le t < T. \end{cases}$$

The solution contains four [constants of integration](wikipedia://en/147939) $A_{1}, A_{2}, A_{3}, A_{4}$, the period $T$ and the boundary $T_{0}$ between $y_{1}(t)$ and $y_{2}(t)$ needs to be found. A boundary condition is derived from the continuity of $y(t)$ and $dy/dt$.[^o4]

Solution of (1) in the stationary mode thus is obtained by solving a system of algebraic equations as

$$\begin{array}{ll} & y_1(0) = -Y_0 & y_1(T_0) = Y_0 \\[6pt] & y_2(T_0) = Y_0 & y_2 \! \left(\tfrac{T}{2}\right) = Y_0 \\[6pt] & \displaystyle \left.\frac{dy_1}{dt}\right|_{T_0} = \left.\frac{dy_2}{dt}\right|_{T_0} \qquad & \displaystyle \left.\frac{dy_1}{dt}\right|_{0} = -\left.\frac{dy_2}{dt}\right|_\frac{T}{2} \end{array}$$

The integration constants are obtained by the [Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm](wikipedia://en/892446). With $f(y)=\mu(-1+y^2)$, $\mu = \text{const.} > 0,$ Eq. (1) named [Van der Pol oscillator](wikipedia://en/1961786). Its solution cannot be expressed by [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412) in closed form.
