# Sobolev orthogonal polynomials

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sobolev orthogonal polynomials"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Sobolev orthogonal polynomials** are [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) with respect to a [Sobolev](wikipedia://en/611964) [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product), i.e. an inner product with [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921).

:::

By having conditions on the derivatives, the Sobolev orthogonal polynomials in general no longer share some of the nice features that classical orthogonal polynomials have.

Sobolev orthogonal polynomials are named after [Sergei Lvovich Sobolev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lvovich_Sobolev).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mu_0,\mu_1,\dots,\mu_n$ be positive [Borel measures](wikipedia://en/4322) on $\mathbb{R}$ with finite moments. Consider the inner product

$$\langle p_r,p_s \rangle_{W^{n,2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_r(x) p_s(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu_0+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_r^{(k)}(x) p_s^{(k)}(x)\;\mathrm{d}\mu_k$$

and let $W^{n,2}$ be the corresponding [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964). The **Sobolev orthogonal polynomials** $\{p_n\}_{n\geq 0}$ are defined as

$$\langle p_n,p_s \rangle_{W^{n,2}}=c_n\delta_{n,s}$$

where $\delta_{n,s}$ denotes the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). One says that these polynomials are *sobolev orthogonal*.[^o4]

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

- Classical orthogonal polynomials are Sobolev orthogonal polynomials, since their derivatives are also orthogonal polynomials.
- Sobolev orthogonal polynomials in general are no longer commutative in the multiplication operator with respect to the inner product, i.e.

$$\langle xp_n,p_s\rangle_{W^{n,2}}\neq\langle p_n,xp_s\rangle_{W^{n,2}}$$

Consequently neither [Favard's theorem](wikipedia://en/32659088), the three term recurrence or the [Christoffel-Darboux formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffel-Darboux_formula) hold. There exist however other recursion formulas for certain types of measures.

- There exist a lot of literature for the case $n=1$.
