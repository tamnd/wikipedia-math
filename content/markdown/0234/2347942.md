# Paolo Frisi

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Paolo Frisi"}

**Paolo Frisi** (13 April 1728 – 22 November 1784) was an Italian [priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priest), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer). Frisi's work laid the foundation for understanding angular velocity as a vector, which is crucial in analyzing [rotational dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_dynamics).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: 8781 Milano - Via Brera - Casa natale Cesare Beccaria - Paolo Frisi - Foto G. Dall'Orto - 14-Apr-2007.jpg.* A 19th century medallion of Paolo Frisi on the facade of Palazzo Beccaria in via Brera street, [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) (birthplace of [Cesare Beccaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Beccaria))

Frisi was born in [Melegnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melegnano) in 1728; his sibling [Antonio Francesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Francesco_Frisi), born in 1735, went on to be a historian.[^o6] Frisi was educated at the local [Barnabite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnabite) monastery and afterwards in that of [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua). When twenty-one years of age he composed a treatise on the figure of the earth, and the reputation which he soon acquired led to his appointment by the [King of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Emmanuel_III_of_Sardinia) to the professorship of philosophy in the College of [Casale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casale_Monferrato). He succeeded [Hyacinthe Sigismond Gerdil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyacinthe_Sigismond_Gerdil), who had been appointed professor of philosophy at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin). His friendship with [Radicati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Radicati), a man of liberal opinions, occasioned Frisi's removal by his clerical superiors to Novara, where he was compelled to do duty as a preacher.[^o4]

In 1753 he was elected a corresponding member of the [Paris Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), and shortly afterwards he became professor of philosophy at the Barnabite College of St Alexander at Milan. An acrimonious attack by a young Jesuit, about this time, upon his dissertation on the figure of the earth laid the foundation of his animosity against the Jesuits, with whose enemies, including [Jean d'Alembert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_d'Alembert), [J. A. N. Condorcet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._A._N._Condorcet) and other Encyclopedists, he later closely associated himself. As an astronomer Frisi gained international recognition for his studies on the daily movement of the earth. His *De motu diurno terrae*, published in 1756, was awarded a prize by the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1756 he was appointed by [Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_I_of_Tuscany), [Grand Duke of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany), to the professorship of mathematics in the [university of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa), a post which he held for eight years. In 1756[^o7] he became an associate of the [St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Academy_of_Sciences), and a foreign member of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London), and in 1758 a member of the Academy of Berlin, in 1766 of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences), and in 1770 of the Academies of Copenhagen and of Bern. From several European crowned heads he received, at various times, marks of special distinction, and the empress [Maria Theresa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Theresa) granted him a yearly pension of 100 [sequins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequin_%28coin%29).[^o4]

In 1764 he was created professor of mathematics in the palatine schools at Milan, and obtained from Pope [Pius VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_VI) the release from ecclesiastical jurisdiction, and authority to become a [secular priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secular_priest). In the same year he began contributing the [literary magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_magazine) *[Il Caffè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Caffè)*, founded by his friend Pietro Verri. In 1766 he visited France and England, and in 1768 Vienna. His knowledge of [hydraulics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics) caused him to be frequently consulted with respect to the management of canals and other watercourses in various parts of Europe. It was through his means that [lightning conductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_rod) were first introduced into Italy for the protection of buildings.[^o4]

He died in Milan on 22 November 1784 and was buried in the Church of [Sant'Alessandro in Zebedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Alessandro_in_Zebedia).[^n1] Several colleges in Italy are named after him.[^n2]
