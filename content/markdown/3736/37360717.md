# Tord Ganelius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tord Ganelius"}

*Image: Tord ganelius.png.* Tord Ganelius

**Tord Hjalmar Ganelius** (born 23 May 1925 in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), dead 14 March 2016 in Stockholm) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus). He served as Permanent Secretary of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) and was a board member of the [Nobel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Foundation) from 1981 to 1989.[^o2][^o3] His primary research interests were [holomorphic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_functions) and [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713).[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Ganelius completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1953 at Stockholms Högskola (known as [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) since 1960) by presenting his dissertation *Sequences of Analytic Functions and Their Zeros*.[^o6]

He was an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) in 1953–56 and in 1957 he was appointed Professor of Mathematics at [University of Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gothenburg), where he served until 1981. During this time he was Dean of the Faculty of Science twice, in 1963–65 and 1977–80.[^o2] In 1972, he was elected a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o7] and he was appointed the permanent secretary in 1981, a position he held until 1989.[^o3] He was a board member of the [Nobel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Foundation) from 1981 to 1989, and every October he announced the [Nobel Laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureates) in Physics, Chemistry and Economic Sciences.[^o2]

Tord Ganelius has also been a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1962, [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1967–68 and the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) in 1972–73.[^o2]

In 1966, Ganelius published the Swedish-language mathematics textbook ”Introduktion till matematiken”, which since 2006 has been available on-line.[^o8]

## Family {#Family}

Tord Ganelius is the son of Hjalmar and Ebba G. In 1951 he married Aggie Hemberg (born 1928).[^o2] They have four children: Per (1952), Truls (1955), Svante (1957) and Aggie Öhman (1963).[^o10]
