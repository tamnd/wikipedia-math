# Ostrowski's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ostrowski's theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Ostrowski's theorem**, due to [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091) (1916), states that every non-trivial [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667) on the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb{Q}$ is equivalent to either the usual real absolute value or a [$p$-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) absolute value.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorem statement"}

## Theorem statement {#Theorem_statement}

An **absolute value** on the rational numbers is a function $|\cdot|_*: \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfying for all $x, y\in\mathbb{Q}$

1. $|x|_* \ge 0$, with equality [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $x = 0$
1. $$|xy|_* = |x|_* |y|_*$$
1. $$|x + y|_* \le |x|_* + |y|_*$$

Two absolute values $|\cdot|$ and $|\cdot|_*$ on the rationals are defined to be **equivalent** if they induce the same [topology](wikipedia://en/30450); this can be shown to be equivalent to the existence of a positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\lambda \in (0, \infty)$ such that

$$|x|_* = |x|^\lambda$$

for all rational $x$. (Note: In general, if $|x|$ is an absolute value, $|x|^\lambda$ is not necessarily an absolute value anymore; however *if* two absolute values are equivalent, then each is a positive power of the other.[^o5]) The **trivial absolute value** on any field *K* is defined to be

$$|x|_0 := \begin{cases} 0, & x = 0, \\ 1, & x \ne 0. \end{cases}$$

The **real absolute value** on the [rationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers) $\mathbb{Q}$ is the standard [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) on the reals, defined to be

$$|x|_\infty := \begin{cases} x, & x \ge 0, \\ -x, & x < 0. \end{cases}$$

This is sometimes written with a subscript 1 instead of [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824).

For a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$, the [$p$-adic absolute value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_absolute_value) on $\mathbb{Q}$ is defined as follows: any non-zero rational $x$ can be written uniquely as $x = p^n \tfrac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are [coprime integers](wikipedia://en/6556) not divisible by $p$, and $n$ is an integer; so we define

$$|x|_p := \begin{cases} 0, & x = 0, \\ p^{-n}, & x, \ne 0. \end{cases}$$

Ostrowski's theorem then states that if $|\cdot|_*: \mathbb{Q} \to \mathbb{R}$ is any absolute value on the rational numbers, then either $|\cdot|_* = |\cdot|_0$, or $|\cdot|_*$ is equivalent to $|\cdot|$, or $|\cdot|_*$ is equivalent to $|\cdot|_p$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The following proof follows the one of Theorem 10.1 in Schikhof (2007).

Let $|\cdot|_*$ be an absolute value on the rationals. We start the proof by showing that it is entirely determined by the values it takes on [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).

From the fact that $1 \times 1 = 1$ and the multiplicativity property of the absolute value, we infer that $|1|_*^2 = |1|_*$. In particular, $|1|_*$ has to be 0 or 1 and since $1 \neq 0$, one must have $|1|_* = 1$. A similar argument shows that $|-1|_* = 1$.

For all [positive integer](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$, the multiplicativity property entails $|-n|_* = |-1|_* \times |n|_* = |n|_*$. In other words, the absolute value of a negative integer coincides with that of its opposite.

Let $n$ be a positive integer. From the fact that $n^{-1} \times n = 1$ and the multiplicativity property, we conclude that $|n^{-1}|_* = |n|_*^{-1}$.

Let now $r$ be a positive rational. There exist two [coprime](wikipedia://en/6556) positive integers $p$ and $q$ such that $r = p q^{-1}$. The properties above show that $|r|_* = |p|_* |q^{-1}|_* = |p|_* |q|_*^{-1}$. Altogether, the absolute value of a positive rational is entirely determined from that of its numerator and denominator.

Finally, let $\mathbb{P}$ be the set of prime numbers. For all positive integer $n$, we can write

$$n = \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} p^{v_p(n)},$$

where $v_p(n)$ is the [p-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618) of $n$. The multiplicativity property enables one to compute the absolute value of $n$ from that of the prime numbers using the following relationship

$$|n|_* = \left| \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} p^{v_p(n)} \right|_{*} = \prod_{p \in \mathbb{P}} |p|_*^{v_p(n)}.$$

We continue the proof by separating two cases:

1. There exists a positive integer $n$ such that $|n|_* > 1$; or
1. For all integer $n$, one has $|n|_* \leq 1$.

### First case {#First_case}

Suppose that there exists a positive integer $n$ such that $|n|_* > 1.$ Let $k$ be a non-negative integer and $b$ be a positive integer greater than $1$. We express $n^k$ in [base](wikipedia://en/701207) $b$: there exist a positive integer $m$ and integers $(c_i)_{0 \leq i < m}$ such that for all $i$, $0 \leq c_i < b$ and $n^k = \sum_{i<m} c_i b^i$. In particular, $n^k \ge b^{m-1}$ so $m \leq 1 + k \log_b n$.

Each term $|c_i b^i|_*$ is at most $(b - 1) |b|_*^i$. (By the multiplicative property, $|c_i b^i|_* = |c_i|_* |b|_*^i$, then using the fact that $c_i$ is a digit, write $c_i = 1 + 1 + \cdots + 1$ so by the triangle inequality, $|c_i| \le |1| + |1| + \cdots + |1| = c_i \le b - 1$.) Besides, $|b|_*^i$ is at most $\max\{1, |b|_*^{m-1}\}$. By the triangle inequality and the above bound on $m$, it follows:

$$\begin{aligned} |n|_*^k & \leq m \max_{i<m} \{|c_i b^i|_*\} \\ & \leq m (b - 1) \max \{1, |b|_*^{m-1}\} \\ & \leq (1 + k \log_b n) (b - 1) \max\{1, |b|_*^{k \log_b n}\}. \end{aligned}$$

Therefore, raising both sides to the power $1/k$, we obtain

$$|n|_* \leq \left((1 + k \log_b n) (b - 1)\right)^{1/k} \max \{1, |b|_*^{\log_b n}\}.$$

Finally, taking the limit as $k$ tends to infinity shows that

$$|n|_* \leq \max \{1, |b|_*^{\log_b n}\}.$$

Together with the condition $|n|_* > 1,$ the above argument leads to $|b|_* > 1$ regardless of the choice of $b$ (otherwise $|b|_*^{\log_b n} \leq 1$ implies $|n|_* \leq 1$). As a result, all integers greater than one have an absolute value strictly greater than one. Thus generalizing the above, for any choice of integers $n$ and $b$ greater than or equal to 2, we get

$$|n|_* \leq |b|_*^{\log_b n},$$

i.e.

$$\log_n |n|_* \leq \log_b |b|_*.$$

By symmetry, this inequality is an equality. In particular, for all $n \geq 2$, $\log_n |n|_* = \log_2 |2|_* = \lambda$, i.e. $|n|_* = n^\lambda = |n|_\infty^\lambda$. Because the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941) implies that for all positive integers $n$ we have $|n|_* \leq n$, in this case we obtain more precisely that $0 < \lambda \leq 1$.

As per the above result on the determination of an absolute value by its values on the prime numbers, we easily see that $|r|_* = |r|_\infty^\lambda$ for all rational $r$, thus demonstrating equivalence to the real absolute value.

### Second case {#Second_case}

Suppose that for all integer $n$, one has $|n|_* \leq 1$. As our absolute value is non-trivial, there must exist a positive integer $n$ for which $|n|_* < 1.$ Decomposing $|n|_*$ on the prime numbers shows that there exists $p \in \mathbb{P}$ such that $|p|_* < 1$. We claim that in fact this is so for one prime number only.

Suppose *[by way of contradiction](wikipedia://en/22456146)* that $p$ and $q$ are two distinct primes with absolute value strictly less than 1. Let $k$ be a positive integer such that $|p|_*^k$ and $|q|_*^k$ are smaller than $1/2$. By [Bézout's identity](wikipedia://en/4947), since $p^k$ and $q^k$ are [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime), there exist two integers $a$ and $b$ such that $a p^k + b q^k = 1.$ This yields a contradiction, as

$$1 = |1|_* \leq |a|_* |p|_*^k + |b|_* |q|_*^k < \frac{|a|_* + |b|_*}{2} \leq 1.$$

This means that there exists a unique prime $p$ such that $|p|_* < 1$ and that for all other primes $q$, one has $|q|_* = 1$ (from the hypothesis of this second case). Let $\lambda = -\log_p |p|_*$. From $|p|_* < 1$, we infer that $0 < \lambda < \infty$. (And indeed in this case, all positive $\lambda$ give absolute values equivalent to the p-adic one.)

We finally verify that $|p|_p^\lambda = p^{-\lambda} = |p|_*$ and that for all other primes $q$, $|q|_p^\lambda = 1 = |q|_*$. As per the above result on the determination of an absolute value by its values on the prime numbers, we conclude that $|r|_* = |r|_p^{\lambda}$ for all rational $r$, implying that this absolute value is equivalent to the $p$-adic one. [$\blacksquare$](wikipedia://en/25228)

:::

## Another Ostrowski's theorem {#Another_Ostrowski's_theorem}

Another theorem states that any field, complete with respect to an [Archimedean absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667#Types_of_absolute_value), is (algebraically and topologically) isomorphic to either the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) or the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers). This is sometimes also referred to as Ostrowski's theorem.[^o12]
