::: {#o8 .subject cue=lead name="Hessian polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Hessian polyhedron** is a [regular complex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_polytope) <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>, , in $\mathbb{C}^3$. It has 27 vertices, 72 <sub>3</sub>{} edges, and 27 [<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>](wikipedia://en/51430790) faces. It is self-dual.

:::

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) named it after [Ludwig Otto Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Otto_Hesse) for sharing the *[Hessian configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_configuration)* $\left [\begin{smallmatrix} 9&4\\3&12 \end{smallmatrix}\right ]$ or (9<sub>4</sub>12<sub>3</sub>), 9 points lying by threes on twelve lines, with four lines through each point.[^o9]

Its [complex reflection group](wikipedia://en/5381096) is <sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub> or , order 648, also called a [Hessian group](wikipedia://en/35283230). It has 27 copies of , order 24, at each vertex. It has 24 order-3 reflections. Its [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) is 12, with degrees of the fundamental invariants 3, 6, and 12, which can be seen in projective symmetry of the polytopes.

The [Witting polytope](wikipedia://en/28363938), <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>, contains the Hessian polyhedron as [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719).

It has a real representation as the [*2<sub>21</sub>* polytope](wikipedia://en/19232473), , in 6-dimensional space, sharing the same 27 vertices. The 216 edges in *2<sub>21</sub>* can be seen as the 72 <sub>3</sub>{} edges represented as 3 simple edges.

The *Hessian polyhedron* can be seen as an alternation of , = . This *double Hessian polyhedron* has 54 vertices, 216 simple edges, and 72 faces. Its vertices represent the union of the vertices and its dual .

Its [complex reflection group](wikipedia://en/5381096) is <sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>\[4\]<sub>2</sub>, or , order 1296. It has 54 copies of , order 24, at each vertex. It has 24 order-3 reflections and 9 order-2 reflections. Its [coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) is 18, with degrees of the fundamental invariants 6, 12, and 18 which can be seen in projective symmetry of the polytopes.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) noted that the three complex polytopes , , resemble the real [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) (), [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) (), and [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) (). The Hessian is analogous to the tetrahedron, like the cube is a [double tetrahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_two_tetrahedra), and the octahedron as a rectified tetrahedron. In both sets the vertices of the first belong to two dual pairs of the second, and the vertices of the third are at the center of the edges of the second.[^o25]

Its real representation 54 vertices are contained by two [2<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19232473) polytopes in symmetric configurations: and . Its vertices can also be seen in the dual polytope of [1<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070548).
