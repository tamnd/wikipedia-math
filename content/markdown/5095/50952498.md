# William Gentle (headmaster)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Gentle"}

**William Gentle** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (19 January 1877 – 31 March 1964) was a Scottish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), and headmaster of [George Heriot's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Heriot's_School) from 1926 to 1942. He was President of the Science Teachers Association in Scotland.[^o2]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee) on 19 January 1877 the third child of Marion Drummond Brand from [Torryburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torryburn) in [Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fife) and William Gentle (1838–1890), a printer. He was raised at 12 Garland Place in Dundee.[^o4] He attended Rosebank School then [Morgan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Academy). In 1889 his family moved to 2 Blackwood Crescent in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh), a Victorian flat in the south side of the city. He then attended George Heriot's School going on to study mathematics and other sciences at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), graduating in 1903 with a BSc. He studied geology under [Professor James Geikie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Geikie) and mathematics under [Professor George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803). In autumn 1903 he returned to George Heriot's School as a teacher of mathematics and physics.[^o5][^o6]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1908. His proposers were [James Gordon MacGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gordon_MacGregor), [David Fowler Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fowler_Lowe), Thomas Burns and [John Brown Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_Clark).[^o7] In the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) he served as an officer in the [Royal Field Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Field_Artillery), and was wounded by shell-fire at [Messines Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messines_Ridge) in 1917. Thereafter he concerned himself with supply of food to the troops, at one point finding himself responsible for feeding 7,000 men.[^o5]

He returned to George Heriot's School after the war. In 1926 he succeeded [John Brown Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_Clark) as headmaster at a salary of £1,000 per annum. He retired in 1942 and was succeeded by William Carnon.

He died in Edinburgh on 31 March 1964.

## Family {#Family}

In 1924 he married Jessie Currie Ainslie.
