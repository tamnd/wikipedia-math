# Harold S. Shapiro

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Harold S. Shapiro"}

**Harold Seymour Shapiro** (2 April 1928[^o3] – 5 March 2021) was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden), best known for inventing the so-called [Shapiro polynomials](wikipedia://en/10338711) (also known as Golay–Shapiro polynomials or Rudin–Shapiro polynomials) and for work on [quadrature domains](wikipedia://en/10186385).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born and raised in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York, to a Jewish family, Shapiro earned a [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) from the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) in 1949 and earned his [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) degree from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1951. He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1952 from MIT; his thesis was written under the supervision of [Norman Levinson](wikipedia://en/1090122). He was the father of cosmologist [Max Tegmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Tegmark), a graduate of the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) and now a professor at MIT. Shapiro died on 5 March 2021, aged 92.[^o6]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

His main research areas were [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). He was also interested in the [pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) of problem-solving. He collaborated with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) in June 1965 on "Large and small subspaces of [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932)", therefore he has an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1.
