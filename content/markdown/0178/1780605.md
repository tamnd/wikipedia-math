# Luigi Poletti (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luigi Poletti"}

**Luigi Poletti** (31 December 1864 – 10 March 1967) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and poet. He was born in [Pontremoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontremoli), where he also died, at the age of 102.[^o2][^o3]

:::

He attended the episcopal seminary in Potremoli, then the high school of [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma), graduated in Turin and started to study [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) there. He did not finish and took a job in a bank. In 1911, he accidentally found the book of [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) tables written by [Lehmer](wikipedia://en/26612218), a mathematician from the United States, in the house of professor [Gino Loria](wikipedia://en/36957490), a friend of his family, when he visited [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa). Since then, he spent many years extending the first table in order to simplify "Eratosthenes Crivello" ([sieve of Eratosthenes](wikipedia://en/73415)), a method from ancient [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) to find [prime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_numbers). He gave his method a new name: "Neocribrum" (Novum Eratosthenes Cribrum), and he got recognition from the scientific community. Apart from that, he was, together with [André Gerardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Gerardin), a member of a study commission of the Association française pour l'avancement des sciences (1946). With the assistance of [N. G. W. H. Beeger](wikipedia://en/1550971) he had the possibility to extend the table of [Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer) beyond the number 10 006 721. In 1955, he was awarded a gold medal and the order of the Republic of [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) by Italian president [Giovanni Gronchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gronchi).

In his long life (102 years), he was a member of the city council in the rank of Commissario Prefettizio. He was a [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet) in his native dialect Pontremolese (poems and texts for popular music). He wrote, for example, Al Campanon d‘ Pontrémal, La Zumniana (for which he also composed the music) or Al cant dal Cont Ugolìn. He translated XXXIII of "[Dante's Inferno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante's_Inferno)". As an amateur, he wrote poems in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin).
