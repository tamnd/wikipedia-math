# Wilfrid Hodges

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Wilfrid Hodges"}

**Wilfrid Augustine Hodges** (born 27 May 1941) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) known for his work in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hodges attended [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford) (1959–65), where he received degrees in both *[Literae Humaniores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literae_Humaniores)* and (Christianic) [Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology). In 1970 he was awarded a doctorate for a thesis in [Logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). He lectured in both [Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Bedford College, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College_%28London%29). He has held visiting appointments in the department of philosophy at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles)[^o5] and in the department of mathematics at [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder). Hodges was Professor of Mathematics at [Queen Mary College, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_College,_University_of_London) from 1987 to 2006 and is the author of books on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Hodges was President of the [British Logic Colloquium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Logic_Colloquium), of the European [Association for Logic, Language and Information](wikipedia://en/22910426) and of the [Division of Logic, Methodology, and Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Logic,_Methodology,_and_Philosophy_of_Science). In 2009 he was elected a Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy).

## Writing style {#Writing_style}

Hodges' books are written in an informal style. The "Notes on Notation" in his book "Model theory" end with the following characteristic sentence:

'I' means I, 'we' means we.

When this 780-page book appeared in 1993, it became one of the standard textbooks on [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858). Due to its success an abbreviated version (but with a new chapter on [stability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_theory_%28model_theory%29)) was published as a paperback.
