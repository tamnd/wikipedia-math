# Fibrant object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fibrant object"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) in the context of a [model category](wikipedia://en/2895304) *M*, a **fibrant object** *A* of *M* is an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) that has a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) to the [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) of the [category](wikipedia://en/53759).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The fibrant objects of a [closed model category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_model_category) are characterized by having a [right lifting property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_lifting_property) with respect to any [trivial cofibration](wikipedia://en/1697216) in the category. This property makes fibrant objects the "correct" objects on which to define [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506). In the context of the theory of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784), the fibrant objects are known as **Kan complexes** after [Daniel Kan](wikipedia://en/2290155). They are the [Kan fibrations](wikipedia://en/8992759) over a point.

Dually is the notion of cofibrant object, defined to be an object $c$ such that the unique morphism $\varnothing\to c$ from the initial object to $c$ is a cofibration.

:::
