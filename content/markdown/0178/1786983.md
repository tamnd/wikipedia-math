# Canada/USA Mathcamp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canada/USA Mathcamp"}

**Canada/USA Mathcamp** is a five-week academic [summer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_camp) for middle and high school students in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

Mathcamp was founded in 1993 by Dr. George Thomas, who believed that students interested in mathematics frequently lacked the resources and camaraderie to pursue their interest. Mira Bernstein became the director when Thomas left in 2002 to found [MathPath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MathPath), a program for younger students.

Mathcamp is held each year at a college campus in the United States or Canada. Past locations have included the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), [Colorado College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_College), [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College), [University of Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Puget_Sound), [Colby College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colby_College), the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College), and the [Colorado School of Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_School_of_Mines). Mathcamp enrolls about 120 students yearly, 55 returning and 65 new.

The application process for new students includes an entrance exam (the "Qualifying Quiz"), personal essay, but no grade reports or letters of recommendation (although a reference, who may receive a few short answer questions, is still required). The process is intended to ensure that the students who are most passionate about math come to camp. Admission is selective: in 2016, the acceptance rate was 15%.[^o2]

Mathcamp courses cover various branches of recreational and college-level mathematics.[^o3] Classes at Mathcamp come in four difficulty levels. The easier classes often include [basic proof techniques](wikipedia://en/82285), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), and [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231), while the more difficult classes cover advanced topics in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392), [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), and [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). There are generally four class periods each day and five classes offered during each period intended for varying student interests and backgrounds. [Graduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_student) mentors teach most of the classes, while [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate) junior counselors, all of them Mathcamp alumni, do most of the behind-the-scenes work. Mathcamp has had a number of renowned guest speakers, including [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807),[^o4] [Avi Wigderson](wikipedia://en/6570979), and [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991).

## Culture {#Culture}

In 2004, some campers created Foodtongue, a [constructed language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructed_language) in which every word is a word that means a [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food) in the [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^o7] One of the cardinal rules of the language is an agreed ban of direct translation. Foodtongue remains popular among campers, and was compiled into an online [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiki), updated and referenced by speakers of the language.[^o8]

:::
