# Glossary of areas of mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of areas of mathematics"}

[Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) is a broad subject that is commonly divided in many **areas** or **branches** that may be defined by [their objects of study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematical_objects), by the used methods, or by both. For example, [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) is a subarea of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) devoted to the use of methods of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) for the study of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474).

:::

This glossary is alphabetically sorted. This hides a large part of the relationships between areas. For the broadest areas of mathematics, see Mathematics#Areas of mathematics. The [Mathematics Subject Classification](wikipedia://en/3008091) is a hierarchical list of areas and subjects of study that has been elaborated by the community of mathematicians. It is used by most publishers for classifying mathematical articles and books.

## A {#A}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=term name="Absolute differential calculus"}

**[Absolute differential calculus](wikipedia://en/35456546)**

An older name of Ricci calculus

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=term name="Absolute geometry"}

**[Absolute geometry](wikipedia://en/699294)**

Also called neutral geometry,[^o5] a [synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484) similar to [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) but without the [parallel postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493).[^o6]

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=term name="Abstract algebra"}

**[Abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384)**

The part of algebra devoted to the study of [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) in themselves.[^o8] Occasionally named modern algebra in course titles.

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=term name="Abstract analytic number theory"}

**[Abstract analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/2035764)**

The study of [arithmetic semigroups](wikipedia://en/2035764) as a means to extend notions from [classical analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o10]

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=term name="Abstract differential geometry"}

**Abstract differential geometry**

A form of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) without the notion of [smoothness](wikipedia://en/1408000) from [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176). Instead it is built using [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) and [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357).

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=term name="Abstract harmonic analysis"}

**[Abstract harmonic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_harmonic_analysis)**

A modern branch of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) that extends upon the generalized [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) that can be defined on [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204).

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=term name="Abstract homotopy theory"}

**[Abstract homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831)**

A part of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) that deals with homotopic functions, i.e. functions from one topological space to another which are homotopic (the functions can be deformed into one another).

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=term name="Actuarial science"}

**[Actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373)**

The discipline that applies [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) and [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) methods to [assess risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_assessment) in [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) and other industries and professions. More generally, actuaries apply rigorous mathematics to model matters of uncertainty.

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=term name="Additive combinatorics"}

**[Additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392)**

The part of [arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381) devoted to the operations of [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) and [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141).

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=term name="Additive number theory"}

**[Additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333)**

A part of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) that studies subsets of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) and their behaviour under addition.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=term name="Affine geometry"}

**[Affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890)**

A branch of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) that deals with properties that are independent from distances and angles, such as [alignment](wikipedia://en/3189581) and [parallelism](wikipedia://en/664497).

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=term name="Affine geometry of curves"}

**[Affine geometry of curves](wikipedia://en/15967139)**

The study of [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) properties that are invariant under [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449).

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=term name="Affine differential geometry"}

**[Affine differential geometry](wikipedia://en/14222703)**

A type of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) dedicated to differential [invariants](wikipedia://en/1126638) under [volume](wikipedia://en/32498)-preserving [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449).

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=term name="Ahlfors theory"}

**[Ahlfors theory](wikipedia://en/34351523)**

A part of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) being the geometric counterpart of [Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135). It was invented by [Lars Ahlfors](wikipedia://en/61636).

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=term name="Algebra"}

**[Algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)**

One of the major [areas of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_mathematics). Roughly speaking, it is the art of manipulating and computing with [operations](wikipedia://en/4140245) acting on symbols called [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) that represent indeterminate [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) or other [mathematical objects](wikipedia://en/19453961), such as [vectors](wikipedia://en/23218956), [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859), or elements of [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364).

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic analysis"}

**[Algebraic analysis](wikipedia://en/20846707)**

motivated by systems of [linear](wikipedia://en/379868) [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), it is a branch of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) that uses methods from [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory) and complex analysis, to study the properties and generalizations of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427). It was started by [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336).

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic combinatorics"}

**[Algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582)**

an area that employs methods of abstract algebra to problems of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). It also refers to the application of methods from combinatorics to problems in abstract algebra.

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic computation"}

**[Algebraic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_computation)**

An older name of [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805).

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic geometry"}

**[Algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997)**

a branch that combines techniques from abstract algebra with the language and problems of geometry. Fundamentally, it studies [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties).

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic graph theory"}

**[Algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216)**

a branch of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) in which methods are taken from algebra and employed to problems about [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806). The methods are commonly taken from [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and linear algebra.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic K-theory"}

**[Algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500)**

an important part of [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) concerned with defining and applying a certain sequence of [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) from [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) to [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974).

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic number theory"}

**[Algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705)**

The part of number theory devoted to the use of algebraic methods, mainly those of commutative algebra, for the study of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) and their [rings of integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rings_of_integers).

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic statistics"}

**[Algebraic statistics](wikipedia://en/17314682)**

the use of algebra to advance [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), although the term is sometimes restricted to label the use of algebraic geometry and [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=term name="Algebraic topology"}

**[Algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801)**

a branch that uses tools from [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) for [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) to study [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450).

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=term name="Algorithmic number theory"}

**[Algorithmic number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_number_theory)**

also known as *computational number theory*, it is the study of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for performing [number theoretic](wikipedia://en/21527) [computations](wikipedia://en/5926).

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=term name="Anabelian geometry"}

**[Anabelian geometry](wikipedia://en/27119265)**

an area of study based on the theory proposed by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in the 1980s that describes the way a geometric object of an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) (such as an [algebraic fundamental group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_fundamental_group)) can be mapped into another object, without it being an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974).

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=term name="Analysis"}

**[Analysis](wikipedia://en/48396)**

A wide area of mathematics centered on the study of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) and including such topics as [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiation_%28mathematics%29), [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_%28mathematics%29), [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602), and [series](wikipedia://en/15287).[^o34]

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=term name="Analytic combinatorics"}

**[Analytic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/75060288)**

part of [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533) where methods of complex analysis are applied to [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993).

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=term name="Analytic geometry"}

**[Analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202)**

Also known as Cartesian geometry, the study of Euclidean geometry using [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates).

Analogue to differential geometry, where [differentiable functions](wikipedia://en/330206) are replaced with [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478). It is a subarea of both complex analysis and algebraic geometry.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=term name="Analytic number theory"}

**[Analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513)**

An area of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) that applies methods from [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) to solve problems about [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=term name="Analytic theory of L-functions"}

**[Analytic theory of L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283)**

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=term name="Applied mathematics"}

**[Applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969)**

a combination of various parts of mathematics that concern a variety of mathematical methods that can be applied to practical and theoretical problems. Typically the methods used are for [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics).

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=term name="Approximation theory"}

**[Approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713)**

part of [analysis](wikipedia://en/1134) that studies how well functions can be approximated by simpler ones (such as [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) or [trigonometric polynomials](wikipedia://en/591568))

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=term name="Arakelov geometry"}

**[Arakelov geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakelov_geometry)**

also known as *Arakelov theory*

:::

::: {#o43 .entry cue=term name="Arakelov theory"}

**[Arakelov theory](wikipedia://en/16364229)**

an approach to [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) used to study [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) in higher dimensions (using techniques from algebraic geometry). It is named after [Suren Arakelov](wikipedia://en/10705349).

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic"}

**[Arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118)**

Also known as elementary arithmetic, the methods and rules for computing with [addition](wikipedia://en/61338), [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) and [division](wikipedia://en/53696) of numbers.

Also known as higher arithmetic, another name for [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic algebraic geometry"}

**[Arithmetic algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_algebraic_geometry)**

See arithmetic geometry.

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic combinatorics"}

**[Arithmetic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243381)**

the study of the estimates from [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) that are associated with [arithmetic operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_operation) such as addition, [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) and [division](wikipedia://en/53696).

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic dynamics"}

**[Arithmetic dynamics](wikipedia://en/17584701)**

Arithmetic dynamics is the study of the number-theoretic properties of [integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_point), [rational](wikipedia://en/2960200), $p$-adic, and/or algebraic points under repeated application of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) or [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210). A fundamental goal is to describe arithmetic properties in terms of underlying geometric structures.

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic geometry"}

**[Arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177)**

The use of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and more specially [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory) for solving problems of number theory.

:::

::: {#o49 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetic topology"}

**[Arithmetic topology](wikipedia://en/23834912)**

a combination of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) studying analogies between [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) and [knots](wikipedia://en/366808)

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=term name="Arithmetical algebraic geometry"}

**[Arithmetical algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetical_algebraic_geometry)**

Another name for arithmetic algebraic geometry

:::

::: {#o51 .entry cue=term name="Asymptotic combinatorics"}

**[Asymptotic combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_combinatorics)**

It uses the internal structure of the objects to derive formulas for their [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993) and then complex analysis techniques to get asymptotics.

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=term name="Asymptotic theory"}

**[Asymptotic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_theory)**

the study of [asymptotic expansions](wikipedia://en/642090)

:::

::: {#o53 .entry cue=term name="Auslander–Reiten theory"}

**[Auslander–Reiten theory](wikipedia://en/30350056)**

the study of the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273)

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=term name="Axiomatic geometry"}

**Axiomatic geometry**

also known as *[synthetic geometry](wikipedia://en/267484)*: it is a branch of geometry that uses [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) and [logical arguments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_argument) to draw conclusions as opposed to [analytic](wikipedia://en/2202) and algebraic methods.

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=term name="Axiomatic set theory"}

**[Axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory)**

the study of systems of [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) in a context relevant to [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).

:::

## B {#B}

::: {#o57 .entry cue=term name="Bifurcation theory"}

**[Bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042)**

the study of changes in the qualitative or topological structure of a given family. It is a part of [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632)

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=term name="Biostatistics"}

**[Biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878)**

the development and application of [statistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical) methods to a wide range of topics in [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology).

:::

::: {#o59 .entry cue=term name="Birational geometry"}

**[Birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733)**

a part of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) that deals with the geometry (of an algebraic variety) that is dependent only on its [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843).

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=term name="Bolyai–Lobachevskian geometry"}

**Bolyai–Lobachevskian geometry**

see *hyperbolic geometry*

:::

## C {#C}

::: {#o62 .entry cue=term name="C*-algebra theory"}

**[C\*-algebra theory](wikipedia://en/7184)**

a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) *A* of [continuous linear operators](wikipedia://en/1802169) on a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) with two additional properties-(i) *A* is a topologically [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) in the [norm topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_topology) of operators.(ii)*A* is closed under the operation of taking [adjoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_of_an_operator) of operators.

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=term name="Cartesian geometry"}

**[Cartesian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_geometry)**

see *analytic geometry*

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=term name="Calculus"}

**[Calculus](wikipedia://en/5176)**

An area of mathematics connected by the [fundamental theorem of calculus](wikipedia://en/19468696).[^o65]

:::

::: {#o66 .entry cue=term name="Calculus of infinitesimals"}

**[Calculus of infinitesimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_calculus)**

Also called **infinitesimal calculus**

A foundation of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), first developed in the 17th century,[^o67] that makes use of [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) numbers.

:::

::: {#o68 .entry cue=term name="Calculus of moving surfaces"}

**[Calculus of moving surfaces](wikipedia://en/28357925)**

an extension of the theory of [tensor calculus](wikipedia://en/29965) to include deforming [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

:::

::: {#o69 .entry cue=term name="Calculus of variations"}

**[Calculus of variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variation)**

the field dedicated to maximizing or minimizing [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948). It used to be called *functional calculus*.

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=term name="Catastrophe theory"}

**[Catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288)**

a branch of [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042) from [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), and also a special case of the more general [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763) from geometry. It analyses the [germs](wikipedia://en/352714) of the catastrophe geometries.

:::

::: {#o71 .entry cue=term name="Categorical logic"}

**[Categorical logic](wikipedia://en/1063799)**

a branch of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) adjacent to the [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). It is based on [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282) for [intuitionistic logics](wikipedia://en/169262).

:::

::: {#o72 .entry cue=term name="Category theory"}

**[Category theory](wikipedia://en/5869)**

the study of the properties of particular mathematical concepts by formalising them as collections of objects and arrows.

:::

::: {#o73 .entry cue=term name="Chaos theory"}

**[Chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295)**

the study of the behaviour of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) that are highly sensitive to their initial conditions.

:::

::: {#o74 .entry cue=term name="Character theory"}

**[Character theory](wikipedia://en/691741)**

a branch of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) that studies the characters of [group representations](wikipedia://en/12695) or [modular representations](wikipedia://en/1110685).

:::

::: {#o75 .entry cue=term name="Class field theory"}

**[Class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695)**

a branch of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) that studies [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) of [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field).

:::

::: {#o76 .entry cue=term name="Classical differential geometry"}

**[Classical differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)**

It includes any work that does not use techniques from [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and is sometimes called *hard analysis*. However it may also refer to mathematical analysis done according to the principles of [classical mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mathematics).

::: {#o230 .entry cue=term name="Geometry"}

**[Geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)**

a branch of mathematics concerned with [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) and the properties of [space](wikipedia://en/5308894). Classically it arose as what is now known as [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960); this was concerning practical knowledge of [length](wikipedia://en/18542), [area](wikipedia://en/1209) and [volume](wikipedia://en/32498). It was then put into an [axiomatic form](wikipedia://en/188401) by [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), giving rise to what is now known as classical Euclidean geometry. The use of [coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate) by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) gave rise to [Cartesian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_geometry) enabling a more analytical approach to geometric entities. Since then many other branches have appeared including [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610), [Fractal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry) and algebraic geometry. Geometry also gave rise to the modern discipline of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).
