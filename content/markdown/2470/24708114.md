# Robert Rumely

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Rumely"}

**Robert Scott Rumely** (born 1952) is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) who specializes in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177).[^o2] He is one of the inventors of the [Adleman–Pomerance–Rumely primality test](wikipedia://en/8198761).[^o3]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Rumely was born on June 23, 1952, in [Pullman, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman,_Washington). He graduated from [Grinnell College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grinnell_College) in 1974,[^o5] and completed his Ph.D. in 1978 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724). After temporary positions at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), he joined the University of Georgia faculty in 1981.[^o5]

Rumely has taught a summer [Research Experiences for Undergraduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Experiences_for_Undergraduates) program on the [mathematics of paper folding](wikipedia://en/232840).[^o7]

## Books {#Books}

He is the author or co-author of four books:

- *Capacity Theory on Algebraic Curves* (*[Lecture Notes in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/47931235)* 1378, 1989)[^o9][^o10]
- *Existence of the Sectional Capacity* (*[Memoirs of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30441553)* 145, 2000)[^o11]
- *Potential Theory and Dynamics on the Berkovich Projective Line* (*[Mathematical Surveys and Monographs](wikipedia://en/28100142)* 159, 2010)[^o12]
- *Capacity Theory with Local Rationality: The Strong Fekete-Szegö Theorem on Curves* (*[Mathematical Surveys and Monographs](wikipedia://en/28100142)* 193, 2013)[^o13]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2015 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822#Fellows) "for contributions to arithmetic potential theory, computational number theory, and arithmetic dynamics".[^o15]
