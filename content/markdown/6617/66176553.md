# Roger C. Alperin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger C. Alperin"}

**Roger Charles Alperin** (January 8, 1947 – November 21, 2019) was an American mathematician, best known for his work in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), including its connections with [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). He was a professor at the [University of Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma) and at [San Jose State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_State_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Alperin was born on January 8, 1947, in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).[^o3] He received a bachelor's degree from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago),[^o4] and his PhD from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) in 1973. His thesis was supervised by [Stephen M. Gersten](wikipedia://en/61897288), and was titled *Whitehead Torsion of Finite Abelian Groups*. After temporary positions at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College), and [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis), Alperin took a permanent position at the [University of Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma) in 1978.[^o6] He was eventually promoted to full professor at the University of Oklahoma, but resigned his position to move to California in 1987.[^o6] Upon moving to California, he found a position at San Jose State University, which he held until his retirement in 2015.[^o6]

Alperin died on November 21, 2019, at his home in [Carlsbad, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsbad,_California).[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Alperin's work on [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914) in the 80s (partly joint with [Hyman Bass](wikipedia://en/7085910) and Kenneth Moss) helped to stimulate interest in these objects, and helped establish them as a basic tool in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).[^o6] Alperin has also done foundational work on the [mathematical theory](wikipedia://en/22468661) of [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami).[^o3]
