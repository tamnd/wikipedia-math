# Dedekind–Hasse norm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dedekind–Hasse norm"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in particular the study of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **Dedekind–Hasse norm** is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) on an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) that generalises the notion of a [Euclidean function](wikipedia://en/10376#Definition) on [Euclidean domains](wikipedia://en/10376).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *R* be an integral domain and *g* : *R* → **Z**<sub>≥0</sub> be a function from *R* to the non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563). Denote by 0<sub>*R*</sub> the additive identity of *R*. The function *g* is called a *Dedekind–Hasse norm* on *R* if the following three conditions are satisfied:

- *g*(*a*) = 0 [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *a* = 0<sub>*R*</sub>,
- for any nonzero elements *a* and *b* in *R* either:
  - *b* [divides](wikipedia://en/32981716) *a* in *R*, or
  - there exist elements *x* and *y* in *R* such that 0 \< *g*(*xa* − *yb*) \< *g*(*b*).

The third condition is a slight generalisation of condition (EF1) of Euclidean functions, as defined in the [Euclidean domain](wikipedia://en/10376) article. If the value of *x* can always be taken as 1 then *g* will in fact be a Euclidean function and *R* will therefore be a Euclidean domain.

:::

## Integral and principal ideal domains {#Integral_and_principal_ideal_domains}

The notion of a Dedekind–Hasse norm was developed independently by [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106) and, later, by [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118). They both noticed it was precisely the extra piece of structure needed to turn an integral domain into a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039). To wit, they [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that if an integral domain *R* has a Dedekind–Hasse norm, then *R* is a principal ideal domain.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let *K* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) and consider the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) *K*\[*X*\]. The function *g* on this domain that maps a nonzero [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) *p* to 2<sup>deg(*p*)</sup>, where deg(*p*) is the [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) of *p*, and maps the zero polynomial to zero, is a Dedekind–Hasse norm on *K*\[*X*\]. The first two conditions are satisfied simply by the definition of *g*, while the third condition can be proved using [polynomial long division](wikipedia://en/314205).

:::
