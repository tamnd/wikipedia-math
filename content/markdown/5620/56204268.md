# Johan Antony Barrau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johan Antony Barrau"}

*Image: Johan Antony Barrau.jpg.* Johan Antony Barrau

**Johan Antony Barrau** (3 April 1873, [Oisterwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oisterwijk) – 8 January 1953, Utrecht) was a Dutch mathematician, specializing in geometry.[^o2]

:::

Barrau was educated at the [Dutch Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College_%28Netherlands%29) at [Willemsoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemsoord,_Den_Helder) and then at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam). From 1891 to 1898, Barrau was an officer with the [Royal Netherlands Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Navy), later with the [Netherlands Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Marine_Corps). However, he left the service and became a mathematics teacher at a [Hogere Burgerschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogere_Burgerschool) in [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht) until 1900, then in Amsterdam.[^o2] In 1907 he obtained his PhD at the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) under the supervision of [Diederik Korteweg](wikipedia://en/1978798). From 1908 to 1913 Barrau was a mathematics professor at the [Delft University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft_University_of_Technology). He was a professor of synthetic, analytical and descriptive differential geometry at the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen) from 1913 to 1928.[^o2] From 1928 until his retirement at age 70, he was a professor at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University).[^o4] He received the military service medal consisting of the [Expedition Cross with the Atjeh clasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_Cross) and was named Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion). Barrau published a textbook on analytical geometry and various articles in national and international journals.[^o2]

He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1920 at Strasbourg[^o5] and in 1924 at Toronto.[^o6]
