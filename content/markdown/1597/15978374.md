# Martin's maximum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Martin's maximum"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), a branch of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **Martin's maximum**, introduced by (Foreman Magidor Shelah 1988) and named after [Donald Martin](wikipedia://en/2270985), is a stronger form of the [proper forcing axiom](wikipedia://en/6250799), itself a stronger form of [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783). It represents the broadest class of [forcings](wikipedia://en/152205) for which a forcing axiom is consistent.

:::

Martin's maximum $(\operatorname{MM})$ states that if *D* is a collection of $\aleph_1$ dense subsets of a notion of forcing that preserves stationary subsets of *ω*<sub>1</sub>, then there is a *D*-generic filter. Forcing with a [ccc](wikipedia://en/1550771) notion of forcing preserves stationary subsets of *ω*<sub>1</sub>, thus $\operatorname{MM}$ extends $\operatorname{MA}(\aleph_1)$. If (*P*,≤) is not a stationary set preserving notion of forcing, i.e., there is a stationary subset of *ω*<sub>1</sub>, which becomes nonstationary when forcing with (*P*,≤), then there is a collection *D* of $\aleph_1$ dense subsets of (*P*,≤), such that there is no *D*-generic filter. This is why $\operatorname{MM}$ is called the maximal extension of Martin's axiom.

The existence of a [supercompact cardinal](wikipedia://en/248116) implies the consistency of Martin's maximum.[^n1] The proof uses [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211)'s theories of semiproper forcing and iteration with revised countable supports.

$\operatorname{MM}$ implies that the value of the [continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901) is $\aleph_2$[^n2] and that the ideal of [nonstationary sets](wikipedia://en/1679067) on ω<sub>1</sub> is $\aleph_2$-saturated.[^n3] It further implies stationary reflection, i.e., if *S* is a stationary subset of some regular cardinal *κ* ≥ *ω*<sub>2</sub> and every element of *S* has countable cofinality, then there is an ordinal *α* \< *κ* such that *S* ∩ *α* is stationary in *α*. In fact, *S* contains a closed subset of order type *ω*<sub>1</sub>.
