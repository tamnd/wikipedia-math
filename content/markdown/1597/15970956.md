# Haagerup property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Haagerup property"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Haagerup property**, named after [Uffe Haagerup](wikipedia://en/46705302) and also known as [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29)'s **a-T-menability**, is a property of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) that is a strong negation of [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724). Property (T) is considered a representation-theoretic form of rigidity, so the Haagerup property may be considered a form of strong nonrigidity; see below for details.

:::

The Haagerup property is interesting to many fields of mathematics, including [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [operator K-theory](wikipedia://en/13314265), and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).

Perhaps its most impressive consequence is that groups with the Haagerup Property satisfy the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498) and the related [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194). Groups with the Haagerup property are also uniformly [embeddable](wikipedia://en/52119) into a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $G$ be a [second countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_countable) [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204). The following properties are all equivalent, and any of them may be taken to be definitions of the Haagerup property:

1. There is a [proper](wikipedia://en/1190262) [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) conditionally [negative definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_definite) [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $\Psi\colon G \to \mathbb{R}^+$.
1. $G$ has the **Haagerup approximation property**, also known as **Property $C_0$**: there is a sequence of normalized continuous [positive-definite functions](wikipedia://en/10640474) $\phi_n$ which vanish at infinity on $G$ and converge to 1 [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on [compact subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_subset) of $G$.
1. There is a [strongly continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_continuous) [unitary representation](wikipedia://en/292788) of $G$ which [weakly contains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_containment) the [trivial representation](wikipedia://en/450573) and whose matrix coefficients vanish at infinity on $G$.
1. There is a proper continuous affine isometric action of $G$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

There are many examples of groups with the Haagerup property, most of which are geometric in origin. The list includes:

- All [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326) (trivially). Note all compact groups also have [property (T)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_%28T%29). The converse holds as well: if a group has both property (T) and the Haagerup property, then it is compact.
- [SO(n,1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%28n,1%29)
- [SU(n,1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28n%29)
- Groups acting properly on trees or on $\mathbb{R}$-trees
- [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004)
- [Amenable groups](wikipedia://en/657430)
- Groups acting properly on [CAT(0)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29) [cubical complexes](wikipedia://en/54829672)

:::
