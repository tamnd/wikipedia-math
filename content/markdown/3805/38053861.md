# Joan Feigenbaum

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joan Feigenbaum"}

**Joan Feigenbaum** (born 1958 in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City)) is a [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) with a background in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). She is the [Grace Murray Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Murray_Hopper) Professor of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University)[^o3] and an Amazon Scholar in the [AWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWS) Cryptography group. At Yale, she also holds a secondary appointment in the Department of [Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics). She has worked in several research areas over the course of her career, including cryptography, security, and privacy; computational complexity; algorithmic mechanism design; massive-data-stream algorithmics; and, most recently, computer science and law.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Feigenbaum did her undergraduate work in Mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). She became interested in computers during the Summer Research Program at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) between her junior and senior years. She then earned a Ph.D. in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), under the supervision of [Andrew Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yao), while working summers at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs). Upon finishing her PhD in 1986, she joined [AT&T Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT&T_Bell_Labs). She moved to Yale in 2000, became the Hopper Professor at Yale in 2008,[^o3] and became an Amazon Scholar in 2018.

## Family {#Family}

She is married to Jeffrey Nussbaum. They have a son, Sam Baum. Baum was chosen as the child's surname, because it is the [greatest common suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_common_substring_problem) of Feigenbaum and Nussbaum.[^o7]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1998, Feigenbaum was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin[^o9] and, in 1999, she was a Plenary Speaker at the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Winter Meeting in San Antonio.[^o10]

In 2001, she was named a fellow of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) for her "foundational and highly influential contributions to cryptographic complexity theory, authorization and trust management, massive-data-stream computation, and algorithmic mechanism design."[^o11] In 2012, she was named a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)[^o12] and chosen by the Connecticut Technology Council as a Woman of Innovation. In 2013, she was elected as a member[^o13] of the [Connecticut Academy of Science and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Academy_of_Science_and_Engineering). In 2024, she was named a fellow of the [Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Electrical_and_Electronics_Engineers)[^o14] and a fellow of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043).[^o15] In May 2020, she won the Test-of-Time Award[^o16] from the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy for her 1996 paper (with [Matt Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Blaze) and Jack Lacy) entitled "Decentralized Trust Management."

Professor Feigenbaum has served the computing community in many roles over the course of her career. Currently, she serves as an Editorial-Board Member for the [Communications of the ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_of_the_ACM)[^o17] and a Steering-Committee Member for the ACM Symposium on Computer Science and Law.[^o18]
