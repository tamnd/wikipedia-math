# Eugène Cosserat

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eugène Cosserat"}

**Eugène-Maurice-Pierre Cosserat** (4 March 1866 – 31 May 1931) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

Born in [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens), he studied at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) from 1883 to 1888.

He was on Science faculty of [Toulouse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse_University) from 1889 and director of its observatory from 1908, a position he held for the rest of his life. He was elected to the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in 1919.

His studies included the [rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rings_of_Saturn) and [satellites of Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellites_of_Saturn), [comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comets) and [double stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_stars), but is best remembered for work with his engineer brother [François](wikipedia://en/57011943) on [surface mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary_surface), particularly problems of [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29).

Their work on elasticity described an extension of the classical theory to include a description of micro-rotation of material points in addition to the classical description of deformation. Their work lacked key elements and lay dormant until the 1960s at which time it was reopened by several authors. It has become known as the theory of [micropolar elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropolar_elasticity) and has remained an active research area ever since.[^o3]
