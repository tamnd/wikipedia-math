# Richtmyer–Meshkov instability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richtmyer–Meshkov instability"}

The **Richtmyer–Meshkov instability (RMI)** occurs when two fluids of different density are impulsively accelerated. Normally this is by the passage of a [shock wave](wikipedia://en/146253). The development of the [instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instability) begins with small amplitude perturbations which initially grow linearly with time. This is followed by a nonlinear regime with bubbles appearing in the case of a light fluid penetrating a heavy fluid, and with spikes appearing in the case of a heavy fluid penetrating a light fluid. A chaotic regime is reached eventually and the two fluids mix.

:::

This instability can be considered the impulsive-acceleration limit of the [Rayleigh–Taylor instability](wikipedia://en/1749848).[^o2]

## History {#History}

[R. D. Richtmyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_D._Richtmyer) provided a theoretical prediction,[^o4] and E. E. Meshkov (Евгений Евграфович Мешков)<sup>(ru)</sup> provided experimental verification.[^o5] Materials in the cores of stars, like Cobalt-56 from [Supernova 1987A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SN_1987A) were observed earlier than expected. This was evidence of mixing due to Richtmyer–Meshkov and [Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities](wikipedia://en/1749848). [^o6]

## In magnetohydrodynamics {#In_magnetohydrodynamics}

Here are the [dispersion relations](wikipedia://en/853778) for ideal [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310) (MHD):

$$(\omega^2 - 2k_\parallel ^2 / \beta )(\omega ^4 - (2/ \beta + 1)k^2 \omega^2 +2k_\parallel^2k^2 / \beta )=0$$

For Hall MHD:

$${\displaystyle (\omega ^{2}-2k_{\parallel }^{2}/\beta )(\omega ^{4}-(2/\beta +1)k^{2}\omega ^{2}+2k_{\parallel }^{2}k^{2}/\beta )-2d_s^2 k_\parallel^2 k^2 \omega^2(\omega^2 - k^2)/ \beta =0}$$

For QMHD:

$${\displaystyle {\displaystyle ((1+2/ \beta c^2)\omega ^{2}-2k_{\parallel }^{2}/\beta )((1+2/ \beta c^2)\omega ^{4}-(2/\beta +1)k^{2}\omega ^{2}+2k_{\parallel }^{2}k^{2}/\beta )-2d_{s}^{2}k_{\parallel }^{2}k^{2}\omega ^{2}(\omega ^{2}-k^{2})/\beta =0}}$$

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

During the implosion of an [inertial confinement fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_confinement_fusion) target, the hot shell material surrounding the cold [D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuterium)–[T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritium) fuel layer is shock-accelerated. This instability is also seen in [magnetized target fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetized_target_fusion) (MTF).[^o10] Mixing of the shell material and fuel is not desired and efforts are made to minimize any tiny imperfections or irregularities which will be magnified by RMI.

Supersonic combustion in a [scramjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramjet) may benefit from RMI as the fuel-oxidants interface is enhanced by the breakup of the fuel into finer droplets. Also in studies of [deflagration to detonation transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflagration_to_detonation_transition) (DDT) processes show that RMI-induced flame acceleration can result in detonation.

:::
