# Felix Browder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Felix Browder"}

**Felix Earl Browder** (; July 31, 1927 – December 10, 2016) was an American mathematician known for his work in [nonlinear functional analysis](wikipedia://en/8546912). He received the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in 1999 and was President of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) until 2000. His two younger brothers also became notable mathematicians, [William Browder](wikipedia://en/6646431) (an [algebraic topologist](wikipedia://en/38801)) and [Andrew Browder](wikipedia://en/53382320)[^o5] (a specialist in [function algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_algebra)).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Felix Earl Browder was born in 1927 in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), while his American father [Earl Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Browder), born in [Wichita, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita,_Kansas), was living and working there. He had gone to the Soviet Union in 1927. His mother was Raisa Berkman, a Russian Jewish woman from St. Petersburg whom Browder met and married while living in the Soviet Union.[^o7] As a child, Felix Browder moved with his family to the United States, where his father Earl Browder for a time was head of the American Communist Party and ran for US president in 1936 and 1940.[^o7] A 1999 book by [Alexander Vassiliev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Vassiliev), published after the fall of the Soviet Union, said that Earl Browder was recruited in the 1940s as a spy for the Soviet Union.[^o8]

Felix Browder was a child prodigy in mathematics; he entered [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) at age 16 in 1944 and graduated in 1946 with his first degree in mathematics. In 1946, at MIT he achieved the rank of a Putnam Fellow in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition).[^o9] In 1948, at age 20, he received his doctorate from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).

## Career {#Career}

Browder had an academic career, encountering difficulty in the 1950s in getting work during the [McCarthy era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthy_era) because of his father's communist activities.

Browder headed the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago)'s mathematics department for 12 years. He also held posts at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University), Brandeis and [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale). In 1986 he became the first vice president for research at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).[^o11]

Browder received the 1999 [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science).[^o12][^o11] He also served as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1999 to 2000.

In his outgoing presidential address at the American Mathematical Society, Browder noted, "ideas and techniques from one set of mathematical sources imping\[ing\] fruitfully on the same thing from another set of mathematical sources" as illustration of [bisociation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisociation) (a term from [Arthur Koestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Koestler)). He also recounted the moves against mathematics in France by [Claude Allègre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Allègre) as problematic.[^o13]

Browder was known for his personal library, which contained some thirty-five thousand books. "The library has a number of different categories," he said. "There is mathematics, physics and science as well as philosophy, literature and history, with a certain number of volumes of contemporary political science and economics. It is a polymath library. I am interested in everything and my library reflects all my interests."[^o14]

## Family {#Family}

Browder married Eva Tislowitz in 1949, born to Jewish parents. Their children included [Thomas Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Browder),[^o16] a physicist specializing in the experimental study of subatomic particles, and [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder), who became CEO of [Hermitage Capital Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Capital_Management) and resides in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).

The late Dr. Browder had two younger brothers who were also research mathematicians, [William](wikipedia://en/6646431) (an [algebraic topologist](wikipedia://en/38801)) and [Andrew Browder](wikipedia://en/53382320)[^o5] (a specialist in [function algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_algebra)). Browder died in 2016 at home in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), aged 89.[^o17] "In addition to his brothers, survivors include the above mentioned two sons, Thomas Browder of Honolulu and Bill Browder of London; and five grandchildren."
