# Truchet tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truchet tiling"}

In [information visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_visualization) and [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design), **Truchet tiles** are square tiles decorated with patterns that are not [rotationally symmetric](wikipedia://en/701096). When placed in a [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) of the plane, they can form varied patterns, and the orientation of each tile can be used to visualize information associated with the tile's position within the tiling.[^o2]

:::

Truchet tiles were first described in a 1704 memoir by [Sébastien Truchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sébastien_Truchet) entitled "Mémoire sur les combinaisons", and were popularized in 1987 by [Cyril Stanley Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Stanley_Smith).[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

### Contrasting triangles {#Contrasting_triangles}

The tiles originally studied by Truchet are split along the diagonal into two triangles of contrasting colors. The tiles have four possible orientations.

### Quarter-circles {#Quarter-circles}

A second common form of the Truchet tiles, from (Smith 1987), decorates each tile with two quarter-circles connecting the midpoints of adjacent sides. Each such tile has two possible orientations.

We have such a tiling:

*Image: Truchet tiling.svg.*

This type of tile has also been used in abstract strategy games [Trax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trax_%28board_game%29) and the [Black Path Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Path_Game), prior to Smith's work.[^o2]

### Diagonal {#Diagonal}

A [labyrinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labyrinth) can be generated by tiles in the form of a white square with a black diagonal. As with the quarter-circle tiles, each such tile has two orientations.[^o10] The connectivity of the resulting labyrinth can be analyzed mathematically using [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805) as [bond percolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_percolation) at the critical point of a diagonally-oriented grid. [Nick Montfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Montfort) considers the single line of [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64) [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC) required to generate such patterns – `10 PRINT CHR$(205.5+RND(1)); : GOTO 10` – to be "a [concrete poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete_poetry), a found poem".[^o10]
