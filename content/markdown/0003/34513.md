# 0

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="0"}

**0** (**zero**, ) is a [number](wikipedia://en/21690) representing an empty [quantity](wikipedia://en/691277). Adding (or subtracting) 0 to any number leaves that number unchanged; in mathematical terminology, 0 is the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225) of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers), [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), and [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers), as well as other [algebraic structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_structures). Multiplying any number by 0 results in 0, and consequently [dividing by 0](wikipedia://en/185663) is generally considered [to be undefined](wikipedia://en/9920876) in [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118). Additionally, any number raised to the power of 0 is equal to 1, with exception of [0 to the power of 0](wikipedia://en/32130416) which is sometimes undefined.

:::

As a [numerical digit](wikipedia://en/280582), 0 plays a crucial role in [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) notation: it indicates that the [power of ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_ten) corresponding to the place containing a 0 does not contribute to the total. For example, "205" in decimal means two hundreds, no tens, and five ones. The same principle applies in [place-value notations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place-value_notation) that uses a base other than ten, such as [binary](wikipedia://en/238686) and [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263). The modern use of 0 in this manner derives from [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052) that was transmitted to Europe via [medieval Islamic mathematicians](wikipedia://en/3304216) and popularized by [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949). It was independently used by the [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_civilization).

Common [names for the number 0 in English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_the_number_0_in_English) include *zero*, *nought*, *naught* (), and *nil*. In contexts where at least one adjacent digit distinguishes it from the [letter O](wikipedia://en/22314), the number is sometimes pronounced as *oh* or *o* (). Informal or [slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slang) terms for 0 include *zilch* and *zip*. Historically, *ought*, *aught* (), and *cipher* have also been used.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *zero* came into the English language via [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) zéro from the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language) zero, a contraction of the [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language) zevero form of Italian zefiro, itself borrowed from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) ar or ar.[^o4]

In pre-Islamic time the word ar (Arabic صفر) had the meaning "empty". ar evolved to mean zero when it was used to translate sa (शून्य) from India.[^o5] The earliest known use of *zero* as a [loanword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loanword) in [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_the_number_0_in_English) was 1598.[^o6]

The Italian mathematician [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) (c. 1170), who grew up in North Africa and is credited with introducing the decimal system to Europe, used the term zephyrum. This became zefiro in Italian, and was then contracted to zero via the Venetian form zevero. The Italian word it was already in existence (meaning "west wind" from Latin and Greek [Zephyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zephyrus)) and may have influenced the spelling when transcribing Arabic ar.[^o7]

### Modern usage {#Modern_usage}

Depending on the context, there may be different words used for the number zero, or the concept of zero. For the simple notion of lacking, the words "[nothing](wikipedia://en/72197)" ([although this is not accurate](wikipedia://en/9566)) and "none" are often used. The English words ["nought" or "naught"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Names_for_the_number_0_in_English#"Nought"_and_"naught"_versus_"ought"_and_"aught"), "nil", and null are also synonymous.[^o9][^o10]

It is often called "oh" in the context of reading out a string of digits, such as [telephone numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_number), [street addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_address), [credit card numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card_number), [military time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_time), or years. For example, the [area code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_code) 201 may be pronounced "two oh one", and the year 1907 is often pronounced "nineteen oh seven". The presence of other digits, indicating that the string contains only numbers, avoids confusion with the letter O. For this reason, systems that include strings with both letters and numbers (such as [postcodes in the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcodes_in_the_United_Kingdom)) may exclude the use of the letter O.[^o11]

Slang words for zero include "zip", "zilch", "nada", and "scratch".[^o12] In the context of sports, "nil" is sometimes used, especially in [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English). Several sports have specific words for a score of zero, such as "[love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_%28tennis%29)" in [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis) – possibly from French l'œuf, "the egg" – and "[duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_%28cricket%29)" in [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_%28sport%29), a shortening of "duck's egg". "Goose egg" is another general slang term used for zero.[^o12]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

The concept of zero plays multiple roles in mathematics: as a digit, it is an important part of positional notation for representing numbers, while it also plays an important role as a number in its own right in many algebraic settings.

### As a digit {#As_a_digit}

In positional number systems (such as the usual [decimal notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_notation) for representing numbers), the digit 0 plays the role of a placeholder, indicating that certain powers of the base do not contribute. For example, the decimal number 205 is the sum of two hundreds and five ones, with the 0 digit indicating that no tens are added. The digit plays the same role in [decimal fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_fractions) and in the [decimal representation](wikipedia://en/3293594) of other real numbers (indicating whether any tenths, hundredths, thousandths, etc., are present) and in bases other than 10 (for example, in binary, where it indicates which powers of 2 are omitted).[^n1]

### Elementary algebra {#Elementary_algebra}

*Image: Number line with numbers -3 to 3.svg.* A [number line](wikipedia://en/287188) from −3 to 3, with 0 in the middle

The number 0 is the smallest nonnegative integer, and the largest nonpositive integer. The [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following 0 is 1 and no natural number precedes 0. The number 0 [may or may not be considered a natural number](wikipedia://en/21474),[^o17][^n2] but it is an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), and hence a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) and a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438).[^n3] Zero is [even](wikipedia://en/13259237)[^o20] (that is, a multiple of 2), and is also an [integer multiple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_multiple) of any other integer.[^o21] It is neither a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) nor a [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289).[^o22]

The number 0 is regarded as neither positive nor negative,[^o23] and is usually displayed as the origin of a [number line](wikipedia://en/287188).[^o24] When the real numbers are extended to form the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), 0 becomes the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) of the complex plane.[^n4]

*Image: AdditionZero.svg.* 5+0=5 illustrated with collections of dots.

The following are some basic rules for dealing with the number 0. These rules apply for any real or complex number *x*, unless otherwise stated.

- [Addition](wikipedia://en/61338): *x* + 0 = 0 + *x* = *x*. That is, 0 is an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) (or neutral element) with respect to addition.[^n5]
- [Subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141): *x* − 0 = *x* and 0 − *x* = −*x*.[^o27]
- [Multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845): *x* · 0 = 0 · *x* = 0.[^n6] The [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) also holds: If *x* · y = 0 then x=0 or y=0.
- [Division](wikipedia://en/53696): $\tfrac{0}{x}$ = 0, for nonzero *x*. But [$\tfrac{x}{0}$](wikipedia://en/185663) is [undefined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defined_and_undefined), because 0 has no [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940) (no real number multiplied by 0 produces 1), a consequence of the previous rule.[^n7]
- [Exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491): *x*<sup>0</sup> = $\tfrac{x}{x}$ = 1, except that the case *x* = 0 is considered undefined in some contexts. For all positive real *x*, 0<sup>*x*</sup> = 0.[^n8]

The expression $\tfrac{0}{0}$, which may be obtained in an attempt to determine the limit of an expression of the form $\tfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ as a result of applying the [lim](wikipedia://en/285759) operator independently to both operands of the fraction, is a so-called "[indeterminate form](wikipedia://en/366723)". That does not mean that the limit sought is necessarily undefined; rather, it means that the limit of $\tfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$, if it exists, must be found by another method, such as [l'Hôpital's rule](wikipedia://en/18531).[^o31]

The sum of 0 numbers (the *[empty sum](wikipedia://en/209748)*) is 0, and the product of 0 numbers (the *[empty product](wikipedia://en/181289)*) is 1. The [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) 0! evaluates to 1, as a special case of the empty product.[^o32]

### Other uses in mathematics {#Other_uses_in_mathematics}

*Image: Nullset.svg.* The empty set has zero elements

The role of 0 as the smallest counting number can be generalized or extended in various ways. In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), 0 is the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) (notated as "{ }", "$\emptyset$", or "∅"): if one does not have any apples, then one has 0 apples. In fact, in certain axiomatic developments of mathematics from set theory, 0 is *[defined](wikipedia://en/7964)* to be the empty set.[^n9] When this is done, the empty set is the [von Neumann cardinal assignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_cardinal_assignment) for a set with no elements, which is the empty set.[^o36]

Also in set theory, 0 is the lowest [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932), corresponding to the empty set viewed as a [well-ordered set](wikipedia://en/33456). In [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924) (and especially its subfield [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory)), 0 may denote the [least element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_element) of a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) or other [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572).

The role of 0 as additive identity generalizes beyond elementary algebra. In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), 0 is commonly used to denote a [zero element](wikipedia://en/3476379), which is the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) for addition (if defined on the structure under consideration) and an [absorbing element](wikipedia://en/14206224) for multiplication (if defined). Examples include identity elements of [additive groups](wikipedia://en/332229) and [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370). Another example is the **zero function** (or **zero map**) on a domain $D$. This is the [constant function](wikipedia://en/320861) with 0 as its only possible output value, that is, it is the function $f$ defined by $f(x) = 0$ for all $x$ in $D$. As a function from the real numbers to the real numbers, the zero function is the only function that is both [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_function) and [odd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_function).

The number 0 is also used in several other ways within various branches of mathematics:

- A *[zero of a function](wikipedia://en/264210)* *f* is a point *x* in the domain of the function such that $f(x) = 0$.
- In [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154), 0 may be used to denote the [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) false.
- In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), 0 is the smallest allowed value for the probability of any event.[^n10]
- [Category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) introduces the idea of a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object), often denoted 0, and the related concept of [zero morphisms](wikipedia://en/320638), which generalize the zero function.[^o38]

## History {#History}

### Ancient Near East {#Ancient_Near_East}

Ancient [Egyptian numerals](wikipedia://en/1149201) were of [base 10](wikipedia://en/8214).[^o42] They used [hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieroglyphs) for the digits and were not [positional](wikipedia://en/437052). In [one papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus_Boulaq_18) written around 1770 BC, a scribe recorded daily incomes and expenditures for the [pharaoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharaoh)'s court, using the *[nfr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefer)* hieroglyph to indicate cases where the amount of a foodstuff received was exactly equal to the amount disbursed. Egyptologist [Alan Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gardiner) suggested that the *nfr* hieroglyph was being used as a symbol for zero.[^o43] The same symbol was also used to indicate the base level in drawings of tombs and pyramids, and distances were measured relative to the base line as being above or below this line.[^o44]

By the middle of the 2nd millennium BC, [Babylonian mathematics](wikipedia://en/3861353) had a sophisticated [base 60](wikipedia://en/135316) positional numeral system, but a positional value of zero was indicated by a *space* between [numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_numerals). In a tablet unearthed at [Kish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kish_%28Sumer%29) (dating to as early as 700 BC), the scribe Bêl-bân-aplu used three hooks as a placeholder.[^n11] By 300 BC, a punctuation symbol (two slanted wedges) was repurposed as a placeholder.[^o46][^o47] Significantly, however, these placeholder signs were not considered a numerical value, they were never used alone “Therefore, they cannot be interpreted as representations of the concept or the number zero.”[^o48] They were also not written at the end of a number (so 1 and 60 and 60×60 were all written as

### Pre-Columbian Americas {#Pre-Columbian_Americas}

*Image: Cero maya.svg.* Maya numeral zero

The [Mesoamerican Long Count calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_Long_Count_calendar) developed in south-central Mexico and Central America required the use of zero as a placeholder within its [vigesimal](wikipedia://en/209196) (base-20) positional numeral system. Many different glyphs, including the partial [quatrefoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quatrefoil) were used as a zero symbol for these Long Count dates, the earliest of which (on Stela 2 at Chiapa de Corzo, [Chiapas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiapas)) has a date of 36 BC.[^o51]

Since the eight earliest Long Count dates appear outside the Maya homeland,[^n13] it is generally believed that the use of zero in the Americas predated the Maya and was possibly the invention of the [Olmecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmec).[^o53] Many of the earliest Long Count dates were found within the Olmec heartland, although the Olmec civilization ended by the 4th century BC,[^o54] several centuries before the earliest known Long Count dates.[^o55]

Although zero became an integral part of [Maya numerals](wikipedia://en/19823), with a different, empty [tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoise)-like "[shell shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastron)" used for many depictions of the "zero" numeral, it is assumed not to have influenced [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World) numeral systems.[^o56]

[Quipu](wikipedia://en/261479), a knotted cord device, used in the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire) and its predecessor societies in the [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) region to record accounting and other digital data, is encoded in a [base ten](wikipedia://en/8214) positional system. Zero is represented by the absence of a knot in the appropriate position.[^o57]

### Classical antiquity {#Classical_antiquity}

The earliest confidently cited exemplar of the Greek use of the [Hellenistic zero](wikipedia://en/166697#Zero) appears in Hipparchus in 140 CE.

The [archaic Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_Greece) had no symbol for zero (μηδέν, pronounced *mēdén*), and did not use a digit placeholder for it.[^o59] According to mathematician [Charles Seife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Seife), after the Babylonian placeholder zero shows up sometime shortly after 500 BC, Greek astronomers began to use the lowercase Greek letter *ό* (*όμικρον*: [omicron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omicron)) as a placeholder or representation of ground-level/null degree value.[^o60] However, after using the Babylonian placeholder zero for astronomical calculations they would typically convert the numbers back into [Greek numerals](wikipedia://en/166697). As with the rejection of infinitesimals by Pythagoras, Greeks appear to maintain to a philosophical opposition to using zero as a number.[^n14] "The whole of the Greek universe rested on this pillar: There is no void."[^n15] Nieder dates the appearance of zero in Greek astronomical texts after 400 BC and mathematician Robert Kaplan further specifies that it must have been after the [conquests of Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Alexander_the_Great).[^o63][^n16]

Greeks seemed unsure about the status of zero as a number. Some of them asked themselves, "How can not being be?", leading to philosophical and, by the [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) period, religious arguments about the nature and existence of zero and the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum). The [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/34535) of [Zeno of Elea](wikipedia://en/143331) depend in large part on the uncertain interpretation of zero.[^o65]

*Image: P. Lund, Inv. 35a.jpg.* Example of the early Greek symbol for zero (lower right corner) from a 2nd-century papyrus

By AD 150, [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979), influenced by [Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600) and the [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia), was using a symbol for zero (—)[^o66][^o67] in his work on [mathematical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_astronomy) called the *Syntaxis Mathematica*, also known as the *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)*.[^o68] This [Hellenistic zero](wikipedia://en/166697#Hellenistic_zero) was perhaps the earliest documented use of a numeral representing zero in the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World).[^o69] Ptolemy used it many times in his *Almagest* (VI.8) for the magnitude of [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_eclipse) and [lunar eclipses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_eclipse). It represented the value of both [digits](wikipedia://en/1555658) and [minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_and_second_of_arc) of immersion at first and last contact. Digits varied continuously from 0 to 12 to 0 as the Moon passed over the Sun (a triangular pulse), where twelve digits was the [angular diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_diameter) of the Sun. Minutes of immersion was tabulated from 00 to 3120 to 00, where 00 used the symbol as a placeholder in two positions of his [sexagesimal](wikipedia://en/135316) positional numeral system, while the combination meant a zero angle. Minutes of immersion was also a continuous function $\tfrac{1}{12}$ 3120 $\sqrt{d(24-d)}$ (a triangular pulse with [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_lens) sides), where d was the digit function and 3120 was the sum of the radii of the Sun's and Moon's discs.[^o70] Ptolemy's symbol was a placeholder as well as a number used by two continuous mathematical functions, one within another, so it meant zero, not none. Over time, Ptolemy's zero tended to increase in size and lose the [overline](wikipedia://en/4780165), sometimes depicted as a large elongated 0-like omicron "Ο" or as omicron with overline "ō" instead of a dot with overline.[^o71]

The earliest use of zero in the calculation of the [Julian Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus) occurred before AD311, at the first entry in a table of [epacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epact) as preserved in an [Ethiopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia) document for the years 311 to 369, using a [Geʽez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geʽez) word for "none" (English translation is "0" elsewhere) alongside Geʽez numerals (based on Greek numerals), which was translated from an equivalent table published by the [Church of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Alexandria) in [Medieval Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Greek).[^o72] This use was repeated in 525 in an equivalent table, that was translated via the Latin nulla ("none") by [Dionysius Exiguus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysius_Exiguus), alongside [Roman numerals](wikipedia://en/25657#Zero).[^o73] When division produced zero as a remainder, *nihil*, meaning "nothing", was used. These medieval zeros were used by all future medieval [calculators of Easter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computus). The initial "N" was used as a zero symbol in a table of Roman numerals by [Bede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bede)—or his colleagues—around AD 725.[^o74]

### China {#China}

*Image: Zero in Rod Calculus.png.* This is a depiction of zero expressed in Chinese [counting rods](wikipedia://en/26661047), based on the example provided by *A History of Mathematics*. An empty space is used to represent zero.[^o76]

The *[Sūnzĭ Suànjīng](wikipedia://en/30291690)*, of unknown date but estimated to be dated from the 1st to 5th centuries AD, describe how the 4th century BC Chinese [counting rods](wikipedia://en/26661047) system enabled one to perform [positional](wikipedia://en/437052) decimal calculations.[^o77][^o78] As noted in the *[Xiahou Yang Suanjing](wikipedia://en/52488778)* (425–468 AD), to multiply or divide a number by 10, 100, 1000, or 10000, all one needs to do, with rods on the counting board, is to move them forwards, or back, by 1, 2, 3, or 4 places. The rods gave the decimal representation of a number, with an empty space denoting zero.[^o76][^o80] A circa 190 AD, manual, the "Supplementary Notes on the Art of Figures", by [Xu Yue](wikipedia://en/52701530), also outlines the techniques to add, subtract, multiply, and divide numbers, containing zero values in a decimal power, on *counting devices*, that include counting rods, and abacus.[^o81][^o82] Chinese authors had been familiar with the idea of negative numbers, and decimal fractions, by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (2nd century AD), as seen in *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)*.[^o83] [Qín Jiǔsháo](wikipedia://en/612837)'s 1247 *[Mathematical Treatise in Nine Sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Treatise_in_Nine_Sections)* is the oldest surviving Chinese mathematical text using a round symbol '〇' for zero.[^o84] The origin of this symbol is unknown; it may have been produced by modifying a square symbol.[^o85] Zero was not treated as a number at that time, but as a "vacant position".[^o86]

#### Chinese Epigraphy {#Chinese_Epigraphy}

A variety of [Chinese characters](wikipedia://en/5781) have been used, through history, to represent zero: 空, 零, 洞, 〇.

### India {#India}

[Pingala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingala) (c. 3rd or 2nd century BC),[^o89] a [Sanskrit prosody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_prosody) scholar,[^o90] used [binary sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system), in the form of short and long syllables (the latter equal in length to two short syllables), to identify the possible valid Sanskrit [meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_%28poetry%29#Disyllables), a notation similar to [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code).[^o91] Pingala used the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *[śūnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śūnyatā)* explicitly to refer to zero.[^o89]

*Image: Bakhshali manuscript zero detail.jpg.* Bakhshali manuscript, with the numeral "zero" represented by a black dot; its date is uncertain.[^o92]

A decimal place value [grapheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapheme) for zero was developed in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent).[^o93]

The *[Lokavibhāga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokavibhaga)*, a [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) text on [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) surviving in a medieval Sanskrit translation of the [Prakrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakrit) original, which is internally dated to AD 458 ([Saka era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka_era) 380), uses a decimal [place-value system](wikipedia://en/437052), including a zero. In this text, *[śūnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śūnyatā)* ("void, empty") is also used to refer to zero.[^n17]

The *[Aryabhatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhatiya)* (c. 499), states *sthānāt sthānaṁ daśaguṇaṁ syāt* "from place to place each is ten times the preceding".[^o95][^o96][^o97]

Rules governing the use of zero appeared in [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206)'s *[Brahmasputha Siddhanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta)* (7th century), which states the sum of zero with itself as zero, and incorrectly describes [division by zero](wikipedia://en/185663) in the following way:[^o98][^n18]

> A positive or negative number when divided by zero is a fraction with the zero as denominator. Zero divided by a negative or positive number is either zero or is expressed as a fraction with zero as numerator and the finite quantity as denominator. Zero divided by zero is zero.

[Bhāskara II](wikipedia://en/480509)'s 12th century treatise *[Līlāvatī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Līlāvatī)* instead proposed that division by zero results in an infinite quantity,[^o100]

> A quantity divided by zero becomes a fraction the denominator of which is zero. This fraction is termed an infinite quantity. In this quantity consisting of that which has zero for its divisor, there is no alteration, though many may be inserted or extracted; as no change takes place in the infinite and immutable God when worlds are created or destroyed, though numerous orders of beings are absorbed or put forth.

#### Early Asian Epigraphy {#Early_Asian_Epigraphy}

There are numerous copper plate inscriptions with the same small o in them, some of them possibly dated to the 6th century, but their date or authenticity may be open to doubt.[^n11]

A stone tablet found in the ruins of a temple near Sambor on the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong), [Kratié Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kratié_Province), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia), includes the inscription of "605" in [Khmer numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_numerals) (a set of numeral glyphs for the [Hindu–Arabic numeral system](wikipedia://en/3393371)). The number is the year of the inscription in the [Saka era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka_era), corresponding to a date of AD 683.[^o103]

The first known use of special [glyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) for the decimal digits that includes the indubitable appearance of a symbol for the digit zero, a small circle, appears on a stone inscription found at the [Chaturbhuj Temple, Gwalior](wikipedia://en/55935179), in India, dated AD 876.[^o104][^n19]

A symbol for zero, a black dot, is used throughout the [Bakhshali manuscript](wikipedia://en/4305817), a practical manual on arithmetic for merchants. The [Bodleian Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodleian_Library) reported [radiocarbon dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating) results for six folio from the manuscript, indicating that they came from different centuries, but date the manuscript to AD 799 – 1102.[^o92]

### Middle Ages {#Middle_Ages}

#### Transmission to Islamic culture {#Transmission_to_Islamic_culture}

The [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)-language inheritance of science was largely [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece),[^o108] followed by Hindu influences.[^o109] In 773, at [Al-Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mansur)'s behest, translations were made of many ancient treatises including Greek, Roman, Indian, and others.

In AD 813, astronomical tables were prepared by a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) mathematician, [Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muḥammad_ibn_Mūsā_al-Khwārizmī), using Hindu numerals;[^o109] and about 825, he published a book synthesizing Greek and Hindu knowledge and also contained his own contribution to mathematics including an explanation of the use of zero.[^o110] This book was later translated into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) in the 12th century under the title *Algoritmi de numero Indorum*. This title means "al-Khwarizmi on the Numerals of the Indians". The word "Algoritmi" was the translator's Latinization of Al-Khwarizmi's name, and the word "[Algorithm](wikipedia://en/775)" or "[Algorism](wikipedia://en/417534)" started to acquire a meaning of any arithmetic based on decimals.[^o109]

[Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Khwarizmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Ahmad_al-Khwarizmi), in 976, stated that if no number appears in the place of tens in a calculation, a little circle should be used "to keep the rows". This circle was called *ṣifr*.[^o111]

#### Transmission to Europe {#Transmission_to_Europe}

The [Hindu–Arabic numeral system](wikipedia://en/3393371) (base 10) reached Western Europe in the 11th century, via [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus), through Spanish [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), the [Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors), together with knowledge of [classical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_astronomy) and instruments like the [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664). [Gerbert of Aurillac](wikipedia://en/24614) is credited with reintroducing the lost teachings into Catholic Europe. For this reason, the numerals came to be known in Europe as "Arabic numerals". The Italian mathematician [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949) or Leonardo of Pisa was instrumental in bringing the system into European mathematics in 1202, stating:

> After my father's appointment by [his homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa) as state official in the customs house of [Bugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béjaïa) for the Pisan merchants who thronged to it, he took charge; and in view of its future usefulness and convenience, had me in my boyhood come to him and there wanted me to devote myself to and be instructed in the study of calculation for some days. There, following my introduction, as a consequence of marvelous instruction in the art, to the nine digits of the Hindus, the knowledge of the art very much appealed to me before all others, and for it I realized that all its aspects were studied in Egypt, Syria, Greece, Sicily, and Provence, with their varying methods; and at these places thereafter, while on business. I pursued my study in depth and learned the give-and-take of disputation. But all this even, and the [algorism](wikipedia://en/417534), as well as the art of [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275), I considered as almost a mistake in respect to the method of the [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) \[Modus Indorum\]. Therefore, embracing more stringently that method of the Hindus, and taking stricter pains in its study, while adding certain things from my own understanding and inserting also certain things from the niceties of [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s geometric art. I have striven to compose this book in its entirety as understandably as I could, dividing it into fifteen chapters. Almost everything which I have introduced I have displayed with exact proof, in order that those further seeking this knowledge, with its pre-eminent method, might be instructed, and further, in order that the [Latin people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_people) might not be discovered to be without it, as they have been up to now. If I have perchance omitted anything more or less proper or necessary, I beg indulgence, since there is no one who is blameless and utterly provident in all things. The nine Indian figures are: 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 1. With these nine figures, and with the sign 0 ... any number may be written.[^o113]

From the 13th century, manuals on calculation (adding, multiplying, extracting roots, etc.) became common in Europe where they were called algorismus after the Persian mathematician [al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khwārizmī). One popular manual was written by [Johannes de Sacrobosco](wikipedia://en/960620) in the early 1200s and was one of the earliest scientific books to be [printed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_printing), in 1488.[^o114][^o115] The practice of calculating on paper using Hindu–Arabic numerals only gradually displaced calculation by [abacus](wikipedia://en/655) and recording with [Roman numerals](wikipedia://en/25657).[^n20] In the 16th century, Hindu–Arabic numerals became the predominant numerals used in Europe.[^o114]

## Symbols and representations {#Symbols_and_representations}

*Image: Text figures 036.svg.*

*Image: Oslo airport train station, Platform 0.jpg.* Oslo airport train station, Platform 0

Today, the numerical digit 0 is usually written as a circle or ellipse. Traditionally, many print [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface) made the capital letter [O](wikipedia://en/22314) more rounded than the narrower, elliptical digit 0.[^o118] [Typewriters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typewriter) originally made no distinction in shape between O and 0; some models did not even have a separate key for the digit 0. The distinction came into prominence on modern character [displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_display_unit).[^o118]

A [slashed zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slashed_zero) ($0\!\!\!{/}$) is often used to distinguish the number from the letter (mostly in computing, navigation and in the military, for example). The digit 0 with a dot in the center seems to have originated as an option on [IBM 3270](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_3270) displays and has continued with some modern computer typefaces such as [Andalé Mono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalé_Mono), and in some airline reservation systems. One variation uses a short vertical bar instead of the dot. Some fonts designed for use with computers made the "0" character more squared at the edges, like a rectangle, and the "O" character more rounded. A further distinction is made in [falsification-hindering typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FE-Schrift) as used on [German car number plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Germany) by slitting open the digit 0 on the upper right side. In some systems either the letter O or the numeral 0, or both, are excluded from use, to avoid confusion.

## Physics {#Physics}

The value zero plays a special role for many physical quantities. For some quantities, the zero level is naturally distinguished from all other levels, whereas for others it is more or less arbitrarily chosen. For example, for an [absolute temperature](wikipedia://en/41789) (typically measured in [kelvins](wikipedia://en/19593121)), [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_zero) is the lowest possible value. ([Negative temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_temperature) can be defined for some physical systems, but negative-temperature systems are not actually colder.) This is in contrast to temperatures on the [Celsius scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius_scale), for example, where zero is arbitrarily defined to be at the [freezing point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_point) of water.[^o120][^n21] Measuring sound intensity in [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel) or [phons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phon), the zero level is arbitrarily set at a reference value—for example, at a value for the threshold of hearing.[^o122][^o123]

## Computer science {#Computer_science}

Modern computers store information in [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_code), that is, using an "alphabet" that contains only two symbols, usually chosen to be "0" and "1". Binary coding is convenient for [digital electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_electronics), where "0" and "1" can stand for the absence or presence of electrical current in a wire.[^n22] [Computer programmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_programming) typically use [high-level programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_programming_language) that are more intelligible to humans than the [binary instructions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_code) that are directly executed by the [central processing unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit). 0 plays various important roles in high-level languages. For example, a [Boolean variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_data_type) stores a value that is either *true* or *false*, and 0 is often the numerical representation of *false.*[^n23]

0 also plays a role in [array indexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Array_%28data_type%29). The most common practice throughout human history has been to start counting at one, and this is the practice in early classic programming languages such as [Fortran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and [COBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COBOL).[^o127] However, in the late 1950s [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LISP) introduced [zero-based numbering](wikipedia://en/34541) for arrays while [Algol 58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algol_58) introduced completely flexible basing for array subscripts (allowing any positive, negative, or zero integer as base for array subscripts), and most subsequent programming languages adopted one or other of these positions. For example, the elements of an array are numbered starting from 0 in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28computer_language%29), so that for an array of *n* items the sequence of array indices runs from 0 to *n*−1.[^o128] Since C arrays are accessed by the array's first element memory address plus an offset, the initial pointer plus with offset zero refers to the first element of the array.

There can be confusion between 0- and 1-based indexing; for example, Java's [JDBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JDBC) indexes parameters from 1 although [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_%28programming_language%29) itself uses 0-based indexing.[^o129]

In C, a [byte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byte) containing the value 0 serves to indicate where a [string](wikipedia://en/27701) of characters ends. Also, 0 is a standard way to refer to a [null pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_pointer) in code.[^o130]

In databases, it is possible for a field not to have a value. It is then said to have a [null value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_%28SQL%29).[^o131] For numeric fields it is not the value zero. For text fields this is not blank nor the empty string. The presence of null values leads to [three-valued logic](wikipedia://en/249904). No longer is a condition either *true* or *false*, but it can be *undetermined*. Any computation including a null value delivers a null result.[^o132]

In mathematics, there is no "positive zero" or "negative zero" distinct from zero; both −0 and +0 represent exactly the same number. However, in some computer hardware [signed number representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_number_representations), zero has two distinct representations, a positive one grouped with the positive numbers and a negative one grouped with the negatives. This kind of dual representation is known as [signed zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_zero), with the latter form sometimes called negative zero. These representations include the [signed magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_magnitude) and [ones' complement](wikipedia://en/30670886) binary integer representations (but not the [two's complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two's_complement) binary form used in most modern computers), and most [floating-point](wikipedia://en/11376) number representations (such as [IEEE 754](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_floating_point) and [IBM S/360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_hexadecimal_floating-point) floating-point formats).

An [epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoch_%28computing%29), in computing terminology, is the date and time associated with a zero timestamp. The [Unix epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix_epoch) begins the midnight before the first of January 1970.[^o133][^o134][^o135] The [Classic Mac OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Mac_OS) epoch and [Palm OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_OS) epoch begin the midnight before the first of January 1904.[^o136]

Many [APIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_programming_interface) and [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) that require applications to return an integer value as an [exit status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_status) typically use zero to indicate success and non-zero values to indicate specific [error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_code) or warning conditions.[^o137]

Programmers often use a [slashed zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slashed_zero#Usage) to avoid confusion with the letter "[O](wikipedia://en/22314)".[^o138]

## Other fields {#Other_fields}

### Biology {#Biology}

In [comparative zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology) and [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science), recognition that some animals display awareness of the concept of zero leads to the conclusion that the capability for numerical abstraction arose early in the [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) of species.[^o141]

### Dating systems {#Dating_systems}

In the [BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Christ) [calendar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_era), the year 1 BC is the first year before AD 1; there is not a [year zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_zero). By contrast, in [astronomical year numbering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_year_numbering), the year 1 BC is numbered 0, the year 2 BC is numbered −1, and so forth.[^o143]
