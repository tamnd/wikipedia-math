# August Ferdinand Möbius

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="August Ferdinand Möbius"}

**August Ferdinand Möbius** (, ; ; 17 November 1790 – 26 September 1868) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and theoretical [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Möbius was born in [Schulpforta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schulpforta), [Electorate of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony), and was descended on his mother's side from religious reformer [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther).[^o5] He was home-schooled until he was 13, when he attended the college in Schulpforta in 1803, and studied there, graduating in 1809. He then enrolled at the University of Leipzig, where he studied astronomy under the mathematician and astronomer [Karl Mollweide](wikipedia://en/7454758).[^o6] In 1813, he began to study astronomy under mathematician [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), while Gauss was the director of the [Göttingen Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Observatory). From there, he went to study with Carl Gauss's instructor, [Johann Pfaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Pfaff), at the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), where he completed his doctoral thesis *The occultation of fixed stars* in 1815.[^o6] In 1816, he was appointed as Extraordinary Professor to the "chair of astronomy and higher mechanics" at the University of Leipzig.[^o6] Möbius died in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) in 1868 at the age of 77.

His son [Theodor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Möbius) was a noted [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He is best known for his discovery of the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817), a [non-orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-orientable) two-dimensional surface with only one side when [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). It was independently discovered by [Johann Benedict Listing](wikipedia://en/1525914) a few months earlier.[^o6] The [Möbius configuration](wikipedia://en/19616038), formed by two mutually inscribed tetrahedra, is also named after him. Möbius was the first to introduce [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) into [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849). He is recognized for the introduction of the [barycentric coordinate system](wikipedia://en/762954).[^o8] Before 1853 and [Schläfli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli)'s discovery of the [4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-polytopes), Möbius (with [Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) and [Grassmann](wikipedia://en/99242)) was one of only three other people who had also conceived of the possibility of geometry in more than three dimensions.[^o9]

Many mathematical concepts are named after him, including the [Möbius plane](wikipedia://en/38245519), the [Möbius transformations](wikipedia://en/314493), important in projective geometry, and the [Möbius transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_transform) of number theory. His interest in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) led to the important [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) μ(*n*) and the [Möbius inversion formula](wikipedia://en/20963). In Euclidean geometry, he systematically developed the use of signed angles and line segments as a way of simplifying and unifying results.[^o10]
