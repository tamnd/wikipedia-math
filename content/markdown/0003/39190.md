# William Thurston

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Thurston"}

**William Paul Thurston** (October 30, 1946 – August 21, 2012) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was a pioneer in the field of [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774) and was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1982 for his contributions to the study of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

:::

Thurston was a professor of mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis), and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). He was also a director of the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

William Thurston was born in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), to Margaret Thurston (Martt), a seamstress, and Paul Thurston, an aeronautical engineer.[^o4] William Thurston suffered from congenital [strabismus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabismus) as a child, causing issues with depth perception.[^o4] His mother worked with him as a toddler to reconstruct three-dimensional images from two-dimensional ones.[^o4]

He received his bachelor's degree from [New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College_of_Florida) in 1967 as part of its inaugural class.[^o4][^o5] For his undergraduate thesis, he developed an [intuitionist](wikipedia://en/19513) foundation for topology.[^o6] Taking advantage of New College's liberal attitude towards student living arrangements, Thurston at various times lived in a tent in nearby woods or slept in academic buildings.

Following this, he received a doctorate in mathematics from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), under [Morris Hirsch](wikipedia://en/32523108), with a thesis titled *Foliations of Three-Manifolds which are Circle Bundles* in 1972.[^o4][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

After completing his Ph.D., Thurston spent a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179),[^o4][^o9] then another year at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) as an assistant professor.[^o4]

In 1974, Thurston was appointed a full professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o4][^o35] He returned to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1991 to be a professor (1991-1996) and was also director of the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) (MSRI) from 1992 to 1997.[^o4][^o37] He was on the faculty at [UC Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis) from 1996 until 2003, when he moved to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o4]

Thurston was an early adopter of computing in pure mathematics research.[^o4] He inspired [Jeffrey Weeks](wikipedia://en/1298792) to develop the [SnapPea](wikipedia://en/3025251) computing program.[^o4]

During Thurston's directorship at MSRI, the institute introduced several innovative educational programs that have since become standard for research institutes.[^o4]

His Ph.D. students include [Danny Calegari](wikipedia://en/21907908), [Richard Canary](wikipedia://en/46914909), [Benson Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776), [William Floyd](wikipedia://en/18986817), [David Gabai](wikipedia://en/6129609), [William Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Goldman_%28professor%29), [Richard Kenyon](wikipedia://en/49980888), [Steven Kerckhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Kerckhoff), [Yair Minsky](wikipedia://en/46905726), [Igor Rivin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Rivin), [Oded Schramm](wikipedia://en/9605580), [Richard Schwartz](wikipedia://en/1213887), and [Jeffrey Weeks](wikipedia://en/1298792).[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

### Foliations {#Foliations}

His early work, in the early 1970s, was mainly in [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) theory. His more significant results include:

- The proof that every [Haefliger structure](wikipedia://en/21045118) on a manifold can be integrated to a foliation (this implies, in particular, that every manifold with zero [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) admits a foliation of [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) one).
- The construction of a continuous family of smooth, codimension-one foliations on the [three-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) whose [Godbillon–Vey invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godbillon–Vey_invariant) (after [Claude Godbillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Godbillon) and Jacques Vey) take every real value. This disproved a prevalent conjecture held by topologists that the Godbillon-Vey invariant had to be an integer value.[^o13]
- With [John N. Mather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_N._Mather), he gave a proof that the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of the group of [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) of a manifold is the same whether the group is considered with its [discrete topology](wikipedia://en/56061) or its [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335).

In fact, Thurston resolved so many outstanding problems in foliation theory in such a short period of time that it led to an exodus from the field, where advisors counselled students against going into foliation theory,[^o14] because Thurston was "cleaning out the subject" (see "On Proof and Progress in Mathematics", especially section 6[^o15]).

### The geometrization conjecture {#The_geometrization_conjecture}

His later work, starting around the mid-1970s, revealed that [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) played a far more important role in the general theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) than was previously realised. Prior to Thurston, there were only a handful of known examples of [hyperbolic 3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1237700) of finite volume, such as the [Seifert–Weber space](wikipedia://en/1233773). The independent and distinct approaches of [Robert Riley](wikipedia://en/64550014) and [Troels Jørgensen](wikipedia://en/31074567) in the mid-to-late 1970s showed that such examples were less atypical than previously believed; in particular their work showed that the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) was [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1248299). This was the first example of a [hyperbolic knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot).

Inspired by their work, Thurston took a different, more explicit means of exhibiting the hyperbolic structure of the [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot) complement. He showed that the figure-eight knot complement could be [decomposed](wikipedia://en/1227329) as the union of two regular ideal hyperbolic tetrahedra whose hyperbolic structures matched up correctly and gave the hyperbolic structure on the figure-eight knot complement. By utilizing [Haken](wikipedia://en/311546)'s [normal surface](wikipedia://en/3141418) techniques, he classified the [incompressible surfaces](wikipedia://en/896174) in the knot complement. Together with his analysis of deformations of hyperbolic structures, he concluded that all but 10 [Dehn surgeries](wikipedia://en/1240699) on the figure-eight knot resulted in [irreducible](wikipedia://en/1001293), non-[Haken](wikipedia://en/236567) non-[Seifert-fibered](wikipedia://en/1631931) 3-manifolds. These were the first such examples; previously it had been believed that except for certain Seifert fiber spaces, all irreducible 3-manifolds were Haken. These examples were actually hyperbolic and motivated his next theorem.

Thurston proved that in fact most Dehn fillings on a cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold resulted in hyperbolic 3-manifolds. This is his celebrated [hyperbolic Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/4450009) theorem.

To complete the picture, Thurston proved a [hyperbolization theorem](wikipedia://en/9155837) for [Haken manifolds](wikipedia://en/236567). A particularly important corollary is that many knots and links are in fact hyperbolic. Together with his hyperbolic Dehn surgery theorem, this showed that closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds existed in great abundance.

The hyperbolization theorem for Haken manifolds has been called *Thurston's Monster Theorem,* due to the length and difficulty of the proof.[^o18] Complete proofs were not written up until almost 20 years later. The proof involves a number of deep and original insights which have linked many apparently disparate fields to [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

Thurston was next led to formulate his [geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642). This gave a conjectural picture of 3-manifolds which indicated that all 3-manifolds admitted a certain kind of geometric decomposition involving eight geometries, now called Thurston model geometries. Hyperbolic geometry is the most prevalent geometry in this picture and also the most complicated. The conjecture was proved by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) in 2002–2003.[^o19][^o20]

### Density conjecture {#Density_conjecture}

Thurston and [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608) generalized [Lipman Bers](wikipedia://en/9335972)' [density conjecture](wikipedia://en/31202113) from singly degenerate Kleinian surface groups to all [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) in the late 1970s and early 1980s.[^o23][^o24] The conjecture states that every finitely generated Kleinian group is an algebraic limit of [geometrically finite](wikipedia://en/31212031) Kleinian groups, and was independently proven by Ohshika and Namazi–Souto in 2011 and 2012 respectively.[^o23][^o24]

:::

::: {#o26 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Orbifold theorem"}

### Orbifold theorem {#Orbifold_theorem}

In his work on hyperbolic Dehn surgery, Thurston realized that [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) structures naturally arose. Such structures had been studied prior to Thurston, but his work, particularly the next theorem, would bring them to prominence. In 1981, he announced the [orbifold theorem](wikipedia://en/293504#3-dimensional_orbifolds), an extension of his geometrization theorem to the setting of 3-orbifolds.[^o27] Two teams of mathematicians around 2000 finally finished their efforts to write down a complete proof, based mostly on Thurston's lectures given in the early 1980s in Princeton. His original proof relied partly on [Richard S. Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911)'s work on the [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291).

:::

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1976, Thurston and [James Harris Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Harris_Simons) shared the [Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry](wikipedia://en/7085075).[^o4]

Thurston received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1982 for "revolutioniz\[ing\] \[the\] study of topology in 2 and 3 dimensions, showing interplay between analysis, topology, and geometry" and "contribut\[ing\] \[the\] idea that a very large class of closed [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) carry a hyperbolic structure."[^o29][^o30]

In 2005, Thurston won the first [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) [Book Prize](wikipedia://en/70444084), for *[Three-dimensional Geometry and Topology](wikipedia://en/31089296)*. The prize "recognizes an outstanding research book that makes a seminal contribution to the research literature".[^o31] He was awarded the 2012 [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) by the American Mathematical Society for seminal contribution to research. The citation described his work as having "revolutionized 3-manifold theory".[^o32]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Thurston met his first wife, Rachel Findley, as members of New College's inaugural class. They had three children: Dylan, Nathaniel, and Emily.[^o35] Dylan was a [MOSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Olympiad_Program) participant (1988–90)[^o34] and is a mathematician at [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College). Thurston had two children with his second wife, Julian Muriel Thurston: Hannah Jade and Liam.[^o35]

Thurston died on August 21, 2012, in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), of a sinus mucosal [melanoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanoma) that was diagnosed in 2011.[^o35][^o36][^o37]
