# Optical depth

*Image: Aerosol Optical Depth (haze) at Geronimo Creek Observatory, Texas (1990-2016).jpg.* Aerosol Optical Depth (AOD) at 830 nm measured with an LED sun photometer (1990–2016) at Geronimo Creek Observatory, Texas. Measurements were recorded at or near solar noon. Peaks indicate smoke, dust, and smog events.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Optical depth"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), **optical depth** or **optical thickness** is the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) of the ratio of incident to transmitted [radiant power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_power) through a material. Thus, the larger the optical depth, the smaller the fraction of transmitted radiant power through the medium. **Spectral optical depth** or **spectral optical thickness** is the natural logarithm of the ratio of incident to transmitted [spectral radiant power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_power) through a material.

:::

Optical depth is a [dimensionless quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity). Although it is a [monotonically](wikipedia://en/48260) increasing function of path length, it is not a physical distance itself, approaching zero as the path length vanishes. The use of the term "optical density" for optical depth is discouraged.[^o2]

In [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), a closely related quantity called [absorbance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorbance) (or decadic absorbance) is used instead of optical depth. Absorbance uses the [common logarithm](wikipedia://en/174482) (base 10) of the ratio of incident to transmitted radiant power. It relates to optical depth by a factor of $log_{e}(10)$.

## Mathematical definitions {#Mathematical_definitions}

### Optical depth {#Optical_depth}

The optical depth of a material, denoted by $\tau$, is defined as:[^o5]

$$\tau = \ln\!\left(\frac{\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{i}}{\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{t}}\right) = -\ln T$$

where

- $\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{i}$ is the [radiant flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_flux) received (incident) by that material;
- $\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{t}$ is the [radiant flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_flux) transmitted by that material;
- $T$ is the [transmittance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmittance) of that material.

The decadic absorbance $A$ is related to optical depth by:

$$\tau = A \ln 10 \approx 2.3026 A$$

### Spectral optical depth {#Spectral_optical_depth}

The spectral optical depth in frequency ($\tau_\nu$) or in wavelength ($\tau_\lambda$) is given by:[^o2]

$$\tau_\nu = \ln\!\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\nu}^\mathrm{i}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\nu}^\mathrm{t}}\right) = -\ln T_\nu$$

$$\tau_\lambda = \ln\!\left(\frac{\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\lambda}^\mathrm{i}}{\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\lambda}^\mathrm{t}}\right) = -\ln T_\lambda$$

where

- $\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\nu}^\mathrm{i}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\nu}^\mathrm{t}$ are the incident and transmitted spectral radiant fluxes in frequency;
- $T_\nu$ is the spectral transmittance in frequency;
- $\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\lambda}^\mathrm{i}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{e},\lambda}^\mathrm{t}$ are the incident and transmitted spectral radiant fluxes in wavelength;
- $T_\lambda$ is the spectral transmittance in wavelength.

Spectral absorbance relates to spectral optical depth via:

$$\tau_\nu = A_\nu \ln 10$$

$$\tau_\lambda = A_\lambda \ln 10$$

## Relationship with attenuation {#Relationship_with_attenuation}

### Attenuation {#Attenuation}

Optical depth quantifies the attenuation of transmitted radiant power in a medium. Attenuation can occur via absorption, scattering, reflection, and other physical processes.

According to the [Beer–Lambert law](wikipedia://en/4476), the transmitted flux decays exponentially with optical depth:

$$T = e^{-\tau}$$

The fractional attenuation ($\text{ATT}$) is given by:

$$\text{ATT} = \frac{\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{att}}{\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{i}} = 1 - e^{-\tau} + E$$

where $E = \Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{e}/\Phi_\mathrm{e}^\mathrm{i}$ is the relative emittance of the medium. For optically thin media ($\tau \ll 1$) with negligible emission ($E \ll \tau$), attenuation reduces approximately to the optical depth itself:

$$\text{ATT} \approx \tau$$

### Attenuation coefficient {#Attenuation_coefficient}

The optical depth along a path of length $l$ is related to the local [attenuation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attenuation_coefficient) $\alpha(z)$ by:

$$\tau = \int_0^l \alpha(z)\, \mathrm{d}z$$

If $\alpha$ is uniform along the path, the relation simplifies to:

$$\tau = \alpha l$$

In terms of the attenuation [cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_section_%28physics%29) $\sigma$ per particle and number density $n(z)$:

$$\tau = \int_0^l \sigma n(z)\, \mathrm{d}z = \sigma N$$

where $N = \int_0^l n(z)\,\mathrm{d}z$ is the [column density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_density) along the line of sight.

## Applications {#Applications}

### Atomic physics {#Atomic_physics}

In [atomic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_physics), the spectral optical depth of a cloud of resonant two-level atoms can be determined from quantum-mechanical dipole transitions:

$$\tau_\nu = \frac{d^2 n \nu}{2 c \hbar \varepsilon_0 A \gamma} \mathcal{L}(\nu)$$

where

- $d$ is the [transition dipole moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_dipole_moment);
- $n$ is the total number of atoms;
- $\nu$ is the frequency of the incident radiation;
- $c$ is the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light);
- $\hbar$ is the [reduced Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_Planck_constant);
- $\varepsilon_0$ is the [vacuum permittivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_permittivity);
- $A$ is the cross-sectional area of the beam;
- $\gamma$ is the [natural linewidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_linewidth) of the atomic transition;
- $\mathcal{L}(\nu)$ is the normalized spectral line profile shape.

### Atmospheric sciences {#Atmospheric_sciences}

In [atmospheric sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_sciences), optical depth often refers to a vertical path extending from Earth's surface to outer space. For a slanted line of sight at zenith angle $\theta$, the slant optical depth $\tau$ relates to vertical optical depth $\tau'$ by the [airmass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airmass) factor $m$:

$$\tau = m \tau' \approx \tau' \sec\theta$$

yielding a total transmittance of:

$$T = e^{-\tau} = e^{-m\tau'}$$

The total atmospheric optical depth encompasses contributions from [Rayleigh scattering](wikipedia://en/26176), [aerosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) optical depth (AOD), and trace gas [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_%28electromagnetic_radiation%29), routinely measured using [Sun photometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_photometer).

With altitude $z$, optical depth in an exponential atmosphere scales as:[^o14]

$$\tau(z) = k_\text{a} w_1 \rho_0 H e^{-z/H}$$

giving a sea-level vertical optical depth of:

$$\tau(0) = k_\text{a} w_1 \rho_0 H$$

where:

- $k_\text{a}$ is the absorption coefficient;
- $w_1$ is the species mixing ratio;
- $\rho_0$ is the air density at sea level;
- $H$ is the atmospheric [scale height](wikipedia://en/1994795);
- $z$ is the altitude above sea level.

For a uniform plane-parallel cloud layer, optical depth can be parameterized as:[^o14]

$$\tau = Q_\text{e} \left[\frac{9\pi L^2 H N}{16\rho_\text{l}^2}\right]^{1/3}$$

where:

- $Q_\text{e}$ is the extinction efficiency;
- $L$ is the [liquid water path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_water_path);
- $H$ is the geometrical thickness of the cloud;
- $N$ is the droplet number concentration;
- $\rho_\text{l}$ is the density of liquid water.

Thus, for a constant cloud thickness and total liquid water content, optical depth scales with droplet concentration as $\tau \propto N^{1/3}$.[^o14]

### Astronomy {#Astronomy}

In astronomy, optical depth governs radiation escape from stellar atmospheres, planetary rings, and nebulae. In stellar physics, the [photosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosphere) is defined as the atmospheric depth where $\tau \approx 2/3$, representing the mean surface from which thermal photons escape into space.

For [planetary rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_rings), optical depth measures the fraction of light blocked by ring material during stellar occultations.
