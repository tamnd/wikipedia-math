# Wacław Sierpiński

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wacław Sierpiński"}

**Wacław Franciszek Sierpiński** (; 14 March 1882 – 21 October 1969) was a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3] He was known for contributions to [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) (research on the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) and the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705)), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), theory of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). He published over 700 papers and 50 books.

:::

Three well-known [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) are named after him (the [Sierpiński triangle](wikipedia://en/29638), the [Sierpiński carpet](wikipedia://en/29635), and the [Sierpiński curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpiński_curve)), as are [Sierpiński numbers](wikipedia://en/169570) and the associated Sierpiński problem.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sierpiński was born in 1882 in Warsaw, [Congress Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland), to a doctor father Konstanty and mother Ludwika (*née* Łapińska).[^o5] His abilities in mathematics were evident from childhood. He enrolled in the Department of Mathematics and Physics at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in 1899 and graduated five years later.[^o6]

In 1903, while still at the University of Warsaw, the Department of Mathematics and Physics offered a prize for the best essay from a student on [Voronoy](wikipedia://en/3033246)'s contribution to number theory. Sierpiński was awarded a gold medal for his essay, thus laying the foundation for his first major mathematical contribution. Unwilling for his work to be published in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), he withheld it until 1907, when it was published in [Samuel Dickstein](wikipedia://en/362489)'s mathematical magazine 'Prace Matematyczno-Fizyczne' (Polish: 'The Works of Mathematics and Physics').

After his graduation in 1904, Sierpiński worked as a school teacher of mathematics and physics in Warsaw. However, when the school closed because of a strike, Sierpiński decided to go to [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków) to pursue a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate). At the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in Kraków, he attended lectures by [Stanisław Zaremba](wikipedia://en/1028345) on [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He also studied [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). In 1906, he received his doctorate and in 1908 was appointed to the [University of Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lwów).[^o7] In 1910, he became head of the Faculty of Mathematics at the university.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Sierpinski square.png.* Sierpinski square, a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913)

In 1907 Sierpiński first became interested in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) when he came across a theorem which stated that points in the plane could be specified with a single coordinate. He wrote to [Tadeusz Banachiewicz](wikipedia://en/1018238) (then at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_August_University_of_Göttingen)), asking how such a result was possible. He received the one-word reply '[Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216)'. Sierpiński began to study [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and, in 1909, he gave the first ever lecture course devoted entirely to the subject.[^o8]

Sierpiński maintained an output of research papers and books. During the years 1908 to 1914, when he taught at the University of [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów), he published three books in addition to many research papers. These books were *The Theory of Irrational Numbers* (1910), *Outline of Set Theory* (1912), and *The Theory of Numbers* (1912).

*Image: Wacław Siepiński grave.JPG.* Grave of Wacław Sierpiński

When [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) began in 1914, Sierpiński and his family were in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia). To avoid the persecution that was common for [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) foreigners, Sierpiński spent the rest of the war years in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) working with [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228). Together they began the study of [analytic sets](wikipedia://en/634261). In 1916, Sierpiński gave the first example of an [absolutely normal number](wikipedia://en/204277).[^o10]

When [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) ended in 1918, Sierpiński returned to [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwow). However shortly after taking up his appointment again in [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv) he was offered a post at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University), which he accepted. In 1919 he was promoted to a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor). He spent the rest of his life in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw).[^o11]

During the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Soviet_War) (1919–1921), Sierpiński helped break [Soviet Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR) [ciphers](wikipedia://en/5244) for the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) [General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Staff)'s [cryptologic agency.](wikipedia://en/625326)

In 1920, Sierpiński, together with [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471) and his former student [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203), founded the mathematical journal *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*.[^o3] Sierpiński edited the journal, which specialized in papers on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553).

During this period, Sierpiński worked predominantly on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), but also on [point set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_set_topology) and [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) of a [real variable](wikipedia://en/1268958). In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) he made contributions on the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) and on the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705). He proved that [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) together with the [Generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis) imply the [Axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840). He also worked on what is now known as the [Sierpiński curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpiński_curve). Sierpiński continued to collaborate with Luzin on investigations of analytic and projective sets. His work on [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) of a [real variable](wikipedia://en/1268958) includes results on [functional series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_series), [differentiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiability) of functions and [Baire's classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baire's_classification).

Sierpiński worked at the [State Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/67042302), which was incorporated into the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1952.[^o12] He retired in 1960 as [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University), but continued until 1967 to give a seminar on the [Theory of Numbers](wikipedia://en/21527) at the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences). He also continued editorial work as editor-in-chief of *[Acta Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Arithmetica)*, and as a member of the editorial board of *[Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendiconti_del_Circolo_Matematico_di_Palermo)*, *Composito Matematica*, and *[Zentralblatt für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralblatt_MATH)*.

In 1964 he was one of the signatories of the so-called [Letter of 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_of_34) to Prime Minister [Józef Cyrankiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Józef_Cyrankiewicz) regarding freedom of culture.[^o13]

Sierpiński is interred at the [Powązki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powązki_Cemetery) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland).[^o14]

## Honors received {#Honors_received}

Honorary Degrees: [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów) (1929), [St. Marks of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Marks_of_Lima) (1930), [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu) (1932), [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) (1932), [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) (1939), [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) (1939), [Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux) (1947), [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) (1948), [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław) (1948), [Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow) (1949), and [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) (1967).

For high involvement with the development of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), Sierpiński was honored with election to the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning) in 1921 and that same year was made [dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of the [faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_%28division%29) at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw). In 1928, he became vice-[chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman) of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society), and that same year was elected chairman of the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791).

He was elected to the Geographic Society of [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima,_Peru) (1931), Royal Scientific Society of [Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liège) (1934), [Bulgarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Academy_of_Sciences) (1936), National Academy of [Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima,_Peru) (1939), Royal Society of Sciences of [Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples) (1939), [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) (1947), [Germany Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Academy_of_Natural_Scientists_Leopoldina) (1950),[^o3] [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1959), [Paris Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) (1960), [Royal Dutch Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1961),[^o16] [Academy of Science of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Academies_for_Science_and_the_Arts_of_Belgium) (1961), [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (1964), [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy) (1965) and Papal Academy of Sciences (1967).

In 1946, he received the [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791#Stefan_Banach_Prize) of the Polish Mathematical Society. In 1949, Sierpiński was awarded Poland's Scientific Prize, first degree.

In 1982, the [Polish Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Post) issued a 6-[złoty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Złoty) commemorative stamp featuring Sierpiński as part of the "Polish Mathematicians" series.[^o17]

In 2014, a sculpture in the form of a tree inspired by a fractal created by Sierpiński was unveiled at the Wallenberg Square in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm) as part of an exhibition organized by the [Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs) on the 10th anniversary of Poland joining the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) and 15th anniversary of Poland joining [NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO).[^o18]

## Publications {#Publications}

Sierpiński authored 724 papers and 50 books, almost all in Polish. His book *[Cardinal and Ordinal Numbers](wikipedia://en/65058773)* was originally published in English in 1958. Two books, *Introduction to General Topology* (1934) and *General Topology* (1952) were translated into English by Canadian mathematician [Cecilia Krieger](wikipedia://en/11581652). Another book, *[Pythagorean Triangles](wikipedia://en/63230609)* (1954), was translated into English by Indian mathematician Ambikeshwar Sharma, published in 1962, and republished by Dover Books in 2003; it also has a Russian translation.[^o20] Another work of his published in English is the *Elementary Theory of Numbers* (translated by A. Hulanicki in 1964), based on his Polish *Teoria Liczb* (1914 and 1959).[^o21] Another book, named "250 Problems in Elementary Number Theory" was translated into English (1970) and Russian (1968).
