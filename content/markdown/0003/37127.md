# Australian Signals Directorate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Australian Signals Directorate"}

The **Australian Signals Directorate** (**ASD**), formerly the **Defence Signals Directorate**, is a [statutory agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_body) of the [Government of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Government) responsible for [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence), providing intelligence support to Australian military operations, conducting [cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) and ensuring [information security](wikipedia://en/15036). The ASD is a part of the larger [Australian Intelligence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Intelligence_Community), and its role within the so-called [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes) intelligence-sharing alliance is to monitor signals intelligence in South and East Asia. The [Australian Cyber Security Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Cyber_Security_Centre) (ACSC) is an agency within the ASD.

:::

The unit was established in 1947 by [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_Instrument) as the **Defence Signals Bureau** within the [Department of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defence_%28Australia%29), and underwent several name changes until its current name ASD was adopted in 2013. ASD was converted to a statutory body by the *[Intelligence Services Act 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_Services_Act_2001)*. ASD is based in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), at the Defence Department Headquarters at [Russell Offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Offices).[^o6] As of May 2026, Abigail Bradshaw is the Director-General of ASD, replacing [Rachel Noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Noble) in September 2024.

In April 2018, a proposal to empower ASD to collect intelligence on Australians was backed by former [Minister for Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Home_Affairs_%28Australia%29) [Peter Dutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Dutton), but was strongly opposed by some in Cabinet who argued it was not necessary.[^o7] Under legislation, the [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) (ASIO) and the [Australian Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Federal_Police) (AFP) are already allowed to seek assistance from ASD in conducting investigations on Australian citizens and businesses.[^o7]

## History {#History}

The Directorate has operated under a number of different names since its founding:

- 1947 – **Defence Signals Bureau** established within the Department of Defence
- 1949 – name changed to **Defence Signals Branch**
- 1964 – name changed to **Defence Signals Division**
- 1977 – name changed to **Defence Signals Directorate** on recommendation of the [Royal Commission on Intelligence and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Commission_on_Intelligence_and_Security) (Hope Commission)[^o9]
- 2013 – name changed to **Australian Signals Directorate**[^o10]

ASD commissioned an [official history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_history) in 2019, which will cover the organisation's history from its establishment to 2001.[^o11]

## Roles and responsibilities {#Roles_and_responsibilities}

The principal functions of ASD are to collect and disseminate foreign signals intelligence (SIGINT) and to provide information security products and services to the Australian Government and [Australian Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Defence_Force) (ADF), its foreign partners and militaries.[^o13]

ASD operates at least three receiving stations:

- the [Australian Defence Satellite Communications Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Defence_Satellite_Communications_Station) (ADSCS), located at Kojarena, near [Geraldton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldton), [Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australia),
- the [Shoal Bay Receiving Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Bay_Receiving_Station), located at [Shoal Bay, Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoal_Bay,_Northern_Territory),[^o14] and
- a small station on the [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_%28Keeling%29_Islands).[^o14]

ASD also maintains a workforce at [Pine Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Gap) in central Australia.[^o15]

ADSCS and Shoal Bay are part of the United States [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT) and [ECHELON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHELON) analysis network.[^o16][^o17] These stations also contribute signals intelligence for many Australian Government bodies, as well as the other UKUSA partners.

[Electronic warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_warfare) operators in the [Royal Australian Corps of Signals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Corps_of_Signals) work closely with ASD. [7 Signal Regiment (Electronic Warfare)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Signal_Regiment_%28Australia%29) at [Borneo Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_Barracks), Cabarlah, [Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland) is also associated with ASD.[^o18]

In addition, it has been reported that many Australian embassies and overseas missions also house small facilities which provide a flow of signals intelligence to ASD.[^o19]

### UKUSA Agreement (Five Eyes) {#UKUSA_Agreement_(Five_Eyes)}

Australia joined the [UKUSA Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UKUSA_Agreement) in 1948,[^o21] a multilateral agreement for cooperation in [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) between [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom), and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). The alliance is also known as the [Five Eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Eyes).[^o22] Other countries, known as "third parties", such as [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), the Philippines, and several [Nordic countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) also joined the UKUSA community.[^o30][^o23] As the Agreement was a secret treaty, its existence was not even disclosed to the [Australian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Australia) until 1973, when [Gough Whitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Whitlam) insisted on seeing it.[^o24] The existence of the UKUSA Agreement was discovered by the Australian government during the [1973 Murphy raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Murphy_raids) on the headquarters of the [Australian Security Intelligence Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Security_Intelligence_Organisation) (ASIO). After learning about the agreement, Whitlam discovered that [Pine Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Gap), a secret surveillance station close to Alice Springs, Australia, had been operated by the U.S. [Central Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence_Agency) (CIA).[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28] Pine Gap is now operated jointly by both Australia and the United States.

The existence of the Agreement was not disclosed to the public until 2005.[^o29] On 25 June 2010, for the first time, the full text of the agreement was publicly released by the United Kingdom and the United States, and can now be [viewed online](http://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukusa/).[^o30][^o31] Under the agreement, ASD's intelligence is shared with UKUSA [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence) partner agencies:

- the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) (NSA) – United States,
- the [Government Communications Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Communications_Headquarters) (GCHQ) – United Kingdom,
- the [Communications Security Establishment](wikipedia://en/231712) (CSE) – Canada,[^o32] and
- the [Government Communications Security Bureau](wikipedia://en/661849) (GCSB) – New Zealand.[^o33]

## Organisational structure {#Organisational_structure}

The Australian Signals Directorate is led by a Director-General and a Principal Deputy Director-General who oversee strategy. The ASD also comprises the [Australian Cyber Security Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Cyber_Security_Centre), a Signals Intelligence and Network Operations Group, and a Corporate and Capability Group.

### SIGINT and Network Operations Group {#SIGINT_and_Network_Operations_Group}

The Signals Intelligence and Network Operations Group is responsible for signals intelligence collection, analysis and production, and ASD's network based access and effects operations. The Group comprises an Intelligence Division and a Network Operations and Access Division responsible for foreign signals intelligence and offensive cyber operations.

### Defence SIGINT and Cyber Command {#Defence_SIGINT_and_Cyber_Command}

The Defence Signals-Intelligence (SIGINT) and Cyber Command (DSCC) was established in January 2018 by the [Chief of the Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Force_%28Australia%29) consolidating all ADF personnel within the ASD within the Joint Cyber Unit and Joint SIGINT Unit. The Commander of the DSCC is responsible to the Head of Information Warfare under the [Chief of Joint Capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Joint_Capabilities) to the [Chief of the Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Defence_Force_%28Australia%29).[^o37]
