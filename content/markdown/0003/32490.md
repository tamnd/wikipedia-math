# Vladimir Arnold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vladimir Arnold"}

Igorevich

**Vladimir Igorevich Arnold** (or **Arnol'd**; Влади́мир И́горевич Арно́льд, ; 12 June 1937 – 3 June 2010)[^o3][^o4][^o12] was a Soviet and Russian mathematician. He is best known for the [Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem](wikipedia://en/16972) regarding the [stability](wikipedia://en/5493795) of [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213), and contributed to several areas, including geometrical theory of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/990632), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), [differential-geometric](wikipedia://en/8625) approach to [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics), [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) and [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763), including posing the [ADE classification](wikipedia://en/648042) problem. In his later years he shifted his research interests, investigating [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492).

:::

His first main result was the solution of [Hilbert's thirteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336190) in 1957 when he was 19. He co-founded three new [branches of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areas_of_mathematics): [topological Galois theory](wikipedia://en/51121181) (with his student [Askold Khovanskii](wikipedia://en/43849047)), [KAM theory](wikipedia://en/16972#KAM_theory) (with [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) and [Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316)) and [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology).

Arnold was also a populariser of mathematics. Through his lectures, seminars, and as the author of several textbooks (such as *[Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics](wikipedia://en/55201705)* and *[Ordinary Differential Equations](wikipedia://en/80208166)*) and popular mathematics books, he influenced many mathematicians and physicists.[^o6] Many of his books were translated into English. His views on education were opposed to those of [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394).

A controversial and often quoted dictum of his is "[Mathematics is the part of physics where experiments are cheap](wikipedia://en/42877569#Mathematics_is_physics)".[^o12][^o7][^o8] The [Arnold principle](wikipedia://en/682348): "Discoveries are rarely attributed to the correct person" is also named after him.[^o3]

Arnold worked at the [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) from 1961 to 1986, at the [Steklov Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Mathematical_Institute) since 1986, and at the [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) since 1993.[^o9] He was one of the founders of the [Independent University of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_University_of_Moscow).[^o10]

Arnold received many major prizes, including the inaugural [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in 1982 (with [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725)), the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2001 and the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize#Mathematical_sciences) in 2008 (with [Ludwig Faddeev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Faddeev)).

## Early life {#Early_life}

*Image: Владимир Арнольд в 1963 г.jpg.* Arnold in 1963

Vladimir Igorevich Arnold was born on 12 June 1937 in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa), [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (now Odesa, Ukraine). His father was Igor Vladimirovich Arnold (1900–1948), a mathematician known for his work in [mathematical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_education) and who learned [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) from [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) in the late 1920s.[^o3] His mother was Nina Alexandrovna Arnold (1909–1986, Isakovich), a Jewish art historian.[^o12]

While a school student, Arnold once asked his father why the multiplication of two negative numbers yielded a positive number, and his father provided an answer involving the [field properties](wikipedia://en/10603#Classic_definition) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and the preservation of the [distributive property](wikipedia://en/103118). Arnold was deeply disappointed with this answer, and developed an aversion to the [axiomatic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_method) that lasted his whole life.[^o13][^o14] When Arnold was thirteen, his uncle Nikolai B. Zhitkov,[^o15] who was an engineer, told him about [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and how it could be used to understand some physical phenomena. This contributed to sparking his interest in mathematics, and he started to study the mathematics books his father had left him, which included some works by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) and [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422).[^o16]

Arnold entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1954.[^o17] Among his teachers there were [A. N. Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), [I. M. Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643), [L. S. Pontriagin](wikipedia://en/582075) and [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245).[^o18] While a student of Andrey Kolmogorov at Moscow State University and still a teenager, Arnold showed in 1957 that any [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) of several variables can be constructed with a finite number of two-variable functions, thereby solving [Hilbert's thirteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336190).[^o19] This is the [Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theorem](wikipedia://en/47321473).

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Arnold obtained his PhD in 1961, with [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) as his advisor (thesis: *On The Representation of Continuous Functions of 3 Variables By The Superpositions of Continuous Functions of 2 Variables*).

He became an academician of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) ([Russian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Science) since 1991) in 1990.[^o22] Arnold can be considered to have initiated the theory of [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology) as a distinct discipline. The [Arnold conjecture](wikipedia://en/72203783) on the number of fixed points of [Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms](wikipedia://en/312304) and [Lagrangian intersections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_intersection) was also a motivation in the development of [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856).[^o23]

Arnold worked at the Steklov Mathematical Institute in Moscow and at [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) until his death. He supervised 46 PhD students, including [Rifkat Bogdanov](wikipedia://en/13493122), [Alexander Givental](wikipedia://en/8488782), [Victor Goryunov](wikipedia://en/24354494), [Sabir Gusein-Zade](wikipedia://en/46248171), [Emil Horozov](wikipedia://en/46252121), [Yulij Ilyashenko](wikipedia://en/56631455), [Boris Khesin](wikipedia://en/45625343), [Askold Khovanskii](wikipedia://en/43849047), [Nikolay Nekhoroshev](wikipedia://en/19943888), [Boris Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Shapiro_%28mathematician%29), [Alexander Varchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Varchenko), [Victor Vassiliev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Anatolyevich_Vassiliev) and [Vladimir Zakalyukin](wikipedia://en/46713170).

Arnold worked on [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics) and [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763).[^o5] [Michèle Audin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michèle_Audin) described him as "a geometer in the widest possible sense of the word" and said that "he was very fast to make connections between different fields".[^o25]

### Hilbert's thirteenth problem {#Hilbert's_thirteenth_problem}

[Hilbert's thirteenth problem](wikipedia://en/2336190) asks whether every [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) of three variables can be expressed as a [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of finitely many continuous functions of two variables. The affirmative answer to this question was given in 1957 by Arnold, then nineteen years old and a student of [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161). Kolmogorov had shown the previous year that any function of several variables can be constructed with a finite number of three-variable functions. Arnold then expanded on this work to show that only two-variable functions were required, thus answering Hilbert's question for the class of continuous functions.[^o27]

### Dynamical systems {#Dynamical_systems}

[Jürgen Moser](wikipedia://en/1752316) and Arnold expanded the ideas of Kolmogorov (who was inspired by questions of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)) and gave rise to what is now known as [Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem](wikipedia://en/16972) (or "KAM theory"), which concerns the persistence of some quasi-periodic motions (nearly integrable [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531)) when they are perturbed. KAM theory shows that, despite the perturbations, such systems can be stable over an infinite period, and specifies what the conditions for this are.[^o29]

In 1961, he introduced [Arnold tongues](wikipedia://en/3741395); they are observed in a large variety of natural phenomena that involve oscillating quantities, such as concentration of enzymes and substrates in biological processes.[^o30]

In 1964, Arnold introduced the [Arnold web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_web), the first example of a stochastic web.[^o31][^o32]

In 1974, Arnold [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) the [Liouville–Arnold theorem](wikipedia://en/40071018), now a classic result deeply geometric in character.[^o3]

In the 1980s, Arnold reformulated [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750), proposing its infinitesimal version (the [Hilbert–Arnold problem](wikipedia://en/78869128)) that inspired many deep works in dynamical systems theory by mathematicians seeking its solution.[^o3]

### Singularity theory {#Singularity_theory}

In 1965, Arnold attended [René Thom](wikipedia://en/61633)'s seminar on [catastrophe theory](wikipedia://en/434288). He later said of it: "I am deeply indebted to Thom, whose singularity seminar at the [Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), which I frequented throughout the year 1965, profoundly changed my mathematical universe."[^o34] After this event, [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763) became one of the major interests of Arnold and his students.[^o35] Among his most famous results in this area is his classification of simple singularities, contained in his paper "Normal forms of functions near degenerate critical points, the Weyl groups of A<sub>k</sub>,D<sub>k</sub>,E<sub>k</sub> and Lagrangian singularities".[^o36][^o37][^o38]

### Fluid dynamics {#Fluid_dynamics}

In 1966, Arnold published the paper "Sur la géométrie différentielle des groupes de Lie de dimension infinie et ses applications à l'hydrodynamique des fluides parfaits" ('On the [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) of [infinite-dimensional Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional_Lie_group) and its applications to the [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics) of [perfect fluids](wikipedia://en/2137509)'), in which he presented a common geometric interpretation for both the [Euler's equations for rotating rigid bodies](wikipedia://en/898792) and the [Euler's equations of fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/396022); this linked topics previously thought to be unrelated, and enabled mathematical solutions to many questions related to flows and [turbulence](wikipedia://en/154664).[^o40][^o41][^o42]

### Real algebraic geometry {#Real_algebraic_geometry}

In 1971, Arnold published "On the arrangement of ovals of real plane algebraic curves, involutions of four-dimensional smooth manifolds, and the arithmetic of integral quadratic forms",[^o44] which gave new life to [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198). In it, he made major advances in towards a solution to [Gudkov's conjecture](wikipedia://en/46586556), by finding a connection between it and [four-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774#Four_dimensions).[^o45] The [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) was later fully solved by [V. A. Rokhlin](wikipedia://en/7703611) building on Arnold's work.[^o46][^o47]

### Symplectic geometry {#Symplectic_geometry}

The [Arnold conjecture](wikipedia://en/72203783), linking the number of fixed points of Hamiltonian [symplectomorphisms](wikipedia://en/312304) and the topology of the subjacent [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), was the motivating source of many of the pioneer studies in [symplectic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_topology).[^o49][^o50] He also proposed the [nearby Lagrangian conjecture](wikipedia://en/80074033), a still open problem in mathematics.[^o51]

According to [Michèle Audin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michèle_Audin), the birth-date of symplectic topology was 27 October 1965, which is the day Arnold's paper "Sur une propriété topologique des applications globalement canoniques de la mécanique classique" was presented to the [Paris Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o52]

### Topology {#Topology}

According to [Victor Vassiliev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Vassiliev), Arnold "worked comparatively little on topology for topology's sake," being motivated by problems on other areas of mathematics where [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) could be of use. His contributions include the invention of a topological form of the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518) and the initial development of some of the consequent ideas, a work which resulted in the creation of the field of [topological Galois theory](wikipedia://en/51121181) in the 1960s.[^o54][^o55]

### Theory of plane curves {#Theory_of_plane_curves}

According to [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363), Arnold revolutionised [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691) theory.[^o57] He developed the theory of smooth closed plane curves in the 1990s.[^o58] Among his contributions are the introduction of the three [Arnold invariants](wikipedia://en/78868401) of plane curves: *J*<sup>\+</sup>, *J*<sup>−</sup> and *St*.[^o59][^o60]

### Discrete mathematics {#Discrete_mathematics}

In the last years of his life, Arnold's interests shifted to [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492). He investigated [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), producing around twenty papers on these topics, according to [Anatoly Vershik](wikipedia://en/9036441). Arnold's conjecture of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) generalization of [Fermat's little theorem](wikipedia://en/53453) dates from this period.[^o62][^o63]

### Other {#Other}

In 1995, Arnold conjectured the existence of the [gömböc](wikipedia://en/9493560), a body with one stable and one unstable [point of equilibrium](wikipedia://en/92290) when resting on a flat surface. This conjecture was proved by [Gábor Domokos](wikipedia://en/52055656) in 2006.[^o65][^o66]

In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), Arnold generalised the results of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783), and [James Ivory](wikipedia://en/497531) on the [shell theorem](wikipedia://en/2025989), showing it to be applicable to algebraic hypersurfaces.[^o67]

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [Arnold's strange duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold's_strange_duality) was one of the first examples of [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671) (for [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116)).[^o68]

In [magnetohydrodynamics](wikipedia://en/40310), Arnold and E. I. Korkina investigated in 1983 the dynamo property of the [ABC flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold–Beltrami–Childress_flow).[^o69]

The [Arnold complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_complexity) in [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632),[^o70] and the [Arnold's stability theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold's_stability_theorems) in [analysis of PDEs](wikipedia://en/52564) are named after him.[^o71]

## Books, anti-Bourbakism, history of mathematics and influences {#Books,_anti-Bourbakism,_history_of_mathematics_and_influences}

Arnold is well known for his lucid writing style, combining mathematical rigour with physical intuition, and an easy conversational style of teaching and education. His writings present a fresh, often [geometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric) approach to traditional mathematical topics like [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753), and his many textbooks have been influential in the development of new areas of mathematics. The standard criticism about Arnold's pedagogy is that his books "are beautiful treatments of their subjects that are appreciated by experts, but too many details are omitted for students to learn the mathematics required to prove the statements that he so effortlessly justifies." His defence was that his books are meant to teach the subject to "those who truly wish to understand it".[^o73]

Arnold was an outspoken critic of the trend towards high levels of abstraction in mathematics during the middle of the 20th century. He strongly believed that this approach—popularly implemented by the [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) school in France—initially had a negative impact on French [mathematical education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_education) and later in other countries.[^o74][^o75] He was very concerned about what he saw as the divorce of mathematics from the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_science) in the 20th century.[^o76] Arnold was very interested in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220),[^o77] and in an interview,[^o75] remarked that he had learned much of what he knew about mathematics through the study of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887)'s book *[Development of Mathematics in the 19th Century](wikipedia://en/80190697)* –a book he often recommended to his students.[^o78] He studied the works of [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127), [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) and [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740),[^o79] and reported finding ideas that had yet to be explored in the works of Newton and Poincaré.[^o80]

## Later life and death {#Later_life_and_death}

In 1999 Arnold suffered a serious bicycle accident in Paris, resulting in a [traumatic brain injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traumatic_brain_injury). He regained consciousness after a few weeks but had [amnesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amnesia) and for some time could not even recognise his own wife at the hospital.[^o82] He went on to make a good recovery.[^o83]

To his students and colleagues Arnold was known also for his sense of humour. For example, once at his seminar in Moscow, at the beginning of the school year, when he usually was formulating new problems, he said:

There is a general principle that a stupid man can ask such questions to which one hundred wise men would not be able to answer. In accordance with this principle I shall formulate some problems.[^o84]

Arnold died of [acute pancreatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_pancreatitis)[^o85] on 3 June 2010 in Paris, nine days before his 73rd birthday.[^o86] He was buried on 15 June in Moscow, at the [Novodevichy Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novodevichy_Convent).[^o87]

At Arnold's tombstone are engraved a quote by him and mathematical formulae from his discoveries.[^o3]

In a telegram to Arnold's family, [Russian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_president) [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) stated:

The death of Vladimir Arnold, one of the greatest mathematicians of our time, is an irretrievable loss for world science. It is difficult to overestimate the contribution made by academician Arnold to modern mathematics and the prestige of Russian science.

Teaching had a special place in Vladimir Arnold's life and he had great influence as an enlightened mentor who taught several generations of talented scientists.

The memory of Vladimir Arnold will forever remain in the hearts of his colleagues, friends and students, as well as everyone who knew and admired this brilliant man.[^o88]

The [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet) [10031 Vladarnolda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10031_Vladarnolda) was named after him in 1981 by [Lyudmila Georgievna Karachkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmila_Georgievna_Karachkina).[^o102]

### Fields Medal omission {#Fields_Medal_omission}

Arnold was nominated for the 1974 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), one of the highest honours a mathematician could receive, but interference from the Soviet government led to it being withdrawn. Arnold's public opposition to the persecution of [dissidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissident#Eastern_bloc) had led him into direct conflict with influential Soviet officials, and he suffered persecution himself, including not being allowed to leave the Soviet Union during most of the 1970s and 1980s.[^o108][^o109]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Arnold was married to Eleonora Arnold. They had two children, Igor Arnold and Dmitry Arnold.[^o109]
