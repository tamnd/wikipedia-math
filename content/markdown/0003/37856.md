# Alcubierre drive

*Image: Alcubierre.png.* Two-dimensional visualization of an Alcubierre drive, showing the opposing regions of expanding and contracting spacetime that displace the central region

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alcubierre drive"}

The **Alcubierre drive** () is a speculative [warp drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_drive) idea according to which a [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacecraft) could achieve apparent [faster-than-light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faster-than-light) travel by contracting space in front of it and expanding space behind it, under the assumption that a configurable [energy-density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) field lower than that of a [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) (that is, [negative mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_mass)) could be created.[^o8][^o9] Proposed by theoretical physicist [Miguel Alcubierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Alcubierre) in 1994, the Alcubierre drive is based on a solution of [Einstein's field equations](wikipedia://en/424540). Since those [solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solutions_of_the_Einstein_field_equations) are [metric tensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_tensor_%28general_relativity%29), the Alcubierre drive is also referred to as **Alcubierre metric**.

:::

Objects cannot accelerate to the speed of light within normal [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758); instead, the Alcubierre drive shifts space around an object so that the object would arrive at its destination more quickly than light would in normal space without breaking any [physical laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_laws).[^o2]

Although the metric proposed by Alcubierre is consistent with the Einstein field equations, construction of such a drive is not necessarily possible. The proposed mechanism of the Alcubierre drive implies a negative [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) and therefore requires [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter) or manipulation of [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy).[^o39] If exotic matter with the correct properties does not exist, then the drive cannot be constructed. At the close of his original article,[^o3] however, Alcubierre argued (following an argument developed by physicists analyzing traversable [wormholes](wikipedia://en/34043)[^o4][^o5]) that the [Casimir vacuum](wikipedia://en/7555) between parallel plates could fulfill the negative-energy requirement for the Alcubierre drive.

Another possible issue is that, although the Alcubierre metric is consistent with Einstein's equations, general relativity does not incorporate [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). Some physicists have presented arguments to suggest that a theory of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312) (which would incorporate both theories) would eliminate those solutions in general relativity that allow for backward [time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel) (see: [Chronology protection conjecture](wikipedia://en/645792)) and thus make the Alcubierre drive invalid.

[Solitons](wikipedia://en/51654) offer a mathematical "loophole" in general relativity to theoretically create a warp bubble with positive energy. They move through spacetime, while the bubble itself could move faster than the speed of light.[^o6]

## History {#History}

In 1994, [Miguel Alcubierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Alcubierre) proposed a method for changing the [geometry of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe) by creating a wave that would cause the fabric of space ahead of a [spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship) to contract and the space behind it to expand.[^o3][^o8][^o9] The ship would then ride this wave inside a region of flat space, known as a *warp bubble*, and would not move within this bubble but instead be carried along as the region itself moves due to the actions of the drive. The local velocity relative to the deformed spacetime would be subluminal, but the speed at which a spacecraft could move would be superluminal, thereby rendering possible interstellar flight, such as a visit to *[Proxima Centauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxima_Centauri)* within a few days.[^o10]

Alcubierre's original paper noted that the [stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) required by the metric violates the [energy conditions](wikipedia://en/2551717), so that the drive would need [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter) of negative energy density.[^o3] Much of the subsequent literature has addressed whether that requirement can be reduced or removed. In 1999 Chris Van Den Broeck showed that modifying the geometry of the bubble could lower the total energy requirement by many orders of magnitude,[^o41] and in 2002 José Natário described a variant in which the volume of space is neither expanded nor contracted, demonstrating that the common description of the drive as expanding space behind the ship and contracting it in front is not an essential feature of the geometry.[^o11]

Research activity increased markedly after 2020. Between 2020 and 2021, Osvaldo Santos-Pereira, Everton Abreu and Marcelo Ribeiro published a series of papers analysing which matter models—[dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_solution), [perfect fluids](wikipedia://en/2137509), charged dust and fluids with a [cosmological constant](wikipedia://en/38992)—are compatible with the warp-drive geometry.[^o12][^o13][^o14] In 2021, Erik Lentz, Alexey Bobrick and Gianni Martire, and Shaun Fell and Lavinia Heisenberg separately proposed warp geometries whose energy density is non-negative as measured by a particular family of observers.[^o15][^o16][^o17] A 2022 analysis by Jessica Santiago, Sebastian Schuster and [Matt Visser](wikipedia://en/6475417) argued that such tests examine only one preferred family of observers, and that the spacetimes concerned still violate the energy conditions once all observers are taken into account.[^o62]

From 2024, work shifted towards numerical methods and towards asking whether a warp geometry could be produced by a physically reasonable source, rather than inferred from a metric chosen in advance. A numerical toolkit released that year made it possible to compute the curvature, stress–energy content and energy-condition maps of metrics that are not analytically simple,[^o18] and was used to construct a subluminal configuration, moving at constant velocity, that was reported to satisfy all of the classical energy conditions.[^o19] Other work has examined warp bubbles embedded in curved backgrounds such as [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) and [de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301666) geometries,[^o28][^o20][^o21] and the evolution of warp-like initial data as a solution of the Einstein equations rather than as a permanently imposed geometry.[^o22]

## Alcubierre metric {#Alcubierre_metric}

The Alcubierre [metric](wikipedia://en/195795) defines the warp-drive [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758). It is a [Lorentzian manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold) that, if interpreted in the context of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), allows a warp bubble to appear in previously flat spacetime and move away at effectively faster-than-light speed. The interior of the bubble is an [inertial reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_reference_frame) and inhabitants experience no proper acceleration. This method of transport does not involve objects in motion at faster-than-light speeds with respect to the contents of the warp bubble; that is, a light beam within the warp bubble would still always move more quickly than the ship. Because objects within the bubble are not moving (locally) more quickly than light, the mathematical formulation of the Alcubierre metric is consistent with the conventional claims of the laws of relativity (namely, that an object with mass cannot attain or exceed the speed of light) and conventional [relativistic](wikipedia://en/26962) effects such as [time dilation](wikipedia://en/297839) would not apply as they would with conventional motion at near-light speeds.[^o24]

An extension of the Alcubierre metric that eliminates the expansion of the volume elements and instead relies on the change in distances along the direction of travel is that of mathematician José Natário. In his metric, spacetime contracts towards the prow of the ship and expands in the direction perpendicular to the motion, meaning that the bubble actually "slides" through space, roughly speaking by "pushing space aside".[^o24][^o25]

The Alcubierre drive remains a hypothetical concept with seemingly difficult problems, although the amount of energy required is no longer thought to be unobtainably large.[^o26] However, Alexey Bobrick and Gianni Martire claim that, in principle, a class of subluminal, spherically symmetric warp drive spacetimes can be constructed based on physical principles presently known to humanity, such as positive energy.[^o27] Furthermore, a study by Remo Garattini and Kirill Zatrimaylov shows that the amount of negative energy density required to sustain a warp bubble can in principle be reduced if the bubble is moving in an external gravitational field, such as that of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650).[^o28][^o29]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Using the [ADM formalism](wikipedia://en/3839357) of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), the [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) is described by a [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) of space-like [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101) of constant coordinate time $t$, with the metric taking the following general form:

$$ds^2 = -\left(\alpha^2 - \beta_i \beta^i\right) \,dt^2 + 2 \beta_i \,dx^i \,dt + \gamma_{ij}\,dx^i\,dx^j,$$

where

- $\alpha$ is the lapse function that gives the interval of proper time between nearby hypersurfaces,
- $\beta^{i}$ is the shift vector that relates the spatial coordinate systems on different hypersurfaces,
- $\gamma_{ij}$ is a positive-definite metric on each of the hypersurfaces.

The particular form that Alcubierre studied[^o3] is defined by:

$$\begin{aligned} \alpha &= 1,\\ \beta^x &= -v_s(t)f\big(r_s(t)\big),\\ \beta^y &= \beta^z = 0,\\ \gamma_{ij} &= \delta_{ij}, \end{aligned}$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} v_s(t) &= \frac{dx_s(t)}{dt},\\ r_s(t) &= \sqrt{\big(x - x_s(t)\big)^2 + y^2 + z^2},\\ f(r_s) &= \frac{\tanh\big(\sigma (r_s + R)\big) - \tanh\big(\sigma (r_s - R)\big)}{2 \tanh(\sigma R)}, \end{aligned}$$

with arbitrary parameters $R > 0$ and $\sigma > 0$. Alcubierre's specific form of the metric can thus be written:

$$ds^2 = \left(v_s(t)^2 f\big(r_s(t)\big)^2 - 1\right)\,dt^2 - 2v_s(t)f\big(r_s(t)\big)\,dx\,dt + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2.$$

With this particular form of the metric, it can be shown that the energy density measured by observers whose 4-velocity is normal to the hypersurfaces is given by:

$$-\frac{c^4}{8 \pi G} \frac{v_s^2 \left(y^2 + z^2\right)}{4 g^2 r_s ^2} \left(\frac{df}{dr_s}\right)^2,$$

where $g$ is the determinant of the metric [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965).

Thus, because the energy density is negative, one needs [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter) to travel more quickly than the speed of light.[^o3] The existence of exotic matter is not theoretically ruled out; however, generating and sustaining enough exotic matter to perform feats such as faster-than-light travel (and to keep open the "throat" of a [wormhole](wikipedia://en/34043)) is thought to be impractical. According to writer Robert Low, within the context of general relativity it is impossible to construct a [warp drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_drive) in the absence of exotic matter.[^o31]

## Connection to dark energy and dark matter {#Connection_to_dark_energy_and_dark_matter}

Astrophysicist [Jamie Farnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Farnes) from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) has proposed a theory, published in the peer-reviewed scientific journal *[Astronomy & Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy_&_Astrophysics)*, that unifies [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy) and [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter) into a single [dark fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_fluid), and which is expected to be testable by the [Square Kilometre Array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_Kilometre_Array) around 2030.[^o33] Farnes found that [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) had explored the idea of gravitationally repulsive negative masses while developing the equations of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), an idea which leads to a "beautiful" hypothesis where the [cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos) has equal amounts of positive and negative qualities. Farnes' theory relies on [negative masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_mass) that behave identically to the physics of the Alcubierre drive, providing a natural solution for the current "crisis in cosmology" due to a time-variable [Hubble parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_parameter).[^o34]

As Farnes' theory allows a positive mass (i.e. a ship) to reach a speed equal to the speed of light, it has been dubbed "controversial".[^o35] If the theory is correct, which has been highly debated in the scientific literature, it would explain dark energy, dark matter, allow [closed timelike curves](wikipedia://en/287091) (see: [Time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel)), and suggest that an Alcubierre drive is physically possible with [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter).[^o36]

## Physics {#Physics}

With regard to certain specific effects of special relativity, such as [Lorentz contraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz–FitzGerald_contraction_hypothesis) and [time dilation](wikipedia://en/297839), the Alcubierre metric has some apparently peculiar aspects. In particular, Alcubierre has shown that a ship using an Alcubierre drive travels on a free-fall geodesic even while the warp bubble is accelerating: its crew would be in free fall while accelerating without experiencing accelerational [g-forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-force). Enormous [tidal forces](wikipedia://en/30719), however, would be present near the edges of the flat-space volume because of the large space curvature there, but a suitable specification of the metric would keep the tidal forces very small within the volume occupied by the ship.[^o3]

The original warp-drive metric and simple variants of it happen to have the [ADM form](wikipedia://en/3839357), which is often used in discussing the initial-value formulation of general relativity. This might explain the widespread misconception that this spacetime is a *solution* of the field equation of general relativity. Metrics in ADM form are *adapted* to a certain family of inertial observers, but these observers are not really physically distinguished from other such families. Alcubierre interpreted his "warp bubble" in terms of a contraction of space ahead of the bubble and an expansion behind, but this interpretation could be misleading,[^o38] since the contraction and expansion actually refer to the relative motion of nearby members of the family of ADM observers.

In general relativity, one often first specifies a plausible distribution of matter and energy, and then finds the geometry of the spacetime associated with it; but it is also possible to run the [Einstein field equations](wikipedia://en/424540) in the other direction, first specifying a metric and then finding the [energy–momentum tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) associated with it, and this is what Alcubierre did in building his metric. This practice means that the solution can violate various [energy conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conditions) and require [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter). The need for exotic matter raises questions about whether one can distribute the matter in an initial spacetime that lacks a warp bubble in such a way that the bubble is created at a later time, although some physicists have proposed models of dynamical warp-drive spacetimes in which a warp bubble is formed in a previously flat space.[^o39] Moreover, according to [Serguei Krasnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serguei_Krasnikov),[^o40] generating a bubble in a previously flat space for a *one-way* faster-than-light trip requires forcing the exotic matter to move at local faster-than-light speeds, something that would require the existence of [tachyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachyons), although Krasnikov also notes that when the spacetime is not flat from the outset, a similar result could be achieved without tachyons by placing in advance some devices along the travel path and programming them to come into operation at preassigned moments and to operate in a preassigned manner. Some suggested methods avoid the problem of tachyonic motion, but would probably generate a [naked singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_singularity) at the front of the bubble.[^o41][^o42] Allen Everett and Thomas Roman comment on Krasnikov's finding ([Krasnikov tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnikov_tube)):

> \[The finding\] does not mean that Alcubierre bubbles, if it were possible to create them, could not be used as a means of superluminal travel. It only means that the actions required to change the metric and create the bubble must be taken beforehand by some observer whose forward [light cone](wikipedia://en/310474) contains the entire trajectory of the bubble.[^o43]

For example, if one wanted to travel to [Deneb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deneb) (2,600 light-years away) and arrive less than 2,600 years in the future according to external clocks, it would be required that someone have already begun work on warping the space from Earth to Deneb at least 2,600 years ago:

> A spaceship appropriately located with respect to the bubble trajectory could then choose to enter the bubble, rather like a passenger catching a passing trolley car, and thus make the superluminal journey ... as Krasnikov points out, causality considerations do not prevent the crew of a spaceship from arranging, by their own actions, to complete a *round trip* from Earth to a distant star and back in an arbitrarily short time, as measured by clocks on Earth, by altering the metric along the path of their outbound trip.[^o43]

## Difficulties {#Difficulties}

### Mass–energy requirement {#Mass–energy_requirement}

The metric of this form has significant difficulties because all known warp-drive spacetime theories violate various [energy conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conditions).[^o46] Nevertheless, an Alcubierre-type warp drive might be realized by exploiting certain experimentally verified quantum phenomena, such as the [Casimir effect](wikipedia://en/7555), that lead to [stress–energy tensors](wikipedia://en/70671) that also violate the energy conditions, such as negative [mass–energy](wikipedia://en/422481), when described in the context of the quantum field theories.[^o47][^o48]

If certain [quantum inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_inequalities) conjectured by Ford and Roman hold,[^o49] the energy requirements for some warp drives may be unfeasibly large as well as negative. For example, the energy equivalent of −10<sup>64</sup> kg might be required[^o50] to transport a small spaceship across the Milky Way—an amount [orders of magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_magnitude) greater than the estimated [mass of the observable universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_of_the_observable_universe). Counterarguments to these apparent problems have also been offered,[^o2] although the energy requirements still generally require a Type III civilization on the [Kardashev scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kardashev_scale).[^o10]

Chris Van Den Broeck of the [Katholieke Universiteit Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven) in Belgium, in 1999, tried to address the potential issues.[^o51] By contracting the 3+1-dimensional surface area of the bubble being transported by the drive, while at the same time expanding the three-dimensional volume contained inside, Van Den Broeck was able to reduce the total energy needed to transport small atoms to less than three [solar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass). Later in 2003, by slightly modifying the Van den Broeck metric, [Serguei Krasnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serguei_Krasnikov) reduced the necessary total amount of [negative mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_mass) to a few milligrams.[^o2][^o46] Van Den Broeck detailed this by saying that the total energy can be reduced dramatically by keeping the surface area of the warp bubble itself microscopically small, while at the same time expanding the spatial volume inside the bubble. However, Van Den Broeck concludes that the energy densities required are still unachievable, as are the small size (a few orders of magnitude above the [Planck scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_scale)) of the spacetime structures needed.[^o41]

In 2012, physicist [Harold White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_G._White) and collaborators announced that modifying the geometry of [exotic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_matter) could reduce the mass–energy requirements for a macroscopic spaceship from the equivalent of the planet [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter) to that of the [Voyager 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_1) spacecraft (c. 700 kg)[^o26] or less,[^o52] and stated their intent to perform small-scale experiments in constructing warp fields.[^o26] White proposed to thicken the extremely thin wall of the warp bubble, so the energy is focused in a larger volume, but the overall peak energy density is actually smaller. In a flat 2D representation, the ring of positive and negative energy, initially very thin, becomes a larger, fuzzy [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) (donut shape). However, as this less energetic warp bubble also thickens toward the interior region, it leaves less flat space to house the spacecraft, which has to be smaller.[^o53] Furthermore, if the intensity of the space warp can be oscillated over time, the energy required is reduced even more.[^o26] According to White, a modified [Michelson–Morley interferometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelson–Morley_experiment) could test the idea: one of the legs of the interferometer would appear to have a slightly different length when the test devices were energised.[^o52][^o54] Alcubierre has expressed skepticism about the experiment, saying: "from my understanding there is no way it can be done, probably not for centuries if at all".[^o55][^o56]

In 2021, physicist Erik Lentz described a way warp drives sourced from known and familiar purely positive energy could exist—warp bubbles based on superluminal self-reinforcing "soliton" waves.[^o57][^o58][^o59][^o60][^o61] The claim is controversial, with other physicists arguing that all physically reasonable warp drives violate the [weak energy condition](wikipedia://en/2551717#Weak_energy_condition), as well as both the [strong](wikipedia://en/2551717#Strong_energy_condition) and [dominant](wikipedia://en/2551717#Dominant_energy_condition) energy conditions.[^o62]

### Placement of matter {#Placement_of_matter}

Krasnikov proposed that if [tachyonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachyon) matter cannot be found or used, then a solution might be to arrange for masses along the path of the vessel to be set in motion in such a way that the required field was produced. But in this case, the Alcubierre drive vessel can only travel routes that, like a railroad, have first been equipped with the necessary infrastructure. The pilot inside the bubble is causally disconnected from its walls and cannot carry out any action outside the bubble: the bubble cannot be used for the first trip to a distant star because the pilot cannot place infrastructure ahead of the bubble while "in transit". For example, traveling to [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega) (which is 25 light-years from Earth) requires arranging everything so that the bubble moving toward Vega with a superluminal velocity would appear; such arrangements will always take more than 25 years.[^o40]

Coule has argued that schemes, such as the one proposed by Alcubierre, are infeasible because matter placed *en route* of the intended path of a craft must be placed at superluminal speed—that constructing an Alcubierre drive requires an Alcubierre drive even if the metric that allows it is physically meaningful. Coule further argues that an analogous objection will apply to *any* proposed method of constructing an Alcubierre drive.[^o42]

### Survivability inside the bubble {#Survivability_inside_the_bubble}

An article by José Natário (2002) argues that crew members could not control, steer or stop the ship in its warp bubble because the ship could not send signals to the front of the bubble.[^o38]

A 2009 article by Carlos Barceló, Stefano Finazzi, and Stefano Liberati uses quantum theory to argue that the Alcubierre drive at faster-than-light velocities is impossible mostly because extremely high temperatures caused by [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724) would destroy anything inside the bubble at superluminal velocities and destabilize the bubble itself; the article also argues that these problems are absent if the bubble velocity is subluminal, although the drive still requires exotic matter.[^o39]

### Damaging effect on destination {#Damaging_effect_on_destination}

Brendan McMonigal, [Geraint F. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraint_F._Lewis), and Philip O'Byrne have argued that were an Alcubierre-driven ship to decelerate from superluminal speed, the particles that its bubble had gathered in transit would be released in energetic outbursts akin to the infinitely-blueshifted radiation hypothesized to occur at the inner [Cauchy event horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_horizon) of a [Kerr black hole](wikipedia://en/456715); forward-facing particles would thereby be energetic enough to destroy anything at the destination directly in front of the ship.[^o66][^o67]

### Wall thickness {#Wall_thickness}

The amount of negative energy required for such a propulsion is not yet known. Pfenning and Allen Everett of [Tufts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University) hold that a warp bubble traveling at 10-times the speed of light must have a wall thickness of no more than 10<sup>−32</sup> meters—close to the limiting [Planck length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_length), 1.6 × 10<sup>−35</sup> meters.[^o69] In Alcubierre's original calculations, a bubble macroscopically large enough to enclose a ship of 200 meters would require a total amount of exotic matter greater than the mass of the observable universe, and straining the exotic matter to an extremely thin band of 10<sup>−32</sup> meters is considered impractical. Similar constraints apply to [Krasnikov's superluminal subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnikov_tube). Chris Van den Broeck constructed a modification of Alcubierre's model that requires much less exotic matter but places the ship in a curved spacetime "bottle" whose neck is about 10<sup>−32</sup> meters.[^o41]

### Causality violation and semiclassical instability {#Causality_violation_and_semiclassical_instability}

Calculations by physicist Allen Everett show that warp bubbles could be used to create [closed timelike curves](wikipedia://en/287091) in general relativity, meaning that the theory predicts that they could be used for backwards [time travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_travel).[^o71] While it is possible that the fundamental laws of physics might allow closed timelike curves, the [chronology protection conjecture](wikipedia://en/645792) hypothesizes that in all cases where the classical theory of general relativity allows them, quantum effects would intervene to eliminate the possibility, making these spacetimes impossible to realize. A possible type of effect that would accomplish this is a buildup of vacuum fluctuations on the border of the region of spacetime where time travel would first become possible, causing the energy density to become high enough to destroy the system that would otherwise become a time machine. Some results in [semiclassical gravity](wikipedia://en/1049453) appear to support the conjecture, including a calculation dealing specifically with quantum effects in warp-drive spacetimes that suggested that warp bubbles would be semiclassically unstable,[^o39][^o72] but ultimately the conjecture can only be decided by a full theory of [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).[^o73]

A 2022 study by Carlos Barceló, Valentin Boyanov, Luis Garay, Eduardo Martín-Martínez and Jose Manuel Sánchez Velázquez re-examined this argument and found that in 2+1 and higher dimensions the locations at which infinite [blueshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueshift) occurs are generically isolated points, so that the total energy accumulated need not diverge; the authors suggested that suitable choices of bubble shape and trajectory could suppress the effect further.[^o74] The result weakens the claim that semiclassical instability rules out warp bubbles in general, but does not bear on the energy-condition, formation or nonlinear stability problems.

Independently of these considerations, Timothy Ralph and Christopher Chang showed in 2020 that a rotating Alcubierre construction can be arranged so as to produce closed timelike curves,[^o75] and a 2025 follow-up by Achintya Sajeendran and Ralph modified the rotation profile so that some of these curves become spatially circular geodesics, which a freely falling particle could enter and leave from an ordinary flat region of spacetime; the authors present the construction as a theoretical model rather than a physically realisable one.[^o76]

Alcubierre briefly discusses some of these issues in a series of lecture slides posted online,[^o77] where he writes: "beware: in relativity, any method to travel faster than light can in principle be used to travel back in time (a time machine)". In the next slide, he brings up the [chronology protection conjecture](wikipedia://en/645792) and writes: "The conjecture has not been proven (it wouldn't be a conjecture if it had), but there are good arguments in its favor based on quantum field theory. The conjecture does not prohibit faster-than-light travel. It just states that if a method to travel faster than light exists, and one tries to use it to build a time machine, something will go wrong: the energy accumulated will explode, or it will create a black hole."

## Research since 2020 {#Research_since_2020}

Research on the Alcubierre metric expanded considerably after 2020, concentrating on four questions: whether the energy the drive requires can be made non-negative, how candidate metrics should be tested, what kind of matter could act as a source, and whether any part of the physics involved can be reproduced in a laboratory.

### Positive-energy proposals {#Positive-energy_proposals}

In 2021 three groups independently reported warp-like geometries whose energy density is non-negative. Erik Lentz described solitonic solutions in an Einstein–Maxwell–plasma setting;[^o15] Alexey Bobrick and Gianni Martire set out a general classification of warp spacetimes and showed that subluminal, spherically symmetric drives can be constructed from ordinary positive-energy matter;[^o16] and Shaun Fell and Lavinia Heisenberg obtained candidate superluminal metrics with positive semi-definite energy density by exploiting additional geometric degrees of freedom.[^o17] Bobrick and Martire also reported that optimising the geometry of the bubble reduces the negative energy required by the original Alcubierre metric by roughly two orders of magnitude, and noted that a warp shell is a material object that must itself be accelerated rather than a reactionless drive.[^o16]

These claims were qualified the following year. Santiago, Schuster and Visser observed that the [energy conditions](wikipedia://en/2551717) are statements about *all* observers, whereas the positive-energy results had been established only for the observers normal to a particular [foliation](wikipedia://en/3839357) of spacetime; within the class of metrics they considered, physically reasonable warp drives violate the [weak](wikipedia://en/2551717#Weak_energy_condition), [strong](wikipedia://en/2551717#Strong_energy_condition) and [dominant](wikipedia://en/2551717#Dominant_energy_condition) energy conditions, and, under additional assumptions, the [null energy condition](wikipedia://en/2551717#Null_energy_condition) as well.[^o62] A related study of the [ADM mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADM_mass) of warp-drive spacetimes distinguished the mass of the payload from that of the warp field itself and again found energy-condition violations in standard configurations.[^o80]

Later work has sought to reduce the violations rather than remove them. A solution published by José Rodal, based on an irrotational shift vector, reports a peak proper energy deficit about 38 times smaller than that of the Alcubierre metric and some 2,600 times smaller than that of the Natário metric at matched parameters, with the largest null-energy-condition violation reduced more than sixtyfold relative to Natário.[^o81]

### Numerical analysis {#Numerical_analysis}

In 2024 Christopher Helmerich and collaborators released *Warp Factory*, a numerical toolkit that computes the [Einstein tensor](wikipedia://en/1057638), stress–energy distribution, curvature invariants and energy-condition maps of an arbitrary candidate metric.[^o18] This removed the requirement that a metric be analytically tractable and made systematic searches over families of geometries possible. Using the same framework, Jared Fuchs and collaborators combined a shell of matter of positive [ADM mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADM_mass) with a warp-like [shift vector](wikipedia://en/3839357) and reported a solution, travelling at constant subluminal velocity, that satisfies all of the classical energy conditions.[^o19] The solution is not superluminal and does not in itself provide a means of accelerating the shell.

### Warp drives in curved backgrounds {#Warp_drives_in_curved_backgrounds}

A separate line of work embeds the bubble in a curved background rather than in [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space). Remo Garattini and Kirill Zatrimaylov found that the gravitational field of a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) can reduce the negative energy a warp bubble requires, particularly when the bubble travels with the background flow.[^o28] Abhishek Chowdhury obtained a comparable effect for bubbles described in Martel–Poisson charts on Minkowski, [anti-de Sitter](wikipedia://en/301670) and [de Sitter](wikipedia://en/301666) backgrounds, finding that matching the direction of travel to the background flow reduces both the expansion and the negative energy involved, although local violations of the null energy condition remain.[^o21]

### Matter models and dynamics {#Matter_models_and_dynamics}

A parallel programme has asked what kind of matter could source the geometry. Santos-Pereira, Abreu and Ribeiro examined dust, perfect and parametrised fluids, charged dust and fluids with a cosmological constant, finding that some algebraic branches admit a positive matter density but that simple fluids place strong constraints on the shift vector.[^o12][^o13][^o14] Gabriel Abellán, Nelson Bolívar and Ivan Vasilev showed that relaxing Alcubierre's assumptions of a unit [lapse function](wikipedia://en/3839357) and isotropic matter—by admitting anisotropic fluids, heat flow, a non-trivial lapse or alternative coordinates—substantially enlarges the space of admissible stress–energy distributions, and that some piecewise spherical models satisfy the weak and null energy conditions globally, with violations of the dominant energy condition confined to finite shells.[^o85][^o86][^o87]

Because most warp metrics are specified in advance rather than evolved from initial data, a further question is whether such a geometry could arise from an ordinary physical process. Thomas Buchert and Antony Frackowiak studied warp-like configurations as solutions of the Einstein equations using covariant 3+1 methods and relativistic Lagrangian perturbation theory, and found that a geodesic construction initialised with Alcubierre-like data generically develops caustics; they proposed intrinsic spatial curvature and tilted fluid flows as directions beyond the conventional assumptions.[^o22]

### Laboratory analogues {#Laboratory_analogues}

No experiment has produced or detected a warp-drive spacetime, and work described as experimental has instead concerned analogues. In 2021 Harold White and collaborators applied [worldline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldline) numerics to a custom micrometre-scale [Casimir](wikipedia://en/7555) cavity geometry and reported a negative vacuum-energy distribution qualitatively resembling a two-dimensional Alcubierre requirement; the calculation does not solve the Einstein equations for the device and no curvature was measured.[^o89] Proposals also exist to simulate the propagation of [Dirac](wikipedia://en/39407) particles in an effective Alcubierre geometry using [trapped ions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapped_ion_quantum_computer), which would reproduce a tilted effective [light cone](wikipedia://en/310474) without curving laboratory spacetime,[^o90] and a 2026 proposal combines arrays of Casimir cavities with a magnetised [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_%28physics%29) to engineer the signs of the stress–energy components of a subluminal analogue, while stating explicitly that it is not a self-consistent Einstein–Maxwell solution and is not intended to generate measurable curvature.[^o91]

## Relation to *Star Trek* warp drive {#Relation_to_Star_Trek_warp_drive}

The *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek)* television series and films use the term "warp drive" to describe their method of faster-than-light travel. Neither the Alcubierre theory, nor anything similar, existed when the series was conceived—the term "warp drive" and general concept originated with [John W. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Campbell)'s 1931 science fiction novel *[Islands of Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_of_Space)*.[^o93] Alcubierre stated in an email to [William Shatner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shatner) that his theory was directly inspired by the term used in the show[^o94] and cites the "'warp drive' of science fiction" in his 1994 article.[^o3] A USS *Alcubierre* appears in the *Star Trek* [tabletop RPG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabletop_role-playing_game) *[Star Trek Adventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek_Adventures)*.[^o95] Since the release of *[Star Trek: The Original Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_The_Original_Series)*, more recent *Star Trek* [spin-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-off_%28media%29) series have made closer use of the theory behind the Alcubierre Drive incorporating warp bubbles/fields into the in-universe science.
