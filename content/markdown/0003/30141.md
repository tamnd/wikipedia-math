# Demographics of Togo

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Togo"}

**The demographics of [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo)** include [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group), [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), age, education level, health, economic status and [religious affiliations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Togo).

:::

Togo's population is estimated to have grown to four times its size between 1960 and 2010. With nearly 60% of its populace under the age of 25 and a high annual growth rate attributed largely to high fertility, Togo's population is likely to continue to expand for the foreseeable future. Reducing fertility, boosting job creation, and improving education will be essential to reducing the country's high poverty rate. In 2008, Togo eliminated primary school enrolment fees, leading to higher enrolment but increased pressure on limited classroom space, teachers, and materials. Togo has a good chance of achieving universal primary education, but educational quality, the underrepresentation of girls, and the low rate of enrolment in secondary and tertiary schools remain concerns.

## Population {#Population}

Population distribution is very uneven due to soil and terrain variations. The population is generally concentrated in the south and along the major north-south highway connecting the coast to the Sahel. Age distribution is also uneven; nearly one-half of Togolese are less than fifteen years old.

French, the official language, is used in administration and documentation. The public primary schools combine [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) with Ewe or Kabye as languages of instruction, depending on the region. [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is spoken in neighboring [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana) and is taught in Togolese secondary schools. As a result, many Togolese, especially in the south and along the Ghana border, speak some English.

## Language and ethnicity {#Language_and_ethnicity}

[Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo)'s population of Togo million people (Year est.) is composed of about 21 ethnic groups, the two biggest being Ewe in the South (about 21% of the population) and the [Tèm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tem_people) in the Centre (Bafilo, Sokodé, Sotouboua (about 22% of the population); they also live in Ghana and Bénin in big numbers. Tèms have a lot ties with 2/3 of the country as they live in the center of Togo. They exercise diversely almost all occupations from farmers, motors mechanic to business people. [Dagomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagomba_language) is the first most common language in the north, where other [Gur languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gur_languages) such as [Mossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossi_language) and [Gourma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourma) are also found.

The ethnic groups of the coastal region, particularly Ewe and [Gen language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_language) (or Mina) (the two major African languages in the south), constitute the bulk of the civil servants, professionals, and merchants, due in part to the former colonial administrations which provided greater infrastructure development in the south. Most of the southern peoples use these two closely related languages, which are spoken in commercial sectors throughout Togo.

The Kabye live on marginal land and traditionally have emigrated south from their home area in the Kara region to seek employment. Their historical means of social advancement has been through the military and law enforcement forces, and they continue to dominate these services.

Other groups include the [Akposso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akposso) on the Central Plateau, the [Ana people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_people) who are related to the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people), and live in the center of the country, in the strip between [Atakpame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atakpame) and [Tchamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchamba), the [Bassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassar) in the Centre-West, the [Tchamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchamba) in the Centre-East and the [Konkombas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkomba_people) in the upper region of [Bassar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassar), the [Lambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamba_people) in the [Kandé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandé) region, the [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people), the [Tamberma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamberma), the [Losso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losso) and the Ouachi.[^o33]
