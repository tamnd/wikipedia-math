# Sanity check

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sanity check"}

A **sanity check** or **sanity test** is a basic test to quickly evaluate whether a claim or the result of a calculation can possibly be true. It is a simple check to see if the produced material is rational (that the material's creator was thinking rationally, applying [sanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanity)). The point of a sanity test is to rule out certain classes of obviously false results, not to catch every possible error. A [rule-of-thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule-of-thumb) or [back-of-the-envelope calculation](wikipedia://en/550392) may be checked to perform the test. The advantage of performing an initial sanity test is that of speedily evaluating basic function.

:::

In arithmetic, for example, when multiplying by 9, using the [divisibility rule](wikipedia://en/991210) for 9 to verify that the [sum of digits](wikipedia://en/1598868) of the result is divisible by 9 is a sanity test—it will not catch *every* multiplication error, but is a quick and simple method to discover *many* possible errors.

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), a *sanity test* is a very brief run-through of the functionality of a [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program), system, calculation, or other analysis, to assure that part of the system or methodology works roughly as expected. This is often prior to a more exhaustive round of testing.

## Use in different fields {#Use_in_different_fields}

### Mathematical {#Mathematical}

A sanity test can refer to various [orders of magnitude](wikipedia://en/22657) and other simple [rule-of-thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_thumb) devices applied to cross-check [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) [calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculations). For example:

- If one were to attempt to [square](wikipedia://en/659942) 738 and calculated 54,464, a quick sanity check could show that this result cannot be true. Consider that 700 \< 738, yet 700${}^{2}$ = 7${}^{2}$ × 100${}^{2}$ = 490,000 > 54,464. Since squaring positive integers preserves their [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489#Applying_a_function_to_both_sides), the result cannot be true, and so the calculated result is incorrect. The correct answer, 738${}^{2}$ = 544,644, is more than 10 times higher than 54,464.
- In multiplication, 918 × 155 is not 142,135 since 918 is divisible by three but 142,135 is not (digits add up to 16, not a [multiple of three](wikipedia://en/208157#Mathematics)). Also, the product must end in the same digit as the product of end-digits: 8 × 5 = 40, but 142,135 does not end in "0" like "40", while the correct answer does: 918 × 155 = 142,290. An even quicker check is that the product of even and odd numbers is even, whereas 142,135 is odd.

### Physical {#Physical}

- [Dimensional analysis](wikipedia://en/8267) may be used as a sanity check of physical equations: the two sides of any equation must be commensurable or have the same dimensions. A person who has calculated the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_%28physics%29) output of a [car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) to be 700 [kJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilojoule) may have omitted a factor, since the unit *joules* is a measure of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy), not power (energy per unit time).
- When determining [physical properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_property), comparing to known or similar substances will often yield insight on whether the result is reasonable. For instance, most metals sink in water, so the [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density) of most metals should be greater than [that of water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_of_water) (~1000 kg/m3).
- [Fermi estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_estimate) will often provide insight on the order of magnitude of an expected value.

### Software development {#Software_development}

In software development, a sanity test (a form of [software testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_testing) which offers "quick, broad, and shallow testing"[^o6]) evaluates the result of a subset of application functionality to determine whether it is possible and reasonable to proceed with further testing of the entire application.[^o7] Sanity tests may sometimes be used interchangeably with [smoke tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoke_testing_%28software%29)[^o8] insofar as both terms denote tests which determine whether it is *possible* and *reasonable* to continue testing further. On the other hand, a distinction is sometimes made that a smoke test is a non-exhaustive test that ascertains whether the most crucial functions of a programme work before proceeding with further testing whereas a sanity test refers to whether specific functionality such as a particular bug fix works as expected without testing the wider functionality of the software. In other words, a sanity test determines whether the intended result of a code change works correctly while a smoke test ensures that nothing else important was broken in the process. Sanity testing and smoke testing avoid wasting time and effort by quickly determining whether an application is too flawed to merit more rigorous [QA testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_quality_assurance), but needs more developer [debugging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debugging).

Groups of sanity tests are often bundled together for automated [unit testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_testing) of functions, libraries, or applications prior to [merging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merge_%28version_control%29) development code into a testing or [trunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trunk_%28software%29) version control [branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branching_%28version_control%29),[^o9] for [automated building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Build_automation),[^o10] or for [continuous integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_integration) and [continuous deployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_deployment).[^o11]

Another common usage of *sanity test* is to denote checks which are performed within programme code, usually on arguments to functions or returns therefrom, to see if the answers can be assumed to be correct. The more complicated the routine, the more important that its response be checked. The trivial case is checking to see whether the [return value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_statement) of a function indicated success or failure, and to therefore cease further processing upon failure. This return value is actually often itself the result of a sanity check. For example, if the function attempted to open, write to, and close a file, a sanity check may be used to ensure that it did not fail on any of these actions—which is a sanity check often ignored by programmers.[^o12]

These kinds of sanity checks may be used during development for debugging purposes and also to aid in [troubleshooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubleshooting) software [runtime errors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runtime_%28program_lifecycle_phase%29).

Sanity checks are also performed upon installation of [stable, production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_release_life_cycle) software code into a new computing [environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runtime_system) to ensure that all [dependencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_%28computer_programming%29) are met, such as a compatible [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) and [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linker_%28computing%29) [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_%28computing%29). When a computing environment has passed all the sanity checks, it is considered a sane environment for the installation programme to proceed with reasonable expectation of success.

A ["Hello, World!" program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/"Hello,_World!"_program) is often used as a sanity test for a [development environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deployment_environment#Development) similarly. Rather than a complicated script running a set of unit tests, if this simple programme fails to compile or execute, it proves that the supporting environment likely has a configuration problem that will prevent *any* code from compiling or executing. But if "Hello world" executes, then any problems experienced with other programmes likely can be attributed to errors in that application's code rather than the environment.

The [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery),[^o13] and software projects such as [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29),[^o14] [MediaWiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaWiki)[^o15] and [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter),[^o16] discourage use of the phrase *sanity check* in favour of other terms such as *confidence test*, *coherence check*, or simply *test*, as part of a wider attempt to avoid [ableist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ableist) language and increase [inclusivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28disability_rights%29).

### Vibe coding {#Vibe_coding}

Sanity checking is often used in [vibe coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_coding), especially between different AI models (although two separate instances of the same model can work in some cases),[^o18] in order to have a different AI model cover any blind spots or check for any potential errors or faulty logic using a third-party source.[^o19] This can also be done by human [debuggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debugger) as with human-written code.
