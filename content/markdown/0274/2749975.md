# Tait conjectures

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tait conjectures"}

The **Tait conjectures** are three conjectures made by 19th-century mathematician [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait) in his [study of knots](wikipedia://en/17616354).[^o2] The Tait conjectures involve concepts in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) such as [alternating knots](wikipedia://en/1236143), [chirality](wikipedia://en/4333700), and [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591). All of the Tait conjectures have been solved, the most recent being the Flyping conjecture.

:::

## Background {#Background}

*Image: Isthmus.svg.* A reduced diagram is one in which all the isthmi are removed.

Tait came up with his conjectures after his attempt to [tabulate](wikipedia://en/17647177) all knots in the late 19th century. As a founder of the field of knot theory, his work lacks a mathematically rigorous framework, and it is unclear whether he intended the conjectures to apply to all knots, or just to [alternating knots](wikipedia://en/1236143). It turns out that most of them are only true for alternating knots.[^o15] In the Tait conjectures, a knot diagram is called "reduced" if all the "isthmi", or "nugatory crossings" have been removed.

## Crossing number of alternating knots {#Crossing_number_of_alternating_knots}

Tait conjectured that in certain circumstances, [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) was a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898), specifically:

> Any reduced [diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) of an alternating link has the fewest possible crossings.

In other words, the crossing number of a reduced, alternating link is an invariant of the knot. This conjecture was proved by [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman), [Kunio Murasugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunio_Murasugi) (村杉 邦男), and [Morwen Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411) in 1987, using the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642).[^o5] [^o6] [^o7]

## Writhe and chirality {#Writhe_and_chirality}

A second conjecture of Tait:

> An amphicheiral (or acheiral) alternating link has zero writhe.

This conjecture was also proved by [Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman) and [Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411).[^o5][^o13]

## Flyping {#Flyping}

*Image: Flype.svg.* A [flype](wikipedia://en/16891247) move.

The Tait flyping conjecture can be stated:

> Given any two reduced alternating diagrams $D_1$ and $D_2$ of an oriented, prime alternating link: $D_1$ may be transformed to $D_2$ by means of a sequence of certain simple moves called *[flypes](wikipedia://en/16891247)*.

The Tait flyping conjecture was proved by [Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411) and [William Menasco](wikipedia://en/16854432) in 1991.[^o11] The Tait flyping conjecture implies some more of Tait's conjectures:

> Any two reduced diagrams of the same alternating [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) have the same writhe.

This follows because flyping preserves writhe. This was proved earlier by Murasugi and [Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411).[^o12][^o13] It also follows from Greene's work.[^o14] For non-alternating knots this conjecture is not true; the [Perko pair](wikipedia://en/16618607) is a counterexample.[^o15] This result also implies the following conjecture:

> Alternating amphicheiral knots have even crossing number.[^o15]

This follows because a knot's mirror image has opposite writhe. This conjecture is again only true for alternating knots: non-alternating [amphichiral](wikipedia://en/1170160) knot with crossing number 15 exist.
