# Conchospiral

*Image: Conchospiral.svg.* An example

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conchospiral"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **conchospiral** a specific type of [space spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_spiral) on the surface of a [cone](wikipedia://en/782427) (a *[conical spiral](wikipedia://en/59734735)*), whose [floor projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floor_plan) is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373). Conchospirals are used in biology for modelling [snail shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snail_shell), and flight paths of insects [^o2][^o3] and in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) for the construction of [antennas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_%28radio%29).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Parameterization {#Parameterization}

In [cylindrical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_coordinates), the conchospiral is described by the parametric equations:

$$r=\mu^t a$$

$$\theta=t$$

$$z=\mu^t c.$$

The projection of a conchospiral on the $(r,\theta)$ plane is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373). The parameter $\mu$ controls the opening angle of the projected spiral, while the parameter $c$ controls the slope of the cone on which the curve lies.

## History {#History}

The name "conchospiral" was given to these curves by 19th-century German mineralogist [Georg Amadeus Carl Friedrich Naumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Amadeus_Carl_Friedrich_Naumann), in his study of the shapes of sea shells.[^o8]

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The conchospiral has been used in the design for [radio antennas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_antenna). In this application, it has the advantage of producing a radio beam in a single direction, towards the apex of the cone.[^o11][^o12]
