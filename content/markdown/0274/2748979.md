# Quadrifolium

*Image: Quadrifolium.svg.* Rotated quadrifolium

*Image: Rose Curve animation with Gears n2 d1.gif.* Quadrifolium created with gears

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quadrifolium"}

The **quadrifolium** (also known as **four-leaved clover**[^o2]) is a type of [rose curve](wikipedia://en/727472) with an [angular frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_frequency) of 2. It has the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation):

:::

$$r = a\cos(2\theta), \,$$

with corresponding algebraic equation

$$(x^2+y^2)^3 = a^2(x^2-y^2)^2. \,$$

Rotated counter-clockwise by 45°, this becomes

$$r = a\sin(2\theta) \,$$

with corresponding algebraic equation

$$(x^2+y^2)^3 = 4a^2x^2y^2. \,$$

In either form, it is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) of [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) zero.

The [dual curve](wikipedia://en/3127321) to the quadrifolium is

$$(x^2-y^2)^4 + 837(x^2+y^2)^2 + 108x^2y^2 = 16(x^2+7y^2)(y^2+7x^2)(x^2+y^2)+729(x^2+y^2). \,$$

*Image: Dualrose.png.* Dual quadrifolium

The area inside the quadrifolium is $\tfrac 12 \pi a^2$, which is exactly half of the area of the circumcircle of the quadrifolium. The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the quadrifolium is

$$8a\operatorname{E}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)=4\pi a\left(\frac{(52\sqrt{3}-90)\operatorname{M}'(1,7-4\sqrt{3})}{\operatorname{M}^2(1,7-4\sqrt{3})}+\frac{7-4\sqrt{3}}{\operatorname{M}(1,7-4\sqrt{3})}\right)$$

where $\operatorname{E}(k)$ is the [complete elliptic integral of the second kind](wikipedia://en/9960#Complete_elliptic_integral_of_the_second_kind) with modulus $k$, $\operatorname{M}$ is the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090) and $'$ denotes the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) with respect to the second variable.[^o3]
