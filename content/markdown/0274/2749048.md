# Quasi-invariant measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-invariant measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **quasi-invariant measure** *μ* with respect to a transformation *T*, from a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) *X* to itself, is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) which, roughly speaking, is multiplied by a [numerical function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_function) of *T*. An important class of examples occurs when *X* is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*, *T* is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of *M*, and *μ* is any measure that locally is a [measure with base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_with_base) the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) on [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). Then the effect of *T* on μ is locally expressible as multiplication by the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) determinant of the derivative ([pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510)) of *T*.

:::

To express this idea more formally in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) terms, the idea is that the [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative) of the transformed measure μ′ with respect to *μ* should exist everywhere; or that the two measures should be [equivalent](wikipedia://en/6612667) (i.e. mutually [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous)):

$$\mu' = T_{*} (\mu) \approx \mu.$$

That means, in other words, that *T* preserves the concept of a set of [measure zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_zero). Considering the whole equivalence class of measures *ν*, equivalent to *μ*, it is also the same to say that *T* preserves the class as a whole, mapping any such measure to another such. Therefore, the concept of quasi-invariant measure is the same as *invariant measure class*.

In general, the 'freedom' of moving within a measure class by multiplication gives rise to [cocycles](wikipedia://en/2537958#Cocycles), when transformations are composed.

As an example, [Gaussian measure](wikipedia://en/6572909) on [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is not invariant under translation (like Lebesgue measure is), but is quasi-invariant under all translations.

It can be shown that if *E* is a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) and *μ* is a [locally finite](wikipedia://en/6621919) [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on *E* that is quasi-invariant under all translations by elements of *E*, then either dim(*E*) \< +∞ or *μ* is the [trivial measure](wikipedia://en/10122069) *μ* ≡ 0.
