# Butterfly curve (transcendental)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Butterfly curve"}

*Image: Animated construction of butterfly curve.gif.* The butterfly curve with animated construction gives an idea of the complexity of the [curve](wikipedia://en/89246).

The **butterfly curve** is a [transcendental](wikipedia://en/297021) [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) discovered by Temple H. Fay of [University of Southern Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_Mississippi) in 1989.[^o2]

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

For $0 \le t \le 12\pi$, the curve is given by the following [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108):

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \sin t \!\left(e^{\cos t} - 2\cos 4t - \sin^5 \left(\frac{t}{12}\right)\right) \\ y &= \cos t \!\left(e^{\cos t} - 2\cos 4t - \sin^5 \left(\frac{t}{12}\right)\right) \end{aligned}$$

or by the following [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation):

$$r = e^{\sin\theta} - 2\cos \left(4\theta\right) + \sin^5\left(\frac{2\theta - \pi}{24}\right).$$

The $\sin$ term has been added for purely aesthetic reasons, to make the butterfly appear fuller and more pleasing to the eye.[^o2]

## Developments {#Developments}

In 2006, two mathematicians using [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica) analyzed the function and found variants where leaves, flowers, or other insects became apparent.[^o6] New developments regarding such curves are still under research by mathematicians.
