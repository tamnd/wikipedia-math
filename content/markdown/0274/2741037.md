# Hom functor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hom functor"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), [hom-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-set) (i.e. sets of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) between [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29)) give rise to important [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829). These functors are called **hom-functors** and have numerous applications in category theory and other branches of mathematics.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let *C* be a [locally small category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_small_category) (i.e. a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) for which hom-classes are actually [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) and not [proper classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class)).

For all objects *A* and *B* in *C* we define two functors to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) as follows:

{| class=wikitable

\|- ! Hom(*A*, –) : *C* → **Set** ! Hom(–, *B*) : *C* → **Set**[^o4] |- | This is a [covariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_functor) given by:

- Hom(*A*, –) [maps](wikipedia://en/516931#As_morphisms) each object *X* in *C* to the set of morphisms, Hom(*A*, *X*)
- Hom(*A*, –) maps each morphism *f* : *X* → *Y* in C to the [function](wikipedia://en/185427)

Hom(*A*, *f*) : Hom(*A*, *X*) → Hom(*A*, *Y*) given by

$g \mapsto f \circ g$ for each *g* in Hom(*A*, *X*).

\| This is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) given by:

- Hom(–, *B*) maps each object *X* in *C* to the set of morphisms, Hom(*X*, *B*)
- Hom(–, *B*) maps each morphism *h* : *X* → *Y* in C to the function

Hom(*h*, *B*) : Hom(*Y*, *B*) → Hom(*X*, *B*) given by

$g \mapsto g \circ h$ for each *g* in Hom(*Y*, *B*).

\|} The functor Hom(–, *B*) is also called the *[functor of points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functor_of_points)* of the object *B*.

Note that fixing the first argument of Hom naturally gives rise to a covariant functor and fixing the second argument naturally gives a contravariant functor. This is an artifact of the way in which one must compose the morphisms.

The pair of functors Hom(*A*, –) and Hom(–, *B*) are related in a [natural manner](wikipedia://en/45022). For any pair of morphisms *f* : *B* → *B*′ and *h* : *A*′ → *A* the following diagram [commutes](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Image: hom functor.svg.*

Both paths send *g* : *A* → *B* to *f*∘*g*∘*h* : *A*′ → *B*′.

The commutativity of the above diagram implies that Hom(–, –) is a [bifunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifunctor) from *C* × *C* to **Set** which is contravariant in the first argument and covariant in the second. Equivalently, we may say that Hom(–, –) is a bifunctor

Hom(–, –) : *C*<sup>op</sup> × *C* → **Set**

where *C*<sup>op</sup> is the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) to *C*. The notation Hom<sub>*C*</sub>(–, –) is sometimes used for Hom(–, –) in order to emphasize the category forming the domain.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Yoneda's lemma"}

## Yoneda's lemma {#Yoneda's_lemma}

Referring to the above commutative diagram, one observes that every morphism

*h* : *A*′ → *A*

gives rise to a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022)

Hom(*h*, –) : Hom(*A*, –) → Hom(*A*′, –)

and every morphism

*f* : *B* → *B*′

gives rise to a natural transformation

Hom(–, *f*) : Hom(–, *B*) → Hom(–, *B*′)

[Yoneda's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda's_lemma) implies that *every* natural transformation between Hom functors is of this form. In other words, the Hom functors give rise to a [full and faithful](wikipedia://en/876534) embedding of the category *C* into the [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039) **Set**<sup>*C*<sup>*op*</sup></sup> (covariant or contravariant depending on which Hom functor is used).

:::

::: {#o8 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Internal Hom"}

## Internal Hom functor {#Internal_Hom_functor}

Some categories may possess a functor that behaves like a Hom functor, but takes values in the category *C* itself, rather than **Set**. Such a functor is referred to as the **internal Hom functor**, and is often written as

$$\left[-\ -\right] : C^\text{op} \times C \to C$$

to emphasize its product-like nature, or as

$$\mathop\Rightarrow : C^\text{op} \times C \to C$$

to emphasize its functorial nature, or sometimes merely in lower-case:

$\operatorname{hom}(-, -) : C^\text{op} \times C \to C .$ For examples, see [Category of relations](wikipedia://en/5930730).

Categories that possess an internal Hom functor are referred to as [closed categories](wikipedia://en/1397197). One has that

$$\operatorname{Hom}(I, \operatorname{hom}(-, -)) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}(-, -),$$

where *I* is the [unit object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_object) of the closed category. For the case of a [closed monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1396924), this extends to the notion of [currying](wikipedia://en/6600), namely, that

$$\operatorname{Hom}(X, Y \Rightarrow Z) \simeq \operatorname{Hom}(X\otimes Y, Z)$$

where $\otimes$ is a [bifunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifunctor), the **internal product functor** defining a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604). The isomorphism is [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) in both *X* and *Z*. In other words, in a closed monoidal category, the internal Hom functor is an [adjoint functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to the internal product functor. The object $Y \Rightarrow Z$ is called the **internal Hom**. When $\otimes$ is the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/237037) $\times$, the object $Y \Rightarrow Z$ is called the [exponential object](wikipedia://en/2237858), and is often written as $Z^Y$.

Internal Homs, when chained together, form a language, called the [internal language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_language) of the category. The most famous of these are [simply typed lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/1986011), which is the internal language of [Cartesian closed categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_closed_categories), and the [linear type system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_type_system), which is the internal language of [closed symmetric monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/1396924).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Note that a functor of the form

Hom(–, *A*) : *C*<sup>op</sup> → **Set**

is a [presheaf](wikipedia://en/4840944); likewise, Hom(*A*, –) is a [copresheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copresheaf).

A functor *F* : *C* → **Set** that is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) to Hom(*A*, –) for some *A* in *C* is called a [representable functor](wikipedia://en/844461) (or representable copresheaf); likewise, a contravariant functor equivalent to Hom(–, *A*) might be called corepresentable.

Note that Hom(–, –) : *C*<sup>op</sup> × *C* → **Set** is a [profunctor](wikipedia://en/4403842), and, specifically, it is the identity profunctor $\operatorname{id}_C \colon C \nrightarrow C$.

The internal hom functor preserves [limits](wikipedia://en/39248); that is, $\operatorname{hom}(X, -) \colon C \to C$ sends limits to limits, while $\operatorname{hom}(-, X) \colon C^\text{op} \to C$ sends limits in $C^\text{op}$, that is [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) in $C$, into limits. In a certain sense, this can be taken as the definition of a limit or colimit.

The [endofunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endofunctor) Hom(*E*, –) : **Set** → **Set** can be given the structure of a [monad](wikipedia://en/355140); this monad is called the [environment (or reader) monad](wikipedia://en/355140#The_environment_monad).

:::

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

If **A** is an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) and *A* is an object of **A**, then Hom<sub>**A**</sub>(*A*, –) is a covariant [left-exact](wikipedia://en/453945) functor from **A** to the category **Ab** of [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974). It is exact [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *A* is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488).[^o12]

Let *R* be a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) and *M* a left *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). The functor Hom<sub>**R**</sub>(*M*, –): **Mod**-*R* → **Ab** is [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) functor – $\otimes$<sub>*R*</sub> *M*: **Ab** → **Mod**-*R*.
