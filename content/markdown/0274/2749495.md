# Crossed product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crossed product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **crossed product** is a basic method of constructing a new [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) from a von Neumann algebra [acted on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) by a [group](wikipedia://en/19447). It is related to the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) construction for groups. (Roughly speaking, *crossed product* is the expected structure for a [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of a semidirect product group. Therefore crossed products have a [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424) aspect also. This article concentrates on an important case, where they appear in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180).)

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

Recall that if we have two [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) *G* and *N* with an action of *G* on *N* we can form the semidirect product $N \rtimes G$. This contains *N* as a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918), and the action of *G* on *N* is given by [conjugation](wikipedia://en/61839) in the semidirect product. We can replace *N* by its complex [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) *C*\[*N*\], and again form a product $C[N] \rtimes G$ in a similar way; this algebra is a [sum of subspaces](wikipedia://en/58899) *gC*\[*N*\] as *g* runs through the elements of *G*, and is the group algebra of $N \rtimes G$. We can generalize this construction further by replacing *C*\[*N*\] by any algebra *A* acted on by *G* to get a crossed product $A \rtimes G$, which is the sum of subspaces *gA* and where the action of *G* on *A* is given by conjugation in the crossed product.

The crossed product of a von Neumann algebra by a group *G* acting on it is similar except that we have to be more careful about [topologies](wikipedia://en/29954), and need to construct a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) acted on by the crossed product. (Note that the von Neumann algebra crossed product is usually larger than the algebraic crossed product discussed above; in fact it is some sort of completion of the algebraic crossed product.)

In physics, this structure appears in presence of the so called gauge group of the first kind. *G* is the gauge group, and *N* the "field" algebra. The observables are then defined as the fixed points of *N* under the action of *G*. A result by Doplicher, Haag and Roberts says that under some assumptions the crossed product can be recovered from the algebra of observables.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Suppose that *A* is a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) of operators acting on a Hilbert space *H* and *G* is a discrete group acting on *A*. We let *K* be the Hilbert space of all square summable *H*-valued functions on *G*. There is an action of *A* on *K* given by

*a*(*k*)(*g*) = *g*<sup>−1</sup>(*a*)*k*(*g*)

for *k* in *K*, *g*, *h* in *G*, and *a* in *A*, and there is an action of *G* on *K* given by

*g*(*k*)(*h*) = *k*(*g*<sup>−1</sup>h).

The crossed product $A \rtimes G$ is the von Neumann algebra acting on *K* generated by the actions of *A* and *G* on *K*. It does not depend (up to isomorphism) on the choice of the Hilbert space *H*.

This construction can be extended to work for any [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) *G* acting on any von Neumann algebra *A*. When *A* is an [abelian von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/1496379), this is the original **group-measure space** construction of [Murray](wikipedia://en/1732264) and [von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

We let *G* be an infinite countable discrete group acting on the abelian von Neumann algebra *A*. The action is called **free** if *A* has no non-zero projections *p* such that some nontrivial *g* fixes all elements of *pAp*. The action is called **ergodic** if the only invariant projections are 0 and 1. Usually *A* can be identified as the abelian von Neumann algebra $L^\infty(X)$ of essentially bounded functions on a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) *X* acted on by *G*, and then the action of *G* on *X* is ergodic (for any measurable invariant subset, either the subset or its complement has measure 0) if and only if the action of *G* on *A* is ergodic.

If the action of *G* on *A* is free and ergodic then the crossed product $A \rtimes G$ is a factor. Moreover:

- The factor is of type I if *A* has a minimal projection such that 1 is the sum of the *G* conjugates of this projection. This corresponds to the action of *G* on *X* being transitive. Example: *X* is the integers, and *G* is the group of integers acting by translations.
- The factor has type II<sub>1</sub> if *A* has a faithful finite normal *G*-invariant trace. This corresponds to *X* having a finite *G* [invariant measure](wikipedia://en/7635201), absolutely continuous with respect to the measure on *X*. Example: *X* is the unit circle in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), and *G* is the group of all roots of unity.
- The factor has type II<sub>∞</sub> if it is not of types I or II<sub>1</sub> and has a faithful semifinite normal *G*-invariant trace. This corresponds to *X* having an infinite *G* invariant measure without atoms, absolutely continuous with respect to the measure on *X*. Example: *X* is the real line, and *G* is the group of rationals acting by translations.
- The factor has type III if *A* has no faithful semifinite normal *G*-invariant trace. This corresponds to *X* having no non-zero absolutely continuous *G*-invariant measure. Example: *X* is the real line, and *G* is the group of all transformations *ax*+*b* for *a* and *b* rational, *a* non-zero.

In particular one can construct examples of all the different types of factors as crossed products.

:::

## Duality {#Duality}

If $A$ is a [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) on which a locally compact Abelian $G$ acts, then $\Gamma$, the [dual group](wikipedia://en/366136) of [characters](wikipedia://en/293418) $\chi$ of $G$, acts by unitaries on $K$ :

- $$(\chi\cdot k)(h) =\chi(h) k(h)$$

These unitaries normalise the crossed product, defining the **dual action** of $\Gamma$. Together with the crossed product, they generate $A\otimes B(L^2(G))$, which can be identified with the iterated crossed product by the dual action $(A\rtimes G) \rtimes \Gamma$ . Under this identification, the double dual action of $G$ (the dual group of $\Gamma$) corresponds to the tensor product of the original action on $A$ and conjugation by the following unitaries on $L^2(G)$ :

- $$(g\cdot f)(h)=f(hg)$$

The crossed product may be identified with the [fixed point algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_algebra) of the double dual action. More generally $A$ is the [fixed point algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_algebra) of $\Gamma$ in the crossed product.

Similar statements hold when $G$ is replaced by a [non-Abelian](wikipedia://en/4791400) locally compact group or more generally a [locally compact quantum group](wikipedia://en/1845113), a class of [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) related to [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185). An analogous theory has also been developed for actions on [C\* algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*_algebras) and their crossed products.

Duality first appeared for actions of the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438) in the work of [Connes](wikipedia://en/340) and Takesaki on the classification of [Type III factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_III_factor). According to [Tomita–Takesaki theory](wikipedia://en/2898625), every vector which is cyclic for the factor and its [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant) gives rise to a 1-parameter *modular automorphism group*. The corresponding crossed product is a Type $II_\infty$ [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) and the corresponding dual action restricts to an [ergodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic) action of the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438) on its centre, an [Abelian von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/1496379). This [ergodic flow](wikipedia://en/2781451) is called the *flow of weights*; it is independent of the choice of cyclic vector. The *Connes spectrum*, a closed subgroup of the [positive reals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_reals) $\mathbb{R}^{+}$, is obtained by applying the exponential to the kernel of this flow.

- When the kernel is the whole of $R$, the factor is type $III_1$.
- When the kernel is $(\log \lambda) Z$ for $\lambda$ in (0,1), the factor is type $III_\lambda$.
- When the kernel is trivial, the factor is type $III_0$.

[Connes](wikipedia://en/340) and Haagerup proved that the Connes spectrum and the flow of weights are [complete invariants](wikipedia://en/9903220) of hyperfinite [Type III factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_III_factor). From this classification and results in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), it is known that every infinite-dimensional hyperfinite factor has the form $L^\infty(X)\rtimes Z$ for some free ergodic action of $Z$.

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If we take $C$ to be the complex numbers, then the crossed product $C \rtimes G$ is called the **von Neumann group algebra** of *G*.
- If $G$ is an infinite discrete group such that every conjugacy class has infinite order then the von Neumann group algebra is a factor of type II<sub>1</sub>. Moreover if every finite set of elements of $G$ generates a finite subgroup (or more generally if *G* is amenable) then the factor is the hyperfinite factor of type II<sub>1</sub>.

:::
