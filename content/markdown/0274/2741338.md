# George E. P. Box

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="George E. P. Box"}

**George Edward Pelham Box** FRS[^o3] (18 October 1919 – 28 March 2013) was a British statistician, who worked in the areas of [quality control](wikipedia://en/41613), [time-series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-series_analysis), [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), and [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571). He has been called "one of the great statistical minds of the 20th century". His quote "[All models are wrong](wikipedia://en/44732699) but some are useful" has been widely discussed.[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8]

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

He was born in [Gravesend, Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravesend,_Kent), England. Upon entering university he began to study [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), but was called up for service before finishing. During [World War&nbsp;II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War&nbsp;II), he performed experiments for the [British Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army) exposing small animals to poison gas. To analyze the results of his experiments, he taught himself statistics from available texts. After the war, he enrolled at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) and obtained a bachelor's degree in mathematics and statistics. He received a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in 1953, under the supervision of [Egon Pearson](wikipedia://en/1643759) and [HO Hartley](wikipedia://en/14562926).[^o2]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

From 1948 to 1956, Box worked as a statistician for [Imperial Chemical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries) (ICI). While at ICI, he took a leave of absence for a year and served as a visiting professor at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) at Raleigh. He later went to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) where he served as Director of the Statistical Research Group.

In 1960, Box moved to the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) to create the Department of Statistics. In 1980, he was named Vilas Research Professor of Statistics, which is the highest honor given to a member of the University of Wisconsin-Madison faculty.[^o12] Box and Bill Hunter co-founded the Center for Quality and Productivity Improvement at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1985.[^o13] Box officially retired in 1992, becoming an [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor).[^o14]

Box published books including *Statistics for Experimenters* (2nd ed., 2005), *Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control* (4th ed., 2008, with [Gwilym Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwilym_Jenkins) and Gregory C. Reinsel) and *Bayesian Inference in Statistical Analysis.* (1973, with [George C. Tiao](wikipedia://en/80202319)).

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Box served as president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1978 and of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 1979. He received the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) from the [American Society for Quality Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality_Control) in 1968, the [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) from the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) in 1972, the [R.&nbsp;A.&nbsp;Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.&nbsp;A.&nbsp;Fisher_Lectureship) in 1974, the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold from the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) in 1993, the [Brumbaugh Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumbaugh_Award) from the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality) in 1987, 1993, 1997, 2007, and 2010. Box was elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1974 and a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1985).[^o3]

His name is associated with results in statistics such as [Box–Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box–Jenkins) models, [Box–Cox transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box–Cox_transformation), [Box–Behnken designs](wikipedia://en/11086228), and others. Additionally, Box famously wrote, in various books and papers, that "[all models are wrong](wikipedia://en/44732699), but some are useful".[^o16][^o17][^o18]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Box was married three times. In 1945, he married Jessie Ward, a sergeant in the Auxiliary Territorial Service. The two later divorced.[^o20][^o3] He then married Joan Fisher, daughter of the British statistician [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824). The two later collaborated on a biography of Ronald Fisher, published in 1978.[^o21] After Fisher and Box divorced, he married Claire Louise Quist in 1985.[^o3]

Box died on 28 March 2013. He was 93 years old.[^o3][^o22]
