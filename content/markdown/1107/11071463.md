# Entropy rate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Entropy rate"}

In the mathematical theory of [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), the **entropy rate** or **source information rate** of a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) is, informally, the time density of the average information in a stochastic process. For stochastic processes with a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) index, the [entropy](wikipedia://en/15445) rate $H(X)$ is the limit of the [joint entropy](wikipedia://en/910967) of $n$ members of the process $X_k$ divided by $n$, as $n$ [tends to](wikipedia://en/26551602) [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824):

:::

$$H(X) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} H(X_1, X_2, \dots X_n)$$

when the limit exists. An alternative, related quantity is:

$$H'(X) = \lim_{n \to \infty} H(X_n|X_{n-1}, X_{n-2}, \dots X_1)$$

For [strongly stationary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_stationary) stochastic processes, $H(X) = H'(X)$. The entropy rate can be thought of as a general property of stochastic sources; this is the [asymptotic equipartition property](wikipedia://en/248710).

The entropy rate may be used to estimate the complexity of stochastic processes. It is used in diverse applications ranging from characterizing the complexity of languages, blind source separation, through to optimizing quantizers and [data compression](wikipedia://en/8013) algorithms. For example, a maximum entropy rate criterion may be used for [feature selection](wikipedia://en/1179950) in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A process $X$ with a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) index gives rise to the sequence of its [joint entropies](wikipedia://en/910967) $H_n(X_1, X_2, \dots X_n)$. If the limit exists, the entropy rate is defined as

$$H(X) := \lim_{n \to \infty} \tfrac{1}{n} H_n.$$

Note that given any sequence $(a_n)_n$ with $a_0=0$ and letting $\Delta a_k := a_k - a_{k-1}$, by [telescoping](wikipedia://en/508070) one has $a_n={\textstyle \sum_{k=1}^n}\Delta a_k$. The entropy rate thus computes the mean of the first $n$ such entropy changes, with $n$ [going to](wikipedia://en/26551602) [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824). The $n$th entropy change is itself the [conditional entropy](wikipedia://en/908548) $H(X_n | X_{n-1}, X_{n-2}, ...)$. The entropy rate is thus the average entropy of the distribution of the $n$th variable once the previous ones are known. The behaviour of joint entropies from one index to the next is also explicitly subject in some [characterizations of entropy](wikipedia://en/15445#Alternative_characterization).

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

While $X$ may be understood as a sequence of random variables, the entropy rate $H(X)$ represents the average entropy change per one random variable, in the long term.

It can be thought of as a general property of stochastic sources - this is the subject of the [asymptotic equipartition property](wikipedia://en/248710).

### For strongly stationary processes {#For_strongly_stationary_processes}

A stochastic process also gives rise to a sequence of conditional entropies, comprising more and more random variables. For strongly stationary stochastic processes, the entropy rate equals the limit of that sequence

$$H(X) = \lim_{n \to \infty} H(X_n|X_{n-1}, X_{n-2}, \dots X_1)$$

The quantity given by the limit on the right is also denoted $H'(X)$, which is motivated to the extent that here this is then again a rate associated with the process, in the above sense.

### For Markov chains {#For_Markov_chains}

Since a stochastic process defined by a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) that is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/60876#Properties) and [aperiodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aperiodic) has a [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727), the entropy rate is independent of the initial distribution.[^o8]

For example, consider a Markov chain defined on a [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) number of states. Given its [right stochastic transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) $P_{ij}$ and an entropy

$$h_i := -\sum_{j} P_{ij} \log P_{ij}$$

associated with each state, one finds

$$\displaystyle H(X) = \sum_{i} \mu_i h_i,$$

where $\mu_i$ is the [asymptotic distribution](wikipedia://en/3001287) of the chain.

In particular, it follows that the entropy rate of an [i.i.d.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically_distributed) [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) is the same as the entropy of any individual member in the process.

### For hidden Markov models {#For_hidden_Markov_models}

The entropy rate of hidden Markov models (HMM) has no known closed-form solution. However, it has known upper and lower bounds. Let the underlying Markov chain $X_{1:\infty}$ be stationary, and let $Y_{1:\infty}$ be the observable states, then we have

$$H(Y_n|X_1 , Y_{1:n-1} ) \leq H(Y) \leq H(Y_n|Y_{1:n-1} )$$

and at the limit of $n \to \infty$, both sides converge to the middle.[^o10]
