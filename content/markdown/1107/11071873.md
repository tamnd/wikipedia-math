# Gottfried E. Noether

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gottfried E. Noether"}

**Gottfried Emanuel Noether** (; 7 January 1915 – 22 August 1991)[^o3] was a German-born American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [educator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator); one of the third generation of a famous family of mathematicians: he was the son of [Fritz Noether](wikipedia://en/7522370) and nephew of [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896), the grandson of [Max Noether](wikipedia://en/2482337), and brother of chemist Herman Noether. He died in [Willimantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willimantic,_Connecticut), [Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Noether was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe), [Grand Duchy of Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Baden), [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1915. He later moved to [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław) (now Wrocław, Poland). The Nazi regime annulled his citizenship.[^o5] He studied mathematics from 1935 to 1937 in Tomsk University and tried after his father's arrest to go to USA. With the help of his relatives he reached Sweden in 1938 and from there he travelled to the United States in 1939.[^o6] There he earned a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) (1940) and a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) (1941).

The following four years, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he served with [US Army intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army_intelligence) in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). Noether was one of the [Ritchie Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Boys).[^o7] After the war, he earned a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) (1949).

He worked in academia for the rest of his career, beginning at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). He moved to [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) in 1952 where he worked until he joined the faculty of the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut) in 1968. There, he eventually became [chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman) of the department of statistics. He retired in 1985.

## Statistician {#Statistician}

Noether served on a statistical advisory [committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee) for the [United States Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Management_and_Budget) and as an associate [editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editing) of *[The American Statistician](wikipedia://en/16876079)*. He was a fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).

As an expert on [non-parametric statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics), he wrote over 50 articles and six books. He also wrote a brief biography of his father Fritz, who was [executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executed) in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1941.

## Honors {#Honors}

In 1999, the [Gottfried E. Noether Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_E._Noether_Awards) were established to "recognize distinguished researchers and teachers and to support research in the field of nonparametric statistics." The initial recipients of the Gottfried E. Noether Senior Scholar Awards were [Erich Leo Lehmann](wikipedia://en/16388476) (2000), [Robert V. Hogg](wikipedia://en/27364999) (2001), and [Pranab K. Sen](wikipedia://en/32302045) (2002).
