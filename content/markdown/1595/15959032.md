# Infinitary combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinitary combinatorics"}

In mathematics, **infinitary combinatorics**, or **combinatorial set theory**, is an extension of ideas in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) to [infinite sets](wikipedia://en/142679). Some of the things studied include [continuous graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_graph) and [trees](wikipedia://en/704160), extensions of [Ramsey's theorem](wikipedia://en/184898), and [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783). Recent developments concern combinatorics of the [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758677)[^o2] and combinatorics on successors of singular cardinals.[^o3]

:::

## Strong colorings {#Strong_colorings}

[Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) showed that the Ramsey theorem does not extend to sets of size $\aleph_1$by showing that $2^{\aleph_0}\nrightarrow(\aleph_1)^2_2$. That is, Sierpiński constructed a coloring of pairs of [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) into two colors such that for every uncountable subset of real numbers $X$, $[X]^2$ takes both colors. Taking any set of real numbers of size $\aleph_1$ and applying the coloring of Sierpiński to it, we get that $\aleph_1\not\rightarrow(\aleph_1)^2_2$. Colorings such as this are known as strong colorings[^o8] and studied in set theory. (Erdős Hajnal Rado 1965) introduced a similar notation as above for this.

## Large cardinals {#Large_cardinals}

Several [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) properties can be defined using this notation. In particular:

- [Weakly compact cardinals](wikipedia://en/272054) $\kappa$ are those that satisfy $\kappa\rightarrow(\kappa)^2$
- α-[Erdős cardinals](wikipedia://en/326540) $\kappa$ are the smallest that satisfy $\kappa\rightarrow(\alpha)^{<\,\omega}$
- [Ramsey cardinals](wikipedia://en/326533) $\kappa$ are those that satisfy $\kappa\rightarrow(\kappa)^{<\,\omega}$
