# MacBeath surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="MacBeath surface"}

In [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) theory and [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), the **MacBeath surface**, also called **MacBeath curve** or the **Fricke–MacBeath surface curve**, is the genus-7 [Hurwitz surface](wikipedia://en/12807841). It is named for [Murray MacBeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_MacBeath).

:::

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the MacBeath surface is the [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) [PSL(2,8)](wikipedia://en/382667), consisting of 504 symmetries.[^n1]

## Triangle group construction {#Triangle_group_construction}

The surface's [Fuchsian group](wikipedia://en/471365) can be constructed as the principal congruence subgroup of the [(2,3,7) triangle group](wikipedia://en/12659803) in a suitable tower of principal congruence subgroups. Here the choices of quaternion algebra and [Hurwitz quaternion order](wikipedia://en/19436533) are described at the triangle group page. Choosing the ideal $\langle 2 \rangle$ in the ring of integers, the corresponding principal congruence subgroup defines this surface of genus 7. Its [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) is about 5.796, and the number of systolic loops is 126 according to R. Vogeler's calculations.[^n2]

It is possible to realize the resulting triangulated surface as a non-convex [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) without self-intersections.[^n3]

## Historical note {#Historical_note}

This surface was originally discovered by (txt), but named after [Alexander Murray Macbeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Macbeath) due to his later independent rediscovery of the same curve.[^n4] Elkies writes that the equivalence between the curves studied by Fricke and Macbeath "may first have been observed by [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) in a 24.vii.1990 letter to [Abhyankar](wikipedia://en/30876162)".[^o9] In a later survey article Macbeath attributes the result to Fricke.[^o10]
