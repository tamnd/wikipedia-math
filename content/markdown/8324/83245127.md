# Jeri Mulrow

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeri Mulrow"}

*Image: Jeri Metzger Mulrow in 2015.jpg.* Mulrow in 2015

**Jeri Metzger Mulrow** is an American statistician, the 2026 president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988). Formerly a government statistician and deputy director at the US [Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Justice_Statistics), she works as a vice president at [Westat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westat), a company that provides data collection and analysis services to the US government and businesses.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mulrow was born in [Denver, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver,_Colorado), the daughter of John K. Metzger and Connie Setoo; she grew up in [Helena, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena,_Montana), where her mother moved with her second husband, Norman E. Waterman. Although she had loved mathematics in school, she entered [Montana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_State_University) intending to major in chemical engineering, but quickly switched to mathematics. After a 1982 bachelor's degree, she received a master's degree in statistics from [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University).

She spent the bulk of her career in service to the US government, in the [National Institute for Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_for_Standards_and_Technology), [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service), [National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics](wikipedia://en/68863308), and the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice), where she became Principal Deputy Director at the Bureau of Justice Statistics in 2016. Outside the government, she also worked at [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University), [NORC at the University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NORC_at_the_University_of_Chicago), and [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young). After she retired from the government, she became a vice president at Westat.

She was elected as president of the American Statistical Association in 2024, for a one-year term beginning in January 2026.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Mulrow was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 2010. She was one of three 2013 recipients of the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224) for distinguished service to the association.
