# G-matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="G-matrix"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) matrix $A$ is called a **G-matrix** if $A^{-T}=D_1AD_2$ (where $A^{-T}$ means $(A^{-1})^T$) for some real [diagonal matrices](wikipedia://en/174080) $D_1$ and $D_2$.

:::

The term "G-matrix" was coined by [Miroslav Fiedler](wikipedia://en/5539109) and Frank J. Hall[^o2]. It is sometimes called a [**semi-orthogonal matrix**](wikipedia://en/34417095) in research literature[^o3], although the latter term may also refer to a different kind of matrix, namely, a non-square matrix with orthogonal columns/rows.

All real [orthogonal matrices](wikipedia://en/105620) and real invertible diagonal matrices, for instances, are G-matrices.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All matrices below are assumed to be real square matrices.

- If $A$ is a G-matrix, so are $A^T$ and $A^{-1}$.
- If $A$ is a G-matrix and $D$ is a nonsingular diagonal matrix, then both $AD$ and $DA$ are G-matrices.
- If $A$ is a G-matrix and $P$ is a [permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849), then both $AP$ and $PA$ are G-matrices.
- If $A$ is a G-matrix, then $A$ and $A^{-T}$ have the same entrywise zero pattern, i.e., $A_{ij}=0$ if and only if $(A^{-T})_{ij}=0$. Thus the entrywise zero patterns of $A$ and $A^{-1}$ are symmetric to each other.
- The [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687#Direct_sum_of_matrices) of G-matrices is again a G-matrix.
- [Compound matrices](wikipedia://en/36846770) of a G-matrix are G-matrices.
- [Kronecker products](wikipedia://en/712430) of G-matrices are G-matrices.
- Every nonsingular [Cauchy matrix](wikipedia://en/7899870) $C$ such that $C^{-1}e$ and $C^{-T}e$ are entrywise nonzero is a G-matrix. Here $e$ denotes the vector of ones.

:::
