# Sublime number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sublime number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **sublime number** is a [positive](wikipedia://en/47172571) [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) which has a [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) of positive [factors](wikipedia://en/82341) (including itself), and whose positive factors add up to another perfect number.[^o2]

:::

The number [12](wikipedia://en/208155), for example, is a sublime number. It has a perfect number of positive factors ([6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29)): 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12, and the sum of these is again a perfect number: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 12 = [28](wikipedia://en/362213).

, there are only two known sublime numbers: 12 and (2<sup>126</sup>)(2<sup>61</sup> − 1)(2<sup>31</sup> − 1)(2<sup>19</sup> − 1)(2<sup>7</sup> − 1)(2<sup>5</sup> − 1)(2<sup>3</sup> − 1) .[^o3] The second of these has 76 decimal digits:

6,086,555,670,238,378,989,670,371,734,243,169,622,657,830,773,351,885,970,528,324,860,512,791,691,264.

It has 8128 positive divisors, which all add up to 2<sup>126</sup>(2<sup>127</sup>-1), the 12th even perfect number (by the [Euclid–Euler theorem](wikipedia://en/43350725), since 2<sup>127</sup>-1 is the 12th [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908)).
