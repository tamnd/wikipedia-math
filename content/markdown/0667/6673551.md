# Sample-continuous process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sample-continuous process"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **sample-continuous process** is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) whose [sample paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_path) are [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (Ω, Σ, **P**) be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325). Let *X* : *I* × Ω → *S* be a stochastic process, where the [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) *I* and state space *S* are both [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). Then the process *X* is called **sample-continuous** (or **almost surely continuous**, or simply **continuous**) if the map *X*(*ω*) : *I* → *S* is [continuous as a function of topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) for **P**-[almost all](wikipedia://en/1309) *ω* in *Ω*.

In many examples, the index set *I* is an interval of time, \[0, *T*\] or \[0, +∞), and the state space *S* is the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) or *n*-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) (the [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984)) on Euclidean space is sample-continuous.
- For "nice" parameters of the equations, solutions to [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454) are sample-continuous. See the existence and uniqueness theorem in the stochastic differential equations article for some sufficient conditions to ensure sample continuity.
- The process *X* : \[0, +∞) × Ω → **R** that makes equiprobable jumps up or down every unit time according to

$$\begin{cases} X_{t} \sim \mathrm{Unif} (\{X_{t-1} - 1, X_{t-1} + 1\}), & t \mbox{ an integer;} \\ X_{t} = X_{\lfloor t \rfloor}, & t \mbox{ not an integer;} \end{cases}$$

is *not* sample-continuous. In fact, it is surely discontinuous.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For sample-continuous processes, the [finite-dimensional distributions](wikipedia://en/6594303) determine the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_%28stochastic_processes%29), and vice versa.

:::
