# Donald J. Newman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald J. Newman"}

**Donald Joseph (D. J.) Newman** (July 27, 1930 – March 28, 2007) was an American mathematician. He gave simple proofs of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692) and the [Hardy–Ramanujan partition formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Ramanujan_partition_formula). He excelled on multiple occasions at the annual [Putnam competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) while studying at [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) and [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), and later received his PhD from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1953.[^o4]

:::

## Life and works {#Life_and_works}

Newman was born in [Brooklyn, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_New_York), in 1930, and studied at New York's [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School).[^o6] When he was 14 he worked with Dubble Bubble Gum to help solve the statistical question of how often a gum purchaser would receive the same joke for their gum wrapper.[^o7] He was an avid problem-solver, and as an undergraduate was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) all three years he took part in the Putnam math competition; only the third person to attain that feat.[^o8] His mathematical specialties included [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). In 1980 he found a short proof of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692), which can now be found in his textbook on *[Complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759)*.[^o9] He also gave a simplified proof of the [Hardy-Ramanujans partition formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy-Ramanujan_partition_formula).[^o10]

Newman was a friend and associate of [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash).[^o11] His career included posts as a professor of mathematics at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University), [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) and a distinguished chair at [Bar Ilan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Ilan_University) in Israel.[^o12]

Newman's love of problem solving comes through in his writing; his published output as a mathematician includes 150 papers and five books. He taught numerous students over the years, including [Robert Feinerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Feinerman), [Jonah Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Mann), [Eli Passow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Passow), [Louis Raymon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Raymon), [Joseph Bak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bak), [Shmuel Weinberger](wikipedia://en/19159322), and [Gerald Weinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Weinstein) at Yeshiva University, and Bo Gao, [Don Kellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Kellman), [Jonathan Knappenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Knappenberger), and Yuan Xu at Temple University.
