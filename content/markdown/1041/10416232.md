# Phase space method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Phase space method"}

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), the **phase space method** is a technique for constructing and analyzing solutions of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), that is, solving time-dependent [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations).

:::

The method consists of first rewriting the equations as a system of differential equations that are first-order in time, by introducing additional variables. The original and the new variables form a vector in the [phase space](wikipedia://en/191101). The solution then becomes a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in the phase space, parametrized by time. The curve is usually called a [trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) or an [orbit](wikipedia://en/252250). The (vector) differential equation is reformulated as a geometrical description of the curve, that is, as a differential equation in terms of the phase space variables only, without the original time parametrization. Finally, a solution in the phase space is transformed back into the original setting.

The phase space method is used widely in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). It can be applied, for example, to find [traveling wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_wave) solutions of [reaction–diffusion systems](wikipedia://en/8635379).[^o2][^o3]
