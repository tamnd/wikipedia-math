# Spherical mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical mean"}

*Image: Spherical mean.png.* The spherical mean of a function $u$ (shown in red) is the average of the values $u(y)$ (top, in blue) with $y$ on a "sphere" of given radius around a given point (bottom, in blue).

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **spherical mean** of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) around a point is the average of all values of that function on a sphere of given radius centered at that point.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *U* in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) *u* defined on *U* with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) values. Let *x* be a point in *U* and *r* > 0 be such that the [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) *B*(*x*, *r*) of center *x* and radius *r* is contained in *U*. The **spherical mean** over the sphere of radius *r* centered at *x* is defined as

$$\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}(r)}\int\limits_{\partial B(x, r)} \! u(y) \, \mathrm{d} S(y)$$

where ∂*B*(*x*, *r*) is the [(*n* − 1)-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) forming the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of *B*(*x*, *r*), d*S* denotes integration with respect to [spherical measure](wikipedia://en/22905952) and *ω*<sub>*n*−1</sub>(*r*) is the "surface area" of this (*n* − 1)-sphere.

Equivalently, the spherical mean is given by

$$\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\int\limits_{\|y\|=1} \! u(x+ry) \, \mathrm{d}S(y)$$

where *ω*<sub>*n*−1</sub> is the area of the (*n* − 1)-sphere of radius 1.

The spherical mean is often denoted as

$$\int\limits_{\partial B(x, r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\, u(y) \, \mathrm{d} S(y).$$

The spherical mean is also defined for Riemannian manifolds in a natural manner.

:::

## Properties and uses {#Properties_and_uses}

- From the continuity of $u$ it follows that the function $$r\to \int\limits_{\partial B(x, r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\, u(y) \,\mathrm{d}S(y)$$ is continuous, and that its [limit](wikipedia://en/285759) as $r\to 0$ is $u(x).$
- Spherical means can be used to solve the Cauchy problem for the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) $\partial^2_t u=c^2\,\Delta u$ in odd space dimension. The result, known as Kirchhoff's formula, is derived by using spherical means to reduce the wave equation in $\mathbb{R}^n$ (for odd $n$) to the wave equation in $\mathbb{R}$, and then using [d'Alembert's formula](wikipedia://en/4474984). The expression itself is presented in [wave equation article](wikipedia://en/33691#Scalar_wave_equation_in_general_dimension_and_Kirchhoff's_formulae).
- If $U$ is an open set in $\mathbb R^n$ and $u$ is a [*C*<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) function defined on $U$, then $u$ is [harmonic](wikipedia://en/55762) if and only if for all $x$ in $U$ and all $r>0$ such that the closed ball $B(x, r)$ is contained in $U$ one has $$u(x)=\int\limits_{\partial B(x, r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\, u(y) \, \mathrm{d}S(y).$$ This result can be used to prove the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104) for harmonic functions.
