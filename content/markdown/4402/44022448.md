# Droz-Farny line theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Droz-Farny line theorem"}

*Image: Droz-Farny line.svg.* The line through $A_0,B_0,C_0$ is Droz-Farny line

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Droz-Farny line theorem** is a property of two perpendicular lines through the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) of an arbitrary triangle.

:::

Let $T$ be a triangle with vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$, and let $H$ be its orthocenter (the common point of its three [altitude lines](wikipedia://en/161241). Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be any two mutually perpendicular lines through $H$. Let $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$ be the points where $L_1$ intersects the side lines $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively. Similarly, let Let $A_2$, $B_2$, and $C_2$ be the points where $L_2$ intersects those side lines. The Droz-Farny line theorem says that the midpoints of the three segments $A_1A_2$, $B_1B_2$, and $C_1C_2$ are [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear).[^o8][^o5][^o7]

The theorem was stated by [Arnold Droz-Farny](wikipedia://en/57749365) in 1899,[^o8] but it is not clear whether he had a proof.[^o10]

## Goormaghtigh's generalization {#Goormaghtigh's_generalization}

A generalization of the Droz-Farny line theorem was proved in 1930 by [René Goormaghtigh](wikipedia://en/28902890).[^o9]

As above, let $T$ be a triangle with vertices $A$, $B$, and $C$. Let $P$ be any point distinct from $A$, $B$, and $C$, and $L$ be any line through $P$. Let $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$ be points on the side lines $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively, such that the lines $PA_1$, $PB_1$, and $PC_1$ are the images of the lines $PA$, $PB$, and $PC$, respectively, by reflection against the line $L$. Goormaghtigh's theorem then says that the points $A_1$, $B_1$, and $C_1$ are collinear.

The Droz-Farny line theorem is a special case of this result, when $P$ is the orthocenter of triangle $T$.

## Dao's generalization {#Dao's_generalization}

The theorem was further generalized by [Dao Thanh Oai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dao_Thanh_Oai). The generalization as follows:

**First generalization:** Let ABC be a triangle, *P* be a point on the plane, let three parallel segments AA', BB', CC' such that its midpoints and *P* are collinear. Then PA', PB', PC' meet *BC, CA, AB* respectively at three collinear points.[^o6]

*Image: Daotheoremonconic1.svg.* Dao's second generalization

**Second generalization:** Let a [conic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) S and a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) P on the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29). Construct three [lines](wikipedia://en/946975) d<sub>a</sub>, d<sub>b</sub>, d<sub>c</sub> through P such that they meet the conic at A, A'; B, B' ; C, C' respectively. Let D be a point on the [polar](wikipedia://en/19451201) of point P with respect to (S) or D lies on the conic (S). Let DA' ∩ BC =A<sub>0</sub>; DB' ∩ AC = B<sub>0</sub>; DC' ∩ AB= C<sub>0</sub>. Then A<sub>0</sub>, B<sub>0</sub>, C<sub>0</sub> are collinear.[^o11][^o12][^o13]
