# Square root of 6

*Image: distances between double cube corners.svg.* Distances between vertices of a double [unit cube](wikipedia://en/407763) are square roots of the first six [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474). ($\surd7$ is not possible due to [Legendre's three-square theorem](wikipedia://en/40708124).)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Square root of 6"}

The **square root of 6** is the positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) that, when multiplied by itself, gives the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29). It is more precisely called the **principal square root of 6**, to distinguish it from the negative number with the same property. This number appears in numerous geometric and number-theoretic contexts.

:::

It is an [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158).[^o3] The first thirty significant digits of its [decimal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_expansion) are: 2.44948. which can be rounded up to 2.45 to within about 99.98% accuracy (about 1 part in 4800).

Since 6 is the product of 2 and 3, the square root of 6 is the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of 2 and 3, and is the product of the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) and the [square root of 3](wikipedia://en/9126665), both of which are irrational algebraic numbers.

[NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) has published more than a million decimal digits of the square root of six.[^o5]

*Image: Slide rule with square roots of 6 and 7.jpg.* A Logarex system Darmstadt [slide rule](wikipedia://en/28743) with 7 and 6 on A and B scales, and square roots of 6 and [of 7](wikipedia://en/70396474) on C and D scales, which can be read as slightly less than 2.45 and somewhat more than 2.64, respectively

## Geometry {#Geometry}

*Image: Root rectangles up to 6.png.* Root rectangles illustrate a construction of the square root of 6

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), the square root of 6 can be constructed via a sequence of [dynamic rectangles](wikipedia://en/21227262), as illustrated here.[^o7][^o8][^o9]

In [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960), the square root of 6 appears as the longest distances between corners ([vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417)) of the double cube, as illustrated above. The square roots of all lower natural numbers appear as the distances between other vertex pairs in the double cube (including the vertices of the included two cubes).[^o9]

The edge length of a cube with total surface area of 1 is $\frac{\sqrt{6}}{6}$ or the reciprocal square root of 6. The edge lengths of a regular [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) ($t$), a regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) ($o$), and a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) ($c$) of equal total surface areas satisfy $\frac{t\cdot o}{c^2} = \sqrt{6}$.[^o4][^o10]

The edge length of a regular [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) is the square root of 6 times the radius of an inscribed sphere (that is, the distance from the center of the solid to the center of each face).[^o11]

## Trigonometry {#Trigonometry}

The square root of 6, with the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) added or subtracted, appears in several [exact trigonometric values](wikipedia://en/17699968) for angles at multiples of 15 degrees ($\pi/12$ radians).[^o13]

## In culture {#In_culture}

*Image: 13th-century fifth-point arch shape.png.* 13th-century fifth-point arch shape, according to Branner's 1960 interpretation (Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS Fr 19093) of the 13th-century [Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picardy) artist Villard de Honnecourt

[Villard de Honnecourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villard_de_Honnecourt)'s 13th century construction of a Gothic "fifth-point arch" with circular arcs of radius 5 has a height of twice the square root of 6, as illustrated here.[^o15][^o16]
