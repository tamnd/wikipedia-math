# Celestial equator

*Image: AxialTiltObliquity.png.* The celestial equator is currently inclined by about 23.44° to the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) plane. The image shows the relations between Earth's [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) (or *obliquity*), [rotation axis](wikipedia://en/39789), and [orbital plane](wikipedia://en/3074135).

*Image: Motion of Earth & Sun Around the Milky Way 07Jul2022 credits & captions - moon 5 deg incl to ecliptic 9.jpg.* Celestial equator in relation to the [galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_plane) and [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) planes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Celestial equator"}

The **celestial equator** is the [great circle](wikipedia://en/48082) of the imaginary [celestial sphere](wikipedia://en/48239) on the [same plane](wikipedia://en/1508434) as the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) of a planet, by convention generally [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228). By extension, it is also a [plane of reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_of_reference) in the [equatorial coordinate system](wikipedia://en/48384).[^o2] Because of the Earth's [axial tilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt), the celestial equator is currently inclined by about 23.44° with respect to the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) (the plane of [Earth's orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_orbit)), but has varied from about 22.0° to 24.5° over the past 5 million years[^o3] as a result of [Milankovitch cycles](wikipedia://en/372608) and [perturbation](wikipedia://en/1490148) from other planets.

:::

An observer standing on the Earth's equator visualizes the celestial equator as a [semicircle](wikipedia://en/617831) passing through the [zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith), the point directly overhead. As the observer moves north (or south), the celestial equator tilts towards the opposite [horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon). The celestial equator is defined to be of infinite reach (since it is on the celestial sphere); thus, the ends of the semicircle always intersect the horizon due east and due west, regardless of the observer's position on the Earth. At the [poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_pole), the celestial equator coincides with the astronomical horizon. At all [latitudes](wikipedia://en/17616), the celestial equator is a uniform arc or circle because the observer is only finitely far from the plane of the celestial equator, but infinitely far from the edge of the celestial equator itself.[^o4]

[Astronomical objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object) near the celestial equator appear above the horizon from most places on the Earth, but they [culminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culmination) highest near the equator. The celestial equator currently passes through these [constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation):[^o5]

| [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_%28constellation%29) (contains the [first point of Aries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_point_of_Aries) above its southern border) [Cetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetus_%28constellation%29) [Taurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_%28constellation%29) [Eridanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eridanus_%28constellation%29) [Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_%28constellation%29) (near [Orion's Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion's_Belt)) | [Monoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoceros_%28constellation%29) [Canis Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canis_Minor) [Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_%28constellation%29) [Sextans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextans) [Leo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_%28constellation%29) | [Virgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgo_%28constellation%29) (contains the first point of Libra) [Serpens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpens) (caput) [Ophiuchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiuchus) [Serpens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpens) (cauda) [Aquila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquila_%28constellation%29) [Aquarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarius_%28constellation%29) |
|---|---|---|

Over thousands of years, the orientation of the Earth's equator and thus the constellations the celestial equator passes through will change due to [axial precession](wikipedia://en/72576).

Celestial bodies other than the Earth also have similarly defined celestial equators.[^o6][^o7]
