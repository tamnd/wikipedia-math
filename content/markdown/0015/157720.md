# The Logic of Scientific Discovery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Logic of Scientific Discovery"}

***The Logic of Scientific Discovery*** is a 1959 book about the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science) by the philosopher [Karl Popper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Popper). Popper rewrote his book in English from the 1934 (imprint '1935') [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) original, titled *Logik der Forschung. Zur Erkenntnistheorie der modernen Naturwissenschaft*, which literally translates as, "Logic of Research: On the Epistemology of Modern Natural Science"'.[^o3]

:::

## Summary {#Summary}

Popper argues that science should adopt a methodology based on [falsifiability](wikipedia://en/11283), because no number of experiments can ever prove a theory, but a reproducible experiment or observation can refute one. According to Popper: "non-reproducible single occurrences are of no significance to science. Thus a few stray basic statements contradicting a theory will hardly induce us to reject it as falsified. We shall take it as falsified only if we discover a reproducible effect which refutes the theory".[^o5] Popper argues that science should adopt a methodology based on "an *asymmetry* between [verifiability](wikipedia://en/3292195) and falsifiability; an asymmetry which results from the logical form of universal statements. For these are never derivable from singular statements, but can be contradicted by singular statements".[^o6]

## Reception {#Reception}

The psychologist [Harry Guntrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Guntrip) wrote that its publication "greatly stimulated the discussion of the nature of scientific knowledge", including by philosophers who did not completely agree with Popper, such as [Thomas Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kuhn) and [Horace Romano Harré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Romano_Harré).[^o8] The psychiatrist [Carl Jung](wikipedia://en/42031), founder of [analytical psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_psychology), valued the work. The biographer [Vincent Brome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Brome) recalls Jung remarking in 1938 that it exposed "some of the shortcomings of science".[^o9] The philosopher [Paul Ricœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ricœur) endorsed "procedures of invalidation" similar to Popper's criteria for falsifiability.[^o10] The historian [Peter Gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gay) described the work as "an important treatise in epistemology".[^o11] The philosopher [Bryan Magee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Magee) considered Popper's criticisms of [logical positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) "devastating". In his view, Popper's most important argument against logical positivism is that, while it claimed to be a scientific theory of the world, its central tenet, the [verification principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verification_principle), effectively destroyed all of science.[^o12] The physicists [Alan Sokal](wikipedia://en/326674) and [Jean Bricmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Bricmont) argued that critiques of Popper's work have provoked an "irrationalist drift", and that a significant part of the problems that currently affect the philosophy of science "can be traced to ambiguities or inadequacies" in *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*.[^o13] The essayist [Nassim Nicholas Taleb](wikipedia://en/1093496), in his book *[The Black Swan](wikipedia://en/8561256)*, mentions Popper's theory of falsification as a way to combat the effects of [confirmation bias](wikipedia://en/59160), crediting his "insight concerning the fundamental, severe, and incurable unpredictability of the world."[^o14]
