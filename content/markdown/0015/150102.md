# Édouard Lucas

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Édouard Lucas"}

**François Édouard Anatole Lucas** (; 4 April 1842 – 3 October 1891) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Lucas is known for his study of the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918) and the [Tower of Hanoi](wikipedia://en/56990). The related [Lucas sequences](wikipedia://en/734635) and [Lucas numbers](wikipedia://en/398404) are named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lucas was born in [Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiens) and educated at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure).[^o4] He worked in the [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory) and later became a professor of mathematics at the Lycée Saint Louis and the Lycée Charlemagne in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o4]

Lucas served as an artillery officer in the French Army during the Franco-Prussian War of 1870–1871.[^o4]

In 1875, Lucas posed a challenge to [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) that the only solution of the [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109)

$$\sum_{n=1}^{N} n^2 = M^2\;$$

with *N* > 1 is when *N* = 24 and *M* = 70. This is known as the [cannonball problem](wikipedia://en/28045698), since it can be visualized as the problem of taking a square arrangement of cannonballs on the ground and building a [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455) out of them. It was not until 1918 that a proof (using [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939)) was found for this remarkable fact, which has relevance to the [bosonic string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_string_theory) in 26 dimensions.[^o5] More recently, [elementary proofs](wikipedia://en/9318685) have been published.[^o6][^o7]

He devised methods for testing the [primality](wikipedia://en/23666) of numbers. In 1857, at age 15, Lucas began testing the primality of 2<sup>127</sup> − 1, a number with 39 decimal digits, by hand, using [Lucas sequences](wikipedia://en/734635). In 1876, after 19 years of testing,[^o8] he finally proved that 2<sup>127</sup> − 1 is prime; it remained the largest known [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) for three-quarters of a century, and remains the largest prime number proved by hand. Later [Derrick Henry Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer) refined Lucas's [primality tests](wikipedia://en/359169) and obtained the [Lucas–Lehmer primality test](wikipedia://en/325739).

He worked on the development of the [umbral calculus](wikipedia://en/214124).

Lucas is credited as the first to publish the [Kempner function](wikipedia://en/11147109).[^o9]

Lucas was also interested in [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). He found an elegant [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_numeral_system) solution to the [Baguenaudier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguenaudier) puzzle.[^o10] He also invented the [Tower of Hanoi](wikipedia://en/56990) puzzle in 1883, which he marketed under the nickname *N. Claus de Siam*, an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) of *Lucas d'Amiens*, and published for the first time a description of the [dots and boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dots_and_boxes) game in 1889.

At the banquet of the annual congress of the *Association française pour l'avancement des sciences*, a waiter dropped some crockery, and a piece of broken plate cut Lucas on the cheek. He died a few days later of a severe skin inflammation, probably caused by [sepsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepsis), at 49 years old.[^o11]
