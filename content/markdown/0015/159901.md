# Jacques Herbrand

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Herbrand"}

**Jacques Herbrand** (12 February 1908 – 27 July 1931) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Although he died at age 23, he was already considered one of "the greatest mathematicians of the younger generation" by his professors [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) and [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238).[^o3]

:::

He worked in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695). He introduced [recursive functions](wikipedia://en/25407#Functional_recursion). *[Herbrand's theorem](wikipedia://en/2518328)* refers to either of two completely different theorems. One is a result from his doctoral thesis in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478), and the other one half of the [Herbrand–Ribet theorem](wikipedia://en/1078637) on the [class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of [cyclotomic number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_fields). The [Herbrand quotient](wikipedia://en/6043076) is a type of [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420), used in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410). He contributed to [Hilbert's program](wikipedia://en/607286) in the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) by providing a [constructive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_mathematics) [consistency proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_proof) for a weak system of arithmetic. The proof uses the above-mentioned, proof-theoretic Herbrand's theorem.

## Biography {#Biography}

Herbrand finished his doctorate at [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure) in Paris under [Ernest Vessiot](wikipedia://en/1142911) in 1929. He joined the army in October 1929, however, and so did not defend his thesis at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) until the following year. He was awarded a [Rockefeller fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) that enabled him to study in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) in 1930-1931, first with [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), then during June with [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), and finally with [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen).

In Berlin, Herbrand followed a course on Hilbert's proof theory given by von Neumann. During the course, von Neumann explained Gödel's first incompleteness theorem and found, independently of Gödel, the second incompleteness theorem that he also presented in the lectures. A letter of Herbrand's of 5 December 1930 to his friend Claude Chevalley contains a description of von Neumann's idea. An earlier letter to Vessiot, of 28 November, explained Gödel's first incompleteness theorem in the form of failure of omega-consistency.[^o5]

Herbrand's last paper was titled "Sur la non-contradiction de l'arithmétique" (On the consistency of arithmetic). It contains a consistency proof for a restricted system of arithmetic, similar to a result of von Neumann's. Herbrand had studied Gödel's incompleteness article in Easter 1931 through the page proofs [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) had lent him. In the last section of his paper, Herbrand makes a comparison of his restricted result to that of Gödel's. The paper was received by the editors the very same day Herbrand lost his life, 27 July, and published posthumously.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

In July 1931, Herbrand was mountain-climbing in the French [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alps) with two friends when he fell to his death in the granite mountains of [Massif des Écrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_des_Écrins). [^o9]

## Quotation {#Quotation}

*"Jacques Herbrand would have hated [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394)"* said French mathematician [Claude Chevalley](wikipedia://en/366040) quoted in Michèle Chouchan, *"Nicolas Bourbaki Faits et légendes"*, Éditions du choix, 1995.
