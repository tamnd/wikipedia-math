# Normal closure (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Normal closure"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **normal closure** of a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $S$ of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ is the smallest [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $G$ containing $S.$

:::

## Properties and description {#Properties_and_description}

Formally, if $G$ is a group and $S$ is a subset of $G,$ the normal closure $\operatorname{ncl}_G(S)$ of $S$ is the intersection of all normal subgroups of $G$ containing $S$:[^o3]

$$\operatorname{ncl}_G(S) = \bigcap_{S \subseteq N \triangleleft G} N.$$

The normal closure $\operatorname{ncl}_G(S)$ is the smallest normal subgroup of $G$ containing $S,$[^o3] in the sense that $\operatorname{ncl}_G(S)$ is a subset of every normal subgroup of $G$ that contains $S.$

The subgroup $\operatorname{ncl}_G(S)$ is the subgroup [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by the set $S^G=\{s^g : s \in S, g\in G\} = \{g^{-1}sg : s \in S, g\in G\}$ of all [conjugates](wikipedia://en/49176) of elements of $S$ in $G.$ Therefore, one can also write the subgroup as the set of all products of conjugates of elements of $S$ or their inverses:

$$\operatorname{ncl}_G(S) = \{g_1^{-1}s_1^{\epsilon_1} g_1\cdots g_n^{-1}s_n^{\epsilon_n}g_n : n \geq 0, \epsilon_i = \pm 1, s_i\in S, g_i \in G\}.$$

Any normal subgroup is equal to its normal closure. The normal closure of the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) $\varnothing$ is the [trivial subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_subgroup).[^o4]

A variety of other notations are used for the normal closure in the literature, including $\langle S^G\rangle,$ $\langle S\rangle^G,$ $\langle \langle S\rangle\rangle_G,$ and $\langle\langle S\rangle\rangle^G.$

Dual to the concept of normal closure is that of normal interior or [normal core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_core), defined as the join of all normal subgroups contained in $S.$[^o5]

## Group presentations {#Group_presentations}

For a group $G$ given by a [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) $G=\langle S \mid R\rangle$ with generators $S$ and defining [relators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relator_%28group_theory%29) $R,$ the presentation notation means that $G$ is the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $G = F(S) / \operatorname{ncl}_{F(S)}(R),$ where $F(S)$ is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on $S.$[^o7]
