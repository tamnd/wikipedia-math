# CPT symmetry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CPT symmetry"}

**Charge, parity, and time reversal symmetry** is a fundamental [symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_in_physics) of [physical laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_law) under the simultaneous [transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_%28mathematics%29) of [charge conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_conjugation) (C), [parity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_transformation) (P), and [time reversal](wikipedia://en/151013) (T). CPT is the only combination of C, P, and T that is observed to be an exact symmetry of nature at the fundamental level.[^o2][^o3] The **CPT theorem** says that CPT symmetry holds for all physical phenomena, or more precisely, that any [Lorentz invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_invariant) local [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) with a [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/187408) [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) must have CPT symmetry. In layman terms, this stipulates that an [antimatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimatter), mirrored, and [time reversed](wikipedia://en/151013) universe would behave exactly the same as the regular universe.

:::

## History {#History}

The CPT theorem appeared for the first time, implicitly, in the work of [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980) in 1951 to prove the [connection between spin and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-statistics_theorem).[^o5] In 1954, [Gerhart Lüders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhart_Lüders) and [Wolfgang Pauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Pauli) derived more explicit proofs,[^o6][^o7] so this theorem is sometimes known as the Lüders–Pauli theorem. At about the same time, and independently, this theorem was also proved by [John Stewart Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stewart_Bell).[^o8][^o9] These proofs are based on the principle of [Lorentz invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_invariance) and the [principle of locality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_locality) in the interaction of quantum fields. Subsequently, [Res Jost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Jost) gave a more general proof in 1958 using the framework of [axiomatic quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/3559472).

Efforts during the late 1950s revealed the violation of [P-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-symmetry#Quantum_mechanics) by phenomena that involve the [weak force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_force), and there were well-known violations of [C-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-symmetry) as well. For a short time, the [CP-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP-symmetry) was believed to be preserved by all physical phenomena, but in the 1960s that was later found to be false too, which implied, by **CPT invariance**, violations of [T-symmetry](wikipedia://en/151013) as well.

## Derivation of the CPT theorem {#Derivation_of_the_CPT_theorem}

Consider a [Lorentz boost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_boost) in a fixed direction *z*. This can be interpreted as a rotation of the time axis into the *z* axis, with an [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554) rotation parameter. If this rotation parameter were [real](wikipedia://en/20646438), it would be possible for a 180° rotation to reverse the direction of time and of *z*. Reversing the direction of one axis is a reflection of space in any number of dimensions. If space has 3 dimensions, it is equivalent to reflecting all the coordinates, because an additional rotation of 180° in the *x-y* plane could be included.

This defines a CPT transformation if we adopt the [Feynman–Stückelberg interpretation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiparticle#Feynman–Stückelberg_interpretation) of antiparticles as the corresponding particles traveling backwards in time. This interpretation requires a slight [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842), which is well-defined only under the following assumptions:

1. The theory is [Lorentz invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_invariant);
1. The vacuum is Lorentz invariant;
1. The energy is bounded below.

When the above hold, [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25267) can be extended to a Euclidean theory, defined by translating all the operators to imaginary time using the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381). The [commutation relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_relation) of the Hamiltonian, and the [Lorentz generators](wikipedia://en/230487), guarantee that [Lorentz invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_invariance) implies [rotational invariance](wikipedia://en/424202), so that any state can be rotated by 180 degrees.

Since a sequence of two CPT reflections is equivalent to a 360-degree rotation, [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) change by a sign under two CPT reflections, while [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) do not. This fact can be used to prove the [spin-statistics theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin-statistics_theorem).

:::

## Consequences and implications {#Consequences_and_implications}

The implication of CPT symmetry is that a "mirror-image" of our universe—with all objects having their positions reflected through an arbitrary point (corresponding to a [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29) inversion), all [momenta](wikipedia://en/20431) reversed (corresponding to a [time inversion](wikipedia://en/151013)) and with all [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) replaced by [antimatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimatter) (corresponding to a [charge](wikipedia://en/9804) inversion)—would evolve under exactly our physical laws. The CPT transformation turns our universe into its "mirror image" and vice versa.[^o13] [Quantum uncertainty](wikipedia://en/31883) causes the universe and antiuniverse to not be exact mirror images of each other.[^o14] CPT symmetry is recognized to be a fundamental property of physical laws; but a mirror anti-universe remains theoretical.

In order to preserve this symmetry, every violation of the combined symmetry of two of its components (such as CP) must have a corresponding violation in the third component (such as T); in fact, mathematically, these are the same thing. Thus violations in T-symmetry are often referred to as [CP violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_violation).

The CPT theorem can be generalized to take into account [pin groups](wikipedia://en/1794902).

In 2002 [Oscar Greenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Greenberg) proved that, with reasonable assumptions, CPT violation implies the breaking of [Lorentz symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_symmetry).[^o15]

CPT violations would be expected by some [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) models, as well as by some other models that lie outside point-particle quantum field theory. Some proposed violations of Lorentz invariance, such as a [compact dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_dimension) of cosmological size, could also lead to CPT violation. Non-unitary theories, such as proposals where black holes violate unitarity, could also violate CPT. As a technical point, fields with infinite spin could violate CPT symmetry.[^o16]

The overwhelming majority of [experimental searches for Lorentz violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_searches_for_Lorentz_violation) have yielded negative results. A detailed tabulation of these results was given in 2011 by Kostelecky and Russell.[^o17]
