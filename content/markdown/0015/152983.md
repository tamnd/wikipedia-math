# Pedal triangle

*Image: Pedal Triangle.svg.* solid black solid #0373fc solid red

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pedal triangle"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), a **pedal triangle** is obtained by projecting a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) onto the sides of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654).

:::

More specifically, consider a triangle $\triangle ABC$, and a point $P$ that is not one of the vertices $A, B, C$. Drop [perpendiculars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendiculars) from $P$ to the three sides of the triangle (these may need to be produced, i.e., [extended](wikipedia://en/43958459)). Label $L, M, N$ the [intersections](wikipedia://en/8240558) of the lines from $P$ with the sides $BC, AC, AB$. The pedal triangle is then $\triangle LMN$.

If $\triangle ABC$ is not an [obtuse triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle) and $P$ is the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244), then the angles of $\triangle LMN$ are $180^{\circ} - 2A$, $180^{\circ} - 2B$ and $180^{\circ} - 2C$.[^o2]

The quadrilaterals $PMAN, PLBN, PLCM$ are [cyclic quadrilaterals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_quadrilaterals).

The location of the chosen point $P$ relative to the chosen triangle $\triangle ABC$ gives rise to some special cases:

- If $P$ is the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244), then $\triangle LMN$ is the [orthic triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_triangle).
- If $P$ is the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837), then $\triangle LMN$ is the [intouch triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intouch_triangle).
- If $P$ is the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter), then $\triangle LMN$ is the [medial triangle](wikipedia://en/3339743).
- If $P$ is on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the triangle, $\triangle LMN$ collapses to a line (the *pedal line* or *[Simson line](wikipedia://en/3307757)*).

*Image: Pedal Line.svg.* **Special case:** $P$ is on the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432). solid black solid #66cc66 solid #0373fc solid red

The vertices of the pedal triangle of an interior point $P$, as shown in the top diagram, divide the sides of the original triangle in such a way as to satisfy [Carnot's theorem](wikipedia://en/60427832):[^o3]

$$|AN|^2 + |BL|^2 + |CM|^2 = |NB|^2 + |LC|^2 + |MA|^2.$$

## Trilinear coordinates {#Trilinear_coordinates}

If $P$ has [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) $p : q : r$, then the vertices $L, M, N$ of the pedal triangle of $P$ are given by

$$\begin{array}{ccccccc} L &=& 0 &:& q+p\cos C &:& r+p\cos B \\[2pt] M &=& p+q\cos C &:& 0 &:& r+q\cos A \\[2pt] N &=& p+r\cos B &:& q+r\cos A &:& 0 \end{array}$$

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Antipedal triangle"}

## Antipedal triangle {#Antipedal_triangle}

One vertex, $L'$, of the **antipedal triangle** of $P$ is the point of intersection of the perpendicular to $BP$ through $B$ and the perpendicular to $CP$ through $C$. Its other vertices, $M'$ and $N'$, are constructed analogously. [Trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) are given by

$$\begin{array}{ccrcrcr} L' &=& -(q+p\cos C)(r+p\cos B) &:& (r+p\cos B)(p+q\cos C) &:& (q+p\cos C)(p+r\cos B) \\[2pt] M' &=& (r+q\cos A)(q+p\cos C) &:& -(r+q\cos A)(p+q\cos C) &:& (p+q\cos C)(q+r\cos A) \\[2pt] N' &=& (q+r\cos A)(r+p\cos B) &:& (p+r\cos B)(r+q\cos A) &:& -(p+r\cos B)(q+r\cos A) \end{array}$$

For example, the [excentral triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excentral_triangle) is the antipedal triangle of the incenter.

Suppose that $P$ does not lie on any of the extended sides $BC, CA, AB$, and let $P^{-1}$ denote the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of $P$. The pedal triangle of $P$ is [homothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) to the antipedal triangle of $P^{-1}$. The [homothetic center](wikipedia://en/15685517) (which is a triangle center if and only if $P$ is a triangle center) is the point given in [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) by

$$ap(p+q\cos C)(p+r\cos B) \ :\ bq(q+r\cos A)(q+p\cos C) \ :\ cr(r+p\cos B)(r+q\cos A)$$

The product of the areas of the pedal triangle of $P$ and the antipedal triangle of $P^{-1}$ equals the square of the area of $\triangle ABC$.

:::

## Pedal circle {#Pedal_circle}

*Image: Isogonal points2 pedal circle.svg.* The pedal circle of the point $P$ and its isogonal conjugate $P*$ are the same.

The pedal circle is defined as the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of the pedal triangle. Note that the pedal circle is not defined for points lying on the circumcircle of the triangle.

### Pedal circle of isogonal conjugates {#Pedal_circle_of_isogonal_conjugates}

For any point $P$ not lying on the circumcircle of the triangle, it is known that $P$ and its [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) $P*$ have a common pedal circle, whose center is the midpoint of these two points.[^o9]
