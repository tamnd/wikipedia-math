# Dedekind group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dedekind group"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **Dedekind group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* such that every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G* is [normal](wikipedia://en/21918). All [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) are Dedekind groups. A non-abelian Dedekind group is called a **Hamiltonian group**.[^o2]

:::

The most familiar (and smallest) example of a Hamiltonian group is the [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130) of order 8, denoted by Q<sub>8</sub>. Dedekind and [Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114) have shown (in the finite and infinite order cases, respectively) that every Hamiltonian group is a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of the form *G* = Q<sub>8</sub> × *B* × *D*, where *B* is an [elementary abelian 2-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_abelian_2-group), and *D* is a [torsion](wikipedia://en/3568695) abelian group with all elements of odd order.

Dedekind groups are named after [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), who investigated them in (Dedekind 1897), proving a form of the above structure theorem (for [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707)). He named the non-abelian ones after [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817), the discoverer of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440).

In 1898 [George Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Abram_Miller) delineated the structure of a Hamiltonian group in terms of its [order](wikipedia://en/493527) and that of its subgroups. For instance, he shows "a Hamilton group of order 2<sup>*a*</sup> has 2<sup>2*a* − 6</sup> quaternion groups as subgroups". In 2005 Horvat *et al*[^o4] used this structure to count the number of Hamiltonian groups of any order *n* = 2<sup>*e*</sup>*o* where *o* is an odd integer. When *e* \< 3 then there are no Hamiltonian groups of order *n*, otherwise there are the same number as there are Abelian groups of order *o*.
