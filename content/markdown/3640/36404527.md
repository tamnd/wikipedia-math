# Dennis Hejhal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dennis Hejhal"}

**Dennis Arnold Hejhal** (born December 10, 1948, in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago)) is an American mathematician. In his mathematical research he frequently uses extensive computer calculation.

:::

In 1967, as a college freshman, Hejhal scored among the top 5 in the U.S. in the [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition).[^o2] Hejhal graduated from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1970 with a bachelor's degree and from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1972 with a PhD in mathematics under the direction of [Menahem Max Schiffer](wikipedia://en/48545257). He became an assistant professor at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) in 1972, then in 1974 an associate professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and starting in 1978 a professor at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota). Additionally, he was a professor at the [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) from 1994 to 2012 (where he has since been a professor emeritus) and a fellow of the [Minnesota Supercomputing Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Supercomputing_Institute) since 1986. He was a guest professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1993 and at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) on several occasions since 1983.

Hejhal works on [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), [automorphic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_forms), the [Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/981694) and [quantum chaos](wikipedia://en/313779). From 1972 to 1974 he was a Sloan Fellow. In 1986 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Berkeley (*Zeros of Epstein Zeta Functions and Supercomputers*). In 1997 he received the Goran Gustafson Prize from the [Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) and in 2005 the Eva and Lars Gårding Prize. He is a member of the Swedish Royal Society of Sciences. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o3]

Among his successful former doctoral students is [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467). He also supervised the undergraduate honors thesis research of [James Z. Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Z._Wang).
