# William Craig (philosopher)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Craig"}

**William Craig** (November 13, 1918[^o3] – January 13, 2016) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_people) [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy#Academic_personnel), logician and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), who taught at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California). His research interests included [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), and the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science),[^o4] and he is best known for the [Craig interpolation](wikipedia://en/2056790) theorem.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

William Craig was born in [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg), [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), on November 13, 1918. He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1951. He married Julia Rebecca Dwight Wilson and had four children: Ruth, [Walter](wikipedia://en/53006848), Sarah, and Deborah. In 1959, he moved to UC Berkeley. He died on January 13, 2016, at the age of 97.

## Achievements {#Achievements}

Craig is particularly remembered in two theorems that bear his name:

- the [Craig interpolation](wikipedia://en/2056790) theorem, and
- [Craig's theorem](wikipedia://en/8465779), also known as *Craig's axiomatization theorem* or *Craig's reaxiomatization theorem*.
