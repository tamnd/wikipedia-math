# B. (Bertram) C. Brookes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="B. (Bertram) C. Brookes"}

**Bertram Claude Brookes** (7 February 1910 – 10 June 1991) was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [information scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science). He was best known for his research in [informetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informetrics),[^o3][^o4] particularly for reformulating [Bradford's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law) for applications in [academic library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_library) management and [bibliometric studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliometrics). Brookes also made pioneering contributions to the philosophical foundations and methodologies of the emerging field of [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science) during the 1970s.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Brookes studied [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o6] After serving in the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_Force) and the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he joined [University College London (UCL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1947.[^o4][^o6] Until 1968, he was a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in UCL's Department of Electrical Engineering.

From 1966 until his retirement from [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1977, he was a professor at the [School of Librarianship and Archives](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/~uczcsuh/slais/aboutslais/aboutslais.html#:~:text=The%20history%20of%20the%20School,Administration%20was%20added%20in%201947.), now known as the Department of Information Studies. From 1964 to 1975 Brookes was a member of the editorial board of the *[Journal of Documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Documentation)*, and he has been on the editorial board of the *[Journal of Information Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Information_Science)* since 1982.[^o3] He subsequently held visiting professorships at the [University of Western Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Ontario) and, from 1984 until his death in 1991, at [City University London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_London).[^o3][^o4] Brookes died on 10 June 1991 at London.[^o4]

## Research and teaching {#Research_and_teaching}

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Research on Bradford's law and bibliometrics"}

### Research on Bradford's law and bibliometrics {#Research_on_Bradford's_law_and_bibliometrics}

In 1968, Brookes published his first major work on the application of [Bradford's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law) in libraries,[^o10] followed by a *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* article in 1969 [^o11] and a series of subsequent publications that spanned his career.[^o12][^o13][^o14] Brookes's research on Bradford's law was not only a highlight of his career[^o3] but also laid the foundation for a systematic study of bibliometric distributions, which continues to shape the field of [bibliometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliometrics) today.[^o3][^o14][^o15]

Bradford's law, first proposed by British mathematician and librarian [Samuel C. Bradford](wikipedia://en/32723423) in 1934,[^o16] describes how scholarly articles on a given subject are distributed across journals. It observes that a small core of journals produces the majority of significant papers on a topic, while a much larger number of journals publish only a few relevant articles each. When Brookes began his research, the application of [Bradford's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law) in library settings was limited. The calculations then required researchers to identify a large number of peripheral papers and perform complex computations—tasks that were time-consuming and discouraged practical use. As a result, the law had remained, in Brookes's words, a mere statistical curiosity.[^o17]<sup>p. 175</sup>

Brookes proposed that, with the emerging use of computer-based search systems, Bradford's law could be transformed into a practical tool for libraries.[^o11] By focusing on the most productive journals, librarians could efficiently generate subject bibliographies and estimate the scope of literature within a given field. He also introduced a modified form of the Bradford distribution that combined its principles with those of the [Zipf distribution](wikipedia://en/43218),[^o10] developed by linguist [George Kingsley Zipf](wikipedia://en/768770) in the 1930s and 1940s. Zipf's distribution describes an inverse relationship between the frequency and rank of an item in naturally occurring datasets, such as word frequencies in language. By linking these two statistical models, Brookes streamlined the process of literature estimation and bibliographic generation, making [Bradford's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law) a practical instrument for [information retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_retrieval) and [library management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_management).[^o10][^o17]

:::

### The fundamental equation {#The_fundamental_equation}

Brookes is often associated with the cognitive turn in information science through his "fundamental equation", sometimes also known as "foundational equation", which models information as a change in a person’s knowledge structure: K\[S\] + ΔI = K\[S + ΔS\]. [^o19]In this equation, information ( ΔI ) is defined as capable of modifying knowledge structure, which views information not only as something that is added to the mind, but also as a state of informativeness, the meaning of which varies by person and situation. [^o19]

### Development of library and information science pedagogy {#Development_of_library_and_information_science_pedagogy}

Although Brookes's most frequently cited and widely remembered works concern his research on [bibliometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliometrics) and the reformulation of [Bradford's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law),[^o3] his teaching was also regarded as one of the pioneering efforts in the pedagogy of [library and information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_and_information_science). Brookes's work as an educator, shaping the discipline's intellectual foundations and classroom practices during its formative decades between the 1950s and 1970s, was equally influential and remains what many of his students remember most vividly.[^o4] After his appointment in 1947 as [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) at [University College London (UCL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), Brookes taught courses in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) for [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering)[^o3] and co-authored the textbook *Introduction to Statistical Method* with W. F. L. Dick,[^o21] with various emended editions published between the 1950s and 1970s.[^o22] The book was widely adopted in U.K. schools[^o22] and was praised as a pioneering work in statistical education.[^o23] Brookes was also among the earliest pioneers to incorporate statistical methods into the training of library and information management. As early as 1967, he taught a course introducing performance measurement and quantitative evaluation techniques for library management,[^o3] and he also published extensively on the pedagogy of library and information science training.[^o3][^o24][^o25]

## Recognition and legacy {#Recognition_and_legacy}

In 1989, Brookes received the [Derek John de Solla Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_J._de_Solla_Price) Memorial Award,[^o27] presented jointly to him and Czechoslovak information scientist Jan Vlachý. The international award, established in 1984 in honor of information scientist [Derek J. de Solla Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_J._de_Solla_Price), recognizes outstanding contributions to the field of scientometrics and information science.[^o27]

Brookes's work has been read internationally and translated into several languages.[^o3] Throughout his career, he traveled widely, teaching and presenting papers at conferences and seminars across Europe and North America. At both [City University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_St_George's,_University_of_London) and [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), he taught students from around the world and became known for his rigorous yet inspiring teaching style and communication skills.[^o3] He was highly regarded by colleagues and students alike, not only as a pioneering researcher, but also as a generous mentor and peer.[^o3][^o4]
