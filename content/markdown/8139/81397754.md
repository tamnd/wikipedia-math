# Double graph

*Image: Double graph of C 5.svg.* The double of the graph [$C_{8}$](wikipedia://en/878962)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double graph"}

In the mathematical field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **double graph** of a [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) $G$ is a graph derived from $G$ by a specific construction. The concept and its elementary properties were detailed in a 2008 paper by Emanuele Munarini, Claudio Perelli Cippo, Andrea Scagliola, and Norma Zagaglia Salvi.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The double graph, denoted as $\mathcal{D}[G]$, of a simple graph $G$ is formally defined as the [direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_graphs) of $G$ with the [total graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_graph) $T_2$.[^o2] The graph $T_2$ is the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_2$ with a [loop](wikipedia://en/1449083) added to each vertex.

An equivalent construction defines the double graph as the [lexicographic product](wikipedia://en/6026731) $G \circ N_2$, where $N_2$ is the [null graph](wikipedia://en/1520758) on two vertices (two vertices with no edges).[^o2]

If a graph $G$ has $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, its double graph $\mathcal{D}[G]$ has $2n$ vertices and $4m$ edges.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Double graphs have several notable properties that relate directly to the properties of the original graph $G$.[^o2]

- [Adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463): If $A$ is the adjacency matrix of $G$, then the adjacency matrix of $\mathcal{D}[G]$ is the [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430) $A \otimes J_2$, where $J_2$ is the 2×2 [matrix of ones](wikipedia://en/690246).
- [Regularity](wikipedia://en/85821): A graph $G$ is $k$-regular if and only if its double $\mathcal{D}[G]$ is $2k$-regular.
- [Connectivity](wikipedia://en/1614492): $G$ is connected if and only if $\mathcal{D}[G]$ is connected. Furthermore, if $G$ is connected, then $\mathcal{D}[G]$ is [Eulerian](wikipedia://en/333219).
- [Bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431): $G$ is a bipartite graph if and only if $\mathcal{D}[G]$ is also bipartite.
- [Spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914): If the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of $G$ are $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$, the spectrum of $\mathcal{D}[G]$ consists of the eigenvalues $2\lambda_1, \dots, 2\lambda_n$ and $n$ additional eigenvalues equal to zero.
- [Chromatic number](wikipedia://en/426743): The chromatic number of the double graph is the same as the original graph: $\chi(\mathcal{D}[G]) = \chi(G)$.
- [Isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577): Two graphs, $G_1$ and $G_2$, are isomorphic if and only if their doubles, $\mathcal{D}[G_1]$ and $\mathcal{D}[G_2]$, are isomorphic.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

A notable example is the double of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_n$. The resulting graph, $\mathcal{D}[K_n]$, is the hyperoctahedral graph $H_n$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Topological indices, including those computed for double graphs, have applications in chemistry and pharmaceutical research. These indices are used in the development of [quantitative structure-activity relationships (QSARs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QSAR) and quantitative structure-property relationships (QSPRs), where the biological activity or other properties of molecules are correlated with their chemical structure.[^o11]

The double graph construction, along with the related extended double cover and strong double graph constructions, has attracted attention in recent years due to its utility in studying various distance-based and degree-based topological indices.[^o11] These graph operations allow researchers to understand how topological properties of composite graphs relate to the properties of their simpler constituent graphs,[^o11] which is particularly useful in [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708) and [mathematical chemistry](wikipedia://en/1570072) applications.

:::

## Topological indices {#Topological_indices}

Various [topological indices](wikipedia://en/14221614) have been studied for double graphs.[^o13] A topological index is a numerical quantity related to a graph that is invariant under graph automorphisms.

### Distance-based indices {#Distance-based_indices}

For a connected graph $G$ with $n$ vertices:[^o13]

- [Wiener index](wikipedia://en/14223173): $W(D[G]) = 4W(G) + 2n$
- [Harary index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harary_index): $H(D[G]) = 4H(G) + n/2$

### Degree-based indices {#Degree-based_indices}

For a graph $G$:[^o13]

- First [Zagreb index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_index): $M_1(D[G]) = 8M_1(G)$
- Second Zagreb index: $M_2(D[G]) = 16M_2(G)$
- [Randić index](wikipedia://en/24257203): $R(D[G]) = 2R(G)$
- Atom-bond connectivity index: $ABC(D[G]) = 2\sqrt{2} \sum_{e=uv \in E(G)} \sqrt{\frac{d(u) + d(v) - 1}{d(u)d(v)}}$
- Geometric-arithmetic index: $GA(D[G]) = 4GA(G)$

### Combined degree-distance indices {#Combined_degree-distance_indices}

For a connected graph $G$ with $m$ edges:[^o13]

- [Schultz index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schultz_index): $S(D[G]) = 8S(G) + 16m$
- Modified Schultz index: $S^*(D[G]) = 16S^*(G) + 8M_1(G)$
- [Szeged index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged_index): $Sz(D[G]) = 16Sz(G)$
- [Padmakar-Ivan index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padmakar-Ivan_index): $PI(D[G]) = 8PI(G)$
- Second geometric-arithmetic index: $GA_2(D[G]) = 4GA_2(G)$

### Eccentric connectivity index {#Eccentric_connectivity_index}

For a connected graph $G$ with $n$ vertices, where $w(G)$ denotes the number of [well-connected vertices](wikipedia://en/325802#well-connected):[^o13]

$$\xi^c(D[G]) = 4\xi^c(G) + 4w(G)(n - 1)$$

For the [lexicographic product](wikipedia://en/6026731) and [complete sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_sum) of graphs $G_1$ and $G_2$:[^o13]

- $$\xi^c(D[G_1 \circ G_2]) = w(G_1)(4n_2^2(n_1 - 1) + 8m_2) + 4n_2^2\xi^c(G_1) + 8m_2\zeta(G_1)$$
- $$\xi^c(D[G_1 \boxplus G_2]) = 16|E(G_1 \boxplus G_2)|$$

where $n_i = |V(G_i)|$, $m_i = |E(G_i)|$, and $\zeta(G_1)$ is the total eccentricity of $G_1$.

## Strong double graph {#Strong_double_graph}

While the double graph of a graph $G$ joins each vertex in one copy with the *open [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/7309251)* of the corresponding vertex in another copy, the **strong double graph** denoted $SD(G)$ joins each vertex with the *closed neighborhood* (neighbors plus the vertex itself) of the corresponding vertex.[^o19]

The strong double graph can be expressed as the [lexicographic product](wikipedia://en/6026731) $SD(G) = G \circ K_2$, where $K_2$ is the complete graph on two vertices.[^o19]

Strong double graphs have several distinct properties:[^o19]

- Size: If $G$ has $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, then $SD(G)$ has $2n$ vertices and $4m + n$ edges.
- Bipartiteness: $SD(G)$ is [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) if and only if $G$ is totally disconnected (i.e., $G = \overline{K_n}$).
- Hamiltonian property: $SD(G)$ is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph) if and only if $G$ is connected with at least one vertex.
- Chromatic number: For any graph $G$ with at least one edge, $4 \leq \chi(SD(G)) \leq 2\Delta(G) + 2$, where $\Delta(G)$ is the maximum degree of $G$.
- Connectivity: The connectivity of $SD(G)$ is $\kappa(SD(G)) = 2\kappa(G)$.
