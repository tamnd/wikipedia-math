# Plateau's laws

*Image: Soap Bubbles (41493399275).jpg.* Bubbles in a foam of soap. Soap films meet in threes at about 120° along Plateau borders and these borders meet at vertices at about the [tetrahedral angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_angle).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plateau's laws"}

**Plateau's laws** describe the structure of [soap films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_film). These laws were formulated in the 19th century by the [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium) physicist [Joseph Plateau](wikipedia://en/331154) from his experimental observations. Many [patterns in nature](wikipedia://en/35659147) are based on foams obeying these laws.[^o2]

:::

## Description {#Description}

Plateau's laws describe the shape and configuration of soap films as follows:[^o4]

1. Soap films are made of entire (unbroken) smooth surfaces.
1. The [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) of a portion of a soap film is everywhere constant on any point on the same piece of soap film.
1. Soap films always meet in threes along an edge called a **Plateau border**, and they do so at an angle of arccos(−$\tfrac{1}{2}$) = 120°.
1. These Plateau borders meet in fours at a vertex, at the [tetrahedral angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_angle) of arccos(−$\tfrac{1}{3}$) ≈ 109.47°.

Configurations other than those of Plateau's laws are unstable, and the film will quickly tend to rearrange itself to conform to these laws.[^o5]

That these laws hold for [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) was proved mathematically by [Jean Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Taylor) using [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694).[^o6][^o7]
