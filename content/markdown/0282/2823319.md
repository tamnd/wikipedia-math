# HNN extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="HNN extension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **HNN extension** is an important construction of [combinatorial group theory](wikipedia://en/979834).

:::

Introduced in a 1949 paper *Embedding Theorems for Groups*[^o2] by [Graham Higman](wikipedia://en/2600152), [Bernhard Neumann](wikipedia://en/5032588), and [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308), it embeds a given group *G* into another group *G'*, in such a way that two given isomorphic subgroups of *G* are conjugate (through a given isomorphism) in *G'*.

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let *G* be a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) $G = \langle S \mid R\rangle$, and let $\alpha\colon H \to K$ be an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) between two subgroups of *G*. Let *t* be a new symbol not in *S*, and define

$$G*_{\alpha} = \left \langle S,t \mid R, tht^{-1}=\alpha(h), \forall h\in H \right \rangle.$$

The group $G*_{\alpha}$ is called the *HNN extension of* *G* *relative to* α. The original group G is called the *base group* for the construction, while the subgroups *H* and *K* are the *associated subgroups*. The new generator *t* is called the *stable letter*.

:::

## Key properties {#Key_properties}

Since the presentation for $G*_{\alpha}$ contains all the generators and relations from the presentation for *G*, there is a natural homomorphism, induced by the identification of generators, which takes *G* to $G*_{\alpha}$. Higman, Neumann, and Neumann proved that this morphism is injective, that is, an embedding of *G* into $G*_{\alpha}$. A consequence is that two isomorphic subgroups of a given group are always conjugate in some [overgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overgroup); the desire to show this was the original motivation for the construction.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Britton's Lemma"}

### Britton's Lemma {#Britton's_Lemma}

A key property of HNN-extensions is a normal form theorem known as **Britton's Lemma**.[^o8] Let $G*_{\alpha}$ be as above and let *w* be the following product in $G*_{\alpha}$:

$$w=g_0 t^{\varepsilon_1} g_1 t^{\varepsilon_2} \cdots g_{n-1} t^{\varepsilon_n}g_n, \qquad g_i \in G, \varepsilon_i = \pm 1.$$

Then Britton's Lemma can be stated as follows:

> **Britton's Lemma.** If *w* = 1 in *G*∗<sub>α</sub> then
>
> either $n = 0$ and *g*<sub>0</sub> = 1 in *G*
>
> or $n > 0$ and for some *i* ∈ {1, ..., *n*−1} one of the following holds:
>
> ε<sub>*i*</sub> = 1, ε<sub>*i*+1</sub> = −1, *g<sub>i</sub>* ∈ *H*,
>
> ε<sub>*i*</sub> = −1, ε<sub>*i*+1</sub> = 1, *g<sub>i</sub>* ∈ *K*.

In contrapositive terms, Britton's Lemma takes the following form:

> **Britton's Lemma (alternate form).** If *w* is such that
>
> either $n = 0$ and *g*<sub>0</sub> ≠ 1 ∈ *G*,
>
> or $n > 0$ and the product *w* does not contain substrings of the form *tht*<sup>−1</sup>, where *h* ∈ *H* and of the form *t*<sup>−1</sup>*kt* where *k* ∈ *K*, then $w\ne 1$ in $G*_{\alpha}$.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Consequences of Britton's Lemma"}

### Consequences of Britton's Lemma {#Consequences_of_Britton's_Lemma}

Most basic properties of HNN-extensions follow from Britton's Lemma. These consequences include the following facts:

- The natural [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from *G* to $G*_{\alpha}$ is injective, so that we can think of $G*_{\alpha}$ as containing *G* as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011).
- Every element of finite order in $G*_{\alpha}$ is [conjugate](wikipedia://en/49176) to an element of *G*.
- Every finite subgroup of $G*_{\alpha}$ is conjugate to a finite subgroup of *G*.
- If $G$ contains an element $g$ such that $g^k$ is contained in neither $H$ nor $K$ for any integer $k$, then $G*_{\alpha}$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank two.

:::

## Applications and generalizations {#Applications_and_generalizations}

Applied to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the HNN extension constructs the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* that has been 'glued back' on itself by a mapping *f : X → X* (see e.g. [Surface bundle over the circle](wikipedia://en/2011690)). Thus, HNN extensions describe the fundamental group of a self-glued space in the same way that [free products with amalgamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_products_with_amalgamation) do for two spaces *X* and *Y* glued along a connected common subspace, as in the [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert-van_Kampen_theorem). The HNN extension is a natural analogue of the amalgamated free product, and comes up in determining the fundamental group of a union when the intersection is not connected.[^o12] These two constructions allow the description of the fundamental group of any reasonable geometric gluing. This is generalized into the [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708) of groups acting on trees, constructing fundamental groups of [graphs of groups](wikipedia://en/12281773).[^o13][^o14]

HNN-extensions play a key role in Higman's proof of the [Higman embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/4061200) which states that every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [recursively presented group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_presented_group) can be homomorphically embedded in a [finitely presented group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group). Most modern proofs of the [Novikov–Boone theorem](wikipedia://en/99494#Novikov–Boone_theorem) about the existence of a [finitely presented group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) with algorithmically undecidable [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) also substantially use HNN-extensions.

The idea of HNN extension has been extended to other parts of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), including [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) theory[^o15].
