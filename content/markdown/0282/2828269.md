# Federico Commandino

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Federico Commandino"}

**Federico Commandino** (1509 – 5 September 1575) was an Italian [humanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanism) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

*Image: Federico Commandino.jpg.* Federico Commandino

Born in [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino), he studied at [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) and then at [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara), where he received his doctorate in [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine) under [Antonio Musa Brassavola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Musa_Brassavola). He had numerous patrons throughout his life. Initially, aided by Grassi, the bishop of Viterbo, he then came under the patronage of Pope [Clement VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_VIII). In [Urbino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbino), he was sponsored by [Guidobaldo II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_II_della_Rovere), but then came to Rome with cardinal [Ranuccio Farnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranuccio_Farnese_%28cardinal%29). In Rome, he was patronized by [Cardinal Cervini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Marcellus_II), who served briefly as Pope. Lured back to Urbino by [Francesco Maria II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_II_della_Rovere). In Urbino, he putatively met [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee), and corresponded with the scholars [Conrad Dasypodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Dasypodius) (il Dasipodio), [Gerolamo Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145), [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico), and [Christopher Clavius](wikipedia://en/633645).[^o2]

He was most famous for his central role as translator of works of ancient mathematicians. In this, his sources were primarily written in Greek and secondarily in Arabic, while his translations were primarily in Latin and secondarily in Italian. He was responsible for the publication of many treatises of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844). He also translated the works of [Aristarchus of Samos](wikipedia://en/74857) (*On the sizes and distances of the Sun and the Moon*), [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) (*Mathematical collection*), [Hero of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/68274) (*Pneumatics*), [Ptolemy of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_of_Alexandria) (*Planisphere* and *Analemma*), [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242) (*Conics*) and [Euclid of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_of_Alexandria) (*Elements*). Among his pupils was [Guidobaldo del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_del_Monte) and [Bernardino Baldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Baldi). Commandino maintained a correspondence with the astronomer [Francesco Maurolico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maurolico). The proposition known as [Commandino's theorem](wikipedia://en/54140352) first appears in his work on centers of gravity.
