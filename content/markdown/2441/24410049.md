# Laplace–Carson transform

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laplace–Carson transform"}

In mathematics, the **Laplace–Carson transform**, named for [Pierre Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783) and [John Renshaw Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Renshaw_Carson), is an [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912) closely related to the standard [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610). It is defined by multiplying the Laplace transform of a function by the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) variable $p$.[^o2] This modification can simplify the analysis of certain functions, particularly the [unit step function](wikipedia://en/87299) and [Dirac delta function](wikipedia://en/37021), whose transforms become simple constants. The transform has applications in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), especially in the study of vibrations and transient phenomena in electrical circuits and mechanical structures.[^o6]

:::

## Relationship to the Laplace transform {#Relationship_to_the_Laplace_transform}

The Laplace–Carson transform $V^\ast(j,p)$ is directly defined from the one-sided Laplace transform $F(s)$. If $F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{V(j,t)\}(s)$ is the Laplace transform of $V(j,t)$, then the Laplace–Carson transform is given by:

$$V^\ast(j,p) = p \mathcal{L}\{V(j,t)\}(p) = p \int^{\infty}_0 V(j,t) e^{-pt} \, dt$$

where the variable $p$ is used in place of the more common $s$ for the frequency domain.[^o2] Due to the multiplication by $p$, the transform of a derivative, $\mathcal{L}\{V'(t)\} = pF(p) - V(0)$, takes a different form that some authors found more convenient for operational calculus.[^o6]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $V(j,t)$ be a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) and $p$ a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) variable. The Laplace–Carson transform is defined as:[^o6]

$$V^\ast(j,p) = p\int^{\infty}_0 V(j,t) e^{-pt} \, dt$$

The inverse Laplace–Carson transform is:

$$V(j,t) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int^{a_0+i\infty}_{a_0-i\infty} e^{tp} \frac{V^\ast(j,p)}{p} \, dp$$

where $a_0$ is a real-valued constant chosen so that the integration path lies to the right of all singularities of the integrand.

:::
