# Bitcoin Satoshi Vision

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bitcoin Satoshi Vision"}

**Bitcoin Satoshi Vision (BSV)** is a cryptocurrency that is a [hard fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_fork) of [Bitcoin Cash](wikipedia://en/54679407).[^o3] Bitcoin Satoshi Vision was created in November 2018 by a group of individuals led by [Craig Steven Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Steven_Wright), who has claimed since 2015 to be [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto), the creator of the original bitcoin.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## History {#History}

### 2018 split from Bitcoin Cash {#2018_split_from_Bitcoin_Cash}

On 15 November 2018, a hard fork chain split of Bitcoin Cash occurred between two rival factions called Bitcoin Cash and Bitcoin SV.[^o8][^o9] On 15 November 2018 Bitcoin Cash traded at about [\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar)289, and Bitcoin SV traded at about \$96.50, down from \$425.01 on 14 November for the un-split Bitcoin Cash.[^o10]

The split originated from what was described as a "civil war" in two competing Bitcoin Cash camps.[^o11] The first camp, supported by entrepreneur [Roger Ver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ver) and [Jihan Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihan_Wu) of [Bitmain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitmain), promoted the software entitled Bitcoin ABC (short for Adjustable Blocksize Cap), which would maintain the block size at 32 [MB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabyte).[^o11] The second camp led by [Craig Steven Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Steven_Wright) and billionaire [Calvin Ayre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Ayre) put forth a competing software version Bitcoin SV, short for "Bitcoin Satoshi Vision", which would increase the block size limit to 128 MB.[^o8][^o11]

### 2019 de-listing from Binance {#2019_de-listing_from_Binance}

In April 2019, an online feud broke out between those who supported the claims of Bitcoin SV supporter Craig Wright that he was Satoshi Nakamoto, and those who did not.[^o13] The feud resulted in cryptocurrency exchange [Binance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binance) de-listing Bitcoin SV from their platform, stating that:[^o13]

At Binance, we periodically review each digital asset we list to ensure that it continues to meet the high level of standard we expect. When a coin or token no longer meets this standard, or the industry changes, we conduct a more in-depth review and potentially delist it. We believe this best protects all of our users.

When we conduct these reviews, we consider a variety of factors. Here are some that drive whether we decide to delist a digital asset:

Commitment of team to project

Level and quality of development activity

Network / smart contract stability

Level of public communication

Responsiveness to our periodic due diligence requests

Evidence of unethical / fraudulent conduct

Contribution to a healthy and sustainable crypto ecosystem

### 2021 network attack {#2021_network_attack}

In August 2021, Bitcoin SV suffered a [51% attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51%_attack), after previously suffering attacks in June and July of the same year.[^o3] Such an attack involves [cryptocurrency miners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptominer) gaining control of more than half of a network's computing power; these kinds of network attacks have the goal of preventing new transactions from gaining confirmations, allowing the attackers to [double-spend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-spending) coins. Adam James, senior editor at OKEx Insights claimed that "In the intermediate term, the attack has seemingly somewhat-negligible impact on its current price action," however "Faith in \[Bitcoin SV\] will likely be reduced following the incident."[^o3]

### 2024 high court ruling {#2024_high_court_ruling}

In March 2024, Mr Justice [James Mellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mellor_%28judge%29) in the British [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice) ruled that Wright is not Satoshi Nakamoto.[^o16][^o17]
