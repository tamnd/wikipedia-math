# Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator** is a type of [integral transform](wikipedia://en/430912). Specifically, given a [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) $\Omega$ in $\mathbf{R}^{n}$, any $k : \Omega \times \Omega \to \mathbf{C}$ such that

:::

$$\int_{\Omega} \int_{\Omega} | k(x, y) |^{2} \,dx \, dy < \infty ,$$

is called a **Hilbert–Schmidt kernel**. The associated [integral operator](wikipedia://en/430912) $T : L^{2}(\Omega) \to L^{2}(\Omega)$ given by

$$(Tf) (x) = \int_{\Omega} k(x, y) f(y) \, dy$$

is called a **Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator**.[^n1][^n2] $T$ is a [Hilbert–Schmidt operator](wikipedia://en/976673) with Hilbert–Schmidt norm

$$\Vert T \Vert_\mathrm{HS} = \Vert k \Vert_{L^2}.$$

Hilbert–Schmidt integral operators are both [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) and [compact](wikipedia://en/577441).[^n3]

The concept of a Hilbert–Schmidt integral operator may be extended to any [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) $X$ equipped with a positive [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322). If $L^{2}(X)$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), and $k$ belongs to $L^{2}(X \times X)$, then the operator $T : L^{2}(X) \to L^{2}(X)$ defined by

$$(Tf)(x) = \int_{X} k(x,y)f(y)\,dy$$

is [compact](wikipedia://en/577441). If

$$k(x,y) = \overline{k(y,x)},$$

then $T$ is also [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408) and so the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) applies. This is one of the fundamental constructions of such operators, which often reduces problems about infinite-dimensional vector spaces to questions about well-understood finite-dimensional eigenspaces.[^n4]
