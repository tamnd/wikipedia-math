# Prüfer domain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prüfer domain"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Prüfer domain** is a type of [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) that generalizes [Dedekind domains](wikipedia://en/145132) in a non-[Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) context. These [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) possess the nice [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977)- and [module](wikipedia://en/276410)-theoretic properties of Dedekind domains, but usually only for [finitely generated modules](wikipedia://en/326430). Prüfer domains are named after the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) mathematician [Heinz Prüfer](wikipedia://en/1655401).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The ring of [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372) on the open [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) $C$ is a Prüfer domain. The ring of [integer valued polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_valued_polynomial) with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) is a Prüfer domain, although the ring $\mathbb{Z}[X]$ of integer [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) is not (Narkiewicz 1995, 56). While every [number ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_ring) is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132), their union, the [ring of algebraic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_algebraic_integers), is a Prüfer domain. Just as a Dedekind domain is locally a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633), a Prüfer domain is locally a [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856), so that Prüfer domains act as non-noetherian analogues of Dedekind domains. Indeed, a [domain](wikipedia://en/15462) that is a [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of its [subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145) that are Prüfer domains is a Prüfer domain (Fuchs Salce 2001, 93–94).

Many Prüfer domains are also [Bézout domains](wikipedia://en/3131407), that is, not only are [finitely generated ideals](wikipedia://en/25977#Types_of_ideals) [projective](wikipedia://en/364488), they are even [free](wikipedia://en/326454) (that is, [principal](wikipedia://en/145126)). For instance, the ring of [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) on any non-compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) is a Bézout domain (Helmer 1940), and the ring of algebraic integers is Bézout.

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A **Prüfer domain** is a [semihereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semihereditary_ring) [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462). Equivalently, a Prüfer domain may be defined as a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) without [zero divisors](wikipedia://en/51441) in which every [non-zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_ideal) finitely generated [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) is invertible. Many different characterizations of Prüfer domains are known. Bourbaki lists fourteen of them, (Gilmer 1972) has around forty, and (Fontana Huckaba Papick 1997, 2) open with nine.

As a sample, the following conditions on an integral domain *R* are equivalent to *R* being a Prüfer domain, i.e. every finitely generated ideal of *R* is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488):

**Ideal arithmetic**

- Every non-zero finitely generated ideal *I* of *R* is **invertible**: i.e. *I* ⋅ <sup>−1</sup> = *R*, where $I^{-1} = \{r\in q(R): rI\subseteq R\}$ and *q*(*R*) is the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of *R*. Equivalently, every non-zero ideal generated by two elements is invertible.
- For any (finitely generated) non-zero ideals *I*, *J*, *K* of *R*, the following distributivity property holds:

$$I \cap (J + K) = (I \cap J) + (I \cap K).$$

- For any (finitely generated) ideals *I*, *J*, *K* of *R*, the following distributivity property holds:

$$I(J \cap K)=IJ \cap IK.$$

- For any (finitely generated) non-zero ideals *I*, *J* of *R*, the following property holds:

$$(I+J)(I \cap J) = IJ.$$

- For any finitely generated ideals *I*, *J*, *K* of *R*, if *IJ* = *IK* then *J* = *K* or *I* = 0.

**Localizations**

- For every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) *P* of *R*, the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) *R*<sub>*P*</sub> of *R* at *P* is a [valuation domain](wikipedia://en/966856).
- For every [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m* in *R*, the localization *R*<sub>*m*</sub> of *R* at *m* is a valuation domain.
- *R* is [integrally closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrally_closed_ring) and every [overring](wikipedia://en/24158853) of *R* (that is, a ring contained between *R* and its field of fractions) is the intersection of localizations of *R*.

**Flatness**

- Every [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/3039330) *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616).
- Every [torsionless](wikipedia://en/27481586) *R*-module is flat.
- Every ideal of *R* is flat.
- Every overring of *R* is *R*-flat.
- Every [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of a flat *R*-module is flat.
- If *M* and *N* are torsion-free *R*-modules then their [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) *M* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *N* is torsion-free.
- If *I* and *J* are two ideals of *R* then *I* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *J* is torsion-free.
- The [torsion submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_submodule) of every [finitely generated module](wikipedia://en/326430) is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) (Kaplansky 1960).

**Integral closure**

- Every overring of *R* is [integrally closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrally_closed_ring).
- *R* is integrally closed and there is some positive integer *n* such that for every *a*, *b* in *R* one has (*a*, *b*)<sup>*n*</sup> = (*a*<sup>*n*</sup>, *b*<sup>*n*</sup>).
- $R$ is integrally closed and each element of the quotient field *K* of *R* is a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) of a polynomial in *R*\[*x*\] whose coefficients generate *R* as an *R*-module (Gilmer Hoffmann 1975, 81).

## Properties {#Properties}

- A commutative ring is a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) if and only if it is a Prüfer domain and [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129).
- Though Prüfer domains need not be Noetherian, they must be [coherent](wikipedia://en/14777654), since finitely generated projective modules are [finitely related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_related_module).
- Though ideals of Dedekind domains can all be generated by two elements, for every positive integer *n*, there are Prüfer domains with finitely generated ideals that cannot be generated by fewer than *n* elements (Swan 1984). However, finitely generated maximal ideals of Prüfer domains are two-generated (Fontana Huckaba Papick 1997, 31).
- If *R* is a Prüfer domain and *K* is its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271), then any ring *S* such that *R* ⊆ *S* ⊆ *K* is a Prüfer domain.
- If *R* is a Prüfer domain, *K* is its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271), and *L* is an [algebraic extension field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_extension_field) of *K*, then the [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) of *R* in *L* is a Prüfer domain (Fuchs Salce 2001, 93).
- A finitely generated module *M* over a Prüfer domain is [projective](wikipedia://en/364488) if and only if it is torsion-free. In fact, this property characterizes Prüfer domains.
- (Gilmer–Hoffmann Theorem) Suppose that $R$ is an integral domain, $K$ its field of fractions, and $S$ is the integral closure of $R$ in $K$. Then $S$ is a Prüfer domain if and only if every element of $K$ is a root of a polynomial in $R[X]$ at least one of whose coefficients is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666) of $R$ (Gilmer Hoffmann 1975, Theorem 2).
- A commutative domain is a Dedekind domain if and only if the torsion submodule is a direct summand whenever it is bounded (*M* is bounded means *rM* = 0 for some *r* in *R*), (Chase 1960). Similarly, a commutative domain is a Prüfer domain if and only if the torsion submodule is a direct summand whenever it is finitely generated (Kaplansky 1960).

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

More generally, a **Prüfer ring** is a commutative ring in which every non-zero finitely generated ideal containing a non-zero-divisor is invertible (that is, projective).

A commutative ring is said to be [arithmetical](wikipedia://en/22284234) if for every [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m* in *R*, the localization *R*<sub>*m*</sub> of *R* at *m* is a [chain ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_ring). With this definition, a Prüfer domain is an arithmetical domain. In fact, an arithmetical domain is the same thing as a Prüfer domain.

Non-commutative right or left semihereditary domains could also be considered as generalizations of Prüfer domains.
