# Ciesielski isomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ciesielski isomorphism"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Ciesielski's isomorphism** establishes an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) of [Hölder continuous](wikipedia://en/4005892) functions $C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R})$, equipped with a [norm](wikipedia://en/990534), and the space of [bounded sequences](wikipedia://en/1701075) $\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$, equipped with the [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm), by [coefficients of a Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662) along a sequence of dyadic partitions.

:::

The statement was proved in 1960 by the Polish mathematician [Zbigniew Ciesielski](wikipedia://en/57933971).[^o2] The result can be applied in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) when dealing with paths of the [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436).[^o3]

## Ciesielski's isomorphism {#Ciesielski's_isomorphism}

Let $[0,T]$ be an intervall and let $\mathbb{T}=(\tau_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ be a sequence of dyadic partitions of $[0,T]$.

Let $C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R})$ for $0<\alpha<1$ be a Banach space of Hölder continuous functions with norm

$$\|f\|_{C^{\alpha}}=\|f\|_{\infty}+|f|_{C^{\alpha}}:=\sup\limits_{t\in [0,T]}|f(t)|+\sup\limits_{\begin{matrix}s,t\in[0,T]\\ s\neq t\end{matrix}}\frac{|f(s)-f(t)|}{|s-t|^{\alpha}}$$

and $\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ be the Banach space of bounded sequence with [supremum norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum_norm)

$$\|a\|_{\infty}:=\sup\limits_{n}|a_n|.$$

The map $S\colon C^{\alpha}([0,T],\mathbb{R})\to \ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ defined as

$$f\mapsto \left(2^{(m+1)\left(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}|\theta(f)^{\mathbb{T}}_{m,k}| \right)_{m,k},\qquad (m,k)\in\mathbb{N}_0\times \{0,1,\dots,2^m-1\}$$

is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), where $\theta(f)^{\mathbb{T}}_{m,k}$ are the Schauder coefficients of $f$ along $\mathbb{T}$ of $[0,T]$.

The Schauder coefficients are

$$\theta(f)^{\mathbb{T}}_{m,k}=\langle f',h_{m,k} \rangle_{L^1}=\int_0^T f'(x)h_{m,k}(x)dx.$$

for [Haar functions](wikipedia://en/50905) $h_{m,k}$ based on the dyadic partition $\tau_m$.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

- The result was generalized in 2025 for general partitions.[^o7]

:::
