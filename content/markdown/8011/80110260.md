# Roy Dyckhoff

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Roy Dyckhoff"}

**Roy Dyckhoff** (4 March 1948 – 23 August 2018) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) who worked in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) in the Department of Pure Mathematics and later Computer Science at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews).[^o4] He is known for his discovery in 1992 of a [terminating](wikipedia://en/913118) [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329) for [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262) [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus).[^o5][^o6] His [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) was 3.[^o7]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Roy Dyckhoff was born on 4 March 1948, in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), to Eric Bernard Charles Dyckhoff, a solicitor, and Muriel Edith Turner. He had an older sister.[^o9] His mother died on 6 October 1955, when he was seven years old. Later remarrying in 1959,[^o10] his father ran the law firm Dyckhoff and Johnson in [Cheadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheadle,_Greater_Manchester) and founded the Cheadle Civic Society.[^o11]

Raised in [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire), he spent his youth at boarding school [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College).[^o12] Dyckhoff grew an interest in church [bell-ringing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell-ringer), joining a group of ringers there.[^o13] Dyckhoff spent a year programming punch-cards at [English Electric Leo Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_Leo_Marconi).[^o4] Enrolling in an undergraduate program at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge) in 1966, he studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) but also spent a year attending only lectures in [Middle Eastern studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_studies).[^o14] During his time at King's College, he rang a [peal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peal) at [Trumpington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpington) with a band of first year students.[^o13]

After receiving his undergraduate degree, he pursued further studies at [New College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_College,_Oxford), completing a [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in Mathematics in 1974. His dissertation, *Topics in General Topology: Bicategories, Projective Covers, Perfect Mappings and Resolutions of Sheaves*, was supervised by Peter J. Collins and [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875).[^o2] He was later appointed Fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford).[^o14]

## Career {#Career}

In 1975, Dyckhoff became a lecturer in the Department of Pure Mathematics at [St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), later moving to Computer Science in 1981[^o12] due to the reduced funding under the [Thatcher government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher).[^o14] Early in his scientific career, he contributed to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).[^o2][^o16] Applying his experience in programming and interest in formal logic, he shifted to [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) and [logic](wikipedia://en/19636) where he came to specialise in [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) and [automated theorem proving](wikipedia://en/2546).[^o14]

His investigations into [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262) led him to discover the contraction-free [intuitionistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionistic) [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329) G4ip in 1992.[^o5][^o17] The contraction rule was known to be problematic for [backward proof-searches](wikipedia://en/2546) as it can cause unwanted loops. By producing a contraction-free calculus and proving the admissibility of contraction, Dyckhoff provided the first [terminating](wikipedia://en/913118) [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262) [propositional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus) [sequent calculus](wikipedia://en/252329). Such a calculus was anticipated in 1950s by the founder of the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) school of [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), [Nikolai Vorobyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Nikolayevich_Vorobyov_%28mathematician%29). Dyckhoff's calculus laid the groundwork for subsequent terminating proof systems.[^o18] Additionally he settled a question posed by [Georg Kreisel](wikipedia://en/11761785) in 1971 on the relationship between cut-elimination, substitution and normalisation. He co-authored several papers with [Sara Negri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Negri) on topics involving [intermediate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_logics) and [modal logics](wikipedia://en/333365).[^o14]

His later works investigated [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic) and [Stoic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoic_logic). With [Susanne Bobzien](wikipedia://en/33162494) he proved the decidability of Stoic logics in a Hertz-[Gentzen](wikipedia://en/46027) system.[^o19]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Dyckhoff married Cecilia Meredith in 1970,[^o14] and the couple spent their time between St Andrews and Glengarry, [Invergarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invergarry) in the [Scottish Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Highlands). They had two children: a daughter, Livia, and a son.[^o21][^o22] As a Tower captain at the [St Salvator's Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Salvator's_Chapel), Dyckhoff rang for various services, occasionally involving his wife and children in the ringing.[^o23] He was involved in the installation of additional bells in 2003 and later in 2010 to commemorate the consecration of the chapel and the founding of the university.[^o13] He was also an avid hiker and bell-ringer in his free time, and supported the [Mountain Bothies Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Bothies_Association).[^o12]

Dyckhoff died on 23 August 2018 from an [acute myeloid leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_myeloid_leukemia).[^o14][^o24]
