# Integer broom topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Integer broom topology"}

In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **integer broom topology** is an example of a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on the so-called integer broom space *X*.[^o2]

:::

## Definition of the integer broom space {#Definition_of_the_integer_broom_space}

*Image: Integer Broom Plot FBN.gif.* A subset of the integer broom

The **integer broom space** *X* is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the plane **R**<sup>2</sup>. Assume that the plane is parametrised by [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates). The integer broom contains the origin and the points (*n*, θ) ∈ **R**<sup>2</sup> such that *n* is a non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) and *θ* ∈ {1/*k* : *k* ∈ **Z**<sup>\+</sup>}, where **Z**<sup>\+</sup> is the set of positive integers.[^o2] The image on the right gives an illustration for 0 ≤ *n* ≤ 5 and 1/15 ≤ θ ≤ 1. Geometrically, the space consists of a collection of [convergent sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_sequence). For a fixed *n*, we have a sequence of points − lying on circle with centre (0, 0) and radius *n* − that converges to the point (*n*, 0).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition of the integer broom topology"}

## Definition of the integer broom topology {#Definition_of_the_integer_broom_topology}

We define the topology on *X* by means of a [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910). The integer broom space is given by the polar coordinates

$$(n, \theta) \in \{ n \in \mathbb{Z} : n \ge 0 \} \times \{ \theta = 1/k : k \in \mathbb{Z}^{+} \} \, .$$

Let us write (*n*,θ) ∈ *U* × *V* for simplicity. The integer broom topology on *X* is the product topology induced by giving *U* the [right order topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_order_topology), and *V* the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) from **R**.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The integer broom space, together with the integer broom topology, is a [compact topological space](wikipedia://en/6042). It is a [T<sub>0</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_space), but it is neither a [T<sub>1</sub> space](wikipedia://en/148721) nor a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). The space is [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected), while neither [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected) nor [arc connected](wikipedia://en/6233#Arc_connectedness).[^o8]
