# Hecke algebra of a pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hecke algebra of a pair"}

In mathematics, the **Hecke algebra** of a pair (*G*, *K*) of [locally compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact) or [reductive Lie groups](wikipedia://en/1088386) is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) of [measures](wikipedia://en/19873) under [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519). It can also be defined for a pair (*g*, *K*) of a maximal compact subgroup *K* of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) with [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) *g*, in which case the Hecke algebra is an algebra with an [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628), whose approximately unital modules are the same as *K*-finite representations of the pairs (*g*, *K*).

:::

The Hecke algebra of a pair is a generalization of the [classical Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907189) studied by [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990), which corresponds to the case (GL<sub>2</sub>(**Q**), GL<sub>2</sub>(**Z**)).

## Locally compact group and a compact subgroup {#Locally_compact_group_and_a_compact_subgroup}

Let (*G*, *K*) be a pair consisting of a [unimodular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodular_group) [locally compact topological group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_compact_topological_group) *G* and a closed compact subgroup *K* of *G*. Then the space of bi-*K*-invariant [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) of [compact support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support)

*C*<sub>*c*</sub><sup>∞</sup>\[*K*\\*G*/*K*\]

can be endowed with a structure of an associative algebra under the operation of [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519).[^o3] This algebra is often denoted

*H*(*G*//*K*)

and called the **Hecke algebra** of the pair (*G*, *K*).

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

If (*G*, *K*) is as above, then the Hecke algebra is commutative if and only if (*G*, *K*) is a [Gelfand pair](wikipedia://en/3018619).

:::

## Reductive Lie groups and Lie algebras {#Reductive_Lie_groups_and_Lie_algebras}

In 1979, Daniel Flath gave a similar construction for general reductive Lie groups *G*.[^o7] The Hecke algebra of a pair (*g*, *K*) of a Lie algebra *g* with Lie group *G* and maximal compact subgroup *K* is the algebra of *K*-finite distributions on *G* with support in *K*, with the product given by [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519).[^o8][^o9]

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Finite groups {#Finite_groups}

When *G* is a finite group and *K* is any subgroup of *G*, then [the Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907272) is spanned by [double cosets](wikipedia://en/982771) of *K*\\*G*/*K*.

### SL(*n*) over a *p*-adic field {#SL(n)_over_a_p-adic_field}

For the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) over the [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423),

*G* = SL<sub>*n*</sub>(**Q**<sub>*p*</sub>) and *K* = SL<sub>*n*</sub>(**Z**<sub>*p*</sub>),

the representations of the corresponding commutative Hecke ring were studied by [Ian G. Macdonald](wikipedia://en/3100378).

### GL(2) over the rationals {#GL(2)_over_the_rationals}

For the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) over the [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers),

*G* = GL<sub>2</sub>(**Q**) and *K* = GL<sub>2</sub>(**Z**)

the Hecke algebra of the pair (*G*, *K*) is the [classical Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/55907189), which is the commutative ring of [Hecke operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_operators) in the theory of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms).

### Iwahori {#Iwahori}

The case leading to the [Iwahori–Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/521808) of a finite Weyl group is when *G* is the finite [Chevalley group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_group) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with *p*<sup>*k*</sup> elements, and *B* is its [Borel subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356). Iwahori showed that the Hecke ring

*H*(*G*//*B*)

is obtained from the generic Hecke algebra *H*<sub>*q*</sub> of the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) *W* of *G* by specializing the indeterminate *q* of the latter algebra to *p*<sup>*k*</sup>, the cardinality of the finite field. [George Lusztig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lusztig) remarked in 1984:[^o16] I think it would be most appropriate to call it the Iwahori algebra, but the name Hecke ring (or algebra) given by Iwahori himself has been in use for almost 20 years and it is probably too late to change it now.

Iwahori and Matsumoto (1965) considered the case when *G* is a group of points of a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) over a non-archimedean [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *F*, such as *Q*<sub>*p*</sub>, and *K* is what is now called an [Iwahori subgroup](wikipedia://en/32092579) of *G*. The resulting Hecke ring is isomorphic to the Hecke algebra of the [affine Weyl group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Weyl_group) of *G*, or the [affine Hecke algebra](wikipedia://en/10361630), where the indeterminate *q* has been specialized to the cardinality of the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) of *F*.
