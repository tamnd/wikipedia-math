# Artin's criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin's criterion"}

In mathematics, **Artin's criteria**[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] are a collection of related [necessary and sufficient conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_and_sufficient_condition) on deformation functors which prove the representability of these functors as either [algebraic spaces](wikipedia://en/2394027)[^o6] or as [algebraic stacks](wikipedia://en/717377). In particular, these conditions are used in the construction of the [moduli stack of elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/49905706)[^o7] and the construction of the [moduli stack of pointed curves](wikipedia://en/3010057).[^o8]

:::

## Notation and technical notes {#Notation_and_technical_notes}

Throughout this article, let $S$ be a scheme of [finite-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_type_scheme) over a field $k$ or an [excellent DVR](wikipedia://en/10016360). $p:F \to (Sch/S)$ will be a [category fibered in groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_fibered_in_groupoids), $F(X)$ will be the groupoid lying over $X \to S$.

A stack $F$ is called **limit preserving** if it is compatible with filtered direct limits in $Sch/S$, meaning given a filtered system $\{X_i\}_{i\in I}$ there is an equivalence of categories

> $$\lim_{\rightarrow}F(X_i) \to F(\lim_{\rightarrow}X_i)$$

An element of $x \in F(X)$ is called an **algebraic element** if it is the [henselization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henselization) of an $\mathcal{O}_S$-algebra of finite type.

A limit preserving stack $F$ over $Sch/S$ is called an **algebraic stack** if

1. For any pair of elements $x \in F(X), y \in F(Y)$ the fiber product $X\times_F Y$ is represented as an algebraic space
1. There is a scheme $X \to S$ locally of finite type, and an element $x \in F(X)$ which is smooth and surjective such that for any $y \in F(Y)$ the induced map $X\times_F Y \to Y$ is smooth and surjective.
