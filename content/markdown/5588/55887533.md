# Émile Merlin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Émile Merlin"}

**Émile Alphonse Louis Merlin** (12 October 1875 in [Mons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mons,_Belgium) – 29 July 1938 in [Le Bourg d'Oisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Bourg_d'Oisans)) was a Belgian mathematician and astronomer.

:::

Merlin attended the secondary school *Athénée royal de Bruxelles*. He then studied at the University of Liège and the [University of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent_University), where in 1900 he received his doctorate in mathematics. This was followed by a stay abroad between 1901 and 1903 in Paris at the Sorbonne, at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) and in Göttingen. In 1904, he became an assistant at the observatory in [Uccle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uccle). In 1909, he was promoted to astronomer adjoint. From 1912, he was a lecturer on astronomy and geodesy at the University of Ghent and in 1919, he became a full professor and director of the geographical station of the University of Ghent.[^o3]

He was an alpinist and died in a mountain accident in the French Alps in Le Bourg d'Oisans.[^o3]

Merlin was one of the editors for the French edition of [Klein's encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_encyclopedia).[^o4]

For his work on celestial mechanics, he was awarded the A. de Potter Prize of the Royal Belgian Academy of Sciences. He was president of the Belgian Mathematical Society, an honorary member of the Astronomical Society of Mexico and a member of the Commission for Mathematics and Astronomy of the National Fund for Research in Spain.[^o3] He was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Toronto 1924 (*Sur les lignes asymptotiques en géométrie infinitésimale*) and Oslo 1936 (*Sur certains mouvements des fluid parfaits*).
