# Carol S. Carson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carol S. Carson"}

**Carol Stine Carson** is an American [economic statistician](wikipedia://en/340363), the former director of the U.S. [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis) and former director of statistics at the [International Monetary Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Monetary_Fund).

:::

Carol Stine did her undergraduate studies at the [College of Wooster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Wooster), graduating in 1961, and was vice president of the [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) honor society [Pi Sigma Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_Sigma_Alpha) there. She earned a master's degree at the [Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletcher_School_of_Law_and_Diplomacy) of [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), and completed her studies with a Ph.D. from [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University). Her dissertation, *The History of the United States Income and Product Accounts: The Development of an Analytical Tool*, was written under the supervision of [John Whitefield Kendrick](wikipedia://en/30165631) in 1971, and concerned "the history of [national accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_accounts) in the U.S.". It followed Kendrick in treating these accounts as an increasingly-honed "tool for [macroeconomic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics)".

In 1972, Carson joined the Bureau of Economic Analysis. Before becoming its director, she was also chief statistician there, and editor-in-chief of their journal, *[Survey of Current Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_of_Current_Business)*. During this time, she also assisted with the 1993 revision of the [United Nations System of National Accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_National_Accounts).

In 1991, Carson won the Julius Shiskin Memorial Award for Economic Statistics of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) Business and Economics Statistics Section, "for her leadership in developing and refining the economic statistical base of the U.S. and for her contributions to the development of the revised U.N. System of National Accounts". She is also an elected member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute).
