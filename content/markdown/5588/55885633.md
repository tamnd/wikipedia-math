# Nikolaos Hatzidakis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolaos Hatzidakis"}

*Image: Nikolaus Hatzidakis.jpg.*

**Nikolaos J. Hatzidakis** (Νικόλαος Χατζιδάκις, also *Nicholas Hadzidakis*, 25 April 1872 – 25 January 1942) was a Greek mathematician.[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Hatzidakis was born in 1872 in Berlin. His parents were from [Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete). He attended secondary school in Athens and studied mathematics at the [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens) where he was awarded a Ph.D. in mathematics. He continued his studies in Paris, Gothenburg and Berlin.

He returned to Greece and was appointed professor of theoretical mechanics and astronomy at the [Hellenic Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Military_Academy), where he taught from 1900 to 1904. He was a professor ordinarius of mathematics at the [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Athens) from 1904 until his retirement in 1939 as professor emeritus.[^o4] He also taught at the [Hellenic Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Naval_Academy).

He was an internationally recognized expert on the mathematics of the kinematic equations of surfaces and was a founding member of the [Hellenic Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/945503). He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1912 at Cambridge UK,[^o5] in 1920 at Strasbourg,[^o6] in 1928 at Bologna,[^o7] and in 1932 in Zurich.

Nikolaos Hatzidakis was a dedicated Greek patriot. In 1897 during the [Greco-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_%281897%29), he interrupted his studies in Paris to take part in the [rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_Revolt_%281897%29) against Ottoman rule that had begun in his family's homeland of Crete. During the [Axis occupation of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_occupation_of_Greece) in World War II, although he knew German and Italian, he did not cooperate with the occupying powers and died of starvation in the [Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_%28Greece%29), on 25 January 1942 in Athens.

His brother was the linguist [Georgios Hatzidakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgios_Hatzidakis) and his daughter was the writer, translator and critic Foula Hatzidaki (1906–1984).[^o9] Nikolaos Hatzidakis was interested in literature, linguistics and foreign languages. He published poetry in prestigious journals under a pseudonym and was said to speak thirteen languages. He published mathematical papers in Greek, German, French, English, Italian, and Danish.[^o4] His father was the noted mathematician [Ioannis "John" Hatzidakis](wikipedia://en/55894972) (with the last name commonly transliterated as *Hazzidakis*).
