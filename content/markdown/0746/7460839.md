# Carsten Thomassen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carsten Thomassen"}

**Carsten Thomassen** (born August 22, 1948 in [Grindsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grindsted)) is a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He has been a Professor of Mathematics at the [Technical University of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Denmark) since 1981, and since 1990 a member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters). His research concerns [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) and more specifically graph theory.[^o2]

:::

Thomassen received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1976 from the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo).

He is editor-in-chief of the *[Journal of Graph Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Graph_Theory)* and the *[Electronic Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Journal_of_Combinatorics)*, and editor of *[Combinatorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorica)*, the *[Journal of Combinatorial Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Combinatorial_Theory)* Series B, [Discrete Mathematics](wikipedia://en/21988115), and the *[European Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Combinatorics)*.

He was awarded the Dedicatory Award of the 6th International Conference on the Theory and Applications of Graphs by the [Western Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Michigan_University) in May 1988, the [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 1993,[^o4] and the Faculty of Mathematics Alumni Achievement Medal by the University of Waterloo in 2005. In 1990, he was an invited speaker (*Graphs, random walks and electric networks*) at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Kyōto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyōto). He was included on the [ISI Web of Knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_Web_of_Knowledge) list of the 250 most cited mathematicians.[^o5]

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

- with Bojan Mohar: *Graphs on Surfaces*, Johns Hopkins University Press, 2001
- 5-choosability of planar graphs (see [List coloring](wikipedia://en/690669))
- works on [Hypohamiltonian graphs](wikipedia://en/18301304)
- Hamilton connectivity of Tournaments (see [Tournament (graph theory)](wikipedia://en/1427502)) and of 4-connected planar graphs
- his proof of [Gr%C3%B6tzsch%27s theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%B6tzsch%27s_theorem)
