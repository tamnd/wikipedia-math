# Pythagorean means

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pythagorean means"}

*Image: MathematicalMeans.svg.* A geometric construction of the quadratic mean and the Pythagorean means (of two numbers *a* and *b*). Harmonic mean denoted by purple*H*, geometric by blue*G*, arithmetic by red*A* and quadratic mean (also known as [root mean square](wikipedia://en/66819)) denoted by green*Q*.

*Image: Comparison\_Pythagorean\_means.svg.* Comparison of the arithmetic, geometric and harmonic means of a pair of numbers. The vertical dashed lines are [asymptotes](wikipedia://en/3107) for the harmonic means.

In mathematics, the three classical **Pythagorean means** are the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) (AM), the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) (GM), and the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) (HM). These [means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) were studied with proportions by [Pythagoreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoreans) and later generations of Greek mathematicians[^o2] because of their importance in geometry and music. They can now be regarded as special cases of a family of functions appropriately called the [generalized means](wikipedia://en/13143).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The three Pythagorean means are defined by the equations

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{AM} \left( x_1,\; \ldots,\; x_n \right) &= \frac{ x_1 + \;\cdots\; + x_n }{n}, \\[9pt] \operatorname{GM} \left( x_1,\; \ldots,\; x_n \right) &= \sqrt[n]{\left\vert x_1 \times \,\cdots\, \times x_n \right\vert}, \text{ and} \\[9pt] \operatorname{HM} \left( x_1,\; \ldots,\; x_n \right) &= \frac{n}{\displaystyle \frac{1}{x_1} + \;\cdots\; + \frac{1}{x_n}} . \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Each mean, $\operatorname{M}$, has the following properties for positive real inputs:

**First-order [homogeneity](wikipedia://en/622844)**

$$\operatorname{M}(bx_1, \ldots, bx_n) = b \operatorname{M}(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$$

This ensures the physical value of the mean must be the same, for any choice of a ratio-scale for its units.

**Invariance under exchange**

$$\operatorname{M}(\ldots, x_i, \ldots, x_j, \ldots) = \operatorname{M}(\ldots, x_j, \ldots, x_i, \ldots)$$

for any $i$ and $j$. This ensures that the mean is a symmetric function whose value does not depend upon the order of its arguments.

**Monotonicity**

if $a \leq b$ then $\operatorname{M}(a,x_1,x_2,\ldots x_n) \leq \operatorname{M}(b,x_1,x_2,\ldots x_n)$

**[Idempotence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent)**

$M(x,x,\ldots x) = x$ for all $x$

Monotonicity and idempotence together imply that a mean of a set always lies between the extremes of the set:

$$\min(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \leq \operatorname{M}(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \leq \max(x_1, \ldots, x_n).$$

The harmonic and arithmetic means are reciprocal duals of each other for positive arguments,

$$\operatorname{HM}\left(\frac{1}{x_1}, \ldots, \frac{1}{x_n}\right) = \frac{1}{\operatorname{AM}\left(x_1, \ldots, x_n\right)},$$

while the geometric mean is its own reciprocal dual:

$$\operatorname{GM}\left(\frac{1}{x_1}, \ldots, \frac{1}{x_n}\right) = \frac{1}{\operatorname{GM}\left(x_1, \ldots, x_n\right)}.$$

:::

## Inequalities among means {#Inequalities_among_means}

There is an ordering to these means (if all of the $x_i$ are positive)

$$\min \leq \operatorname{HM} \leq \operatorname{GM} \leq \operatorname{AM} \leq \max$$

with equality holding [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the $x_i$ are all equal.

This is a generalization of the [inequality of arithmetic and geometric means](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_of_arithmetic_and_geometric_means) and a special case of an inequality for generalized means. The proof follows from the [arithmetic–geometric mean inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inequality_of_arithmetic_and_geometric_means), $\operatorname{AM} \leq \max$, and reciprocal duality ($\min$ and $\max$ are also reciprocal dual to each other).

The study of the Pythagorean means is closely related to the study of [majorization](wikipedia://en/2864495) and [Schur-convex functions](wikipedia://en/3897244). The harmonic and geometric means are concave symmetric functions of their arguments, and hence Schur-concave, while the arithmetic mean is a linear function of its arguments and hence is both concave and convex.

## History {#History}

Almost everything that we know about the Pythagorean means came from arithmetic handbooks written in the first and second century. [Nicomachus of Gerasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachus_of_Gerasa) says that they were "acknowledged by all the ancients, Pythagoras, Plato and Aristotle."[^o9] Their earliest known use is a fragment of the Pythagorean philosopher [Archytas of Tarentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archytas_of_Tarentum):

The name "harmonic mean", according to [Iamblichus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iamblichus), was coined by Archytas and [Hippasus](wikipedia://en/920575). The Pythagorean means also appear in [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s [Timaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_%28dialogue%29). Another evidence of their early use is a commentary by [Pappus](wikipedia://en/509300).

The term "mean" (Ancient Greek μεσότης, *mesótēs*) appears in the [Neopythagorean](wikipedia://en/1128528) arithmetic handbooks in connection with the term "proportion" (Ancient Greek ἀναλογία, *analogía*).

## Smallest distinct positive integer means {#Smallest_distinct_positive_integer_means}

*Smallest distinct positive integer means*

| *a* | *b* | HM | GM | AM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 45 | 9 | 15 | 25 |
| 10 | 40 | 16 | 20 | 25 |

:::

*Image: Pythagorean\_means\_nomograms.svg.* [Nomograms](wikipedia://en/271164) to graphically calculate arithmetic (1), geometric (2) and harmonic (3) means, *z* of *x*=40 and *y*=10 (red), and *x*=45 and *y*=5 (blue)

Of all pairs of different natural numbers of the form (*a*, *b*) such that *a* \< *b*, the smallest (as defined by least value of *a* + *b*) for which the arithmetic, geometric and harmonic means are all also natural numbers are (5, 45) and (10, 40).[^o14]

For the pair of natural numbers, the arithmetic mean is a natural number when the sum is even. However, the mean is not a natural number when the sum is odd.

For the pair of natural numbers, the geometric mean is rational, esp. a natural number, when the product is a perfect square, esp. a natural perfect square number. However, the mean is irrational when the product is not a perfect square, esp. when the product is not a natural perfect square number.

For the pair of natural numbers, the harmonic mean is a natural number when twice the product is divisible by the sum. However, the mean is not a natural number when twice the product is not divisible by the sum.
