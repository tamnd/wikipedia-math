# List of cohomology theories

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of cohomology theories"}

This is a list of some of the ordinary and [generalized (or extraordinary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_cohomology_theory) homology and cohomology theories in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) that are defined on the categories of [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) or [spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29). For other sorts of homology theories see the links at the end of this article.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

- $S=\pi=S^0$ is the sphere spectrum.
- $S^n$ is the spectrum of the $n$-dimensional sphere

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="suspension"}

- $S^nY=S^n\land Y$ is the $n$th [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702) of a spectrum $Y$.

:::

- $[X,Y]$ is the abelian group of morphisms from the spectrum $X$ to the spectrum $Y$, given (roughly) as homotopy classes of maps.
- $$[X,Y]_n=[S^nX,Y]$$
- $[X,Y]_*$ is the graded abelian group given as the sum of the groups $[X,Y]_n$.
- $\pi_n(X)=[S^n,X]=[S,X]_n$ is the $n$th stable homotopy group of $X$.
- $\pi_*(X)$ is the sum of the groups $\pi_n(X)$, and is called the **coefficient ring** of $X$ when $X$ is a ring spectrum.

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Smash product"}

- $X\land Y$ is the [smash product](wikipedia://en/698010) of two spectra.

:::

If $X$ is a spectrum, then it defines generalized homology and cohomology theories on the category of spectra as follows:

- $X_n(Y)=[S,X\land Y]_n=[S^n,X\wedge Y]$ is the generalized homology of $Y$,
- $X^n(Y)=[Y,X]_{-n}=[S^{-n}Y, X]$ is the generalized cohomology of $Y$

:::

## Ordinary homology theories {#Ordinary_homology_theories}

These are the theories satisfying the "dimension axiom" of the [Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms](wikipedia://en/3122600) that the homology of a point vanishes in dimension other than 0. They are determined by an [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) coefficient group $G$, and denoted by $H(X,G)$ (where $G$ is sometimes omitted, especially if it is $\mathbb{Z}$). Usually $G$ is the integers, the rationals, the reals, the complex numbers, or the integers mod a prime $p$.

The cohomology functors of ordinary cohomology theories are represented by [Eilenberg–MacLane spaces](wikipedia://en/1073230).

On simplicial complexes, these theories coincide with [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) and cohomology.

### Homology and cohomology with integer coefficients. {#Homology_and_cohomology_with_integer_coefficients.}

**Spectrum:** $H$ ([Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum](wikipedia://en/12401224) of the integers.)

**Coefficient ring:** $\pi_n(H)=\mathbb{Z}$ if $n=0$, $0$ otherwise.

The original homology theory.

### Homology and cohomology with rational (or real or complex) coefficients. {#Homology_and_cohomology_with_rational_(or_real_or_complex)_coefficients.}

**Spectrum:** $HQ$ (Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectrum of the rationals.)

**Coefficient ring:** $\pi_n(HQ)=\mathbb{Q}$ if $n=0$, $0$ otherwise.

These are the easiest of all homology theories. The homology groups $HQ_n(X)$ are often denoted by $H_n(X,Q)$. The homology groups $H(X,\mathbb{Q})$, $H(X,\mathbb{R})$, $H(X,\mathbb{C})$ with [rational](wikipedia://en/19727024), [real](wikipedia://en/20646438), and [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) coefficients are all similar, and are used mainly when torsion is not of interest (or too complicated to work out). The [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition) writes the complex cohomology of a complex [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) as a sum of [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) groups.

### Homology and cohomology with mod *p* coefficients. {#Homology_and_cohomology_with_mod_p_coefficients.}

**Spectrum:** $HZ_p$ (Eilenberg–Maclane spectrum of the integers mod $p$.)

**Coefficient ring:** $\pi_n(HZ_p)=\mathbb{Z}_p$ (integers mod $p$) if $n=0$, $0$ otherwise.

## K-theories {#K-theories}

The simpler [K-theories](wikipedia://en/246748) of a space are often related to [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over the space, and different sorts of K-theories correspond to different structures that can be put on a vector bundle.

### Real K-theory {#Real_K-theory}

**Spectrum:** KO

**Coefficient ring:** The coefficient groups π<sub>*i*</sub>(KO) have period 8 in *i*, given by the sequence **Z**, **Z**<sub>2</sub>, **Z**<sub>2</sub>,0, **Z**, 0, 0, 0, repeated. As a ring, it is generated by a class *η* in degree 1, a class *x*<sub>4</sub> in degree 4, and an invertible class *v*<sub>1</sub><sup>4</sup> in degree 8, subject to the relations that 2*η* = *η*<sup>3</sup> = *ηx*<sub>4</sub> = 0, and *x*<sub>4</sub><sup>2</sup> = 4*v*<sub>1</sub><sup>4</sup>.

KO<sup>0</sup>(*X*) is the ring of stable equivalence classes of real vector bundles over *X*. [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) implies that the K-groups have period 8.

### Complex K-theory {#Complex_K-theory}

**Spectrum:** KU (even terms BU or **Z** × BU, odd terms *U*).

**Coefficient ring:** The coefficient ring *K*<sup>\*</sup>(point) is the ring of [Laurent polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_polynomials) in a generator of degree 2.

*K*<sup>0</sup>(*X*) is the ring of stable equivalence classes of complex vector bundles over *X*. [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) implies that the K-groups have period 2.

### Quaternionic K-theory {#Quaternionic_K-theory}

**Spectrum:** KSp

**Coefficient ring:** The coefficient groups π<sub>*i*</sub>(KSp) have period 8 in *i*, given by the sequence **Z**, 0, 0, 0,**Z**, **Z**<sub>2</sub>, **Z**<sub>2</sub>,0, repeated.

KSp<sup>0</sup>(*X*) is the ring of stable equivalence classes of quaternionic vector bundles over *X*. [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity) implies that the K-groups have period 8.

### K theory with coefficients {#K_theory_with_coefficients}

**Spectrum:** KG

*G* is some abelian group; for example the localization **Z**<sub>(*p*)</sub> at the prime *p*. Other K-theories can also be given coefficients.

### Self conjugate K-theory {#Self_conjugate_K-theory}

**Spectrum:** KSC

**Coefficient ring:** *to be written...*

The coefficient groups $\pi_i$(KSC) have period 4 in *i*, given by the sequence **Z**, **Z**<sub>2</sub>, 0, **Z**, repeated. Introduced by Donald W. Anderson in his unpublished 1964 [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) Ph.D. dissertation, "A new cohomology theory".

### Connective K-theories {#Connective_K-theories}

**Spectrum:** ku for connective K-theory, ko for connective real K-theory.

**Coefficient ring:** For ku, the coefficient ring is the ring of polynomials over *Z* on a single class *v*<sub>1</sub> in dimension 2. For ko, the coefficient ring is the quotient of a polynomial ring on three generators, *η* in dimension 1, *x*<sub>4</sub> in dimension 4, and *v*<sub>1</sub><sup>4</sup> in dimension 8, the periodicity generator, modulo the relations that 2*η* = 0, *x*<sub>4</sub><sup>2</sup> = 4*v*<sub>1</sub><sup>4</sup>, *η*<sup>3</sup> = 0, and *ηx* = 0.

Roughly speaking, this is K-theory with the negative dimensional parts killed off.

### KR-theory {#KR-theory}

This is a cohomology theory defined for spaces with involution, from which many of the other K-theories can be derived.

## Bordism and cobordism theories {#Bordism_and_cobordism_theories}

[Cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) studies [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), where a manifold is regarded as "trivial" if it is the boundary of another compact manifold. The cobordism classes of manifolds form a ring that is usually the coefficient ring of some generalized cohomology theory. There are many such theories, corresponding roughly to the different structures that one can put on a manifold.

The functors of cobordism theories are often represented by [Thom spaces](wikipedia://en/1243451) of certain groups.

::: {#o20 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Stable cohomotopy theory"}

### [Stable homotopy](wikipedia://en/2946655) and [cohomotopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomotopy) {#Stable_homotopy_and_cohomotopy}

**Spectrum:** S ([sphere spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118593)).

**Coefficient ring:** The coefficient groups π<sub>*n*</sub>(*S*) are the [stable homotopy groups of spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_groups_of_spheres), which are notoriously hard to compute or understand for *n* > 0. (For *n* \< 0 they vanish, and for *n* = 0 the group is **Z**.)

Stable homotopy is closely related to cobordism of [framed manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framed_manifold) (manifolds with a trivialization of the normal bundle).

:::

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Unoriented cobordism ring"}

### Unoriented cobordism {#Unoriented_cobordism}

**Spectrum:** MO ([Thom spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_spectrum) of [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954))

**Coefficient ring:** π<sub>\*</sub>(MO) is the ring of cobordism classes of unoriented manifolds, and is a polynomial ring over the field with 2 elements on generators of degree *i* for every *i* not of the form 2<sup>*n*</sup>−1. That is: $\mathbb{Z}_2 [ x_2 , x_4 , x_5 , x_6 , x_8 \cdots]$ where $x_{2n}$ can be represented by the classes of $\mathbb{RP}^{2n}$ while for odd indices one can use appropriate [Dold](wikipedia://en/31337135) manifolds.

Unoriented bordism is 2-torsion, since *2M* is the boundary of $M \times I$.

MO is a rather weak cobordism theory, as the spectrum MO is isomorphic to H(π<sub>\*</sub>(MO)) ("homology with coefficients in π<sub>\*</sub>(MO)") – MO is a product of [Eilenberg–MacLane spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–MacLane_spectra). In other words, the corresponding homology and cohomology theories are no more powerful than homology and cohomology with coefficients in **Z**/2**Z**. This was the first cobordism theory to be described completely.

:::

### Complex cobordism {#Complex_cobordism}

**Spectrum:** MU (Thom spectrum of [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993))

**Coefficient ring:** π<sub>\*</sub>(*MU*) is the polynomial ring on generators of degree 2, 4, 6, 8, ... and is naturally isomorphic to [Lazard's universal ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazard_ring), and is the cobordism ring of stably [almost complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/514258).

### Oriented cobordism {#Oriented_cobordism}

**Spectrum:** MSO (Thom spectrum of [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group))

**Coefficient ring:** The oriented cobordism class of a manifold is completely determined by its characteristic numbers: its [Stiefel–Whitney numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stiefel–Whitney_number) and [Pontryagin numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin_number), but the overall coefficient ring, denoted $\Omega_* = \Omega(*) = MSO(*)$ is quite complicated. Rationally, and at 2 (corresponding to Pontryagin and Stiefel–Whitney classes, respectively), MSO is a product of [Eilenberg–MacLane spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–MacLane_spectra) – $MSO_{\mathbf Q} = H(\pi_*(MSO_{\mathbf Q}))$ and $MSO[2] = H(\pi_*(MSO[2]))$ – but at odd primes it is not, and the structure is complicated to describe. The ring has been completely described integrally, due to work of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), Boris Averbuch, [Vladimir Rokhlin](wikipedia://en/7703611), and [C. T. C. Wall](wikipedia://en/2613478).

### Brown–Peterson cohomology {#Brown–Peterson_cohomology}

**Spectrum:** BP

**Coefficient ring:** π<sub>\*</sub>(BP) is a polynomial algebra over *Z*<sub>(*p*)</sub> on generators *v*<sub>*n*</sub> of dimension 2(*p*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1) for *n* ≥ 1.

Brown–Peterson cohomology BP is a summand of MU<sub>*p*</sub>, which is complex cobordism MU localized at a prime *p*. In fact MU<sub>(*p*)</sub> is a sum of suspensions of BP.

### Morava K-theory {#Morava_K-theory}

**Spectrum:** K(*n*) (They also depend on a prime *p*.)

**Coefficient ring:** **F**<sub>*p*</sub>\[*v*<sub>*n*</sub>, *v*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>−1</sup>\], where *v*<sub>*n*</sub> has degree 2(*p*<sup>*n*</sup> -1).

These theories have period 2(*p*<sup>*n*</sup> − 1). They are named after [Jack Morava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Morava).

### Topological modular forms {#Topological_modular_forms}

**Spectra:** tmf, TMF (previously called eo<sub>2</sub>.)

The coefficient ring π<sub>\*</sub>(tmf) is called the ring of [topological modular forms](wikipedia://en/10274436). TMF is tmf with the 24th power of the modular form Δ inverted, and has period 24<sup>2</sup>=576. At the prime *p* = 2, the completion of tmf is the spectrum eo<sub>2</sub>, and the K(2)-localization of tmf is the Hopkins-Miller Higher Real K-theory spectrum EO<sub>2</sub>.
