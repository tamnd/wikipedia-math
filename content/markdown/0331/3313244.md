# Exponential factorial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exponential factorial"}

In mathematics, the **exponential factorial** of a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) $n$ is a quickly growing function of $n$ defined by [iterating](wikipedia://en/1448702) the process of [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491), analogously to the way that the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) can be defined by iterated [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The exponential factorial is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ [raised to the power](wikipedia://en/99491) of $n-1$, which in turn is raised to the power of $n-2$, and so on in a right-grouping manner. That is,

$$n^{\left((n - 1)^{\left(n - 2)^{\cdot^{\cdot^\cdot}}\right)}\right)}$$

The exponential factorial can also be defined with the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$a_1 = 1,\quad a_n = n^{a_{n - 1}}.$$

## Growth rate {#Growth_rate}

The exponential factorials were introduced by Dee David Smith, in his 1974 book *Orthogonic Taxonomy of Fundamental Concepts*. Smith used a notation for the $n$th exponential factorial that enclosed the number $n$ in a three-sided box (leaving the left side open). He asked "whether or not there are other methods that give larger numbers". The answer is yes: these numbers grow quickly, but not more quickly than other known methods.[^o6]

The exponential factorials exhibit growth equivalent to [tetration](wikipedia://en/482379) (in the sense that, for any base $b > e^{1/e}$, the exponential factorial is bounded above and below by expressions of the form $b \uparrow \uparrow (n + c)$ with constant $c$). The exponential factorial of 6 (a number with approximately 5 × 10<sup>183 230</sup> decimal digits) is larger than a [googolplex](wikipedia://en/12365).

## Reciprocal sum {#Reciprocal_sum}

The sum of the [reciprocals](wikipedia://en/229940) of the exponential factorials from 1 onwards is the following [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325):

$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{1}+\frac{1}{2^1}+\frac{1}{3^{2^1}}+\frac{1}{4^{3^{2^1}}}+\frac{1}{5^{4^{3^{2^1}}}}+\frac{1}{6^{5^{4^{3^{2^1}}}}}+\ldots\\ &=1.611114925808376736\underbrace{111111111111\ldots 111111111111}_{183212}272243682859\ldots \end{aligned}$$

This sum is transcendental because it is a [Liouville number](wikipedia://en/50964): its [partial sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sum) provide a sequence of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) that approximate it more accurately, relative to their denominators, than would be possible for an [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158).
