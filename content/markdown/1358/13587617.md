# Cameron–Erdős conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cameron–Erdős conjecture"}

In [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), the **Cameron–Erdős conjecture** (now a theorem) is the statement that the number of [sum-free sets](wikipedia://en/11120026) contained in $[N] = \{1,\ldots,N\}$ is $O\big({2^{N/2}}\big).$

:::

The sum of two [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) numbers is [even](wikipedia://en/143135), so a set of odd numbers is always sum-free. There are $\lceil N/2\rceil$ odd numbers in \[*N*\], and so $2^{N/2}$ [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of odd numbers in \[*N*\]. The Cameron–Erdős conjecture says that this counts a constant proportion of the sum-free sets.

The [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) was stated by [Peter Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Cameron_%28mathematician%29) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) in 1988.[^o2] It was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Ben Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_J._Green)[^o3] and independently by Alexander Sapozhenko[^o4][^o5] in 2003.
