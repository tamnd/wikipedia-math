# Manfred Kochen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Manfred Kochen"}

**Manfred "Fred" Kochen** (4 July 1928 – 7 January 1989) was an Austrian research scientist and professor. He is best known for his academic work and research career with various companies, including [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM). Kochen's research emphasized [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science) and [behavioural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioural_sciences) research, as well as early studies of concepts in the field of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence), where he questioned the possibility of [artificial general intelligence](wikipedia://en/586357).[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Manfred Kochen was born on 4 July 1928, in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), Austria. Some time before the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), his family fled [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) to escape the threat of Nazi persecution, escaping via [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal).[^o2]

Prior to 1947, Manfred Kochen moved to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), where he attended Brooklyn Technical High School, graduating with a Mechanical Course diploma. Kochen received his Bachelor's of Science from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in Cambridge, Massachusetts in 1950, with a concentration in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).[^o2] Kochen would subsequently go on to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), where he would receive both his Master of Arts and PhD in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) in 1951 and 1955 respectively.[^o2] During his doctoral studies, he served as an analyst with the John von Neumann project at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) with [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o2] After earning his doctorate, Kochen also served as a postdoctoral fellow at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

Kochen met his wife, Paula, in New York City. They were married from 1954 until his death.[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

After concluding his postdoctoral research, Kochen began working as a research scientist at IBM's [Thomas J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson_Research_Center), where he worked until 1964.[^o6] In 1965, he began teaching at the [University of Michigan School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_Medicine) as a professor of information science, while he also held simultaneous posts as an adjunct professor in the [Ross School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_School_of_Business), as well as acting as a research mathematician in the [Mental Health Research Institute (Michigan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_Health_Research_Institute_%28Michigan%29).[^o6][^o2]

In 1972, Kochen was elevated to a full professorship, and would continue to teach until his death.[^o2]

Kochen died on 7 January 1989. His death was reported in the [Journal of the American Society for Information Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_Association_for_Information_Science_and_Technology),[^o7] notices of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o8] and his legacy was analyzed by [Don R. Swanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_R._Swanson).[^o9]
