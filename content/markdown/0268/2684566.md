# Drag count

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Drag count"}

A **drag count** is a dimensionless unit used by [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) engineers. 1 drag count is equal to a $C_d$ of 0.0001.[^o2][^o3]

:::

As the drag forces present on automotive vehicles are smaller than for aircraft, 1 drag count is commonly referred to as 0.0001 of $C_d$.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A drag count $\Delta C_\mathrm d\,$ is defined as:

$\Delta C_\mathrm d = 10^{4} \dfrac{2 F_\mathrm d}{\rho v^2 A}\, ,$ [^o6]

where:

$F_\mathrm d\,$ is the [drag force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_%28physics%29), which is by definition the force component in the direction of the flow velocity,

$\rho\,$ is the [mass density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_density) of the fluid,

$v\,$ is the [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed) of the object relative to the fluid, and

$A\,$ is the reference [area](wikipedia://en/1209).

The [drag coefficient](wikipedia://en/172291) is used to compare the solutions of different geometries by means of a dimensionless number. A drag count is more user-friendly than the drag coefficient, as the latter is usually much less than 1. A drag count of 200 to 400 is typical for an airplane at cruise.[^o7] A reduction of one drag count on a subsonic civil transport airplane means about 200 more in payload.[^o8]

:::
