# Topological algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **topological algebra** $A$ is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) and at the same time a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), where the algebraic and the topological structures are coherent in a specified sense.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **topological algebra** $A$ over a [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field) $K$ is a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) together with a bilinear multiplication

$$\cdot: A \times A \to A,$$

$$(a,b) \mapsto a \cdot b$$

that turns $A$ into an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over $K$ and is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) in some definite sense. Usually the *continuity of the multiplication* is expressed by one of the following (non-equivalent) requirements:

- *joint continuity*:[^n1] for each [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of zero $U\subseteq A$ there are neighbourhoods of zero $V\subseteq A$ and $W\subseteq A$ such that $V \cdot W\subseteq U$ (in other words, this condition means that the multiplication is continuous as a map between topological spaces $A \times A \to A$), or
- *stereotype continuity*:[^n2] for each [totally bounded set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_bounded_set#Definitions_in_other_contexts) $S\subseteq A$ and for each neighbourhood of zero $U\subseteq A$ there is a neighbourhood of zero $V\subseteq A$ such that $S \cdot V\subseteq U$ and $V \cdot S\subseteq U$, or
- *separate continuity*:[^n3] for each element $a\in A$ and for each neighbourhood of zero $U\subseteq A$ there is a neighbourhood of zero $V\subseteq A$ such that $a\cdot V\subseteq U$ and $V\cdot a\subseteq U$.

(Certainly, joint continuity implies stereotype continuity, and stereotype continuity implies separate continuity.) In the first case $A$ is called a "*topological algebra with jointly continuous multiplication*", and in the last, "*with separately continuous multiplication*".

A unital [associative](wikipedia://en/2112) topological algebra is (sometimes) called a [topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097).

:::

## History {#History}

The term was coined by [David van Dantzig](wikipedia://en/528853); it appears in the title of his [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) (1931).

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

\1. [Fréchet algebras](wikipedia://en/4102202) are examples of associative topological algebras with jointly continuous multiplication.

\2. [Banach algebras](wikipedia://en/4665) are special cases of [Fréchet algebras](wikipedia://en/4102202).

\3. [Stereotype algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) are examples of associative topological algebras with stereotype continuous multiplication.

:::
