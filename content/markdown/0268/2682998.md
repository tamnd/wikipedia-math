# Generalized inverse Gaussian distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized inverse Gaussian distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **generalized inverse Gaussian distribution** (**GIG**) is a three-parameter family of continuous [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) with [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487)

:::

$$f(x) = \frac{(a/b)^{p/2}}{2 K_p(\sqrt{ab})} x^{(p-1)} e^{-(ax + b/x)/2},\qquad x>0,$$

where *K<sub>p</sub>* is a [modified Bessel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Bessel_function) of the second kind, *a* > 0, *b* > 0 and *p* a real parameter. It is used extensively in [geostatistics](wikipedia://en/51531), statistical linguistics, finance, etc. This distribution was first proposed by [Étienne Halphen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne_Halphen).[^o2][^o3][^o4] It was rediscovered and popularised by [Ole Barndorff-Nielsen](wikipedia://en/6328849), who called it the generalized inverse Gaussian distribution. Its statistical properties are discussed in Bent Jørgensen's lecture notes.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Alternative parametrization {#Alternative_parametrization}

By setting $\theta = \sqrt{ab}$ and $\eta = \sqrt{b/a}$, we can alternatively express the GIG distribution as

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2\eta K_p(\theta)} \left(\frac{x}{\eta}\right)^{p-1} e^{-\theta(x/\eta + \eta/x)/2},$$

where $\theta$ is the concentration parameter while $\eta$ is the scaling parameter.

### Summation {#Summation}

Barndorff-Nielsen and Halgreen proved that the GIG distribution is [infinitely divisible](wikipedia://en/13588803).[^o10]

### Entropy {#Entropy}

The entropy of the generalized inverse Gaussian distribution is given as

$$\begin{aligned} H = \frac{1}{2} \log \left( \frac b a \right) & {} +\log \left(2 K_p\left(\sqrt{ab} \right)\right) - (p-1) \frac{\left[\frac{d}{d\nu}K_\nu\left(\sqrt{ab}\right)\right]_{\nu=p}}{K_p\left(\sqrt{a b}\right)} \\ & {} + \frac{\sqrt{a b}}{2 K_p\left(\sqrt{a b}\right)}\left( K_{p+1}\left(\sqrt{ab}\right) + K_{p-1}\left(\sqrt{a b}\right)\right) \end{aligned}$$

where $\left[\frac{d}{d\nu}K_\nu\left(\sqrt{a b}\right)\right]_{\nu=p}$ is a derivative of the modified Bessel function of the second kind with respect to the order $\nu$ evaluated at $\nu=p$

### Characteristic Function {#Characteristic_Function}

The characteristic of a random variable $X\sim GIG(p, a, b)$ is given as[^o5]

$$E(e^{itX}) = \left(\frac{a }{a-2it }\right)^{\frac{p}{2}} \frac{K_{p}\left( \sqrt{(a-2it)b} \right)}{ K_{p}\left( \sqrt{ab} \right) }$$

for $t \in \mathbb{R}$ where $i$ denotes the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358).

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

### Special cases {#Special_cases}

The [inverse Gaussian](wikipedia://en/5246161) and [gamma](wikipedia://en/207079) distributions are special cases of the generalized inverse Gaussian distribution for *p* = −1/2 and *b* = 0, respectively.[^o25] Specifically, an inverse Gaussian distribution of the form

$$f(x;\mu,\lambda) = \left[\frac{\lambda}{2 \pi x^3}\right]^{1/2} \exp{ \left( \frac{-\lambda (x-\mu)^2}{2 \mu^2 x} \right)}$$

is a GIG with $a = \lambda/\mu^2$, $b = \lambda$, and $p=-1/2$. A gamma distribution of the form

$$g(x;\alpha,\beta) = \beta^\alpha \frac 1 {\Gamma(\alpha)} x^{\alpha-1} e^{-\beta x}$$

is a GIG with $a = 2 \beta$, $b = 0$, and $p = \alpha$.

Other special cases include the [inverse-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/1653565), for *a* = 0.[^o25]

:::

### Conjugate prior for Gaussian {#Conjugate_prior_for_Gaussian}

The GIG distribution is [conjugate](wikipedia://en/846412) to the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) when serving as the mixing distribution in a [normal variance-mean mixture](wikipedia://en/6526281).[^o17][^o18] Let the prior distribution for some hidden variable, say $z$, be GIG:

$$P(z\mid a,b,p) = \operatorname{GIG}(z\mid a,b,p)$$

and let there be $T$ observed data points, $X=x_1,\ldots,x_T$, with normal likelihood function, conditioned on $z:$

$$P(X\mid z,\alpha,\beta) = \prod_{i=1}^T N(x_i\mid\alpha+\beta z,z)$$

where $N(x\mid\mu,v)$ is the normal distribution, with mean $\mu$ and variance $v$. Then the posterior for $z$, given the data is also GIG:

$$P(z\mid X,a,b,p,\alpha,\beta) = \text{GIG}\left(z\mid a+T\beta^2,b+S,p-\frac T 2 \right)$$

where $S = \sum_{i=1}^T (x_i-\alpha)^2$.[^o19]

### Sichel distribution {#Sichel_distribution}

The Sichel distribution results when the GIG is used as the mixing distribution for the [Poisson](wikipedia://en/23009144) parameter $\lambda$.[^o21][^o22]
