# René-François de Sluse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="René-François de Sluse"}

**René-François Walter de Sluse** (; also **Renatus Franciscus Slusius** or **Walther de Sluze**; 2 July 1622 – 19 March 1685) was a [Walloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloons) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and clergyman who was a [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_%28priest%29) of [Saint Lambert's Cathedral, Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lambert's_Cathedral,_Liège) and [abbé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbé) of [Amay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amay).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Visé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visé), [Prince-Bishopric of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Liège) (in present-day Belgium), and studied at the [University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_University_of_Leuven) (1638–1642) before becoming [doctor of laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_laws) of the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) in 1643.[^o5] He spent a total of ten years in Italy, at Rome, Perugia and Florence, studying several languages, [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy). Aside from mathematics he also produced works on astronomy, [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history), general [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History), and [theological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) subjects related to his work in the Church. His most important publication, *Mesolabum*, was printed in 1659, with a second edition in 1668.[^o6]

Sluse became a canon of Liège cathedral in 1650, a privy councillor on 22 March 1659, abbé of Amay in 1666, an ordinary councillor to Prince-Bishop [Maximilian-Henry of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian-Henry_of_Bavaria) on 20 October 1666, and vice-provost of the cathedral on 20 January 1676.[^o5] His responsibilities prevented him from visiting other mathematicians, but he corresponded with many of the thinkers of the day, including [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068), [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127), [Henry Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Oldenburg), [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290), and [Michelangelo Ricci](wikipedia://en/34287470).[^o5] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1674.[^o3] He died in Liège on 19 March 1685.

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Sluse contributed to the development of calculus, focusing upon [spirals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirals), [tangents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangents), turning points and points of [inflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflection). He and [Johannes Hudde](wikipedia://en/1365598) found algebraic algorithms for finding [tangents](wikipedia://en/31482), minima and maxima that were later utilized by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627). These algorithms greatly improved upon the complicated algebraic methods of [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) and [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525), who themselves had improved upon [Roberval's](wikipedia://en/196880) kinematic, but geometric, non-algorithmic methods of determining tangents.[^o8]

[Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289) has the following to say about de Sluse's contribution to Newton's [method of fluxions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_fluxions) in his discussion of the [Leibniz–Newton calculus controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz–Newton_calculus_controversy):

> When they state that [Collins](wikipedia://en/2361164) had been four years in circulating the letter in which the method of fluxions was sufficiently described to any intelligent person, they suppress two facts: first, that the letter itself was in consequence of Newton's learning that Sluse had a method of tangents; secondly, that it revealed no more than Sluse had done. ...this method of Sluse is never allowed to appear ...Sluse wrote an account of the method which he had previously signified to Collins, for the Royal Society, for whom it was printed. The rule is precisely that of Newton... To have given this would have shown the world that the grand communication which was asserted to have been sent to Leibniz in June 1676 might have been seen in print, and learned from Sluse, at any time in the previous years: accordingly it was buried under reference. ...Leibniz had seen Hudde at Amsterdam, and had found that Hudde was in possession of even more than Sluse.[^o9]

He found for the [subtangent](wikipedia://en/472551) of a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246)

*f*(*x*, *y*) = 0

an expression equivalent to

$${-y {\partial f \over \partial y} \over {\partial f \over \partial x}}.$$

He also wrote numerous tracts, and in particular discussed at some length [spirals](wikipedia://en/61563) and points of [inflexion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflexion). The [Conchoid of de Sluze](wikipedia://en/898316) is named after him. He is described by John Wallis in his *Algebra* as "a very accurate and ingenious person." Several of his works were included in the *[Transactions of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society)*, e.g. his method of drawing tangents to geometrical curves.
