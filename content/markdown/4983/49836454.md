# Kundu equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kundu equation"}

The **Kundu equation** is a general form of [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213) that is gauge-equivalent to the mixed [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748). It was proposed by Anjan Kundu[^o2] as

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$i q_t + q_{xx} + i\alpha (| q|^2q)_x + c |q|^2q -(\theta_t+ \theta_x^2-i\theta_{xx} )q + \theta_x( 2iq_x - \alpha | q|^2 q)=0, \tag{1}$$

:::

with arbitrary function $\theta (t,x)$ and the subscripts denoting partial derivatives. Equation (1) is shown to be reducible for the choice of $\theta_{x} = -\kappa |q|^2$ to an integrable class of mixed [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748) with cubic–quintic nonlinearity, given in a representative form

::: {#o4 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$q_t + q_{xx} + i\alpha (| q|^2q)_x + c |q|^2q + \gamma |q|^4q + 4i \kappa (| q|^2)_x q = 0 . \tag{2}$$

:::

Here $\alpha, c, \kappa$ are independent parameters, while $\gamma = \kappa(4 \kappa + \alpha) .$ Equation ((1)), more specifically equation ((2)) is known as the **Kundu equation**.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties and applications"}

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

The Kundu equation is a completely [integrable system](wikipedia://en/2573213), allowing [Lax pair](wikipedia://en/5527029) representation, exact solutions, and higher [conserved quantity](wikipedia://en/2458485). Along with its different particular cases, this equation has been investigated for finding its exact [travelling wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelling_wave) solutions,[^o7] exact [solitary wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitary_wave_%28water_waves%29) solutions[^o8] via bilinearization,[^o9] and Darboux transformation[^o10][^o11] together with the [orbital stability](wikipedia://en/28842205) for such solitary wave solutions.[^o12]

The Kundu equation has been applied to various physical processes such as [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics), and [nonlinear optics](wikipedia://en/21723).[^o12] It is linked to the mixed nonlinear Schrödinger equation through a [gauge transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation) and is reducible to a variety of known integrable equations such as the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748) (NLSE), derivative NLSE, higher nonlinear derivative NLSE, Chen–Lee–Liu, Gerjikov-Vanov, and [Kundu–Eckhaus equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kundu–Eckhaus_equation), for different choices of the parameters.[^o2]

:::

## Kundu-Eckhaus equation {#Kundu-Eckhaus_equation}

A generalization of the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schroedinger_equation) with additional quintic nonlinearity and a nonlinear dispersive term was proposed in the form[^o2]

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$\psi_t+\psi_{xx} -2 c |\psi|^2\psi+\kappa^2|\psi|^4\psi \pm 2i \kappa (| \psi|^2)_x \psi=0, \tag{3}$$

:::

which may be obtained from the **Kundu Equation** ((2)), when restricted to $\alpha =0$. The same equation, limited further to the particular case $c =0,$ was introduced later as [the Eckhaus equation](wikipedia://en/42069938), following which equation ((3)) is presently known as the **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation. The **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation can be reduced to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation through a nonlinear transformation of the field and known therefore to be gauge equivalent integrable systems, since they are equivalent under the [gauge transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_transformation).

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties and Applications"}

### Properties and Applications {#Properties_and_Applications}

The **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation is associated with a [Lax pair](wikipedia://en/5527029), higher [conserved quantity](wikipedia://en/2458485), exact [soliton solution](wikipedia://en/51654), [rogue wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_wave) solution etc. Over the years various aspects of this equation, its generalizations and link with other equations have been studied. In particular, relationship of **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation with the Johnson's hydrodynamic equation near criticality is established,[^o17] its [discretizations](wikipedia://en/330017),[^o18] reduction via Lie symmetry,[^o19] complex structure via Bernoulli subequation,[^o20] bright and dark soliton solutions via [Bäcklund transformation](wikipedia://en/701077)[^o21] and Darboux transformation[^o22] with the associated [rogue wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_wave) solutions,[^o23][^o24] are studied.

:::

### RKL equation {#RKL_equation}

A multi-component generalisation of the **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation ((3)), known as **Radhakrishnan, Kundu and Laskshmanan** (RKL) equation was proposed in nonlinear optics for [fiber optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_optics) communication through [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) pulses in a [birefringent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birefringent) non-Kerr medium[^o26] and analysed subsequently for its exact soliton solution and other aspects in a series of papers.[^o27][^o28][^o29][^o30]

### Quantum Aspects {#Quantum_Aspects}

Though the **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation (3) is gauge equivalent to the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schroedinger_equation), they differ with respect to their [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) structures and field commutation relations. The [Hamiltonian operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_operator) of the **Kundu-Ekchaus** equation quantum field model given by

$${H} =\int dx \left[ : \left( (\psi^\dagger_x \psi_x + c \rho^2 +i \kappa \rho (\psi^\dagger \psi_x- \psi^\dagger_x \psi) \right): +\kappa^2 (\psi^\dagger \rho ^2 \psi) \right], \ \ \ \ \rho \equiv (\psi^\dagger \psi)$$

and defined through the [bosonic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosonic_field) operator [commutation relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_relation) $[\psi (x), \psi^\dagger(y)]= \delta(x-y)$, is more complicated than the well-known bosonic Hamiltonian of the quantum nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Here $\ : \ \ : \$ indicates [normal ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_ordering) in bosonic operators. This model corresponds to a double $\delta$-function interacting Bose gas and is difficult to solve directly.

### One-dimensional Anion gas {#One-dimensional_Anion_gas}

However, under a nonlinear transformation of the field below:

$$\tilde \psi (x)= e^{-i \kappa \int^x_{- \infty} \psi^\dagger (x') \psi (x') dx'} \psi (x)$$

the model can be transformed to:

$$\tilde H=\int dx \vdots \left( \tilde \psi^\dagger_x \tilde \psi_x + c (\tilde \psi^\dagger \tilde \psi)^2 \right) \vdots ,$$

i.e. in the same form as the quantum model of the [Nonlinear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Schroedinger_equation) (NLSE), though it differs from the NLSE in its contents, since now the fields involved are no longer bosonic operators but exhibit [anion](wikipedia://en/367654) like properties.

$$\tilde \psi^\dagger (x_1) \tilde \psi^\dagger (x_2)=e^{i \kappa\epsilon (x_1-x_2)} \tilde \psi^\dagger (x_2)\tilde \psi^\dagger (x_1) , \ \tilde \psi (x_1) \tilde \psi^\dagger (x_2)=e^{-i \kappa \epsilon (x_1-x_2)} \tilde \psi^\dagger (x_2)\tilde \psi (x_1)+ \delta (x_1-x_2)$$

etc. where

$\epsilon (x-y)= + \ , -, 0 \ \ ~$ for $\ ~ x >y, \ x< y, \ \ x = y ,$

though at the coinciding points the bosonic [commutation relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_relation) still holds. In analogy with the Lieb Limiger model of $\delta$ function [bose gas](wikipedia://en/1064839), the quantum **Kundu-Ekchaus** model in the N-particle sector therefore corresponds to a one-dimensional (1D) anion gas interacting via a $\delta$ function interaction. This model of interacting anion gas was proposed and exactly solved by the [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884) in [^o33] and this basic anion model is studied further for investigating various aspects of the 1D anion gas as well as extended in different directions. [^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37][^o38]
