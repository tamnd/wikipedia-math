# Hexadecagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexadecagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In mathematics, a **hexadecagon** (sometimes called a **hexakaidecagon** or **16-gon**) is a sixteen-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).[^o2]

:::

## Regular hexadecagon {#Regular_hexadecagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) hexadecagon* is a hexadecagon in which all angles are equal and all sides are congruent. Its [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) is {16} and can be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [octagon](wikipedia://en/314575), t{8}, and a twice-truncated [square](wikipedia://en/659939) tt{4}. A truncated hexadecagon, t{16}, is a triacontadigon, {32}.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

As 16 = 2<sup>4</sup> (a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948)), a regular hexadecagon is [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) using [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge): this was already known to ancient Greek mathematicians.[^o6]

:::

## Measurements {#Measurements}

Each angle of a regular hexadecagon is 157.5 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294), and the total angle measure of any hexadecagon is 2520 degrees.

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a regular hexadecagon with edge length *t* is

$$\begin{aligned} A = 4t^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{16} =& 4t^2 \left(1+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{ 4+2\sqrt{2} }\right)\\ =& 4t^2 (\sqrt{2}+1)(\sqrt{4-2\sqrt{2}}+1) .\end{aligned}$$

Because the hexadecagon has a number of sides that is a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948), its area can be computed in terms of the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) *R* by truncating [Viète's formula](wikipedia://en/1609504):

$$A=R^2\cdot\frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2}}}=4R^2\sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}.$$

Since the area of the circumcircle is $\pi R^2,$ the regular hexadecagon fills approximately 97.45% of its circumcircle.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Symmetry*

::: {#o10 .table cue=table name="Symmetry"}

| *Image: Symmetries\_of\_hexadecagon.png.* | The 14 symmetries of a regular hexadecagon. Lines of reflections are blue through vertices, purple through edges, and gyration orders are given in the center. Vertices are colored by their symmetry position. |
|---|---|

:::

The *regular hexadecagon* has Dih<sub>16</sub> symmetry, order 32. There are 4 dihedral subgroups: Dih<sub>8</sub>, Dih<sub>4</sub>, Dih<sub>2</sub>, and Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 5 [cyclic subgroups](wikipedia://en/52327): Z<sub>16</sub>, Z<sub>8</sub>, Z<sub>4</sub>, Z<sub>2</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>, the last implying no symmetry.

On the regular hexadecagon, there are 14 distinct symmetries. John Conway labels full symmetry as **r32** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars) Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.[^o11]

The most common high symmetry hexadecagons are **d16**, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) hexadecagon constructed by eight mirrors can alternate long and short edges, and **p16**, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) hexadecagon constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular hexadecagon.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g16** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m*-1)/2 parallelograms. [^o14] In particular this is true for [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the *regular hexadecagon*, *m*=8, and it can be divided into 28: 4 squares and 3 sets of 8 rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of an [8-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cube), with 28 of 1792 faces. The list enumerates the number of solutions as 1232944, including up to 16-fold rotations and chiral forms in reflection.

A **skew hexadecagon** is a [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) with 24 vertices and edges but not existing on the same plane. The interior of such a hexadecagon is not generally defined. A *skew zig-zag hexadecagon* has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A **regular skew hexadecagon** is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with equal edge lengths. In 3-dimensions it will be a zig-zag skew hexadecagon and can be seen in the vertices and side edges of an [octagonal antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagonal_antiprism) with the same D<sub>8d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,16\] symmetry, order 32. The [octagrammic antiprism](wikipedia://en/25927916), s{2,16/3} and [octagrammic crossed-antiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octagrammic_crossed-antiprism), s{2,16/5} also have regular skew octagons.

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

A **hexadecagram** is a 16-sided star polygon, represented by symbol {16/n}. There are three regular [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303), {16/3}, {16/5}, {16/7}, using the same vertices, but connecting every third, fifth or seventh points. There are also three compounds: {16/2} is reduced to 2{8} as two [octagons](wikipedia://en/314575), {16/4} is reduced to 4{4} as four squares and {16/6} reduces to 2{8/3} as two [octagrams](wikipedia://en/2667613), and finally {16/8} is reduced to 8{2} as eight [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728).

Deeper truncations of the regular octagon and octagram can produce isogonal ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)) intermediate hexadecagram forms with equally spaced vertices and two edge lengths.[^o26]

A truncated octagon is a hexadecagon, t{8}={16}. A quasitruncated octagon, inverted as {8/7}, is a hexadecagram: t{8/7}={16/7}. A truncated octagram {8/3} is a hexadecagram: t{8/3}={16/3} and a quasitruncated octagram, inverted as {8/5}, is a hexadecagram: t{8/5}={16/5}.

## In art {#In_art}

*Image: Raffaello Sanzio - Spozalizio (detail) - WGA18625.jpg.* The hexadecagonal tower from Raphael's [*The Marriage of the Virgin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marriage_of_the_Virgin_%28Raphael%29)

In the early 16th century, [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael) was the first to construct a [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405) image of a regular hexadecagon: the tower in his painting [*The Marriage of the Virgin*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marriage_of_the_Virgin_%28Raphael%29) has 16 sides, elaborating on an eight-sided tower in a previous painting by [Pietro Perugino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Perugino).[^o29]

*Image: ReconquistaAlhambra.JPG.* A hexadecagrammic pattern from the [Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra)

Hexadecagrams (16-sided [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303)) are included in the [Girih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girih) patterns in the [Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra).[^o30]
