# Regular 4-polytope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Regular 4-polytope"}

*Image: Hypercube.svg.* The [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) is one of 6 convex regular 4-polytopes

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **regular 4-polytope** or **regular polychoron** is a [regular](wikipedia://en/518693) [four-dimensional polytope](wikipedia://en/24979). They are the four-dimensional analogues of the [regular polyhedra](wikipedia://en/333299) in three dimensions and the [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) in two dimensions.

:::

There are six [convex](wikipedia://en/1817885) and ten [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_polytope) regular 4-polytopes, giving a total of sixteen.

## History {#History}

The convex regular 4-polytopes were first described by the Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) in the mid-19th century.[^n1] He discovered that there are precisely six such figures.

Schläfli also found four of the regular star 4-polytopes: the [grand 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604599), [great stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604610), [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538), and [great grand stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604513). He skipped the remaining six because he would not allow forms that failed the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) on cells or vertex figures (for zero-hole tori: *F* − *E* + *V* = 2). That excludes cells and vertex figures such as the [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441) {5,$\tfrac{5}{2}$} and [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843) $\tfrac{5}{2}$,5}.

[Edmund Hess](wikipedia://en/9191575) (1843–1903) published the complete list in his 1883 German book *Einleitung in die Lehre von der Kugelteilung mit besonderer Berücksichtigung ihrer Anwendung auf die Theorie der Gleichflächigen und der gleicheckigen Polyeder*.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The existence of a regular 4-polytope $\{p,q,r\}$ is constrained by the existence of the regular polyhedra $\{p,q\}, \{q,r\}$ which form its cells and a [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) constraint

$$\sin\frac{\pi}p \sin\frac{\pi}r > \cos\frac{\pi}q$$

to ensure that the cells meet to form a closed 3-surface.

The six convex and ten star polytopes described are the only solutions to these constraints.

There are four nonconvex [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r} that have valid cells {p,q} and vertex figures {q,r}, and pass the dihedral test, but fail to produce finite figures: {3,$\tfrac{5}{2}$,3}, {4,3,$\tfrac{5}{2}$}, $\tfrac{5}{2}$,3,4}, $\tfrac{5}{2}$,3,$\tfrac{5}{2}$}.

:::

## Regular convex 4-polytopes {#Regular_convex_4-polytopes}

The regular convex 4-polytopes are the four-dimensional analogues of the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) in three dimensions and the convex [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) in two dimensions.

Each convex regular 4-polytope is bounded by a set of 3-dimensional *[cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29)* which are all Platonic solids of the same type and size. These are fitted together along their respective faces (face-to-face) in a regular fashion, forming the *surface* of the 4-polytope which is a closed, curved 3-dimensional space (analogous to the way the surface of the earth is a closed, curved 2-dimensional space).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

Like their 3-dimensional analogues, the convex regular 4-polytopes can be naturally ordered by size as a measure of 4-dimensional content (hypervolume) for the same radius. Each greater polytope in the sequence is *rounder* than its predecessor, enclosing more content within the same radius.[^n2] The 4-simplex (5-cell) has the smallest content, and the 120-cell has the largest.

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) advocated the names simplex, orthoplex, tesseract, octaplex or polyoctahedron (pO), tetraplex or polytetrahedron (pT), and dodecaplex or polydodecahedron (pD).[^o17]

[Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) advocated the names n-cell, or pentachoron, hexadecachoron, tesseract or octachoron, icositetrachoron, hexacosichoron, and hecatonicosachoron (or dodecacontachoron), coining the term *polychoron* being a 4D analogy to the 3D polyhedron, and 2D polygon, expressed from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) roots *poly* ("many") and *choros* ("room" or "space").[^o18][^o19]

The [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) for all 4-polytopes is zero, we have the 4-dimensional analogue of Euler's polyhedral formula:

$$N_0 - N_1 + N_2 - N_3 = 0\,$$

where *N*<sub>*k*</sub> denotes the number of *k*-faces in the polytope (a vertex is a 0-face, an edge is a 1-face, etc.).

The topology of any given 4-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o20]

:::

### As configurations {#As_configurations}

A regular 4-polytope can be completely described as a [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/56419724) containing counts of its component elements. The rows and columns correspond to vertices, edges, faces, and cells. The diagonal numbers (upper left to lower right) say how many of each element occur in the whole 4-polytope. The non-diagonal numbers say how many of the column's element occur in or at the row's element. For example, there are 2 vertices *in* each edge (each edge *has* 2 vertices), and 2 cells meet *at* each face (each face *belongs to* 2 cells), in any regular 4-polytope. The configuration for the dual polytope can be obtained by rotating the matrix by 180 degrees.[^o22][^o23]

## Regular star (Schläfli–Hess) 4-polytopes {#Regular_star_(Schläfli–Hess)_4-polytopes}

*Image: Relationship among regular star polychora.png.* This shows the relationships among the four-dimensional starry polytopes. The 2 convex forms and 10 starry forms can be seen in 3D as the vertices of a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280).[^o32]

*Image: Relationship among regular star polychora-8.png.* A subset of relations among 8 forms from the 120-cell, polydodecahedron (pD). The three operations {a,g,s} are commutable, defining a cubic framework. There are 7 [densities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_%28geometry%29) seen in vertical positioning, with 2 dual forms having the same density.

The **Schläfli–Hess 4-polytopes** are the complete set of 10 [regular](wikipedia://en/518693) self-intersecting [**star polychora**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_polytope) ([four-dimensional polytopes](wikipedia://en/24979)).[^o33] They are named in honor of their discoverers: [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) and [Edmund Hess](wikipedia://en/9191575). Each is represented by a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {*p*,*q*,*r*} in which one of the numbers is [$\tfrac{5}{2}$](wikipedia://en/197544). They are thus analogous to the regular nonconvex [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler&ndash;Poinsot_polyhedron), which are in turn analogous to the pentagram.

### Names {#Names}

Their names given here were given by [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), extending [Cayley's](wikipedia://en/311034) names for the [Kepler&ndash;Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler&ndash;Poinsot_polyhedra): along with *stellated* and *great*, he adds a *grand* modifier. Conway offered these operational definitions:

1. **[stellation](wikipedia://en/180253)** – replaces edges with longer edges in same lines. (Example: a [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014) stellates into a [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544))
1. **greatening** – replaces the faces with large ones in same planes. (Example: an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) greatens into a [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898))
1. **aggrandizement** – replaces the cells with large ones in same 3-spaces. (Example: a [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) aggrandizes into a [grand 600-cell](wikipedia://en/8604538))

John Conway names the 10 forms from 3 regular celled 4-polytopes: pT=polytetrahedron {3,3,5} (a tetrahedral [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447)), pI=polyicosahedron {3,5,$\tfrac{5}{2}$} (an [icosahedral 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604585)), and pD=polydodecahedron {5,3,3} (a dodecahedral [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358)), with prefix modifiers: *g*, *a*, and *s* for great, (ag)grand, and stellated. The final stellation, the *great grand stellated polydodecahedron* contains them all as *gaspD*.

::: {#o36 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

All ten polychora have \[3,3,5\] ([H<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/297004#Finite_Coxeter_groups)) [hexacosichoric symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexacosichoric_symmetry). They are generated from 6 related [Goursat tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/24104450) [rational-order symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/24104450#Rational_solutions): \[3,5,5/2\], \[5,5/2,5\], \[5,3,5/2\], \[5/2,5,5/2\], \[5,5/2,3\], and \[3,3,5/2\].

Each group has 2 regular star-polychora, except for two groups which are self-dual, having only one. So there are 4 dual-pairs and 2 self-dual forms among the ten regular star polychora.

:::
