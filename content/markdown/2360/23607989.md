# Noether identities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noether identities"}

In mathematics, **Noether identities** characterize the degeneracy of a Lagrangian system. Given a Lagrangian system and its [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/24585634) *L*, Noether identities can be defined as a [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) whose kernel contains a range of the [Euler–Lagrange operator](wikipedia://en/24585634) of *L*. Any [Euler–Lagrange operator](wikipedia://en/24585634) obeys Noether identities which therefore are separated into the trivial and non-trivial ones. A [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/24585634) *L* is called degenerate if the [Euler–Lagrange operator](wikipedia://en/24585634) of *L* satisfies non-trivial Noether identities. In this case [Euler&ndash;Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler&ndash;Lagrange_equation) are not independent.

:::

Noether identities need not be independent, but satisfy first-stage Noether identities, which are subject to the second-stage Noether identities and so on. Higher-stage Noether identities also are separated into trivial and non-trivial cases. A degenerate Lagrangian is called reducible if there exist non-trivial higher-stage Noether identities. [Yang–Mills gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang&ndash;Mills_theory) and [gauge gravitation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_gravitation_theory) exemplify irreducible Lagrangian field theories.

Different variants of [second Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/18021657) state the one-to-one correspondence between the non-trivial reducible Noether identities and the non-trivial reducible [gauge symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_symmetry_%28mathematics%29). Formulated in a very general setting, [second Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/18021657) associates to the Koszul–Tate complex of reducible Noether identities, parameterized by [antifields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalin&ndash;Vilkovisky_formalism), the BRST complex of reducible gauge symmetries parameterized by [ghosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faddeev&ndash;Popov_ghost). This is the case of [covariant classical field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariant_classical_field_theory) and Lagrangian [BRST theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRST_formalism).
