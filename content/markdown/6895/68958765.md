# Ellen Burrell

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ellen Burrell"}

**Ellen Louisa Burrell** (June 12, 1850 – December 3, 1938) was an American mathematics professor, head of the Department of Pure Mathematics at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) from 1897 to 1916.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Burrell was born in [Lockport, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockport_%28city%29,_New_York), the daughter of Myron Louis Burrell and Mary Jones Burrell. She earned a bachelor's degree from Wellesley College in 1880, in the same class as her future colleagues [Katharine Lee Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Lee_Bates) and [Charlotte Fitch Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Fitch_Roberts).[^o4] She went to Germany for further studies at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) in 1896 and 1897.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Burrell taught at [Rockford Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockford_University) in Illinois for several years, from 1881 to 1886. She returned to Wellesley to teach in 1886.[^o7] In 1897, as a solution to her contentious relationship with fellow mathematics professor [Ellen Hayes](wikipedia://en/30803079), she was made head of the Department of Pure Mathematics (and Hayes became head of Applied Mathematics).[^o8] Her department included professors [Roxana Vivian](wikipedia://en/46199463) and [Helen Abbott Merrill](wikipedia://en/17201962).[^o9] She and Hayes both retired from Wellesley in 1916, and the departments were reunited.[^o10] She was also curator of the college's herbarium.[^o5] Her class notes were privately published as "The Number System" and "Synthetic Projection Geometry".[^o8]

Burrell attended the fourth colloquium of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in Boston in 1903,[^o11] and another 1903 meeting of the society held at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o12] She was also active in the Association of Mathematics Teachers of New England.[^o13] She visited the American School for Girls in Constantinople in 1907.[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Burrell enthusiastically voted for [Warren G. Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_G._Harding) for president in 1920.[^o16] She died in 1938, aged 88 years, in Roxbury, Massachusetts.[^o5] Her papers are in the Wellesley College Archives.[^o8]
