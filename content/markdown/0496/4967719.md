# Fisher's z-distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fisher's z-distribution"}

*Image: Youngronaldfisher2.JPG.* Ronald Fisher

**Fisher's *z*-distribution** is the [statistical distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_distribution) of half the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of an [*F*-distribution](wikipedia://en/332116) [variate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variate):

:::

$$z = \frac 1 2 \log F$$

It was first described by [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) in a paper delivered at the [International Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Congress) of 1924 in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto).[^o2] Nowadays one usually uses the *F*-distribution instead.

The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) and [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) can be found by using the *F*-distribution at the value of $x' = e^{2x} \,$. However, the mean and variance do not follow the same transformation.

The probability density function is[^o3][^o4]

$$f(x; d_1, d_2) = \frac{2d_1^{d_1/2} d_2^{d_2/2}}{B(d_1/2, d_2/2)} \frac{e^{d_1 x}}{\left(d_1 e^{2 x} + d_2\right)^{(d_1+d_2)/2}},$$

where *B* is the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660).

When the [degrees of freedom](wikipedia://en/2321606) becomes large ($d_1, d_2 \rightarrow \infty$), the distribution approaches [normality](wikipedia://en/21462) with mean[^o3]

$$\bar{x} = \frac 1 2 \left( \frac 1 {d_2} - \frac 1 {d_1} \right)$$

and variance

$$\sigma^2_x = \frac 1 2 \left( \frac 1 {d_1} + \frac 1 {d_2} \right).$$

## Related distribution {#Related_distribution}

- If $X \sim \operatorname{FisherZ}(n,m)$ then $e^{2X} \sim \operatorname{F}(n,m) \,$ ([*F*-distribution](wikipedia://en/332116))
- If $X \sim \operatorname{F}(n,m)$ then $\tfrac{\log X}{2} \sim \operatorname{FisherZ}(n,m)$
