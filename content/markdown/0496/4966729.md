# Characteristically simple group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Characteristically simple group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **characteristically simple** if it has no proper nontrivial [characteristic subgroups](wikipedia://en/7198). Characteristically simple groups are sometimes also termed **elementary groups**. Characteristically simple is a *weaker* condition than being a [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600), as simple groups must not have any proper nontrivial [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918), which include characteristic subgroups.

:::

A [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is characteristically simple if and only if it is a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) simple groups. In particular, a finite [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012) is characteristically simple if and only if it is an [elementary abelian group](wikipedia://en/4843202). This does not hold in general for [infinite groups](wikipedia://en/4587030); for example, the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) form a characteristically simple group that is not a direct product of simple groups.

A **minimal normal subgroup** of a group *G* is a nontrivial normal subgroup *N* of *G* such that the only proper [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *N* that is normal in *G* is the trivial subgroup. Every minimal normal subgroup of a group is characteristically simple. This follows from the fact that a characteristic subgroup of a normal subgroup is normal.
