# Absolutely simple group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Absolutely simple group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **absolutely simple** if it has no proper nontrivial [serial subgroups](wikipedia://en/40735495).[^o2] That is, $G$ is an absolutely simple group if the only serial subgroups of $G$ are $\{ e \}$ (the trivial subgroup), and $G$ itself (the whole group).

:::

In the finite case, a group is absolutely simple if and only if it is [simple](wikipedia://en/47600). However, in the infinite case, absolutely simple is a stronger property than simple. The property of being strictly simple is somewhere in between.
