# William S. Massey

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="William S. Massey"}

**William Schumacher Massey** (August 23, 1920[^o4] – June 17, 2017) was an American mathematician, known for his work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). The [Massey product](wikipedia://en/2676948) is named for him. He worked also on the formulation of [spectral sequences](wikipedia://en/803894) by means of [exact couples](wikipedia://en/9626951), and wrote several textbooks, including *A Basic Course in Algebraic Topology* ().

:::

## Life {#Life}

William Massey was born in [Granville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville,_Illinois), in 1920, the son of Robert and Alma Massey, and grew up in [Peoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois). He was an undergraduate student at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). After serving as a meteorologist aboard aircraft carriers in the [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy) for 4 years during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he received a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1949.[^o6] His dissertation, titled *Classification of mappings of an $(n+1)$-dimensional space into an n-sphere,* was written under the direction of [Norman Steenrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Steenrod). He spent two additional years at Princeton as a post-doctoral research assistant. He then taught for ten years on the faculty of [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). In 1958 he was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o8] From 1960 till his retirement he was a professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). He died on June 17, 2017, in [Hamden, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamden,_Connecticut). He had 23 PhD students, including Donald Kahn, Larry Smith, and [Robert Greenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Greenblatt_%28anti_war_activist%29).
