# Manifold decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Manifold decomposition"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* may be decomposed or split by writing *M* as a combination of smaller pieces. When doing so, one must specify both what those pieces are and how they are put together to form *M*.

:::

Manifold decomposition[^o2] works in two directions: one can start with the smaller pieces and build up a manifold, or start with a large manifold and decompose it. The latter has proven a very useful way to study manifolds: without tools like decomposition, it is sometimes very hard to understand a manifold. In particular, it has been useful in attempts to classify [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) and also in proving the higher-dimensional [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798).

The table below is a summary of the various manifold-decomposition techniques. The column labeled "*M*" indicates what kind of manifold can be decomposed; the column labeled "How it is decomposed" indicates how, starting with a manifold, one can decompose it into smaller pieces; the column labeled "The pieces" indicates what the pieces can be; and the column labeled "How they are combined" indicates how the smaller pieces are combined to make the large manifold.

::: {#o3 .table cue=table}

| Type of decomposition | *M* | How it is decomposed | The pieces | How they are combined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440976) | Depends on dimension. In dimension 3, a theorem by [Edwin E. Moise](wikipedia://en/19236494) gives that every 3-manifold has a unique triangulation, unique up to common subdivision. In dimension 4, not all manifolds are triangulable. For higher dimensions, general existence of triangulations is unknown. |  | [Simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) | Glue together pairs of codimension-one faces |
| [Jaco-Shalen/Johannson torus decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599) | [Irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_%28mathematics%29), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable), [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) | Cut along embedded [tori](wikipedia://en/74800) | [Atoroidal](wikipedia://en/1241614) or [Seifert-fibered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert-fibered_space) 3-manifolds | [Union along their boundary, using the trivial homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/1240842) |
| [Prime decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29) | Essentially [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) and [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257). The decomposition is unique when the manifold is orientable. | Cut along embedded [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859#Topology); then [union by the trivial homeomorphism along the resultant boundaries](wikipedia://en/1240842) with disjoint [balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball#Topology). | [Prime manifolds](wikipedia://en/19045780) | [Connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) |
| [Heegaard splitting](wikipedia://en/864418) | [Closed](wikipedia://en/669475), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) |  | Two [handlebodies](wikipedia://en/864472) of equal genus | [Union along the boundary by some homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/1240842) |
| [Handle decomposition](wikipedia://en/902592) | Any compact ([smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold)) [n-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-manifold) (and the decomposition is never unique) | Through [Morse functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function) a handle is associated to each [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424). | [Balls](wikipedia://en/160556#Topology) (called [handles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handle_%28mathematics%29)) | [Union along a subset of the boundaries](wikipedia://en/1240842). Note that the handles must generally be added in a specific order. |
| [Haken hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haken_hierarchy) | Any [Haken manifold](wikipedia://en/236567) | Cut along a sequence of incompressible surfaces | [3-balls](wikipedia://en/160556#Topology) |  |
| Disk decomposition | Certain [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) | [Suture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutured_manifold) the manifold, then cut along special surfaces (condition on boundary curves and sutures...) | [3-balls](wikipedia://en/160556#Topology) |  |
| [Open book decomposition](wikipedia://en/5608629) | Any [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) |  | A [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) and a family of [2-manifolds](wikipedia://en/27865) that share a [boundary](wikipedia://en/2073470) with that link |  |
| [Trigenus](wikipedia://en/3789546) | [Compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) 3-manifolds | [Surgeries](wikipedia://en/5961115) | Three orientable handlebodies | Unions along subsurfaces on boundaries of handlebodies |

:::
