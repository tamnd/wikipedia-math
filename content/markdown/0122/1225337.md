# Principal part

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Principal part"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **principal part** has several independent meanings but usually refers to the negative-power portion of the [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213) of a function.

:::

## Laurent series definition {#Laurent_series_definition}

The **principal part** at $z=a$ of a function

$$f(z) = \sum_{k=-\infty}^\infty a_k (z-a)^k$$

is the portion of the [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213) consisting of terms with negative degree.[^o3] That is,

$$\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_{-k} (z-a)^{-k}$$

is the principal part of $f$ at $a$. If the Laurent series has an inner radius of convergence of $0$, then $f(z)$ has an [essential singularity](wikipedia://en/80862) at $a$ if and only if the principal part is an infinite sum. If the inner radius of convergence is not $0$, then $f(z)$ may be regular at $a$ despite the Laurent series having an infinite principal part.

## Other definitions {#Other_definitions}

### Calculus {#Calculus}

Consider the difference between the function [differential](wikipedia://en/23997203) and the actual increment:

$$\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=f'(x)+\varepsilon$$

$$\Delta y=f'(x)\Delta x +\varepsilon \Delta x = dy+\varepsilon \Delta x$$

The differential *dy* is sometimes called the **principal (linear) part** of the function increment *Δy*.

### Distribution theory {#Distribution_theory}

The term **principal part** is also used for certain kinds of [distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) having a [singular support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_support) at a single point.
