# Vaughan Jones

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Vaughan Jones"}

**Sir Vaughan Frederick Randal Jones** (31 December 1952 – 6 September 2020) was a [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealanders) mathematician known for his work on [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) and [knot polynomials](wikipedia://en/313735). He was awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1990.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Jones was born in [Gisborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisborne,_New_Zealand), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand), on 31 December 1952.[^o4] He was brought up in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_New_Zealand), New Zealand, where he attended [St Peter's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Peter's_School,_Cambridge). He subsequently transferred to [Auckland Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Grammar_School) after winning the Gillies Scholarship,[^o5] and graduated in 1969 from Auckland Grammar.[^o6] He went on to complete his undergraduate studies at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), obtaining a BSc in 1972 and an MSc in 1973. For his graduate studies, he went to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) where he completed his PhD at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) in 1979. His thesis, titled *Actions of finite groups on the hyperfinite II<sub>1</sub> factor*, was written under the supervision of [André Haefliger](wikipedia://en/20280849), and won him the Vacheron Constantin Prize.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Jones moved to the United States in 1980. There, he taught at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (1980–1981), and the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1981–1985), before being appointed as professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o8][^o9] His work on [knot polynomials](wikipedia://en/313735), with the discovery of what is now called the [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642), was from an unexpected direction with origins in the theory of [von Neumann algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebras),[^o5] an area of [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) already much developed by [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340). It led to the solution of a number of classical problems of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), to increased interest in [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774),[^o10] and to the development of [quantum topology](wikipedia://en/28075395).

Jones taught at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) as Stevenson Distinguished Professor of mathematics from 2011 until his death.[^o11] He remained Professor Emeritus at University of California, Berkeley, where he had been on the faculty from 1985 to 2011[^o12] and was a Distinguished Alumni Professor at the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland).[^o13]

Jones was made an honorary vice-president for life of the [International Guild of Knot Tyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Guild_of_Knot_Tyers) in 1992.[^o6] The Jones Medal, created by the [Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Te_Apārangi) in 2010, is named after him.[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Jones met his wife, Martha Myers, during a ski camp for foreign students while they were studying in Switzerland.[^o16] She was there as a [Fulbright scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program),[^o16] and subsequently became an associate professor of medicine, health and society.[^o6] Together, they have three children.[^o5][^o6]

Jones died on 6 September 2020 at age 67 from health complications resulting from a severe [ear infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ear_infection).[^o17][^o5]

Jones was a certified barista.[^o18]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

- 1990 – awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)[^o5]
- 1990 – elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o20][^o21]
- 1991 – awarded the [Rutherford Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_Medal_%28Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand%29) by the [Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand)[^o6][^o14]
- 1991 – awarded the degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) by the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland)[^o22]
- 1991 – elected Honorary Fellow of the Royal Society of New Zealand[^o23]
- 1992 – elected to the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) as a Corresponding Fellow[^o24]
- 1992 – awarded a [Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) Professorship at the University of California Berkeley[^o25]
- 2002 – appointed [Distinguished Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Companion_of_the_New_Zealand_Order_of_Merit) (DCNZM) in the [2002 Queen's Birthday and Golden Jubilee Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Birthday_Honours_%28New_Zealand%29), for services to mathematics[^o26]
- 2009 – his DCNZM redesignated to a [Knight Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Companion_of_the_New_Zealand_Order_of_Merit) in the [2009 Special Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Special_Honours_%28New_Zealand%29)[^o27]
- 2012 – elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o28]
