# Adams operation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adams operation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **Adams operation**, denoted ψ<sup>*k*</sup> for natural numbers *k*, is a [cohomology operation](wikipedia://en/1226719) in [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468), or any allied operation in [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) or other types of algebraic construction, defined on a pattern introduced by [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959). The basic idea is to implement some fundamental identities in [symmetric function](wikipedia://en/22510359) theory, at the level of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) or other representing object in more abstract theories.

:::

Adams operations can be defined more generally in any [λ-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_ring).

## Adams operations in K-theory {#Adams_operations_in_K-theory}

Adams operations ψ<sup>*k*</sup> on K theory (algebraic or topological) are characterized by the following properties.

1. ψ<sup>*k*</sup> are [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411).
1. ψ<sup>*k*</sup>(l)= l<sup>k</sup> if l is the class of a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366).
1. ψ<sup>*k*</sup> are [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial).

The fundamental idea is that for a vector bundle *V* on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X*, there is an analogy between Adams operators and [exterior powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power), in which

ψ<sup>*k*</sup>(*V*) is to Λ<sup>*k*</sup>(*V*)

as

the [power sum](wikipedia://en/10195749) Σ α<sup>*k*</sup> is to the *k*-th [elementary symmetric function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_symmetric_function) σ<sub>*k*</sub>

of the roots α of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) *P*(*t*). (Cf. [Newton's identities](wikipedia://en/2468892).) Here Λ<sup>*k*</sup> denotes the *k*-th exterior power. From classical algebra it is known that the power sums are certain [integral polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_polynomial) *Q*<sub>*k*</sub> in the σ<sub>*k*</sub>. The idea is to apply the same polynomials to the Λ<sup>*k*</sup>(*V*), taking the place of σ<sub>*k*</sub>. This calculation can be defined in a *K*-group, in which vector bundles may be formally combined by addition, subtraction and multiplication ([tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844)). The polynomials here are called **Newton polynomials** (not, however, the [Newton polynomials](wikipedia://en/216333) of [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) theory).

Justification of the expected properties comes from the line bundle case, where *V* is a [Whitney sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum) of line bundles. In this special case the result of any Adams operation is naturally a vector bundle, not a linear combination of ones in *K*-theory. Treating the line bundle direct factors formally as roots is something rather standard in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) (cf. the [Leray–Hirsch theorem](wikipedia://en/8620449)). In general a mechanism for reducing to that case comes from the [splitting principle](wikipedia://en/15958803) for vector bundles.

## Adams operations in group representation theory {#Adams_operations_in_group_representation_theory}

The Adams operation has a simple expression in [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695) theory.[^o4] Let *G* be a group and ρ a representation of *G* with character χ. The representation ψ<sup>*k*</sup>(ρ) has character

$$\chi_{\psi^k(\rho)}(g) = \chi_\rho(g^k) \ .$$
