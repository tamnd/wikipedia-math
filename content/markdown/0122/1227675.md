# David Gale

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Gale"}

**David Gale** (December 13, 1921 – March 7, 2008) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist). He was a professor emeritus at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), affiliated with the departments of mathematics, economics, and industrial engineering and operations research. He has contributed to the fields of [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255), [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), and [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201).

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Gale graduated with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College), obtained a [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1947, and earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1949. He taught at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) from 1950 to 1965 and then joined the faculty at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

Gale lived in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), and [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), with his partner [Sandra Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Gilbert), feminist literary scholar and poet. He has three daughters and two grandsons. He died on March 7, 2008 of a heart attack at the age of 86.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Gale's contributions to [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) include an early proof of the existence of competitive [equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45938), his solution of the *n*-dimensional [Ramsey problem](wikipedia://en/1498759), in the [theory of optimal economic growth](wikipedia://en/4701722).

Gale and F. M. Stewart initiated the study of infinite games with [perfect information](wikipedia://en/321438). This work led to fundamental contributions to [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).

Gale is the inventor of the game of [Bridg-It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridg-It) (also known as "Game of Gale") and [Chomp](wikipedia://en/1306265).

Gale played a fundamental role in the development of the theory of [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) and linear inequalities. His classic 1960 book *The Theory of Linear Economic Models* continues to be a standard reference for this area.

The [Gale transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gale_transform) is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) on sets of points in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135). The concept is important in [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization), [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

Gale's 1962 paper with [Lloyd Shapley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Shapley) on the [stable marriage problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_marriage_problem) provides the first formal statement and proof of a problem that has far-reaching implications in many matching markets. The resulting [Gale–Shapley algorithm](wikipedia://en/26978338) is currently being applied in New York and Boston public school systems in assigning students to schools. In 2012 The Nobel Prize in Economics was awarded to Shapley for this work.[^o5]

Gale wrote a Mathematical Entertainments column for *[The Mathematical Intelligencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Intelligencer)* from 1991 through 1997. The book *Tracking the Automatic Ant* collects these columns.

In 2004 Gale developed MathSite, a pedagogic website that uses interactive exhibits to illustrate important mathematical ideas. MathSite won the 2007 [Pirelli Internetional Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli_Internetional_Award) for Science Communication in Mathematics.
