# Frank Adams

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Adams"}

**John Frank Adams** [^o4] (5 November 1930 – 7 January 1989) was a British mathematician, one of the major contributors to [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Woolwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolwich), a suburb in south-east London, and attended [Bedford School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_School). He had a younger brother, [Michael Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Adams_%28RAF_officer%29), who rose to the rank of Air Vice-Marshal in the Royal Air Force. He began his academic career at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), as a student of [Abram Besicovitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abram_Samoilovitch_Besicovitch), but soon switched to [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1956. His thesis, written under the direction of [Shaun Wylie](wikipedia://en/8753274), was titled *On spectral sequences and self-obstruction invariants*. He held the [Fielden Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielden_Chair_of_Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) (1964–1970), and became [Lowndean Professor of Astronomy and Geometry](wikipedia://en/648990) at the University of Cambridge (1970–1989). He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1964.

His interests included [mountaineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaineering)—he would demonstrate how to climb right round a table at parties (a [Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) traverse)—and the game of [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29).

He died in a car crash in [Brampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brampton,_Cambridgeshire). There is a memorial plaque for him in the Chapel of [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).

## Work {#Work}

In the 1950s, [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) was at an early stage of development, and unsolved problems abounded. Adams made a number of important theoretical advances in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), but his innovations were always motivated by specific problems. Influenced by the French school of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), he reformulated and strengthened their method of [killing homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/1104697) in [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) terms, creating the basic tool of [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655) now known as the [Adams spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/4720689). This begins with [Ext groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ext_group) calculated over the ring of [cohomology operations](wikipedia://en/1226719), which is the [Steenrod algebra](wikipedia://en/2146220) in the classical case. He used this [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) to attack the celebrated [Hopf invariant one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_invariant_one) problem, which he completely solved in a 1960 paper by making a deep analysis of [secondary cohomology operations](wikipedia://en/35123141). The [Adams–Novikov spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams–Novikov_spectral_sequence) is an analogue of the Adams spectral sequence using an [extraordinary cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraordinary_cohomology_theory) in place of classical cohomology: it is a computational tool of great potential scope.

Adams was also a pioneer in the application of [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748). He invented the [Adams operations](wikipedia://en/1226666) in K-theory, which are derived from the [exterior powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power); they are now also widely used in purely algebraic contexts. Adams introduced them in a 1962 paper to solve the famous [vector fields on spheres](wikipedia://en/3100908) problem. Subsequently he used them to investigate the [Adams conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_conjecture), which is concerned (in one instance) with the image of the [J-homomorphism](wikipedia://en/3061995) in the stable [homotopy groups of spheres](wikipedia://en/1104697). A later paper of Adams and [Michael F. Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) uses the Adams operations to give an extremely elegant and much faster version of the above-mentioned [Hopf invariant one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_invariant_one) result.

In 1974 Adams became the first recipient of the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize), awarded by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o10] He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1957–58.[^o11]

Adams had many talented students, and was highly influential in the development of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) in Britain and worldwide. His [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) lectures were published in a 1996 series titled "Chicago Lectures in Mathematics Series", such as *Lectures on Exceptional Lie Groups* and *Stable Homotopy and Generalised Homology* .

## Recognition {#Recognition}

The main mathematics research seminar room in the [Alan Turing Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing_Building) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) is named in his honour.
