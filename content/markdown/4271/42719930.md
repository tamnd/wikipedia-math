# Idempotent relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Idempotent relation"}

In mathematics, an **idempotent binary relation** is a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) *R* on a set *X* (a subset of [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) *X* × *X*) for which the [composition of relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relation_composition) *R* ∘ *R* is the same as *R*.[^o2][^o3] This notion generalizes that of an [idempotent function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_function) to relations.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

As a shorthand, the notation *xRy* indicates that a pair (*x*,*y*) belongs to a relation *R*. The [composition of relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relation_composition) *R* ∘ *R* is the relation *S* defined by setting *xSz* to be true for a pair of elements *x* and *z* in *X* whenever there exists *y* in *X* with *xRy* and *yRz* both true. *R* is idempotent if *R* = *S*.

Equivalently, relation *R* is idempotent if and only if the following two properties are true:

- *R* is a [transitive relation](wikipedia://en/200463), meaning that *R* ∘ *R* ⊆ *R*. Equivalently, in terms of individual elements, for every *x*, *y*, and *z* for which *xRy* and *yRz* are both true, *xRz* is also true.
- *R* ∘ *R* ⊇ *R*. Equivalently, in terms of individual elements, for every *x* and *z* for which *xRz* is true, there exists *y* with *xRy* and *yRz* both true. Some authors call such an *R* a [dense relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_relation).[^o6]

Because idempotence incorporates both transitivity and the second property above, it is a stronger property than transitivity.

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For example, the relation \< on the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) is idempotent. The strict ordering relation is transitive, and whenever two rational numbers *x* and *z* obey the relation *x* \< *z* there necessarily exists another rational number *y* between them (for instance, their average) with *x* \< *y* and *y* \< *z*.

In contrast, the same ordering relation \< on the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) is not idempotent. It is still transitive, but does not obey the second property of an idempotent relation. For instance, 1 \< 2 but there does not exist any other integer *y* between 1 and 2.

An [outer product of logical vectors](wikipedia://en/4109196#Logical_vectors) forms an idempotent relation. Such a relation may be contained in a more complex relation, in which case it is called a **concept** in that larger context. The description of relations in terms of their concepts is called [formal concept analysis](wikipedia://en/313845).

:::

::: {#o10 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

Idempotent relations have been used as an example to illustrate the application of Mechanized Formalisation of mathematics using the interactive theorem prover Isabelle/HOL. Besides checking the mathematical properties of finite idempotent relations, an algorithm for counting the number of idempotent relations has been derived in Isabelle/HOL.[^o11][^o12]

Idempotent relations defined on [weakly countably compact spaces](wikipedia://en/9861462) have also been shown to satisfy "condition Γ": that is, every nontrivial idempotent relation on such a space contains points $\langle x,x\rangle, \langle x,y\rangle,\langle y,y\rangle$ for some $x,y$. This is used to show that certain [subspaces](wikipedia://en/910926) of an uncountable [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of spaces, known as Mahavier products, cannot be [metrizable](wikipedia://en/20018) when defined by a nontrivial idempotent relation.[^o13]

:::
