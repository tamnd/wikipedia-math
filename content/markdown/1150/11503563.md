# H-vector

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="H-vector"}

In [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), the ***h*-vector** of a [simplicial polytope](wikipedia://en/8853373) is a fundamental [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) of the polytope which encodes the number of faces of different dimensions and allows one to express the [Dehn–Sommerville equations](wikipedia://en/8276156) in a particularly simple form. A characterization of the set of *h*-vectors of simplicial polytopes was conjectured by [Peter McMullen](wikipedia://en/40964822)[^o2] and proved by [Lou Billera](wikipedia://en/22444348) and Carl W. Lee[^o3][^o4] and [Richard Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397)[^o5] ([*g*-theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-theorem)). The definition of *h*-vector applies to arbitrary [abstract simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/723105). The [*g*-conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-conjecture) stated that for [simplicial spheres](wikipedia://en/22359636), all possible *h*-vectors occur already among the *h*-vectors of the boundaries of convex simplicial polytopes. It was proven in December 2018 by [Karim Adiprasito](wikipedia://en/47874333).[^o6][^o7]

:::

Stanley introduced a generalization of the *h*-vector, the **toric *h*-vector**, which is defined for an arbitrary [ranked poset](wikipedia://en/10158881), and proved that for the class of [Eulerian posets](wikipedia://en/26703335), the Dehn–Sommerville equations continue to hold. A different, more combinatorial, generalization of the *h*-vector that has been extensively studied is the **flag *h*-vector** of a ranked poset. For Eulerian posets, it can be more concisely expressed by means of a noncommutative polynomial in two variables called the ***cd*-index**.

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let Δ be an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) of dimension *d* − 1 with *f*<sub>*i*</sub> *i*-dimensional faces and *f*<sub>−1</sub> = 1. These numbers are arranged into the ***f*-vector** of Δ,

$$f(\Delta)=(f_{-1},f_0,\ldots,f_{d-1}).$$

An important special case occurs when Δ is the boundary of a *d*-dimensional convex polytope.

For *k* = 0, 1, …, *d*, let

$$h_k = \sum_{i=0}^k (-1)^{k-i}\binom{d-i}{k-i}f_{i-1}.$$

The tuple

$$h(\Delta)=(h_0,h_1,\ldots,h_d)$$

is called the ***h*-vector** of Δ. In particular, $h_{0} = 1$, $h_{1} = f_{0} - d$, and $h_{d} = (-1)^{d} (1 - \chi(\Delta))$, where $\chi(\Delta)$ is the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420#Topological_definition) of $\Delta$. The *f*-vector and the *h*-vector uniquely determine each other through the linear relation

$$\sum_{i=0}^{d}f_{i-1}(t-1)^{d-i}= \sum_{k=0}^{d}h_{k}t^{d-k},$$

from which it follows that, for $i = 0, \dotsc, d$,

$$f_{i-1} = \sum_{k=0}^i \binom{d-k}{i-k} h_{k}.$$

In particular, $f_{d-1} = h_{0} + h_{1} + \dotsb + h_{d}$. Let *R* = **k**\[Δ\] be the [Stanley–Reisner ring](wikipedia://en/24394535) of Δ. Then its [Hilbert–Poincaré series](wikipedia://en/17006296) can be expressed as

$$P_{R}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{f_{i-1}t^i}{(1-t)^{i}}= \frac{h_0+h_1t+\cdots+h_d t^d}{(1-t)^d}.$$

This motivates the definition of the *h*-vector of a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661) [positively graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) of [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) *d* as the numerator of its Hilbert–Poincaré series written with the denominator (1 − *t*)<sup>*d*</sup>.

The *h*-vector is closely related to the *h*<sup>\*</sup>-vector for a convex lattice polytope, see [Ehrhart polynomial](wikipedia://en/316904).

:::

## Recurrence relation {#Recurrence_relation}

The $\textstyle h$-vector $(h_{0}, h_{1}, \dotsc, h_{d})$ can be computed from the $\textstyle f$-vector $(f_{-1}, f_{0}, \dotsc, f_{d-1})$ by using the recurrence relation

$$h^{i}_{0} = 1, \qquad -1 \le i \le d$$

$$h^{i}_{i+1} = f_{i}, \qquad -1 \le i \le d-1$$

$$h^{i}_{k} = h^{i-1}_{k} - h^{i-1}_{k-1}, \qquad 1 \le k \le i \le d.$$

and finally setting $\textstyle h_{k} = h^{d}_{k}$ for $\textstyle 0 \le k \le d$. For small examples, one can use this method to compute $\textstyle h$-vectors quickly by hand by recursively filling the entries of an array similar to [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497). For example, consider the boundary complex $\textstyle \Delta$ of an [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635). The $\textstyle f$-vector of $\textstyle \Delta$ is $\textstyle (1, 6, 12, 8)$. To compute the $\textstyle h$-vector of $\Delta$, construct a triangular array by first writing $d+2$ $\textstyle 1$s down the left edge and the $\textstyle f$-vector down the right edge.

$$\begin{matrix} & & & & 1 & & & \\ & & & 1 & & 6 & & \\ & & 1 & & & & 12 & \\ & 1 & & & & & & 8 \\ 1 & & & & & & & & 0 \end{matrix}$$

(We set $f_{d} = 0$ just to make the array triangular.) Then, starting from the top, fill each remaining entry by **subtracting** its upper-left neighbor from its upper-right neighbor. In this way, we generate the following array:

$$\begin{matrix} & & & & 1 & & & \\ & & & 1 & & 6 & & \\ & & 1 & & 5 & & 12 & \\ & 1 & & 4 & & 7 & & 8 \\ 1 & & 3 & & 3 & & 1 & & 0 \end{matrix}$$

The entries of the bottom row (apart from the final $0$) are the entries of the $\textstyle h$-vector. Hence, the $\textstyle h$-vector of $\textstyle \Delta$ is $\textstyle (1, 3, 3, 1)$.

## Toric *h*-vector {#Toric_h-vector}

To an arbitrary graded poset *P*, Stanley associated a pair of polynomials *f*(*P*,*x*) and *g*(*P*,*x*). Their definition is recursive in terms of the polynomials associated to intervals \[0,*y*\] for all *y* ∈ *P*, y ≠ 1, viewed as ranked posets of lower rank (0 and 1 denote the minimal and the maximal elements of *P*). The coefficients of *f*(*P*,*x*) form the **toric *h*-vector** of *P*. When *P* is an [Eulerian poset](wikipedia://en/26703335) of rank *d* + 1 such that *P* − 1 is simplicial, the toric *h*-vector coincides with the ordinary *h*-vector constructed using the numbers *f*<sub>*i*</sub> of elements of *P* − 1 of given rank *i* + 1. In this case the toric *h*-vector of *P* satisfies the [Dehn–Sommerville equations](wikipedia://en/8276156)

$$h_k = h_{d-k}.$$

The reason for the adjective "toric" is a connection of the toric *h*-vector with the [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) of a certain [projective](wikipedia://en/320469) [toric variety](wikipedia://en/737087) *X* whenever *P* is the boundary complex of rational convex polytope. Namely, the components are the dimensions of the even [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) groups of *X*:

$$h_k = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \operatorname{IH}^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})$$

(the odd [intersection cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_cohomology) groups of *X* are all zero). The Dehn–Sommerville equations are a manifestation of the [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) in the intersection cohomology of *X*. [Kalle Karu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalle_Karu) proved that the toric *h*-vector of a polytope is unimodal, regardless of whether the polytope is rational or not.[^o12]

## Flag *h*-vector and *cd*-index {#Flag_h-vector_and_cd-index}

A different generalization of the notions of *f*-vector and *h*-vector of a convex polytope has been extensively studied. Let $P$ be a finite [graded poset](wikipedia://en/2514970) of rank *n*, so that each [maximal chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_chain) in $P$ has length *n*. For any $S$, a subset of $\left\{0, \ldots, n\right\}$, let $\alpha_P(S)$ denote the number of chains in $P$ whose ranks constitute the set $S$. More formally, let

$$rk: P\to\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$$

be the rank function of $P$ and let $P_S$ be the **$S$-rank selected subposet**, which consists of the elements from $P$ whose rank is in $S$:

$$P_S=\{x\in P: rk(x)\in S\}.$$

Then $\alpha_P(S)$ is the number of the maximal chains in $P_S$ and the function

$$S \mapsto \alpha_P(S)$$

is called the **flag *f*-vector** of *P*. The function

$$S \mapsto \beta_P(S), \quad \beta_P(S) = \sum_{T \subseteq S} (-1)^{|S|-|T|} \alpha_P(S)$$

is called the **flag *h*-vector** of $P$. By the [inclusion–exclusion principle](wikipedia://en/342684),

$$\alpha_P(S) = \sum_{T\subseteq S}\beta_P(T).$$

The flag *f*- and *h*-vectors of $P$ refine the ordinary *f*- and *h*-vectors of its [order complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_complex) $\Delta(P)$:[^o14]

$$f_{i-1}(\Delta(P)) = \sum_{|S|=i} \alpha_P(S), \quad h_{i}(\Delta(P)) = \sum_{|S|=i} \beta_P(S).$$

The flag *h*-vector of $P$ can be displayed via a polynomial in noncommutative variables *a* and *b*. For any subset $S$ of {1,…,*n*}, define the corresponding monomial in *a* and *b*,

$$u_S = u_1 \cdots u_n, \quad u_i=a \text{ for } i\notin S, u_i=b \text{ for } i\in S.$$

Then the noncommutative generating function for the flag *h*-vector of *P* is defined by

$$\Psi_P(a,b) = \sum_{S} \beta_P(S) u_{S}.$$

From the relation between *α*<sub>*P*</sub>(*S*) and *β*<sub>*P*</sub>(*S*), the noncommutative generating function for the flag *f*-vector of *P* is

$$\Psi_P(a,a+b) = \sum_{S} \alpha_P(S) u_{S}.$$

[Margaret Bayer](wikipedia://en/63919419) and [Louis Billera](wikipedia://en/22444348) determined the most general linear relations that hold between the components of the flag *h*-vector of an [Eulerian poset](wikipedia://en/26703335) *P*.[^o15]

Fine noted an elegant way to state these relations: there exists a noncommutative polynomial Φ<sub>*P*</sub>(*c*,*d*), called the ***cd*-index** of *P*, such that

$$\Psi_P(a,b) = \Phi_P(a+b, ab+ba).$$

Stanley proved that all coefficients of the *cd*-index of the boundary complex of a convex polytope are non-negative. He conjectured that this positivity phenomenon persists for a more general class of Eulerian posets that Stanley calls **Gorenstein\* complexes** and which includes [simplicial spheres](wikipedia://en/22359636) and complete fans. This conjecture was proved by [Kalle Karu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalle_Karu).[^o16] The combinatorial meaning of these non-negative coefficients (an answer to the question "what do they count?") remains unclear.
