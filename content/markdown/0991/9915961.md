# Superperfect group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superperfect group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **superperfect** when its first two [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology) are [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059): *H*<sub>1</sub>(*G*, **Z**) = *H*<sub>2</sub>(*G*, **Z**) = 0. This is stronger than a perfect group, which is one whose first homology group vanishes. In more classical terms, a superperfect group is one whose [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) and [Schur multiplier](wikipedia://en/1121587) both vanish; abelianization equals the first homology, while the Schur multiplier equals the second homology.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The first homology group of a group is the [abelianization](wikipedia://en/8847) of the group itself, since the homology of a group *G* is the homology of any [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) of type *K*(*G*, 1); the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a *K*(*G*, 1) is *G*, and the first homology of *K*(*G*, 1) is then abelianization of its fundamental group. Thus, if a group is superperfect, then it is [perfect](wikipedia://en/585271).

A [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) perfect group is superperfect if and only if it is its own [universal central extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_central_extension) (UCE), as the second homology group of a perfect group parametrizes central extensions.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For example, if *G* is the fundamental group of a [homology sphere](wikipedia://en/585388), then *G* is superperfect. The smallest finite, non-trivial superperfect group is the [binary icosahedral group](wikipedia://en/3195145) (the fundamental group of the [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) homology sphere).

The alternating group *A*<sub>5</sub> is perfect but not superperfect: it has a non-trivial central extension, the binary icosahedral group (which is in fact its UCE) is superperfect. More generally, the [projective special linear groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) PSL(*n*, *q*) are simple (hence perfect) except for PSL(2, 2) and PSL(2, 3), but not superperfect, with the [special linear groups](wikipedia://en/144823) SL(*n*,*q*) as central extensions. This family includes the binary icosahedral group (thought of as SL(2, 5)) as UCE of *A*<sub>5</sub> (thought of as PSL(2, 5)).

Every [acyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_space) is superperfect, but the converse is not true: the binary icosahedral group is superperfect, but not acyclic.

:::
