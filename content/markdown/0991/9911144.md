# (−2,3,7) pretzel knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="(−2,3,7) pretzel knot"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **(−2, 3, 7) pretzel knot**, sometimes called the **Fintushel–Stern knot** (after [Ron Fintushel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Fintushel) and [Ronald J. Stern](wikipedia://en/46991782)), is an important example of a [pretzel knot](wikipedia://en/1286130) which exhibits various interesting phenomena under three-dimensional and four-dimensional [surgery](wikipedia://en/5961115) constructions.

:::

## Mathematical properties {#Mathematical_properties}

The (−2, 3, 7) pretzel knot has 7 *exceptional* slopes, [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) slopes which give non-[hyperbolic 3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1237700). Among the enumerated knots, the only other hyperbolic knot with 7 or more is the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565), which has 10. All other hyperbolic knots are conjectured to have at most 6 exceptional slopes.
