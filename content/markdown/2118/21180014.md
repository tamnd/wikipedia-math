# Markov kernel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov kernel"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **Markov kernel** (also known as a **stochastic kernel** or **probability kernel**) is a map that in the general theory of [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) plays the role that the [transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) does in the theory of Markov processes with a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_space).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $(X,\mathcal A)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B)$ be [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333). A *Markov kernel* with source $(X,\mathcal A)$ and target $(Y,\mathcal B)$, sometimes written as $\kappa:(X,\mathcal{A})\to(Y,\mathcal{B})$, is a function $\kappa : \mathcal B \times X \to [0,1]$ with the following properties:

1. For every (fixed) $B_0 \in \mathcal B$, the map $x \mapsto \kappa(B_0, x)$ is $\mathcal A$-[measurable](wikipedia://en/44055)
1. For every (fixed) $x_0 \in X$, the map $B \mapsto \kappa(B, x_0)$ is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on $(Y, \mathcal B)$

In other words it associates to each point $x \in X$ a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) $\kappa(dy|x): B \mapsto \kappa(B, x)$ on $(Y,\mathcal B)$ such that, for every measurable set $B\in\mathcal B$, the map $x\mapsto \kappa(B, x)$ is measurable with respect to the [$\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal A$](wikipedia://en/29586).[^o5]

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### [Simple random walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_random_walk) on the integers {#Simple_random_walk_on_the_integers}

Take $X=Y=\mathbb{Z}$, and $\mathcal A = \mathcal B = \mathcal P(\mathbb{Z})$ (the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $\mathbb{Z}$). Then a Markov kernel is fully determined by the probability it assigns to singletons $\{m\},\, m \in Y = \mathbb{Z}$ for each $n \in X = \mathbb{Z}$:

$$\kappa(B|n )=\sum_{m \in B}\kappa(\{m\}|n), \qquad \forall n \in \mathbb{Z}, \, \forall B \in \mathcal B.$$

Now the random walk $\kappa$ that goes to the right with probability $p$ and to the left with probability $1 - p$ is defined by

$$\kappa(\{m\}|n)= p \delta_{m, n + 1}+ (1-p) \delta_{m, n - 1}, \quad \forall n,m \in \mathbb{Z}$$

where $\delta$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). The transition probabilities $P(m|n) = \kappa(\{m\}|n)$ for the random walk are equivalent to the Markov kernel.

### General [Markov processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) with countable state space {#General_Markov_processes_with_countable_state_space}

More generally take $X$ and $Y$ both countable and $\mathcal A = \mathcal P(X),\ \mathcal B = \mathcal P(Y)$. Again a Markov kernel is defined by the probability it assigns to singleton sets for each $i \in X$

$$\kappa(B|i)=\sum_{j \in B}\kappa(\{j\}|i), \qquad \forall i \in X, \, \forall B \in \mathcal B,$$

We define a Markov process by defining a transition probability $P(j|i) = K_{ji}$ where the numbers $K_{ji}$ define a (countable) [stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/217313) $(K_{ji})$ i.e.

$$\begin{aligned} K_{ji} &\ge 0, \qquad &\forall (j,i) \in Y\times X, \\ \sum_{j \in Y}K_{ji}&=1, \qquad &\forall i \in X.\\ \end{aligned}$$

We then define

$$\kappa(\{j\} | i) = K_{ji} = P(j|i), \qquad \forall i \in X, \quad \forall B \in \mathcal B.$$

Again the transition probability, the stochastic matrix and the Markov kernel are equivalent reformulations.

### Markov kernel defined by a kernel function and a measure {#Markov_kernel_defined_by_a_kernel_function_and_a_measure}

Let $\nu$ be a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873#Definition) on $(Y, \mathcal B)$, and $k: Y \times X\to [0, \infty]$ a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) with respect to the [product $\sigma$-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra#Product_.CF.83-algebra) $\mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$ such that

$$\int_Y k(y, x)\nu(\mathrm{d} y) = 1, \qquad \forall x \in X,$$

then $\kappa(dy |x) = k(y, x)\nu(dy)$ i.e. the mapping

$$\begin{cases} \kappa:\mathcal B \times X \to [0,1] \\ \kappa(B|x)=\int_{B}k(y, x)\nu(\mathrm{d} y) \end{cases}$$

defines a Markov kernel.[^o11] This example generalises the countable Markov process example where $\nu$ was the [counting measure](wikipedia://en/191786). Moreover it encompasses other important examples such as the convolution kernels, in particular the Markov kernels defined by the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415). The latter example includes the [Gaussian kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_kernel) on $X = Y = \mathbb R$ with $\nu(dx) = dx$ standard [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) and

$$k_t(y, x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}t}e^{-(y - x)^2/(2t^2)}.$$

### Measurable functions {#Measurable_functions}

Take $(X, \mathcal{A})$ and $(Y, \mathcal{B})$ arbitrary measurable spaces, and let $f:X \to Y$ be a measurable function. Now define $\kappa(dy|x) = \delta_{f(x)}(dy)$ i.e.

$\kappa(B|x) = \mathbf{1}_B(f(x)) = \mathbf{1}_{f^{-1}(B)}(x) = \begin{cases}1 & \text{if } f(x) \in B\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$ for all $B \in \mathcal{B}$.

Note that the indicator function $\mathbf{1}_{f^{-1}(B)}$ is $\mathcal{A}$-measurable for all $B \in \mathcal{B}$ iff $f$ is measurable.

This example allows us to think of a Markov kernel as a generalised function with a (in general) random rather than certain value. That is, it is a [multivalued function](wikipedia://en/222367) where the values are not equally weighted.

### [Galton–Watson process](wikipedia://en/350596) {#Galton–Watson_process}

As a less obvious example, take $X = \mathbb{N}, \mathcal A = \mathcal P(\mathbb{N})$, and $(Y, \mathcal B)$ the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ with the standard sigma algebra of [Borel sets](wikipedia://en/43327). Then

$$\kappa(B|n)=\begin{cases} \mathbf{1}_B(0) & n=0\\ \Pr(\xi_1 + \cdots + \xi_x \in B) & n \neq 0 \\ \end{cases}$$

where $x$ is the number of element at the state $n$, $\xi_i$ are [i.i.d.](wikipedia://en/30876902) [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) (usually with mean 0) and where $\mathbf{1}_B$ is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790). For the simple case of [coin flips](wikipedia://en/199189) this models the different levels of a [Galton board](wikipedia://en/243904).

:::

## Composition of Markov Kernels {#Composition_of_Markov_Kernels}

Given measurable spaces $(X, \mathcal A)$, $(Y, \mathcal B)$ we consider a Markov kernel $\kappa: \mathcal B \times X \to [0,1]$ as a morphism $\kappa: X \to Y$. Intuitively, rather than assigning to each $x \in X$ a sharply defined point $y \in Y$ the kernel assigns a "fuzzy" point in $Y$ which is only known with some level of uncertainty, much like actual physical measurements. If we have a third measurable space $(Z, \mathcal C)$, and probability kernels $\kappa: X \to Y$ and $\lambda: Y \to Z$, we can define a composition $\lambda \circ \kappa : X \to Z$ by the [Chapman-Kolmogorov equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman-Kolmogorov_equation)

$$(\lambda \circ \kappa) (dz|x) = \int_Y \lambda(dz | y)\kappa(dy|x).$$

The composition is associative by the [Monotone Convergence Theorem](wikipedia://en/146263) and the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) considered as a Markov kernel (i.e. the delta measure $\kappa_{1}(dx'|x) = \delta_x(dx')$) is the unit for this composition.

This composition defines the structure of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) on the measurable spaces with Markov kernels as morphisms, first defined by Lawvere,[^o15] the [category of Markov kernels](wikipedia://en/75969668).

## Probability Space defined by Probability Distribution and a Markov Kernel {#Probability_Space_defined_by_Probability_Distribution_and_a_Markov_Kernel}

A composition of a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) $(X, \mathcal A, P_X)$ and a probability kernel $\kappa: (X, \mathcal A) \to (Y, \mathcal B)$ defines a probability space $(Y, \mathcal B, P_Y = \kappa \circ P_X)$, where the probability measure is given by

$$P_Y(B) = \int_X \int_B \kappa(dy|x) P_X(dx) = \int_X \kappa(B|x)P_X(dx) = \mathbb{E}_{P_X}\kappa(B|\cdot).$$

::: {#o18 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Semidirect product {#Semidirect_product}

Let $(X, \mathcal A, P)$ be a probability space and $\kappa$ a Markov kernel from $(X, \mathcal A)$ to some $(Y, \mathcal B)$. Then there exists a unique measure $Q$ on $(X \times Y, \mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B)$, such that:

$$Q(A \times B) = \int_A \kappa(B|x)\,P(dx), \quad \forall A \in \mathcal A, \quad \forall B \in\mathcal B.$$

### Regular conditional distribution {#Regular_conditional_distribution}

Let $(S,Y)$ be a [Borel space](wikipedia://en/43327#Standard_Borel_spaces_and_Kuratowski_theorems), $X$ a $(S,Y)$-valued random variable on the measure space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ and $\mathcal G \subseteq \mathcal F$ a sub-$\sigma$-algebra. Then there exists a Markov kernel $\kappa$ from $(\Omega, \mathcal G)$ to $(S,Y)$, such that $\kappa(\cdot,B)$ is a version of the [conditional expectation](wikipedia://en/435099) $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf 1_{\{X \in B\}} \mid \mathcal G]$ for every $B \in Y$, i.e.

$$P(X \in B\mid\mathcal G)=\mathbb{E} \left [\mathbf 1_{\{X \in B\}}\mid\mathcal G \right ] = \kappa(\cdot,B), \qquad P\text{-a.s.}\,\, \forall B \in Y.$$

It is called regular conditional distribution of $X$ given $\mathcal G$ and is not uniquely defined.

:::

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

[Transition kernels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_kernel) generalize Markov kernels in the sense that for all $x \in X$, the map

$$B \mapsto \kappa(B| x)$$

can be any type of (non negative) measure, not necessarily a probability measure.

:::
