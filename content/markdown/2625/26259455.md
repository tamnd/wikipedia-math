# Maurice Nivat

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maurice Nivat"}

**Maurice Paul Nivat** (21 December 1937 – 21 September 2017) was a French [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist). His research in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) spanned the areas of [formal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_languages), [programming language semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language_semantics), and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468). A 2006 citation for an honorary [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) (Ph.D.) called Nivat one of the fathers of [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).[^o3] He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [University Paris Diderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Paris_Diderot) until 2001.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Nivat was born in [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand), France. His parents were high-school teachers; his father taught languages while his mother taught mathematics. His sister, [Aline](wikipedia://en/58019333), became a notable mathematician.[^o5] In 1954, Nivat moved with his family to Paris.[^o5] Nivat was admitted to the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure) in 1956, but began working at the [Blaise Pascal Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Pascal_Institute) of the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research), a newly established computing laboratory, in 1959.[^o3][^o5] He returned to study mathematics in 1961 under the supervision of [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337).[^o3] His 1967 thesis was entitled *Transductions des langages de Chomsky* ("[Transductions](wikipedia://en/1071289) of [Chomsky Languages](wikipedia://en/6011)").

## Career {#Career}

In 1969, Nivat became a professor at [Paris Diderot University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University) and taught until 2002.[^o7] He remained as professor emeritus until his death in 2017.

He was involved in many endeavours in theoretical computer science in Europe: he was one of the founders of the [European Association for Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Association_for_Theoretical_Computer_Science) (EATCS) in 1972 and organized the first [International Colloquium on Automata, Languages and Programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Colloquium_on_Automata,_Languages_and_Programming) (ICALP) conference in the same year at [French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation](wikipedia://en/318413) (INRIA, then called IRIA) in Paris. In 1975, he was a founder of the journal *[Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Computer_Science_%28journal%29)*. He was editor-in-chief of the journal for over 25 years.[^o3]

He was a member of the [International Federation for Information Processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Information_Processing) (IFIP) [IFIP Working Group 2.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFIP_Working_Group_2.1) on Algorithmic Languages and Calculi,[^o8] which [specified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specification_%28technical_standard%29), supports, and maintains the [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015) [ALGOL 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_60) and [ALGOL 68](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALGOL_68).[^o9]

## Awards {#Awards}

Since 1983, Nivat was a corresponding member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o5] Nivat was also an officer of both the [Legion d'honneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_d'honneur) and the [Ordre national du Mérite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_national_du_Mérite), and a commander of the [Ordre des Palmes Académiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Palmes_Académiques) in France. Nivat won the [EATCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EATCS) award in 2002. He received honorary doctorates from the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) in 1997[^o11] and the [University of Quebec at Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Quebec_at_Montreal) in 2006.[^o3]
