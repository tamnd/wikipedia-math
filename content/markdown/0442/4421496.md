# Kurt Mahler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Mahler"}

**Kurt Mahler** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (26 July 1903 – 25 February 1988) was a German mathematician who worked in the fields of [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), and the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Mahler was a student at the universities in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), graduating with a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Johann Wolfgang Goethe University of Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_Goethe_University_of_Frankfurt_am_Main) in 1927; his advisor was [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602). He left Germany with the rise of [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) and accepted an invitation by [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell) to go to [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester). However, at the start of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he was interned as an [enemy alien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_alien) in Central Camp in [Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas,_Isle_of_Man), [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man), where he met [Kurt Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Hirsch), although he was released after only three months.[^o7] He became a British citizen in 1946.

Mahler held the following positions:

- [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen)
  - Assistant 1934–1936
- [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester)
  - Assistant Lecturer at 1937–1939, 1941–1944
  - Lecturer, 1944–1947; Senior Lecturer, 1948–1949; Reader, 1949–1952
  - Professor of Mathematical Analysis, 1952–1963
- Professor of Mathematics, Institute of Advanced Studies, [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), 1963–1968 and 1972–1975
- Professor of Mathematics, [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University), USA, 1968–1972
- [Professor Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus), [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), from 1975.

## Research {#Research}

Mahler worked in a broad variety of mathematical disciplines, including [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698), [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558), [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), and the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153).[^o3][^o4]

Mahler proved that the [Prouhet–Thue–Morse constant](wikipedia://en/808716) and the [Champernowne constant](wikipedia://en/541351) $0.1234567891011121314151617181920$... are [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325).[^o9][^o10]

Mahler was the first to give an explicit [irrationality measure](wikipedia://en/5968488) for $\pi$, in 1953 (of $42$).[^o11][^o12] Although some have suggested the irrationality measure of $\pi$ is likely to be $2$, the current best estimate is $7.103205334137$…, due to [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) and [Wadim Zudilin](wikipedia://en/4311292).[^o13]

## Awards {#Awards}

He was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1948[^o3] and a fellow of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 1965. He was awarded the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097)'s [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) in 1950, the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532), 1971, and the [Thomas Ranken Lyle Medal](wikipedia://en/27622712), 1977.[^o3]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mahler spoke [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) and was an expert photographer.[^o3]
