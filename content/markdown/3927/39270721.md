# List of polyhedral stellations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of polyhedral stellations"}

In [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853), applying the [operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_operation) of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) to a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) extends its [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) (or edges and planes) until they generate new [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) that bound a newly formed figure. Stellation represents the dual [action](wikipedia://en/12781) to *[faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537)* a polyhedron.[^n1]

:::

Originating from studies of [star polyhedra](wikipedia://en/7087334) in 14th century [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe), a proper mathematical account of polyhedral stellations was given by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in his 1619 classic work, *[Harmonices Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonices_Mundi)*.[^n2] Progress later ensued on detailing and enumerating stellations of prominent stars, such as the regular [Kepler-Poinsot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot) polyhedra,[^n3] with developments on different stellation methods occurring in the 1900s, principally from (Coxeter du Val Flather Petrie 1938) and soon afterward, (Pawley 1975).

A short generalized table of the most notable polyhedral stellations belonging to convex [uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223) polyhedra is provided, with complete sets of stellations for the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) (including the [fifty-nine icosahedral stellations](wikipedia://en/13181245)), as well as for select [Catalan solids](wikipedia://en/564393) (e.g., the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) and the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025)). Stellations of non-convex uniform polyhedra of structure with facial planes passing through their centers (i.e., [hemipolyhedral](wikipedia://en/24084383#Duals_of_hemipolyhedra)), which render unbounded vertices, are also included; these are *stellations to infinity* — per (Wenninger 1983) — conforming to extensions on traditional definitions of polyhedra.

## Background {#Background}

### Star polytopes {#Star_polytopes}

*Image: Stellated dodecahedra Harmonices Mundi.jpg.* The [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843) (left), and [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878) (right), as found in *[Harmonices Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonices_Mundi)* by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736).[^n4]

Experimentation with [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303) and [star polyhedra](wikipedia://en/7087334) since the fourteenth century AD led the way to formal theories for stellating polyhedra: 14th c. AD: [Thomas Bradwardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bradwardine) was the first to detail studies of star polygons by extending the sides of polygons.[^n5]

It was in 1619 that the first geometric description of a **[stellation](wikipedia://en/180253)** was given, by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in his landmark book, *[Harmonices Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonices_Mundi)*: the process of extending the edges (or faces) of a figure until new vertices are generated, which collectively form a new figure.[^n6][^n7] Using this method, Kepler was able to discover the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843) and the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878).[^n8][^n9][^n10] In 1809, [Louis Poinsot](wikipedia://en/764639) rediscovered Kepler's star figures and discovered a further two, the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898) and [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441);[^n11] he achieved this by experimenting assembling regular star polygons and convex regular polygons on vertices of the regular icosahedron and dodecahedron (i.e., [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014), [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544) and [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285)).[^n12] Three years later, [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) proved, using concepts of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741), that these four stellations are the only regular star-polyhedra,[^n13][^n14] eventually termed the [Kepler&ndash;Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler&ndash;Poinsot_polyhedra). As with most non-convex polyhedra including stellations and other star polyhedra, the Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra, with regular self-intersecting faces, are now known to be inequivalent to the [topological sphere](wikipedia://en/27859#Generalizations) as a [simple connected surface](wikipedia://en/523879)[^n15] (this is in contrast with the traditional convex [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223) and their corresponding [homotopy invariance](wikipedia://en/212250#Invariance)).

### Stellation process {#Stellation_process}

(Coxeter du Val Flather Petrie 1938) details, for the first time, all stellations of the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) with specific rules proposed by [J. C. P. Miller](wikipedia://en/20706909).[^n16] Generalizing these ([Miller's rules](wikipedia://en/180253#Miller's_rules)) for stellating any [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223) yields the following:[^n17]

The faces must lie in face-planes, i.e., the bounding planes of the regular [solid](wikipedia://en/23470).

These rules are ideal for stellating smaller uniform solids, such as the regular polyhedra; however, when assessing stellations of other larger uniform polyhedra, this method can quickly become overwhelming. (For example, there are a total of 358,833,072 stellations to the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025) using this set of rules.)[^n18] To address this, (Pawley 1973) proposed a set of rules that restrict the number of stellations to a more manageable set of *[fully supported stellations](wikipedia://en/180253#Stellating_polyhedra)* that are [radially convex](wikipedia://en/6292),[^n19][^n20] such that an outward [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-line_%28geometry%29) from the center of the original polyhedron (in any direction) crosses the stellation surface only once[^o36] (that is to say, all visible parts of a face are seen from the same side).

In the 1948 first edition of *[Regular Polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Polytopes)*, [H. S. M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) describes the stellation process as the reciprocal [action](wikipedia://en/12781) to *[faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537)*,[^n1] identifying the four Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra as stellations and facetings of the regular dodecahedron and icosahedron.[^n21][^n22] He specifies the construction of a [star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7087334) as a stellation of its *core* (with [congruent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congruency_%28geometry%29) face-planes), or by faceting its *case* — the former requires the addition of solid pieces that generate new vertices, while the latter involves the removal of solid pieces, without forming any new vertices (the core of a star polyhedron or compound is the largest convex solid that can be drawn inside them, while their case is the smallest convex solid that contains them).[^n23]

## Stellations of Platonic solids {#Stellations_of_Platonic_solids}

Only three of the five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) produce stellations: the [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), and [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). The [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521) and [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) are unable to generate stellations when extending their [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), since extending their vertices only form one possible [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634).[^n28]

## Hemipolychrons {#Hemipolychrons}

In (Wenninger 1983), a unique family of stellations with [unbounded vertices](wikipedia://en/24084383#Duals_of_the_hemipolyhedra) are identified.[^n39] These originate from orthogonal [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) of faces that pass through centers of their corresponding dual [hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemipolyhedra). The following is a list of these stellations; specifically, of non-convex uniform hemipolyhedra (with coincidental figures in parentheses):

*[Stellations to infinity](wikipedia://en/24084383#Duals_of_the_hemipolyhedra)[^n40]*

::: {#o134 .table cue=table name="Stellations to infinity"}

| Image | Name | [Dual figure](wikipedia://en/8815) | Stellation core[^n41] |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Image: Tetrahemihexacron.png.* | [Tetrahemihexacron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahemihexacron) | [Tetrahemihexahedron](wikipedia://en/2851834) | [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) |
| *Image: Hexahemioctacron.png.* | [Octahemioctacron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexahemioctacron) (hexahemioctacron) | [Octahemioctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157168) ([cubohemioctahedron](wikipedia://en/4156879)) | [Rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) |
| *Image: Small dodecahemidodecacron.png.* | [Small icosihemidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24081922) (small dodecahemidodecacron) | [Small icosihemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157141) ([small dodecahemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157127)) | [Rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025)[^n42] |
| *Image: Great dodecahemidodecacron.png.* | [Great dodecahemidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24087334) (great icosihemidodecacron) | [Great dodecahemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157594) ([great icosihemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157613)) |  |
| *Image: Small dodecahemicosacron.png.* | [Great dodecahemicosacron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_dodecahemicosacron) (small dodecahemicosacron) | [Great dodecahemicosahedron](wikipedia://en/4157609) ([small icosihemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157141)) |  |
| *Image: Great dirhombicosidodecacron.png.* | [Great dirhombicosidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24368309) (great disnub dirhombidodecacron) | [Great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030) ([great disnub dirhombidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4145225)) | [Deltoidal hexecontahedron](wikipedia://en/724753)[^n43] |

:::

This family of stellations does not strictly fulfill the definition of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) that is bound by vertices, and Wenninger notes that at the limit their facets can be interpreted as forming unbounded elongated [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), or equivalently, [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) (indistinguishably).[^n44] As with their dual polyhedra, these hemipolyhedral stellations are [isotoxal polyhedra](wikipedia://en/24612402#Isotoxal_star-polyhedra) (in their case, *at [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824)*). The final polyhedron on this list, the [great dirhombicosidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24368309),[^n45] is the only stellation whose dual figure — the last-indexed and most complex non-degenerate [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223), the [great dirhombicosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2910030) (U<sub>75</sub>) — is constructed using a spherical quadrilateral [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) (rather than with spherical triangles).[^n46]

The [tetrahemihexacron](wikipedia://en/2851834#Tetrahemihexacron) is the only hemipolycron to lack a [coincidental figure](wikipedia://en/24084383#Duals_of_the_hemipolyhedra) arising from duals of distinct hemipolyhedra with common [facetings](wikipedia://en/8613537).
