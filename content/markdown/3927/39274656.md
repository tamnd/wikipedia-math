# Adhesive category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adhesive category"}

In mathematics, an **adhesive category** is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) where [pushouts](wikipedia://en/582024) of monomorphisms exist and work more or less as they do in the category of sets. An example of an adhesive category is the category of directed multigraphs, or [quivers](wikipedia://en/355100), and the theory of adhesive categories is important in the theory of [graph rewriting](wikipedia://en/1252846).

:::

More precisely, an adhesive category is one where any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

- *C* has all [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/9413508), it has pushouts along [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538), and pushout squares of monomorphisms are also pullback squares and are stable under pullback.
- *C* has all pullbacks, it has pushouts along monomorphisms, and the latter are also (bicategorical) pushouts in the [bicategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory) of [spans](wikipedia://en/10318351) in *C*.

If *C* is small, we may equivalently say that *C* has all pullbacks, has pushouts along monomorphisms, and admits a full embedding into a [Grothendieck topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_topos) preserving pullbacks and preserving pushouts of monomorphisms.
