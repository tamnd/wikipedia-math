# I. Martin Isaacs

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="I. Martin Isaacs"}

**Irving Martin Isaacs** (April 14, 1940 – February 17, 2025) was an American [group theorist](wikipedia://en/41890) and [representation theorist](wikipedia://en/19378200). He was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Wisconsin&ndash;Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin&ndash;Madison).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Isaacs was born in [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx), in New York City, on April 14, 1940.[^o16][^o4] He received a BS from the [Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_Institute_of_Brooklyn) in 1960.[^o4] While a student there, he was named a Putnam Fellow for his performance in the 1959 [William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition).[^o5]

Isaacs went on to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) for graduate study. He received a master's degree in 1961,[^o4] and completed his PhD in 1964. His thesis was advised by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134),[^o6] and was titled *Finite $p$-solvable linear groups*.[^o7] After a few years at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) as an instructor and visiting assistant professor, Isaacs moved to the [University of Wisconsin&ndash;Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin&ndash;Madison) in 1969.[^o4] He was hired as an associate professor, and promoted to full professor in 1971.[^o4] According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), he supervised 29 doctoral students over his career.[^o6]

In 2011, Isaacs retired and became a professor emeritus.[^o8][^o9][^o10] In retirement, he lived in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California) and was an occasional participant on [MathOverflow](wikipedia://en/26227167).[^o10] Near the end of his life, he endowed a prize for "Excellence in Mathematical Writing," first awarded in 2025.[^o16][^o11]

## Work {#Work}

Isaacs is known as the author of graduate-level textbooks on [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).[^o8] His book *Character Theory of Finite Groups* has been described as a classic in the field.[^o24]

Isaacs is also known for formulating the Isaacs–Navarro conjecture along with [Gabriel Navarro](wikipedia://en/78201740), a widely cited generalization of the [McKay conjecture](wikipedia://en/51116107).[^o13][^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Isaacs was seriously injured in an automobile accident in France in 1964, shortly after receiving his PhD. The accident left him scarred and disabled.[^o16][^o8]

He died of kidney failure on February 17, 2025.[^o16][^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2009, a conference was held at the [Universitat de Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universitat_de_Valencia) in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain) to honor his contributions.[^o18] Following the conference, a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) was published by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o19]

Isaacs was the recipient of a [Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Foundation) research award in 1971.[^o8] He was inaugurated as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) in 2013.[^o20]

Isaacs was a [Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) lecturer for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) between 2003 and 2005.[^o8]
