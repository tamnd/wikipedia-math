# Margaret E. Martin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Margaret E. Martin"}

**Margaret E. Martin** (May 6, 1912 – May 16, 2012) was an economist and statistician at the U.S. [Bureau of the Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_the_Budget) from 1942 to 1973. She was influential in the development of U.S. economic statistics and became president of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o2]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Margaret Elizabeth Martin was the first child born to Harry Martin, a teacher, and Frances Martin in New York in 1912. Her younger siblings were Jane, Eleanor and Robert.[^o2][^o4] As an elementary student, her teachers included noted economist [Clara Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Eliot).[^o6]

## Education {#Education}

In 1933, Martin received a bachelor's degree in economics from [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College), and went on to earn an MA and PhD in economics from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Martin worked for the Division of Statistical Standards of the Bureau of the Budget beginning in 1942. The [Current Population Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_Population_Survey), which has been the primary source of labor statistics within the country, was developed by Martin and others. The survey is produced by the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) and the U.S. [Bureau of Labor Statistics](wikipedia://en/72528) now.[^o2]

From 1973 to 1978 Martin was the first executive director of the Committee on National Statistics (CNSTAT) created by the [United States National Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Research_Council).[^o8]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

She was elected a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1961[^o10] and became president in 1980.[^o2] In 1989 the Association awarded her the [Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224).[^o8]

She was elected to the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) in 1973.[^o11]

## Death {#Death}

Martin died May 16, 2012, of pneumonia and congestive heart failure at [Suburban Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban_Hospital) in [Bethesda, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Maryland). One of her sisters was alive at the time of her death.[^o2]
