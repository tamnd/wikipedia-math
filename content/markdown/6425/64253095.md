# Alexander Mathis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Mathis"}

**Alexander Mathis** is an Austrian mathematician, computational neuroscientist and software developer. He is currently an assistant professor at the [École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) in Switzerland. He is working at the intersection of [computational neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_neuroscience), and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), focusing on trying to understand the statistics of behavior and how the brain creates behavior.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Mathis studied mathematics, [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [theory of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_science) at [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), Germany.[^o4] His interest in computing and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) led him to pursue a PhD in [computational neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_neuroscience) at the Graduate School for Systemic Neuroscience under the supervision of Prof. Andreas Herz at the department of neurobiology at LMU Munich.[^o5] During his PhD work, he studied optimal coding approaches to reveal the properties of [grid cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_cell)[^o6] and how distributed population activity readout can be implemented in plausible bio-physical models.[^o7] The predictions of this theory were confirmed in rats by the Moser laboratory[^o8] and artificial systems optimized for navigation by DeepMind.[^o9]

He spent an exchange year at the [Autonomous University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Barcelona) in Spain.[^o4]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After completing his PhD, Mathis went in 2013 as a postdoctoral fellow to work under the mentorship of Prof. [Venkatesh N. Murthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venkatesh_N._Murthy) at the Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o11][^o12] In addition, in 2015, he joined the research group of Prof. Matthias Bethge at the Bernstein Center for Computational Neuroscience in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen) and the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) in Germany.[^o13] His postdoctoral research positions were funded by a DFG postdoctoral fellowship[^o28] and a [Marie-Curie fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Curie_Fellowship).[^o29]

Mathis conducted research in odor-guided [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation), [social behaviors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_behavior), motor learning, and the [cocktail party problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocktail_party_problem).[^o14] He employed [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) methods and experimentally testable [computational models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_model) to study [animal behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Behavior) and neural data. He has developed tools such as DeepLabCut and DeepDraw to accurately measure animal and human behavior.[^o15] He is one of the initiators and developers of the open-source research tool DeepLabCut that estimates animal postures via computer vision and machine learning.[^o16] Mathis has also created models and theories on adaptive behavior, in particular on [motor control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_control) and sensorimotor transformations.[^o17] Several publications appeared during this research period, including the highly cited paper "DeepLabCut: markerless pose estimation of user-defined body parts with deep learning" by Mathis et al. published in 2018 in [Nature Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Neuroscience).[^o18]

In August 2020, he moved as an assistant professor to the [École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) where he started his own research laboratory "the Mathis Group", dedicated to research at the intersection of [computational neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_neuroscience) and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488). The Mathis Group is committed to enhancing machine learning tools for animal behavior analysis and to developing of [neural network models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network_models) of [sensorimotor representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensorimotor_network).[^o19] The group has made significant contributions to understanding proprioception, highlighting that task-driven models can reproduce neural population dynamics along the proprioceptive pathway[^o20] and that task-driven models are also subject to proprioceptive illusions.[^o21] This research has garnered public attention, including coverage on [Radio France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_France) discussing how artificial neurons help understand body perception.[^o22] His research was also featured in [The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic),[^o23] [Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29),[^o24] and [Quanta Magazine](wikipedia://en/51804976).[^o25]

## Awards and grants {#Awards_and_grants}

[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Zuckerberg_Initiative) (CZI) awarded funding for Mathis' open source project DeepLabCut.[^o27] Mathis further was awarded with a postdoctoral fellowship by the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft)[^o28] and a [Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Skłodowska-Curie_Actions) fellowship by the [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union)[^o29]
