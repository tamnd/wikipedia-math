# Continuous Bernoulli distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuous Bernoulli distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), the **continuous Bernoulli distribution**[^o3][^o4][^o5] is a family of continuous [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) parameterized by a single [shape parameter](wikipedia://en/9210865) $\lambda \in (0, 1)$, defined on the unit interval $x \in [0, 1]$, by:

:::

$$p(x | \lambda) \propto \lambda^x (1-\lambda)^{1-x}.$$

The continuous Bernoulli distribution arises in [deep learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_learning) and [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision), specifically in the context of [variational autoencoders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_autoencoder),[^o6][^o7] for modeling the pixel intensities of natural images. As such, it defines a proper probabilistic counterpart for the commonly used binary [cross entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_entropy) loss, which is often applied to continuous, $[0,1]$-valued data.[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] This practice amounts to ignoring the normalizing constant of the continuous Bernoulli distribution, since the binary cross entropy loss only defines a true log-likelihood for discrete, $\{0,1\}$-valued data.

The continuous Bernoulli also defines an [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174) of distributions. Writing $\theta = \log\left(\lambda/(1-\lambda)\right)$ for the [natural parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_parameter), the density can be rewritten in canonical form: $p(x | \theta) \propto \exp (\theta x)$. [^o12]

## Statistical inference {#Statistical_inference}

Given an independent sample of $n$ points $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with $x_i\in[0,1]\,\forall i$ from continuous Bernoulli, the log-likelihood of the natural parameter $\theta$ is

$$\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \theta \sum_{i=1}^n x_i - n \log \{(e^\theta - 1)/\theta\}$$

and the [maximum likelihood estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimator) of the natural parameter $\theta$ is the solution of $\mathcal{L}'(\theta) =0$, that is, $\hat{\theta}$ satisfies

$$\frac{e^{\hat{\theta}}}{e^{\hat{\theta}} - 1} - \frac{1}{\hat{\theta}}= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$$

where the left hand side $e^{\hat{\theta}}/(e^{\hat{\theta}} - 1) - \hat{\theta}^{-1}$ is the expected value of continuous Bernoulli with parameter $\hat\theta$. Although $\hat{\theta}$ does not admit a closed-form expression, it can be easily calculated with numerical inversion.

## Further properties {#Further_properties}

The entropy of a continuous Bernoulli distribution is

$$\operatorname{H}[X] = \begin{cases} 0 &\text{ if } \lambda = \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{\lambda\log\left(\lambda\right) - \left(1 - \lambda\right)\log\left(1 - \lambda\right)}{1 - 2\lambda} - \log\left(\frac{2 \tanh^{-1}\left(1 - 2\lambda\right)}{e\left(1 - 2\lambda\right)}\right) &\text{ otherwise} \end{cases}\!$$

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

### Bernoulli distribution {#Bernoulli_distribution}

The continuous Bernoulli can be thought of as a continuous relaxation of the [Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/199189), which is defined on the discrete set $\{0,1\}$ by the [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725):

$$p(x) = p^x (1-p)^{1-x},$$

where $p$ is a scalar parameter between 0 and 1. Applying this same functional form on the continuous interval $[0,1]$ results in the continuous Bernoulli [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487), up to a normalizing constant.

### Uniform distribution {#Uniform_distribution}

The [Uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699223) between the unit interval \[0,1\] is a special case of continuous Bernoulli when $\lambda = 1/2$ or $\theta = 0$.

### Exponential distribution {#Exponential_distribution}

An [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) with rate $\Lambda$ restricted to the unit interval \[0,1\] corresponds to a continuous Bernoulli distribution with natural parameter $\theta = -\Lambda < 0$.

### Continuous categorical distribution {#Continuous_categorical_distribution}

The multivariate generalization of the continuous Bernoulli is called the [continuous-categorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous-categorical).[^o20]
