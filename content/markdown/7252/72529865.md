# Moreau envelope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moreau envelope"}

The **Moreau envelope** (or the Moreau-Yosida regularization) $M_f$ of a proper [lower semi-continuous](wikipedia://en/57824) [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568) $f$ is a smoothed version of $f$. It was proposed by [Jean-Jacques Moreau](wikipedia://en/34674927) in 1965.[^o2]

:::

The Moreau envelope has important applications in [mathematical optimization](wikipedia://en/52033): minimizing over $M_f$ and minimizing over $f$ are equivalent problems in the sense that the sets of minimizers of $f$ and $M_f$ are the same. However, first-order optimization algorithms can be directly applied to $M_f$, since $f$ may be non-differentiable while $M_f$ is always continuously differentiable. Indeed, many [proximal gradient methods](wikipedia://en/39587805) can be interpreted as a [gradient descent](wikipedia://en/201489) method over $M_f$.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Moreau envelope of a proper lower semi-continuous convex function $f$ from a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal{X}$ to $(-\infty,+\infty]$ is defined as[^o5]

$$M_{f}(v) = \inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}} \left(f(x) + \frac{1}{2} \|x - v\|_2^2\right).$$

Given a parameter $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, the Moreau envelope of $\lambda f$ is also called as the Moreau envelope of $f$ with parameter $\lambda$.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The Moreau envelope can also be seen as the [infimal convolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimal_convolution) between $f$ and $(1/2)\| \cdot \|^2_2$.
- The [proximal operator](wikipedia://en/51026323) of a function is related to the gradient of the Moreau envelope by the following identity:

$\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x) = \frac{1}{\lambda} (x - \mathrm{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))$. By defining the sequence $x_{k+1} = \mathrm{prox}_{\lambda f}(x_k)$ and using the above identity, we can interpret the proximal operator as a gradient descent algorithm over the Moreau envelope.

- Using [Fenchel's duality theorem](wikipedia://en/3077734), one can derive the following dual formulation of the Moreau envelope:

$$M_{\lambda f}(v) = \max_{p \in \mathcal X} \left( \langle p, v \rangle - \frac{\lambda}{2} \| p \|^2 - f^*(p)\right),$$

where $f^*$ denotes the [convex conjugate](wikipedia://en/1001490) of $f$. Since the subdifferential of a proper, convex, lower semicontinuous function on a Hilbert space is inverse to the subdifferential of its convex conjugate, we can conclude that if $p_0 \in \mathcal X$ is the maximizer of the above expression, then $x_0 := v - \lambda p_0$ is the minimizer in the primal formulation and vice versa.

- By [Hopf–Lax formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf–Lax_formula), the Moreau envelope is a [viscosity solution](wikipedia://en/2300687) to a [Hamilton–Jacobi equation](wikipedia://en/897539).[^o8] [Stanley Osher](wikipedia://en/3008100) and co-authors used this property and [Cole–Hopf transformation](wikipedia://en/73078347) to derive an algorithm to compute approximations to the proximal operator of a function.[^o9]

:::
