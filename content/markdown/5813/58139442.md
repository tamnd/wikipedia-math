# Julia Ward

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julia Ward"}

**Julia Ward** (December 1900 – June 18, 1962) was the founder of the central reference division of the [National Security Agency (NSA)](wikipedia://en/21939). She was inducted into the [Cryptologic Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Hall_of_Honor) in 2002.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Ward received her A.B. from [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) in 1923 and her Ph.D from Bryn Mawr College in 1940.[^o6]

## World War II {#World_War_II}

Ward joined the cryptologic service during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) and worked for the [Signal Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Security_Agency) (the Army's cryptologic organization). She also worked as a librarian and eventually built a collection of [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information) and [unclassified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unclassified) materials for use by analysts. In 1945, she became the deputy chief of the reference section. Within a few years, she turned the section from one that was known as poorly organized, into a section that was widely recognized and highly respected.[^o8]

In 1949, Ward was named head of the Collateral Branch, thereby becoming the only female branch head in the [Office of Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Operations). In 1955, Ward was promoted to deputy chief of NSA's Liaison and Foreign Operations Section.[^o2]

## Cryptologic Hall of Honor {#Cryptologic_Hall_of_Honor}

Ward was inducted into the NSA's Cryptologic Hall of Honor in June 2002 at the National Cryptologic Museum in Baltimore, MD. Bryn Mawr College has a plaque to commemorate her placement in the Hall of Honor.[^o2][^o10]
