# Sims conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sims conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Sims conjecture** is a result in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), originally proposed by [Charles Sims](wikipedia://en/20022166).[^o2] He [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) that if $G$ is a [primitive permutation group](wikipedia://en/2739252) on a finite set $S$ and $G_\alpha$ denotes the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_%28group_theory%29) of the point $\alpha$ in $S$, then there exists an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563)-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ such that $f(d) \geq |G_\alpha|$ for $d$ the length of any [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) of $G_\alpha$ in the set $S \setminus \{\alpha\}$.

:::

The conjecture was [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Peter Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Cameron_%28mathematician%29), [Cheryl Praeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_Praeger), [Jan Saxl](wikipedia://en/63906865), and [Gary Seitz](wikipedia://en/39300192) using the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), in particular the fact that only finitely many [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) types of [sporadic groups](wikipedia://en/324806) exist.

The theorem reads precisely as follows.[^o3]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

There exists a function $f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}$ such that whenever $G$ is a primitive permutation group and $h > 1$ is the length of a non-trivial orbit of a point stabilizer $H$ in $G$, then the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of $H$ is at most $f(h)$.

:::

Thus, in a primitive permutation group with "large" stabilizers, these stabilizers cannot have any small orbit. A consequence of their proof is that there exist only finitely many [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) [distance-transitive](wikipedia://en/7768222) [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) having [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) greater than 2.[^o5][^o6][^o7]
