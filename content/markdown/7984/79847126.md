# Ampersand curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ampersand curve"}

*Image: Ampersandcurve.svg.* The ampersand curve on the Cartesian plane.

In geometry, the **ampersand curve** is a type of [quartic plane curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_plane_curve). It was named after its resemblance to the [ampersand symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/&) by [Henry Cundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyn_Cundy) and Arthur Rollett.[^o2][^o3]

:::

The ampersand curve is the graph of the equation

$$6x^4+4y^4-21x^3+6x^2y^2+19x^2-11xy^2-3y^2=0.$$

The graph of the ampersand curve has three [crunode](wikipedia://en/3090324) points where it intersects itself at (0,0), (1,1), and (1,−1).[^o4] The curve has a [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of 0.[^o5]

The curve was originally constructed by [Julius Plücker](wikipedia://en/52146) as a quartic plane curve that has 28 real [bitangents](wikipedia://en/2605212), the maximum possible for [bitangents of a quartic](wikipedia://en/5204204).[^o6]

It is the special case of the Plücker quartic

$$(x+y)(y-x)(x-1)\left(x-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)-2\left(y^2+x(x-2)\right)^2-k=0,$$

with *k* = 0.

The curve has six real horizontal tangents at

$$\left(\frac{1}{2}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\right), \qquad \left(\frac{159-\sqrt{201}}{120}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{1389+67\sqrt\frac{67}{3}}}{40}\right), \qquad \left(\frac{159+\sqrt{201}}{120}, \pm\frac{\sqrt{1389-67\sqrt\frac{67}{3}}}{40}\right),$$

and four real vertical tangents at

$$\left(-\frac{1}{10},\pm\frac{\sqrt{23}}{10}\right), \qquad \left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right).$$

It is an example of a curve that has no value of *x* in its [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) with only one *y* value.
