# Measure theory in topological vector spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Measure theory in topological vector spaces"}

In mathematics, **measure theory in topological vector spaces** refers to the extension of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) to [topological vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_vector_spaces). Such spaces are often infinite-dimensional, but many results of classical measure theory are formulated for finite-dimensional spaces and cannot be directly transferred. This is already evident in the case of the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/6607484), which does not exist in general infinite-dimensional spaces. In fact, there is no nontrivial left-invariant [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) on any Hausdorff, non-locally compact [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) and infinite-dimensional vector spaces are not locally compact.

:::

The article considers only topological vector spaces, which also possess the [Hausdorff property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_property). Vector spaces without topology are mathematically not that interesting because concepts such as convergence and continuity are not defined there.

## σ-Algebras {#σ-Algebras}

Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological vector space, $X^*$ the [algebraic dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) and $X'$ the [topological dual space](wikipedia://en/7988#Topological_dual_space). In topological vector spaces there exist three prominent [σ-algebras](wikipedia://en/29586):

- the [Borel σ-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_σ-algebra) $\mathcal{B}(X)$: is generated by the open sets of $\mathcal{T}$.
- the [cylindrical σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/21236657) $\mathcal{E}(X, X')$: is generated by the dual space $X'$.
- the [Baire σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/3976202) $\mathcal{B}_0(X)$: is generated by all continuous functions $C(X,\mathbb{R})$. The Baire σ-algebra is also notated $\mathcal{Ba}(X)$.

The following relationship holds:

$$\mathcal{E}(X,X')\subseteq \mathcal{B}_0(X)\subseteq \mathcal{B}(X)$$

where $\mathcal{E}(X,X')\subseteq \mathcal{B}_0(X)$ is obvious.

### Cylindrical σ-algebra {#Cylindrical_σ-algebra}

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two vector spaces in duality. A set of the form

$$C_{f_1,\dots,f_n,B}:=\{x\in X\colon (\langle x,f_1\rangle,\dots,\langle x,f_n\rangle)\in B\}$$

for $B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and $f_1,\dots,f_n\in Y$ is called a [cylinder set](wikipedia://en/2613658) and if $B$ is open, then it's an open cylinder set. The set of all cylinders is $\mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$ and the set of all open cylinders is $\mathfrak{A}^{O}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$. If one takes the product $\otimes_{n\in \mathbb{N}} \mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}$, this only produces an [algebra](wikipedia://en/1018676). The σ-algebra

$$\mathcal{E}(X,Y)=\sigma\left(\mathcal{Zyl}(X,Y)\right)=\sigma\left(\bigotimes_{n\in \mathbb{N}} \mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n}\right)$$

is called the cylindrical σ-algebra.[^o4] The sets of cylinders and the set of open cylinders generate the same cylindrical σ-algebra, i.e. $\sigma(\mathfrak{A}_{f_1,\dots,f_n})=\sigma(\mathfrak{A}^{O}_{f_1,\dots,f_n})$.

For the [weak topology](wikipedia://en/33662) $T_s:=T_s(X,X')$ the cylindrical σ-algebra $\mathcal{E}(X,X')$ is the Baire σ-algebra of $(X,T_s)$.[^o5] One uses the cylindrical σ-algebra because the Borel σ-algebra can lead to measurability problems in infinite-dimensional space. In connection with integrals of continuous functions it is difficult or even impossible to extend them to arbitrary borel sets.[^o6] For non-separable spaces it can happen that the vector addition is no longer measurable to the product algebra of borel σ-algebras because in general $\mathcal{B}(X)\otimes \mathcal{B}(Y) \subset \mathcal{B}(X \times Y)$, however for the cylindrical σ-algebra one has $\mathcal{E}(X,X')\otimes \mathcal{E}(Y,Y') = \mathcal{E}(X\times Y,X'\times Y')$.[^o7]

### Equality of the σ-algebras {#Equality_of_the_σ-algebras}

- Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be a topological vector space and let $T_s:=T_s(X,X')$ be the weak topology, then $\mathcal{E}(X,X')$ is exactly the Baire σ-algebra of $(X,T_s)$.[^o5]
- Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be a separable, metrizable locally convex space and $T_s:=T_s(X,X')$ be the weak topology. Then $\mathcal{E}(X,X')$, $\mathcal{B}_0(X)$ and $\mathcal{B}(X)$ are equivalent under $\mathcal{T}$ and $T_s$.[^o5]

## Measures {#Measures}

One way to construct a measure on an infinite-dimensional space is to first define the measure on finite-dimensional spaces and then extend it to infinite-dimensional spaces as a [projective system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_system). This leads to the notion of [cylindrical measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_measure), which according to [Israel Moiseevich Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643) and [Naum Yakovlevich Vilenkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naum_Yakovlevich_Vilenkin), originates from [Andrei Nikolayevich Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Nikolayevich_Kolmogorov).[^o10]

### Cylindrical Measures {#Cylindrical_Measures}

Let $(X, \mathcal{T})$ be a topological vector space over $\mathbb{R}$ and $X^*$ its [algebraic dual space](wikipedia://en/7988#Algebraic_dual_space). Furthermore, let $F$ be a vector space of [linear functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948#Linear_functionals) on $X$, that is $F \subseteq X^*$.

A set function

$$\nu: \mathcal{Zyl}(X, F) \to \mathbb{R}+$$

is called a cylindrical measure if, for every finite subset $G := \{f_1, \dots, f_n\} \subseteq F$ with $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the restriction

$$\nu: \mathcal{E}(X, G) \to \mathbb{R}+$$

is a σ-additive function, i.e. $\nu$ is a measure.[^o4]

Let $\Gamma \subset X^*$. A cylindrical measure $\mu$ on $X$ is said to have weak order $p$ (or to be of weak type $p$) if the $p$-th weak [moment](wikipedia://en/368684) exists, that is,

$$\int_E |\langle f, x \rangle|^p d\mu(f) < \infty$$

for all $f \in \Gamma$.[^o12]

### Radon measure {#Radon_measure}

Every [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) induces a cylindrical measure but the converse is not true.[^o14] Let $E$ and $G$ be two locally convex space, then an operator $T:E\to G$ is called a $(q,p)$-radonifying operator, if for a cylindrical measure $\mu$ of order $q$ on $E$ the image measure $T_*\mu$ is a Radon measure of order $p$ on $G$.[^o15][^o16][^o17]

### Some results {#Some_results}

There are many results on when a cylindrical measure can be extended to a Radon measure, such as [Minlos theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minlos_theorem)[^o19] and [Sazonov theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sazonov_theorem).[^o20]

Let $A$ be a [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238), [convex](wikipedia://en/6292), [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) and [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) subset of a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space) $E$, then $E_A$ denoted the subspace of $E$ which is generated by $A$. A balanced, convex, bounded subset $A$ of a locally convex Hausdorff space $E$ is called a Hilbert set if the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $E_A$ has a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) structure, i.e. the norm $\|\cdot\|_{E_A}$ of $E_A$ can be deduced from a [scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product) and $E_A$ is complete.[^o21]

::: {#o23 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="A theorem by Sazonov-Badrikian"}

#### A theorem by Sazonov-Badrikian {#A_theorem_by_Sazonov-Badrikian}

Let $E$ be a [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) locally convex Hausdorff space and $E'_c$ be its dual equipped with the topology of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) on [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) subsets in $E$ . Assume that every subset of $E$ is contained in a balanced, convex, compact Hilbert set. A function of [positive type](wikipedia://en/3142115) $f$ on $E'_c$ is the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of a Radon measure on $E$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the function is continuous for the [Hilbert-Schmidt topology](wikipedia://en/976673) associated with the topology of $E'_c$.[^o24]

:::

::: {#o26 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Minlos–Sasonov theorem"}

#### Minlos–Sasonov theorem {#Minlos–Sasonov_theorem}

A slight variant of the theorem is the [Minlos–Sazonov theorem](wikipedia://en/78916718) which states that a cylindrical measure is σ-additive and Radon if it's [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) is continuous in zero in the [Sazonov topology](wikipedia://en/78916718#Sazonov_topology).

:::
