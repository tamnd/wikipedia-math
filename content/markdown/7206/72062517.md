# Hunter Snevily

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Hunter Snevily"}

**Hunter Snevily** (1956–2013) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) with expertise and contributions in [Set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [Graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [Discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), and [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175) on the integers.[^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hunter received his undergraduate degree from [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) in 1981,[^o2] and his Ph.D. degree from the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) under the supervision of [Douglas West](wikipedia://en/22813653) in 1991.[^o2][^o6] After a postdoctoral fellowship at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech), where he mentored many students, Hunter took a faculty position at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho) in 1993 where he was a professor until 2010.[^o2] He retired early [^o7] while fighting with [Parkinsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsons),[^o2][^o4] but continued research in mathematics till his last days.[^o2][^o4]

## Mathematics research {#Mathematics_research}

The following are some of Hunter's most important contributions (as discussed in [^o4]):

- Hunter formulated a conjecture (1991) [^o9] bounding the size of a family of sets under intersection constraints. He conjectured that if ${\mathcal L}$ is a set of $k$ positive integers and $\{A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_m\}$ is a family of subsets of an $n$-set satisfying $|A_i \cap A_j| \in {\mathcal L}$ whenever $i \neq j$, then $m \leq \sum_{i=0}^k{n-1 \choose i}$. His conjecture was ambitious in a way it would beautifully unify classical results of [Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn](wikipedia://en/3166399) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) (1948),[^o10] Bose (1949),[^o11] Majumdar (1953),[^o12] [H. J. Ryser](wikipedia://en/20825197) (1968),[^o13] Frankl and Füredi (1981),[^o14] and Frankl and Wilson (1981).[^o15] Hunter finally proved his conjecture in 2003[^o16]
- Hunter made important contribution to the well known [Chvátal's Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chvátal's_Conjecture) (1974)[^o17] which states that every hereditary family ${\mathcal F}$ of sets has a largest intersecting subfamily consisting of sets with a common element. Schönheim[^o18] proved this when the maximal members of ${\mathcal F}$ have a common element. [Vašek Chvátal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vašek_Chvátal) proved it when there is a linear order on the elements such that $\{b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_k\}\in{\mathcal F}$ implies $\{a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k\}\in{\mathcal F}$ when $a_i \leq b_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq k$. A family ${\mathcal F}$ has $x$ as a dominant element if substituting $x$ for any element of a member of ${\mathcal F}$ not containing $x$ yields another member of ${\mathcal F}$. Hunter's 1992 result[^o19] greatly strengthened both Schönheim's result and Chvátal's result by proving the conjecture for all families having a dominant element; it was major progress on the problem.
- One of his most cited papers[^o20] is with [Lior Pachter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lior_Pachter) and Bill Voxman[^o21] on [Graph pebbling](wikipedia://en/3042715). This paper and Hunter's later paper[^o22] with Foster added several conjectures on the subject and together have been cited in more than 50 papers.
- Hunter made important contributions[^o23][^o24][^o25] on the [Snake-in-the-box](wikipedia://en/1850040) problem and on the [Graceful labeling](wikipedia://en/8978968) of graphs.
- One of Hunter's conjectures (1999)[^o26] became known as Snevily's Conjecture:[^o27] Given an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $G$ of odd order, and subsets $\{a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k\}$ and $\{b_1,b_2,\ldots,b_k\}$ of $G$, there exists a [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\pi$ of $[k]$ such that $a_1+b_{\pi}(1), a_2+b_{\pi}(2), \ldots, a_k+b_{\pi}(k)$ are distinct. [Noga Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651)[^o28] proved this for [cyclic groups](wikipedia://en/52327) of prime order. Dasgupta et al. (2001).[^o29] proved it for all cyclic groups. Finally, after a decade, the conjecture was proved for all groups by a young mathematician Arsovski.[^o30] [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) devoted a section to Snevily's Conjecture in his well-known book [Additive Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_Combinatorics).
- Hunter collaborated the most[^o31][^o32][^o33][^o25][^o34][^o35][^o36][^o37] with his long-term friend[^o4] [André Kézdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Kézdy). After retirement, he became friends with [Tanbir Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanbir_Ahmed)[^o4] and explored [experimental mathematics](wikipedia://en/256363) that resulted in several publications [^o38][^o39][^o40][^o41][^o42][^o43]
