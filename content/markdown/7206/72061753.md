# Sergey Kislitsyn

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sergey Kislitsyn"}

**Sergey S. Kislitsyn**, (Серге́й Серге́евич Кисли́цын) is a Russian mathematician, specializing in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869).

:::

Kislitsyn was born January 5, 1935, in [Ivanovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivanovo), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). He received his M.S. in mathematics from [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) in 1957. From 1962 until 1970 he worked at Yekaterinburg branch of the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) (Krasovsky Institute of Mathematics and Mechanics). He defended his Ph.D. thesis in 1964 and continued working as a lecturer at [Krasnoyarsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoyarsk_State_University).[^o2]

Kislitsyn is known for posing the [1/3–2/3 conjecture](wikipedia://en/28745947) for [linear extensions](wikipedia://en/11726298) of finite [posets](wikipedia://en/23572), which he published in 1968.[^o3] The conjecture is established in several special cases but open in full generality.[^o4][^o5]
