# Degree of a continuous mapping

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Degree of a continuous mapping"}

*Image: Sphere wrapped round itself.png.* A degree two map of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) onto itself.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **degree** or **Brouwer degree** of a [continuous mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) between two [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [oriented](wikipedia://en/187446) [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) of the same [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) is a number that represents the number of times that the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) manifold wraps around the [range](wikipedia://en/275991) manifold under the mapping. The degree is always an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563), but may be positive or negative depending on the orientations.

:::

The degree of a map between general manifolds was first defined by [Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer),[^o2] who showed that the degree is [homotopy invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_invariant) and used it to prove the [Brouwer fixed point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brouwer_fixed_point_theorem). Less general forms of the concept existed before Brouwer, such as the [winding number](wikipedia://en/86113) and the **Kronecker characteristic** (or **Kronecker integral**).[^o3]

In modern mathematics, the degree of a map plays an important role in topology and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the degree of a continuous map (for instance a map from space to some order parameter set) is one example of a [topological quantum number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_quantum_number).

## Definitions of the degree {#Definitions_of_the_degree}

### From *S*<sup>*n*</sup> to *S*<sup>*n*</sup> {#From_Sn_to_Sn}

The simplest and most important case is the degree of a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) from the [$n$-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) $S^n$ to itself (in the case $n=1$, this is called the winding number):

Let $f\colon S^n\to S^n$ be a continuous map. Then $f$ induces a [pushforward](wikipedia://en/16649410) homomorphism $f_*\colon H_n\left(S^n\right) \to H_n\left(S^n\right)$, where $H_n\left(\cdot\right)$ is the $n$th [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group). Considering the fact that $H_n\left(S^n\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, we see that $f_*$ must be of the form $f_*\colon x\mapsto\alpha x$ for some fixed $\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}$. This $\alpha$ is then called the degree of $f$.

### Between manifolds {#Between_manifolds}

#### Algebraic topology {#Algebraic_topology}

Let *X* and *Y* be closed [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation_%28mathematics%29) *m*-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) implies that the manifold's top [homology group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) is isomorphic to **Z**. Choosing an orientation means choosing a generator of the top homology group.

A continuous map *f* : *X* →*Y* induces a homomorphism *f*<sub>∗</sub> from *H<sub>m</sub>*(*X*) to *H<sub>m</sub>*(*Y*). Let \[*X*\], resp. \[*Y*\] be the chosen generator of *H<sub>m</sub>*(*X*), resp. *H<sub>m</sub>*(*Y*) (or the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of *X*, *Y*). Then the **degree** of *f* is defined to be *f*<sub>∗</sub>(\[*X*\]). In other words,

$$f_*([X]) = \deg(f)[Y] \, .$$

If *y* in *Y* and *f* <sup>−1</sup>(*y*) is a finite set, the degree of *f* can be computed by considering the *m*-th [local homology groups](wikipedia://en/1248647) of *X* at each point in *f* <sup>−1</sup>(*y*). Namely, if $f^{-1}(y)=\{x_1,\dots,x_m\}$, then

$$\deg(f) = \sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg(f|_{x_i}) \, .$$

#### Differential topology {#Differential_topology}

In the language of [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), the degree of a smooth map can be defined as follows: If *f* is a smooth map whose domain is a compact manifold and *p* is a [regular value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_value) of *f*, consider the finite set

$$f^{-1}(p) = \{x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n\} \,.$$

By *p* being a regular value, in a neighborhood of each *x*<sub>*i*</sub> the map *f* is a local [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564). Diffeomorphisms can be either orientation preserving or orientation reversing. Let *r* be the number of points *x*<sub>*i*</sub> at which *f* is orientation preserving and *s* be the number at which *f* is orientation reversing. When the codomain of *f* is connected, the number *r* − *s* is independent of the choice of *p* (though *n* is not!) and one defines the **degree** of *f* to be *r* − *s*. This definition coincides with the algebraic topological definition above.

The same definition works for compact manifolds with [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) but then *f* should send the boundary of *X* to the boundary of *Y*.

One can also define **degree modulo 2** (deg<sub>2</sub>(*f*)) the same way as before but taking the *fundamental class* in **Z**<sub>2</sub> homology. In this case deg<sub>2</sub>(*f*) is an element of **Z**<sub>2</sub> (the [field with two elements](wikipedia://en/11137263)), the manifolds need not be orientable and if *n* is the number of preimages of *p* as before then deg<sub>2</sub>(*f*) is *n* modulo 2.

Integration of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) gives a pairing between (C<sup>∞</sup>-)[singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) and [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530): $\langle c, \omega\rangle = \int_c \omega$, where $c$ is a homology class represented by a cycle $c$ and $\omega$ a closed form representing a de Rham cohomology class. For a smooth map *f*: *X* →*Y* between orientable *m*-manifolds, one has

$$\left\langle f_* [c], [\omega] \right\rangle = \left\langle [c], f^*[\omega] \right\rangle,$$

where *f*<sub>∗</sub> and *f*<sup>∗</sup> are induced maps on chains and forms respectively. Since *f*<sub>∗</sub>\[*X*\] = deg *f* · \[*Y*\], we have

$$\deg f \int_Y \omega = \int_X f^*\omega \,$$

for any *m*-form *ω* on *Y*.

### Maps from closed region {#Maps_from_closed_region}

If $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ is a bounded [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_%28mathematical_analysis%29), $f: \bar\Omega \to \mathbb{R}^n$ smooth, $p$ a [regular value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_value) of $f$ and $p \notin f(\partial\Omega)$, then the degree $\deg(f, \Omega, p)$ is defined by the formula

$$\deg(f, \Omega, p) := \sum_{y\in f^{-1}(p)} \operatorname{sgn} \det(Df(y))$$

where $Df(y)$ is the [Jacobian matrix](wikipedia://en/195351) of $f$ in $y$.

This definition of the degree may be naturally extended for non-regular values $p$ such that $\deg(f, \Omega, p) = \deg\left(f, \Omega, p'\right)$ where $p'$ is a point close to $p$. The topological degree can also be calculated using a [surface integral](wikipedia://en/528867) over the boundary of $\Omega$,[^o10] and if $\Omega$ is a connected *n*-[polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), then the degree can be expressed as a sum of determinants over a certain subdivision of its [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).[^o11]

The degree satisfies the following properties:[^o12]

- If $\deg\left(f, \bar\Omega, p\right) \neq 0$, then there exists $x \in \Omega$ such that $f(x) = p$.
- $\deg(\operatorname{id}, \Omega, y) = 1$ for all $y \in \Omega$.
- Decomposition property: $$\deg(f, \Omega, y) = \deg(f, \Omega_1, y) + \deg(f, \Omega_2, y),$$ if $\Omega_1, \Omega_2$ are disjoint parts of $\Omega = \Omega_1 \cup \Omega_2$ and $y \not\in f{\left(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\left(\Omega_1 \cup \Omega_2\right)\right)}$.
- *Homotopy invariance*: If $f$ and $g$ are [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) via a homotopy $F(t)$ such that $F(0) = f,\, F(1) = g$ and $p \notin F(t)(\partial\Omega)$, then $\deg(f, \Omega, p) = \deg(g, \Omega, p)$.
- The function $p \mapsto \deg(f, \Omega, p)$ is locally constant on $\mathbb{R}^n - f(\partial\Omega)$.

These properties characterise the degree uniquely and the degree may be defined by them in an axiomatic way.

In a similar way, we could define the degree of a map between compact oriented [manifolds with boundary](wikipedia://en/2073470#Manifold_with_boundary).

## Properties {#Properties}

The degree of a map is a [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) invariant; moreover for continuous maps from the [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) to itself it is a *complete* homotopy invariant, i.e. two maps $f, g: S^n \to S^n \,$ are homotopic if and only if $\deg(f) = \deg(g)$.

In other words, degree is an isomorphism between $\left[S^n, S^n\right] = \pi_n S^n$ and $\mathbf{Z}$.

Moreover, the [Hopf theorem](wikipedia://en/32796318) states that for any $n$-dimensional closed oriented [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M*, two maps $f, g: M \to S^n$ are homotopic if and only if $\deg(f) = \deg(g).$

A self-map $f: S^n \to S^n$ of the *n*-sphere is extendable to a map $F: B_{n+1} \to S^n$ from the *n+1*-ball to the *n*-sphere if and only if $\deg(f) = 0$. (Here the function *F* extends *f* in the sense that *f* is the restriction of *F* to $S^n$.)

:::

## Calculating the degree {#Calculating_the_degree}

There is an algorithm for calculating the topological degree deg(*f*, *B*, 0) of a continuous function *f* from an *n*-dimensional box *B* (a product of *n* intervals) to $\mathbb{R}^n$, where *f* is given in the form of arithmetical expressions.[^o16] An implementation of the algorithm is available in [TopDeg](https://sourceforge.net/projects/topdeg/) - a software tool for computing the degree (LGPL-3).
