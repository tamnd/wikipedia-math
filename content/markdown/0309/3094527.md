# Castelnuovo–de Franchis theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Castelnuovo–de Franchis theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Castelnuovo–de Franchis theorem** is a classical result on complex [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311). Let *X* be such a surface, projective and [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities), and let

:::

ω<sub>1</sub> and ω<sub>2</sub>

be two [differentials of the first kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentials_of_the_first_kind) on *X* which are linearly independent but with [wedge product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedge_product) 0. Then this data can be represented as a [pullback](wikipedia://en/9413508) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260): there is a non-singular algebraic curve *C*, a [morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_function)

φ: *X* → *C*,

and differentials of the first kind ω<sub>1</sub> and ω<sub>2</sub> on *C* such that

φ\*(*ω*<sub>1</sub>) = *ω*<sub>1</sub> and φ\*(*ω*<sub>2</sub>) = *ω*<sub>2</sub>.

This result is due to [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891) and [Michele de Franchis](wikipedia://en/56153013) (1875–1946).

The converse, that two such pullbacks would have wedge 0, is immediate.
