# Crunode

*Image: Cubic with double point.svg.* A crunode at the origin of the curve defined by $y^2 - x^2(x+1)=0.$

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crunode"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **crunode**[^o2] (archaic; from Latin *crux* "cross" + *node*[^o3]) or **node** of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) is a type of [singular point](wikipedia://en/4230456) at which the curve intersects itself so that both branches of the curve have distinct [tangent lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_line) at the point of intersection. A crunode is also known as an *ordinary double point*.[^o4][^o5]

:::

In the case of a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) real [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) $f(x, y) = 0$, a point is a crunode provided that both first [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) vanish

$$\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{y}} = 0$$

and the [Hessian determinant](wikipedia://en/412108) is negative:

$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} - \left(\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x ~\partial y}\right)^2 < 0.$[^o6]
