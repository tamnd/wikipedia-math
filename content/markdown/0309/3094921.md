# Tate module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tate module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Tate module** of an abelian group, named for [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933), is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) constructed from an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *A*. Often, this construction is made in the following situation: *G* is a [commutative group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*, *K<sup>s</sup>* is the [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of *K*, and *A* = *G*(*K<sup>s</sup>*) (the [*K<sup>s</sup>*-valued points of *G*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#point)). In this case, the Tate module of *A* is equipped with an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of *K*, and it is referred to as the Tate module of *G*.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given an abelian group *A* and a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*, the ***p*-adic Tate module of *A*** is

$$T_p(A)=\underset{\longleftarrow}{\lim} A[p^n]$$

where *A*\[*p<sup>n</sup>*\] is the [*p<sup>n</sup>* torsion](wikipedia://en/144052) of *A* (i.e. the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of the multiplication-by-*p<sup>n</sup>* map), and the [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) is over [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) *n* with [transition morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_morphism) given by the multiplication-by-*p* map *A*\[*p*<sup>*n*+1</sup>\] → *A*\[*p<sup>n</sup>*\]. Thus, the Tate module encodes all the *p*-power torsion of *A*. It is equipped with the structure of a [**Z**<sub>*p*</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer)-module via

$$z(a_n)_n=((z\text{ mod }p^n)a_n)_n.$$

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

### *The* Tate module {#The_Tate_module}

When the abelian group *A* is the group of [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) in a separable closure *K<sup>s</sup>* of *K*, the *p*-adic Tate module of *A* is sometimes referred to as *the* Tate module (where the choice of *p* and *K* are tacitly understood). It is a [free rank one module](wikipedia://en/326454) over *Z*<sub>*p*</sub> with a linear action of the absolute Galois group *G<sub>K</sub>* of *K*. Thus, it is a [Galois representation](wikipedia://en/486929) also referred to as the [*p*-adic cyclotomic character](wikipedia://en/18846136) of *K*. It can also be considered as the Tate module of the [multiplicative group scheme](wikipedia://en/504697) **G**<sub>*m*,*K*</sub> over *K*.

### The Tate module of an abelian variety {#The_Tate_module_of_an_abelian_variety}

Given an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) *G* over a field *K*, the *K<sup>s</sup>*-valued points of *G* are an abelian group. The *p*-adic Tate module *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*G*) of *G* is a Galois representation (of the absolute Galois group, *G<sub>K</sub>*, of *K*).

Classical results on abelian varieties show that if *K* has [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero), or characteristic ℓ where the prime number *p* ≠ ℓ, then *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*G*) is a free module over *Z*<sub>*p*</sub> of rank 2*d*, where *d* is the dimension of *G*.[^o8] In the other case, it is still free, but the rank may take any value from 0 to *d* (see for example [Hasse–Witt matrix](wikipedia://en/3028181)).

In the case where *p* is not equal to the characteristic of *K*, the *p*-adic Tate module of *G* is the [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) of the [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231) $H^1_{\text{et}}(G\times_KK^s,\mathbf{Z}_p)$.

A special case of the [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804) can be phrased in terms of Tate modules.[^o9] Suppose *K* is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661) over its [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) (e.g. a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822), a [global function field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_function_field)), of characteristic different from *p*, and *A* and *B* are two abelian varieties over *K*. The Tate conjecture then predicts that

$$\mathrm{Hom}_K(A,B)\otimes\mathbf{Z}_p\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_K}(T_p(A),T_p(B))$$

where Hom<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*, *B*) is the group of [morphisms of abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) from *A* to *B*, and the right-hand side is the group of *G<sub>K</sub>*-linear maps from *T<sub>p</sub>*(*A*) to *T<sub>p</sub>*(*B*). The case where *K* is a finite field was proved by Tate himself in the 1960s.[^o10] [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) proved the case where *K* is a number field in his celebrated "Mordell paper".[^o11]

In the case of a Jacobian over a curve *C* over a finite field *k* of characteristic prime to *p*, the Tate module can be identified with the Galois group of the composite extension

$$k(C) \subset \hat k (C) \subset A^{(p)} \$$

where $\hat k$ is an extension of *k* containing all *p*-power roots of unity and *A*<sup>(*p*)</sup> is the maximal unramified abelian *p*-extension of $\hat k (C)$.[^o12]

## Tate module of a number field {#Tate_module_of_a_number_field}

The description of the Tate module for the function field of a curve over a finite field suggests a definition for a Tate module of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822), the other class of [global field](wikipedia://en/288358), introduced by [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa). For a number field *K* we let *K*<sub>*m*</sub> denote the extension by *p*<sup>*m*</sup>-power roots of unity, $\hat K$ the union of the *K*<sub>*m*</sub> and *A*<sup>(*p*)</sup> the maximal unramified abelian *p*-extension of $\hat K$. Let

$$T_p(K) = \mathrm{Gal}(A^{(p)}/\hat K) \ .$$

Then *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) is a pro-*p*-group and so a **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-module. Using [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) one can describe *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) as isomorphic to the inverse limit of the class groups *C*<sub>*m*</sub> of the *K*<sub>*m*</sub> under norm.[^o12]

Iwasawa exhibited *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) as a module over the completion **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>\[\[*T*\]\] and this implies a formula for the exponent of *p* in the order of the class groups *C*<sub>*m*</sub> of the form

$$\lambda m + \mu p^m + \kappa \ .$$

The [Ferrero–Washington theorem](wikipedia://en/37976622) states that μ is zero.[^o15]
