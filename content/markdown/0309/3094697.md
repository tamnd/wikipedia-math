# De Franchis theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Franchis theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **de Franchis theorem** is one of a number of closely related statements applying to [compact Riemann surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface), or, more generally, [algebraic curves](wikipedia://en/253260), *X* and *Y*, in the case of [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) *g* > 1. The simplest is that the [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) group of *X* is finite (see though [Hurwitz's automorphisms theorem](wikipedia://en/1113637)). More generally,

:::

- the set of non-constant [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) from *X* to *Y* is finite;
- fixing *X*, for all but a finite number of such *Y*, there is no non-constant morphism from *X* to *Y*.

These results are named for Michele De Franchis (1875–1946). It is sometimes referenced as the De Franchis-[Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) theorem. It was used in an important way by [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) to prove the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture).
