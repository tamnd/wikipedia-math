# Intermediate Jacobian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Intermediate Jacobian"}

In mathematics, the **intermediate Jacobian** of a compact [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) or [Hodge structure](wikipedia://en/10297305) is a [complex torus](wikipedia://en/3055042) that is a common generalization of the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of a curve and the [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety) and the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828). It is obtained by putting a [complex structure](wikipedia://en/509742) on the torus $H^n(M,\mathbb{R})/H^n(M,\mathbb{Z})$ for *n* odd. There are several different natural ways to put a complex structure on this torus, giving several different sorts of intermediate Jacobians, including one due to (txt) and one due to (txt). The ones constructed by Weil have natural polarizations if *M* is projective, and so are abelian varieties, while the ones constructed by Griffiths behave well under [holomorphic deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_deformation).

:::

A complex structure on a real vector space is given by an automorphism *I* with square $-1$. The complex structures on $H^n(M,\mathbb{R})$ are defined using the [Hodge decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_decomposition)

$$H^{n}(M,{\mathbb{R}}) \otimes {\mathbb{C}} = H^{n,0}(M)\oplus\cdots\oplus H^{0,n}(M).$$

On $H^{p,q}$ the Weil complex structure $I_W$ is multiplication by $i^{p-q}$, while the Griffiths complex structure $I_G$ is multiplication by $i$ if $p > q$ and $-i$ if $p < q$. Both these complex structures map $H^n(M,\mathbb{R})$ into itself and so defined complex structures on it.

For $n=1$ the intermediate Jacobian is the [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety), and for $n=2 \dim (M)-1$ it is the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828). In these two extreme cases the constructions of Weil and Griffiths are equivalent.

(Clemens Griffiths 1972) used intermediate Jacobians to show that non-singular [cubic threefolds](wikipedia://en/23560713) are not [rational](wikipedia://en/865939), even though they are [unirational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unirational).
