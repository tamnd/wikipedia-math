# Zariski surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zariski surface"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Zariski surface** is a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p* > 0 such that there is a dominant inseparable map of degree *p* from the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) to the surface. In particular, all Zariski surfaces are [unirational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unirational). They were named by Piotr Blass in 1977 after [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) who used them in 1958 to give examples of unirational surfaces in characteristic *p* > 0 that are not rational. (In characteristic 0 by contrast, [Castelnuovo's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelnuovo's_theorem) implies that all unirational surfaces are rational.)

:::

Zariski surfaces are [birational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birational_isomorphism) to surfaces in [affine](wikipedia://en/298834) 3-space *A*<sup>3</sup> defined by [irreducible polynomials](wikipedia://en/188725) of the form

$$z^p = f(x, y).\$$

The following problem was posed by Oscar Zariski in 1971: Let *S* be a Zariski surface with vanishing geometric [genus](wikipedia://en/61891). Is *S* necessarily a rational surface? For *p* = 2 and for *p* = 3 the answer to the above problem is negative as shown in 1977 by Piotr Blass in his [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) Ph.D. thesis and by William E. Lang in his Harvard Ph.D. thesis in 1978. (txt) announced further examples giving a negative answer to Zariski's question in every characteristic *p* > 0. His method, however, is non-constructive at the moment and no explicit equations for *p* > 3 are known.
