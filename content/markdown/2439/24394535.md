# Stanley–Reisner ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanley–Reisner ring"}

In mathematics, a **Stanley–Reisner ring**, or **face ring**, is a quotient of a [polynomial algebra](wikipedia://en/373065) over a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28algebra%29) by a square-free [monomial](wikipedia://en/43023424) [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977). Such ideals are described more geometrically in terms of finite [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686). The Stanley–Reisner ring construction is a basic tool within [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) and [combinatorial commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/11503412).[^o2] Its properties were investigated by [Richard Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397), [Melvin Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster), and Gerald Reisner in the early 1970s.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and properties"}

## Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

Given an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) Δ on the vertex set {*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>} and a field **k**, the corresponding **Stanley–Reisner ring**, or **face ring**, denoted **k**\[Δ\], is obtained from the polynomial ring **k**\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>\] by quotienting out the ideal *I*<sub>Δ</sub> generated by the square-free monomials corresponding to the non-faces of Δ:

$$I_\Delta=(x_{i_1}\ldots x_{i_r}: \{i_1,\ldots,i_r\}\notin\Delta), \quad k[\Delta]=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I_\Delta.$$

The ideal *I*<sub>Δ</sub> is called the **Stanley–Reisner ideal** or the **face ideal** of Δ.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

- The Stanley–Reisner ring **k**\[Δ\] is multigraded by **Z**<sup>*n*</sup>, where the degree of the variable *x*<sub>*i*</sub> is the *i*th standard basis vector *e*<sub>*i*</sub> of **Z**<sup>*n*</sup>.
- As a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over **k**, the Stanley–Reisner ring of Δ admits a direct sum decomposition

$$k[\Delta] = \bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Delta}k[\Delta]_\sigma,$$

whose summands **k**\[Δ\]<sub>*σ*</sub> have a basis of the monomials (not necessarily square-free) supported on the faces *σ* of Δ.

- The [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of **k**\[Δ\] is one larger than the dimension of the simplicial complex Δ.
- The multigraded, or *fine*, [Hilbert series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_series) of **k**\[Δ\] is given by the formula

$$H(k[\Delta]; x_1,\ldots,x_n) = \sum_{\sigma\in\Delta}\prod_{i\in\sigma}\frac{x_i}{1-x_i}.$$

- The ordinary, or *coarse*, Hilbert series of **k**\[Δ\] is obtained from its multigraded Hilbert series by setting the degree of every variable *x*<sub>*i*</sub> equal to 1:

$$H(k[\Delta]; t,\ldots,t) = \frac{1}{(1-t)^n}\sum_{i=0}^d f_{i-1} t^i(1-t)^{n-i},$$

where *d* = dim(Δ) + 1 is the Krull dimension of **k**\[Δ\] and *f*<sub>*i*</sub> is the number of *i*-faces of Δ. If it is written in the form

$$H(k[\Delta]; t,\ldots,t) = \frac{h_0 + h_1 t + \cdots + h_d t^d}{(1-t)^d}$$

then the coefficients (*h*<sub>0</sub>, ..., *h*<sub>*d*</sub>) of the numerator form the *h*-vector of the simplicial complex Δ.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

It is common to assume that every vertex {*x*<sub>*i*</sub>} is a simplex in Δ. Thus none of the variables belongs to the Stanley–Reisner ideal *I*<sub>Δ</sub>.

- Δ is a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) {*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>}. Then *I*<sub>Δ</sub> is the zero ideal and

$$k[\Delta]=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$$

is the polynomial algebra in *n* variables over **k**.

- The simplicial complex Δ consists of *n* isolated vertices {*x*<sub>1</sub>}, ..., {*x*<sub>*n*</sub>}. Then

$$I_\Delta=\{x_i x_j: 1\leq i < j \leq n\}$$

and the Stanley–Reisner ring is the following truncation of the polynomial ring in *n* variables over **k**:

$$k[\Delta] = k\oplus\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq n} x_i k[x_i].$$

- Generalizing the previous two examples, let Δ be the *d*-skeleton of the simplex {*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>}, thus it consists of all (*d* + 1)-element subsets of {*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>}. Then the Stanley–Reisner ring is following truncation of the polynomial ring in *n* variables over **k**:

$$k[\Delta] = k\oplus\bigoplus_{0\leq r\leq d} \bigoplus_{i_0<\ldots<i_r}x_{i_0}\ldots x_{i_r} k[x_{i_0},\ldots,x_{i_r}].$$

- Suppose that the abstract simplicial complex Δ is a simplicial join of abstract simplicial complexes Δ<sup>′</sup> on *x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*m*</sub> and Δ<sup>′′</sup> on *x*<sub>*m*+1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>. Then the Stanley–Reisner ring of Δ is the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) over **k** of the Stanley–Reisner rings of Δ<sup>′</sup> and Δ<sup>′′</sup>:

$$k[\Delta]\simeq k[\Delta']\otimes_k k[\Delta''].$$

## Cohen–Macaulay condition and the upper bound conjecture {#Cohen–Macaulay_condition_and_the_upper_bound_conjecture}

The face ring **k**\[Δ\] is a multigraded algebra over **k** all of whose components with respect to the fine grading have dimension at most 1. Consequently, its homology can be studied by combinatorial and geometric methods. An abstract simplicial complex Δ is called **Cohen–Macaulay** over **k** if its face ring is a [Cohen–Macaulay ring](wikipedia://en/497407).[^o12] In his 1974 thesis, Gerald Reisner gave a complete characterization of such complexes. This was soon followed up by more precise homological results about face rings due to Melvin Hochster. Then Richard Stanley found a way to prove the [Upper Bound Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bound_Conjecture) for [simplicial spheres](wikipedia://en/22359636), which was open at the time, using the face ring construction and Reisner's criterion of Cohen–Macaulayness. Stanley's idea of translating difficult conjectures in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) into statements from [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and proving them by means of [homological](wikipedia://en/139410) techniques was the origin of the rapidly developing field of [combinatorial commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/11503412).

::: {#o14 .statement cue=named-heading name="Reisner's criterion"}

### Reisner's criterion {#Reisner's_criterion}

A simplicial complex Δ is Cohen–Macaulay over **k** [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) for all simplices *σ* ∈ Δ, all reduced [simplicial homology](wikipedia://en/1060236) groups of the link of *σ* in Δ with coefficients in **k** are zero, except the top dimensional one:[^o12]

$$\tilde{H}_{i}(\operatorname{link}_\Delta(\sigma); k)=0\quad \text{for all} \quad i<\dim \operatorname{link}_\Delta(\sigma).$$

A result due to Munkres then shows that the Cohen–Macaulayness of Δ over **k** is a [topological property](wikipedia://en/1337282): it depends only on the [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) class of the simplicial complex Δ. Namely, let |Δ| be the [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/723105) of Δ. Then the vanishing of the simplicial homology groups in Reisner's criterion is equivalent to the following statement about the reduced and relative [singular homology](wikipedia://en/431041) groups of |Δ|:

$$\text{For all } p\in|\Delta|\text{ and for all } i<\dim |\Delta| = d-1, \quad \tilde{H}_i(\operatorname |\Delta|; k) = H_i(\operatorname |\Delta|, \operatorname |\Delta| - p; k) = 0.$$

In particular, if the complex Δ is a [simplicial sphere](wikipedia://en/22359636), that is, |Δ| is homeomorphic to a [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782), then it is Cohen–Macaulay over any field. This is a key step in Stanley's proof of the Upper Bound Conjecture. By contrast, there are examples of simplicial complexes whose Cohen–Macaulayness depends on the characteristic of the field **k**.
