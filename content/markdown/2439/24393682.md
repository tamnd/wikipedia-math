# Jim Geelen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jim Geelen"}

**Jim Geelen** is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the Department of [Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [Optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29) in the [faculty of mathematics](wikipedia://en/4320685) at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo), where he holds the [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in [Combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555).[^o3] He is known for his work on [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory) and the extension of the [Graph Minors Project](wikipedia://en/351769) to [representable matroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representable_matroid). In 2003, he won the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) with his co-authors A. M. H. Gerards, and A. Kapoor for their research on [Rota's excluded minors conjecture](wikipedia://en/36760081).[^o4][^o5] In 2006, he won the [Coxeter–James Prize](wikipedia://en/18739444) presented by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o6]

:::

He received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in 1992 from [Curtin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtin_University) in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), and obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1996 at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) under the supervision of William Cunningham.[^o7] After brief postdoctoral fellowships in the Netherlands, Germany, and Japan, he returned to the University of Waterloo in 1997.[^o8]
