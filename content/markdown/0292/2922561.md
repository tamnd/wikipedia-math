# The Lady Tasting Tea

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Lady Tasting Tea"}

***The Lady Tasting Tea: How Statistics Revolutionized Science in the Twentieth Century*** () is a book by [David Salsburg](wikipedia://en/2921764) about the history of modern [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and the role it played in the development of science and industry.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The title comes from the "[lady tasting tea](wikipedia://en/26297705)", an example from the famous book, *[The Design of Experiments](wikipedia://en/17229561)*, by [Ronald A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_A._Fisher). Regarding Fisher's example, the statistician [Debabrata Basu](wikipedia://en/1978708) wrote that "the famous case of the '[lady tasting tea](wikipedia://en/26297705)'" was "one of the two supporting pillars \[...\] of the randomization analysis of experimental data".[^o5]

## Summary {#Summary}

The book discusses the statistical revolution which took place in the twentieth century, where science shifted from a deterministic view ([Clockwork universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockwork_universe)) to a perspective concerned primarily with probabilities and distributions and parameters. Salsburg does this through a collection of stories about the people who were fundamental in the change, starting with men like [R.A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.A._Fisher) and [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695). He discusses at length how many of these people had their own [philosophy of statistics](wikipedia://en/19634230), and in particular their own understanding of [statistical significance](wikipedia://en/160995).[^o7] Throughout, he introduces in a very nontechnical fashion a variety of statistical ideas and methods, such as [maximum likelihood estimation](wikipedia://en/140806) and [bootstrapping](wikipedia://en/6885770).

## Reception {#Reception}

The book was generally well-received, receiving coverage in a variety of medical[^o9][^o10] and statistical journals.[^o11] Reviewers from the medical field enjoyed Salsburg's coverage of Fisher's opposition to early research on the [health effects of tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_tobacco). Critics disagreed with certain opinions that Salsburg voiced, like his barebones portrayal of [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412) and his seeming disdain for [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210).[^o3] Nevertheless, almost all reviewers appreciated the interesting read and recommended the book to people in their field as well as a general audience.
