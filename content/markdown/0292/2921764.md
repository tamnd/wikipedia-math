# David Salsburg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Salsburg"}

**David S. Salsburg** (born 1931) is an American author. His 2002 book *[The Lady Tasting Tea](wikipedia://en/2922561)*, subtitled *How Statistics Revolutionized Science in the Twentieth Century*, provides a layman's overview of important developments in the field of statistics in the late 19th and early 20th century, particularly in the areas of [experiment design](wikipedia://en/9541), the study of [random distributions](wikipedia://en/23543), and the careers of major researchers in the field such as [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695), and [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106).[^o2]

:::

Salsburg is a retired pharmaceutical company statistician (having been a senior research fellow at [Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer)'s central research department until 1995) who has taught at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), [Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale), [Connecticut College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_College), the [University of Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Connecticut), the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), [Rhode Island College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_College), and [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_%28Connecticut%29)[^o3] and has been a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) since 1978.[^o4] Salsburg was also the first [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) hired by [Pfizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizer).[^o5] In 1994, Salsburg was awarded the *Career Achievement Award of the Biostatistics Section of the [Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_Research_and_Manufacturers_of_America)*, given annually for "significant contributions to the advancement of biostatistics in the pharmaceutical industry".[^o6] The [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) characterised him as follows

"Salsburg believes that the public is not fully aware of the degree to which recent developments in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) impact the way we perceive the world. He correctly points out that the twentieth century saw the fading of a [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic) outlook and the rise of a statistical/[probabilistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic) way of looking at the world. This ongoing revolution is not only in the [physical sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_sciences), it also touches the social sciences and even the [humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities). Though profound, it is a quiet revolution that has been unnoticed by many."[^o7]

Salsburg's most recent book, *Errors, Blunders and Lies: How to Tell the Difference* was published in 2017.[^o8]
