# Odious number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Odious number"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **odious number** is a positive integer that has an odd [number of 1s](wikipedia://en/1127884) in its [binary expansion](wikipedia://en/238686). [Non-negative integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative_integer) that are not odious are called [evil numbers](wikipedia://en/4444651).

:::

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), an odious number is said to have [odd parity](wikipedia://en/194467#Parity).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The first odious numbers are 1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 11, 13, 14, 16, 19, 21, 22, 25, 26, 28, 31, 32, 35, 37, 38, and so on.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If $a(n)$ denotes the $n$th odious number (with $a(0)=1$), then for all $n$, $a(a(n))=2a(n)$.[^o7]

Every positive integer $n$ has an odious multiple that is at most $n(n+4)$. The numbers for which this bound is tight are exactly the [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number) with even exponents, the numbers of the form $n = 2^{2r}-1$, such as 3, 15, 63, etc. For these numbers, the smallest odious multiple is exactly $n(n+4) = 2^{4r}+2^{2r+1}-3$.[^o8]

:::

## Related sequences {#Related_sequences}

The odious numbers give the positions of the non-zero values in the [Thue–Morse sequence](wikipedia://en/491903). Every [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948) is odious, because its binary expansion has only one non-zero bit. Except for 3, every [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908) is odious, because its binary expansion consists of an odd prime number of consecutive non-zero bits.

Non-negative integers that are not odious are called [evil numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_numbers). The partition of the non-negative integers into the odious and evil numbers is the unique partition of these numbers into two sets that have equal [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303) of pair-wise sums.[^o10]
