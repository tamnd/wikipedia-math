# List of combinatorial computational geometry topics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of combinatorial computational geometry topics"}

**List of combinatorial computational geometry topics** enumerates the topics of [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927) that states problems in terms of geometric objects as [discrete](wikipedia://en/8492) entities and hence the methods of their solution are mostly theories and [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) of [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) character.

:::

See [List of numerical computational geometry topics](wikipedia://en/2920916) for another flavor of computational geometry that deals with geometric objects as continuous entities and applies methods and algorithms of nature characteristic to [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).

## Extremal shapes {#Extremal_shapes}

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Minimum bounding box"}

- [Minimum bounding box](wikipedia://en/12087300) ([Smallest enclosing box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallest_enclosing_box), [Smallest bounding box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallest_bounding_box))

:::

  - 2-D case: [Smallest bounding rectangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallest_bounding_rectangle) ([Smallest enclosing rectangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallest_enclosing_rectangle))
  - There are two common variants of this problem.
    - In many areas of computer graphics, the [bounding box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounding_box) (often abbreviated to bbox) is understood to be the smallest box delimited by sides parallel to coordinate axes which encloses the objects in question.
    - In other applications, such as [packaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packaging), the problem is to find the smallest box the object (or objects) may fit in ("packaged"). Here the box may assume an arbitrary orientation with respect to the "packaged" objects.
