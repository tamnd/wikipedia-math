# Florence Weldon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Florence Weldon"}

**Florence Joy Weldon** (*née* **Tebb**, 1858 – 1936) was an English mathematician who worked as "one of the first college-educated [human computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computer),"[^o2] analysing data about [biological variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_variation).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Florence was the daughter of businessman and activist [William Tebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tebb). Her younger sister was physiologist [Mary Tebb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Tebb). In the early 1880s she studied mathematics at [Girton College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girton_College,_Cambridge).[^o4] She married evolutionary biologist [Raphael Weldon](wikipedia://en/1557459) in March 1883.[^o5][^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computation"}

## Computation {#Computation}

Florence joined with Weldon's work on biological variation in shrimps and crabs. The pair travelled around England, Italy and the Bahamas, collecting about a thousand specimens at a time. Florence worked as Weldon's chief computer in analysing the data collected by measuring the specimens.[^o9] With no calculating machines available, she used [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860) and [Crelle](wikipedia://en/973559) tables.[^o2] One study involved taking twenty-three measurements each from a thousand shore crab specimens from the Bay of Naples, which demonstrated that twenty-two of the features measured were [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) and the other was [bimodal](wikipedia://en/1068378).[^o10] Another study included taking measurements in duplicate from over eight thousand crabs.[^o11]

Florence's contribution was acknowledged by her long-time friend and correspondent [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695), who completed her husband's manuscripts after his death.[^o10]

:::

## Art collection and legacy {#Art_collection_and_legacy}

In 1928, Florence received an honorary degree of Master of Arts from the University of Oxford after making a significant bequest of French paintings there.[^o13] The [Ashmolean Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashmolean_Museum) also contains a room named in her honour.[^o10]

Florence died in 1936, leaving the residue of her estate to establish a Chair of [Biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry) at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).[^o14]
