# William Marrat

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Marrat"}

**William Marrat** (1772–1852) was an English printer, publisher and educator, known as a mathematician and antiquarian.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born at [Sibsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibsey), [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire), on 6 April 1772, Marrat was self-taught through wide reading and study of modern language. While at [Boston, Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Lincolnshire), he for some years worked as a printer and publisher. In 1811–12 he, in conjunction with [Pishey Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishey_Thompson), ran *The Enquirer, or Literary, Mathematical, and Philosophical Repository*, Boston.[^o3]

At other times Marrat was a teacher of mathematics, in Lincolnshire and elsewhere. He lived in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) from 1817 to 1820, and edited there *The Scientific Journal* (imprint "Perth Amboy, N. J. and New York", 1818, nine numbers). He returned to England, and at [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool), where he settled in 1821.[^o3] [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) taught at his school, in 1833.[^o4]

From 1833 to 1836 Marrat was mathematical tutor in a school at [Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter), but on the death of his wife he returned to Liverpool. He died suddenly there on 26 March 1852, and was buried at the necropolis near that city.[^o3]

## Works {#Works}

Marrat was for fifty years a contributor to mathematical serials, including *[The Ladies' Diary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ladies'_Diary)* and *The Gentlemen's Diary*, *The Receptacle*, *The Student*, and the *[Leeds Correspondent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Correspondent)*. His first book was *An Introduction to the Theory and Practice of Mechanics*, Boston, 1810, pp. 468. During 1814–16 he wrote *The History of Lincolnshire*, which came out in parts, and after three volumes had been published, it was stopped: Marrat alleged this was a consequence of [Sir Joseph Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Joseph_Banks)'s refusal to allow access to his papers. In 1816 his *Historical Description of Stamford* was published at Lincoln.[^o3]

An anonymous *Geometrical System of Conic Sections*, Cambridge, 1822, was ascribed to Marrat in the catalogue of the Liverpool Free Library. He compiled *Lunar Tables*, Liverpool, 1823, and wrote *The Elements of Mechanical Philosophy*, 1825. At this period he compiled the *Liverpool Tide Table*, and was a contributor to *[Blackwood's Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackwood's_Magazine)*.[^o3]

## Family {#Family}

His son Frederick Price Marrat was known as a [conchologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchologist).[^o3]
