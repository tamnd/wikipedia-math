::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Great duoantiprism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great duoantiprism** is the only [uniform](wikipedia://en/6842392) star-[duoantiprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duoantiprism) solution $p = 5,$ $q = \tfrac{5}{3},$ in [4-dimensional geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-dimensional_geometry). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{5\}\otimes\{5/3\},$ $s\{5\}s\{5/3\}$ or $\operatorname{ht}_{0,1,2,3}\{5,2,5/3\},$ [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , constructed from 10 [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091), 10 [pentagrammic crossed-antiprisms](wikipedia://en/4157338), and 50 [tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedra).

:::

Its vertices are a subset of those of the [small stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604577).

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The great duoantiprism can be constructed from a nonuniform variant of the 10-10/3 duoprism (a duoprism of a [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393) and a [decagram](wikipedia://en/19546742)) where the decagram's edge length is around 1.618 ([golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)) times the edge length of the decagon via an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) process. The [decagonal prisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagonal_prism) alternate into [pentagonal antiprisms](wikipedia://en/2860091), the [decagrammic prisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decagrammic_prism) alternate into [pentagrammic crossed-antiprisms](wikipedia://en/4157338) with new regular [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) created at the deleted vertices. This is the only uniform solution for the p-q duoantiprism aside from the regular [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) (as a 2-2 duoantiprism).
