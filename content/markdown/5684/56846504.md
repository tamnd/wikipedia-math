# Rug plot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rug plot"}

*Image: Comparison of 1D bandwidth selectors.png.* A rug plot of 100 data points appears in blue along the x-axis. (The points are sampled from the normal distribution shown in gray. The other curves show various [kernel density estimates](wikipedia://en/2090057) of the data.)

A **rug plot** is a [plot](wikipedia://en/19774918) of data for a single quantitative variable, displayed as marks along an axis. It is used to visualise the distribution of the data. As such it is analogous to a [histogram](wikipedia://en/13266) with zero-width bins, or a one-dimensional [scatter plot](wikipedia://en/412544).

:::

Rug plots are often used in combination with two-dimensional scatter plots by placing a rug plot of the x values of the data along the x-axis, and similarly for the y values. This is the origin of the term "rug plot", as these rug plots with perpendicular markers look like tassels along the edges of the rectangular "rug" of the scatter plot.
