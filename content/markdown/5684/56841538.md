# Weyl sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weyl sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Weyl sequence** is a sequence from the [equidistribution theorem](wikipedia://en/2541664) proven by [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544):[^o2]

:::

The sequence of all multiples of an irrational *α*,

0, *α*, 2*α*, 3*α*, 4*α*, ...

is [equidistributed](wikipedia://en/1699223) modulo 1.[^o3]

In other words, the sequence of the fractional parts of each term will be uniformly distributed in the interval \[0, 1).

## In computing {#In_computing}

In [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing), an integer version of this sequence is often used to generate a [discrete uniform distribution](wikipedia://en/1699214) rather than a continuous one. Instead of using an irrational number, which cannot be calculated on a digital computer, the ratio of two integers is used in its place. An integer *k* is chosen, [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime) to an integer modulus *m*. In the common case that *m* is a power of 2, this amounts to requiring that *k* is odd.

The sequence of all multiples of such an integer *k*,

0, *k*, 2*k*, 3*k*, 4*k*, …

is equidistributed modulo *m*.

That is, the sequence of the remainders of each term when divided by *m* will be uniformly distributed in the interval \[0, *m*).

The term appears to originate with [George Marsaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marsaglia)’s paper "[Xorshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xorshift) RNGs".[^o5] The following C code generates what Marsaglia calls a "Weyl sequence":

d += 362437;

In this case, the odd integer is 362437, and the results are computed modulo *m* = 2<sup>*32*</sup> because d is a 32-bit quantity. The results are equidistributed modulo 2<sup>32</sup>.
