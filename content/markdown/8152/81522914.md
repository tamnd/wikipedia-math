# Wildlife population

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wildlife population"}

A **wildlife population** or **species population** is the concept of "[population](wikipedia://en/22949)" applied to any species other than humans. Understanding a wildlife population requires a [census](wikipedia://en/6889) of the species, and the technique for accomplishing that vary depending on the species and circumstance.[^o2] Characteristics which define a wildlife population include [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density), [birth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_rate), [mortality rate](wikipedia://en/191305), [age distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_distribution), [biotic potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biotic_potential), [biological dispersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_dispersal), and [growth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_form).[^o3]

:::

Methods for measuring wildlife populations include [mark and recapture](wikipedia://en/1507717), [distance sampling](wikipedia://en/20758913), and examining a [harvest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest).[^o4] Biologists who survey wildlife populations often work in a business context where they balance maintaining quality data, but complete the survey quickly, and work at low cost.[^o5] Economic reasons for doing wildlife surveys can include harvesting wildlife, controlling disease, or negotiating conflicts between multiple business interests who claim control of a resource.[^o6]

Motivations for studying wildlife populations include [wildlife conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_conservation)[^o7] and [wildlife management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_management).[^o8][^o9]

Wildlife populations are declining globally at fast rates. The [Living Planet Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Planet_Index) reported that from 1970-2020, the global populations in [freshwater ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_ecosystem) have decreased by 85%, [Terrestrial animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_animal) by 69%, and [marine life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life) by 56%.[^o10] Counting the number of individuals in a population is key to predicting and identifying [ecosystem collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem_collapse) when populations of wildlife will go extinct.[^o11][^o12] [Population viability analysis](wikipedia://en/826478) is a method for determining if a population will go extinct in given circumstances.[^o13]
