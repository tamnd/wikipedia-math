# Oriented projective geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oriented projective geometry"}

**Oriented projective geometry** is an [oriented](wikipedia://en/187446) version of real [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).

:::

Whereas the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) describes the set of all unoriented lines through the origin in **R**<sup>3</sup>, the **oriented projective plane** describes lines with a given orientation. There are applications in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) and [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) where it is necessary to distinguish between rays light being emitted or absorbed by a point.

Elements in an oriented projective space are defined using signed [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316). Let $\mathbb{R}_{*}^n$ be the set of elements of $\mathbb{R}^n$ excluding the origin.

1. **Oriented projective line**, $\mathbb{T}^1$: $(x,w) \in \mathbb{R}^2_*$, with the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) $(x,w)\sim(a x,a w)\,$ for all $a>0$.
1. **Oriented projective plane**, $\mathbb{T}^2$: $(x,y,w) \in \mathbb{R}^3_*$, with $(x,y,w)\sim(a x,a y,a w)\,$ for all $a>0$.

These spaces can be viewed as extensions of [euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). $\mathbb{T}^1$ can be viewed as the union of two copies of $\mathbb{R}$, the sets (*x*,1) and (*x*,-1), plus two additional points at infinity, (1,0) and (-1,0). Likewise $\mathbb{T}^2$ can be viewed as two copies of $\mathbb{R}^2$, (*x*,*y*,1) and (*x*,*y*,-1), plus one copy of $\mathbb{T}$ (*x*,*y*,0).

An alternative way to view the spaces is as points on the circle or sphere, given by the points (*x*,*y*,*w*) with

*x*<sup>2</sup>+*y*<sup>2</sup>+*w*<sup>2</sup>=1.

## Oriented real projective space {#Oriented_real_projective_space}

Let *n* be a nonnegative integer. The **(analytical model of**, or**canonical[^n1]) oriented (real) projective space** or **(canonical[^n2]) two-sided projective[^n3] space** $\mathbb T^n$ is defined as

$\mathbb T^n=\{\{\lambda Z:\lambda\in\mathbb R_{>0}\}:Z\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\}=\{\mathbb R_{>0}Z:Z\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\}.$[^n4]

Here, we use $\mathbb T$ to stand for *two-sided*.

### Distance in oriented real projective space {#Distance_in_oriented_real_projective_space}

The [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) between two points $p=(p_x,p_y,p_w)$ and $q=(q_x,q_y,q_w)$ in $\mathbb{T}^2$ can be defined as elements

$$(\sqrt{(p_x q_w-q_x p_w)^2+(p_y q_w-q_y p_w)^2},p_w q_w))$$

in $\mathbb{T}^1$.[^n5]

The [squared Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932#Squared_Euclidean_distance) is then:

$$((p_x q_w-q_x p_w)^2+(p_y q_w-q_y p_w)^2,\mathrm{sign}(p_w q_w)(p_w q_w)^2)$$

## Oriented complex projective geometry {#Oriented_complex_projective_geometry}

Let *n* be a nonnegative integer. The **oriented complex projective space** ${\mathbb{CP}}^n_{S^1}$ is defined as

${\mathbb{CP}}^n_{S^1}=\{\{\lambda Z:\lambda\in\mathbb R_{>0}\}:Z\in\mathbb C^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\}=\{\mathbb R_{>0}Z:Z\in\mathbb C^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\}$.[^n6] Here, we write $S^1$ to stand for the [1-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782).
