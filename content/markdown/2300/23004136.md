# Empty semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Empty semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **semigroup with no elements** (the **empty semigroup**) is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) in which the [underlying set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underlying_set) is the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566). Many authors do not admit the existence of such a semigroup. For them a semigroup is by definition a *non-empty* set together with an [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) binary operation.[^o2][^o3] However not all authors insist on the underlying set of a semigroup being non-empty.[^o4] One can logically define a semigroup in which the underlying set *S* is empty. The binary operation in the semigroup is the [empty function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_function) from *S* × *S* to *S*. This operation [vacuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuously) satisfies the closure and associativity axioms of a semigroup. Not excluding the empty semigroup simplifies certain results on semigroups. For example, the result that the intersection of two subsemigroups of a semigroup *T* is a subsemigroup of *T* becomes valid even when the intersection is empty.

:::

When a semigroup is defined to have additional structure, the issue may not arise. For example, the definition of a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) requires an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962), which rules out the empty semigroup as a monoid.

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the empty semigroup is always admitted. It is the unique [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) of the category of semigroups.

A semigroup with no elements is an [inverse semigroup](wikipedia://en/1757814), since the necessary condition is vacuously satisfied.
