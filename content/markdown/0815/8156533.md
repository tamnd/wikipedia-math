# Egbert van Kampen

van Kampen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Egbert van Kampen"}

**Egbert Rudolf van Kampen** (28 May 1908 – 11 February 1942) was a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He made important contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), especially to the study of [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Van Kampen was born to Dutch parents in [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium), where his father had recently taken a job as an accountant in [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp). At the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) the family moved back to the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), first to [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) and in 1918 to [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague). At the age of 16 he graduated from high school and entered [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) to study mathematics. After his undergraduate studies he continued with a doctorate study at the same university under the guidance of [Willem van der Woude](wikipedia://en/47912777). In 1927, Van Kampen traveled to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) to meet with [Bartel van der Waerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartel_van_der_Waerden) and [Pavel Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Aleksandrov). In the summer of 1928 he worked with [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg). Around that time, while still only 20 years old, he was offered a position by [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in the United States. He received his Ph.D. degree with Van der Woude in Leiden in 1929, writing a dissertation entitled *Die kombinatorische Topologie und die Dualitaetssaetze*.[^o4]

In 1931, Van Kampen took up the position which he had been offered at Johns Hopkins University in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland), and travelled to the United States. There he met [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) who had taught at Johns Hopkins as a Johnston Scholar from 1927 until 1929, when he had joined the Faculty. Zariski had been working on the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of the [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of an [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260), and he had found generators and relations for the fundamental group but was unable to show that he had found sufficient relations to give a [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) for the group. Van Kampen solved the problem, showing that Zariski's relations were sufficient, and the result is now known as the Zariski–Van Kampen theorem. This led Van Kampen to formulate and prove what is nowadays known as the [Seifert–Van Kampen theorem](wikipedia://en/382771).[^o4]

By the late 1930s, Van Kampen started to suffer from headaches which in 1941 were diagnosed to arise from a tumor originating from a birth mark near his ear. After three surgeries in 1941 and 1942, Van Kampen succumbed to cancer and died on 11 February 1942.[^o4]
