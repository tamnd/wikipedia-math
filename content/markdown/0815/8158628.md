# Hyperbolic volume

*Image: Blue Figure-Eight Knot.png.* The hyperbolic volume of the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565) is 2.0298832.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperbolic volume"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **hyperbolic volume** of a [hyperbolic link](wikipedia://en/1248299) is the volume of the link's [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) with respect to its complete hyperbolic metric. The volume is necessarily a finite real number, and is a [topological invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_invariant) of the link.[^o5] As a link invariant, it was first studied by [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) in connection with his [geometrization conjecture](wikipedia://en/220642).[^o4]

:::

## Knot and link invariant {#Knot_and_link_invariant}

A [hyperbolic link](wikipedia://en/1248299) is a [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) in the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) whose [complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) (the space formed by removing the link from the 3-sphere) can be given a complete [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) of constant negative [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770), giving it the structure of a [hyperbolic 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1237700), a quotient of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742) by a group acting freely and discontinuously on it. The components of the link will become cusps of the 3-manifold, and the manifold itself will have finite volume. By [Mostow rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostow_rigidity), when a link complement has a hyperbolic structure, this structure is uniquely determined, and any geometric invariants of the structure are also topological invariants of the link. In particular, the hyperbolic volume of the complement is a [knot invariant](wikipedia://en/166898). In order to make it well-defined for all knots or links, the hyperbolic volume of a non-hyperbolic knot or link is often defined to be zero.

There are only finitely many hyperbolic knots for any given volume.[^o4] A [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_%28knot_theory%29) of a hyperbolic knot will have the same volume,[^o3] so it is possible to concoct examples with equal volumes; indeed, there are arbitrarily large finite sets of distinct knots with equal volumes.[^o4] In practice, hyperbolic volume has proven very effective in distinguishing knots, utilized in some of the extensive efforts at [knot tabulation](wikipedia://en/17647177). [Jeffrey Weeks](wikipedia://en/1298792)'s computer program [SnapPea](wikipedia://en/3025251) is the ubiquitous tool used to compute hyperbolic volume of a link.[^o5]

## Arbitrary manifolds {#Arbitrary_manifolds}

More generally, the hyperbolic volume may be defined for any [hyperbolic 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1237700). The [Weeks manifold](wikipedia://en/4201216) has the smallest possible volume of any closed manifold (a manifold that, unlike link complements, has no cusps); its volume is approximately 0.9427.[^o17]

Thurston and Jørgensen proved that the set of real numbers that are hyperbolic volumes of orientable 3-manifolds is [well-ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-ordered), with [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) $\omega^{\omega}$. For each $0 \le k < l$, each volume of a manifold with exactly $l$ cusps is a [limit point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) of a sequence of manifolds with $k$ cusps, and every convergent sequence of volumes is obtainable by [Dehn surgeries](wikipedia://en/1240699) on a manifold at the limiting volume; volumes of closed manifolds are exactly the [isolated points](wikipedia://en/390557).[^o18] The smallest limit point in this set of volumes is given by the [knot complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) of the [figure-eight knot](wikipedia://en/59565),[^o19] and the smallest limit point of limit points is given by the complement of the [Whitehead link](wikipedia://en/1966668).[^o20]
