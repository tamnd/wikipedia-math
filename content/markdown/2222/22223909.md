# Segre class

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Segre class"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Segre class** is a [characteristic class](wikipedia://en/363530) used in the study of [cones](wikipedia://en/50394182), a generalization of [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524). For vector bundles the total Segre class is inverse to the total [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349), and thus provides equivalent information; the advantage of the Segre class is that it generalizes to more general cones, while the Chern class does not. The Segre class was introduced in the non-singular case by [Beniamino Segre](wikipedia://en/3534031) (1953).[^o2] In the modern treatment of [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), as developed e.g. in the definitive book of Fulton (1998), Segre classes play a fundamental role.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose that $C$ is a [cone](wikipedia://en/50394182) over $X$, that $q$ is the projection from the [projective completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_completion_of_a_cone) $\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)$ of $C$ to $X$, and that $\mathcal{O}(1)$ is the [anti-tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tautological_line_bundle) on $\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)$. Viewing the [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))$ as a group endomorphism of the [Chow group](wikipedia://en/4282051) of $\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)$, the total Segre class of $C$ is given by:

$$s(C) = q_* \left( \sum_{i \geq 0} c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^{i} [\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)] \right).$$

The $i$th Segre class $s_i(C)$ is simply the $i$th graded piece of $s(C)$. If $C$ is of pure dimension $r$ over $X$ then this is given by:

$$s_i(C) = q_* \left( c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))^{r+i} [\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)] \right).$$

The reason for using $\mathbb{P}(C \oplus 1)$ rather than $\mathbb{P}(C)$ is that this makes the total Segre class stable under addition of the trivial bundle $\mathcal{O}$.

If *Z* is a closed subscheme of an algebraic scheme *X*, then $s(Z, X)$ denotes the Segre class of the [normal cone](wikipedia://en/4959227) to $Z \hookrightarrow X$.

### Relation to Chern classes for vector bundles {#Relation_to_Chern_classes_for_vector_bundles}

For a [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) $E$ over a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) $M$ a total Segre class $s(E)$ is the inverse to the total [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) $c(E)$, see e.g. Fulton (1998).[^o7]

Explicitly, for a total Chern class

$$c(E) = 1+c_1(E) + c_2(E) + \cdots \,$$

one gets the total Segre class

$$s(E) = 1 + s_1 (E) + s_2 (E) + \cdots \,$$

where

$$c_1(E) = -s_1(E), \quad c_2(E) = s_1(E)^2 - s_2(E), \quad \dots, \quad c_n(E) = -s_1(E)c_{n-1}(E) - s_2(E) c_{n-2}(E) - \cdots - s_n(E)$$

Let $x_1, \dots, x_k$ be Chern roots, i.e. formal eigenvalues of $\frac{ i \Omega }{ 2\pi}$ where $\Omega$ is a curvature of a [connection](wikipedia://en/308815) on $E$.

While the Chern class c(E) is written as

$$c(E) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} (1+x_i) = c_0 + c_1 + \cdots + c_k \,$$

where $c_i$ is an [elementary symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/1440695) of degree $i$ in variables $x_1, \dots, x_k$,

the Segre for the [dual bundle](wikipedia://en/7824691) $E^\vee$ which has Chern roots $-x_1, \dots, -x_k$ is written as

$$s(E^\vee) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac {1} { 1 - x_i } = s_0 + s_1 + \cdots$$

Expanding the above expression in powers of $x_1, \dots x_k$ one can see that $s_i (E^\vee)$ is represented by a [complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial](wikipedia://en/10176565) of $x_1, \dots x_k$.

## Properties {#Properties}

Here are some basic properties.

- For any cone *C* (e.g., a vector bundle), $s(C \oplus 1) = s(C)$.[^o10]
- For a cone *C* and a vector bundle *E*,

$c(E)s(C \oplus E) = s(C).$[^o11]

- If *E* is a vector bundle, then[^o12]

$s_i(E) = 0$ for $i < 0$.

$s_0(E)$ is the identity operator.

$s_i(E) \circ s_j(F) = s_j(F) \circ s_i(E)$ for another vector bundle *F*.

- If *L* is a line bundle, then $s_1(L) = -c_1(L)$, minus the first Chern class of *L*.[^o12]
- If *E* is a vector bundle of rank $e + 1$, then, for a line bundle *L*,

$s_p(E \otimes L) = \sum_{i=0}^p (-1)^{p-i} \binom{e+p}{e+i} s_i(E) c_1(L)^{p-i}.$[^o13]

A key property of a Segre class is birational invariance: this is contained in the following. Let $p: X \to Y$ be a [proper morphism](wikipedia://en/603782) between [algebraic schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_scheme) such that $Y$ is irreducible and each [irreducible component](wikipedia://en/2652476) of $X$ maps onto $Y$. Then, for each closed subscheme $W \subset Y$, $V = p^{-1}(W)$ and $p_V: V \to W$ the restriction of $p$,

${p_V}_*(s(V, X)) = \operatorname{deg}(p) \, s(W, Y).$[^o14]

Similarly, if $f: X \to Y$ is a [flat morphism](wikipedia://en/542520) of constant relative dimension between pure-dimensional algebraic schemes, then, for each closed subscheme $W \subset Y$, $V = f^{-1}(W)$ and $f_V: V \to W$ the restriction of $f$,

${f_V}^*(s(W, Y)) = s(V, X).$[^o15]

A basic example of birational invariance is provided by a blow-up. Let $\pi: \widetilde{X} \to X$ be a blow-up along some closed subscheme *Z*. Since the [exceptional divisor](wikipedia://en/14271694) $E := \pi^{-1}(Z) \hookrightarrow \widetilde{X}$ is an effective Cartier divisor and the normal cone (or normal bundle) to it is $\mathcal{O}_E(E) := \mathcal{O}_X(E)|_E$,

$$\begin{aligned} s(E, \widetilde{X}) &= c(\mathcal{O}_E(E))^{-1} [E] \\ &= [E] - E \cdot [E] + E \cdot (E \cdot [E]) + \cdots, \end{aligned}$$

where we used the notation $D \cdot \alpha = c_1(\mathcal{O}(D))\alpha$.[^o16] Thus,

$$s(Z, X) = g_* \left( \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{k-1} E^k \right)$$

where $g: E = \pi^{-1}(Z) \to Z$ is given by $\pi$.

## Examples {#Examples}

### Example 1 {#Example_1}

Let *Z* be a smooth curve that is a complete intersection of effective Cartier divisors $D_1, \dots, D_n$ on a variety *X*. Assume the dimension of *X* is *n* + 1. Then the Segre class of the [normal cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_cone) $C_{Z/X}$ to $Z \hookrightarrow X$ is:[^o20]

$$s(C_{Z/X}) = [Z] - \sum_{i=1}^n D_i \cdot [Z].$$

Indeed, for example, if *Z* is regularly embedded into *X*, then, since $C_{Z/X} = N_{Z/X}$ is the normal bundle and $N_{Z/X} = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n N_{D_i/X}|_Z$ (see [Normal cone#Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_cone#Properties)), we have:

$$s(C_{Z/X}) = c(N_{Z/X})^{-1}[Z] = \prod_{i=1}^d (1-c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D_i))) [Z] = [Z] - \sum_{i=1}^n D_i \cdot [Z].$$

### Example 2 {#Example_2}

The following is Example 3.2.22. of Fulton (1998).[^o3] It recovers some classical results from Schubert's book on [enumerative geometry](wikipedia://en/3069092).

Viewing the dual [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\breve{\mathbb{P}^3}$ as the [Grassmann bundle](wikipedia://en/50406093) $p: \breve{\mathbb{P}^3} \to *$ parametrizing the 2-planes in $\mathbb{P}^3$, consider the tautological [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

$$0 \to S \to p^* \mathbb{C}^3 \to Q \to 0$$

where $S, Q$ are the tautological sub and quotient bundles. With $E = \operatorname{Sym}^2(S^* \otimes Q^*)$, the [projective bundle](wikipedia://en/35183882) $q: X = \mathbb{P}(E) \to \breve{\mathbb{P}^3}$ is the variety of conics in $\mathbb{P}^3$. With $\beta = c_1(Q^*)$, we have $c(S^* \otimes Q^*) = 2 \beta + 2\beta^2$ and so, using [Chern class#Computation formulae](wikipedia://en/294349#Computation_formulae),

$$c(E) = 1 + 8 \beta + 30 \beta^2 + 60 \beta^3$$

and thus

$$s(E) = 1 + 8 h + 34 h^2 + 92 h^3$$

where $h = -\beta = c_1(Q).$ The coefficients in $s(E)$ have the enumerative geometric meanings; for example, 92 is the number of conics meeting 8 general lines.

### Example 3 {#Example_3}

Let *X* be a surface and $A, B, D$ effective Cartier divisors on it. Let $Z \subset X$ be the [scheme-theoretic intersection](wikipedia://en/52580781) of $A + D$ and $B + D$ (viewing those divisors as closed subschemes). For simplicity, suppose $A, B$ meet only at a single point *P* with the same multiplicity *m* and that *P* is a smooth point of *X*. Then[^o23]

$$s(Z, X) = [D] + (m^2[P] - D \cdot [D]).$$

To see this, consider the blow-up $\pi: \widetilde{X} \to X$ of *X* along *P* and let $g: \widetilde{Z} = \pi^{-1}Z \to Z$, the strict transform of *Z*. By the formula at #Properties,

$$s(Z, X) = g_* ([\widetilde{Z}]) - g_*(\widetilde{Z} \cdot [\widetilde{Z}]).$$

Since $\widetilde{Z} = \pi^* D + mE$ where $E = \pi^{-1} P$, the formula above results.

:::

## Multiplicity along a subvariety {#Multiplicity_along_a_subvariety}

Let $(A, \mathfrak{m})$ be the [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) of a variety *X* at a closed subvariety *V* [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) *n* (for example, *V* can be a [closed point](wikipedia://en/33086408)). Then $\operatorname{length}_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^t)$ is a polynomial of degree *n* in *t* for large *t*; i.e., it can be written as ${ e(A)^n \over n!} t^n +$ the lower-degree terms and the integer $e(A)$ is called the [multiplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicity_of_a_module) of *A*.

The Segre class $s(V, X)$ of $V \subset X$ encodes this multiplicity: the coefficient of $[V]$ in $s(V, X)$ is $e(A)$.[^o25]
