# Locally catenative sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally catenative sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **locally catenative sequence** is a sequence of [words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28formal_language_theory%29) in which each word can be constructed as the concatenation of previous words in the sequence.[^o2]

:::

Formally, an infinite sequence of words *w*(*n*) is locally catenative if, for some positive integers *k* and *i*<sub>1</sub>,...*i*<sub>*k*</sub>:

$$w(n)=w(n-i_1)w(n-i_2)\ldots w(n-i_k) \text{ for } n \ge \max\{i_1, \ldots, i_k\} \, .$$

Some authors use a slightly different definition in which encodings of previous words are allowed in the concatenation.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The sequence of [Fibonacci words](wikipedia://en/1702139) *S*(*n*) is locally catenative because

$$S(n)=S(n-1)S(n-2) \text{ for } n \ge 2 \, .$$

The sequence of [Thue–Morse words](wikipedia://en/491903) *T*(*n*) is not locally catenative by the first definition. However, it is locally catenative by the second definition because

$$T(n)=T(n-1)\mu(T(n-1)) \text{ for } n \ge 1 \, ,$$

where the encoding *μ* replaces 0 with 1 and 1 with 0.

:::
