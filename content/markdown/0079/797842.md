# Fortezza

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fortezza"}

*Image: Mykotronx Fortezza card.navy.jpg.* A Fortezza card made by Mykotronx Corp.

**Fortezza** is an [information security](wikipedia://en/15036) system[^o2] that uses the **Fortezza Crypto Card**, a [PC Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_Card)-based [security token](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_token).[^o3] It was developed for the U.S. government's [Clipper chip](wikipedia://en/409665) project and has been used by the U.S. Government in various applications.

:::

Each individual who is authorized to see protected information is issued a *Fortezza card* that stores [private keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key) and other data needed to gain access. It contains an NSA approved security microprocessor called *[Capstone](wikipedia://en/4819580)* ([MYK-80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MYK-80)) that implements the [Skipjack](wikipedia://en/474702) [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294) algorithm.

The original Fortezza card (KOV-8) is a [Type 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_2_encryption) product which means it cannot be used for [classified information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information). The most widely used [Type 1 encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_encryption) card is the KOV-12 Fortezza card which is used extensively for the [Defense Message System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Message_System) (DMS). The KOV-12 is cleared up to TOP SECRET/SCI. A later version, called [KOV-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOV-14) or **Fortezza Plus**, uses a [Krypton microprocessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krypton_microprocessor) that implements stronger, [Type 1 encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_1_encryption) and may be used for information [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classified_information_in_the_United_States) up to TOP SECRET/SCI. It, in turn, is being replaced by the newer [KSV-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSV-21) PC card with more modern algorithms and additional capabilities. The cards are interchangeable within the many types of equipment that support Fortezza and can be rekeyed and reprogrammed by the owners, making them easy to issue and reuse. This simplifies the process of rekeying equipment for crypto changes: instead of requiring an expensive [fill device](wikipedia://en/4319754), a technician is able to put a new Fortezza card in the device's PCMCIA slot.

The Fortezza Plus card and its successors are used with NSA's [Secure Terminal Equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Terminal_Equipment) voice and data encryption systems that are replacing the [STU-III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STU-III). It is manufactured by the [Mykotronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykotronx) Corporation and by [Spyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spyrus). Each card costs about \$240 and they are commonly used with card readers sold by [Litronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litronic) Corporation.

The Fortezza card has been used in government, military, and banking applications to protect sensitive data.[^o4]
