# Projective Hilbert space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective Hilbert space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and the foundations of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), the **projective Hilbert space** or **ray space** $\mathbf{P}(H)$ of a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H$ is the set of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) $[v]$ of non-zero vectors $v \in H$, for the [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) $\sim$ on $H$ given by

:::

$w \sim v$ if and only if $v = \lambda w$ for some non-zero complex number $\lambda$.

This is the usual construction of [projectivization](wikipedia://en/21861125), applied to a complex Hilbert space.[^n1] In quantum mechanics, the equivalence classes $[v]$ are also referred to as **rays** or **projective rays**. Each such projective ray is a copy of the nonzero complex numbers, which is topologically a two-dimensional plane after one point has been removed.

## Overview {#Overview}

The physical significance of the projective Hilbert space is that in [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25202), the [wave functions](wikipedia://en/145343) $\psi$ and $\lambda \psi$ represent the same *physical state*, for any $\lambda \ne 0$. The [Born rule](wikipedia://en/4313746) demands that if the system is physical and measurable, its wave function has unit [norm](wikipedia://en/21538), $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$, in which case it is called a [normalized wave function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalized_wave_function). The unit norm constraint does not completely determine $\psi$ within the ray, since $\psi$ could be multiplied by any $\lambda$ with [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) 1 (the [circle group](wikipedia://en/508177) $U(1)$ action) and retain its normalization. Such a $\lambda$ can be written as $\lambda = e^{i\phi}$ with $\phi$ called the global [phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_factor).

Rays that differ by such a $\lambda$ correspond to the same state (cf. [quantum state (algebraic definition)](wikipedia://en/30876419#Mathematical_generalizations), given a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of observables and a representation on $H$). No measurement can recover the phase of a ray; it is not observable. One says that $U(1)$ is a [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) of the first kind.

If $H$ is an [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of the algebra of observables then the rays induce [pure states](wikipedia://en/30876419#Formalism_in_quantum_physics). Convex linear combinations of rays naturally give rise to [density matrix](wikipedia://en/62844) which (still in case of an irreducible representation) correspond to mixed states.

In the case $H$ is finite-dimensional, i.e., $H=H_n$, the Hilbert space reduces to a finite-dimensional [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856) and the set of projective rays may be treated as a [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977); it is a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) for a [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) $\mathrm{U}(n)$. That is,

$$\mathbf{P}(H_{n})=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{n-1},$$

which carries a [Kähler metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric), called the [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486), derived from the Hilbert space's norm.[^n2][^n3]

As such, the projectivization of, e.g., two-dimensional complex Hilbert space (the space describing one [qubit](wikipedia://en/25284)) is the [complex projective line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_projective_line) $\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}$. This is known as the [Bloch sphere](wikipedia://en/729572) or, equivalently, the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799). See [Hopf fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) for details of the projectivization construction in this case.

## Product {#Product}

The [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of projective Hilbert spaces is not a projective space. The [Segre mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segre_mapping) is an embedding of the Cartesian product of two projective spaces into the projective space associated to the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of the two Hilbert spaces, given by $\mathbf{P}(H) \times \mathbf{P}(H') \to \mathbf{P}(H \otimes H'), ([x], [y]) \mapsto [x \otimes y]$. In quantum theory, it describes how to make states of the composite system from states of its constituents. It is only an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119), not a surjection; most of the tensor product space does not lie in its [image](wikipedia://en/275991) and represents *[entangled states](wikipedia://en/25336)*.
