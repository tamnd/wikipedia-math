# Triangle conic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Triangle conic"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **triangle conic** is a [conic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) in the plane of the reference [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and associated with it in some way. For example, the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) and the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle) of the reference triangle are triangle conics. Other examples are the [Steiner ellipse](wikipedia://en/19106257), which is an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) passing through the vertices and having its centre at the [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) of the reference triangle; the [Kiepert hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_hyperbola) which is a conic passing through the vertices, the centroid and the [orthocentre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthocentre) of the reference triangle; and the Artzt parabolas, which are [parabolas](wikipedia://en/23231) touching two [sidelines](wikipedia://en/43958459) of the reference triangle at vertices of the triangle.

:::

The terminology of *triangle conic* is widely used in the literature without a formal definition; that is, without precisely formulating the relations a conic should have with the reference triangle so as to qualify it to be called a triangle conic (see [^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5]). However, Greek mathematician Paris Pamfilos defines a triangle conic as a "conic circumscribing a triangle $\triangle ABC$ (that is, passing through its vertices) or inscribed in a triangle (that is, [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to its side-lines)".[^o6][^o7] The terminology *triangle circle* (respectively, *[ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052), [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231)*) is used to denote a circle (respectively, ellipse, hyperbola, parabola) associated with the reference triangle is some way.

Even though several triangle conics have been studied individually, there is no comprehensive encyclopedia or catalogue of triangle conics similar to [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia_of_Triangle_Centres) or Bernard Gibert's [Catalogue of Triangle Cubics](wikipedia://en/69374512).[^o8]

## Equations of triangle conics in trilinear coordinates {#Equations_of_triangle_conics_in_trilinear_coordinates}

The equation of a general triangle conic in [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) $x : y : z$ has the form

$$rx^2 + sy^2 + tz^2 + 2uyz + 2vzx + 2wxy = 0.$$

The equations of triangle [circumconics and inconics](wikipedia://en/9610491) have respectively the forms

$$\begin{aligned} & uyz + vzx + wxy = 0 \\[2pt] & l^2 x^2 + m^2 y^2 + n^2 z^2 - 2mnyz - 2nlzx - 2lmxy = 0 \end{aligned}$$

## Perspector and dual conics {#Perspector_and_dual_conics}

The **perspector** of a circumconic or inconic is the [perspector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspector) of the reference triangle and its [polar](wikipedia://en/19451201) triangle with respect to the conic.

- A circumconic is the locus of [trilinear poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilinear_pole) of lines through its perspector.[^o11] Conversely, the perspector of a circumconic lies on the trilinear polar of any point on the conic other than the triangle vertices.[^o12]
- The perspector of an inconic is its [Brianchon point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brianchon_point).[^o13]

A circumconic and an inconic are said to be *dual* if, using [barycentric coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barycentric_coordinates), coordinates of any point on the circumconic yield coefficients of an equation of a tangent to the inconic.

- Pairs of dual conics include the Steiner ellipse and inellipse, and the Kiepert hyperbola and parabola.
- Perspectors of dual conics are [isotomic conjugates](wikipedia://en/9492657).[^o14]
- The dual circumconic of an inconic is the isotomic conjugate of the trilinear polar of its perspector.[^o15]

Note: Paris Pamfilos describes a different notion of dual conics by the property of sharing the same perspector. This notion also includes the Steiner ellipse and inellipse.[^o16]

Not all conics associated with a triangle are circumconics or inconics; for instance, the Artzt parabolas each only touch two vertices.

## Special triangle conics {#Special_triangle_conics}

In the following, a few typical special triangle conics are discussed. In the descriptions, the standard notations are used: the reference triangle is always denoted by $\triangle ABC$. The angles at the vertices $A, B, C$ are denoted by $A, B, C$ and the lengths of the sides opposite to the vertices $A, B, C$ are respectively $a, b, c$. The equations of the conics are given in the trilinear coordinates $x : y : z$. The conics are selected as illustrative of the several different ways in which a conic could be associated with a triangle.

## Families of triangle conics {#Families_of_triangle_conics}

### Hofstadter ellipses {#Hofstadter_ellipses}

*Image: Hofstadter.gif.* Family of Hofstadter conics of $\triangle ABC$

An [Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) ellipse[^o42] is a member of a one-parameter family of ellipses in the plane of $\triangle ABC$ defined by the following equation in trilinear coordinates:

$$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + yz\left[D(t) + \frac{1}{D(t)}\right] + zx\left[E(t) + \frac{1}{E(t)}\right] + xy\left[F(t) + \frac{1}{F(t)}\right] = 0$$

where $t$ is a parameter and

$$\begin{aligned} D(t) &= \cos A - \sin A \cot tA \\ E(t) &= \cos B - \sin B \cot tB \\ F(t) &= \sin C - \cos C \cot tC \end{aligned}$$

The ellipses corresponding to $t$ and $1 - t$ are identical. When $t = 1/2$ we have the inellipse

$$x^2+y^2+z^2 - 2yz- 2zx - 2xy =0$$

and when $t \to 0$ we have the circumellipse

$$\frac{a}{Ax}+\frac{b}{By}+\frac{c}{Cz}=0.$$

### Conics of Thomson and Darboux {#Conics_of_Thomson_and_Darboux}

The family of Thomson conics consists of those conics inscribed in the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$ having the property that the normals at the points of contact with the sidelines are concurrent. The family of Darboux conics contains as members those circumscribed conics of the reference $\triangle ABC$ such that the normals at the vertices of $\triangle ABC$ are concurrent. In both cases the points of concurrency lie on the Darboux cubic.[^o44][^o45]

*Image: EllipseOfParallelIntercepts.png.* Conic associated with parallel intercepts

### Conics associated with parallel intercepts {#Conics_associated_with_parallel_intercepts}

Given an arbitrary point in the plane of the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$, if lines are drawn through $P$ parallel to the sidelines $BC, CA, AB$ intersecting the other sides at $X_{b}, X_{c}, Y_{c}, Y_{a}, Z_{a}, Z_{b}$ then these six points of intersection lie on a conic. If P is chosen as the symmedian point, the resulting conic is a circle called the first Lemoine circle. If the trilinear coordinates of $P$ are $u : v : w$ the equation of the six-point conic is[^o14]

$$-(au + bv + cw)^2(uyz + vzx + wxy) + (ax + by + cz)(vw(bv + cw)x + wu(cw + au)y + uv(au + bv)z) = 0$$

::: {#o48 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Yff conics"}

### Yff conics {#Yff_conics}

*Image: YffConics.gif.* Yff Conics

The members of the one-parameter family of conics defined by the equation

$$x^2+y^2+z^2-2\lambda(yz+zx+xy)=0,$$

where $\lambda$ is a parameter, are the Yff conics associated with the reference triangle $\triangle ABC$.[^o49] A member of the family is associated with every point $P(u : v : w)$ in the plane by setting

$$\lambda=\frac{u^2+v^2+w^2}{2(vw+wu+uv)}.$$

The Yff conic is a parabola if

$$\lambda=\frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{a^2+b^2+c^2-2(bc+ca+ab)}=\lambda_0$$

(say). It is an ellipse if $\lambda < \lambda_0$ and $\lambda_0 > \frac{1}{2}$ and it is a hyperbola if $\lambda_0 < \lambda < -1$. For $-1 < \lambda <\frac{1}{2}$, the conics are imaginary.

:::
