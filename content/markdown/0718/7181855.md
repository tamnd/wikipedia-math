# Borel conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Borel conjecture"}

In [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **Borel conjecture** (named for [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672)) asserts that an [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287) [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) is determined by its [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), up to [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660). It is a [rigidity](wikipedia://en/1458875) conjecture, asserting that a weak, algebraic notion of equivalence (namely, [homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/212250)) should imply a stronger, topological notion (namely, homeomorphism).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Precise formulation of the conjecture"}

## Precise formulation of the conjecture {#Precise_formulation_of_the_conjecture}

Let $M$ and $N$ be [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) and [aspherical](wikipedia://en/7147287) topological [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), and let

$$f \colon M \to N$$

be a [homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/212250). The **Borel conjecture** states that the map $f$ is homotopic to a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660). Since aspherical manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups are homotopy equivalent, the Borel conjecture implies that aspherical closed manifolds are determined, up to homeomorphism, by their fundamental groups.

This conjecture is false if [topological manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119193) and homeomorphisms are replaced by [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) and [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564); counterexamples can be constructed by taking a [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) with an [exotic sphere](wikipedia://en/1114931).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="The origin of the conjecture"}

## The origin of the conjecture {#The_origin_of_the_conjecture}

In a May 1953 letter to [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483),[^o6] [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) raised the question whether two aspherical manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups are homeomorphic. A positive answer to the question "**Is every homotopy equivalence between closed aspherical manifolds homotopic to a homeomorphism?**" is referred to as the "so-called Borel Conjecture" in a 1986 paper of [Jonathan Rosenberg](wikipedia://en/41108807).[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Motivation for the conjecture"}

## Motivation for the conjecture {#Motivation_for_the_conjecture}

A basic question is the following: if two closed manifolds are homotopy equivalent, are they homeomorphic? This is not true in general: there are homotopy equivalent [lens spaces](wikipedia://en/885725) which are not homeomorphic.

Nevertheless, there are classes of manifolds for which homotopy equivalences between them can be homotoped to homeomorphisms. For instance, the [Mostow rigidity theorem](wikipedia://en/2782164) states that a homotopy equivalence between closed [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) is homotopic to an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777)—in particular, to a homeomorphism. The Borel conjecture is a topological reformulation of Mostow rigidity, weakening the hypothesis from hyperbolic manifolds to aspherical manifolds, and similarly weakening the conclusion from an isometry to a homeomorphism.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Relationship to other conjectures"}

## Relationship to other conjectures {#Relationship_to_other_conjectures}

- The Borel conjecture implies the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194) for the special case in which the reference map $f \colon M \to BG$ is a homotopy equivalence.
- The [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798) asserts that a closed manifold homotopy equivalent to $S^3$, the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792), is homeomorphic to $S^3$. This is not a special case of the Borel conjecture, because $S^3$ is not aspherical. Nevertheless, the Borel conjecture for the [3-torus](wikipedia://en/74800) $T^3 = S^1 \times S^1 \times S^1$ implies the Poincaré conjecture for $S^3$.[^o12]

:::
