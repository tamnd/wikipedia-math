# Facet (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Facet"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **facet** is a feature of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), or related geometric structure, generally of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) one less than the structure itself. More specifically:

:::

- In three-dimensional geometry, some authors call a **[facet of a polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_of_a_polyhedron)** any [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) whose corners are vertices of the polyhedron, including polygons that are not *[faces](wikipedia://en/149926)*.[^o2][^o3] To *[facet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facetting)* a polyhedron is to find and join such facets to form the faces of a new polyhedron; this is the reciprocal process to *[stellation](wikipedia://en/180253)* and may also be applied to higher-dimensional [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471).[^o4]
- In [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519) and in the general theory of [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471), a [face](wikipedia://en/149926) that has dimension *n* − 1 (an (*n* − 1)-face or hyperface) is called a [**facet**](wikipedia://en/149926#Facet). In this terminology, every facet is a face.[^o5] A facet of a facet, that is a (*n* − 2)-face, may be called a **[ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29)**.
- A **[facet of a simplicial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_of_a_simplicial_complex)** is a maximal simplex, that is a simplex that is not a face of another simplex of the complex.[^o6] For (boundary complexes of) [simplicial polytopes](wikipedia://en/8853373) this coincides with the meaning from polyhedral combinatorics.

For example:

- The facets of a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) are its 0-faces or [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417).
- The facets of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) are its 1-faces or [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034).
- The facets of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or plane [tiling](wikipedia://en/12126635) are its [2-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-face).
- The facets of a [4D polytope](wikipedia://en/24979) or [3-honeycomb](wikipedia://en/2961) are its [3-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-face) or cells.
- The facets of a [5D polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670) or 4-honeycomb are its [4-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-face).
