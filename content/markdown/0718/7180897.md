# Per Enflo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Per Enflo"}

**Per H. Enflo** (; born 20 May 1944) is a Swedish mathematician working primarily in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a field in which he solved [problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_problems) that had been considered fundamental. Three of these problems had been [open](wikipedia://en/1333335) for more than forty years:[^o3]

:::

- The [basis problem](wikipedia://en/3460662#Basis_problem) and the [approximation problem](wikipedia://en/628962)[^o4] and later
- the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/691927) for [Banach spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_spaces).[^o5]

In solving these problems, Enflo developed new techniques which were then used by other researchers in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) for years. Some of Enflo's research has been important also in other mathematical fields, such as [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), especially [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805) and [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105).

Enflo works at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University), where he holds the title of University Professor. Enflo has earlier held positions at the [Miller Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Institute) for Basic Research in Science at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique), ([Paris](wikipedia://en/22989)) and The [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology), [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm).

Enflo is also a [concert pianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_pianist).

## Enflo's contributions to functional analysis and operator theory {#Enflo's_contributions_to_functional_analysis_and_operator_theory}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/48396), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) is concerned with the study of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) and [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) acting upon them. It has its historical roots in the study of [functional spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_space), in particular transformations of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), such as the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247), as well as in the study of [differential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) and [integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_equations) equations. In functional analysis, an important class of vector spaces consists of the [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) over the [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) numbers, which are called [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). An important example of a Banach space is a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932), where the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) arises from an [inner product](wikipedia://en/14856). Hilbert spaces are of fundamental importance in many areas, including the mathematical formulation of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes), and [time-series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-series_analysis). Besides studying spaces of functions, functional analysis also studies the [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) on spaces of functions.

### Hilbert's fifth problem and embeddings {#Hilbert's_fifth_problem_and_embeddings}

At Stockholm University, Hans Rådström suggested that Enflo consider [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760) in the spirit of functional analysis.[^o8] In two years, 1969–1970, Enflo published five papers on Hilbert's fifth problem; these papers are collected in Enflo (1970), along with a short summary. Some of the results of these papers are described in Enflo (1976) and in the last chapter of Benyamini and [Lindenstrauss](wikipedia://en/20653945).

#### Applications in computer science {#Applications_in_computer_science}

Enflo's techniques have found application in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). [Algorithm theorists](wikipedia://en/775) derive [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105) that embed finite metric spaces into low-dimensional [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697) with low "distortion" (in [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29)'s terminology for the [Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/51731) [category](wikipedia://en/5869); cf. [Banach–Mazur distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Mazur_distance)). Low-dimensional problems have lower [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/2230), of course. More importantly, if the problems embed well in either the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) or the three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), then [geometric algorithms](wikipedia://en/176927) become exceptionally fast.

However, such [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) techniques have limitations, as shown by Enflo's (1969) theorem:[^o10]

For every $m\geq 2$, the [Hamming cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamming_cube) $C_m$ cannot be embedded with "distortion $D$" (or less) into $2^m$-dimensional Euclidean space if $D < \sqrt{ m }$. Consequently, the optimal embedding is the natural embedding, which realizes $\{0,1\}^m$ as a subspace of $m$-dimensional Euclidean space.[^o11]

This theorem, "found by Enflo \[1969\], is probably the first result showing an unbounded distortion for [embeddings](wikipedia://en/52119) into [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697). Enflo considered the problem of [uniform](wikipedia://en/32337) embeddability among [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), and the distortion was an auxiliary device in his proof."[^o12]

### Geometry of Banach spaces {#Geometry_of_Banach_spaces}

A **uniformly convex space** is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) so that, for every $\epsilon>0$ there is some $\delta>0$ so that for any two vectors with $\|x\|\le1$ and $\|y\|\le 1,$

$$\|x+y\|>2-\delta$$

implies that

$$\|x-y\|<\epsilon.$$

Intuitively, the center of a line segment inside the [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) must lie deep inside the unit ball unless the segment is short.

In 1972 Enflo proved that "every [super-reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091#Super-reflexive_space) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) admits an equivalent [uniformly convex](wikipedia://en/9133418) norm".[^o14][^o15]

### The basis problem and Mazur's goose {#The_basis_problem_and_Mazur's_goose}

With one paper, which was published in 1973, Per Enflo solved three problems that had stumped functional analysts for decades: The [basis problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_problem) of [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646), the "[Goose problem](wikipedia://en/4233115)" of [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162), and the [approximation problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_problem) of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). Grothendieck had shown that his approximation problem was the central problem in the [theory](wikipedia://en/5869) of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) and [continuous linear operators](wikipedia://en/1802169).

#### Basis problem of Banach {#Basis_problem_of_Banach}

The basis problem was posed by Stefan Banach in his book, *Theory of Linear Operators*. Banach asked whether every separable [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) has a [Schauder basis](wikipedia://en/3460662).

A **Schauder basis** or **countable basis** is similar to the usual (Hamel) [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370); the difference is that for Hamel bases we use [linear combinations](wikipedia://en/55632) that are *finite* sums, while for Schauder bases they may be *infinite* sums. This makes Schauder bases more suitable for the analysis of infinite-dimensional [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752) including [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).

Schauder bases were described by [Juliusz Schauder](wikipedia://en/685314) in 1927.[^o18][^o19] Let *V* denote a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) over the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F*. A *Schauder basis* is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) (*b*<sub>*n*</sub>) of elements of *V* such that for every element *v* ∈ *V* there exists a *unique* sequence (α<sub>*n*</sub>) of elements in *F* so that

$$v = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{N}} \alpha_n b_n \,$$

where the [convergence](wikipedia://en/15287#Series_in_Banach_and_seminormed_spaces) is understood with respect to the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). Schauder bases can also be defined analogously in a general [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752).

*Image: KsiegaSzkocka1.JPG.* In 1937, Polish mathematician [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) promised a "live goose" as the prize for solving [problem 153](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_problem) in the [Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115). In 1972, Mazur presented the goose to Per Enflo.

#### Problem 153 in the Scottish Book: Mazur's goose {#Problem_153_in_the_Scottish_Book:_Mazur's_goose}

*Image: MazurGes.jpg.* In 1972 [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) awarded Enflo the promised live goose for solving a problem in the [Scottish book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_cafe).

Banach and other Polish mathematicians would work on mathematical problems at the [Scottish Café](wikipedia://en/362651). When a problem was especially interesting and when its solution seemed difficult, the problem would be written down in the book of problems, which soon became known as the *[Scottish Book](wikipedia://en/4233115)*. For problems that seemed especially important or difficult or both, the problem's proposer would often pledge to award a prize for its solution.

On 6 November 1936, [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162) posed a problem on representing continuous functions. Formally writing down *problem 153* in the *Scottish Book*, Mazur promised as the reward a "live [goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose)", an especially rich prize during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) and on the eve of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).

Fairly soon afterwards, it was realized that Mazur's problem was closely related to Banach's problem on the existence of Schauder bases in separable Banach spaces. Most of the other problems in the *Scottish Book* were solved regularly. However, there was little progress on Mazur's problem and a few other problems, which became famous [open problems](wikipedia://en/1333335) to mathematicians around the world.[^o21]

#### Grothendieck's formulation of the approximation problem {#Grothendieck's_formulation_of_the_approximation_problem}

Grothendieck's work on the [theory](wikipedia://en/5869) of Banach spaces and [continuous linear operators](wikipedia://en/1802169) introduced the [approximation property](wikipedia://en/628962). A [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) is said to have the [approximation property](wikipedia://en/628962), if every [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) is a limit of [finite-rank operators](wikipedia://en/8405295). The converse is always true.[^o23]

In a long monograph, Grothendieck proved that if every Banach space had the approximation property, then every Banach space would have a Schauder basis. Grothendieck thus focused the attention of functional analysts on deciding whether every Banach space have the approximation property.[^o23]

#### Enflo's solution {#Enflo's_solution}

In 1972, Per Enflo constructed a separable Banach space that lacks the approximation property and a Schauder basis.[^o25] In 1972, Mazur awarded a [live goose](http://www.apprendre-math.info/history/photos/Mazur_2.jpeg) to Enflo in a ceremony at the [Stefan Banach Center](wikipedia://en/29646) in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw,_Poland); the "goose reward" ceremony was broadcast throughout [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland).[^o26]

### Invariant subspace problem and polynomials {#Invariant_subspace_problem_and_polynomials}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), one of the most prominent problems was the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/691927), which required the evaluation of the truth of the following proposition:

Given a complex [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *H* of [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) > 1 and a [bounded linear operator](wikipedia://en/455961) *T* : *H* → *H*, then *H* has a non-trivial [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [*T*-invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826), i.e. there exists a closed [linear subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) *W* of *H* which is different from {0} and *H* such that *T*(*W*) ⊆ *W*.

For [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), the first example of an operator without an invariant subspace was constructed by Enflo. (For [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932), the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/691927) remains [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsolved_problems_in_mathematics).)

Enflo proposed a solution to the invariant subspace problem in 1975, publishing an outline in 1976. Enflo submitted the full article in 1981 and the article's complexity and length delayed its publication to 1987[^o28] Enflo's long "manuscript had a world-wide circulation among mathematicians"[^o29] and some of its ideas were described in publications besides Enflo (1976).[^o30][^o31] Enflo's works inspired a similar construction of an operator without an invariant subspace for example by Beauzamy, who acknowledged Enflo's ideas.[^o28]

In the 1990s, Enflo developed a "constructive" approach to the [invariant subspace problem](wikipedia://en/691927) on Hilbert spaces.[^o32]

#### Multiplicative inequalities for homogeneous polynomials {#Multiplicative_inequalities_for_homogeneous_polynomials}

An essential idea in Enflo's construction was "**concentration of polynomials at low degrees**": For all positive integers $m$ and $n$, there exists $C(m,n) > 0$ such that for all [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977) $P$ and $Q$ of degrees $m$ and $n$ (in $k$ variables), then

> $$|PQ|\geq C(m,n)|P|\,|Q|,$$

where $|P|$ denotes the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of $P$. Enflo proved that $C(m,n)$ does not depend on the number of variables $k$. Enflo's original proof was simplified by [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Montgomery_%28mathematician%29).[^o34]

This result was generalized to other [norms](wikipedia://en/21538) on the vector space of [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977). Of these norms, the most used has been the [Bombieri norm](wikipedia://en/12055125).

##### Bombieri norm {#Bombieri_norm}

The [Bombieri norm](wikipedia://en/12055125) is defined in terms of the following [scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product): For all $\alpha,\beta \in \mathbb{N}^N$ we have

$\langle X^\alpha | X^\beta \rangle = 0$ if $\alpha \neq \beta$

For every $\alpha \in \mathbb{N}^N$ we define $\Vert X^\alpha\Vert^2 = \frac{|\alpha|!}{\alpha!},$

where we use the following notation: if $\alpha = (\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_N) \in \mathbb{N}^N$, we write $|\alpha| = \Sigma_{i=1}^N \alpha_i$ and $\alpha! = \Pi_{i=1}^N (\alpha_i!)$ and $X^\alpha = \Pi_{i=1}^N X_i^{\alpha_i}.$

The most remarkable property of this norm is the Bombieri inequality:

Let $P,Q$ be two [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977) respectively of degree $d^\circ(P)$ and $d^\circ(Q)$ with $N$ variables, then, the following inequality holds:

$$\frac{d^\circ(P)!d^\circ(Q)!}{(d^\circ(P)+d^\circ(Q))!}\Vert P\Vert^2 \, \Vert Q\Vert^2 \leq \Vert P\cdot Q\Vert^2 \leq \Vert P\Vert^2 \, \Vert Q\Vert^2.$$

In the above statement, the Bombieri inequality is the left-hand side inequality; the right-hand side inequality means that the [Bombieri norm](wikipedia://en/12055125) is a [norm](wikipedia://en/1991870) of the [algebra](wikipedia://en/1991870) of polynomials under multiplication.

The Bombieri inequality implies that the product of two polynomials cannot be arbitrarily small, and this lower-bound is fundamental in applications like [polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization) (or in Enflo's construction of an operator without an invariant subspace).

Enflo's idea of "concentration of polynomials at low degrees" has led to important publications in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527)[^o38] [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1997) [and Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205),[^o39] and [polynomial factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_factorization).[^o40]

## Mathematical biology: Population dynamics {#Mathematical_biology:_Population_dynamics}

In [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), Per Enflo has published several papers in [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), specifically in [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466).

### Human evolution {#Human_evolution}

Enflo has also published in [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268) and [paleoanthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoanthropology).[^o43]

Today, all humans belong to one population of *[Homo sapiens sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens_sapiens)*, which is individed by species barrier. However, according to the "Out of Africa" model this is not the first species of hominids: the first species of genus *Homo*, *[Homo habilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_habilis)*, evolved in [East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa) at least 2 Ma, and members of this species populated different parts of Africa in a relatively short time. *[Homo erectus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus)* evolved more than 1.8 Ma, and by 1.5 Ma had spread throughout the [Old World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_World).

[Anthropologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) have been divided as to whether the current human population evolved as one interconnected population (as postulated by the [Multiregional Evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiregional_Evolution) hypothesis), or evolved only in East Africa, where they [speciated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speciation), then migrated out of Africa, replacing human populations in [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) (referred to as the "Out of Africa" Model or the "Complete Replacement" Model).

[Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) and modern humans coexisted in Europe for several thousand years, but the duration of this period is uncertain.[^o44] Modern humans may have first migrated to Europe 40–43,000 years ago.[^o45] Neanderthals may have lived as recently as 24,000 years ago in [refugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugium_%28population_biology%29) on the south coast of the Iberian peninsula such as [Gorham's Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorham's_Cave).[^o46][^o47] Inter-stratification of Neanderthal and modern human remains has been suggested,[^o48] but is disputed.[^o49]

With [Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Hawks) and [Wolpoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milford_H._Wolpoff), Enflo published an explanation of fossil evidence on the [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) of [Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) and [modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_human). This article tries to resolve a debate in the [evolution of modern humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_evolution#Models_of_human_evolution) between theories suggesting either [multiregional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiregional_origin_of_modern_humans) and [single African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recent_African_origin_of_modern_humans) origins. In particular, the [extinction of Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal_extinction_hypotheses) could have happened due to waves of modern humans entered Europe – in technical terms, due to "the continuous influx of modern human DNA into the Neandertal gene pool."[^o50][^o51][^o52]

Enflo has also written about the population dynamics of [zebra mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zebra_mussels) in [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie).[^o53]

## Piano {#Piano}

Per Enflo is also a [concert pianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_pianist).

A [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy) in both music and mathematics, Enflo won the Swedish competition for young pianists at age 11 in 1956, and he won the same competition in 1961.[^o55] At age 12, Enflo appeared as a soloist with the Royal Opera Orchestra of Sweden. He debuted in the [Stockholm Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Concert_Hall) in 1963. Enflo's teachers included [Bruno Seidlhofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Seidlhofer), [Géza Anda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Géza_Anda), and Gottfried Boon (who himself was a student of [Artur Schnabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Schnabel)).[^o56]

In 1999 Enflo competed in the first annual [Van Cliburn Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cliburn_Foundation)'s [International Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Cliburn_International_Piano_Competition) for [Outstanding Amateurs](http://www.cliburn.org/index.php?page=amateur_competition) (2009-04-19). <https://web.archive.org/web/20090419114604/http://www.cliburn.org/index.php?page=amateur_competition>.[^o57]

Enflo performs regularly around [Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent,_Ohio) and in a [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozart) series in [Columbus, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio) (with the Triune Festival Orchestra). His solo piano recitals have appeared on the Classics Network of the radio station [WOSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOSU-FM), which is sponsored by [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University).[^o56]
