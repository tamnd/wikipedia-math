::: {#o9 .subject cue=lead name="5-demicube"}

In [five-dimensional](wikipedia://en/2851410) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **demipenteract** or **5-demicube** is a semiregular [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670), constructed from a *5-hypercube* ([penteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penteract)) with [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) vertices removed.

:::

It was discovered by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset). Since it was the only [semiregular](wikipedia://en/11684875) 5-polytope (made of more than one type of regular [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647)), he called it a [5-ic semi-regular](wikipedia://en/11684875). [E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, labeling it as HM<sub>5</sub> for a 5-dimensional *half measure* polytope.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) named this polytope as **1<sub>21</sub>** from its [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram), which has branches of length 2, 1 and 1 with a ringed node on one of the short branches, and [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\left\{3 \begin{array}{l}3, 3\\3\end{array}\right\}$ or {3,3<sup>2,1</sup>}.

It exists in the [*k*<sub>21</sub> polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope) family as 1<sub>21</sub> with the Gosset polytopes: [2<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_2_21_polytope), [3<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_3_21_polytope), and [4<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_4_21_polytope).

The graph formed by the vertices and edges of the demipenteract is sometimes called the [Clebsch graph](wikipedia://en/23970662), though that name sometimes refers to the [folded cube graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folded_cube_graph) of order five instead.

## Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of a demipenteract centered at the origin and edge length 2$\sqrt{2}$ are alternate halves of the [penteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penteract):

(±1,±1,±1,±1,±1)

with an odd number of plus signs.

## As a configuration {#As_a_configuration}

This [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/18801624#As_configurations) represents the 5-demicube. The rows and columns correspond to vertices, edges, faces, cells and 4-faces. The diagonal numbers say how many of each element occur in the whole 5-demicube. The nondiagonal numbers say how many of the column's element occur in or at the row's element.[^o12][^n1]

The diagonal f-vector numbers are derived through the [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777), dividing the full group order of a subgroup order by removing one mirror at a time.

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

It is a part of a dimensional family of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) called [demihypercubes](wikipedia://en/6833695) for being [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) of the [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) family.

There are 23 [uniform 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338) that can be constructed from the D<sub>5</sub> symmetry of the demipenteract, 8 of which are unique to this family, and 15 are shared within the [penteractic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penteract) family.

The 5-demicube is third in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope). Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the previous polytope. [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) identified this series in 1900 as containing all [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) facets, containing all [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex) ([5-simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex) and [5-orthoplexes](wikipedia://en/7594615) in the case of the 5-demicube). In [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter)'s notation the 5-demicube is given the symbol 1<sub>21</sub>.
