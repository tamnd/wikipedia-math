# Unity amplitude

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unity amplitude"}

A sinusoidal waveform is said to have a **unity amplitude** when the amplitude of the wave is equal to 1.

:::

$$x(t) = a \sin(\theta(t))$$

where $a = 1$. This terminology is most commonly used in [digital signal processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processing) and is usually associated with the [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) and [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) sinusoids that involve a [duty cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty_cycle), $\alpha$, and a defined [fundamental period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_period), $T_o$.

Analytic signals with unit amplitude satisfy the [Bedrosian theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrosian_theorem).[^o2]
