# Grace Wahba

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Grace Wahba"}

**Grace Goldsmith Wahba** (born August 3, 1934) is an American statistician and retired I. J. Schoenberg-Hilldale Professor of [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison).[^o3] She is a pioneer in methods for [smoothing](wikipedia://en/4155662) [noisy data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noisy_data). Best known for the development of generalized [cross-validation](wikipedia://en/416612)[^o4] and "[Wahba's problem](wikipedia://en/31998221)",[^o3] she has developed methods with applications in [demographic studies](wikipedia://en/45642), [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [DNA microarrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_microarrays), [risk modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_modeling), [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging), and [climate prediction](wikipedia://en/1505381).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Wahba had an interest in science from an early age, when she was in junior high she was given a chemistry set.[^o7] At this time she was also interested in becoming an engineer.[^o7]

Wahba studied at Cornell University for her undergraduate degree; in 1952, Cornell and [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) were the only Ivy League universities that admitted women.[^o7][^o10] When she was there women were severely restricted in their privileges, for example she was required to live in a dorm and had a curfew.[^o7] She received her bachelor's degree from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1956 and a master's degree from the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park) in 1962.[^o3] She worked in industry for several years before receiving her doctorate from Stanford University in 1966 and settling in [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin) in 1967.

She is the author of *Spline Models for Observational Data*.[^o6] She retired in August 2018 from the University of Wisconsin-Madison.[^o7] Her life and career are discussed in interviews in 2007,[^o8] 2018,[^o9] and 2020.[^o10]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

*Image: Grace Wahba 2010.jpg.* Wahba in 2010

Wahba was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1997[^o12] and to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2000.[^o13] She is also a fellow of several academic societies including the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), and the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o14]

Over the years she has received a selection of notable awards in the statistics community:

- [International Prize in Statistics](wikipedia://en/52048719), 2025[^o15]
- [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship), [COPSS](wikipedia://en/44155344), August 2014[^o16]
- [Gottfried E. Noether](wikipedia://en/11071873) Senior Researcher Award, Joint Statistics Meetings, August 2009
- Committee of Presidents of Statistical Societies Elizabeth Scott Award, 1996[^o16]
- First [Emanuel and Carol Parzen Prize for Statistical Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parzen_Prize), 1994

She received honorary [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degrees from the University of Chicago in 2007 and The Ohio State University in 2022.

The Institute of Mathematical Statistics announced the IMS Grace Wahba Award and Lecture in 2021.[^o17]
