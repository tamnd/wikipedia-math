# Atiyah conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atiyah conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Atiyah conjecture** is a collective term for a number of statements about restrictions on possible values of [$l^2$-Betti numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L2_cohomology).

:::

## History {#History}

In 1976, [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) introduced [$l^2$-cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L2_cohomology) of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) with a free co-compact [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of a discrete [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) [group](wikipedia://en/19447) (e.g. the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) manifold together with the action of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) by [deck transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deck_transformations).) Atiyah defined also $l^2$-Betti numbers as [von Neumann dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_dimension) of the resulting $l^2$-cohomology groups, and computed several examples, which all turned out to be [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). He therefore asked if it is possible for $l^2$-Betti numbers to be [irrational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_numbers).

Since then, various researchers asked more refined questions about possible values of $l^2$-Betti numbers, all of which are customarily referred to as "Atiyah conjecture".

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

Many positive results were proven by [Peter Linnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Linnell). For example, if the group acting is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735), then the $l^2$-Betti numbers are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

The most general question open as of late 2011 is whether $l^2$-Betti numbers are rational if there is a bound on the orders of [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the group which acts. In fact, a precise relationship between possible denominators and the orders in question is [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138); in the case of [torsion-free groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group), this statement generalizes the [zero-divisors conjecture](wikipedia://en/349223#Group_rings_over_an_infinite_group). For a discussion see the article of B. Eckmann.

In the case there is no such bound, [Tim Austin](wikipedia://en/81078882) showed in 2009 that $l^2$-Betti numbers can assume [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325) values. Later it was shown that in that case they can be any non-negative [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).
