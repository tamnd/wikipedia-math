# Additive K-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Additive K-theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **additive K-theory** means some version of [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) in which, according to [Spencer Bloch](wikipedia://en/25794633), the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) *GL* has everywhere been replaced by its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) *gl*.[^o2] It is not, therefore, one theory but a way of creating additive or infinitesimal analogues of multiplicative theories.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Following [Boris Feigin](wikipedia://en/53823921) and [Boris Tsygan](wikipedia://en/81474217),[^o5] let $A$ be an algebra over a field $k$ of [characteristic zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_zero) and let ${\mathfrak gl}(A)$ be the algebra of infinite matrices over $A$ with only finitely many nonzero entries. Then the [Lie algebra homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebra_homology)

$$H_\cdot ({\mathfrak gl}(A),k)$$

has a natural structure of a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714). The space of its [primitive elements](wikipedia://en/43337163) of degree $i$ is denoted by $K^+_i(A)$ and called the $i$-th **additive K-functor** of *A*.

The additive K-functors are related to [cyclic homology](wikipedia://en/1460172) groups by the isomorphism

$$HC_i(A) \cong K^+_{i+1}(A).$$

:::
