# David Benney

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="David Benney"}

**David John Benney** (8 April 1930 – 9 October 2015) was a New Zealand applied mathematician, known for work on the [nonlinear partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/16678376) of [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034).[^o4][^n1][^o6]

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Born in [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington), New Zealand, on 8 April 1930 to [Cecil Henry (Matt) Benney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Benney) and Phyllis Marjorie Jenkins,[^n2] Benney was educated at [Wellington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_College_%28New_Zealand%29).[^o9] He graduated BSc from [Victoria University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington) in 1950, and MSc from the same institution in 1951. He then went to [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge), from where he graduated BA in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) in 1954. He was at [Canterbury University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_University_College) for two years as a lecturer,[^o4] before taking leave of absence in August 1957 to undertake doctoral studies at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT), graduating PhD in 1959.[^o9]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Benney joined the mathematics faculty at MIT in 1960. He spent the rest of his career there, as a prolific researcher in fluid dynamics and supervisor of students, becoming [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor).[^o4] He received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in 1964.[^o11]
