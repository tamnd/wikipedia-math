# Products in algebraic topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Products in algebraic topology"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), several types of products are defined on homological and cohomological theories.

:::

## The cross product {#The_cross_product}

$$H_p(X) \otimes H_q(Y) \to H_{p+q}(X\times Y)$$

When *X* and *Y* are [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022), then the product $X \times Y$ has a natural CW structure, and the cross product can be understood as induced by the chain map sending a *p*-cell $e_p$ in *X* and a *q*-cell $e_q$ in *Y* to the product cell $e_p \times e_q$ in $X \times Y$. An equivalent but slightly more complicated definition can be given for singular homology. The cross product is used to prove the [Künneth theorem](wikipedia://en/918047) relating the homology of *X* and *Y* to the homology of $X \times Y$.

## The cap product {#The_cap_product}

$$\frown\ : H_p(X;R)\times H^q(X;R) \rightarrow H_{p-q}(X;R)$$

## The slant product {#The_slant_product}

$$/ : H_p(X;R)\times H^q(X\times Y;R) \rightarrow H^{q-p}(Y;R)$$

## The cup product {#The_cup_product}

$$H^p(X) \otimes H^q(X) \to H^{p+q}(X)$$

This product can be understood as induced by the exterior product of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) in [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530).[^o6] It makes the singular cohomology of a connected manifold into a unitary supercommutative ring.
