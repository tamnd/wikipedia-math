# Necklace ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Necklace ring"}

In mathematics, the **necklace ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) introduced by (txt) to elucidate the multiplicative properties of [necklace polynomials](wikipedia://en/430792).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *A* is a commutative ring then the necklace ring over *A* consists of all infinite sequences $(a_1, a_2, ...)$ of elements of *A*. Addition in the necklace ring is given by pointwise addition of sequences. Multiplication is given by a sort of arithmetic convolution: the product of $(a_1, a_2, ...)$ and $(b_1, b_2, ...)$ has components

$$\displaystyle c_n=\sum_{[i,j]=n}(i,j)a_ib_j$$

where $[i,j]$ is the [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961) of $i$ and $j$, and $(i,j)$ is their [greatest common divisor](wikipedia://en/12354).

This ring structure is isomorphic to the multiplication of formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) written in "necklace coordinates": that is, identifying an integer sequence $(a_1, a_2, ...)$ with the power series $\textstyle\prod_{n\geq 0} (1{-}t^n)^{-a_n}$.

:::
