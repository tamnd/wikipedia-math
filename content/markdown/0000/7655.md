# Clay Mathematics Institute

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Clay Mathematics Institute"}

The **Clay Mathematics Institute** (**CMI**) is a private, non-profit [foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_%28nonprofit%29) dedicated to increasing and disseminating [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) knowledge.[^o3] It was established in 1998 through an endowment from American businessman [Landon T. Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landon_T._Clay).[^o3] The institute supports mathematical research and education through funding, programs, and publications.

:::

CMI is best known for its [Millennium Prize Problems](wikipedia://en/27599234). It carries out activities, including conferences, workshops, summer schools, and a [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral) program supporting Clay Research Fellows.[^o4]

## Governance {#Governance}

The institute is run according to a standard structure comprising a scientific advisory committee that decides on grant-awarding and research proposals, and a board of directors that oversees and approves the committee's decisions. , the board is made up of members of the Clay family, whereas the scientific advisory committee is composed of [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305), [Michael Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569), [Andrei Okounkov](wikipedia://en/6607318), [Gigliola Staffilani](wikipedia://en/44916025), [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027), and [Martin R. Bridson](wikipedia://en/36378173). Bridson is the current president of CMI.[^o6]

## Millennium Prize Problems {#Millennium_Prize_Problems}

The institute is best known for establishing the Millennium Prize Problems on May 24, 2000. These seven problems are considered by CMI to be "important classic questions that have resisted solution over the years." For each problem, the first person to solve it will be awarded US\$1,000,000 by the CMI. In announcing the prize, CMI drew a parallel to [Hilbert's problems](wikipedia://en/154584), which were proposed in 1900, and had a substantial impact on 20th century mathematics. Of the initial 23 Hilbert problems, most of which have been solved, only the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) (formulated in 1859) is included in the seven Millennium Prize Problems.[^o17]

For each problem, the Institute had a professional mathematician write up an official statement of the problem, which will be the main standard against which a given solution will be measured. The seven problems are:

- [P versus NP](wikipedia://en/6115)
- The [Hodge conjecture](wikipedia://en/244518)
- The [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798) – solved, by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266)[^o18]
- The [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125)
- [Yang–Mills existence and mass gap](wikipedia://en/2393975)
- [Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness](wikipedia://en/6013654) – OpenAI published a solution in September 2026 however the result has yet to be verified by the Clay Institute or the independent mathematical community and is the subject of a [priority dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_priority#Priority_disputes).[^o19]
- The [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161)

Some of the mathematicians who were involved in the selection and presentation of the seven problems were [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), [Enrico Bombieri](wikipedia://en/10475), [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340), [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831), [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594), [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027), and [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894).

## Other activities {#Other_activities}

Besides the Millennium Prize Problems, the Clay Mathematics Institute supports mathematics via the awarding of research fellowships (which range from two to five years and are aimed at younger mathematicians), as well as shorter-term scholarships for programs, individual research, and book writing. The institute also has a yearly [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167), recognizing major breakthroughs in mathematical research. Finally, the institute organizes a number of summer schools, conferences, workshops, public lectures, and outreach activities aimed primarily at junior mathematicians (from the high school to the postdoctoral level). CMI publications are available in PDF form at most six months after they appear in print.
