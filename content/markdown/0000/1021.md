# Aspect ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aspect ratio"}

The **aspect ratio** of a [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) shape is the [ratio](wikipedia://en/87837) of its sizes in different dimensions. For example, the aspect ratio of a [rectangle](wikipedia://en/70048) is the ratio of its longer side to its shorter side—the ratio of width to height,[^o2][^o3] when the rectangle is oriented as a "[landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_format)".

:::

The aspect ratio is most often expressed as two integer numbers separated by a colon (x:y), less commonly as a simple or decimal [fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29). The values x and y do not represent actual widths and heights but, rather, the proportion between width and height. As an example, 8:5, 16:10, 1.6:1, $\tfrac{8}{5}$ and 1.6 are all ways of representing the same aspect ratio.

In objects of more than two dimensions, such as [hyperrectangles](wikipedia://en/1688550), the aspect ratio can still be defined as the ratio of the longest side to the shortest side.

### Ellipses {#Ellipses}

For an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), the aspect ratio denotes the ratio of the [major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_axis) to the [minor axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_axis). An ellipse with an aspect ratio of 1:1 is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220).

## Aspect ratios of general shapes {#Aspect_ratios_of_general_shapes}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), there are several alternative definitions to aspect ratios of general [compact sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) in a d-dimensional space:[^o9]

- The diameter-width aspect ratio (DWAR) of a compact set is the ratio of its diameter to its width. A circle has the minimal DWAR which is 1. A square has a DWAR of $\sqrt{2}$.
- The cube-volume aspect ratio (CVAR) of a compact set is the *d*-th root of the ratio of the *d*-volume of the smallest enclosing axes-parallel *d*-cube, to the set's own *d*-volume. A square has the minimal CVAR which is 1. A circle has a CVAR of $\sqrt{2}$. An axis-parallel rectangle of width *W* and height *H*, where *W*>*H*, has a CVAR of $\sqrt{W^2/WH} = \sqrt{W/H}$.

If the dimension *d* is fixed, then all reasonable definitions of aspect ratio are equivalent to within constant factors.

## Notations {#Notations}

Aspect ratios are mathematically expressed as *x*:*y* (pronounced "x-to-y").

Cinematographic aspect ratios are usually denoted as a (rounded) decimal multiple of width vs unit height, while photographic and videographic aspect ratios are usually defined and denoted by whole number ratios of width to height. In [digital images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_image) there is a subtle distinction between the *display* aspect ratio (the image as displayed) and the [*storage* aspect ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storage_aspect_ratio) (the ratio of pixel dimensions); see [Distinctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio_%28image%29#Distinctions).
