# Alexander

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander"}

**Alexander** (Ἀλέξανδρος) is a masculine [name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_name) of Greek origin. The most prominent bearer of the name is [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), the king of the Ancient Greek kingdom of [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_%28ancient_kingdom%29) who created one of the largest empires in ancient history.[^o3]

:::

Variants listed here are **Aleksandar**, **Aleksander**, **Oleksandr**, **Oleksander**, **Aleksandr**, and **Alekzandr**. Related names and diminutives include [Iskandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_%28name%29), [Alec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec), [Alek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alek), [Alekos, Alex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex), [Alexsander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexsander), [Alexandre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_%28given_name%29), [Aleks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleks_%28given_name%29), [Aleksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksa_%28given_name%29), [Aleksandre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandre), [Alejandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro), [Alessandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro), [Alasdair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair), [Sasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_%28name%29), [Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_%28given_name%29), [Sandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro), [Sikandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_%28disambiguation%29), [Skander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skander), [Sander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sander_%28name%29) and [Xander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xander); feminine forms include [Alexandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_%28given_name%29), and Sasha.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The name *Alexander* originates from the Ἀλέξανδρος (Aléxandros; 'defending men'[^o5] or 'protector of men'). It is a compound of the verb ἀλέξειν (aléxein; 'to ward off, avert, defend')[^o6] and the noun ἀνήρ (anḗr, [genitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetive): ἀνδρός, andrós; meaning 'man').[^o7]

The earliest [attested form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attested_language) of the name, is the [Mycenaean Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greek) feminine [anthroponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthroponym) 𐀀𐀩𐀏𐀭𐀅𐀨, a-re-ka-sa-da-ra, (/[Alexandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra)/), written in the [Linear B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B) syllabic script.[^o8] [Alaksandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaksandu), alternatively called *Alakasandu* or *Alaksandus*, was a king of [Wilusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilusa) who sealed a treaty with the [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) king [Muwatalli II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muwatalli_II) c. 1280 BC; this is generally assumed to have been a Greek called Alexandros.

The name was one of the [epithets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithet) given to the Greek goddess [Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera) and as such is usually taken to mean "one who comes to save warriors". In the *[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad)*, the character [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_%28mythology%29) is also known as Alexander.[^o9] The name's popularity was spread throughout the Greek world by the military conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great). Most later Alexanders in various countries were directly or indirectly named after him.[^o10][^o11]
