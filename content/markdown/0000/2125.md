# Algebraic extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic extension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **algebraic extension** is a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) $L/K$ such that every element of the larger [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $L$ is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/186291) over the smaller field $K$; that is, every element of $L$ is a root of a non-zero [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) with coefficients in $K$.[^o2][^o3] A field extension that is not algebraic, is said to be [transcendental](wikipedia://en/287364), and must contain [transcendental elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_element), that is, elements that are not algebraic.[^o4][^o5]

:::

The finite algebraic extensions of the field $\mathbb{Q}$ of the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) are called [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822) and are the main objects of study of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). Another example of a common algebraic extension is the extension $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$ of the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) by the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

## Some properties {#Some_properties}

All transcendental extensions are of infinite [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697). This in turn implies that all finite extensions are algebraic.[^o7] The [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) is not true however: there are infinite extensions which are algebraic.[^o8] For instance, the field of all [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) is an infinite algebraic extension of the rational numbers.[^o9]

Let $E$ be an extension field of $K$, and $a \in E$. The smallest subfield of $E$ that contains $K$ and $a$ is commonly denoted $K(a).$ If $a$ is algebraic over $K$, then the elements of $K(a)$ can be expressed as polynomials in $a$ with coefficients in *K*; that is, $K(a)=K[a]$, the smallest [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) containing $K$ and $a$. In this case, $K(a)$ is a finite extension of $K$ and all its elements are algebraic over $K$. In particular, $K(a)$ is a $K$-vector space with basis $\{1,a,...,a^{d-1}\}$, where *d* is the degree of the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of $a$.[^o10] These properties do not hold if $a$ is not algebraic. For example, $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)\neq \mathbb{Q}[\pi],$ and they are both infinite dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{Q}.$[^o11]

An [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) *F* has no proper algebraic extensions, that is, no algebraic extensions *E* with *F* \< *E*.[^o12] An example is the field of complex numbers. Every field has an algebraic extension which is algebraically closed (called its [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129)), but [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) this in general requires some form of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).[^o13]

An extension *L*/*K* is algebraic [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) every sub *K*-[algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) of *L* is a field.

## Properties {#Properties}

The following three properties hold:[^o16]

1. If *E* is an algebraic extension of *F* and *F* is an algebraic extension of *K* then *E* is an algebraic extension of *K*.
1. If *E* and *F* are algebraic extensions of *K* in a common overfield *C*, then the [compositum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositum) *EF* is an algebraic extension of *K*.
1. If *E* is an algebraic extension of *F* and *E* > *K* > *F* then *E* is an algebraic extension of *K*.

These finitary results can be generalized using transfinite induction:

The [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of any chain of algebraic extensions over a base field is itself an algebraic extension over the same base field.

This fact, together with [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442) (applied to an appropriately chosen [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset)), establishes the existence of [algebraic closures](wikipedia://en/3129).

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

[Model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) generalizes the notion of algebraic extension to arbitrary theories: an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) of *M* into *N* is called an **algebraic extension** if for every *x* in *N* there is a [formula](wikipedia://en/404582) *p* with parameters in *M*, such that *p*(*x*) is true and the set

$$\left\{y\in N \mid p(y)\right\}$$

is finite. It turns out that applying this definition to the theory of fields gives the usual definition of algebraic extension. The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of *N* over *M* can again be defined as the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160), and it turns out that most of the theory of Galois groups can be developed for the general case.

:::

::: {#o20 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Relative algebraic closure"}

## Relative algebraic closures {#Relative_algebraic_closures}

Given a field *k* and a field *K* containing *k*, one defines the **relative algebraic closure** of *k* in *K* to be the subfield of *K* consisting of all elements of *K* that are algebraic over *k*, that is all elements of *K* that are a root of some nonzero polynomial with coefficients in *k*.

:::
