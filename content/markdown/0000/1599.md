# Alexander of Aphrodisias

*Image: Alexander of Aphrodisias de Fato 1658 page 7.jpg.* Opening paragraph of the treatise *On Fate* (*Peri eimarmenes*) by Alexander of Aphrodisias dedicated to the Emperors (*tous autokratoras*). From an anonymous edition published in 1658.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander of Aphrodisias"}

**Alexander of Aphrodisias** (Ἀλέξανδρος ὁ Ἀφροδισιεύς; 200 AD) was a [Peripatetic](wikipedia://en/529396) philosopher and the most celebrated of the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) [commentators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commentaries_on_Aristotle) on the writings of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308). He was a native of [Aphrodisias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisias) in [Caria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caria) and lived and taught in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) at the beginning of the 3rd century, where he held a position as head of the [Peripatetic school](wikipedia://en/529396). He wrote many commentaries on the works of [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), extant are those on the *[Prior Analytics](wikipedia://en/491442)*, *[Topics](wikipedia://en/1692819)*, *[Meteorology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology_%28Aristotle%29)*, *[Sense and Sensibilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_and_Sensibilia_%28Aristotle%29)*, and *[Metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics_%28Aristotle%29)*. Several original treatises also survive, and include a work *On Fate*, in which he argues against the [Stoic](wikipedia://en/19224834) doctrine of necessity; and one *On the Soul*. His commentaries on Aristotle were considered so useful that he was styled, by way of pre-eminence, "the commentator" (ὁ ἐξηγητής).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Alexander was a native of [Aphrodisias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisias) in [Caria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caria) (present-day [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey))[^o4] and came to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) towards the end of the 2nd century. He was a student of the two [Stoic](wikipedia://en/19224834),[^o5] or possibly [Peripatetic](wikipedia://en/529396), philosophers [Sosigenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosigenes_the_Peripatetic)[^o6] and [Herminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herminus),[^o7] and perhaps of [Aristotle of Mytilene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle_of_Mytilene).[^o8] At Athens he became head of the [Peripatetic school](wikipedia://en/529396) and lectured on Peripatetic philosophy. Alexander's dedication of *On Fate* to [Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus) and [Caracalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracalla), in gratitude for his position at Athens, indicates a date between 198 and 209. A recently published inscription from Aphrodisias confirms that he was head of one of the Schools at Athens and gives his full name as Titus Aurelius Alexander.[^o4] His full nomenclature shows that his grandfather or other ancestor was probably given Roman citizenship by the emperor [Antoninus Pius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoninus_Pius), while proconsul of Asia. The inscription honours his father, also called Alexander and also a philosopher. This fact makes it plausible that some of the suspect works that form part of Alexander's corpus should be ascribed to his father.[^o9]

### Commentaries {#Commentaries}

*Image: Alexander - Commentaria in Analytica priora Aristotelis, 1549 - 4725692.tif.* *Commentaria in Analytica priora Aristotelis*, 1549

*Image: Andrea briosco, aristotele e alessandro di afrodisia.JPG.* [Andrea Briosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Briosco), *Aristotle and Alexander of Aphrodisias*, 16th century [plaquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaquette), [Bode-Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bode_Museum)

*Image: Alexander Aphrodisiensis – Commentaria in meteorologica Aristotelis, 1548 – BEIC 4779395.jpg.* *Commentaria in meteorologica Aristotelis*, 1548

Alexander composed several [commentaries on the works of Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commentaries_on_Aristotle), in which he sought to escape a [syncretistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncretism) tendency and to recover the pure doctrines of Aristotle. His extant commentaries are on *[Prior Analytics](wikipedia://en/491442)* (Book 1), *[Topics](wikipedia://en/1692819)*, *[Meteorology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorology_%28Aristotle%29)*, *[Sense and Sensibilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_and_Sensibilia_%28Aristotle%29)*, and *[Metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics_%28Aristotle%29)* (Books 1–5).[^o12] The commentary on the *[Sophistical Refutations](wikipedia://en/1692779)* is deemed spurious, as is the commentary on the final nine books of the *Metaphysics*.[^o13] The lost commentaries include works on the *[De Interpretatione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Interpretatione)*, *[Posterior Analytics](wikipedia://en/1692809)*, *[Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_%28Aristotle%29)*, *[On the Heavens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Heavens)*, *[On Generation and Corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Generation_and_Corruption)*, *[On the Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Soul)*, and *[On Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Memory)*.[^o13] [Simplicius of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicius_of_Cilicia) mentions that Alexander provided commentary on the [quadrature of the lunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrature_of_the_lune), and the corresponding problem of [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359).[^o14] In April 2007, it was reported that imaging analysis had discovered an early commentary on Aristotle's *[Categories](wikipedia://en/1692652)* in the [Archimedes Palimpsest](wikipedia://en/167417), and [Robert Sharples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sharples_%28classicist%29) suggested Alexander as the most likely author.[^o15]

### Original treatises {#Original_treatises}

There are also several extant original writings by Alexander. These include: *On the Soul*, *Problems and Solutions*, *Ethical Problems*, *On Fate*, and *On Mixture and Growth*.[^o12] Three works attributed to him are considered spurious: *Medical Questions*, *Physical Problems*, and *On Fevers*.[^o12] Additional works by Alexander are preserved in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) translation, these include: *On the Principles of the Universe*,[^o17] *On Providence*, and *Against [Galen](wikipedia://en/12326) on Motion*.[^o18]

*On the Soul* (*De anima*) is a treatise on the [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_%28spirit%29) written along the lines suggested by Aristotle in his own *[De anima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Soul)*.[^o19] Alexander contends that the undeveloped reason in man is material (*nous hylikos*) and inseparable from the body.[^o11] He argued strongly against the doctrine of the soul's immortality.[^o11] He identified the active intellect (*nous poietikos*), through whose agency the potential intellect in man becomes actual, with [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God).[^o11] A second book is known as the *Supplement to On the Soul* (*Mantissa*). The *Mantissa* is a series of twenty-five separate pieces of which the opening five deal directly with [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology).[^o20] The remaining twenty pieces cover problems in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics), of which the largest group deals with questions of [vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception) and [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light), and the final four with [fate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destiny) and [providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence).[^o20] The *Mantissa* was probably not written by Alexander in its current form, but much of the actual material may be his.[^o21]

*Problems and Solutions* (*Quaestiones*) consists of three books which, although termed "problems and solutions of physical questions," treat of subjects which are not all physical, and are not all problems.[^o22] Among the sixty-nine items in these three books, twenty-four deal with physics, seventeen with psychology, eleven with [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), and six with questions of fate and providence.[^o22] It is unlikely that Alexander wrote all of the *Quaestiones*, some may be Alexander's own explanations, while others may be exercises by his students.[^o23]

*Ethical Problems* was traditionally counted as the fourth book of the *Quaestiones*.[^o22] The work is a discussion of ethical issues based on Aristotle, and contains responses to questions and problems deriving from Alexander's school.[^o24] It is likely that the work was not written by Alexander himself, but rather by his pupils on the basis of debates involving Alexander.[^o24]

*On Fate* is a treatise in which Alexander argues against the [Stoic](wikipedia://en/19224834) doctrine of necessity.[^o11] In *On Fate* Alexander denied three things - necessity (ἀνάγκη), the foreknowledge of fated events that was part of the Stoic identification of God and Nature, and [determinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinism) in the sense of a sequence of causes that was laid down beforehand (προκαταβεβλημένα αἴτια) or predetermined by antecedents (προηγούμενα αἴτια). He defended a view of moral responsibility we would call [libertarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism_%28metaphysics%29) today.[^o25]

*On Mixture and Growth* discusses the topic of [mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture) of physical bodies.[^o26] It is both an extended discussion (and polemic) on [Stoic physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoic_physics), and an exposition of Aristotelian thought on this theme.[^o26]

*On the Principles of the Universe* is preserved in Arabic translation. This treatise is not mentioned in surviving Greek sources, but it enjoyed great popularity in the Muslim world, and a large number of copies have survived.[^o27] The main purpose of this work is to give a general account of Aristotelian cosmology and metaphysics, but it also has a polemical tone, and it may be directed at rival views within the Peripatetic school.[^o28] Alexander was concerned with filling the gaps of the Aristotelian system and smoothing out its inconsistencies, while also presenting a unified picture of the world, both physical and ethical.[^o29] The topics dealt with are the nature of the heavenly motions and the relationship between the unchangeable celestial realm and the [sublunar world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublunary_sphere) of generation and decay.[^o29] His principal sources are the *[Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_%28Aristotle%29)* (book 7), *[Metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics_%28Aristotle%29)* (book 12), and the [Pseudo-Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Aristotle) *[On the Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Universe)*.[^o29]

*On Providence* survives in two Arabic versions.[^o30] In this treatise, Alexander opposes the Stoic view that [divine Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence) extends to all aspects of the world; he regards this idea as unworthy of the gods.[^o30] Instead, providence is a power that emanates from the heavens to the sublunar region, and is responsible for the generation and destruction of earthly things, without any direct involvement in the lives of individuals.[^o30]

## Influence {#Influence}

By the 6th century Alexander's commentaries on Aristotle were considered so useful that he was referred to as "the commentator" (ὁ ἐξηγητής).[^o32] His commentaries were greatly esteemed among the [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab), who translated many of them,[^o11] and he is heavily quoted by [Maimonides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimonides).

In 1210, the Church Council of Paris issued [a condemnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condemnations_%28University_of_Paris%29#Condemnation_of_1210), which probably targeted the writings of Alexander among others.[^o33]

In the early [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) his doctrine of the soul's mortality was adopted by [Pietro Pomponazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Pomponazzi) (against the [Thomists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomists) and the [Averroists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Averroists)),[^o11] and by his successor [Cesare Cremonini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Cremonini_%28philosopher%29). This school is known as [Alexandrists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrists).

[Alexander's band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander's_band), an [optical phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_phenomenon), is named after him.

## Modern editions {#Modern_editions}

Several of Alexander's works were published in the [Aldine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldine_Press) edition of Aristotle, Venice, 1495–1498; his *De Fato* and *De Anima* were printed along with the works of [Themistius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Themistius) at Venice (1534); the former work, which has been translated into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) by [Grotius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grotius) and also by [Schulthess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schulthess), was edited by [J. C. Orelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Caspar_von_Orelli), [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich), 1824; and his commentaries on the *Metaphysica* by [H. Bonitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Bonitz), Berlin, 1847.[^o11] In 1989 the first part of his *On Aristotle's Metaphysics* was published in English translation as part of the [Ancient commentators project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_commentators_project). Since then, other works of his have been translated into English.
