# List of cryptographers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of cryptographers"}

This is a **list of cryptographers**. [Cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) is the practice and study of techniques for [secure communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_communication) in the presence of third parties called [adversaries](wikipedia://en/789126).

:::

## Pre twentieth century {#Pre_twentieth_century}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi"}

- [Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khalil_ibn_Ahmad_al-Farahidi): wrote a (now lost) book on cryptography titled the "*Book of Cryptographic Messages*".

:::

::: {#o4 .entry cue=list-entry name="Al-Kindi"}

- [Al-Kindi](wikipedia://en/272065), 9th century [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) polymath and originator of [frequency analysis](wikipedia://en/157934).

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=list-entry name="Athanasius Kircher"}

- [Athanasius Kircher](wikipedia://en/93815), attempts to decipher encrypted messages.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Augustus the Younger, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg"}

- [Augustus the Younger, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_the_Younger,_Duke_of_Brunswick-Lüneburg), wrote a standard book on cryptography.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ibn Wahshiyya"}

- [Ibn Wahshiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Wahshiyya): published several cipher alphabets that were used to encrypt magic formulas.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="John Dee"}

- [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee), wrote an occult book, which in fact was a cover for crypted text.

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ibn 'Adlan"}

- [Ibn 'Adlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_'Adlan): 13th-century cryptographer who made important contributions on the sample size of the frequency analysis.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Duke of Mantua"}

- [Duke of Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_Mantua) [Francesco I Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_I_Gonzaga) is the one who used the earliest example of homophonic [Substitution cipher](wikipedia://en/48356) in the early 1400s.[^o12][^o13]

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ibn al-Durayhim"}

- [Ibn al-Durayhim](wikipedia://en/35575543): gave detailed descriptions of eight cipher systems that discussed substitution ciphers, leading to the earliest suggestion of a "tableau" of the kind that two centuries later became known as the "Vigenère table".

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ahmad al-Qalqashandi"}

- [Ahmad al-Qalqashandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_al-Qalqashandi): Author of *Subh al-a 'sha*, a fourteen volume encyclopedia in Arabic, which included a section on cryptology. The list of ciphers in this work included both substitution and transposition, and for the first time, a cipher with multiple substitutions for each plaintext letter.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Charles Babbage"}

- [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), UK, 19th century mathematician who, about the time of the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), secretly developed an effective attack against [polyalphabetic substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyalphabetic_substitution) ciphers.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Leone Battista Alberti"}

- [Leone Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leone_Battista_Alberti), [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath)/universal [genius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius), inventor of [polyalphabetic substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyalphabetic_substitution) (more specifically, the [Alberti cipher](wikipedia://en/7900075)), and what may have been the first mechanical encryption aid.

:::

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Giovanni Battista della Porta"}

- [Giovanni Battista della Porta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_della_Porta), author of a seminal work on [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715).

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Étienne Bazeries"}

- [Étienne Bazeries](wikipedia://en/401047), French, military, considered one of the greatest natural cryptanalysts. Best known for developing the "[Bazeries Cylinder](wikipedia://en/854993)" and his influential 1901 text *Les Chiffres secrets dévoilés* ("Secret ciphers unveiled").

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Giovan Battista Bellaso"}

- [Giovan Battista Bellaso](wikipedia://en/15928585), Italian cryptologist.

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Giovanni Fontana (engineer)"}

- [Giovanni Fontana (engineer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Fontana_%28engineer%29) wrote two encrypted books.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hildegard of Bingen"}

- [Hildegard of Bingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_of_Bingen) used her own alphabet to write letters.

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=list-entry name="Julius Caesar"}

- [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) general/politician, has the [Caesar cipher](wikipedia://en/48405) named after him, and a [lost work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_literary_work) on cryptography by Probus (probably Valerius Probus) is claimed to have covered his use of military cryptography in some detail. It is likely that he did not invent the cipher named after him, as other [substitution ciphers](wikipedia://en/48356) were in use well before his time.

:::

::: {#o24 .entry cue=list-entry name="Friedrich Kasiski"}

- [Friedrich Kasiski](wikipedia://en/512712), author of the first published attack on the [Vigenère cipher](wikipedia://en/69509), now known as the [Kasiski test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasiski_test).

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Auguste Kerckhoffs"}

- [Auguste Kerckhoffs](wikipedia://en/240985), known for contributing cipher design principles.

:::

::: {#o26 .entry cue=list-entry name="Edgar Allan Poe"}

- [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe), author of the book, *A Few Words on Secret Writing*, an essay on cryptanalysis, and *[The Gold Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gold_Bug)*, a short story featuring the use of letter frequencies in the solution of a cryptogram.

:::

::: {#o27 .entry cue=list-entry name="Johannes Trithemius"}

- [Johannes Trithemius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Trithemius), mystic and first to describe *tableaux* (tables) for use in [polyalphabetic substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyalphabetic_substitution). Wrote an early work on [steganography](wikipedia://en/28733) and cryptography generally.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Philips van Marnix, lord of Sint-Aldegonde"}

- [Philips van Marnix, lord of Sint-Aldegonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips_van_Marnix,_lord_of_Sint-Aldegonde), deciphered Spanish messages for [William the Silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_Orange) during the Dutch revolt against the Spanish.

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=list-entry name="John Wallis"}

- [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) codebreaker for [Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Cromwell) and [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England).

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sir Charles Wheatstone"}

- [Sir Charles Wheatstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Wheatstone), inventor of the so-called [Playfair cipher](wikipedia://en/173586) and general polymath.

:::

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="William F. Friedman"}

- [William F. Friedman](wikipedia://en/175115), US, [SIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_Intelligence_Service), introduced statistical methods into [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

::: {#o46 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cecilia Elspeth Giles"}

- [Cecilia Elspeth Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecilia_Elspeth_Giles), UK, [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037)

:::

::: {#o47 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jack Good"}

- [Jack Good](wikipedia://en/404404) UK, [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Code_and_Cypher_School), [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) worked with [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) on the statistical approach to cryptanalysis.

::: {#o49 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dillwyn Knox"}

::: {#o50 .entry cue=list-entry name="Solomon Kullback"}

The first to break the cipher in history.
