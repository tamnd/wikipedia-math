# D

D#

D

d

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="D"}

**D** ([minuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case): **d**) is the fourth [letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_%28alphabet%29) of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), used in the [modern English alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet), the alphabets of other [Western European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) languages and others worldwide. Its name in English is [*dee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_alphabet#Letter_names) (pronounced ), plural *dees*.[^o3]

:::

The [Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages) letter [Dāleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daleth) may have developed from the [logogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logogram) for a fish or a door.[^o6] There are many different [Egyptian hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyph) that might have inspired this. In Semitic, [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek)[^o7] and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin),[^o8] the letter represented ; in the [Etruscan alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_alphabet)[^o9] the letter was archaic but still retained. The equivalent [Greek letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_letter) is delta, [Δ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_%28letter%29).[^o7]

The [minuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_case) (lower-case) form of 'd' consists of a lower-story left [bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface_anatomy#Strokes) and a [stem ascender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface_anatomy#Strokes). It most likely developed by gradual variations on the [majuscule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_case) (capital) form 'D', and is now composed as a stem with a full [lobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface_anatomy#Strokes) to the right. In handwriting, it was common to start the arc to the left of the vertical stroke, resulting in a [serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serif) at the top of the arc. This serif was extended while the rest of the letter was reduced, resulting in an angled stroke and loop. The angled stroke slowly developed into a vertical stroke.[^o10]

### English {#English}

In [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_orthography), $\langle d\rangle$ generally represents the [voiced alveolar plosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_plosive) .

The letter $\langle d\rangle$[^o24] is the [tenth most frequently used](wikipedia://en/6767165) in the English language.

### Other languages {#Other_languages}

*Image: Boundary stone on the Demeljoch - 2.jpg.* The letter D, standing for "Deutschland" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) for "[Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)"), on a [boundary stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_stone) at the border between Austria and Germany.

In most languages that use the Latin alphabet, $\langle d\rangle$ generally represents the [voiced alveolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_plosive) or [voiced dental plosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_dental_plosive) .

In the [Vietnamese alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_alphabet), it represents the sound in northern dialects or in southern dialects. In [Fijian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fijian_language), it represents a [prenasalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenasalized_consonant) stop .[^o26]

In some languages where [voiceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_%28phonetics%29) [unaspirated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspiration_%28phonetics%29) stops contrast with voiceless aspirated stops, $\langle d\rangle$ represents an unaspirated , while $\langle t\rangle$ represents an aspirated . Examples of such languages include [Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language), [Scottish Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic), [Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_language) and the [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin) transliteration of [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese).

### Other systems {#Other_systems}

In the [International Phonetic Alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Phonetic_Alphabet), $\langle d\rangle$ represents the [voiced alveolar plosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiced_alveolar_plosive) .

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

- In the [hexadecimal](wikipedia://en/13263) (base 16) numbering system, D is a number that corresponds to the number 13 in [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) (base 10) counting.[^o29]
- The [Roman numeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_numeral) D represents the number [500](wikipedia://en/463981).[^o30]
- [Unit prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_prefix) d, meaning one tenth.
- D is the grade below C but above E/F in the [school grading system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grading_%28education%29).
- D is the [international vehicle registration code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_vehicle_registration_code) for [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) (also [.de](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.de) as its [top-level domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top-level_domain)).
- In [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese): Because the lack of Unicode CJK support in early computer systems, many [Hong Kongers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongkongers) and [Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporeans) used the capitalized D to represent .[^o31]
- In the [Gregory-Aland system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_manuscript#Gregory-Aland) for cataloging Biblical manuscripts, D can refer to documents in the [Western text-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_text-type) tradition, either [Codex Bezae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Bezae) or [Codex Claromontanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Claromontanus).
- d. is the standard abbreviation for the [Penny (British pre-decimal coin)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_%28British_pre-decimal_coin%29) (from [denarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denarius))

## Other representations {#Other_representations}

### Computing {#Computing}

The Latin letters $\langle D\rangle$ and $\langle d\rangle$ have [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) encodings 0044 and 0064. These are the same [code points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) as those used in [ASCII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCII) and [ISO 8859](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_8859). There are also [precomposed character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precomposed_character) encodings for $\langle D\rangle$ and $\langle d\rangle$ with diacritics, for most of those listed above; the remainder are produced using [combining diacritics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combining_diacritic).

Variant forms of the letter have unique code points for specialist use: the [alphanumeric symbols set](wikipedia://en/971549) in mathematics and science, [plosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plosive) sounds in linguistics and [halfwidth and fullwidth forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfwidth_and_fullwidth_forms) for legacy [CJK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJK_characters) font compatibility.

### Other {#Other}

In [British Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Sign_Language) (BSL), the letter 'd' is indicated by signing with the right hand held with the index and thumb extended and slightly curved, and the tip of the thumb and finger held against the extended index of the left hand.
