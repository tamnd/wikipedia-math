# Demographics of Armenia

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Armenia"}

The population of [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) – corresponding to the [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) as well as [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) and [post-Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia) – has undergone significant changes in the 20th century.

:::

Following Turkey's [genocide of Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_genocide) in 1915, between 300,000–600,000 Armenians fled from [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) to Eastern Armenia,[^o5][^o6][^o7] a number which increased further following [Turkey's invasion of Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish–Armenian_War) in 1920. After registering steady increases during the Soviet period due to repatriation campaigns and low emigration rates, the [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censuses_of_Armenia) declined from its peak value of 3.633 million in 1992 to 3.075 million in 2025.[^o2] Primary factors contributing to this emigration include the [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_conflict) and the [economic hardship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_Armenian_energy_crisis) caused by the [ongoing blockade imposed by Turkey and Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish–Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia_%281989–present%29) (1989–present). Recently there has been a moderate influx of [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians) returning to [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia).

## Historical statistics {#Historical_statistics}

1831

Citing Armenia's conquest and occupation by the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) (11th century) and [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) (13th–15th centuries), historians [Edmund Herzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Herzig) and Marina Kurkchiyan write "the combination of progressive Turkish (and Kurdish) immigration and Armenian decline, through massacre, famine and emigration, changed the demographic balance in a way that Arab immigration had never done".[^n1]

As a result of "deliberate relocation policies employed by both the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) and [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids)" during the [Ottoman–Safavid War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman–Safavid_War_%281603–1618%29), there was a large-scale displacement of Armenians; Armenians also emigrated "to escape the insecurity and hardship of life in war-torn Armenia". Whilst [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbas_I) [relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Surgun) Armenians to [Isfahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan) and "Armenian colonies in other parts of Iran" in 1604–1605, "the Ottomans also removed Armenian artisans to their capital".[^n2]

Following the [Russian annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay), 45,000 Armenians from [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) and 100,000 from the [Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) migrated to [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia), with another 25,000 migrating following the [1878 Russo-Turkish war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_%281877–1878%29). As a result of the repatriation, Armenians had regained a majority in their homeland "for the first time in several hundred years".[^n3] As a result of persecution and massacres in the Ottoman Empire, some 100,000 Armenians immigrated to Eastern Armenia between 1870 and 1910.[^n4] The areas with Armenian-majorities would later "form the nucleus in the twentieth century of an independent Armenian state".[^n3]

Historian Sen Hovhannisian writes that during the 80 years of peace during which Eastern Armenia was part of [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), there was "unprecedented" population growth: it tripled from 161,700 to 496,100 between 1831 and 1873, and doubled in the following forty years until it reached 1,000,100 in 1913. The population between 1831 and 1913 increased 6.18 times, yielding an average annual growth rate of 10,200 people. Following the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), the population, which was 1,014,300 in 1914, fell by 20,500 in 1916 due to the [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians) population being drafted. As a result of "wars and civil clashes, hunger and diseases" of 1918–1920, 432,000 people (35.8 percent of the population) were "exterminated".[^n5]

Upon its [sovietisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovietization_of_Armenia), the territory of modern-day Armenia had a population of some 720,000, a decline of nearly 30 percent—"almost half" consisted of refugees.[^n6] American historian [Richard Pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pipes) states that "according to Soviet estimates, the Armenian population of Transcaucasia declined between 1914 and 1920 by one half million: 200,000 in consequence of Turkish, and, presumably, Communist, massacres, and 300,000 from other causes, mostly famine and disease".[^n7]

The drastic decline of the population was addressed by the [Soviet Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) government by repatriating displaced Azerbaijanis to districts where they had formed a significant population in Armenia. The Azerbaijani population of Armenia which numbered some 10,000 in 1920 (attributed to the [ARF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation) government's expulsion of at least 200,000 Turks and Kurds) rose to 72,596 in 1922 as a result of the return of 60,000 refugees.[^n8] In addition to this, the Soviet government welcomed 44,000 Armenian refugees from [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece), [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey), and elsewhere throughout the 1920s and 1930s.[^n9] In 1946–1948, 86,000 Armenians were repatriated to Soviet Armenia to offset the country's [wartime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Patriotic_War) losses.[^n10] At the same time, by agreement of Armenian and Azerbaijani Soviet leaderships, tens of thousands of Azerbaijanis in Armenia were resettled to Azerbaijan to make room for the repatriates.[^n11]

## Population size and structure {#Population_size_and_structure}

According to the 2018 [HDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) statistical update (with data for 2017), compared to all its neighbouring countries Armenia has:[^o31]

- the lowest coefficient of human inequality,
- the lowest gender inequality (ranked 55th on [Gender Inequality Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Inequality_Index)),
- highest percentage of men and highest percentage of women with at least some secondary education,
- highest share of seats in parliament held by women,
- highest share of women who reported to feel safe,
- highest GDP growth rate.

Since 1990, Armenia recorded steady growth of average annual [HDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) scores in every reported period (1990–2000, 2000–2010, 2010–2017).[^o31]

According to the 2016 [Sustainable Society Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Society_Index), Armenia has a higher rank of Human Wellbeing than all its neighbours. At the same time its Economic Wellbeing rank is below neighbouring countries.[^o32]

The 2011 census counted 539,394 persons (19.4 percent of the population above 6 years of age) with higher professional education.[^o33]

### Fertility rate {#Fertility_rate}

In 2016, natural increase of population comprised 12,366 persons and the crude rate of natural increase reached 4.1%, per 1000 population, decreasing by 0.4 percent compared to the previous year.[^o2]

After double-digit crude natural increase rates between 1982 and 1992, rates did not exceed 5.5 after 1998. At a regional level, slightly better rates were recorded in the capital [Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan), where the value of 5.5 is consistently being surpassed since 2009. Particularly weak is natural increase in [Tavush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavush_Province) and [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province) provinces, not much better off are [Lori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Province) and [Vayots Dzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayots_Dzor_Province) provinces.[^o2]

The mean age of mothers at birth was 26.8 years and at first birth it was 24.7 years in 2016.[^o2] Adolescent birth rate, as well as, share of women married aged 18 was lowest in Armenia compared to its neighbouring countries.[^o31]

In 2016, infant mortality rate (in the first year of their life) was 8.6 per 1,000 live births.[^o2]

A study revealed that population growth rate *changes* were more favourable in Armenia than in its surrounding countries between 2005 and 2015.

### Life expectancy {#Life_expectancy}

*Image: Life expectancy in Armenia.svg.* Life expectancy in Armenia since 1950

*Image: Life expectancy by WBG -Armenia -diff.png.* Life expectancy in Armenia since 1960 by gender

According to the 2018 [HDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) statistical update, compared to all its neighbouring countries Armenia has the highest health expenditures as percentage of its GDP and the highest [healthy life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_life_expectancy).[^o31]

In 2016, the average life expectancy at birth for males was 71.6 years and for females was 78.3 years, with the average at 75.0 years.[^o2]

After a setback during 1986–1996, mostly due to the [Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake), and the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War), Armenia regained its position and was consistently among the top three former Soviet republics during 1997–2016, topping the list in 2007.[^o90]

During the [Soviet period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), life expectancy was traditionally high in Armenia and topped all other republics of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and most other countries in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) between 1978 and 1980.[^o91][^o92]

## Religions {#Religions}

Most Armenians are [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian), primarily of the [Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) rite. Armenia is considered the first nation to officially adopt Christianity, which was first preached in Armenia by two Apostles of Jesus, [St. Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bartholomew) and [St. Thaddeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thaddeus) in the 1st century. The [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) can trace its roots back to the 3rd and 4th centuries. The country formally adopted the Christian faith in 301 A.D. Over 90 percent of Armenians belong to the Armenian Apostolic Church. Armenia also has a population of [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) and [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) Protestants.

According to the 2022 Armenian census, number of adherents of primary religions in Armenia are the following: 2,793,042 (95.2%) [Armenian Apostolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church), 15,836 (0.5%) [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), 14,349 (0.5%) [Yazidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidism), 17,884 (0.6%) Armenian and Roman (Latin) [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church), 6,316 (0.2%) [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), 5,282 (0.2%) [Jehovah's Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), 2,000 (0.1%) [Molokan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molokan) (non-Orthodox Russians), 524 [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East) (Nestorian), 2,132 [Paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paganism), 515 [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), 118 [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism), 204 [Hare Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Krishna_Consciousness), 7675 others. 17,501 (0.6%) people chose [No Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) and 49,353 people chose (1.7%) No Response.[^o125]

## Emigration {#Emigration}

*Image: Net migration per 1000 in Armenia.png.* Annual net migration rate (per 1000 population). Source data and projections (2019) from UN.

Compared to its neighbouring countries, Armenia has the highest share of immigrants (6.5 percent of total population, 2017 data).[^o31]

The estimated number of population net migration is −24.8 thousand persons, according to the Integrated living conditions survey of households of 2016; for urban population −13.8 thousand and for rural population −11.0 thousand persons.[^o2]

\24.9% of households were involved in external and internal migration processes over the period of 2013– 2016. Migration directions were distributed as follows: 12% – internal, 10.5% – [Republic of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Artsakh), rest (76.4%) – international (of which 89.8% – Russia). Among household members of age 15 and above, who left their permanent residence in 2013–2016 for 3 months and longer and had not returned as of 2016, 11.9% were in Armenia, 13.0% in Artsakh, and 75.1% in other countries, predominantly in Russia. More than 54% of migrant household members of the age 15 years and above sent money and/ or goods to their families/relatives/friends within 12 months preceding the survey.[^o127]

According to 2019 [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) data, the emigration rate averaged annually around 1.7 per 1000 inhabitants in years 2015–2020 and is expected to remain the same until year 2045. These are below average emigration rate of 11.5 per 1000 in years 2000–2010 and even below the emigration rate of 3.2 per 1000 in years 1980–1985.[^o128]

### Migration during post-Soviet period {#Migration_during_post-Soviet_period}

It is estimated that 740,000–1,300,000 people left Armenia between 1988 and 2005.[^o130]

Economically recessed situation in Armenia during the 1990s enhanced the emigration of 125,000 refugees and displaced persons. Human and natural disasters also caused approximately 192,000 individuals to become internally displaced persons in Armenia. Among the disasters, the major impact was the [1988 Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake).[^o130]

After the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), borders that were once formal, now assumed real significance. Nonetheless, increased political, inter-ethnic, and social tensions prompted more and more people to migrate between Armenia and its neighbouring countries. As a result, approximately 100,000 persons or 3 percent of the country's population emigrated during the beginning of 1990s.[^o130]

Refugees and forcibly displaced persons started arriving to Armenia in spring 1988 and continued coming until late 1991. During this time, Armenia gave shelter to approximately 419,000 refugees and displaced persons, 360,000 of whom migrated from Azerbaijan. The rest immigrated from other regions of the former Soviet Union.[^o130]

Migration flows during the post-soviet period can be divided into 3 stages:

- The first stage, prior to 1995, was characterized by mass emigration due to economic reasons, a drop in living standards, and a rapid deterioration in the delivery and quality of public utilities. It is estimated that over 800,000 people emigrated from Armenia during this period and that only 400,000 of them have returned since then.
- During the second stage, from 1995 to 2001, emigration decreased, with most of those leaving to be labour migrants in search of better economic and social opportunities. 180,000 people (6 per cent of the population) emigrated from Armenia during these six years These emigrants tended to resettle abroad permanently and were later joined by relatives through family reunification.
- The third stage, from 2002 to the present, is marked by a constant yearly increase in the number of persons travelling to and from Armenia. This stage was also characterized by a shift to a positive migration balance.[^o130]

According to government records, over 55 per cent of all emigrants are unmarried and 60 per cent are males between the ages of 20 and 44 (very few are children and even fewer are elderly people). Most have an educational level far higher than the national average and have no intention of returning to Armenia. Although no hard data exists, emigrant families appear to be even less likely to return.[^o130]

The emigration of the major part of the Armenian population has brought about important changes. For example, a decrease in the number of people of reproductive age in Armenia has led to a progressive drop in marriages and birth rates. There has also been a considerable change in the ethnic composition of the population in Armenia due to a higher rate of emigration among ethnic minorities.[^o130]

## Wealth and poverty {#Wealth_and_poverty}

::: {#o137 .statement cue=named-heading name="Inequality"}

### Inequality {#Inequality}

Out of 41 [emerging economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_economies), Armenia was among only four, which recorded rising inequality (measured by [Gini coefficient](wikipedia://en/12883)) in years 2007–2015.[^o138]

:::

### Wealth {#Wealth}

According to Global Wealth Report, prepared by Credit Suisse, [mean wealth per adult](wikipedia://en/40103183) in Armenia in 2019 is estimated at \$19,517 (rising 9 times from estimated \$2,177 in year 2000). Mean wealth per adult in Armenia surpasses corresponding values for neighboring countries Georgia and Azerbaijan by over 50%, all [CIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Independent_States) countries except Russia and Kazakhstan, and neighboring Iran. Growth rate of mean wealth per adult between 2000 and 2019 with the value of 9 times beats all neighboring countries, most of CIS countries as well as Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania.[^o140][^o141]

Median wealth per adult is reported at \$8,309 in 2019, above the world average, rising 9.6 times from \$862 in year 2000.[^o140][^o141]

Between 2000 and 2019, average debts per adult grew 28.7 times to \$1,261, or 6.5% of wealth per adult (below the 11.9% world average).[^o140][^o141]

55% of adults own less than \$10,000, 42.7% **—** \$10,000–\$100,000, 2.2% **—** \$100,000–\$1 million and 0.1% **—** over \$1 million. The share of adults owning less than \$10,000 with the value of 55% is less than corresponding value in each of CIS countries, neighboring Iran and Turkey, as well as the world average. Gini coefficient for wealth is reported at 66.3%, less than 82.4% the European average and 88.5% the world average.[^o140][^o141]

### Poverty {#Poverty}

As much as 53.5% of the country's population was officially considered poor in 2004. Poverty fell significantly in the following years amid double-digit economic growth that came to an end due to the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis). It soared to almost 36% in 2010, one year after Armenia's Gross Domestic Product shrunk by over 14%. Afterwards, there was a decreasing trend throughout the last years reaching 23.5% in 2018, down from 25.7% in 2017.[^o143]

The poverty indicators in [Shirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Province), [Lori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Province), [Kotayk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotayk_Province), [Tavush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavush_Province) and [Armavir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armavir_Province) provinces are higher than the country average. The highest poverty rate in the country has been recorded in [Shirak province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirak_Province), where 46% of the population is below the poverty line. To overcome poverty, Armenia would need AMD 63.2 billion, or an amount equal to 1.2% of GDP, in addition to the resources already allocated for social assistance, assuming that such assistance would be efficiently targeted to the poor.[^o40]

In terms of the international poverty line corresponding to US\$1.25 in 2005 PPP, poverty in Armenia went down from 19.3% in the year 2001 to 1.5% in the year 2008 and remained nearly unchanged until the year 2015 moving in the range of 1.5% – 2.7%.[^o40]
