# Abel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Abel"}

**Abel** (הֶבֶל *Hébel*, in [pausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausa) הָבֶל *Hā́ḇel*; Ἅβελ *Hábel*; هابيل, *Hābēl*) is a biblical figure in the [Book of Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) within the [Abrahamic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrahamic_religion).[^o3] Born as the second son of [Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam) and [Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eve), the first two humans created by [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Judaism), he was a [shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shepherd) who offered his firstborn flock to God as a religious offering (Genesis 4:1–8). God accepted Abel's offering but not that of his older brother [Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cain), leading Cain to kill Abel out of jealousy; some later interpretations suggest that Cain may have slain him with a stone.[^o4][^o5] This act marked the first death in biblical history, making Abel the first murder victim.[^o3]

## Interpretations {#Interpretations}

### Jewish and Christian interpretations {#Jewish_and_Christian_interpretations}

According to the narrative in [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis), Abel is Eve's second son. His name in Hebrew is composed of the same three consonants as a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_root) meaning "the air that remains after you exhale" also synonymous in Hebrew to "nothing", as stated in [Ecclesiastes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastes). [Julius Wellhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Wellhausen) has proposed that the name is independent of the root.[^o9] [Eberhard Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_Schrader) had previously put forward the [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_%28language%29) (Old Assyrian dialect) *ablu* ("son") as a more likely etymology.[^o10]

*Image: William Bouguereau - El primer duelo.jpg.* *[The First Mourning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_First_Mourning)* (Adam and Eve mourn the death of Abel); oil on canvas 1888 painting by [William-Adolphe Bouguereau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William-Adolphe_Bouguereau)

In Christianity, comparisons are sometimes made between the death of Abel and that of Jesus, the former thus seen as being the first martyr. In Matthew 23:35 Jesus speaks of Abel as "righteous", and the [Epistle to the Hebrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews) states that "The blood of sprinkling ... \[speaks\] better things than that of Abel" (Hebrews 12:24). The blood of Jesus is interpreted as bringing mercy; but that of Abel as demanding vengeance (hence the curse and mark).[^o11]

Abel is invoked in the [litany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litany) for the dying in the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church), and his sacrifice is mentioned in the [Canon of the Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass) along with those of [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) and [Melchizedek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchizedek). The [Alexandrian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrian_Rite) commemorates him with a [feast day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_day) on 28 December.[^o12]

According to the Coptic [Book of Adam and Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Adam_and_Eve) (at 2:1–15), and the [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) [Cave of Treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_Treasures), Abel's body, after many days of mourning, was placed in the *Cave of Treasures*, before which Adam and Eve, and descendants, offered their prayers. In addition, the [Sethite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth) line of the [Generations of Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generations_of_Adam) swear by Abel's blood to segregate themselves from the *unrighteous*.

In the [Book of Enoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Enoch) (22:7), regarded by most Christian and Jewish traditions as extra-biblical, the soul of Abel is described as having been appointed as the chief of martyrs, crying for vengeance, for the destruction of the seed of Cain. A similar view is later shown in the [Testament of Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testament_of_Abraham) (A:13 / B:11), where Abel has been raised to the position as the judge of the souls.

In [Bereshit Rabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereshit_Rabbah) (22:2), a discussion of Gen. 4:1 ff. has Rabbi [Yehoshua ben Korcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_ben_Karha) mentioning that Cain was born with a twin sister, and Abel with two twin sisters. This is based on the principle that the otherwise superfluous accusative article "et" always conveys some additional teaching ([Pesachim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesachim) 22b). The "et"'s are parsed slightly differently in [Yebamot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yebamot) 62a where the two "et"'s in Gen. 4:2 indicate Cain and his sister, and Abel and his (one) sister.

### Sethian Gnostic interpretation {#Sethian_Gnostic_interpretation}

In the [Apocryphon of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocryphon_of_John), a work belonging to [Sethian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sethianism) [Gnosticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnosticism), Abel is the offspring of [Yaldaboath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demiurge#Yaldaboath) and [Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eve), who is placed over the elements of water and earth as [Elohim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elohim), but was only given his name as a form of deception.[^o14][^o15]

### Mandaean interpretation {#Mandaean_interpretation}

According to [Mandaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeism) beliefs and scriptures including the [Qulasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qulasta), the [Book of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draša_D-Iahia) and [Genzā Rabbā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ginza_Rba), Abel is cognate with the angelic [soteriological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soteriology) figure **Hibil Ziwa**,[^o17] (ࡄࡉࡁࡉࡋ ࡆࡉࡅࡀ, sometimes translated "Splendid Hibel"),[^o18] who is spoken of as a son of [Hayyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayyi_Rabbi)[^o19] or of [Manda d-Hayyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manda_d-Hayyi),[^o23][^o20] and as a brother to [Anush (Enosh)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enos_%28biblical_figure%29#In_Mandaeism) and to [Sheetil (Seth)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth#Mandaeism),[^o24] who is the son of [Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam).[^o21] Elsewhere, Anush is spoken of as the son of Sheetil, and Sheetil as the son of Hibil, where Hibil came to Adam and Eve as a young boy when they were still virgins, but was called their son.[^o22] Hibil is an important [lightworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightworld) being ([uthra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthra)) who conquered the [World of Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Darkness_%28Mandaeism%29).[^o23] As **Yawar Hibil**, he is one of multiple figures known as **[Yawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yawar_Ziwa)** (ࡉࡀࡅࡀࡓ), being so named by and after his father.[^o24]

### Islamic interpretation {#Islamic_interpretation}

*Image: NabiHabeel02.JPG.* Grave of Abel within the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque)

*Image: NabiHabeel01.jpg.* The [Mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum) of Abel in the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque)

According to [Shi'a Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam) belief, Abel (*"Habeel"*) is buried in the [Nabi Habeel Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Habeel_Mosque), located on the west mountains of [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus), near the [Zabadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabadani) Valley, overlooking the villages of the [Barada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barada) river (Wadi Barada), in Syria. Shi'a are frequent visitors of this mosque for [ziyarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyarat). The mosque was built by [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) [Wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali_%28administrative_title%29) Ahmad Pasha in 1599.

## In modern media {#In_modern_media}

- Abel is portrayed by [Franco Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Nero) in the film *[The Bible: In the Beginning...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bible:_In_the_Beginning...)* (1966).
- [Paul Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rudd) played the role of Abel in the 2009 film *[Year One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_One_%28film%29)*.
- In the [SCP series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCP_Foundation), an anomaly named Able (with the codename SCP-076) is based on Abel.
- In *[Shin Megami Tensei: Devil Survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Megami_Tensei:_Devil_Survivor)*, the main character Kazuya is the reincarnation of Abel.
- [Oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis_%28band%29) frontman [Liam Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Gallagher) penned a song titled "Guess God Thinks I'm Abel", which appears on the band's 2005 album *[Don't Believe the Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Believe_the_Truth)*.
- The adult animated series *[Hazbin Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazbin_Hotel)* features Abel as a character voiced by [Patrick Stump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Stump).
- Abel is depicted in the manga *[Record of Ragnarok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_of_Ragnarok)* as a supporter of Adam during his fight with [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus) in the second round. He has seemingly reconciled with Cain by the time the events of the story take place.
