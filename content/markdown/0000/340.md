# Alain Connes

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alain Connes"}

**Alain Connes** (; born 1 April 1947[^o4]) is a French mathematician, known for his contributions to the study of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987) and [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917). He was a professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France), [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) and [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1982.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Alain Connes attended high school at Lycée Saint-Charles (Marseille) in Marseille, and was then a student of the [classes préparatoires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classe_préparatoire_aux_grandes_écoles) in Lycée Thiers. Between 1966 and 1970 he studied at [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) in Paris, and in 1973 he obtained a PhD from [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University), under the supervision of [Jacques Dixmier](wikipedia://en/9169420).[^o4]

From 1970 to 1974 he was research fellow at the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) and during 1975 he held a visiting position at [Queen's University at Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in Canada.[^o8]

In 1976 he returned to France and worked as professor at Pierre and Marie Curie University until 1980 and at [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) between 1981 and 1984. Moreover, since 1979 he holds the Léon Motchane Chair at [IHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques).[^o9] From 1984 until his retirement in 2017 he held the chair of Analysis and Geometry at [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o8][^o10]

In parallel, he was awarded a distinguished professorship at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) between 2003 and 2012,[^o11] and at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) between 2012 and 2021.

## Research {#Research}

Connes's main research interests revolved around [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987). Besides [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917), he has applied his works in various areas of mathematics and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).[^o4][^o13][^o5][^o6] Since the 1990s, he developed [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917).[^o14]

In his early work on [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185) in the 1970s, he succeeded in obtaining the almost complete classification of injective [factors](wikipedia://en/294185#Factors). He also formulated the [Connes embedding problem](wikipedia://en/25228957).

Following this, he made contributions in [operator K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) and [index theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_theory), which culminated in the [Baum–Connes conjecture](wikipedia://en/11774498). He also introduced [cyclic cohomology](wikipedia://en/1460172) in the early 1980s as a first step in the study of [noncommutative differential geometry](wikipedia://en/295917).[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18]

He was a member of [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394).[^o19] Over many years, he collaborated extensively with [Henri Moscovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Moscovici).[^o4]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Connes was awarded the [Peccot-Vimont Prize](wikipedia://en/60897406) in 1976,[^o21] the [Ampère Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampère_Prize) in 1980, the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1982,[^o22][^o23] the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) in 2000[^o24] and the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) in 2001.[^o25][^o26][^o27] The [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) granted him the [silver medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Silver_Medal) in 1977[^o28] and the [gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS_Gold_Medal) in 2004.[^o29][^o30][^o31][^o32][^o33]

He was an [invited speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1974 at Vancouver and in 1986 at Berkeley, and a plenary speaker at the ICM in 1978 at Helsinki.

*Image: Villeneuve-d'Ascq - IUT A - Alain Connes - 3.jpg.* Alain Connes at a conference in 2012 in [Villeneuve-d'Ascq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeneuve-d'Ascq)

He was awarded [honorary degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) from [Queen's University at Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in 1979,[^o34] [University of Rome Tor Vergata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_Tor_Vergata) in 1997,[^o35] [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo) in 1999,[^o36] [University of Southern Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_Denmark) in 2009, [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles) in 2010[^o37] and [Shanghai Fudan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fudan_University) in 2017.

Since 1982 he has been a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o38] He was elected member of several foreign academies and societies, including the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters) in 1980,[^o39] the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) in 1983,[^o40] the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1989,[^o41] the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1994,[^o42] the Canadian Academy of Sciences in 1995 (incorporated since 2002 in the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada)),[^o43] the [US National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1997,[^o44] the [Russian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2003[^o45] and the [Royal Academy of Science, Letters and Fine Arts of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy_of_Science,_Letters_and_Fine_Arts_of_Belgium) in 2016.[^o46]

In 2001 he received (together with his co-authors [André Lichnerowicz](wikipedia://en/2389188) and [Marco Schutzenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Schutzenberger)) the Peano Prize for his work *Triangle of Thoughts.*[^o47]

## Family {#Family}

Alain Connes is the middle-born of three sons. Both his parents lived to be 101 years old. He married in 1971.[^o4]
