# Algebraic closure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic closure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **algebraic closure** of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is an [algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125) of *K* that is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018). It is one of many [closures](wikipedia://en/293802) in mathematics.

:::

Using [Zorn's lemma](wikipedia://en/51442)[^o2][^o3][^o4] or the weaker [ultrafilter lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrafilter_lemma),[^o5][^o6] it can be shown that every field has an algebraic closure, and that the algebraic closure of a field *K* is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) that [fixes](wikipedia://en/449738) every member of *K*. Because of this essential uniqueness, we often speak of *the* algebraic closure of *K*, rather than *an* algebraic closure of *K*.

The algebraic closure of a field *K* can be thought of as the largest algebraic extension of *K*. To see this, note that if *L* is any algebraic extension of *K*, then the algebraic closure of *L* is also an algebraic closure of *K*, and so *L* is contained within the algebraic closure of *K*. The algebraic closure of *K* is also the smallest algebraically closed field containing *K*, because if *M* is any algebraically closed field containing *K*, then the elements of *M* that are [algebraic over](wikipedia://en/2125) *K* form an algebraic closure of *K*.

The algebraic closure of a field *K* has the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6173) as *K* if *K* is infinite, and is [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) if *K* is finite.[^o4]

## Examples {#Examples}

- By the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414), the algebraic closure of the field $\mathbf R$ of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is the field $\mathbf C$ of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).
- The algebraic closure of the field $\mathbf Q$ of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) is the field $\overline{\mathbf Q}$ of [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158).
- There are many countable algebraically closed fields within the complex numbers, and strictly containing the field of algebraic numbers; these are the algebraic closures of [transcendental extensions](wikipedia://en/287364) of the rational numbers, e.g. the algebraic closure of $\mathbf{Q} (\pi)$.
- For any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) $p$, there is a [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) field $\overline{\mathbf F_p}$ (also denoted $\mathbf F_{p^\infty}$) which is the algebraic closure of the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) $\mathbf F_{p^k}$ for every positive integer $k$. The field $\overline{\mathbf F_p}$ contains a copy of $\mathbf F_{p^n}$ for each positive integer *$n$*, and in fact, it is the union (more precisely, the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566)) of the fields $\mathbf F_{p^n}$.[^o9]
- By [Puiseux's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puiseux's_theorem), the algebraic closure of the field $\mathbf C((x))$ of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) is the field of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429).

:::

## Existence of an algebraic closure and splitting fields {#Existence_of_an_algebraic_closure_and_splitting_fields}

Let $S = \{ f_{\lambda} \mid \lambda \in \Lambda\}$ be the set of all [monic](wikipedia://en/314730) [irreducible polynomials](wikipedia://en/188725) in $K[x]$. For each $f_{\lambda} \in S$, introduce new variables $u_{\lambda,1},\ldots,u_{\lambda,d}$ where $d = {\rm degree}(f_{\lambda})$. Let $R$ be the polynomial ring over $K$ generated by $u_{\lambda,i}$ for all $\lambda \in \Lambda$ and all $i \leq {\rm degree}(f_{\lambda}).$ Write

$$f_{\lambda} - \prod_{i=1}^d (x-u_{\lambda,i}) = \sum_{j=0}^{d-1} r_{\lambda,j} \cdot x^j \in R[x]$$

with $r_{\lambda,j} \in R$. Let $I$ be the [ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_of_a_ring) in $R$ generated by the $r_{\lambda,j}$. Since $I$ is strictly smaller than $R$, Zorn's lemma implies that there exists a maximal ideal $M$ in $R$ that contains $I$. The field $K_1=R/M$ has the property that every polynomial $f_{\lambda}$ with coefficients in $K$ splits as the product of $x-(u_{\lambda,i} + M),$ and hence has all roots in $K_1$. In the same way, an extension $K_2$ of $K_1$ can be constructed, etc. The union of all these extensions is the algebraic closure of $K$, because any polynomial with coefficients in this new field has its coefficients in some $K_n$ with sufficiently large $n$, and then its roots are in $K_{n+1}$, and hence in the union itself.

It can be shown along the same lines that for any subset $S$ of $K[x]$, there exists a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) of $S$ over $K$.

## Separable closure {#Separable_closure}

An algebraic closure $K^{\text{alg}}$of *$K$* contains a unique [separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340) *$K^{\text{sep}}$* of *K* containing all (algebraic) separable extensions of *$K$* within $K^{\text{alg}}$. This subextension is called a **separable closure** of *$K$*. Since a separable extension of a separable extension is again separable, there are no finite separable extensions of $K^{\text{sep}}$, of degree > 1. Saying this another way, *$K$* is contained in a *separably-closed* algebraic extension field. It is unique ([up to](wikipedia://en/44787) isomorphism).[^o13]

The separable closure is the full algebraic closure if and only if *$K$* is a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088). For example, if *$K$* is a field of [characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_of_a_field) *$p$* and if *$X$* is transcendental over *$K$*, $K(X)(\sqrt[p]{X}) \supset K(X)$ is a non-separable algebraic field extension.

In general, the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of *$K$* is the Galois group of $K^{\text{sep}}$ over *$K$*.[^o14]
