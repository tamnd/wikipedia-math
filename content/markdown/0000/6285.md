# Cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cube"}

A **cube** is a [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3054853) solid object in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). It has eight vertices and twelve straight edges of the same length, so that these edges form six [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) faces of the same size. It is an example of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). It is a special case of a [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802), a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975), and a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406) in which all six quadrilateral faces are squares. It is a three-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783), a family of [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) that also includes the two-dimensional square and four-dimensional [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112).

:::

The cube is found in many popular cultures, including toys and games, the arts, optical illusions, and architectural buildings. Cubes can be found in crystal structures, science, and technological devices. It is also found in ancient texts, such as [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954)'s work [*Timaeus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_%28dialogue%29), which described a set of solids now called [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), associating a cube with the [classical element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element) of [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_%28classical_element%29). A [cube with unit length](wikipedia://en/407763) is the canonical unit of [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) in three-dimensional space, relative to which other solid objects are measured.

The cube can be represented in many ways. One of them is by drawing a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) connected with an [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) in a plane. Such a graph is called the **cubical graph**, a special case of the [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815).

The cube is the core of many polyhedra's construction or other geometrical shapes. For example, [truncating](wikipedia://en/4224429) a cube's vertices results in a [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529). Joining one or more cubes face-to-face yields a [polycube](wikipedia://en/710426), a three-dimensional version of [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634). Two or more cubes can have the same centre, forming [polyhedral compounds](wikipedia://en/23901). Cubes can form a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) by attaching face-to-face, [filling a space without leaving a gap](wikipedia://en/58607583).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Hexahedron.stl.* 3D model of a cube

A cube is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with eight vertices and twelve equal-length edges, forming six [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) as its faces. A cube is a special case of a [rectangular cuboid](wikipedia://en/38848329), which has six [rectangular](wikipedia://en/70048) faces, each of which has a pair of opposite equal-length and parallel edges. Both polyhedra have the same [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046), the angle between two adjacent faces at a common edge, a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956) or 90°, obtained from the [interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angle) (an angle formed between two adjacent sides at a common point of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) within) of a square. More generally, the cube and the rectangular cuboid are special cases of a [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802), a polyhedron with six [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278) (four-sided polygons). As for all convex polyhedra, the cube has [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of 2, according to the formula $V - E + F = 2$; the three letters denote respectively the number of vertices, edges, and faces.

All three square faces surrounding a vertex are [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) to each other, meaning the planes are perpendicular, forming a right angle between two adjacent squares. Hence, the cube is classified as an [orthogonal polyhedron](wikipedia://en/69902387). The cube is a special case of other cuboids. These include a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975), a polyhedron with six [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496) faces, because its pairs of opposite faces are congruent; a [rhombohedron](wikipedia://en/2706406), as a special case of a parallelepiped with six [rhombi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombi) faces, because the interior angle of all of the faces is right;[^n1] and a [trigonal trapezohedron](wikipedia://en/3504388), a polyhedron with congruent quadrilateral faces, since its square faces are the special cases of rhombi.

The cube is a [non-composite or an elementary polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-composite_polyhedron). That is, no plane intersecting its surface only along edges, thereby cutting into two or more convex, regular-faced polyhedra.

### Measurement {#Measurement}

*Image: Cube diagonals.svg.* A face diagonal is denoted as $AC$ and a space diagonal is $AC'$

Given a cube with edge length $a$, the [face diagonal](wikipedia://en/10830506) of the cube is the [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) of a square $a\sqrt{2}$, and the [space diagonal](wikipedia://en/1337505) of the cube is a line connecting two vertices that are not in the same face, formulated as $a \sqrt{3}$. Both formulas can be determined by using the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034). The surface area of a cube $A$ is six times the area of a square:

$$A = 6a^2.$$

The volume of a rectangular cuboid is calculated by multiplying its length, width, and height together. Because all the edges of a cube are equal in length, the formula for the volume of a cube is the third power of its side length. This leads to the use of the term *[cube](wikipedia://en/451445)* as a [verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verb), to mean raising any number to the third power:

$$V = a^3.$$

The cube has three types of [closed geodesics](wikipedia://en/19903176), or paths on a cube's surface that are locally straight. In other words, they avoid the vertices, follow line segments across the faces that they cross, and form [complementary angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_angle) on the two incident faces of each edge that they cross. One configuration lies in a plane parallel to a face of the cube and forms a square congruent to that face, with a side length four times that of the cube's edge. Another type lies in a plane perpendicular to the long diagonal, forming a regular hexagon; its length is $3 \sqrt 2$ times that of an edge. The third type is a non-planar hexagon.

An [insphere](wikipedia://en/991786) of a cube $r_i$ is a sphere tangent to the faces of a cube at their [centroids](wikipedia://en/187926). Its [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281) $r_m$ is a sphere tangent to the edges of a cube. Its [circumsphere](wikipedia://en/991784) $r_c$ is a sphere tangent to the vertices of a cube. With edge length $a$, they are respectively:

$$r_i = \frac{1}{2}a = 0.5a, \qquad r_m = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}a \approx 0.707a, \qquad r_c = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a \approx 0.866a.$$

### Unit cube {#Unit_cube}

*Image: Prince Ruperts cube.png.* [Prince Rupert's cube](wikipedia://en/25923933)

A [unit cube](wikipedia://en/407763) is a cube with [1 unit in length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_length) along each edge. It follows that each face is a [unit square](wikipedia://en/285301) and that the entire figure has a volume of 1 cubic unit. [Prince Rupert of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Rupert_of_the_Rhine), known for [Prince Rupert's drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Rupert's_drop), wagered whether [a cube could be passed through a hole made in another cube of the same size](wikipedia://en/25923933). The story recounted in 1693 by English mathematician [John Wallis](wikipedia://en/239290) answered that it is possible, although there were some errors in Wallis's presentation. Roughly a century later, Dutch mathematician [Pieter Nieuwland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Nieuwland) provided a better solution that the edges of a cube passing through the unit cube's hole could be as large as approximately 1.06 units in length. One way to obtain this result is by using the Pythagorean theorem or the formula for [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) in three-dimensional space.

An ancient problem of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110) requires the construction of a cube with a volume twice the original by using only a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge). This was concluded by French mathematician [Pierre Wantzel](wikipedia://en/2610953) in 1837, proving that it is impossible to implement since a cube with twice the volume of the original—the [cube root](wikipedia://en/292506) of 2, $\sqrt[3]{2}$—is not [constructible](wikipedia://en/7439). However, this problem was solved with [folding an origami paper](wikipedia://en/232840#Doubling_the_cube) by (Messer 1986).

### Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Dual Cube-Octahedron.svg.* The dual polyhedron of a cube is the regular octahedron, and vice versa. The white marks × in this illustration annotate the vertices of a regular octahedron tangent to the faces of a cube. Both have [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805).

The cube has [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805) $\mathrm{O}_\mathrm{h}$ of order 48. In other words, the cube has 48 [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) (including [identity](wikipedia://en/14962)), each of which transforms the cube to itself. These transformations include nine [reflection symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_symmetries) (where two halves cut by a plane are identical): three cut the cube at the midpoints of its edges, and six cut diagonally. The cube also has thirteen axes of [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) (whereby rotation around the axis results in an identical appearance): three axes pass through the centroids of opposite faces, six through the midpoints of opposite edges, and four through opposite vertices; these axes are respectively four-fold rotational symmetry (0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°), two-fold rotational symmetry (0° and 180°), and three-fold rotational symmetry (0°, 120°, and 240°).

The [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) can be obtained from each of the polyhedra's vertices tangent to a plane by a process known as [polar reciprocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_reciprocation). One property of dual polyhedra is that the polyhedron and its dual share their [three-dimensional symmetry point group](wikipedia://en/2694525). In this case, the dual polyhedron of a cube is the [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), and both of these polyhedra have the same octahedral symmetry.

The cube is [face-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face-transitive), meaning its two square faces are alike and can be mapped by rotation and reflection. It is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), meaning all of its vertices are equivalent and can be mapped [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) under its symmetry. It is also [edge-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-transitive), meaning the same kind of faces surround each of its vertices in the same or reverse order, and each pair of adjacent faces has the same dihedral angle. Therefore, the cube is a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299). Each vertex is surrounded by three squares, so the cube is $4.4.4$ by [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) or $\{4,3\}$ by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394).

## Appearances {#Appearances}

### In popular cultures {#In_popular_cultures}

Cubes have appeared in many roles in popular culture. It is the most common form of [dice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dice). Puzzle toys such as pieces of a [Soma cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soma_cube), [Rubik's Cube](wikipedia://en/25971), and [Skewb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skewb) are built of cubes. *[Minecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minecraft)* is an example of a [sandbox video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbox_game) of cubic blocks. The outdoor sculpture [*Alamo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_%28sculpture%29) (1967) is a cube that spins around its vertical axis. [Optical illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusions) such as the [impossible cube](wikipedia://en/98292) and [Necker cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necker_cube) have been explored by artists such as [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127). The cube was applied in [Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti)'s treatise on [Renaissance architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture), *[De re aedificatoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_re_aedificatoria)* (1450). [Cube houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubuswoningen) in the Netherlands are a set of cubical houses whose [hexagonal](wikipedia://en/59733) space diagonals become the main floor. Trees can be [pruned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topiary) to have a cube-shaped form as living sculptures.

### In nature and science {#In_nature_and_science}

Cubes are also found in various fields of natural science and technology. It is applied to the [unit cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cell) of a crystal known as a [cubic crystal system](wikipedia://en/666697). [Table salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_salt) is an example of a [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) with a commonly cubic shape. Other examples are [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite) (although there are many variations) and [uranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium) cubic-shaped in nuclear program. The [radiolarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiolarian) *Lithocubus geometricus*, discovered by [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel), has a cubic shape. [Cubane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubane) is a synthetic [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) consisting of eight carbon [atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom) arranged at the corners of a cube, with one [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) atom attached to each carbon atom.

A historical attempt to unify three physics ideas of [relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_relativity), [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation), and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) used the framework of a cube known as a [*cGh* cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CGh_physics).

Technological cubes include the spacecraft device [CubeSat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat), [thermal radiation](wikipedia://en/185239) demonstration device [Leslie cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_cube), and [web server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_server) machine [Cobalt Qube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt_Qube). Cubical grids are usual in three-dimensional [Cartesian coordinate systems](wikipedia://en/7706). In [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics), [an algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_cubes) divides the input volume into a discrete set of cubes known as the unit on [isosurface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosurface), and the faces of a cube can be used for [mapping a shape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_mapping). In various areas of [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), including [traffic signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_signs) and [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar), the corner of a cube is useful as a [retroreflector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retroreflector), called a [corner reflector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_reflector), which redirects any [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28optics%29) or [wave](wikipedia://en/33516) back to its source.

### In antiquity {#In_antiquity}

The [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) are five polyhedra known since antiquity. The set is named for [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), who attributed these solids to nature in his dialogue [*Timaeus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timaeus_%28dialogue%29). One of them, the cube, represented the [classical element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element) of [earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_%28classical_element%29) because of the building blocks of Earth's foundation. [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) defined the Platonic solids, including the cube, and showed how to find the ratio of the circumscribed sphere's diameter to the edge length.

Following Plato's use of the regular polyhedra as symbols of nature, [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in his *[Harmonices Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonices_Mundi)* sketched each of the Platonic solids; he decorated the cube's side with a tree.[^n2] In his *[Mysterium Cosmographicum](wikipedia://en/3194945)*, Kepler proposed the structure of [Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System) and the relationships between its [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) with the set of Platonic solids, inscribed and circumscribed by [spherical orbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orb_%28astronomy%29). Each solid encased in a sphere, within one another, would produce six layers, corresponding to the six known planets. [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_%28planet%29), [Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus), [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228), [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars), [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter), and [Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn). From innermost to outermost, these solids were arranged from [octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), followed by the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968), [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521), and eventually the cube.

## Constructions {#Constructions}

*Image: The 11 cubic nets 2.svg.* The eleven nets of a cube

The cube has eleven different [nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_%28geometry%29), each of which consists of an arrangement of edge-joined squares. If each boundary between squares is folded to a right angle, the squares become the faces of a cube.

In [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202), a cube can be constructed using the [Cartesian coordinate systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate_systems). For a cube centered at the origin, with edges parallel to the axes and with an edge length of 2, the [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) of the vertices are $(\pm 1, \pm 1, \pm 1)$. Its interior consists of all points $(x_0, x_1, x_2)$ with $-1 < x_i < 1$ for all $i$. A cube's surface with center $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ and edge length of $2a$ is the [locus](wikipedia://en/542465) of all points $(x,y,z)$ such that

$$\max\{ |x-x_0|,|y-y_0|,|z-z_0| \} = a.$$

The cube is a [Hanner polytope](wikipedia://en/39408400), because it can be constructed by using the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of three line segments. Its dual polyhedron, the regular octahedron, is constructed by the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of three line segments.

### As a graph {#As_a_graph}

*Image: Cube skeleton.svg.* The graph of a cube

The cube can be drawn into a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806), a structure in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) consisting of a set of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) that are connected with an [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29). It is attainable according to [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817), which states that a graph can be represented as the [vertex-edge graph](wikipedia://en/74718534) of a polyhedron, as long as it possesses the following two properties. These are [planarity](wikipedia://en/24314) (the edges of a graph are connected to every vertex without crossing other edges), and [3-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) (whenever a graph with more than three vertices, and two of the vertices are removed, the edges remain connected). The skeleton of a cube, represented as the graph, is called the **cubical graph**, a [Platonic graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_graph). It has the same number of vertices and edges as the cube, twelve vertices and eight edges. The cubical graph is also classified as a [prism graph](wikipedia://en/45012199), resembling the skeleton of a cuboid.

The cubical graph is a special case of [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) or $n$-cube—denoted as $Q_n$—because it can be constructed by using the [Cartesian product of graphs](wikipedia://en/2856852): two graphs connecting the pair of vertices with an edge to form a new graph. In the case of the cubical graph, it is the product of $Q_2 \mathbin{\Box} Q_1$, where $\mathbin{\Box}$ denotes the Cartesian product of graphs. In other words, the cubical graph is constructed by connecting each vertex of two squares with an edge. Notationally, the cubical graph is $Q_3$. Like any hypercube graph, it has a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) which visits [every vertex exactly once](wikipedia://en/244437), and it is also an example of a [unit distance graph](wikipedia://en/7158665).

The cubical graph is [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431), meaning every [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) of four vertices can be [disjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjoint_set) and the edges connected in those sets. However, every vertex in one set cannot connect all vertices in the second, so this bipartite graph is not [complete](wikipedia://en/525320). It is an example of both a [crown graph](wikipedia://en/21062705) and a [bipartite Kneser graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartite_Kneser_graph).

:::

### In orthogonal projection {#In_orthogonal_projection}

An object illuminated by parallel rays of light casts a shadow on a plane perpendicular to those rays, called an [orthogonal projection](wikipedia://en/203052). A polyhedron is considered [equiprojective](wikipedia://en/79635682) if, for some position of the light, its orthogonal projection is a regular polygon. The cube is equiprojective because, if the light is parallel to one of the four lines joining a vertex to the opposite vertex, its projection is a [regular hexagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_hexagon).

### As a configuration matrix {#As_a_configuration_matrix}

The cube can be represented as a [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/23905#As_a_configuration), a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) in which the rows and columns correspond to the elements of a polyhedron as the vertices, edges, and faces. The [diagonal](wikipedia://en/194990) of a matrix denotes the number of each element that appears in a polyhedron, whereas the non-diagonal of a matrix denotes the number of the column's elements that occur in or at the row's element. The cube's eight vertices, twelve edges, and six faces are denoted by each element in a matrix's diagonal (8, 12, and 6). The first column of the middle row indicates that there are two vertices on each edge, denoted as 2; the middle column of the first row indicates that three edges meet at each vertex, denoted as 3. The configuration matrix of a cube is:

$$\begin{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}8 & 3 & 3 \\ 2 & 12 & 2 \\ 4 & 4 & 6 \end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}$$

## Related topics {#Related_topics}

### Construction of polyhedra {#Construction_of_polyhedra}

Many polyhedra can be constructed based on a cube. Examples include:

- When [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) a cube, meaning removing part of the polygonal faces without creating new vertices of a cube, the resulting polyhedron is the [stellated octahedron](wikipedia://en/2601866).
- New convex polyhedra can be constructed by attaching less-regular polyhedra to a cube's faces. The cube is thus a component of two [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034), the [elongated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1177939) and [elongated square bipyramid](wikipedia://en/1177952), the latter being a cube with [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) on opposite faces.
- Attaching a low pyramid to each face of a cube produces its [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912), the [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554), [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) to the [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654).
- The [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651) of a cube (or its dual, the regular octahedron) is the [disdyakis dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/724752), a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393).
- The corner region of a cube can also be truncated by a plane (e.g., spanned by the three neighboring vertices), resulting in a [trirectangular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/34275979).[^n3]
- The [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) is an Archimedean solid that can be constructed by separating the cube's faces, and filling the gaps with twisted angle equilateral triangles, a process known as a [snub](wikipedia://en/9428917).
- Each of the cube's vertices can be [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429), and the resulting polyhedron is the [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), the [truncated cube](wikipedia://en/280529).[^n4] When its edges are truncated, it is a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213). Relatedly, the rhombicuboctahedron can also be constructed by separating the cube's faces and then spreading away, after which adding other triangular and square faces between them; this is known as the "expanded cube". The same figure can be derived in the same way from the cube's dual, the regular octahedron.
- The chamfered cube is constructed from a cube by a truncating operator called [chamfer](wikipedia://en/43468103). The resulting polyhedron has twelve hexagonal and six square [centrally symmetric faces](wikipedia://en/55312103), a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402).
- Three mutually perpendicular [golden rectangles](wikipedia://en/219374) can be constructed from a pair of vertices located on the midpoints of the opposite edges on a cube's surface, drawing a segment line between those two, and dividing that segment line in a [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386) from its midpoint. The corners of these rectangles are the vertices of a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968) with twenty equilateral triangles.[^n5]

### Polycubes {#Polycubes}

A [polycube](wikipedia://en/710426) is a [solid figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_figure) formed by joining one or more equal cubes face-to-face. Polycubes are the three-dimensional analogues of two-dimensional [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634).

When four cubes are stacked vertically, and four others are attached to the second-from-top cube of the stack, the resulting polycube is the [Dalí cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalí_cross), named after Spanish [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist) artist [Salvador Dalí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Dalí), whose painting *[Corpus Hypercubus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Hypercubus)* (1954) contains a tesseract unfolding into a six-armed cross; a similar construction is central to [Robert A. Heinlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_A._Heinlein)'s short story "[And He Built a Crooked House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_He_Built_a_Crooked_House)" (1940). The Dalí cross can be folded in a fourth dimension to enclose a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112). A cube is a three-dimensional instance of a [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) (also known as a 3-cube); the two-dimensional hypercube (2-cube) is a [square](wikipedia://en/659939), and the four-dimensional hypercube (4-cube) is a tesseract.

### Space-filling {#Space-filling}

*Image: Partial cubic honeycomb.svg.* [Cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) is an example of [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) in Euclidean three-dimensional space

A cube is a [space-filling polyhedron](wikipedia://en/58607583), meaning it [tessellates](wikipedia://en/321671) with its copy in three-dimensional space. Such a tessellation leaves without a gap, in what is called a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654). The cube is a [plesiohedron](wikipedia://en/53396858), a special kind of space-filling polyhedron that can be defined as the [Voronoi cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronoi_cell) of a symmetric [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512). The plesiohedra include the [parallelohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallelohedra), which can be [translated](wikipedia://en/204682) without rotating to fill a space in which each face of any of its copies is attached to a like face of another copy. There are five kinds of parallelohedra, one of which is the parallelepiped. Every three-dimensional parallelohedron is a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402), a [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrally_symmetric) polyhedron whose faces are [centrally symmetric polygons](wikipedia://en/55312103).

An example of a honeycomb with a cubic type only, called a [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29), is a [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) that consists of four cubes around its edges in Euclidean three-dimensional space. More examples in three-dimensional non-Euclidean space are the honeycomb with three cubes around its edges in a [three-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) and [the honeycomb with five cubes around its edges](wikipedia://en/3870617) in [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742).

Any parallelepiped, including a cube, can achieve a honeycomb if its [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051) is zero. The Dehn invariant's inception dates back to [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703), whether every two equal-volume polyhedra can always be dissected into polyhedral pieces and reassembled into each other. If yes, then the volume of any polyhedron could be defined axiomatically as the volume of an equivalent cube into which it could be reassembled. This problem was solved by [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602), inventing his [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638), answering that not all polyhedra can be reassembled into a cube. It showed that two equal volume polyhedra should have the same Dehn invariant, except for the two tetrahedra whose Dehn invariants were different.

### Miscellanea {#Miscellanea}

The [polyhedral compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound), in which the cubes share the same centre, are [uniform polyhedron compounds](wikipedia://en/8606809), meaning they are polyhedral compounds whose constituents are identical—although possibly [enantiomorphous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomorphous)—[uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra), in an arrangement that is also uniform. Respectively, the list of compounds enumerated by (Skilling 1976) in the seventh to ninth uniform compounds for the compound of six cubes with rotational freedom, [three cubes](wikipedia://en/14809983), and [five cubes](wikipedia://en/8010502). Two compounds, consisting of two and three cubes were found in [Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)'s [wood engraving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_engraving) print [*Stars*](wikipedia://en/1170784) and [Max Brückner](wikipedia://en/48703370)'s book *Vielecke und Vielflache*.

The spherical cube represents the [spherical polyhedron](wikipedia://en/10393861), which can be modeled with the [arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) of [great circles](wikipedia://en/48082), creating bounds as the edges of a [spherical square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_polygon). Hence, the spherical cube consists of six spherical squares with 120° interior angles on each vertex. It has [vector equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_equilibrium), meaning that the distance from the centroid and each vertex is the same as the distance from that to each edge. Its dual is the [spherical octahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_octahedron).

The topological object [three-dimensional torus](wikipedia://en/2455103) is a topological space defined to be [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the Cartesian product of three circles. It can be represented as a three-dimensional model of the cube shape.

Several [fractal shapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry) have a cubic [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634), including the [Menger sponge](wikipedia://en/208811) (analogous to the two-dimensional [Sierpiński carpet](wikipedia://en/29635)), the Jerusalem cube and [Mosely snowflake](wikipedia://en/48794726).

A cube can be subdivided into six [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455). These pyramids have a height of half the cube's edge length, with their apices meeting at the center.
