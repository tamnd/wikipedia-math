# Andrew Wiles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Wiles"}

**Sir Andrew John Wiles** (born 11 April 1953) is an English mathematician and a [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) Research Professor at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He is best known for [proving](wikipedia://en/21950759) [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953), for which he was awarded the 2016 [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) and the 2017 [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) and for which he was appointed a [Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) in 2000.[^o4] In 2018, Wiles was appointed the first [Regius Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Mathematics) at Oxford.[^o5] Wiles is also a [1997 MacArthur Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program).

:::

Wiles was born in Cambridge to theologian [Maurice Frank Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Frank_Wiles) and Patricia Wiles. While spending much of his childhood in Nigeria, Wiles developed an interest in mathematics and in Fermat's Last Theorem in particular. After moving to Oxford and graduating from there in 1974, he worked on unifying [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929), [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) and [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), starting with [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679)'s generalizations of [Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/396150). In the early 1980s, Wiles spent a few years at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) before moving to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he worked on expanding out and applying [Hilbert modular forms](wikipedia://en/2333880). In 1986, upon reading [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850)'s seminal work on Fermat's Last Theorem, Wiles set out to prove the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) for semistable elliptic curves, which implied Fermat's Last Theorem. By 1993, he had been able to convince a knowledgeable colleague that he had a proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, though a flaw was subsequently discovered. After an insight on 19 September 1994, Wiles and his student [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) were able to circumvent the flaw, and published the results in 1995, to widespread acclaim.

In proving Fermat's Last Theorem, Wiles developed new tools for mathematicians to begin unifying disparate ideas and theorems. His former student Taylor along with three other mathematicians were able to prove the full modularity theorem by 2000, using Wiles's work. Upon receiving the Abel Prize in 2016, Wiles reflected on his legacy, expressing his belief that he did not just prove Fermat's Last Theorem, but pushed the whole of mathematics as a field towards the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) of unifying number theory.[^o6]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wiles was born on 11 April 1953 in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_England), England, the son of [Maurice Frank Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Wiles) (1923–2005) and Patricia Wiles (née Mowll). From 1952 to 1955, his father worked as the chaplain at [Ridley Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Hall,_Cambridge), and later became the [Regius Professor of Divinity at the University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor_of_Divinity_at_the_University_of_Oxford).

Wiles began his formal schooling in Nigeria, while living there as a very young boy with his parents. However, according to letters written by his parents, for at least the first several months after he was supposed to be attending classes, he refused to go. From that fact, Wiles himself concluded that in his earliest years, he was not enthusiastic about spending time in academic institutions. In an interview with [Nadia Hasnaoui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadia_Hasnaoui) in 2021, he said he trusted the letters, yet he could not remember a time when he did not enjoy solving mathematical problems.[^o9]

Wiles attended [King's College School, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_School,_Cambridge),[^o10] and [The Leys School, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Leys_School,_Cambridge).[^o11] Wiles told [WGBH-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGBH-TV) in 1999 that he came across Fermat's Last Theorem on his way home from school when he was 10 years old. He stopped at his local library where he found a book *The Last Problem*, by [Eric Temple Bell](wikipedia://en/255236), about the theorem.[^o37] Fascinated by the existence of a theorem that was so easy to state that he, a ten-year-old, could understand it, but that no one had proven, he decided to be the first person to prove it. However, he soon realised that his knowledge was too limited, so he abandoned his childhood dream until it was brought back to his attention at the age of 33 by [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850)'s 1986 proof of the [epsilon conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_conjecture), which [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312) had previously linked to Fermat's equation.[^o12]

## Early career {#Early_career}

In 1974, Wiles earned his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford).[^o8] Wiles's graduate research was guided by [John Coates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coates_%28mathematician%29), beginning in the summer of 1975. Together they worked on the arithmetic of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) with [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) by the methods of [Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/396150). He further worked with [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) on the [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332) over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), and soon afterward, he generalised this result to [totally real fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field).[^o18][^o14]

In 1980, Wiles earned a PhD while at [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge).[^o15] After a stay at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), in 1981, Wiles became a [Professor of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Department_of_Mathematics) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o16]

In 1985–86, Wiles was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow) at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) near Paris and at the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure).[^o16]

In 1988, Wiles married Nada Canaan, a Princeton student who obtained her Ph.D. in molecular biology in the same year. The couple have three daughters.[^o17]

In 1989, Wiles was elected to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society). At that point according to his election certificate, he had been working "on the construction of ℓ-adic representations attached to [Hilbert modular forms](wikipedia://en/2333880), and has applied these to prove the 'main conjecture' for cyclotomic extensions of totally real fields".[^o18]

## Proof of Fermat's Last Theorem {#Proof_of_Fermat's_Last_Theorem}

From 1988 to 1990, Wiles was a Royal Society Research Professor at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and then he returned to Princeton. From 1994 to 2009, Wiles was a [Eugene Higgins Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Higgins) at Princeton.

Starting in mid-1986, based on successive progress of the previous few years of [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312), [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850), it became clear that [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) (the statement that no three [positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_number) [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $a$, $b$, and $c$ satisfy the equation $a^{n} + b^{n} = c^{n}$ for any integer value of $n$ greater than $2$) could be proven as a [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of a limited form of the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) (unproven at the time and then known as the "Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture").[^o34] The modularity theorem involved elliptic curves, which was also Wiles's own specialist area, and stated that all such curves have a modular form associated with them.[^o21][^o22] These curves can be thought of as mathematical objects resembling solutions for a torus's surface, and if Fermat's Last Theorem were false and solutions existed, "a peculiar curve would result". A proof of the modularity theorem therefore would have as a consequence that such a curve would not exist.[^o26]

The conjecture was seen by contemporary mathematicians as important, but extraordinarily difficult or perhaps impossible to prove.[^o23] For example, Wiles's ex-supervisor [John Coates](wikipedia://en/230705) stated that it seemed "impossible to actually prove",[^o23] and [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850) considered himself "one of the vast majority of people who believed \[it\] was completely inaccessible", adding that "Andrew Wiles was probably one of the few people on earth who had the audacity to dream that you can actually go and prove \[it\]."[^o23]

Despite this, Wiles, with his from-childhood fascination with Fermat's Last Theorem, decided to undertake the challenge of proving the conjecture, at least to the extent needed for [Frey's curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frey's_elliptic_curve).[^o23] He dedicated all of his research time to this problem for over six years in near-total secrecy, covering up his efforts by releasing prior work in small segments as separate papers and confiding only in his wife.[^o23]

Wiles's research involved creating a [proof by contradiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_by_contradiction) of Fermat's Last Theorem, which Ribet in his [1986 work](wikipedia://en/700981) had found to have an elliptic curve and thus an associated modular form if true. Starting by assuming that the theorem was incorrect, Wiles then contradicted the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture as formulated under that assumption, with Ribet's theorem (which stated that if $n$ were a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), no such elliptic curve could have a modular form, so no odd prime counterexample to Fermat's equation could exist). Wiles also proved that the conjecture applied to the special case known as the [semistable elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistable_elliptic_curve) to which Fermat's equation was tied. In other words, Wiles had found that the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture was true in the case of Fermat's equation, and Ribet's finding (that the conjecture holding for semistable elliptic curves could mean Fermat's Last Theorem is true) prevailed, thus proving Fermat's Last Theorem.[^o24][^o25][^o34]

In June 1993, he presented his proof to the public for the first time at a conference in Cambridge. [Gina Kolata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Kolata) of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* summed up the presentation as follows: He gave a lecture a day on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday with the title "Modular Forms, Elliptic Curves and Galois Representations". There was no hint in the title that Fermat's last theorem would be discussed, Dr. Ribet said. ... Finally, at the end of his third lecture, Dr. Wiles concluded that he had proved a general case of the Taniyama conjecture. Then, seemingly as an afterthought, he noted that that meant that Fermat's last theorem was true. Q.E.D.[^o26] In August 1993, it was discovered that the proof contained a flaw in several areas, related to properties of the [Selmer group](wikipedia://en/1970862) and use of a tool called an [Euler system](wikipedia://en/1052176).[^o28][^o27] Wiles tried and failed for over a year to repair his proof. According to Wiles, the crucial idea for circumventing—rather than closing—this area came to him on 19 September 1994, when he was on the verge of giving up. The circumvention used [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) to replace elliptic curves, reduced the problem to a [class number formula](wikipedia://en/3021101) and solved it, among other matters, all using [Victor Kolyvagin](wikipedia://en/4559839)'s ideas as a basis for fixing [Matthias Flach](wikipedia://en/23809349)'s approach with Iwasawa theory.[^o27][^o28] Together with his former student [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931), Wiles published a second paper which contained the circumvention and thus completed the proof. Both papers were published in May 1995 in a dedicated issue of the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785).*[^o29][^o30]

## Later career {#Later_career}

In 2011, Wiles rejoined the University of Oxford as Royal Society Research Professor.[^o16]

In May 2018, Wiles was appointed [Regius Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Professor) of Mathematics at Oxford, the first in the university's history.[^o5]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

*Image: Czech stamp 2000 m259.jpg.* Czech stamp from 2000, celebrating Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem

Wiles's work has been used in many fields of mathematics. Notably, in 1999, three of his former students, [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931), [Brian Conrad](wikipedia://en/4099038), and [Fred Diamond](wikipedia://en/8356786), working with [Christophe Breuil](wikipedia://en/20281471), built upon Wiles's proof to prove the full modularity theorem.[^o33][^o34] Wiles's doctoral students have also included [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088) (2014 winner of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859)), [Ehud de Shalit](wikipedia://en/60008192), [Ritabrata Munshi](wikipedia://en/48460141) (winner of the [SSB Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanti_Swarup_Bhatnagar_Prize_for_Science_and_Technology) and [ICTP Ramanujan Prize](wikipedia://en/13595525)), [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin) (son of [Vera Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Rubin)), [Christopher Skinner](wikipedia://en/59264582), and [Vinayak Vatsal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinayak_Vatsal) (2007 winner of the [Coxeter–James Prize](wikipedia://en/18739444)).

In 2016, upon receiving the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), Wiles said about his proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, "The methods that solved it opened up a new way of attacking one of the big webs of conjectures of contemporary mathematics called the [Langlands Program](wikipedia://en/171905), which as a grand vision tries to unify different branches of mathematics. It’s given us a new way to look at that".[^o6]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

*Image: Wiles vor Sockel.JPG.* Andrew Wiles in front of the statue of [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) in [Beaumont-de-Lomagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaumont-de-Lomagne) in 1995, Fermat's birthplace in southern France

Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem has stood up to the scrutiny of the world's other mathematical experts. Wiles was interviewed for an episode of the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) documentary series *[Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_%28BBC_TV_series%29)*[^o36] about Fermat's Last Theorem. This was broadcast as a 1997 episode of the PBS science television series *[Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_%28American_TV_series%29)* in [Season 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nova_episodes_%28seasons_21–40%29#Season_25:_1997–98) with the title "The Proof".[^o37] His work and life are also described in great detail in [Simon Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Singh)'s popular book *[Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/25905119)*.

In 1988, Wiles was awarded the Junior [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) (1988).[^o8] In 1989, he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1989)[^o38][^o18]

In 1994, Wiles was elected member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o39] Upon completing his proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 1995, he was awarded the [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize),[^o16] [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195),[^o50] and [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) that year.[^o16] Wiles was elected a [Foreign Associate of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Associate_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o14] and won an [NAS Award in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAS_Award_in_Mathematics) from the National Academy of Sciences,[^o40] the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal), and the [Ostrowski Prize](wikipedia://en/3159411) in 1996.[^o41] He won the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)'s [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177),[^o42] a [MacArthur Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Fellows_Program), and the [Wolfskehl Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfskehl_Prize) in 1997,[^o43] and was elected member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) that year.[^o44]

In 1998, Wiles was awarded a silver plaque from the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) recognising his achievements, in place of the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), which is restricted to those under the age of 40 (Wiles was 41 when he proved the theorem in 1994).[^o45] That same year, he was awarded the [King Faisal Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_Foundation#King_Faisal_International_Prize)[^o46] along with the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) in 1999,[^o16] the year the [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid) [9999 Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9999_Wiles) was named after him.[^o47]

In 2000, he was awarded [Knight Commander of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire). In 2004, Wiles won the Premio Pitagora.[^o49] In 2005, he won the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize).[^o50]

The building at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) housing the [Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/7905850) was named after Wiles in 2013.[^o51] Later that year he won the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285).[^o52][^o53][^o54][^o55][^o56] In 2017, Wiles won the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal).[^o4] In 2019, he won the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532).[^o57]
