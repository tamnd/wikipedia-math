# Alternative algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alternative algebra"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **alternative algebra** is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) in which multiplication need not be [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative), only [alternative](wikipedia://en/186050). That is, one must have

:::

- $$x(xy) = (xx)y$$
- $$(yx)x = y(xx)$$

for all *x* and *y* in the algebra.

Every [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) is alternative, given that alternativity is simply a weak form of associativity. However, so too are some strictly [non-associative algebras](wikipedia://en/6533841) such as the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436).

## The associator {#The_associator}

Alternative algebras are so named because they are the algebras for which the [associator](wikipedia://en/943321) is [alternating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_form). The associator is a [trilinear map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilinear_map) given by

$$[x,y,z] = (xy)z - x(yz).$$

By definition, a [multilinear map](wikipedia://en/221525) is alternating if it [vanishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanish_%28mathematics%29) whenever two of its arguments are equal. The left and right alternative identities for an algebra are equivalent to[^n1]

$$[x,x,y] = 0$$

$$[y,x,x] = 0$$

Both of these identities together imply that:

$$[x,y,x]=[x,x,x]+[x,y,x]+$$

$$-[x,x+y,x+y] =$$

$$= [x,x+y,-y] =$$

$$= [x,x,-y] - [x,y,y] = 0$$

for all $x$ and $y$. This is equivalent to the *[flexible identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flexible_identity)*[^n2]

$$(xy)x = x(yx).$$

The associator of an alternative algebra is therefore alternating. [Conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), any algebra whose associator is alternating is clearly alternative. By symmetry, any algebra which satisfies any two of:

- left alternative identity: $x(xy) = (xx)y$
- right alternative identity: $(yx)x = y(xx)$
- flexible identity: $(xy)x = x(yx).$

is alternative and therefore satisfies all three identities.

An alternating associator is always totally skew-symmetric. That is,

$$[x_{\sigma(1)}, x_{\sigma(2)}, x_{\sigma(3)}] = \operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)[x_1,x_2,x_3]$$

for any [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\sigma$. The converse holds so long as the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the base [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is not 2.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every associative algebra is alternative.
- The [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) form a non-associative alternative algebra, a [normed division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_division_algebra) of dimension 8 over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438).[^o7]
- More generally, any [octonion algebra](wikipedia://en/10277277) is alternative.

::: {#o9 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Non-examples"}

### Non-examples {#Non-examples}

- The [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434), [trigintaduonions](wikipedia://en/25474499), and all higher [Cayley–Dickson algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley–Dickson_algebra) lose alternativity. [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) are usually not alternative.

## Properties {#Properties}

**Artin's theorem** states that in an alternative algebra the [subalgebra](wikipedia://en/45239) generated by any two elements is [associative](wikipedia://en/2112).[^n3] Conversely, any algebra for which this is true is clearly alternative. It follows that expressions involving only two variables can be written unambiguously without parentheses in an alternative algebra. A generalization of Artin's theorem states that whenever three elements $x,y,z$ in an alternative algebra associate (i.e., $[x,y,z] = 0$), the subalgebra generated by those elements is associative.

A [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of Artin's theorem is that alternative algebras are [power-associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-associative), that is, the subalgebra generated by a single element is associative.[^n4] The converse need not hold: the sedenions are power-associative but not alternative.

The [Moufang identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moufang_identities)

- $$a(x(ay)) = (axa)y$$
- $$((xa)y)a = x(aya)$$
- $$(ax)(ya) = a(xy)a$$

hold in any alternative algebra.[^n2]

In a unital alternative algebra, multiplicative [inverses](wikipedia://en/45199) are unique whenever they exist. Moreover, for any invertible element $x$ and all $y$ one has

$$y = x^{-1}(xy).$$

This is equivalent to saying the associator $[x^{-1},x,y]$ vanishes for all such $x$ and $y$.

If $x$ and $y$ are invertible then $xy$ is also invertible with inverse $(xy)^{-1} = y^{-1}x^{-1}$. The set of all invertible elements is therefore closed under multiplication and forms a [Moufang loop](wikipedia://en/755329). This *loop of units* in an alternative ring or algebra is analogous to the [group of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_units) in an [associative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_ring) or algebra.

Kleinfeld's theorem states that any simple non-associative alternative ring is a generalized octonion algebra over its [center](wikipedia://en/53796787).[^n5] The structure theory of alternative rings is presented in the book *Rings That Are Nearly Associative* by Zhevlakov, Slin'ko, Shestakov, and Shirshov.[^n6]

Every finite alternative division ring is a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) by the [Artin–Zorn theorem](wikipedia://en/14818089).

:::

## Occurrence {#Occurrence}

The [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) over any alternative [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) is a [Moufang plane](wikipedia://en/14860374).

Every [composition algebra](wikipedia://en/3861635) is an alternative algebra.[^o18]
