# Biconditional elimination

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Biconditional elimination"}

**Biconditional elimination.**

If $P \leftrightarrow Q$ is true, then one may infer that $P \to Q$ is true, and also that $Q \to P$ is true.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Biconditional elimination"}

**Biconditional elimination** is the name of two [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [rules of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) of [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). It allows for one to [infer](wikipedia://en/317465) a [conditional](wikipedia://en/658808) from a [biconditional](wikipedia://en/228783). If $P \leftrightarrow Q$ is true, then one may infer that $P \to Q$ is true, and also that $Q \to P$ is true.[^o3] For example, if it's true that I'm breathing [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) I'm alive, then it's true that if I'm breathing, I'm alive; likewise, it's true that if I'm alive, I'm breathing. The rules can be stated formally as:

:::

$$\frac{P \leftrightarrow Q}{\therefore P \to Q}$$

and

$$\frac{P \leftrightarrow Q}{\therefore Q \to P}$$

where the rule is that wherever an instance of "$P \leftrightarrow Q$" appears on a line of a proof, either "$P \to Q$" or "$Q \to P$" can be placed on a subsequent line.

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

The *biconditional elimination* rule may be written in [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) notation:

$$(P \leftrightarrow Q) \vdash (P \to Q)$$

and

$$(P \leftrightarrow Q) \vdash (Q \to P)$$

where $\vdash$ is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) symbol meaning that $P \to Q$, in the first case, and $Q \to P$ in the other are [syntactic consequences](wikipedia://en/37531624) of $P \leftrightarrow Q$ in some [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102);

or as the statement of a truth-functional [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) or [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of propositional logic:

$$(P \leftrightarrow Q) \to (P \to Q)$$

$$(P \leftrightarrow Q) \to (Q \to P)$$

where $P$, and $Q$ are propositions expressed in some [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).
