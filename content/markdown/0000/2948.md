# Agner Krarup Erlang

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Agner Krarup Erlang"}

**Agner Krarup Erlang** (1 January 1878 – 3 February 1929) was a [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer), who invented the fields of [teletraffic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletraffic_engineering)[^o3] and [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578).[^o3]

:::

Erlang's 1909 paper, and subsequent papers over the decades, are regarded as containing some of most important concepts and techniques for queueing theory.[^o4]

By the time of his relatively early death at the age of 51, Erlang had created the field of [telephone networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSTN) analysis. His early work in scrutinizing the use of local, exchange and trunk telephone line usage in a small community to understand the theoretical requirements of an efficient network led to the creation of the [Erlang formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlang_formula), which became a foundational element of modern [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications) network studies.

## Life {#Life}

Erlang was born at [Lønborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lønborg,_Ringkøbing-Skjern), near [Tarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarm), in [Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jutland). He was the son of a schoolmaster, and a descendant of [Thomas Fincke](wikipedia://en/76073) on his mother's side. At age 14, he passed the Preliminary Examination of the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) with distinction, after receiving dispensation to take it because he was younger than the usual minimum age. For the next two years he taught alongside his father.p. 10-12

A distant relative provided free board and lodging, and Erlang prepared for and took the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) entrance examination in 1896, and passed with distinction. He won a scholarship to the university and majored in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and also studied [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). He graduated in 1901 with an MA and over the next 7 years taught at several schools.p. 13 He maintained his interest in mathematics, and received an award for a paper that he submitted to the University of Copenhagen.p. 14

He was a member of the Danish Mathematicians' Association (DMF) and through this met amateur mathematician [Johan Jensen](wikipedia://en/1430666), the Chief Engineer of the Copenhagen Telephone Company ([KTAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TDC_A/S) in Danish), an [offshoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off) of the [International Bell Telephone Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bell_Telephone_Company).p. 14 Erlang worked for the Copenhagen Telephone Company from 1908 for almost 20 years, until his death in Copenhagen after an abdominal operation.p. 19

He was an associate of the British [Institution of Electrical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institution_of_Electrical_Engineers).p. 18

## Contributions {#Contributions}

While working for the CTC, Erlang was presented with the classic problem of determining how many circuits were needed to provide an acceptable telephone service. His thinking went further by finding how many [telephone operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switchboard_operator) were needed to handle a given volume of calls. Most [telephone exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_exchange) then used human operators and [cord boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_switchboard) to switch [telephone calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_call) by means of [jack plugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_plug).

Out of necessity, Erlang was a hands-on researcher. He would conduct measurements and was prepared to climb into street [manholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhole) to do so.p. 17 He was also an expert in the history and calculation of the numerical tables of mathematical functions, particularly [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860). He devised new calculation methods for certain forms of tables.p. 109-110

He developed his theory of telephone traffic over several years. His significant publications include:

- 1909 – "The Theory of Probabilities and Telephone Conversations", which proves that the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) applies to random telephone traffic.
- 1917 – "Solution of some Problems in the Theory of Probabilities of Significance in Automatic Telephone Exchanges", which contains his classic formulae for [call loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_of_service#Erlang's_lost_call_assumptions) and waiting time.
- 1920 – "Telephone waiting times", which is Erlang's principal work on waiting times, assuming constant holding times.

These and other notable papers were translated into English, French and German. His papers were prepared in a very brief style and can be difficult to understand without a background in the field. One [Bell Telephone Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Telephone_Laboratories) researcher is said to have learned Danish to study them.p. 17

The [British Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BT_Group_plc) accepted his formula as the basis for calculating circuit facilities.p. 17

In 1946, the [CCITT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCITT) named the international unit of telephone traffic the "[erlang](wikipedia://en/10283)".p. 19-22 A statistical distribution and programming language listed below have also been named in his honour.

Erlang also made an important contribution to physiologic modeling with the [Krogh-Erlang capillary cylinder model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krogh_model) describing oxygen supply to living tissue.
