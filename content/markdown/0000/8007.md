# Diameter

*Image: Circle-withsegments.svg.* Circle with black solid 3px blue solid 2px red solid 2px green solid 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diameter"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **diameter** of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) is any straight [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) that passes through the centre of the circle and whose endpoints lie on the circle. It can also be defined as the longest [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) of the circle. Both definitions are also valid for the diameter of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859).

:::

In more modern usage, the length $d$ of a diameter is also called the diameter. In this sense one speaks of the diameter rather than a diameter (which refers to the line segment itself), because all diameters of a circle or sphere have the same length, this being twice the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) $r$:

$$d = 2r; \qquad r = \tfrac12 d.$$

The word "diameter" is derived grc, .[^o2] It is often abbreviated $\operatorname{DIA}$, $\operatorname{dia}$, $d$, or $\varnothing.$

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

With [straightedge and compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straightedge_and_compass), a diameter of a given circle can be constructed as the [perpendicular bisector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular_bisector) of an arbitrary chord. Drawing two diameters in this way can be used to locate the center of a circle, as their crossing point.[^o5] To construct a diameter parallel to a given line, choose the chord to be perpendicular to the line.

The circle having a given line segment as its diameter can be constructed by straightedge and compass, by finding the [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of the segment and then drawing the circle centered at the midpoint through one of the ends of the line segment.

:::

## Symbol {#Symbol}

*Image: Technical Drawing Hole 01.svg.* A diameter sign in a technical drawing

*Image: Hama UV Filter 1.jpg.* A [photographic filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_filter) marked as having a 58 mm thread diameter

The [symbol](wikipedia://en/37673) or [variable](wikipedia://en/3728109) for diameter, ⌀, is sometimes used in technical drawings or specifications as a prefix or suffix for a number (e.g. "⌀ 55 mm"), indicating that it represents diameter.[^o9] Photographic [filter thread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_thread) sizes are often denoted in this way.[^o10]

The symbol has a [code point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_point) in [Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode) at 2300, in the [Miscellaneous Technical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscellaneous_Technical) set. It should not be confused with several other characters (such as 00D8 or 2205) that resemble it but have unrelated meanings.[^o11] It has the [compose sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compose_sequence) [Compose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compose_key).[^o12]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The definitions given above are only valid for circles and spheres. However, they are special cases of a more general definition that is valid for any kind of $n$-dimensional object, or a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of scattered points. The *[diameter of a set](wikipedia://en/68283333)* is the [least upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of the set of all distances between pairs of points in the subset.

A different and incompatible definition is sometimes used for the diameter of a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673). In this context, a diameter is any [chord](wikipedia://en/297947) which passes through the [conic's centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_%28geometry%29#Projective_conics). A diameter of an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) is any line passing through the centre of the ellipse.[^o15] Half of any such diameter may be called a ***semidiameter***, although this term is most often a synonym for the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of a circle or sphere.[^o16] The longest and shortest diameters are called the *[major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_axis)* and *minor axis*, respectively. *[Conjugate diameters](wikipedia://en/24531598)* are a pair of diameters where one is parallel to a tangent to the ellipse at the endpoint of the other diameter.

Several kinds of object can be measured by *[equivalent diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalent_diameter)*, the diameter of a circular or spherical approximation to the object. This includes [hydraulic diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_diameter), the equivalent diameter of a channel or pipe through which liquid flows, and the [Sauter mean diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauter_mean_diameter) of a collection of particles.

The diameter of a circle is exactly twice its radius. However, this is true only for a circle, and only in the [Euclidean metric](wikipedia://en/53932). [Jung's theorem](wikipedia://en/7503941) provides more general inequalities relating the diameter to the radius.
