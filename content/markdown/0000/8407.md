::: {#o5 .subject cue=lead name="Dodecahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **dodecahedron** or **duodecahedron**[^o6] is any [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) with twelve flat [faces](wikipedia://en/149926). The most familiar dodecahedron is the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) with regular pentagons as faces, which is a [Platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905). There are also three [regular star dodecahedra](wikipedia://en/16814), which are constructed as [stellations](wikipedia://en/180253) of the convex form. All of these have [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844), order 120.

:::

Some dodecahedra have the same combinatorial structure as the regular dodecahedron (in terms of the graph formed by its vertices and edges), but their pentagonal faces are not regular: The pyritohedron, a common crystal form in [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite), has [pyritohedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedral_symmetry), while the tetartoid has [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472).

The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) can be seen as a limiting case of the pyritohedron, and it has [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805). The [elongated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3515524) and [trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3521213) variations, along with the rhombic dodecahedra, are [space-filling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-filling_polyhedra). There are numerous other dodecahedra.

While the regular dodecahedron shares many features with other Platonic solids, one unique property of it is that one can start at a corner of the surface and draw an infinite number of straight lines across the figure that return to the original point without crossing over any other corner.

## Regular dodecahedron {#Regular_dodecahedron}

The [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) is a convex polyhedron with regular pentagonal faces, three meeting at each vertex. It has 12 faces, 30 edges, and 20 vertices. It is one of the five regular [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), named after [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954) who described them and considered the other four to symbolize the [classical elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element); he assigned the regular dodecahedron to the cosmos. Its [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) is the [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).

The regular dodecahedron has three [stellations](wikipedia://en/180253), all of which are regular star dodecahedra. They form three of the four [Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra](wikipedia://en/16814). They are the [small stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029843), the [great dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/2860441), and the [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878).[^n1] The small stellated dodecahedron and great dodecahedron are dual to each other; the great stellated dodecahedron is dual to the [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898). All of these regular star dodecahedra have regular pentagonal or [pentagrammic](wikipedia://en/197544) faces. The convex regular dodecahedron and great stellated dodecahedron are different realisations of the same [abstract regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/1211056); the small stellated dodecahedron and great dodecahedron are different realisations of another abstract regular polyhedron.

While the three star forms satisfy the requirements for regularity and have twelve faces, and therefore any of them could also be referred to as a "regular dodecahedron", this specific term is exclusively reserved for the first, convex form. A fifth shape which could be classified as a regular dodecahedron, namely the [dodecagonal](wikipedia://en/508486) [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192), is also not referred to as such, in part because it exists only as a [spherical polyhedron](wikipedia://en/10393861) and is degenerate in Euclidean space.

## Other pentagonal dodecahedra {#Other_pentagonal_dodecahedra}

In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), two important dodecahedra can occur as crystal forms in some [symmetry classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_point_groups) of the [cubic crystal system](wikipedia://en/666697) that are topologically equivalent to the regular dodecahedron but less symmetrical: the pyritohedron with [pyritohedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedral_symmetry), and the [tetartoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetartoid) with [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472):

### Pyritohedron {#Pyritohedron}

*Image: Polyhedron pyritohedron transparent max.gif.* Pyritohedron

A **pyritohedron** (or pentagonal dodecahedron) is a dodecahedron with [pyritohedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedral_symmetry) symmetry T<sub>h</sub>. Like the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), it has twelve identical [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) faces, with three meeting in each of the 20 vertices. However, the pentagons are not constrained to be regular, and the underlying atomic arrangement has no true fivefold symmetry axis. Its 30 edges are divided into two sets, containing 24 and 6 edges of the same length. The only axes of [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096) are three mutually perpendicular twofold axes and four threefold axes.

Although regular dodecahedra do not exist in crystals, the pyritohedron form occurs in the crystals of the [mineral pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite), and it may be an inspiration for the discovery of the regular [Platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905) form. The true regular dodecahedron can occur as a shape for [quasicrystals](wikipedia://en/25350) (such as [holmium–magnesium–zinc quasicrystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmium–magnesium–zinc_quasicrystal)) with [icosahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2877844), which includes true fivefold rotation axes.

*Image: Modell eines Kristalls des Minerals Pyrit (Eisernes Kreuz) -Krantz 375- (2), crop.jpg.* Dual positions in pyrite [crystal models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_model)

#### Crystal pyrite {#Crystal_pyrite}

The name *crystal pyrite* comes from one of the two common [crystal habits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_habit) shown by [pyrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrite) (the other one being the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)). In pyritohedral pyrite, the faces have a [Miller index](wikipedia://en/1089079) of (210), which means that the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) is 2·arctan(2) ≈ 126.87° and each pentagonal face has one angle of approximately 121.6° in between two angles of approximately 106.6° and opposite two angles of approximately 102.6°. The following formulas show the measurements for the face of a perfect crystal (which is rarely found in nature).

$$\text{Height} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2} \cdot \text{Long side}$$

$$\text{Width} = \frac{4}{3} \cdot \text{Long side}$$

$$\text{Short sides} = \sqrt{\frac{7}{12}} \cdot \text{Long side}$$

#### Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

The eight vertices of a cube have the coordinates (±1, ±1, ±1).

The coordinates of the 12 additional vertices are

- (0, ±(1 + *h*), ±(1 − *h*<sup>2</sup>)),
- (±(1 + *h*), ±(1 − *h*<sup>2</sup>), 0) and
- (±(1 − *h*<sup>2</sup>), 0, ±(1 + *h*)).

*h* is the height of the [wedge](wikipedia://en/2951640)-shaped "roof" above the faces of that cube with edge length 2.

An important case is *h* = $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (a quarter of the cube edge length) for perfect natural pyrite (also the pyritohedron in the [Weaire–Phelan structure](wikipedia://en/2795211)).

Another one is *h* = $\tfrac{1}{\varphi}$ = 0.618... for the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361). See section *Geometric freedom* for other cases.

Two pyritohedra with swapped nonzero coordinates are in dual positions to each other like the dodecahedra in the [compound of two dodecahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_two_dodecahedra).

The body of the dodecahedron can also be described as an intersection of twelve half-spaces,

$$\{ (x,y,z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid \| (x,y,z) \|_D \le 1 \}$$

where

$$\| (x,y,z) \|_D = \max \Big\{ |\alpha x + \beta z|,\ |\alpha x - \beta z|,\ |\alpha y + \beta x|,\ |\alpha y - \beta x|,\ |\alpha z + \beta y|,\ |\alpha z - \beta y| \Big\}$$

and

$$\alpha = \frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}, \qquad \beta = \frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2} = \frac{1}{\varphi}.$$

#### Geometric freedom {#Geometric_freedom}

The pyritohedron has a geometric degree of freedom with [limiting cases](wikipedia://en/40400729) of a cubic [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) at one limit of collinear edges, and a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) as the other limit as 6 edges are degenerated to length zero. The regular dodecahedron represents a special intermediate case where all edges and angles are equal.

It is possible to go past these limiting cases, creating concave or nonconvex pyritohedra. The *endo-dodecahedron* is concave and equilateral; it can tessellate space with the convex regular dodecahedron. Continuing from there in that direction, we pass through a degenerate case where twelve vertices coincide in the centre, and on to the regular [great stellated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3029878) where all edges and angles are equal again, and the faces have been distorted into regular [pentagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagram_%28geometry%29). On the other side, past the rhombic dodecahedron, we get a nonconvex equilateral dodecahedron with fish-shaped self-intersecting equilateral pentagonal faces.

### Tetartoid {#Tetartoid}

*Image: Tetartoid perspective.gif.* Tetartoid

A **tetartoid** (also **tetragonal pentagonal dodecahedron**, **pentagon-tritetrahedron**, and **tetrahedric pentagon dodecahedron**) is a dodecahedron with chiral [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472) (T). Like the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361), it has twelve identical [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) faces, with three meeting in each of the 20 vertices. However, the pentagons are not regular and the figure has no fivefold symmetry axes.

*Image: Cobaltite-d05-67a.jpg.* [Cobaltite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobaltite)

Although regular dodecahedra do not exist in crystals, the tetartoid form does. The name tetartoid comes from the Greek root for one-fourth because it has one fourth of full octahedral symmetry, and half of pyritohedral symmetry.[^o19] The mineral [cobaltite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobaltite) can have this symmetry form.[^o20]

Abstractions sharing the solid's [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and symmetry can be created from the cube and the tetrahedron. In the cube each face is bisected by a slanted edge. In the tetrahedron each edge is trisected, and each of the new vertices connected to a face center. (In [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705) this is a gyro tetrahedron.)

#### Cartesian coordinates {#Cartesian_coordinates}

The following points are vertices of a tetartoid pentagon under [tetrahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2904472):

(*a*, *b*, *c*); (−*a*, −*b*, *c*); (−$\tfrac{n}{d_{1}}$, −$\tfrac{n}{d_{1}}$, $\tfrac{n}{d_{1}}$); (−*c*, −*a*, *b*); (−$\tfrac{n}{d_{2}}$, $\tfrac{n}{d_{2}}$, $\tfrac{n}{d_{2}}$),

under the following conditions:[^o24]

0 ≤ *a* ≤ *b* ≤ *c*,

*n* = *a*<sup>2</sup>*c* − *bc*<sup>2</sup>,

*d*<sub>1</sub> = *a*<sup>2</sup> − *ab* + *b*<sup>2</sup> + *ac* − 2*bc*,

*d*<sub>2</sub> = *a*<sup>2</sup> + *ab* + *b*<sup>2</sup> − *ac* − 2*bc*,

*nd*<sub>1</sub>*d*<sub>2</sub> ≠ 0.

#### Geometric freedom {#Geometric_freedom}

The [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) is a tetartoid with more than the required symmetry. The [triakis tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/722548) is a degenerate case with 12 zero-length edges. (In terms of the colors used above this means, that the white vertices and green edges are absorbed by the green vertices.)

## Rhombic dodecahedron {#Rhombic_dodecahedron}

*Image: Rhombicdodecahedron.jpg.* Rhombic dodecahedron

The [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) is a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) with twelve rhombic faces and octahedral symmetry. It is dual to the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), an [Archimedean solid](wikipedia://en/1847), and occurs in nature as a crystal form. The rhombic dodecahedron packs together to fill space with centrally symmetric faces, a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402).

The *rhombic dodecahedron* can be seen as a degenerate [pyritohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedron) where the 6 special edges have been reduced to zero length, reducing the pentagons into rhombic faces.

The rhombic dodecahedron has several [stellations](wikipedia://en/180253), the [first of which](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_stellation_of_rhombic_dodecahedron) is also a [parallelohedral spacefiller](wikipedia://en/607631#Parallelohedron).

Another important rhombic dodecahedron, the [Bilinski dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/50652823), has twelve faces congruent to those of the [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025), i.e. the diagonals are in the ratio of the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). It is also a [zonohedron](wikipedia://en/669402) and was described by [Bilinski](wikipedia://en/44812552) in 1960.[^o28] This figure is another spacefiller, and can also occur in non-periodic [spacefillings](wikipedia://en/6008654) along with the rhombic triacontahedron, the rhombic icosahedron, and rhombic hexahedra.

## Other dodecahedra {#Other_dodecahedra}

There are 6,384,634 topologically distinct *convex* dodecahedra, excluding mirror images—the number of vertices ranges from 8 to 20.[^o30] Two polyhedra are *topologically distinct* if they have intrinsically different arrangements of faces and vertices, such that it is impossible to distort one into the other simply by changing the lengths of edges or the angles between edges or faces.

[Armand Spitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Spitz) used a dodecahedron as the "globe" equivalent for his [Digital Dome planetarium projector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium_projector), based upon a suggestion from [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736).
