# Douglas Hofstadter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Douglas Hofstadter"}

*Image: Douglas Hofstadter, Festival della Matematica, a Roma, 18 marzo 2007.png.* Autograph by Douglas Hofstadter, 2007

**Douglas Richard Hofstadter** (born 15 February 1945) is an American cognitive and computer scientist whose research includes concepts such as the sense of self in relation to the external world,[^o5][^o6] [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness), analogy-making, [strange loops](wikipedia://en/47929), [ambigrams](wikipedia://en/440995),[^o7][^o40] [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence), and discovery in mathematics and physics. His 1979 book *[Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid](wikipedia://en/48103)* won the [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize) for general nonfiction,[^o8][^o9] and a [National Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award) (at that time called The American Book Award) for Science.[^o10]*Gödel, Escher, Bach* won the 1980 [award for hardcover science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_winners_of_the_National_Book_Award#Science). His 2007 book *[I Am a Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_a_Strange_Loop)* won the [*Los Angeles Times* Book Prize for Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize_for_Science_and_Technology).[^o11][^o12]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hofstadter was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to future Nobel Prize-winning physicist [Robert Hofstadter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hofstadter) and Nancy Givan Hofstadter.[^o16] He grew up on the campus of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where his father was a professor, and attended the [International School of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_of_Geneva) in 1958–59. He graduated with [distinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors#United_States) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1965, and attended graduate school in mathematics, but dropped out after feeling unable to keep up in what he called a "hugely bitter blow to my ego", and "traumatic".[^o17] Hofstadter received his PhD in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939)[^o3][^o18] from the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) in 1975. His graduate studies on the [energy levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_level) of [Bloch electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch_electron) in a magnetic field led to his discovery of the [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) known as [Hofstadter's butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter's_butterfly).[^o18]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Hofstadter was initially appointed to Indiana University's computer science department faculty in 1977, and at that time he launched his research program in computer modeling of mental processes (which he called "artificial intelligence research", a label he has since dropped in favor of "cognitive science research"). In 1984, he moved to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in Ann Arbor, where he was hired as a professor of psychology and was also appointed to the Walgreen Chair for the Study of Human Understanding.

In 1988, Hofstadter returned to IU as College of Arts and Sciences Professor in cognitive science and computer science. He was also appointed adjunct professor of history and philosophy of science, philosophy, comparative literature, and psychology, but has said that his involvement with most of those departments is nominal.[^o20][^o21][^o22]

Since 1988, Hofstadter has been the College of Arts and Sciences [Distinguished Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Professor) of Cognitive Science and Comparative Literature at [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_%28Bloomington%29) in Bloomington, where he directs the Center for Research on Concepts and Cognition, which consists of himself and his graduate students, forming the "Fluid Analogies Research Group" (FARG).[^o23] In 1988, he received the *In Praise of Reason* award, the [Committee for Skeptical Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Skeptical_Inquiry)'s highest honor.[^o24] In 2009, he was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences)[^o25] and became a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o26] In 2010, he was elected a member of the [Royal Society of Sciences in Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Sciences_in_Uppsala), Sweden.[^o27]

## Work and publications {#Work_and_publications}

At the University of Michigan and Indiana University, Hofstadter and [Melanie Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Mitchell) coauthored a computational model of "high-level perception"—[Copycat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copycat_%28software%29)—and several other models of analogy-making and [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition), including the Tabletop project, co-developed with [Robert M. French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._French).[^o29] The Letter Spirit project, implemented by Gary McGraw and John Rehling, aims to model artistic creativity by designing stylistically uniform "gridfonts" (typefaces limited to a grid). Other more recent models include Phaeaco (implemented by Harry Foundalis) and SeqSee (Abhijit Mahabal), which model high-level perception and analogy-making in the microdomains of [Bongard problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongard_problem) and number sequences, respectively, as well as George (Francisco Lara-Dammer), which models the processes of perception and discovery in triangle geometry.[^o30][^o31][^o32]

Hofstadter's thesis about consciousness, first expressed in *[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103)* but also present in several of his later books, is that it is "an emergent consequence of seething lower-level activity in the brain." In *Gödel, Escher, Bach* he draws an analogy between the social organization of a colony of ants and the mind seen as a coherent "colony" of neurons. In particular, Hofstadter claims that our sense of having (or being) an "I" comes from the abstract pattern he terms a "[strange loop](wikipedia://en/47929)", an abstract cousin of such concrete phenomena as [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_feedback) and [video feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_feedback) that Hofstadter has defined as "a level-crossing feedback loop". The prototypical example of a strange loop is the self-referential structure at the core of [Gödel's incompleteness theorems](wikipedia://en/58863). Hofstadter's 2007 book *[I Am a Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_a_Strange_Loop)* carries his vision of consciousness considerably further, including the idea that each human "I" is distributed over numerous brains, rather than being limited to one.[^o33]

In 1999, the bicentennial year of the Russian poet and writer [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin), Hofstadter published a verse translation of Pushkin's classic novel-in-verse *[Eugene Onegin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Onegin)*. He has translated other poems and two novels: *[La Chamade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_chamade)* (*That Mad Ache*) by [Françoise Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Françoise_Sagan), and *La Scoperta dell'Alba* (*The Discovery of Dawn*) by [Walter Veltroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Veltroni), the then-head of the Partito Democratico in Italy. *The Discovery of Dawn* was published in 2007, and *That Mad Ache* was published in 2009, bound together with Hofstadter's essay "Translator, Trader: An Essay on the Pleasantly Pervasive Paradoxes of Translation". *[Le Ton beau de Marot: In Praise of the Music of Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ton_beau_de_Marot)* is a long book devoted to language and translation, especially poetry translation, and one of its leitmotifs is a set of 88 translations of "Ma Mignonne", a highly constrained poem by 16th-century French poet [Clément Marot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clément_Marot). In this book, Hofstadter jokingly describes himself as "[pilingual](wikipedia://en/23601)" (meaning that the sum total of the varying degrees of mastery of all the languages that he has studied comes to 3.14159 ...), as well as an "oligoglot" (someone who speaks "a few" languages).[^o34][^o35]

### Hofstadter's law {#Hofstadter's_law}

[Hofstadter's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter's_law) is "It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law." The law is stated in *Gödel, Escher, Bach*.

### [Ambigrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambigrams) {#Ambigrams}

*Image: Rainbow reflection ambigram.jpg.* *Musings on the Rainbow Ambigram*, Douglas Hofstadter's mirror design featuring the names of the seven rainbow colors, followed by a perceptual shift ambigram "2006" / "Doug", fusion of the date and his signature.[^o39]

Hofstadter coined the term [ambigram](wikipedia://en/440995) in 1983-1984[^o40][^o41] to define a calligraphic composition of glyphs (letters, numbers, symbols or other shapes) that can yield different meanings depending on the orientation of observation.[^o42][^o43] It is a [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) composed of the Latin prefix ambi- ("both") and the Greek suffix \-gram ("drawing, writing").[^o40]

To explain visually the numerous types of possible ambigrams, Hofstadter created many pieces with different constraints and symmetries.[^o44] Various university galleries have exhibited his work.[^o45][^o46]

In 1987, a book of 200 of his ambigrams, together with a long dialogue with his alter ego Egbert G. Gebstadter on ambigrams and creativity, was published in Italy.[^o42][^o47]

Hofstadter's 2025 book *Ambigrammia Between Creation and Discovery* ("ABCD") presents hundreds of his hand-crafted ambigrams in English, as well as a few drawn by his friends.[^o48] The book is a reflection on this art form. His works of all types are presented in their historical context, with explanations of the particularities of each.[^o49][^o50]

## Public image {#Public_image}

*Image: Hofstadter2002.jpg.* Hofstadter in Bologna, Italy, in 2002

Hofstadter has said that he feels "uncomfortable with the nerd culture that centers on computers". He admits that "a large fraction \[of his audience\] seems to be those who are fascinated by technology", but when it was suggested that his work "has inspired many students to begin careers in computing and artificial intelligence" he replied that he was pleased about that, but that he himself has "no interest in computers".[^o54][^o55] In that interview he also mentioned a course he has twice given at Indiana University, in which he took a "skeptical look at a number of highly touted AI projects and overall approaches".[^o22] For example, upon the defeat of [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov) by [Deep Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Deep_Blue), he commented: "It was a watershed event, but it doesn't have to do with computers becoming intelligent."[^o56] Yet in his book *Metamagical Themas*, he says that "in this day and age, how can anyone fascinated by creativity and beauty fail to see in computers the ultimate tool for exploring their essence?"[^o57]

In 1988, Dutch director Piet Hoenderdos created a docudrama about Hofstadter and his ideas, *[Victim of the Brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victim_of_the_Brain)*, based on *[The Mind's I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mind's_I)*. It includes interviews with Hofstadter about his work.[^o58]

Provoked by predictions of a [technological singularity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_singularity) (a hypothetical moment in the future of humanity when a self-reinforcing, runaway development of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence) causes a radical change in technology and culture), Hofstadter has both organized and participated in several public discussions of the topic. At Indiana University in 1999 he organized such a symposium, and in April 2000, he organized a larger symposium titled "Spiritual Robots" at Stanford University, in which he moderated a panel consisting of [Ray Kurzweil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Kurzweil), [Hans Moravec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Moravec), [Kevin Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Kelly_%28editor%29), [Ralph Merkle](wikipedia://en/25849), [Bill Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Joy), [Frank Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Drake), [John Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Holland) and [John Koza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Koza). Hofstadter was also an invited panelist at the first [Singularity Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singularity_Summit), held at Stanford in May 2006. Hofstadter expressed doubt that the singularity will occur in the foreseeable future.[^o59][^o60][^o61][^o62][^o63][^o64]

In a 2023 interview, Hofstadter said that rapid progress in AI made some of his "core beliefs" about AI's limitations "collapse".[^o65] Hinting at an [AI takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_takeover), he added that human beings may soon be eclipsed by "something else that is far more intelligent and will become incomprehensible to us".[^o67][^o68]

## Columnist {#Columnist}

When [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) retired from writing his "[Mathematical Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Games_%28column%29)" column for *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* magazine, Hofstadter succeeded him in 1981–83 with a column titled *[Metamagical Themas](wikipedia://en/169419)* (an [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) of "Mathematical Games"). An idea he introduced in one of these columns was the concept of "Reviews of This Book", a book containing nothing but cross-referenced reviews of itself that has an online implementation.[^o70]

One of Hofstadter's columns in *Scientific American* concerned the damaging effects of sexist language, and two chapters of his book *[Metamagical Themas](wikipedia://en/169419)* are devoted to that topic, one of which is an analogy-based satire, "[A Person Paper on Purity in Language](http://www.cs.virginia.edu/~evans/cs655/readings/purity.html)" (1985), in which the reader's presumed revulsion at racism and racist language is used as a lever to motivate an analogous revulsion at sexism and sexist language; Hofstadter published it under the pseudonym William Satire, an allusion to [William Safire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Safire).[^o71] This chapter appears in the book but never appeared in *Scientific American.*[^o72]

Another column reported on the discoveries made by University of Michigan professor [Robert Axelrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Axelrod_%28political_scientist%29) in his computer tournament pitting many iterated [prisoner's dilemma](wikipedia://en/43717) strategies against each other, and a follow-up column discussed a similar tournament that Hofstadter and his graduate student Marek Lugowski organized.

The "Metamagical Themas" columns ranged over many themes, including patterns in [Frédéric Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frédéric_Chopin)'s piano music (particularly his [études](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Études_%28Chopin%29)), the concept of [superrationality](wikipedia://en/277125) (choosing to cooperate when the other party/adversary is assumed to be equally intelligent as oneself), the "relation between the individual and the collective",[^o73] the dangers of nuclear war, and the self-modifying game of [Nomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomic), based on the way the legal system modifies itself, and developed by philosopher [Peter Suber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Suber).[^o74]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Hofstadter was married to Carol Ann Brush until her death. They met in Bloomington, and married in Ann Arbor in 1985. They had two children. Carol died in 1993 from the sudden onset of a brain tumor, [glioblastoma multiforme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glioblastoma_multiforme), when their children were young. The Carol Ann Brush Hofstadter Memorial Scholarship[^o76] for Bologna-bound Indiana University students was established in 1996 in her name.[^o77] Hofstadter's book *[Le Ton beau de Marot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Ton_beau_de_Marot)* is dedicated to their two children and its dedication reads "To M. & D., living sparks of their Mommy's soul". In 2010, Hofstadter met his second wife, Baofen Lin, in a [cha-cha-cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-cha-cha_%28dance%29) class. They married in 2012 in Bloomington.[^o78]

Hofstadter has composed pieces for piano and for piano and voice. He created an audio CD, *DRH/JJ*, of these compositions performed mostly by pianist Jane Jackson, with a few performed by Brian Jones, Dafna Barenboim, Gitanjali Mathur, and Hofstadter.[^o79]

The dedication for *I Am A Strange Loop* is: "To my sister Laura, who can understand, and to our sister Molly, who cannot."[^o80] Hofstadter explains in the preface that his younger sister Molly never developed the ability to speak or understand language.[^o81]

As a consequence of his attitudes about consciousness and empathy, Hofstadter became a vegetarian in his teenage years, and has remained primarily so since that time.[^o82][^o83]

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

In the 1982 novel *[2010: Odyssey Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010:_Odyssey_Two)*, [Arthur C. Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_C._Clarke)'s first sequel to *[2001: A Space Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001:_A_Space_Odyssey_%28novel%29)*, [HAL 9000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_9000) is described by the character "Dr. Chandra" as being caught in a "Hofstadter–[Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875) loop". The movie uses the term "H. Möbius loop". On 3 April 1995, Hofstadter's book *[Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies: Computer Models of the Fundamental Mechanisms of Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_Concepts_and_Creative_Analogies)* was the first book sold by [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com).[^o85] [Michael R. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_R._Jackson)'s musical *[A Strange Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Strange_Loop)* makes reference to Hofstadter's concept and the title of his 2007 book.
