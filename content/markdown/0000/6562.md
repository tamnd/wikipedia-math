# Conditional proof

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conditional proof"}

A **conditional proof** is a [proof](wikipedia://en/1197184) that takes the form of asserting a [conditional](wikipedia://en/658808), and proving that the [antecedent](wikipedia://en/804755) of the conditional necessarily leads to the [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The assumed antecedent of a conditional proof is called the **conditional proof assumption** (**CPA**). Thus, the goal of a conditional proof is to demonstrate that if the CPA were true, then the desired conclusion [necessarily follows](wikipedia://en/37531624). The validity of a conditional proof does not require that the CPA be true, only that *if it were true* it would lead to the consequent.

Conditional proofs are of great importance in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). Conditional proofs exist linking several otherwise unproven [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138), so that a proof of one conjecture may immediately imply the validity of several others. It can be much easier to show a proposition's truth to follow from another proposition than to prove it independently.

A famous network of conditional proofs is the [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) class of complexity theory. There is a large number of interesting tasks (see *[List of NP-complete problems](wikipedia://en/1707754)*), and while it is not known if a polynomial-time solution exists for any of them, it is known that if such a solution exists for some of them, one exists for all of them. Similarly, the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) has many consequences already proven.

## Symbolic logic {#Symbolic_logic}

As an example of a conditional proof in [symbolic logic](wikipedia://en/19636), suppose we want to prove A → C (if A, then C) from the first two premises below:

| \1. | A → B | ("If A, then B") |
|---|---|---|
| \2. | B → C | ("If B, then C") |
| \3. | A | (conditional proof assumption, "Suppose A is true") |
| \4. | B | (follows from lines 1 and 3, [modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900); "If A then B; A, therefore B") |
| \5. | C | (follows from lines 2 and 4, [modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900); "If B then C; B, therefore C") |
| \6. | A → C | (follows from lines 3–5, conditional proof; "If A, then C") |
