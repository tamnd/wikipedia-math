# Disjunctive syllogism

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Disjunctive syllogism"}

**Disjunctive syllogism.**

If $P$ is true or $Q$ is true, and $P$ is false, then $Q$ is true.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Disjunctive syllogism"}

In [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), **disjunctive syllogism**[^o3][^o4] (historically known as ***modus tollendo ponens*** (**MTP**),[^o5] [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "mode that affirms by denying")[^o6] is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [argument form](wikipedia://en/3331706) which is a [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046) having a [disjunctive statement](wikipedia://en/7962) for one of its [premises](wikipedia://en/7993337).[^o7][^o8]

:::

An example in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language):

1. I will choose soup or I will choose salad.
1. I will not choose soup.
1. Therefore, I will choose salad.

## Propositional logic {#Propositional_logic}

In [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus), **disjunctive syllogism** (also known as **disjunction elimination** and **or elimination**, or abbreviated **∨E**),[^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13] is a valid [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311). If it is known that at least one of two statements is true, and that it is not the former that is true; we can [infer](wikipedia://en/317465) that it has to be the latter that is true. Equivalently, if *P* is true or *Q* is true and *P* is false, then *Q* is true. The name "disjunctive syllogism" derives from its being a syllogism, a three-step [argument](wikipedia://en/13018310), and the use of a logical disjunction (any "or" statement.) For example, "P or Q" is a disjunction, where P and Q are called the statement's *disjuncts*. The rule makes it possible to eliminate a [disjunction](wikipedia://en/7962) from a [logical proof](wikipedia://en/1197184). It is the rule that

$$\frac{P \lor Q, \neg P}{\therefore Q}$$

where the rule is that whenever instances of "$P \lor Q$", and "$\neg P$" appear on lines of a proof, "$Q$" can be placed on a subsequent line.

Disjunctive syllogism is closely related and similar to [hypothetical syllogism](wikipedia://en/177633), which is another rule of inference involving a syllogism. It is also related to the [law of noncontradiction](wikipedia://en/17636), one of the [three traditional laws of thought](wikipedia://en/1252308#Three_traditional_laws:_identity,_non-contradiction,_excluded_middle).

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

For a [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102) that validates it, the *disjunctive syllogism* may be written in [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) notation as

$$P \lor Q, \lnot P \vdash Q$$

where $\vdash$ is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) symbol meaning that $Q$ is a [syntactic consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) of $P \lor Q$, and $\lnot P$.

It may be expressed as a truth-functional [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) or [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in the object language of propositional logic as

$$((P \lor Q) \land \neg P) \to Q$$

where $P$, and $Q$ are propositions expressed in some [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).

## Natural language examples {#Natural_language_examples}

Here is an example:

1. It is red or it is blue.
1. It is not blue.
1. Therefore, it is red.

Here is another example:

1. The breach is a safety violation, or it is not subject to fines.
1. The breach is not a safety violation.
1. Therefore, it is not subject to fines.

## Strong form {#Strong_form}

*Modus tollendo ponens* can be made stronger by using [exclusive disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_disjunction) instead of inclusive disjunction as a premise:

$$\frac{P \underline\lor Q, \neg P}{\therefore Q}$$

:::

## Related argument forms {#Related_argument_forms}

Unlike *[modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900)* and *[modus ponendo tollens](wikipedia://en/536317)*, with which it should not be confused, disjunctive syllogism is often not made an explicit rule or axiom of [logical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_system), as the above arguments can be proven with a combination of [reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146) and [disjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/8529).

Other forms of syllogism include:

- [hypothetical syllogism](wikipedia://en/177633)
- [categorical syllogism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_syllogism)

Disjunctive syllogism holds in classical propositional logic and [intuitionistic logic](wikipedia://en/169262), but not in some [paraconsistent logics](wikipedia://en/421085).[^o19]
