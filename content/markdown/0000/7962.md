# Logical disjunction

*Image: Venn 0111 1111.svg.* [Venn diagram](wikipedia://en/61701) of $\scriptstyle A \lor B \lor C$

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Logical disjunction"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), **disjunction** (also known as **logical disjunction**, **logical or**, **logical addition**, or **inclusive disjunction**) is a [logical connective](wikipedia://en/18153) typically notated as ∨ and read aloud as "or". For instance, the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) language sentence "it is sunny or it is warm" can be represented in logic using the disjunctive formula $S$ ∨ $W$, assuming that $S$ abbreviates "it is sunny" and $W$ abbreviates "it is warm".

:::

In [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868), disjunction is given a [truth functional](wikipedia://en/604707) semantics according to which a formula $\phi \lor \psi$ is true unless both $\phi$ and $\psi$ are false. Because this semantics allows a disjunctive formula to be true when both of its disjuncts are true, it is an *inclusive* interpretation of disjunction, in contrast with [exclusive disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_disjunction). Classical [proof theoretical](wikipedia://en/183478) treatments are often given in terms of rules such as [disjunction introduction](wikipedia://en/8528) and [disjunction elimination](wikipedia://en/8529). Disjunction has also been given numerous [non-classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonclassical_logic) treatments, motivated by problems including [Aristotle's sea battle argument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle's_sea_battle_argument), [Heisenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg)'s [uncertainty principle](wikipedia://en/31883), as well as the numerous mismatches between classical disjunction and its nearest equivalents in [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173).[^o3][^o4]

An [operand](wikipedia://en/22656) of a disjunction is a **disjunct**.[^o5]

## Inclusive and exclusive disjunction {#Inclusive_and_exclusive_disjunction}

Because the logical *or* means a disjunction formula is true when either one or both of its parts are true, it is referred to as an *inclusive* disjunction. This is in contrast with an [exclusive disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_disjunction), which is true when one or the other of the arguments is true, but not both (referred to as *exclusive or*, or *XOR*).

When it is necessary to clarify whether inclusive or exclusive *or* is intended, English speakers sometimes use the phrase *[and/or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And/or)*. In terms of logic, this phrase is identical to *or*, but makes the inclusion of both being true explicit.

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

In logic and related fields, disjunction is customarily notated with an infix operator ∨ (Unicode 2228).[^o3] Alternative notations include +, used mainly in [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), as well as | and || in many [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015). The English word *or* is sometimes used as well, often in capital letters. In [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064)'s [prefix notation for logic](wikipedia://en/25056#Polish_notation_for_logic), the operator is $A$, short for Polish *alternatywa* (English: alternative).[^o9]

In mathematics, the disjunction of an arbitrary number of elements $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ can be denoted as an [iterated binary operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterated_binary_operation) using a larger ⋁ (Unicode 22C1):[^o10]

$$\bigvee_{i=1}^{n} a_i = a_1 \lor a_2 \lor \ldots a_{n-1} \lor a_{n}$$

:::

## Classical disjunction {#Classical_disjunction}

### Semantics {#Semantics}

In the [semantics of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics_of_logic), classical disjunction is a [truth functional](wikipedia://en/604707) [operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_operation) which returns the [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) *true* unless both of its arguments are *false*. Its semantic entry is standardly given as follows:

$\models \phi \lor \psi$ if $\models \phi$ or $\models \psi$ or both

This semantics corresponds to the following [truth table](wikipedia://en/25512250):[^o3]

0

### Defined by other operators {#Defined_by_other_operators}

In [classical logic](wikipedia://en/55868) systems where logical disjunction is not a primitive, it can be defined in terms of the primitive *[and](wikipedia://en/18152)* (∧) and *[not](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_negation)* (¬) as:

$A$ ∨ $B$ = ¬((¬$A$) ∧ (¬$B$)).

Alternatively, it may be defined in terms of *[implies](wikipedia://en/658808)* ($\to$) and *not* as:[^o14]

$A$ ∨ $B$ = (¬$A$) → $B$.

The latter can be checked by the following truth table:

1

It may also be defined solely in terms of $\to$:

$$A \lor B = (A \to B) \to B.$$

It can be checked by the following truth table:

1

### Properties {#Properties}

The following properties apply to disjunction:

- [Associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity): $a$ ∨ ($b$ ∨ $c$) ≡ ($a$ ∨ $b$) ∨ $c$ **[^o17]**
- [Commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity): $a$ ∨ $b$ ≡ $b$ ∨ $a$
- [Distributivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributivity): ($a$ ∧ ($b$ ∨ $c$)) ≡ (($a$ ∧ $b$) ∨ ($a$ ∧ $c$))

($a$ ∨ ($b$ ∧ $c$)) ≡ (($a$ ∨ $b$) ∧ ($a$ ∨ $c$))

($a$ ∨ ($b$ ∨ $c$)) ≡ (($a$ ∨ $b$) ∨ ($a$ ∨ $c$))

($a$ ∨ ($b$ ≡ $c$)) ≡ (($a$ ∨ $b$) ≡ ($a$ ∨ $c$))

- [Idempotency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotency): $a$ ∨ $a$ ≡ $a$
- [Monotonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity): ($a$ → $b$) → (($c$ ∨ $a$) → ($c$ ∨ $b$))

($a$ → $b$) → (($a$ ∨ $c$) → ($b$ ∨ $c$))

- *Truth-preserving*: The interpretation under which all variables are assigned a [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) of 'true', produces a truth value of 'true' as a result of disjunction.
- *Falsehood-preserving*: The interpretation under which all variables are assigned a [truth value](wikipedia://en/161711) of 'false', produces a truth value of 'false' as a result of disjunction.

:::

## Applications in computer science {#Applications_in_computer_science}

*Image: Or-gate-en.svg.* OR [logic gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_gate)

[Operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28programming%29) corresponding to logical disjunction exist in most [programming languages](wikipedia://en/23015).

### Bitwise operation {#Bitwise_operation}

Disjunction is often used for [bitwise operations](wikipedia://en/264399). Examples:

- 0 or 0 = 0
- 0 or 1 = 1
- 1 or 0 = 1
- 1 or 1 = 1
- 1010 or 1100 = 1110

The `or` operator can be used to set bits in a [bit field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit_field) to 1, by `or`-ing the field with a constant field with the relevant bits set to 1. For example, `x = x | 0b00000001` will force the final bit to 1, while leaving other bits unchanged.

::: {#o21 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Parallel OR"}

### Logical operation {#Logical_operation}

Many languages distinguish between bitwise and logical disjunction by providing two distinct operators; in languages following [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29), [bitwise disjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitwise_disjunction) is performed with the single pipe operator (`|`), and logical disjunction with the double pipe (`||`) operator.

Logical disjunction is usually [short-circuited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-circuit_evaluation); that is, if the first (left) operand evaluates to `true`, then the second (right) operand is not evaluated. The logical disjunction operator thus usually constitutes a [sequence point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_point).

In a parallel (concurrent) language, it is possible to short-circuit both sides: they are evaluated in parallel, and if one terminates with value true, the other is interrupted. This operator is thus called the *parallel or*.

Although the type of a logical disjunction expression is Boolean in most languages (and thus can only have the value `true` or `false`), in some languages (such as [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_programming_language) and [JavaScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript)), the logical disjunction operator returns one of its operands: the first operand if it evaluates to a true value, and the second operand otherwise.[^o22][^o23] This allows it to fulfill the role of the [Elvis operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_operator).

:::

### Constructive disjunction {#Constructive_disjunction}

The [Curry–Howard correspondence](wikipedia://en/254299) relates a [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29) form of disjunction to [tagged union](wikipedia://en/453395) types.[^o25][^o26]

## Set theory {#Set_theory}

The [membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) of an element of a [union set](wikipedia://en/47949) in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) is defined in terms of a logical disjunction: $x\in A\cup B\Leftrightarrow (x\in A)\vee(x\in B)$. Because of this, logical disjunction satisfies many of the same identities as set-theoretic union, such as [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity), [commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity), [distributivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributivity), and [de Morgan's laws](wikipedia://en/64669), identifying [logical conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) with [set intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_intersection), [logical negation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_negation) with [set complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_complement).[^o28]

## Natural language {#Natural_language}

Disjunction in [natural languages](wikipedia://en/21173) does not precisely match the interpretation of ∨ in classical logic. Notably, classical disjunction is inclusive while natural language disjunction is often understood exclusively, as the following English example typically would be.[^o3]

- Mary is eating an apple or a pear.

This inference has sometimes been understood as an [entailment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entailment), for instance by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), who suggested that natural language disjunction is [ambiguous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexical_ambiguity) between a classical and a nonclassical interpretation. More recent work in [pragmatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatics) has shown that this inference can be derived as a [conversational implicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversational_implicature) on the basis of a [semantic](wikipedia://en/31395652) denotation which behaves classically. However, disjunctive constructions including [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language) *vagy... vagy* and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) *soit... soit* have been argued to be inherently exclusive, rendering un[grammaticality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammaticality) in contexts where an inclusive reading would otherwise be forced.[^o3]

Similar deviations from classical logic have been noted in cases such as [free choice disjunction](wikipedia://en/66382863) and [simplification of disjunctive antecedents](wikipedia://en/66659052), where certain [modal operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_modality) trigger a [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152)-like interpretation of disjunction. As with exclusivity, these inferences have been analyzed both as implicatures and as entailments arising from a nonclassical interpretation of disjunction.[^o3]

- You can have an apple or a pear.

$\rightsquigarrow$ You can have an apple and you can have a pear (but you cannot have both)

In many languages, disjunctive expressions play a role in question formation.

- Is Mary a philosopher or a linguist?

For instance, while the above English example can be interpreted as a [polar question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_question) asking whether it's true that Mary is either a philosopher or a linguist, it can also be interpreted as an [alternative question](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_question) asking which of the two professions is hers. The role of disjunction in these cases has been analyzed using nonclassical logics such as [alternative semantics](wikipedia://en/66422858) and [inquisitive semantics](wikipedia://en/58208186), which have also been adopted to explain the free choice and simplification inferences.[^o3]

In English, as in many other languages, disjunction is expressed by a [coordinating conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinating_conjunction). Other languages express disjunctive meanings in a variety of ways, though it is unknown whether disjunction itself is a [linguistic universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_universal). In many languages such as [Dyirbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyirbal_language) and [Maricopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricopa_language), disjunction is marked using a verb [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix). For instance, in the Maricopa example below, disjunction is marked by the suffix *šaa*.[^o3]

## Notes {#Notes}

- [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298), closely following analogy with ordinary mathematics, premised, as a necessary condition to the definition of x + y, that x and y were mutually exclusive. [Jevons](wikipedia://en/207021), and practically all mathematical logicians after him, advocated, on various grounds, the definition of *logical addition* in a form that does not necessitate mutual exclusiveness.
