# Demographics of Canada

::: {#o10 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Canada"}

[Statistics Canada](wikipedia://en/186760) conducts a country-wide census that collects demographic data every five years in the first and sixth year of each decade. The [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census) enumerated a [total population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Canada_by_year) of 36,991,981, an increase of around 5.2 percent over the 2016 figure.[^o11] It is estimated that Canada's population surpassed 40 million in 2023 and 41 million in 2024.[^o12] The main driver of population growth is [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada),[^o13][^o49] with 6.2% of the country's population being made up of [temporary residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_residency_in_Canada) as of 2023,[^o14] or about 2.5 million people.[^o15] In 2025, Canada's population officially stood at 41.5 million, an estimated decrease of 76,068. 2024 saw the lowest recorded total fertility rate in the country's history, at around 1.25; this rate is among the lowest in the world.[^o16]

:::

Canada has one of the highest [per-capita immigration rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_net_migration_rate) in the world,[^o17] driven mainly by [economic policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_immigration_to_Canada) and, to a lesser extent, [family reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_categories_%28Canada%29).[^o18][^o19] In 2024–2025, a total of 435,421 immigrants were admitted to Canada.[^o20] New immigrants to Canada settle mostly in major urban areas such as [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), [Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), and [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver).[^o21] Canada also accepts large numbers of [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee),[^o22] accounting for over 10 percent of annual global [refugee resettlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_country_resettlement).[^o23][^o24]

### Fertility rate {#Fertility_rate}

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) is the number of children born in a specific year cohort to the total number of women who can give birth in the country.

In 1971, the birth rate for the first time dipped below replacement[^o38][^o39] and since then has not rebounded.[^o38]

Canada's [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) hit a record low of 1.25 children born per woman in 2024, maintaining its second year in a row with 'ultra-low fertility' (\<1.30).[^o40] In 2022, Canada also experienced the country's lowest absolute number of births in 17 years.[^o41] From 2021 to 2022, Canada's TFR decreased by −7.4%, its largest drop in 50 years.[^o42] The total birth rate is 10.17 births/1,000 population in 2022.[^o29]

### Population projection {#Population_projection}

According to the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development) (OECD)/[World Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank), the population of Canada increased from 1990 to 2008 by 5.6 million, a 20.4% growth in population, compared to 21.7% growth in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), 31.2% growth in [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico), 8% in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_France#Vital_statistics) and 27% or 1,423 million people [globally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population).[^o53] From 1991 to 2011, the population of the [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_United_Kingdom#Population_change_over_time) increased by 10%.

The population growth rate for Canada in 2022 was 0.75%.[^o29]

## Ethnicity and visible minorities {#Ethnicity_and_visible_minorities}

### Canadians as ethnic group by province {#Canadians_as_ethnic_group_by_province}

All citizens of Canada are classified as "Canadians" as defined by [Canada's nationality laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_nationality_law). ["Canadian" as an ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_ethnicity) has since 1996 been added to census questionnaires for possible ancestral origin or descent. "Canadian" was included as an example on the English questionnaire and "Canadien" as an example on the French questionnaire.[^o78] The majority of respondents to this selection are from the eastern part of the country that was first settled. Respondents generally are visibly European (Anglophones and Francophones) and no longer self-identify directly with their ethnic ancestral origins. This response is attributed to a multitude of reasons such as generational distance from ancestral lineage, intermarriage with a variety of ethnic groups, and loss of ancestral language.[^o79][^o80]

According to the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census), over 450 "[ethnic or cultural origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origins_of_people_in_Canada)" were self-reported by Canadians.[^o83] The major [panethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panethnicity) groups chosen were: [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) ( 19062115 ), [North American](wikipedia://en/21139) ( 8329945 ), [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Asia) ( 7013835 ), [North American Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) ( 2204475 ), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_of_Africa) ( 1394170 ), [Latin, Central and South American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America) ( 900495 ), [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_people) ( 774515 ), [Oceanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanian_people) ( 105010 ), and Other ( 2163380 ).[^o83][^o84] Statistics Canada reports that 35.5% of the population reported multiple ethnic origins, thus the overall total is greater than 100%.[^o83]

The country's ten largest self-reported specific ethnic or cultural origins in 2021 were [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_ethnicity) (accounting for 15.6 percent of the population), followed by [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadians) (14.7 percent), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Canadians) (12.1 percent), [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Canadians) (12.1 percent), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians) (11.0 percent), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Canadians) (8.1 percent), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Canadians) (5.1 percent), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadians) (4.7 percent), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Canadians) (4.3 percent), and [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians) (3.5 percent).[^o85]

Of the 36.3 million people enumerated in 2021 approximately 25.4 million reported being "[White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Canadians)", representing 69.8 percent of the population.[^o9][^o87][^o369] The Indigenous population representing 5 percent or 1.8 million individuals, grew by 9.4 percent compared to the non-Indigenous population, which grew by 5.3 percent from 2016 to 2021.[^o86] One out of every four Canadians or 26.5 percent of the population belonged to a non-White and non-Indigenous [visible minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_minority),[^o369] the largest of which in 2021 were [South Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians) (2.6 million people; 7.1 percent), Chinese (1.7 million; 4.7 percent) and [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canadians) (1.5 million; 4.3 percent).[^o87]

As data is completely self-reported, and reporting individuals may have varying definitions of "Ethnic origin" (or may not know their ethnic origin), these figures should not be considered an exact record of the relative prevalence of different ethno-cultural ancestries but rather how Canadians self-identify.

### Future projections {#Future_projections}

Statistics Canada projects that visible minorities will make up between 38.2% and 43.0% of the total Canadian population by 2041,[^o370][^o371] compared with 26.5% in 2021.[^o161][^o369] Among the working-age population (15 to 64 years), meanwhile, visible minorities are projected to represent between 42.1% and 47.3% of Canada's total population,[^o370][^o371] compared to 28.5% in 2021.[^o161][^o369]

## Immigration {#Immigration}

According to the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census), immigrants in Canada number 8.3 million persons and make up approximately 23 percent of Canada's total population.[^o161] This represents the eighth-largest [immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration) population in the world, while the proportion represents one of the highest ratios for [industrialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialized_world) [Western countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world).[^o364]

Immigrants from specific countries are divided into several ethnic groups. For example, there are both Punjabis and [Muhajirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhajir_%28Pakistan%29) from [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan), both Turks and Kurds from [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) and both Sinhalese and Tamil from [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka). Immigrants from [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran) are divided into [Mazandaranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaranis), [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians), [Azeris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis), Persians, Kurds, Gilaks and [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurs).[^o365]

Since [confederation in 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) through to the contemporary era, [decadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decade) and demi-decadal census reports have detailed immigration statistics. During this period, the highest annual immigration rate in Canada occurred in 1913, when 400,900 new immigrants accounted for 5.3 percent of the total population,[^o366][^o367] while the greatest number of foreign-born individuals admitted to Canada in single year occurred in 2021, with 405,330 new immigrants accounting for 1.1 percent of the total population.

Statistics Canada projects that immigrants will represent between 29.1% and 34.0% of Canada's population in 2041, compared with 23.0% in 2021,[^o368] while the Canadian population with at least one foreign born parent ([first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_generations#First_generation) and [second generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_generations#Second_generation) persons) could rise to between 49.8% and 54.3%, up from 44.0% in 2021.[^o369][^o370][^o371]

## Religion {#Religion}

In 2021, 53.3% of Canadians were [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians),[^o386] down from 67.3% in 2011.[^o387] 29.9% were [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) while 11.4% were [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) (all other listed denominations excluding Christian Orthodox, Latter Day Saints and Jehovah's Witnesses). 7.6% were Christian not otherwise specified, 2.1% were "other Christian and Christian-related traditions", 1.7% were [Christian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church), 0.4% were [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) and 0.2% were [Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement) adherents.

\34.6% of Canadians were non-religious or secular, up from 23.9% in 2011. Of the non-Christian religions listed, 4.9% of Canadians were [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Canada) (3.2% in 2011), 2.3% were [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Canada) (1.5% in 2011), 2.1% were [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Canada) (1.4% in 2011), 1.0% were [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Canada) (1.1% in 2011), 0.9% were [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) (1.0% in 2011), 0.2% were believers of traditional (North American Indigenous) spirituality (same as 2011), and 0.6% were believers of other religions and spiritual traditions (0.4% in 2011).
