# Standard deviation line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard deviation line"}

*Image: SD line vs regression line.png.* Plot of the standard deviation line (SD line), dashed, and the regression line, solid, for a scatter diagram of 20 points.

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **standard deviation line** (or SD line) marks points on a [scatter plot](wikipedia://en/412544) that are an equal number of [standard deviations](wikipedia://en/27590) away from the average in each dimension. For example, in a 2-dimensional scatter diagram with variables $x$ and $y$, points that are 1 standard deviation away from the mean of $x$ and also 1 standard deviation away from the mean of $y$ are on the SD line.[^o2] The SD line is a useful visual tool since points in a scatter diagram tend to cluster around it,[^o2] more or less tightly depending on their [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

### Relation to regression line {#Relation_to_regression_line}

The SD line goes through the point of averages and has a slope of $\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$ when the correlation between $x$ and $y$ is positive, and $-\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$ when the correlation is negative.[^o2][^o6] Unlike the [regression line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regression_line), the SD line does not take into account the relationship between $x$ and $y$.[^o7] The slope of the SD line is related to that of the regression line by $a = r \frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$ where $a$ is the slope of the regression line, $r$ is the [correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/29018709), and $\frac{\sigma_y}{\sigma_x}$ is the magnitude of the slope of the SD line.[^o6]

### Typical distance of points to SD line {#Typical_distance_of_points_to_SD_line}

The [root mean square](wikipedia://en/66819) vertical distance of points from the SD line is $\sqrt{2(1 - |r|)} \times\sigma_y$.[^o2] This gives an idea of the spread of points around the SD line.
