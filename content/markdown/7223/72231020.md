# Mikhail Birman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mikhail Birman"}

**Mikhail Shlyomovich Birman** (Russian: Михаил Шлёмович Бирман; born 17 January 1928 in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad); died 2 July 2009) was a Russian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and university professor. His research included [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). In particular, he did research in the fields of [scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory), operators in [Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_spaces) and the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) of [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886).[^o2]

:::

Together with [Mikhail Zakharovich Solomyak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Zakharovich_Solomyak) he developed the theory of [double operator integrals](wikipedia://en/73052423).

## Life {#Life}

Birman was born in 1928 in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad) (now St. Petersburg) to a father that was a professor and specialist in [theoretical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_mechanics) and a mother that was a school teacher. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) the family fled to [Sverdlovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg) (now Yekaterinburg). After the war the family went back to Leningrad. In 1950 Birman graduated from the Mathematical-Mechanical Faculty of [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) with a diploma. Although Birman was one of the best students, he was denied a doctorate at Leningrad University because of his [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) origins and the state [antisemitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) policy of the time. In 1947 he married Tatyana Petrovna Ilyina. Eventually in 1954 he did his doctorate at the [Leningrad Mining University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Mining_University).

In 1956 he joined the Department of Mathematical Physics at the Physics Faculty of Leningrad University. In 1962 he received a [Sc.D. (Doctor of Science)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences) for his work *The Spectrum of Singular Boundary Value Problems*. In 1974 he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver), but was prohibited to leave the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).

Birman was the supervisor for more than 20 students, including Boris Pavlov and Timo Weidl. He was also editor of the Russian mathematics journals "Algebra i Analiz" and "Funktsional'Nyi Analiz i Ego Prilozheniya". He remained in the Department of Mathematical Physics at Saint Petersburg University until his death in 2009.[^o2]

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

Birman wrote three monographs, six books and over 160 scholarly papers.
