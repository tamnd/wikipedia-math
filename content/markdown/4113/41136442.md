# Abraham Stern (inventor)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Abraham Stern"}

*Image: Abraham Stern a Varsovie.jpg.* Abraham Stern demonstrating one of his calculating machines in Warsaw

**Abraham Jacob Stern** (אברהם יעקב שטערן; 1762 or 1769 – 3 February 1842) was a [Polish Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Jewish) *[maskil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskalah)*, inventor, educator, and poet. He is known for his [mechanical calculators](wikipedia://en/432897).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Abraham Stern was born in [Hrubieszow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrubieszow) to poor [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) parents Miriam Liba (Halpern) and Menachem Mendel Stern (Morgenstern). He received a traditional [Jewish education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheder), and trained as a [watchmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchmaker). His natural aptitude in the study of philosophy, languages, and mathematics was discovered by reformer [Stanisław Staszic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Staszic), who invited him to [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) to continue his studies.

His first major invention was a [mechanical calculator](wikipedia://en/432897), perfected in 1817, which could calculate the [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208) of numbers. This attracted wide attention, and led to his being elected in 1817 the first Jewish member of the [Warsaw Society of the Friends of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Society_of_Friends_of_Learning). In 1816, and again in 1818, he was presented to Tsar [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia), who granted him an annual pension of 350 [rubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruble) from the state treasury, promising, in case of his death, to pay half of this sum to his widow. Encouraged by his friends, Stern developed a [topographical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) wagon for the measurement of level surfaces, an invention of great value to both civil and military engineers. The committee appointed by the academy to examine this invention reported very favorably upon it. Stern rendered great services to agriculture by his improvements in the construction of [thrashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshing_machine) and [harvesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest) machines, as well as by his invention of a new form of [sickle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sickle).

Stern always remained an [Orthodox Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism); he wore a *[kippah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kippah)* in the presence of his eminent friends, and when staying in the castle of [Adam Czartoryski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Jerzy_Czartoryski), a Jewish cook prepared his meals. He was also a known opponent of [Hasidic Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasidic_Judaism).[^o6] Stern took an active interest in educational affairs, and accepted the post of inspector of Jewish schools and that of [censor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_the_Russian_Empire) of Hebrew texts. The [rabbinical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva) in Warsaw was organized according to the plan suggested by him while a member of the Komitet Starozakonnych (Jewish Advisory Council to the Committee for Jewish Affairs).

His official duties, however, did not prevent him from making contributions to [Hebrew literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_literature). He wrote an ode in honor of the [coronation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_the_Russian_monarch) of [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia), which appeared in Hebrew under the title "Rinnah u-tefillah" ("Song and Prayer," 1925) and was translated into Polish by J. Gluegenberg (Warsaw, 1829). He wrote also "Shirim" ("Poems"), which appeared in the *Shire musar haskel* collection (Warsaw, 1835).
