# Friedman translation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedman translation"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the **Friedman translation** is a certain transformation of [formulas](wikipedia://en/404582). Among other things it can be used to show that the [Π<sup>0</sup><sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/186475)-theorems of various [first-order theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theories) of [classical mathematics](wikipedia://en/55868) are also theorems of [intuitionistic](wikipedia://en/169262) mathematics. It is named after its discoverer, [Harvey Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *A* and *B* be formulas, where no [free variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) of *B* is [quantified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_variable) in *A*. The translation *A<sup>B</sup>* is defined by replacing each [atomic](wikipedia://en/4472066) [subformula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subformula) *C* of *A* by *C* ∨ *B*. For purposes of the translation, ⊥ is considered to be an atomic formula as well; hence it is replaced with ⊥ ∨ *B* (which is equivalent to *B*). The translation is relevant in an intuitionistic context and here ¬*A* is generally defined as an abbreviation for *A* → ⊥; hence (¬*A*)<sup>*B*</sup> = *A<sup>B</sup>* → *B*.

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

The Friedman translation can be used to show the closure of many intuitionistic theories under the [Markov rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_rule), and to obtain [partial conservativity](wikipedia://en/1840214) results. The key condition is that the $\Delta^0_0$-sentences of the logic be [decidable](wikipedia://en/913118), allowing the unquantified theorems of the intuitionistic and classical theories to coincide.

For example, if *A* is provable in [Heyting arithmetic](wikipedia://en/2545815) (HA), then *A<sup>B</sup>* is also provable in HA.[^o6] Moreover, if *A* is a [Σ<sup>0</sup><sub>1</sub>-formula](wikipedia://en/186475), then *A<sup>B</sup>* is in HA equivalent to *A* ∨ *B*. By setting *B* = *A*, this implies that:

- Heyting arithmetic is closed under the primitive recursive Markov rule (MP<sub>PR</sub>): if the formula ¬¬*A* is provable in HA, where *A* is a Σ<sup>0</sup><sub>1</sub>-formula, then *A* is also provable in HA.
- [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) is Π<sup>0</sup><sub>2</sub>-conservative over Heyting arithmetic: if Peano arithmetic proves a Π<sup>0</sup><sub>2</sub>-formula *A*, then *A* is already provable in HA.

:::
