# Jenő Egerváry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jenő Egerváry"}

**Jenő Elek Egerváry** (April 16, 1891 – November 30, 1958) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Egerváry was born in [Debrecen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrecen) in 1891. In 1914, he received his doctorate at the [Pázmány Péter University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in Budapest, where he studied under the supervision of [Lipót Fejér](wikipedia://en/3934357). He then worked as an assistant at the Seismological Observatory in Budapest, and since 1918 as a professor at the Superior Industrial School in Budapest. In 1938 he was appointed [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at the Pázmány Péter University in Budapest.[^n1]

In 1941 he became a full professor at the [Technical University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Budapest), and in 1950 he was appointed Chairman of the Scientific Council of the [Research Institute for Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^n2]

Egerváry received the [Gyula Kőnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Kőnig) Prize in 1932 and the [Kossuth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kossuth_Prize) in 1949 and 1953.[^n2]

He committed suicide in 1958 because of the troubles caused to him by the communist bureaucracy.[^o7]

## Works {#Works}

Egerváry's interests spanned the [theory of algebraic equations](wikipedia://en/583600), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), and [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/20556859).[^n1]

In what later became a classic result in the field of [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555),[^o10] Egerváry generalized [Kőnig's theorem](wikipedia://en/5465118) to the case of [weighted graphs](wikipedia://en/325806#Weighted_graph).[^o11] This contribution was translated and published in 1955 by [Harold W. Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_W._Kuhn),[^o12] who also showed how to apply [Kőnig](wikipedia://en/1327120)'s and Egerváry's method to solve the [assignment problem](wikipedia://en/140592); the resulting algorithm has since been known as the "[Hungarian method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_method)".[^o13]
