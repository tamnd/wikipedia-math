# Lissajous-toric knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lissajous-toric knot"}

*Image: Lissajous toric knot 5 6 22.png.* Lissajous-toric knot with parameters 5, 6 and 22 in braid form (with z-axis in horizontal direction)

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), a **Lissajous-toric knot** is a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) defined by [parametric equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_equations) of the form:

:::

$$x(t)=(2+\sin qt)\cos Nt, \qquad y(t)=(2+\sin qt)\sin Nt, \qquad z(t)=\cos p(t+\phi),$$

where $N$, $p$, and $q$ are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), the [phase shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_shift) $\phi$ is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) and the parameter $t$ varies between 0 and $2\pi$.[^o2]

For $p=q$ the knot is a [torus knot](wikipedia://en/1165182).

## Braid and billiard knot definitions {#Braid_and_billiard_knot_definitions}

*Image: Lissajous-toric-knot T 4 7 35.png.* Lissajous-toric knot T(4,7,35) as a billiard knot, showing period 7

In [braid](wikipedia://en/550138) form these knots can be defined in a square solid torus (i.e. the cube $[-1,1]^3$ with identified top and bottom) as

$$x(t)=\sin 2\pi qt, \qquad y(t)=\cos 2\pi p(t+\phi), \qquad z(t)=2(N t - \lfloor N t\rfloor )-1, \qquad t \in [0,1].$$

The projection of this Lissajous-toric knot onto the x-y-plane is a [Lissajous curve](wikipedia://en/753756).

Replacing the sine and cosine functions in the parametrization by a [triangle wave](wikipedia://en/52151) transforms a Lissajous-toric knot isotopically into a billiard curve inside the solid torus. Because of this property Lissajous-toric knots are also called billiard knots in a solid torus.[^o4]

Lissajous-toric knots were first studied as billiard knots and they share many properties with billiard knots in a cylinder.[^o5] They also occur in the analysis of singularities of [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) with branch points[^o6] and in the study of the [Three-body problem](wikipedia://en/1208420).[^o7]

The knots in the subfamily with $p = q \cdot l$, with an integer $l \ge 1$, are known as ′Lemniscate knots′.[^o8] Lemniscate knots have period $q$ and are [fibred](wikipedia://en/1358592). The knot shown on the right is of this type (with $l=5$).

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Lissajous-toric knot T 3 8 7.svg.* Symmetries of the Lissajous-toric knot T(3,8,7): symmetric union (vertical axis), rotation into mirror image and palindromic property within Q (horizontal axis)

Lissajous-toric knots are denoted by $K(N,q,p,\phi)$. To ensure that the knot is traversed only once in the parametrization the conditions $\gcd(N,q)=\gcd(N,p)=1$ are needed. In addition, singular values for the phase, leading to self-intersections, have to be excluded.

The isotopy class of Lissajous-toric knots surprisingly does not depend on the phase $\phi$ (up to mirroring). If the distinction between a knot and its mirror image is not important, the notation $K(N,q,p)$ can be used.

The properties of Lissajous-toric knots depend on whether $p$ and $q$ are coprime or $d=\gcd(p,q)>1$. The main properties are:

- Interchanging $p$ and $q$:

$K(N,q,p)=K(N,p,q)$ (up to mirroring).

- Ribbon property:

If $p$ and $q$ are coprime, $K(N,q,p)$ is a symmetric union and therefore a [ribbon knot](wikipedia://en/9809292).

- Periodicity:

If $d=\gcd(p,q)>1$, the Lissajous-toric knot has period $d$ and the factor knot is a ribbon knot.

- Strongly positive amphicheirality:

If $p$ and $q$ have different parity, then $K(N,q,p)$ is strongly positive amphicheiral.

- Period 2:

If $p$ and $q$ are both odd, then $K(N,q,p)$ has period 2 (for even $N$) or is freely 2-periodic (for odd $N$).

The knot T(3,8,7), shown in the graphics, is a symmetric union and a ribbon knot (in fact, it is the composite knot $5_1 \sharp -5_1$). An additional horizontal symmetry occurs as a combination of the vertical symmetry and the rotation (′double palindromicity′ in Kin/Nakamura/Ogawa).

## ′Classification′ of billiard rooms {#′Classification′_of_billiard_rooms}

In the following table a systematic overview of the possibilities to build billiard rooms from the interval and the circle (interval with identified boundaries) is given:

::: {#o14 .table cue=table}

| Billiard room | Billiard knots |
|---|---|
| $[-1,1]^3$ | [Lissajous knots](wikipedia://en/12328965) |
| $[-1,1]^2 \times \mathbb{S}^1$ | Lissajous-toric knots |
| $[-1,1] \times \mathbb{S}^1 \times \mathbb{S}^1$ | Torus knots |
| $\mathbb{S}^1 \times \mathbb{S}^1 \times \mathbb{S}^1$ | (room not embeddable into $\mathbb{R}^3$) |

:::

In the case of Lissajous knots reflections at the boundaries occur in all of the three cube's dimensions. In the second case reflections occur in two dimensions and we have a uniform movement in the third dimension. The third case is nearly equal to the usual movement on a torus, with an additional triangle wave movement in the first dimension.
