# Giovanni Camillo Glorioso

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giovanni Camillo Glorioso"}

**Giovanni Camillo Glorioso** (or **Gloriosi**) (1572 – 8 January 1643) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer). He was a friend of [Marino Ghetaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marino_Ghetaldi) and [Galileo Galilei](wikipedia://en/29688374)'s successor as professor of mathematics at [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Giovanni Camillo Glorioso was born in the village of [Montecorvino Rovella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montecorvino_Rovella), near [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno).[^n1] He earned degrees in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) from the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II) and studied mathematics with [Vincenzo Filliucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Filliucci) and Giovanni Giacomo Staserio at the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) college in Naples.[^n1] He was a friend and correspondent of Galileo Galilei and replaced him as professor of mathematics at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) in 1613.[^n1] He became famous for his observations of the comet of 1618, of [Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars), and of [Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn). He was a close friend of the mathematician Antonio Santini (1577-1662) and was involved in a series of bitter arguments with the [Aristotelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism) philosophers [Scipione Chiaramonti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipione_Chiaramonti) and [Fortunio Liceti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortunio_Liceti) and the Swiss mathematician [Barthélemy Souvey](wikipedia://en/54059808), who succeeded him in the chair of mathematics at Padua in 1624.[^n1]

Glorioso was particularly harsh in his attack on Scipione Chiaramonti's efforts to defend traditional Aristotelian cosmology. He criticised Chiaramonti's *De tribus novis stellis* and in 1636 Charamonti published a refutation, *Examen censurae Gloriosi*, to which Glorioso replied the following year *Castigatio examinis*. To this Chiaramonti responded in turn with *Castigatio Ioannis Camilli Gloriosi aduersus Scipionem Claramontium Caesenatem* (1638). Glorioso's final contribution to this dispute was his *Responsio* (1641). As he died soon after, this allowed Chiaramonti the last word, which he took with a volume of more than 500 pages, summarising his Aristotelian positions on a wide range of topics, his *Opus Scipionis Claramontis Caesenatis de Universo* (1644).[^o16]

In contrast with Galileo, Glorioso shared [Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe)'s conclusion that [comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet) were [heavenly bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_object), a position in agreement with our modern understanding. In a letter written on 29 May 1610 to his friend [Johann Schreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Schreck), Glorioso attributed the invention of the [sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_%28instrument%29) to [Michiel Coignet](wikipedia://en/45298424) and not to Galileo, although the instrument is now mainly attributed to Coignet's friend [Fabrizio Mordente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_Mordente).[^o17] In the same letter Glorioso also claimed that Galileo did not invent the [telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope), but he did only modify a previous invention by a Belgian scholar.[^n2]

Glorioso was one of the leading italian [algebraists](wikipedia://en/18716923) of the time.[^n3] In his most important work, *Exercitationum Mathematicarum Decades tres* (1627-1639), he confutes the [quadrature of the circle](wikipedia://en/201359) by [Giambattista della Porta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giambattista_della_Porta), comments on [Viète](wikipedia://en/310238), and gives the solution of interesting questions respecting the [theory of numbers](wikipedia://en/21527).[^n4] It's one of the first work to use the notation $Aqqc$ for the equivalent of the current $A^{8}$.

Late in his life Glorioso returned to Naples, where he befriended [Francesco Fontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Fontana). Glorioso encouraged Fontana to devote himself to astronomical research and gave him access to his own library. Glorioso died in Naples on 8 January 1643. After his death, his library was sold to the [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_viceroys_of_Naples) [Ramiro Núñez de Guzmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramiro_Núñez_de_Guzmán).[^n5]
