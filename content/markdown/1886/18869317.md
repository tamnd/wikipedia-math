# Kayles

*Image: Bowling ball and pins for strike - front view.jpg.* A row of bowling pins. On their turn, a player may choose to eliminate a single pin, or two adjacent ones.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kayles"}

**Kayles** is a simple [impartial game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impartial_game) in [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231), invented by [Henry Dudeney](wikipedia://en/465317) in 1908. Given a row of imagined bowling pins, players take turns to knock out either one pin, or two adjacent pins, until all the pins are gone. Using the notation of [octal games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octal_games), Kayles is denoted **\0.77**.

:::

## Rules {#Rules}

Kayles is played with a row of tokens, which represent bowling pins. The row may be of any length. The two players alternate; each player, on his or her turn, may remove either any one pin (a ball bowled directly at that pin), or two adjacent pins (a ball bowled to strike both). Under the [normal play convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_play_convention), a player loses when they have no legal move (that is, when all the pins are gone). The game can also be played using [misère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misère) rules; in this case, the player who cannot move *wins*.

## History {#History}

Kayles was invented by [Henry Dudeney](wikipedia://en/465317).[^o4][^o5] [Richard Guy](wikipedia://en/816308) and [Cedric Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Smith_%28statistician%29) were first to completely analyze the normal-play version, using [Sprague-Grundy theory](wikipedia://en/41372).[^o6][^o7] The misère version was analyzed by [William Sibert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Sibert) in 1973, but he did not publish his work until 1989.[^o8]

The name "Kayles" is an Anglicization of the French *quilles*, meaning "bowling pins".

## Analysis {#Analysis}

Most players quickly discover that the first player has a guaranteed win in normal Kayles whenever the row length is greater than zero. This win can be achieved using a [symmetry strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_strategy). On their first move, the first player should move so that the row is broken into two sections of equal length. This restricts all future moves to one section or the other. Now, the first player merely imitates the second player's moves in the opposite row.

It is more interesting to ask what the [nim-value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nim-value) is of a row of length $n$. This is often denoted $K_n$; it is a [nimber](wikipedia://en/164501), not a [number](wikipedia://en/21690). By the [Sprague–Grundy theorem](wikipedia://en/41372), $K_n$ is the [mex](wikipedia://en/2395424) over all possible moves of the [nim-sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nim-sum) of the [nim-values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nim-value) of the two resulting sections. For example,

$$K_5 = \mbox{mex}\{K_0 + K_4, K_1 + K_3, K_2 + K_2, K_0 + K_3, K_1 + K_2\},\,$$

because from a row of length 5, one can move to the positions

$$K_0 + K_4,\quad K_1 + K_3,\quad K_2 + K_2,\quad K_0 + K_3,\text{ and } K_1 + K_2.\,$$

## Applications {#Applications}

Because certain positions in [dots and boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dots_and_boxes) reduce to Kayles positions,[^o13] it is helpful to understand Kayles in order to analyze a generic dots and boxes position.

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Under normal play, Kayles can be solved [in polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) using Sprague-Grundy theory.[^o6]

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In the generalization of Kayles to [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806), each bowl “knocks down” (removes) a desired vertex and all its neighboring vertices[^o17],[^o18]. Alternatively, this game can be viewed as two players finding an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) together. Winner determination of the normal variant (the last to play wins) is solvable in polynomial time for any family of graphs with bounded asteroidal number (defined as the size of a largest subset of vertices such that the removal of the closed neighborhood of any vertex in the set leaves the remaining vertices of the set in the same connected component).[^o18]

Similarly, in the *clique-forming* game, two players must find a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) in the graph. In the normal variant (the last to play wins), Schaefer[^o19] proved in 1978 that deciding the outcome of these games is [PSPACE-complete](wikipedia://en/54685) (the same holds for the partisan versions, in which, for every vertex, only one of the players is allowed to choose it as the knock down target). The misère variants of these game were proved [PSPACE-complete](wikipedia://en/54685) in 2024 [^o20] and the optimization variants in 2025 [^o21].
