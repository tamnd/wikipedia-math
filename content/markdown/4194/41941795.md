# Clarence Lemuel Elisha Moore

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clarence Lemuel Elisha Moore"}

**Clarence Lemuel Elisha Moore** (12 May 1876, in [Bainbridge, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bainbridge,_Ross_County,_Ohio) – 5 December 1931) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) mathematics professor, specializing in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243). He is chiefly remembered for the memorial eponymous [C. L. E. Moore instructorship](wikipedia://en/21957255) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology); this prestigious instructorship has produced many famous mathematicians, including four [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) winners: [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen_%28mathematician%29), [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519), [Curtis T. McMullen](wikipedia://en/680168), and [Akshay Venkatesh](wikipedia://en/6647964).

:::

C. L. E. Moore received his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) from [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) (1901) and then his [A.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) (1902) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) (1904) from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). His doctoral dissertation was entitled *Classification of the surfaces of singularities of the quadratic spherical complex* with [Virgil Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_Snyder) as thesis advisor. Moore also studied geometry at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin) with [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061), and the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) with [Eduard Study](wikipedia://en/663997). In 1904, Moore joined the MIT mathematics department as an instructor and was successively promoted to assistant professor, associate professor, and full professor. In 1920, he was one of the founders of the [*MIT Journal of Mathematics and Physics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studies_in_Applied_Mathematics). He remained at MIT until his death in 1931 following a surgical operation.[^o3]
