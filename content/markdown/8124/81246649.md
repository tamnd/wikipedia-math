# 2002 Serbian Census

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="2002 Serbian Census"}

The **2002 Serbian census** was conducted on 1 April 2002 by the [Statistical Office of the Republic of Serbia](wikipedia://en/50755624). It was the first [census](wikipedia://en/6889) held in Serbia after the breakup of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia) and the last before the separation of [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro) in 2006. The census counted a total population of approximately **\7.5 million**.[^o3]

:::

## Background {#Background}

Following the 1990s conflicts in the [Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans) and the dissolution of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia), [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia) carried out its first independent census in 2002. The census provided updated demographic data after more than a decade without reliable statistics. Due to political instability, [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo) was not included in the census count.[^o5]

### Population by religion {#Population_by_religion}

The census also collected data on religious affiliation:[^o3]

- [Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) – 85%
- [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) – 5%
- [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Serbia) – 3%
- [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Serbia) – 1%
- Other or undeclared – 6%

### Population by language {#Population_by_language}

The most common mother tongues were Serbian, Hungarian, Bosnian, Romani, Slovak, Romanian, and Rusyn.[^o3]
