# Luna Lomonaco

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Luna Lomonaco"}

**Luciana Luna Anna Lomonaco** is an Italian mathematician specialized in [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) recognized by her works in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). She is the first woman to receive the [Brazilian Mathematical Society Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Mathematical_Society_Award). This honor was achieved by working on [mandelbrot sets](wikipedia://en/19562).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Lumonaco was born in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan), Italy. Her family moved from Milan to [Peschiera del Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peschiera_del_Garda).[^o4][^o5]

Lumonaco studied in a school focus on [human sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_science). She studied [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language), [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). And because of philosophy she then studied mathematics, because, to her, the problems of mathematics could trigger important thoughts.[^o6]

## Academic formation and career {#Academic_formation_and_career}

Lomonaco graduated in mathematics from the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), Italy. Then, she earn her master's degree from the [Complutense University of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid), Spain, and her PhD's title from [Roskilde University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_University), Denmark,[^o8] supervised by Carsten Lunde Petersen.[^o4]

After her PhD, she recognized gaps in her formation. Then, she obtained an opportunity to study in the [Chinese Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences), where she began to recognize herself professionally. Still in China, an American professor recommended the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo).[^o4]

She arrived in Brazil on 24 April 2014 and became a professor at the [Institute of Mathematics and Statistics, University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematics_and_Statistics,_University_of_São_Paulo).[^o6] Lumonaco taught a course of complex dynamic systems.[^o4]

In 2019, she became the first woman to ever receive the Brazilian Mathematical Society Award with the work *On Quasi-Conformal (In-)Compatibility of Satellite Copies of the Mandelbrot Set: I*, published in 2017.[^o9]

She served as teacher at USP until 2020, when she went to the *[Instituto Nacional de Matemática Pura e Aplicada](wikipedia://en/2334708)*. With Carolina Araujo, they were the only woman in the institute where 47 researchers worked.[^o10]

Lomonaco is interested in [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518) and mandelbrot sets, but also dedicates herself to scientific dissemination and to disseminate mathematics to women.[^o11]

## Awards {#Awards}

- 2018: *Prêmio Para Mulheres na Ciência.*[^o13]
- 2019: Brazilian Mathematical Society Award.[^o14]
- 2020: *Prêmio de Reconhecimento União Matemática da América Latina e Caribe (Umalca).*[^o10]
- 2023: *Prêmio Jovem Cientista.*[^o15]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- 2015: *Parabolic-like mappings.*[^o17]
- 2017: *On parabolic external maps.*[^o18]
- 2017: *On quasi-conformal (in-) compatibility of satellite copies of the Mandelbrot set: I.*[^o19]
- 2019: *Mating quadratic maps with the modular group II.*[^o20]
- 2022: *Dynamics of modular matings.*[^o21]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Lomonaco appreciates sea baths and practices singing. She is a fan of [Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimamanda_Ngozi_Adichie)'s books.[^o8]
