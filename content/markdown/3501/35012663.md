# Equidiagonal quadrilateral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equidiagonal quadrilateral"}

*Image: Equidiagonal quadrilateral.svg.* An equidiagonal quadrilateral, showing its equal diagonals, Varignon rhombus, and perpendicular bimedians

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), an **equidiagonal quadrilateral** is a [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) whose two [diagonals](wikipedia://en/349251) have equal length. Equidiagonal quadrilaterals were important in ancient [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052), where quadrilaterals were classified first according to whether they were equidiagonal and then into more specialized types.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

Examples of equidiagonal quadrilaterals include the [isosceles trapezoids](wikipedia://en/511691), [rectangles](wikipedia://en/70048) and [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29).

*Image: Reuleaux kite.svg.* An equidiagonal kite that maximizes the ratio of perimeter to diameter, inscribed in a [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252)

Among all quadrilaterals, the shape that has the greatest ratio of its [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) to its [diameter](wikipedia://en/68283333) is an equidiagonal [kite](wikipedia://en/84840) with angles π/3, 5π/12, 5π/6, and 5π/12.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A convex quadrilateral is equidiagonal if and only if its [Varignon parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varignon_parallelogram), the parallelogram formed by the midpoints of its sides, is a [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884). An equivalent condition is that the [bimedians](wikipedia://en/25278#Special_line_segments) of the quadrilateral (the diagonals of the Varignon parallelogram) are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944).[^o13]

A convex quadrilateral with diagonal lengths $p$ and $q$ and bimedian lengths $m$ and $n$ is equidiagonal if and only if[^o10]

$$pq=m^2+n^2.$$

:::

## Area {#Area}

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) *K* of an equidiagonal quadrilateral can easily be calculated if the length of the [bimedians](wikipedia://en/25278#Special_line_segments) *m* and *n* are known. A quadrilateral is equidiagonal if and only if[^o9] [^o10]

$$\displaystyle K=mn.$$

This is a direct consequence of the fact that the area of a convex quadrilateral is twice the area of its Varignon parallelogram and that the diagonals in this parallelogram are the bimedians of the quadrilateral. Using the formulas for the [lengths of the bimedians](wikipedia://en/25278#Bimedians), the area can also be expressed in terms of the sides *a, b, c, d* of the equidiagonal quadrilateral and the distance *x* between the [midpoints](wikipedia://en/437979) of the diagonals as[^o9]

$$K=\tfrac{1}{4}\sqrt{(2(a^2+c^2)-4x^2)(2(b^2+d^2)-4x^2)}.$$

Other area formulas may be obtained from setting *p* = *q* in the formulas for the [area of a convex quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278#Area_of_a_convex_quadrilateral).

## Relation to other types of quadrilaterals {#Relation_to_other_types_of_quadrilaterals}

A [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) is equidiagonal if and only if it is a rectangle,[^o12] and a [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) is equidiagonal if and only if it is an [isosceles trapezoid](wikipedia://en/511691). The [cyclic](wikipedia://en/194131) equidiagonal quadrilaterals are exactly the isosceles trapezoids.

There is a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) between equidiagonal quadrilaterals and [orthodiagonal quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/30425383): a quadrilateral is equidiagonal if and only if its Varignon parallelogram is orthodiagonal (a rhombus), and the quadrilateral is orthodiagonal if and only if its Varignon parallelogram is equidiagonal (a rectangle).[^o13] Equivalently, a quadrilateral has equal diagonals if and only if it has perpendicular bimedians, and it has perpendicular diagonals if and only if it has equal bimedians.[^o14] (Silvester 2006) gives further connections between equidiagonal and orthodiagonal quadrilaterals, via a generalization of [van Aubel's theorem](wikipedia://en/3021425).[^o16]

Quadrilaterals that are both orthodiagonal and equidiagonal are called [midsquare quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/45005169) because they are the only ones for which the [Varignon parallelogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varignon_parallelogram) (with vertices at the midpoints of the quadrilateral's sides) is a square.[^o10]
