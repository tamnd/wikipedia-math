# Semigroup with three elements

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semigroup with three elements"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **[semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) with three elements** is an object consisting of three elements and an [associative operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_operation) defined on them. The basic example would be the three integers 0, 1, and −1, together with the operation of multiplication. Multiplication of integers is associative, and the product of any two of these three integers is again one of these three integers.

:::

There are 18 inequivalent ways to define an associative operation on three elements: while there are, altogether, a total of 3<sup>9</sup> = 19683 different binary operations that can be defined, only 113 of these are associative, and many of these are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) or [antiisomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiisomorphic) so that there are essentially only 18 possibilities.[^o2][^o3]

One of these is C<sub>3</sub>, the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) with three elements. The others all have a [semigroup with two elements](wikipedia://en/22717084) as [subsemigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsemigroup). In the example above, the set {−1,0,1} under multiplication contains both {0,1} and {−1,1} as subsemigroups (the latter is a sub*group*, [C<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/52327)).

Six of these are [bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_%28mathematics%29), meaning that all three elements are [idempotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent), so that the product of any element with itself is itself again. Two of these bands are [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative), therefore [semilattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semilattices) (one of them is the three-element totally ordered set, and the other is a three-element semilattice that is not a lattice). The other four come in anti-isomorphic pairs.

One of these non-commutative bands results from adjoining an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) to LO<sub>2</sub>, the [left zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_zero_semigroup) with two elements (or, dually, to RO<sub>2</sub>, the [right zero semigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_zero_semigroup)). It is sometimes called the **flip-flop monoid**, referring to [flip-flop circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip-flop_%28electronics%29) used in electronics: the three elements can be described as "set", "reset", and "do nothing". This semigroup occurs in the [Krohn–Rhodes decomposition](wikipedia://en/513506) of finite semigroups.[^o4] The irreducible elements in this decomposition are the [finite simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_groups) plus this three-element semigroup, and its subsemigroups.

There are two [cyclic semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_semigroup), one described by the equation *x*<sup>4</sup> = *x*<sup>3</sup>, which has O<sub>2</sub>, the [null semigroup](wikipedia://en/22694038) with two elements, as a subsemigroup. The other is described by *x*<sup>4</sup> = *x*<sup>2</sup> and has C<sub>2</sub>, the group with two elements, as a subgroup. (The equation *x*<sup>4</sup> = *x* describes C<sub>3</sub>, the group with three elements, already mentioned.)

There are seven other non-cyclic non-band commutative semigroups, including the initial example of {−1, 0, 1}, and O<sub>3</sub>, the null semigroup with three elements. There are also two other anti-isomorphic pairs of non-commutative non-band semigroups.
