# Reductive Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reductive Lie algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is **reductive** if its [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_endomorphism) is [completely reducible](wikipedia://en/318712), hence the name. More concretely, a Lie algebra is reductive if it is a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_Lie_algebras) of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) and an [abelian Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_Lie_algebra): $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{s} \oplus \mathfrak{a};$ there are alternative characterizations, given below.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The most basic example is the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{gl}_n$ of $n \times n$ matrices with the commutator as Lie bracket, or more abstractly as the [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) algebra of an *n*-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), $\mathfrak{gl}(V).$ This is the Lie algebra of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(*n*), and is reductive as it decomposes as $\mathfrak{gl}_n = \mathfrak{sl}_n \oplus \mathfrak{k},$ corresponding to [traceless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traceless) matrices and [scalar matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_matrices).

Any [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) or [abelian Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_Lie_algebra) is *a fortiori* reductive.

Over the real numbers, [compact Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/25262324) are reductive.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over a field of characteristic 0 is called reductive if any of the following equivalent conditions are satisfied:

1. The [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group) (the action by bracketing) of $\mathfrak{g}$ is [completely reducible](wikipedia://en/1337587) (a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_representations) of irreducible representations).
1. $\mathfrak{g}$ admits a faithful, completely reducible, finite-dimensional representation.
1. The [radical](wikipedia://en/11484689) of $\mathfrak{g}$ equals the center: $\mathfrak{r}(\mathfrak{g}) = \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}).$

The radical always contains the center, but need not equal it.

1. $\mathfrak{g}$ is the direct sum of a semisimple ideal $\mathfrak{s}_0$ and its center $\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}):$ $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{s}_0 \oplus \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}).$

Compare to the [Levi decomposition](wikipedia://en/2774198), which decomposes a Lie algebra as its radical (which is solvable, not abelian in general) and a Levi subalgebra (which is semisimple).

1. $\mathfrak{g}$ is a direct sum of a semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{s}$ and an abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak{a}$: $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{s} \oplus \mathfrak{a}.$
1. $\mathfrak{g}$ is a direct sum of prime ideals: $\mathfrak{g} = \textstyle{\sum \mathfrak{g}_i}.$

Some of these equivalences are easily seen. For example, the center and radical of $\mathfrak{s} \oplus \mathfrak{a}$ is $\mathfrak{a},$ while if the radical equals the center the Levi decomposition yields a decomposition $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{s}_0 \oplus \mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}).$ Further, simple Lie algebras and the trivial 1-dimensional Lie algebra $\mathfrak{k}$ are prime ideals.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Reductive Lie algebras are a generalization of semisimple Lie algebras, and share many properties with them: many properties of semisimple Lie algebras depend only on the fact that they are reductive. Notably, the [unitarian trick](wikipedia://en/1569065) of [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) works for reductive Lie algebras.

The associated [reductive Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_Lie_group) are of significant interest: the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) is based on the premise that what is done for one reductive Lie group should be done for all.

The intersection of reductive Lie algebras and solvable Lie algebras is exactly abelian Lie algebras (contrast with the intersection of semisimple and solvable Lie algebras being trivial).
