# M. S. Bartlett

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="M. S. Bartlett"}

**Maurice Stevenson Bartlett** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (18 June 1910 – 8 January 2002) was an English [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who made particular contributions to the analysis of [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) with [spatial](wikipedia://en/27667) and [temporal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) patterns. He is also known for his work in the theory of [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) and in [multivariate analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_analysis).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London),[^o6] Bartlett was raised in a poor family but won a scholarship to [Latymer Upper School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latymer_Upper_School) in [Hammersmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammersmith), where he was inspired to the study of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) by a chapter in Hall and Knight's *Algebra*. In 1929, he won a scholarship to [Queens' College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens'_College,_Cambridge) where he read [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), graduating with the rank of [wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29). He attended lectures on statistics by [John Wishart](wikipedia://en/944879), on [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) by [Arthur Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Eddington) and on [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) by [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742). In one of his lectures Wishart described his geometric derivation of the [Wishart distribution](wikipedia://en/364996). Overnight Bartlett worked out a proof using [characteristic functions](wikipedia://en/3229401). Bartlett was Wishart's first post-graduate student and they wrote two papers together. This was the beginning of Bartlett's involvement with multivariate analysis. During his Queens years, he [rowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_%28sport%29) for the college.[^o7]

In 1933, Bartlett was recruited by [Egon Pearson](wikipedia://en/1643759) to the new statistics department at [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London). Pearson was already working with [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106). Also in the college were [Ronald A. Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_A._Fisher) and [J. B. S. Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane). Bartlett was stimulated by all of them, most of all by the work of Fisher, criticising some of it (for example, [fiducial inference](wikipedia://en/7044662)) while developing other parts (for example [conditional inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_inference)). Relations between the two men fluctuated; sometimes Bartlett was in Fisher's good books, but often not. In 1934, Bartlett became statistician at the [Imperial Chemical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries) (ICI) agricultural research station at [Jealott's Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jealott's_Hill). Not only did he deal with practical problems but he worked on statistical theory, as well as on problems in [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) but he became interested in the characterisation of [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_%28trait%29). He remembered Jealott's Hill as the best working environment of his career. Bartlett left ICI for the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 1938 but at the outset of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) was mobilised into the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply_%28United_Kingdom%29), conducting [rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket) research alongside [Frank Anscombe](wikipedia://en/15385810), [David Kendall](wikipedia://en/4631370) and [Pat Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Alfred_Pierce_Moran).

After the war Bartlett's renewed Cambridge work focused on [time-series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-series_analysis) and [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895). With [Jo Moyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/José_Enrique_Moyal) he planned a large book on probability, but the collaboration did not work out and Bartlett went ahead and published his own book on stochastic processes. He made a number of visits to the United States. In 1947 he became professor of mathematical statistics at the School of Mathematics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) where he not only developed his interests in [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997) but also served as an able and active administrator. In 1960, he took up the chair of statistics at [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London) before serving the last eight years of his academic life as professor of biomathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). He retired in 1975.

After his retirement Bartlett remained active in statistics, visiting the Institute of Advanced Studies at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) several times. He had married Sheila, daughter of C. E. Chapman, in 1957, the couple parenting a daughter. Bartlett died in [Exmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exmouth,_Devon), [Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon).

Bartlett is known for [Bartlett's method](wikipedia://en/17220224) for estimating [power spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectrum) and [Bartlett's test](wikipedia://en/2223782) for [homoscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoscedasticity).

## Honours {#Honours}

- [Rayleigh Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize), (1933);
- [Guy Medals](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Silver (1952) and Gold (1969) of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664);
- President of the [Manchester Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/15199159), (1959–1960);
- Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), (1961);
- President of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) (1966);
- Honorary Member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute), (1980);
- Foreign Associate of the [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences), (1993);
- [D.Sc.s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.Sc.) from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1966) and the [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull) (1976).
