# Gerd Faltings

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerd Faltings"}

**Gerd Faltings** (; born 28 July 1954) is a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1986 for his [proofs](wikipedia://en/82285) of the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) and several related [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138).[^o7][^o8] He won the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2026 for these achievements.[^o9][^o10]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Gerd Faltings was born on 28 July 1954 in [Gelsenkirchen-Buer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buer,_Germany), [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).[^o12] From 1972 to 1978, Faltings studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). Interrupted by 15 months of obligatory military service, he received his [Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.) in mathematics in 1978.[^o6][^o13][^o14]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

In 1981, he obtained the [venia legendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venia_legendi) in mathematics from the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster), where he was an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor). He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [University of Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wuppertal) from 1982 to 1984[^o16] and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1985 to 1994. In the autumn of 1988 and in the [academic year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_year) 1992–1993, he was a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o17]

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1986[^o18] for proving the [Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/3124804) for [Abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) over [number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822), the [Shafarevich conjecture](wikipedia://en/172199) for Abelian varieties over number fields and the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture),[^o19][^o20] which states that any non-singular [projective curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_curve) of genus *g* > 1 defined over a number field *K* contains only finitely many [*K*-rational points](wikipedia://en/2960200).[^o21]

In 1994, as an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invited_speaker_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) he gave a talk Mumford-Stabilität in der algebraischen Geometrie (Mumford stability in algebraic geometry). Extending methods of [Paul Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312), he proved the [Mordell–Lang conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell–Lang_conjecture),[^o22] which is a generalization of the Mordell conjecture. Together with [Gisbert Wüstholz](wikipedia://en/6429912),[^o23] he re-proved [Roth's theorem](wikipedia://en/787853), for which Roth had been awarded the Fields medal in 1958.

He returned to Germany the same year, and from 1994 to 2018, he was a director of the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn). In 1996, he received the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize) of the [Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Forschungsgemeinschaft).

Faltings has been the formal supervisor of over a dozen [PhD students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_student), including [Shinichi Mochizuki](wikipedia://en/36940578),[^o3] [Wiesława Nizioł](wikipedia://en/47387522),[^o4] and [Nikolai Durov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Durov).[^o24]

On 19 March 2026, the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters) announced Faltings as the winner of the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285), "for introducing powerful tools in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and resolving long-standing [diophantine](wikipedia://en/9109) conjectures of [Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) and [Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell–Lang_conjecture)." He is the first German mathematician to have received both the Fields Medal and the Abel Prize.[^o25]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Faltings married Angelika Tschimmel in 1984; she died in 2011.[^o27]
