# John G. Thompson

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="John G. Thompson"}

**John Griggs Thompson** (born October 13, 1932) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) at the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida) noted for his work in the field of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1970, the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 1992, and the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2008.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Thompson received his [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1955 and his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1959 under the supervision of [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574). After spending some time on the mathematics faculty at the University of Chicago, he moved to the UK, in 1970, to take up the [Rouse Ball Professorship in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) and later moved to the Mathematics Department of the [University of Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florida) as a Graduate Research Professor. He is currently a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_Emeritus) of [pure mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at the University of Cambridge, and a professor of mathematics at the University of Florida. He received the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2008 together with [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570).[^o6]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Thompson's doctoral thesis introduced new techniques and included the solution of a problem in [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/41890#Finite_group_theory) which had stood for around sixty years: the [nilpotency](wikipedia://en/144569) of [Frobenius kernels](wikipedia://en/1716123). At the time, this achievement was noted in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*.[^o8]

Thompson became a figure in the progress toward the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445). In 1963, he and [Walter Feit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Feit) proved that all [nonabelian](wikipedia://en/2974) finite [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600) are of [even](wikipedia://en/143135) [order](wikipedia://en/493527) (the *[Odd Order Paper](wikipedia://en/1656473)*, filling a whole issue of the *[Pacific Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Journal_of_Mathematics)*). This work was recognised by the award of the 1965 [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). His [N-group](wikipedia://en/29504243) papers classified all finite simple groups for which the [normalizer](wikipedia://en/145661) of every non-identity [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) is solvable. This included, as a by-product, the classification of all minimal finite simple groups (simple groups for which every proper subgroup is solvable). This work had some influence on later developments in the classification of finite simple groups, and was quoted in the citation by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) for the award of Thompson's Fields Medal in 1970 (Proceedings of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), 1970).

The [Thompson group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_group_%28finite%29) *Th* is one of the 26 [sporadic finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/324806). Thompson also made major contributions to the [inverse Galois problem](wikipedia://en/739166). He found a criterion for a finite group to be a [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673), that in particular implies that the [monster simple group](wikipedia://en/47422) is a Galois group.[^o9]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 1971, Thompson was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1982, he was awarded the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097), and, in 1988, he received the honorary degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). In 1992 he was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278). Thompson was awarded the United States [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) in 2000.[^o11] He is a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) and a recipient of its [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) in 1985.[^o12] He is a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o13]
