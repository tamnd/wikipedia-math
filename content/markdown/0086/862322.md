# Continuous wavelet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuous wavelet"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **continuous [wavelets](wikipedia://en/50903)** are functions used by the [continuous wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/679596). These functions are defined as [analytical expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_expression), as functions either of time or of frequency. Most of the continuous wavelets are used for both wavelet decomposition and composition transforms. That is they are the continuous counterpart of [orthogonal wavelets](wikipedia://en/3352878).[^o2][^o3]

:::

The following continuous wavelets have been invented for various applications:[^o4]

- [Poisson wavelet](wikipedia://en/44070426)
- [Morlet wavelet](wikipedia://en/186963)
- [Modified Morlet wavelet](wikipedia://en/4982979)
- [Mexican hat wavelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_hat_wavelet)
- [Complex Mexican hat wavelet](wikipedia://en/857896)
- [Shannon wavelet](wikipedia://en/11800092)
- [Meyer wavelet](wikipedia://en/38833005)
- [Difference of Gaussians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_of_Gaussians)
- [Hermitian wavelet](wikipedia://en/857867)
- [Beta wavelet](wikipedia://en/7503909)
- [Causal wavelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causal_wavelet)
- [μ wavelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Μ_wavelet)
- [Cauchy wavelet](wikipedia://en/72669698)
- [Addison wavelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addison_wavelet)
