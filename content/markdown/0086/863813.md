# Representation theory of the Poincaré group

Physics

*Image: Henri Poincare.jpg.* [H Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Representation theory of the Poincaré group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **the representation theory of the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487)** is an example of the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) that is neither a [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326) nor a [semisimple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_group). It is fundamental in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).

:::

In a physical theory having [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) as the underlying [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758), the space of physical states is typically a representation of the Poincaré group. (More generally, it may be a [projective representation](wikipedia://en/348860), which amounts to a representation of the [double cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_covering_group) of the group.)

In a [classical field theory](wikipedia://en/1293340), the physical states are sections of a Poincaré-equivariant [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over Minkowski space. The equivariance condition means that the group acts on the total space of the vector bundle, and the projection to Minkowski space is an [equivariant map](wikipedia://en/422711). Therefore, the Poincaré group also acts on the space of sections. Representations arising in this way (and their subquotients) are called covariant field representations, and are not usually unitary.

For a discussion of such [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788), see [Wigner's classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner's_classification).

In quantum mechanics, the state of the system is determined by the Schrödinger equation, which [is invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_group) under Galilean transformations. Quantum field theory is the relativistic extension of quantum mechanics, where relativistic (Lorentz/Poincaré invariant) wave equations are solved, "quantized", and act on a Hilbert space composed of [Fock states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fock_states).

There are no finite-dimensional unitary representations of the full Lorentz (and thus Poincaré) transformations due to the non-compact nature of Lorentz boosts (rotations in Minkowski space along a space and time axis). However, there are finite-dimensional non-unitary indecomposable representations of the Poincaré algebra, which may be used for modelling of unstable particles.[^o2][^o3]

In case of spin 1/2 particles, it is possible to find a construction that includes both a finite-dimensional representation and a scalar product preserved by this representation by associating a 4-component [Dirac spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor) $\psi$ with each particle. These spinors transform under Lorentz transformations generated by the [gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966) ($\gamma_{\mu}$). It can be shown that the scalar product

$$\langle\psi|\phi\rangle = \bar{\psi}\phi = \psi^{\dagger}\gamma_0\phi$$

is preserved. It is not, however, positive definite, so the representation is not unitary.
