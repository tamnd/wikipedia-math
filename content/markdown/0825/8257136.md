# Bulverism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bulverism"}

*Image: CS Lewis photo on dust jacket.jpg.* The term was coined by [C. S. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Lewis), a British author, scholar and [Christian apologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologist).

**Bulverism** is a rhetorical [fallacy](wikipedia://en/53986) that combines [circular reasoning](wikipedia://en/313944), the [genetic fallacy](wikipedia://en/157941) and *[ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226)* with presumption or condescension. The Bulverist [presumes](wikipedia://en/1965727) that a speaker's argument is false or invalid and then explains why the speaker made that argument (even if said argument is actually correct) by [attacking the speaker](wikipedia://en/2226#Circumstantial) or the speaker's [motive](wikipedia://en/985983).[^o2]

:::

The term *Bulverism* was coined by [C. S. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Lewis) (after "its imaginary inventor, Ezekiel Bulver") to poke fun at a serious error in thinking that, he alleged, frequently occurred in a variety of religious, political, and philosophical debates.[^n1]

## Threat and remedy {#Threat_and_remedy}

The special threat of this fallacy lies in that it applies equally to the person who errs as to that person's opponent. Lewis says, "Until Bulverism is crushed, reason can play no effective part in human affairs. Each side snatches it early as a weapon against the other; but between the two reason itself is discredited."[^n1]

The remedy, according to Lewis, is to accept that some reasoning is not tainted by the reasoner.[^n4]
