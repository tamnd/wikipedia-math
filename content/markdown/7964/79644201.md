# Luigi Ferrucci

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Luigi Ferrucci"}

**Luigi Ferrucci** is an Italian [geriatrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geriatrician) and [epidemiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiologist) who conducts research on the causal pathways leading to progressive physical and [cognitive decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_decline) in older persons. He has served as the scientific director of the [National Institute on Aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_on_Aging) since 2011.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Ferrucci received a M.D. and board certification in 1980, a board certification in [geriatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geriatrics) in 1982 and Ph.D. in biology and [pathophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathophysiology) of aging in 1998 at the [University of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence).[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Between 1985 and 2002, Ferrucci was chief of geriatric rehabilitation at the department of geriatric medicine and director of the laboratory of [clinical epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_epidemiology) at the [Istituto Superiore di Sanità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Superiore_di_Sanità). During the same period, he collaborated with the [National Institute on Aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_on_Aging)'s (NIA) laboratory of epidemiology, [demography](wikipedia://en/45642), and [biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry) where he spent several periods as visiting scientist at [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) (NIH).[^o4]

In September 2002, Ferrucci became the chief of the [longitudinal studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_studies) section at NIA and the director of the [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore) Longitudinal Study on Aging.[^o4] That year, he refined the design of the Baltimore study to focus on the [geroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geroscience) hypothesis, which states the pace of biological aging is the [root cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_cause_analysis) of many age-related chronic diseases, as well as physical and [cognitive disability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_disability).[^o4]

In May 2011, Ferrucci became the NIA scientific director.[^o4] He reorganized NIA to foster interactions between disciplines, such as [neuroimaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroimaging) and [neurophysiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurophysiology).[^o6] He has worked on projects with biochemists and led longitudinal studies in humans, such as the Genetic and Epigenetic Signatures of Translational Aging Laboratory Testing (GESTALT) study.[^o6] Ferrucci conducts research on the causal pathways leading to progressive physical and [cognitive decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_decline) in older persons.[^o4] In April 2025, during the [federal mass layoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_federal_mass_layoffs), he was among the NIH scientists who were initially laid off due to a "coding error" but was subsequently called back to work.[^o7]
