# G/M/1 queue

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="G/M/1 queue"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), the **G/M/1 queue** represents the queue length in a system where interarrival times have a general (meaning arbitrary) distribution and service times for each job have an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906).[^o2] The system is described in [Kendall's notation](wikipedia://en/7345405) where the G denotes a general distribution, M the exponential distribution for service times and the 1 that the model has a single server.

:::

The arrivals of a G/M/1 queue are given by a [renewal process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewal_process). It is an extension of an [M/M/1 queue](wikipedia://en/14032611), where this renewal process must specifically be a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) (so that interarrival times have exponential distribution).

Models of this type can be solved by considering one of two [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064) dual systems, one proposed by Ramaswami and one by Bright.[^o3]

## Queue size at arrival times {#Queue_size_at_arrival_times}

Let $(X_t, t \ge 0)$ be a $G/M(\mu)/1$ queue with arrival times $(A_n, n \in \mathbb{N})$ that have interarrival distribution *A*. Define the size of the queue immediately before the *n*th arrival by the process $U_n=X_{A_n-}$. This is a discrete-time [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) with [stochastic matrix](wikipedia://en/217313):

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1-a_0 & a_0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 1-(a_0+a_1) & a_1 & a_0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 1-(a_0+a_1+a_2) & a_2 & a_1 & a_0 & 0 & \cdots \\ 1-(a_0+a_1+a_2+a_3) & a_3 & a_2 & a_1 & a_0 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$

where $a_v=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{(\mu X)^v e^{-\mu A}}{v!}\right)$.[^o5]

The Markov chain $U_n$ has a stationary distribution if and only if the traffic intensity $\rho=(\mu \mathbb{E}(A))^{-1}$ is less than 1, in which case the unique such distribution is the [geometric distribution](wikipedia://en/45922) with probability $\eta$ of failure, where $\eta$ is the smallest root of the equation $\mathbb{E}(\exp(\mu(\eta-1)A))$.[^o5]

In this case, under the assumption that the queue is [first-in first-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFO_%28computing_and_electronics%29) (FIFO), a customer's waiting time *W* is distributed by:[^o5]

$$\mathbb{P}(W\le x)=1-\eta \exp(-\mu(1-\eta)x) ~\text{ for } x \geq 0$$

## Busy period {#Busy_period}

The busy period can be computed by using a duality between the G/M/1 model and [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064) generated by the Christmas tree transformation.[^o7]

## Response time {#Response_time}

The response time is the amount of time a job spends in the system from the instant of arrival to the time they leave the system. A [consistent](wikipedia://en/1497569) and [asymptotically normal estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotically_normal_estimator) for the mean response time, can be computed as the fixed point of an empirical Laplace transform.[^o9]
