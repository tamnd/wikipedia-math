# Small icosihemidodecacron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Small icosihemidodecacron"}

Uniform dual polyhedron stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **small icosihemidodecacron** is the dual of the [small icosihemidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157141), and is one of nine [dual hemipolyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_hemipolyhedra). It appears visually indistinct from the [small dodecahemidodecacron](wikipedia://en/24081922).

:::

Since the hemipolyhedra have [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) passing through the center, the [dual figures](wikipedia://en/8815) have corresponding [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity; properly, on the [real projective plane](wikipedia://en/411325) at infinity.[^o2] In [Magnus Wenninger](wikipedia://en/8864685)'s *Dual Models*, they are represented with intersecting [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), each extending in both directions to the same vertex at infinity, in order to maintain symmetry. In practice the model prisms are cut off at a certain point that is convenient for the maker. Wenninger suggested these figures are members of a new class of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) figures, called *stellation to infinity*. However, he also suggested that strictly speaking they are not polyhedra because their construction does not conform to the usual definitions.

The small icosihemidodecahedron has six [decagonal](wikipedia://en/327393) [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) passing through the model center, the small icosihemidodecacron has six [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) at infinity.
