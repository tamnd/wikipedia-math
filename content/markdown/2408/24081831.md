In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **great triambic icosahedron** and **medial triambic icosahedron** (or **midly triambic icosahedron**) are visually identical [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra). The exterior surface also represents the **[De<sub>2</sub>f<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/13181245)** [stellation](wikipedia://en/24081831#As_a_stellation) of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084). These figures can be differentiated by marking which intersections between edges are true [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) and which are not. In the above images, true vertices are marked by gold spheres, which can be seen in the concave Y-shaped areas. Alternatively, if the faces are filled with the [even–odd rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even–odd_rule), the internal structure of both shapes will differ.

:::

The 12 vertices of the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) matches the [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) of an [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084).

## Great triambic icosahedron {#Great_triambic_icosahedron}

The **great triambic icosahedron** is the dual of the [great ditrigonal icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4156959), U47. It has 20 inverted-hexagonal (triambus) faces, shaped like a three-bladed [propeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller). It has 32 vertices: 12 exterior points, and 20 hidden inside. It has 60 edges.

The faces have alternating angles of $\arccos(\frac{1}{4})-60^{\circ}\approx 15.522\,487\,814\,07^{\circ}$ and $\arccos(-\frac{1}{4})\approx 104.477\,512\,185\,93^{\circ}$. The sum of the six angles is $360^{\circ}$, and not $720^{\circ}$ as might be expected for a hexagon, because the polygon turns around its center twice. The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) equals $\arccos(-\frac{1}{3})\approx 109.471\,220\,634\,49$.

## Medial triambic icosahedron {#Medial_triambic_icosahedron}

The **medial triambic icosahedron** is the dual of the [ditrigonal dodecadodecahedron](wikipedia://en/4157586), U41. It has 20 faces, each being simple concave [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733) or triambi. It has 24 vertices: 12 exterior points, and 12 hidden inside. It has 60 edges.

The faces have alternating angles of $\arccos(\frac{1}{4})-60^{\circ}\approx 15.522\,487\,814\,07^{\circ}$ and $\arccos(-\frac{1}{4})+120^{\circ}\approx 224.477\,512\,185\,93^{\circ}$. The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) equals $\arccos(-\frac{1}{3})\approx 109.471\,220\,634\,49$.

Unlike the great triambic icosahedron, the medial triambic icosahedron is topologically a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) of index two.[^o14] By distorting the triambi into regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733), one obtains a quotient space of the [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/241291) [order-5 hexagonal tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-5_hexagonal_tiling):
