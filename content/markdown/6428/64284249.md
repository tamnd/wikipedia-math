# Winfried Scharlau

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Winfried Scharlau"}

*Image: Winfried Scharlau 2006.jpg.* Winfried Scharlau in 2006.

**Winfried Scharlau** (12 August 1940, in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) – 26 November 2020) was a German mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Scharlau received his doctorate in 1967 from the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). His doctoral thesis *Quadratische Formen und Galois-Cohomologie* (Quadratic Forms and Galois Cohomology) was supervised by [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495).[^o4] Scharlau was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) for the academic year 1969–1970 and in spring 1972.[^o5] From 1970 he was a professor (most recent [*Institutsdirektor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany#Overview)) at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster), from where he retired.

Scharlau's research deals with [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and, in particular, the theory of quadratic forms, about which he wrote a 1985 monograph *Quadratic and Hermitian Forms* in Springer's series *Grundlehren der mathematischen Wissenschaften*.[^o6][^o7]

Scharlau was also an amateur [ornithologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithologist) and author of two novels, *I megali istoria - die große Geschichte* (2nd edition 2001), set on the Greek island of [Naxos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxos), and *Scharife* (2001), set on the island of [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) in the 19th century.[^o8] He also deals with the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and wrote, with Hans Opolka,[^o9] a historically-oriented introduction to number theory. Their book presents, among other topics, the analytical class number formula of [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) and the geometry of the numbers in the 19th century.[^o10] Scharlau wrote a multi-part biography of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042).[^o8]

Scharlau was a corresponding member of the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities). From 1991 to 1992 he was president of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973). In 1974 he was invited as speaker with talks *On subspaces of inner product spaces* at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver).[^o11]

He was the father of the cognitive psychologist Ingrid Scharlau.[^o12]
