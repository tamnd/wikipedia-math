# Samuel S. Wilks

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel S. Wilks"}

**Samuel Stanley Wilks** (June 17, 1906 – March 7, 1964) was an American mathematician and academic who played an important role in the development of [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), especially in regard to practical applications.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wilks was born in [Little Elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Elm), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas) and raised on a farm. He studied Industrial Arts at the North Texas State Teachers College in Denton, Texas, obtaining his bachelor's degree in 1926. He received his master's degree in mathematics in 1928 from the University of Texas. He obtained his Ph.D. at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa) under [Henry Louis Rietz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Louis_Rietz).[^n1]

## Career {#Career}

Wilks became an instructor in mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1933; in 1938 he assumed the editorship of the journal *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)* in place of [Harry C. Carver](wikipedia://en/8135657). Wilks assembled an advisory board for the journal that included major figures in statistics and probability, among them [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), [Jerzy Neyman](wikipedia://en/2386106), and [Egon Pearson](wikipedia://en/1643759).

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he was a consultant with the [Office of Naval Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Naval_Research). Both during and after the War he had a profound impact on the application of statistical methods to all aspects of military planning.

Wilks was named professor of mathematics and director of the Section of Mathematical Statistics at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1944, and became chairman of the Division of Mathematics at the university in 1958.

Wilks died in 1964 in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey).

## Work in mathematical statistics {#Work_in_mathematical_statistics}

He was noted for his work on [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384). He also conducted work on [unit-weighted regression](wikipedia://en/3620926), proving the idea that under a wide variety of common conditions, almost all sets of weights will yield composites that are very highly correlated,[^n2] a result that has been dubbed Wilks's theorem.[^n3]

Another result, also called “[Wilks' theorem](wikipedia://en/30612745)” occurs in the theory of [likelihood ratio tests](wikipedia://en/45035#Wilks'_theorem), where Wilks showed the distribution of log likelihood ratios is asymptotically $\chi^2$.

From the start of his career, Wilks favored a strong focus on practical applications for the increasingly abstract field of mathematical statistics; he also influenced other researchers, notably [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974), in a similar direction. Drawing upon the background of his thesis, Wilks worked with the Educational Testing Service in developing the standardized tests like the [SAT](wikipedia://en/144716) that have had a profound effect on American education. He also worked with [Walter Shewhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Shewhart) on statistical applications in quality control in manufacturing.

[Wilks's lambda distribution](wikipedia://en/8219348) is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) related to two [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) [Wishart distributed](wikipedia://en/364996) variables. It is important in [multivariate statistics](wikipedia://en/19384) and [likelihood-ratio tests](wikipedia://en/45035).

## Honors {#Honors}

The [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) named its [Wilks Memorial Award](wikipedia://en/15966180) in his honor. Wilks was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1948 and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1963.[^o10][^o11] He was elected a fellow of the [Econometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Econometric_Society) in 1947.
