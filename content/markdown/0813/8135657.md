# Harry C. Carver

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harry C. Carver"}

*Image: Harry Carver, portrait (Prof. of Mathematics, 1916-1961).jpg.* University of Michigan faculty portrait

**Harry Clyde Carver** (December 4, 1890 – January 30, 1977) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [academic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_staff), primarily associated with the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). He was a major influence in the development of [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) as an academic discipline.

:::

Born in [Waterbury, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterbury,_Connecticut), Carver was educated at the University of Michigan, earning his B.S. degree in 1915, and the next year becoming an instructor in mathematics; he taught statistics in [actuarial](wikipedia://en/189373) applications. At the time, the University of Michigan was only the second such institution in the United States to offer this type of course, after the pioneering [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University). Carver was appointed assistant professor at Michigan in 1918, then associate professor (1921) and full professor (1936); during this period the University's program in mathematical statistics and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) underwent significant expansion.

In 1930, Carver founded the journal *Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, which over time became an important periodical in the field. Financial support, however, was lacking in the midst of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression); in January 1934, Carver undertook financial responsibility for the Annals and maintained the existence of the journal at his own expense. In 1935, he helped to start the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), which in 1938 assumed control over the journal; [Samuel S. Wilks](wikipedia://en/8136395) succeeded Carver as editor in the same year. The Institute has named its Harry C. Carver Medal after him.

With the coming of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Carver devoted his energies to solving problems in [aerial navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_navigation), an interest he maintained for the remainder of his life.

In one lecture titled "Let Us Own Data Science" given by [Bin Yu](wikipedia://en/39209141) in 2014 at [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University), Carver was described as in the following formula: Turner[^o2] + Carver = "Data Scientist".[^o3][^o4]
