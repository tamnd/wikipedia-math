# Grand mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grand mean"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **grand mean**, **pooled mean**, or **grand average** is the overall [average](wikipedia://en/60167) of the values in a set of numbers, regardless of how they may be grouped.[^o2] The adjective *grand* or *pooled* is used for emphasis, to distinguish it from any of the *group means*, the averages computed within a particular subset of values.

:::

In [certain statistical models](wikipedia://en/33580814#Model) such as those used in [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634), the term *grand, overall,* or *general mean* can also refer to a [parameter](wikipedia://en/413291), usually denoted $\mu$, describing an effect common to all observations.[^o3] It may be thought of as an overall "average" in the sense of an [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) under the appropriate assumptions. This meaning of "grand mean" will not be treated here.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Suppose there are three groups of numbers: Group A has 2, 3, 7; group B has 6, 7, 12, 4; group C has 1, 3.

The grand mean of all numbers = (2+3+7+6+7+12+4+1+3)/9 = 5.

The group means are (2 + 3 + 7)/3 = 4 for group A, (6 + 7 + 12 + 4)/4 = 7.25 for group B, and (1 + 3)/2 = 2 for group C. Note that the average of the group means does *not* equal the grand mean, as is discussed below.

:::

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

Suppose one wishes to determine which states in America have the tallest men. To do so, one measures the heights of a suitably sized sample of men in each state and the grand mean of heights across all the men sampled. A comparison of the state means with the national mean gives an indication of states where the average height is unusually high or low. (Determining with [statistical significance](wikipedia://en/160995) whether the states actually differ, and whether a particular state has unusually tall or short men, requires the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/14344439#The_hypothesis_test). The calculation there depends on the separate group [variances](wikipedia://en/32344#Sample_variance) and the [pooled variance](wikipedia://en/10910481) of the data.)

:::

::: {#o9 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

In order to represent averages algebraically, the elements of a grouped data set are often denoted by $x_{ij}$, where *i* indicates the group and *j* the data point within the group. The mean of group *i* is similarly denoted by $\bar{x}_{i\cdot}$, while $\bar{x}_{\cdot \cdot}$ denotes the grand mean. The grand mean is then given by a [double sum](wikipedia://en/246160#Notation): $\bar{x}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} x_{ij},$ where *g* is the number of groups, *n<sub>i</sub>* is the number of observations (numbers) in group *i*, and *N* is the total number of observations, namely

$$N = n_1 + n_2 + \cdots + n_g.$$

Similarly, the mean of group *i* is given by $\bar{x}_{i\cdot} = \frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i}x_{ij} .$ Written out, this sum is $\bar{x}_{i\cdot} = \frac{1}{n_i} (x_{i1}+ x_{i2} + \cdots + x_{in_i}).$

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

The grand mean can be computed from the group means by $\bar{x}_{\cdot\cdot} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{g} n_i \bar{x}_{i\cdot}$. This may be viewed as a [*weighted average*](wikipedia://en/33274) of the group means, where the weight assigned to the *i* th mean is $n_i/N$. In general, the grand mean is *not* a simple average of the individual group means. For instance, in the opening example [above](wikipedia://en/8132847#Example), the (unweighted) average of the three group means is (4 + 7.25 + 2)/3 = 4.4166..., while the grand mean is 5.

However, there is one case where the grand mean *can* be computed as the simple average of the group means, namely when the groups all have the same size, that is, when $n_1 = n_2 = \cdots = n_g.$ This follows easily from the above formula.

**Example** Suppose the data consists of three equal-size groups: Group A has 2, 6, 7, 11, 4; group B has 4, 6, 8, 14, 8; group C has 8, 7, 4, 1, 5.

The mean of group A = (2+6+7+11+4)/5 = 6,

The mean of group B = (4+6+8+14+8)/5 = 8,

The mean of group C = (8+7+4+1+5)/5 = 5,

Therefore, the grand mean of all the numbers = (6+8+5)/3 = 6.333..., as could be computed by averaging all 15 values pooled together.

To avoid possible confusion, the term "grand mean" is *never* used for the average of the individual group means.
