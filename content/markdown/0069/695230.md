# Resistance distance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Resistance distance"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **resistance distance** between two [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of a [simple](wikipedia://en/325806), [connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph), $G$, is equal to the [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance) between two equivalent points on an [electrical network](wikipedia://en/9559), constructed so as to correspond to $G$, with each [edge](wikipedia://en/325806) being replaced by a [resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_resistance) of one [ohm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohm). It is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) on [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

On a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$, the **resistance distance** $\Omega_{i,j}$ between two vertices $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ is[^o4]

$$\Omega_{i,j}:=\Gamma_{i,i}+\Gamma_{j,j}-\Gamma_{i,j}-\Gamma_{j,i},$$

where $\Gamma = \left(L + \frac{1}{|V|}\Phi\right)^+,$

with ${}^{+}$ denotes the [Moore–Penrose inverse](wikipedia://en/331258), $L$ the [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472) of $G$, $\lvert V\rvert$ is the number of vertices in $G$, and $\Phi$ is the $\lvert V\rvert \times \lvert V\rvert$ matrix containing all 1s.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties of resistance distance"}

## Properties of resistance distance {#Properties_of_resistance_distance}

If $i = j$ then $\Omega_{i,j} = 0$. For an undirected graph

$$\Omega_{i,j}=\Omega_{j,i}=\Gamma_{i,i}+\Gamma_{j,j}-2\Gamma_{i,j}$$

### General sum rule {#General_sum_rule}

For any $N$-vertex [simple connected graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G = (V, E)$ and arbitrary $N\times N$ [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) $M$:

$$\sum_{i,j \in V}(LML)_{i,j}\Omega_{i,j} = -2\operatorname{tr}(ML)$$

From this generalized sum rule a number of relationships can be derived depending on the choice of $M$. Two of note are;

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{(i,j) \in E}\Omega_{i,j} &= N - 1 \\ \sum_{i<j \in V}\Omega_{i,j} &= N\sum_{k=1}^{N-1} \lambda_k^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

where the $\lambda_{k}$ are the non-zero [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of the [Laplacian matrix](wikipedia://en/1448472). This unordered sum

$$\sum_{i<j} \Omega_{i,j}$$

is called the Kirchhoff index of the graph.

### Relationship to the number of spanning trees of a graph {#Relationship_to_the_number_of_spanning_trees_of_a_graph}

For a simple connected graph $G = (V, E)$, the **resistance distance** between two vertices may be expressed as a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) of the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [spanning trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanning_tree_%28mathematics%29), $T$, of $G$ as follows:

$$\Omega_{i,j}=\begin{cases} \frac{\left | \{t:t \in T,\, e_{i,j} \in t\} \right \vert}{\left | T \right \vert}, & (i,j) \in E\\ \frac{\left | T'-T \right \vert}{\left | T \right \vert}, &(i,j) \not \in E \end{cases}$$

where $T'$ is the set of spanning trees for the graph $G' = (V, E + e_{i,j})$. In other words, for an edge $(i,j)\in E$, the resistance distance between a pair of nodes $i$ and $j$ is the probability that the edge $(i,j)$ is in a random spanning tree of $G$.

### Relationship to random walks {#Relationship_to_random_walks}

The resistance distance between vertices $u$ and $v$ is proportional to the **commute time** $C_{u,v}$ of a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) between $u$ and $v$. The commute time is the expected number of steps in a random walk that starts at $u$, visits $v$, and returns to $u$. For a graph with $m$ edges, the resistance distance and commute time are related as $C_{u,v}=2m\Omega_{u,v}$.[^o10]

Resistance distance is also related to the **escape probability** between two vertices. The escape probability $P_{u,v}$ between $u$ and $v$ is the probability that a random walk starting at $u$ visits $v$ before returning to $u$. The escape probability equals

$$P_{u,v} = \frac{1}{\deg(u)\Omega_{u,v}},$$

where $\deg(u)$ is the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of $u$.[^o11] Unlike the commute time, the escape probability is not symmetric in general, i.e., $P_{u,v}\neq P_{v,u}$.

### As a squared Euclidean distance {#As_a_squared_Euclidean_distance}

Since the Laplacian $L$ is symmetric and positive semi-definite, so is

$$\left(L+\frac{1}{|V|}\Phi\right),$$

thus its pseudo-inverse $\Gamma$ is also symmetric and positive semi-definite. Thus, there is a $K$ such that $\Gamma = KK^\textsf{T}$ and we can write:

$$\Omega_{i,j} = \Gamma_{i,i} + \Gamma_{j,j} - \Gamma_{i,j} - \Gamma_{j,i} = K_iK_i^\textsf{T} + K_j K_j^\textsf{T} - K_i K_j^\textsf{T} - K_j K_i^\textsf{T} = \left(K_i - K_j\right)^2$$

showing that the square root of the resistance distance corresponds to the [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) in the space spanned by $K$.

### Connection with Fibonacci numbers {#Connection_with_Fibonacci_numbers}

A fan graph is a graph on $n + 1$ vertices where there is an edge between vertex $i$ and $n + 1$ for all $i = 1, 2, 3, \ldots, n$, and there is an edge between vertex $i$ and $i + 1$ for all $i = 1, 2, 3, \ldots, n - 1$.

The resistance distance between vertex $n + 1$ and vertex $i \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, n\}$ is

$$\frac{ F_{2(n-i)+1} F_{2i-1} }{ F_{2n} }$$

where $F_{j}$ is the $j$-th Fibonacci number, for $j \ge 0$.[^o14]
