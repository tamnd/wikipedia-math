# Smash product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smash product"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **smash product** of two [pointed spaces](wikipedia://en/1236666) (i.e. [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) with distinguished basepoints) $(X, x_{0})$ and $(Y, y_{0})$ is the [quotient](wikipedia://en/237213) of the [product space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) $X \times Y$ under the identifications $(x, y_{0}) \sim (x_{0}, y)$ for all $x$ in $X$ and $y$ in $Y$. The smash product is itself a pointed space, with basepoint being the [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of $(x_{0}, y_{0}).$ The smash product is usually denoted $X \land Y$ or $X \text{⨳} Y$. The smash product depends on the choice of basepoints (unless both *X* and *Y* are [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/363325)).

:::

One can think of $X$ and $Y$ as sitting inside $X \times Y$ as the [subspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28topology%29) $X \times \{y_{0}$} and$\{x_{0}\} \times Y.$ These subspaces intersect at a single point: $(x_{0}, y_{0}),$ the basepoint of $X \times Y.$ So the union of these subspaces can be identified with the [wedge sum](wikipedia://en/684143) $X \vee Y = (X \amalg Y) \; / {\sim}$. In particular, $\{x_{0}\} \times Y$ in $X \times Y$ is identified with $Y$ in $X \vee Y$, ditto for $X \times \{y_{0}$} and $X$. In $X \vee Y$, subspaces $X$ and $Y$ intersect in the single point $x_0 \sim y_0$. The smash product is then the quotient

$$X \wedge Y = (X \times Y) / (X \vee Y).$$

The smash product shows up in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), a branch of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). In homotopy theory, one often works with a different [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of spaces than the [category of all topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051). In some of these categories the definition of the smash product must be modified slightly. For example, the smash product of two [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) is a CW complex if one uses the product of CW complexes in the definition rather than the [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910). Similar modifications are necessary in other categories.

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Visualization\_of\_the\_smash\_product\_of\_two\_circles.gif.* A visualization of $S^1 \wedge S^1$ as the quotient $(S^1\times S^1)/(S^1\vee S^1)$.

- The smash product of any pointed space $X$ with a [0-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-sphere) (a [discrete space](wikipedia://en/56061) with two points) is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to $X$.
- The smash product of two [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) is a quotient of the [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) homeomorphic to the 2-sphere. That is, it is the quotient space of the torus $(S^1\times S^1)$ by the [figure-8 space](wikipedia://en/3526954) $(S^1\vee S^1)$. This can be visualized by taking the union of the innermost line of latitude of the torus and a given line of longitude and assuming their intersection is the basepoint. The union of two circles intersecting at a point is homeomorphic to the figure-8 space, which is then collapsed to a single point, resulting in a quotient space homeomorphic to the 2-sphere (see diagram).
- More generally, the smash product of two spheres *S*<sup>*m*</sup> and *S*<sup>*n*</sup> is homeomorphic to the sphere *S*<sup>*m*+*n*</sup>.
- The smash product of a space *X* with a circle is homeomorphic to the [reduced suspension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_suspension) of *X*: $$\Sigma X \cong X \wedge S^1.$$
- The *k*-fold iterated reduced suspension of *X* is homeomorphic to the smash product of *X* and a *k*-sphere $$\Sigma^k X \cong X \wedge S^k.$$
- In [domain theory](wikipedia://en/325077), taking the product of two domains (so that the product is strict on its arguments).

:::

## As a symmetric monoidal product {#As_a_symmetric_monoidal_product}

For any pointed spaces *X*, *Y*, and *Z* in an appropriate "convenient" category (e.g., that of [compactly generated spaces](wikipedia://en/899382)), there are natural (basepoint preserving) [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660)

$$\begin{aligned} X \wedge Y &\cong Y\wedge X, \\ (X\wedge Y)\wedge Z &\cong X \wedge (Y\wedge Z). \end{aligned}$$

However, for the naive category of pointed spaces, this fails, as shown by the counterexample $X=Y=\mathbb{Q}$ and $Z=\mathbb{N}$ found by [Dieter Puppe](wikipedia://en/63815664).[^o5] A proof due to Kathleen Lewis that Puppe's counterexample is indeed a counterexample can be found in the book of [J. Peter May](wikipedia://en/14644098) and Johann Sigurdsson.[^o6]

These [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) make the appropriate [category of pointed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_pointed_spaces) into a [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249) with the smash product as the monoidal product and the pointed [0-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-sphere) (a two-point discrete space) as the unit object. One can therefore think of the smash product as a kind of [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) in an appropriate category of pointed spaces.

## Adjoint relationship {#Adjoint_relationship}

[Adjoint functors](wikipedia://en/53991) make the analogy between the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) and the smash product more precise. In the category of [*R*-modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*, the tensor functor $(- \otimes_R A)$ is left adjoint to the internal [Hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037) $\mathrm{Hom}(A,-)$, so that

$$\mathrm{Hom}(X\otimes A,Y) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(X,\mathrm{Hom}(A,Y)).$$

In the [category of pointed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_pointed_spaces), the smash product plays the role of the tensor product in this formula: if $A, X$ are compact Hausdorff then we have an adjunction

$$\mathrm{Maps_*}(X\wedge A,Y) \cong \mathrm{Maps_*}(X,\mathrm{Maps_*}(A,Y))$$

where $\operatorname{Maps_*}$ denotes continuous maps that send basepoint to basepoint, and $\mathrm{Maps_*}(A,Y)$ carries the [compact-open topology](wikipedia://en/726335).[^o8]

In particular, taking $A$ to be the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $S^1$, we see that the reduced suspension functor $\Sigma$ is left adjoint to the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) functor $\Omega$:

$$\mathrm{Maps_*}(\Sigma X,Y) \cong \mathrm{Maps_*}(X,\Omega Y).$$
