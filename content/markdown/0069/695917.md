# Dirac adjoint

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dirac adjoint"}

In [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), the **Dirac adjoint** defines the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space) operation of a [Dirac spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor). The Dirac adjoint is motivated by the need to form well-behaved, measurable quantities out of Dirac spinors, replacing the usual role of the [Hermitian adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175).

:::

Possibly to avoid confusion with the usual [Hermitian adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175), some textbooks do not provide a name for the Dirac adjoint but simply call it "$\psi$-bar".

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\psi$ be a [Dirac spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor). Then its Dirac adjoint is defined as

$$\bar\psi \equiv \psi^\dagger \gamma^0$$

where $\psi^\dagger$ denotes the [Hermitian adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) of the spinor $\psi$, and $\gamma^0$ is the time-like [gamma matrix](wikipedia://en/2907966).

:::

## Spinors under Lorentz transformations {#Spinors_under_Lorentz_transformations}

The [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489) of [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) is not [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), therefore [spinor](wikipedia://en/10160091) [representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representations_of_the_Lorentz_group) of [Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404) are generally not [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999). That is, if $\lambda$ is a [projective representation](wikipedia://en/348860) of some Lorentz transformation,

$$\psi \mapsto \lambda \psi,$$

then, in general,

$$\lambda^\dagger \ne \lambda^{-1}.$$

The Hermitian adjoint of a spinor transforms according to

$$\psi^\dagger \mapsto \psi^\dagger \lambda^\dagger.$$

Therefore, $\psi^\dagger\psi$ is not a [Lorentz scalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_scalar) and $\psi^\dagger\gamma^\mu\psi$ is not even [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/187408).

Dirac adjoints, in contrast, transform according to

$$\bar\psi \mapsto \left(\lambda \psi\right)^\dagger \gamma^0.$$

Using the identity $\gamma^0 \lambda^\dagger \gamma^0 = \lambda^{-1}$, the transformation reduces to

$$\bar\psi \mapsto \bar\psi \lambda^{-1},$$

Thus, $\bar\psi\psi$ transforms as a Lorentz scalar and $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ as a [four-vector](wikipedia://en/226424).

## Usage {#Usage}

Using the Dirac adjoint, the [probability four-current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_current) **$J$** for a spin-1/2 particle field can be written as

$$J^\mu = c \bar\psi \gamma^\mu \psi$$

where $c$ is the speed of light and the components of **$J$** represent the probability density $\rho$ and the probability 3-current **$j$**:

$$\boldsymbol J = (c \rho, \boldsymbol j).$$

Taking $\mu = 0$ and using the relation for [gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966)

$$\left(\gamma^0\right)^2 = I,$$

the probability density becomes

$$\rho = \psi^\dagger \psi.$$
