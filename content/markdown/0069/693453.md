# Skolem–Noether theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skolem–Noether theorem"}

In [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Skolem–Noether theorem** characterizes the [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of [simple rings](wikipedia://en/443387). It is a fundamental result in the theory of [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082).

:::

The [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) was first published by [Thoralf Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765) in 1927 in his paper *Zur Theorie der assoziativen Zahlensysteme* ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *On the theory of associative number systems*) and later rediscovered by [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

In a general formulation, let *A* and *B* be simple unitary rings, and let *k* be the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of *B*. The center *k* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) since given *x* nonzero in *k*, the simplicity of *B* implies that the nonzero two-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *Bx* = *xB* is the whole of *B*, and hence that *x* is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666). If the [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) of *B* over *k* is finite, i.e. if *B* is a [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082), and *A* is also a *k*-[algebra](wikipedia://en/191788), then given *k*-algebra [homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13564)

*f*, *g* : *A* → *B*,

there exists a unit *b* in *B* such that for all *a* in *A*[^o4][^o5]

*g*(*a*) = *b* · *f*(*a*) · *b*<sup>−1</sup>.

In particular, every automorphism of a central simple *k*-algebra is an [inner automorphism](wikipedia://en/61839).[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

First suppose $B = \operatorname{M}_n(k) = \operatorname{End}_k(k^n)$. Then *f* and *g* define the actions of *A* on $k^n$; let $V_f, V_g$ denote the *A*-[modules](wikipedia://en/276410) thus obtained. Since $f(1) = 1 \neq 0$ the map *f* is injective by simplicity of *A*, so *A* is also finite-dimensional. Hence two simple *A*-modules are isomorphic and $V_f, V_g$ are finite direct sums of simple *A*-modules. Since they have the same dimension, it follows that there is an isomorphism of *A*-modules $b: V_g \to V_f$. But such *b* must be an element of $\operatorname{M}_n(k) = B$. For the general case, $B \otimes_k B^{\text{op}}$ is a matrix algebra and that $A \otimes_k B^{\text{op}}$ is simple. By the first part applied to the maps $f \otimes 1, g \otimes1 : A \otimes_k B^{\text{op}} \to B \otimes_k B^{\text{op}}$, there exists $b \in B \otimes_k B^{\text{op}}$ such that

$$(f \otimes 1)(a \otimes z) = b (g \otimes 1)(a \otimes z) b^{-1}$$

for all $a \in A$ and $z \in B^{\text{op}}$. Taking $a = 1$, we find

$$1 \otimes z = b (1\otimes z) b^{-1}$$

for all *z*. That is to say, *b* is in $Z_{B \otimes B^{\text{op}}}(k \otimes B^{\text{op}}) = B \otimes k$ and so we can write $b = b' \otimes 1$. Taking $z = 1$ this time we find

$$f(a)= b' g(a) {b'^{-1}},$$

which is what was sought.

:::
