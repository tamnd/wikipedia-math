# Diamond principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Diamond principle"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the **diamond principle**, denoted $\Diamond$, is a [combinatorial principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_principle) introduced by [Ronald Jensen](wikipedia://en/3785996) that holds in the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) and that implies the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705).[^n1] Jensen extracted the diamond principle from his proof that the [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472) implies the existence of a [Suslin tree](wikipedia://en/5028195).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The diamond principle $\lozenge$ says that there exists a **◊-sequence**; that is, a family of sets $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$ for $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ such that for any subset $A$ of [ω<sub>1</sub>](wikipedia://en/20860540) the set of $\alpha$ with $A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}$ is [stationary](wikipedia://en/1679067) in $\omega_{1}$.

There are several equivalent forms of the diamond principle. One states that there is a countable collection $\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ for each countable ordinal $\alpha$ such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is a stationary subset $C$ of $\omega_{1}$ such that for all $\alpha$ in $C$ we have $A \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ and $C \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$. Notice that, a weaken form which states that, there exist sets $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$ for $\alpha < \omega_{1}$ such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is at least one infinite $\alpha$ with $A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}$ , is equivalent to the [Continuum Hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705).

More generally, for a given [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) $\kappa$ and a [stationary set](wikipedia://en/1679067) $S \subseteq \kappa$, the statement $\lozenge_{S}$ (sometimes written $\lozenge(S)$ or $\lozenge_{\kappa}(S)$) is the statement that there is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) $\langle A_{\alpha} : \alpha \in S\rangle$ such that

- each $A_{\alpha} \subseteq \alpha$
- for every $A \subseteq \kappa$, $\{\alpha \in S : A \cap \alpha = A_{\alpha}\}$ is stationary in $\kappa$

The principle $\lozenge_{\omega_{1}}$ is the same as $\lozenge$.

The diamond-plus principle $\lozenge^{+}$ states that there exists a **$\lozenge^{+}$-sequence**, in other words a countable collection $\mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ for each countable ordinal α such that for any subset $A$ of $\omega_{1}$ there is a closed unbounded subset $C$ of $\omega_{1}$ such that for all $\alpha$ in $C$ we have $A \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$ and $C \cap \alpha \in \mathbf{A}_{\alpha}$.

:::

## Properties and use {#Properties_and_use}

Jensen showed that the diamond principle $\Diamond$ implies the existence of [Suslin trees](wikipedia://en/5028195). He also showed that the axiom of constructibility implies the stronger diamond-plus principle $\Diamond^+$, which implies the diamond principle, which implies the continuum hypothesis.[^n1] The diamond principle does not imply the existence of a [Kurepa tree](wikipedia://en/8267820), but $\Diamond^+$ does. Both $\Diamond$ and $\Diamond^+$ are [independent](wikipedia://en/1141208) of the axioms of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC). Also, the [club principle](wikipedia://en/691634) $\text{♣}$ and the continuum hypothesis $\operatorname{CH}$ together imply $\Diamond$. However, there exist models of $\text{♣} + \neg \operatorname{CH}$, so $\Diamond$ and $\text{♣}$ are not equivalent, rather, $\text{♣}$ is weaker than $\Diamond$.[^n2]

Matet proved the related principle $\Diamond_\kappa$, equivalent to a property of partitions of $\kappa$ with diagonal intersection of initial segments of the partitions stationary in $\kappa$.[^n3]

Akemann and Weaver used $\Diamond$ to construct a [$C*$-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) serving as a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to [Naimark's problem](wikipedia://en/1732798).[^n4]

For all cardinals $\kappa$ and [stationary subsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_subset) $S\subseteq\kappa^+$, $\lozenge_{S}$ holds in the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335). [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) proved that for $\kappa>\aleph_0$, $\Diamond_{\kappa^+}(S)$ follows from $2^\kappa=\kappa^+$ for stationary $S$ that do not contain ordinals of cofinality $\kappa$.[^n5] He also showed that the diamond principle solves the [Whitehead problem](wikipedia://en/212619) by implying that every [Whitehead group](wikipedia://en/212619) is free.
