# Acyclic coloring

*Image: Acyclic coloring.svg.* The acyclic chromatic number of the [McGee graph](wikipedia://en/23714466) is 3.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Acyclic coloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **acyclic coloring** is a (proper) [vertex coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) in which every 2-chromatic subgraph is [acyclic](wikipedia://en/325802). The **acyclic chromatic number** $A(G)$ of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) $G$ is the fewest colors needed in any acyclic coloring of $G$.

:::

Acyclic coloring is often associated with graphs [embedded](wikipedia://en/8149170) on non-plane surfaces.

## Upper bounds {#Upper_bounds}

A(*G*) ≤ 2 if and only if *G* is acyclic.

Bounds on A(*G*) in terms of Δ(*G*), the [maximum degree](wikipedia://en/325802) of *G*, include the following:

- A(*G*) ≤ 4 if Δ(*G*) = 3. (Grünbaum 1973)
- A(*G*) ≤ 5 if Δ(*G*) = 4. (Burstein 1979)
- A(*G*) ≤ 7 if Δ(*G*) = 5. (Kostochka Stocker 2011)
- A(*G*) ≤ 12 if Δ(*G*) = 6. (Varagani Venkaiah Yadav Kothapalli 2009)

A milestone in the study of acyclic coloring is the following affirmative answer to a conjecture of Grünbaum:

**Theorem** (Borodin 1979) A(*G*) ≤ 5 if *G* is planar graph.

(Grünbaum 1973) introduced acyclic coloring and acyclic chromatic number, and conjectured the result in the above theorem. Borodin's proof involved several years of painstaking inspection of 450 reducible configurations. One consequence of this theorem is that every planar graph can be decomposed into an [independent set](wikipedia://en/325802) and two [induced](wikipedia://en/1655188) [forests](wikipedia://en/325802). ## Algorithms and complexity {#Algorithms_and_complexity}

It is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete) to determine whether A(*G*) ≤ 3. (Kostochka 1978)

(Coleman Cai 1986) showed that the decision variant of the problem is NP-complete even when *G* is a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431).

(Gebremedhin Tarafdar Pothen Walther 2008) demonstrated that every proper vertex coloring of a [chordal graph](wikipedia://en/744165) is also an acyclic coloring. Since chordal graphs can be optimally colored in O(*n* + *m*) time, the same is also true for acyclic coloring on that class of graphs.

A linear-time algorithm to acyclically color a graph of maximum degree ≤ 3 using 4 colors or fewer was given by (Skulrattanakulchai 2004).
