# Real representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real representation"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) a **real representation** is usually a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) on a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *U*, but it can also mean a representation on a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) vector space *V* with an invariant [real structure](wikipedia://en/10775645), i.e., an [antilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilinear) [equivariant map](wikipedia://en/19378200#Equivariant_maps_and_isomorphisms)

:::

$$j\colon V\to V$$

which satisfies

$$j^2=+1.$$

The two viewpoints are equivalent because if *U* is a real vector space acted on by a group *G* (say), then *V* = *U*⊗**C** is a representation on a complex vector space with an antilinear equivariant map given by [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation). Conversely, if *V* is such a complex representation, then *U* can be recovered as the [fixed point set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_set) of *j* (the [eigenspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace) with [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) 1).

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), where representations are often viewed concretely in terms of matrices, a real representation is one in which the entries of the matrices representing the group elements are real numbers. These matrices can act either on real or complex column vectors.

A real representation on a complex vector space is isomorphic to its [complex conjugate representation](wikipedia://en/1442618), but the converse is not true: a representation which is isomorphic to its complex conjugate but which is not real is called a [pseudoreal representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoreal_representation). An irreducible pseudoreal representation *V* is necessarily a [quaternionic representation](wikipedia://en/695046): it admits an invariant [quaternionic structure](wikipedia://en/39227817), i.e., an antilinear equivariant map

$$j\colon V\to V$$

which satisfies

$$j^2=-1.$$

A [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_representations) of real and quaternionic representations is neither real nor quaternionic in general.

A representation on a complex vector space can also be isomorphic to the [dual representation](wikipedia://en/1442471) of its complex conjugate. This happens precisely when the representation admits a nondegenerate invariant [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138), e.g. a [hermitian form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_form). Such representations are sometimes said to be complex or (pseudo-)hermitian.

## Frobenius-Schur indicator {#Frobenius-Schur_indicator}

A criterion (for [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326) *G*) for reality of irreducible representations in terms of [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741) is based on the **Frobenius-Schur indicator** defined by

$$\int_{g\in G}\chi(g^2)\,d\mu$$

where *χ* is the character of the representation and *μ* is the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) with μ(*G*) = 1. For a finite group, this is given by

$${1\over |G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^2).$$

The indicator may take the values 1, 0 or −1. If the indicator is 1, then the representation is real. If the indicator is zero, the representation is complex (hermitian),[^o3] and if the indicator is −1, the representation is quaternionic.

## Examples {#Examples}

All representation of the [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) are real (and in fact rational), since we can build a complete set of [irreducible representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_representations) using [Young tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux).

All representations of the [rotation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) on odd-dimensional spaces are real, since they all appear as subrepresentations of [tensor products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_products) of copies of the fundamental representation, which is real.

Further examples of real representations are the [spinor](wikipedia://en/29276) representations of the [spin groups](wikipedia://en/411231) in 8*k*−1, 8*k*, and 8*k*+1 dimensions for *k* = 1, 2, 3 ... This periodicity *[modulo](wikipedia://en/20087)* 8 is known in mathematics not only in the theory of [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305), but also in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), in [KO-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KO-theory); see [spin representation](wikipedia://en/10160091) and [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity).
