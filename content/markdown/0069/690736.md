# Complete coloring

*Image: Complete coloring clebsch graph.svg.* Complete coloring of the [Clebsch graph](wikipedia://en/23970662) with 8 colors. Every pair of colors appears on at least one edge. No complete coloring with more colors exists: in any 9-coloring some color would appear only at one vertex, and there would not be enough neighboring vertices to cover all pairs involving that color. Therefore, the achromatic number of the Clebsch graph is 8.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complete coloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **complete coloring** is a (proper) [vertex coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) in which every pair of colors appears on *at least* one pair of adjacent [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899). Equivalently, a complete coloring is minimal in the sense that it cannot be transformed into a proper coloring with fewer colors by merging pairs of color classes. The **achromatic number** $\psi(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the maximum number of colors possible in any complete coloring of $G$.

:::

A complete coloring is the opposite of a [harmonious coloring](wikipedia://en/690728), which requires every pair of colors to appear on *at most* one pair of adjacent vertices.

## Complexity theory {#Complexity_theory}

Finding $\psi(G)$ is an [optimization problem](wikipedia://en/1126536). The [decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336) for complete coloring can be phrased as:

INSTANCE: a graph $G = (V, E)$ and positive integer $k$

QUESTION: does there exist a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) of $V$ into $k$ or more [disjoint sets](wikipedia://en/39359) $V_{1}, V_{2}, \ldots, V_{k}$ such that each $V_{i}$ is an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) for $G$ and such that for each pair of distinct sets $V_{i}, V_{j}, V_{i} \cup V_{j}$ is not an independent set.

Determining the achromatic number is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard); determining if it is greater than a given number is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete), as shown by Yannakakis and Gavril in 1978 by transformation from the minimum maximal matching problem.[^o3]

Note that any coloring of a graph with the minimum number of colors must be a complete coloring, so minimizing the number of colors in a complete coloring is just a restatement of the standard [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) problem.

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Algorithms"}

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

For any fixed *k*, it is possible to determine whether the achromatic number of a given graph is at least *k*, in linear time.[^o8]

The optimization problem permits approximation and is approximable within a $O\left(|V|/\sqrt{\log |V|}\right)$ [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio).[^o6]

:::

## Special classes of graphs {#Special_classes_of_graphs}

The NP-completeness of the achromatic number problem holds also for some special classes of graphs: [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431),[^o8] [complements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_complement) of [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431) (that is, graphs having no independent set of more than two vertices),[^o3] [cographs](wikipedia://en/1175666) and [interval graphs](wikipedia://en/363423),[^o9] and even for trees.[^o10]

For complements of trees, the achromatic number can be computed in polynomial time.[^o11] For trees, it can be approximated to within a constant factor.[^o6]

The achromatic number of an *n*-dimensional [hypercube graph](wikipedia://en/6706815) is known to be proportional to $\sqrt{n2^n}$, but the constant of proportionality is not known precisely.[^o12]
