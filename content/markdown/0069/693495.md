# Raphael M. Robinson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raphael M. Robinson"}

**Raphael Mitchel Robinson** (November 2, 1911 – January 27, 1995[^n1]) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Born in [National City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_City,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California), Robinson was the youngest of four children of a lawyer and a teacher. He was awarded from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in mathematics: the BA (1932), MA (1933), and Ph.D. (1935). His Ph.D. thesis, on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), was titled *Some results in the theory of [Schlicht functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlicht_function)*.

In 1941, Robinson married his former student [Julia Bowman](wikipedia://en/952987). She became his Berkeley colleague and the first woman president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

Robinson worked on [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). In 1937 he set out a simpler and more conventional version of the [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) 1923 [axiomatic set theory](wikipedia://en/528491). Soon after [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) joined Berkeley's mathematics department in 1942, Robinson began to do major work on the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358), building on Tarski's concept of [essential undecidabilility](wikipedia://en/913118#Decidability_of_a_theory), by proving a number of mathematical theories [undecidable](wikipedia://en/8336). In 1950 Robinson proved that an essentially undecidable theory need not have an infinite number of [axioms](wikipedia://en/928) by coming up with a counterexample: [Robinson arithmetic *Q*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinson_arithmetic_Q).[^n2] *Q* is finitely axiomatizable because it lacks [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic)'s axiom schema of [induction](wikipedia://en/18881); nevertheless *Q*, like Peano arithmetic, is [incomplete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_Incompleteness_Theorem) and undecidable in the sense of [Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736). Robinson's work on undecidability culminated in his coauthoring Tarski et al. (1953), which established, among other things, the undecidability of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory), abstract [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), and [closure algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closure_algebra).[^n3]

Robinson worked in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), even employing very early computers to obtain results. For example, he coded the [Lucas–Lehmer primality test](wikipedia://en/325739) to determine whether 2<sup>*n*</sup> − 1 was prime for all prime *n* \< 2304 on a [SWAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAC_%28computer%29). In 1952, he showed that these [Mersenne numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersenne_number) were all composite except for 17 values of *n* = 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 17, 19, 31, 61, 89, 107, 127, 521, 607, 1279, 2203, 2281. He discovered the last five of these [Mersenne primes](wikipedia://en/18908), the largest ones known at the time.[^n4]

Robinson wrote several papers on [tilings of the plane](wikipedia://en/65798), in particular a clear and remarkable 1971 paper *Undecidability and nonperiodicity for tilings of the plane* simplifying what had been a tangled theory.[^n5]

Robinson became a full professor at Berkeley in 1949, retired in 1973, and remained active in his educational interests for the duration of his life having published late in his life:

- (age 80 years) *[Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky)'s small universal Turing machine*,[^n6] describing a [universal Turing machine](wikipedia://en/71435) with four symbols and seven states;
- (age 83 years) *Two figures in the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane)*.[^n7]
