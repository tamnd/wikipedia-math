# L-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="L-theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), algebraic ***L*-theory** is the [*K*-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478); the term was coined by [C. T. C. Wall](wikipedia://en/2613478), with *L* being used as the letter after *K*. Algebraic *L*-theory, also known as "Hermitian *K*-theory", is important in [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

One can define *L*-groups for any [ring with involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_with_involution) *R*: the quadratic *L*-groups $L_*(R)$ (Wall) and the symmetric *L*-groups $L^*(R)$ (Mishchenko, Ranicki).

### Even dimension {#Even_dimension}

The even-dimensional *L*-groups $L_{2k}(R)$ are defined as the [Witt groups](wikipedia://en/5578523) of [ε-quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ε-quadratic_forms) over the ring *R* with $\epsilon = (-1)^k$. More precisely,

$$L_{2k}(R)$$

is the [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) of equivalence classes $[\psi]$ of non-degenerate ε-quadratic forms $\psi \in Q_\epsilon(F)$ over R, where the underlying R-modules F are finitely generated free. The equivalence relation is given by stabilization with respect to [hyperbolic ε-quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_ε-quadratic_forms):

$$[\psi] = [\psi'] \Longleftrightarrow n, n' \in {\mathbb N}_0: \psi \oplus H_{(-1)^k}(R)^n \cong \psi' \oplus H_{(-1)^k}(R)^{n'}.$$

The addition in $L_{2k}(R)$ is defined by

$$[\psi_1] + [\psi_2] := [\psi_1 \oplus \psi_2].$$

The zero element is represented by $H_{(-1)^k}(R)^n$ for any $n \in {\mathbb N}_0$. The inverse of $[\psi]$ is $[-\psi]$.

### Odd dimension {#Odd_dimension}

Defining odd-dimensional *L*-groups is more complicated; further details and the definition of the odd-dimensional *L*-groups can be found in the references mentioned below.

:::

## Examples and applications {#Examples_and_applications}

The *L*-groups of a group $\pi$ are the *L*-groups $L_*(\mathbf{Z}[\pi])$ of the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbf{Z}[\pi]$. In the applications to topology $\pi$ is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1 (X)$ of a space $X$. The quadratic *L*-groups $L_*(\mathbf{Z}[\pi])$ play a central role in the surgery classification of the homotopy types of $n$-dimensional [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds) of dimension $n > 4$, and in the formulation of the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194).

The distinction between symmetric *L*-groups and quadratic *L*-groups, indicated by upper and lower indices, reflects the usage in group homology and cohomology. The [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) $H^*$ of the cyclic group $\mathbf{Z}_2$ deals with the fixed points of a $\mathbf{Z}_2$-action, while the [group homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_homology) $H_*$ deals with the orbits of a $\mathbf{Z}_2$-action; compare $X^G$ (fixed points) and $X_G = X/G$ (orbits, quotient) for upper/lower index notation.

The quadratic *L*-groups: $L_n(R)$ and the symmetric *L*-groups: $L^n(R)$ are related by a symmetrization map $L_n(R) \to L^n(R)$ which is an isomorphism modulo 2-torsion, and which corresponds to the [polarization identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarization_identities).

The quadratic and the symmetric *L*-groups are 4-fold periodic (the comment of Ranicki, page 12, on the non-periodicity of the symmetric *L*-groups refers to another type of *L*-groups, defined using "short complexes").

In view of the applications to the [classification of manifolds](wikipedia://en/14069478) there are extensive calculations of the quadratic $L$-groups $L_*(\mathbf{Z}[\pi])$. For finite $\pi$ algebraic methods are used, and mostly geometric methods (e.g. controlled topology) are used for infinite $\pi$.

More generally, one can define *L*-groups for any [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854) with a *chain duality*, as in Ranicki (section 1).

### Integers {#Integers}

The **simply connected *L*-groups** are also the *L*-groups of the integers, as $L(e) := L(\mathbf{Z}[e]) = L(\mathbf{Z})$ for both $L$ = $L^*$ or $L_*.$ For quadratic *L*-groups, these are the surgery obstructions to [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) surgery.

The quadratic *L*-groups of the integers are:

$$\begin{aligned} L_{4k}(\mathbf{Z}) &= \mathbf{Z} && \text{signature}/8\\ L_{4k+1}(\mathbf{Z}) &= 0\\ L_{4k+2}(\mathbf{Z}) &= \mathbf{Z}/2 && \text{Arf invariant}\\ L_{4k+3}(\mathbf{Z}) &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

In [doubly even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubly_even) dimension (4*k*), the quadratic *L*-groups detect the [signature](wikipedia://en/1152311); in [singly even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singly_even) dimension (4*k*+2), the *L*-groups detect the [Arf invariant](wikipedia://en/6950659) (topologically the [Kervaire invariant](wikipedia://en/13816037)).

The symmetric *L*-groups of the integers are:

$$\begin{aligned} L^{4k}(\mathbf{Z}) &= \mathbf{Z} && \text{signature}\\ L^{4k+1}(\mathbf{Z}) &= \mathbf{Z}/2 && \text{de Rham invariant}\\ L^{4k+2}(\mathbf{Z}) &= 0\\ L^{4k+3}(\mathbf{Z}) &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

In doubly even dimension (4*k*), the symmetric *L*-groups, as with the quadratic *L*-groups, detect the signature; in dimension (4*k*+1), the *L*-groups detect the [de Rham invariant](wikipedia://en/25901631).
