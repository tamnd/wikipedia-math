# Total coloring

*Image: Total coloring foster cage.svg.* Proper total coloring of [Foster cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_cage) with 6 colors. The **total chromatic number** of this graph is 6 since the degree of each vertex is 5 (5 adjacent edges + 1 vertex = 6).

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

**Conjecture:** $\chi''(G) \le \Delta(G)+2.$

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Total coloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **total coloring** is a type of [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) on the [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) of a graph. When used without any qualification, a total coloring is always assumed to be *proper* in the sense that no adjacent edges, no adjacent vertices and no edge and either endvertex are assigned the same color. The **total chromatic number** $\chi''(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the fewest colors needed in any total coloring of $G$.

:::

The **total graph** $T = T(G)$ of a graph $G$ is a graph such that (i) the vertex set of $T$ corresponds to the vertices and edges of $G$ and (ii) two vertices are adjacent in $T$ if and only if their corresponding elements are either adjacent or incident in $G$. Then total coloring of $G$ becomes a [(proper) vertex coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) of $T(G)$. A total coloring is a partitioning of the vertices and edges of the graph into [total independent sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_independent_set).

Some inequalities for $\chi''(G)$:

1. $$\chi''(G) \geq \Delta(G) + 1.$$
1. $\chi''(G) \leq \Delta(G) + 10^{26}.$ (Molloy, Reed 1998)
1. $\chi''(G) \leq \Delta(G) + 8 \ln^8(\Delta(G))$ for sufficiently large $\Delta(G)$. (Hind, Molloy, Reed 1998)
1. $$\chi''(G) \leq \operatorname{ch}'(G) + 2.$$

Here $\Delta(G)$ is the [maximum degree](wikipedia://en/325802); and $\operatorname{ch}'(G)$, the [edge choosability](wikipedia://en/690686).

Total coloring arises naturally since it is simply a mixture of vertex and edge colorings. The next step is to look for any [Brooks](wikipedia://en/21042117)-typed or [Vizing](wikipedia://en/690647)-typed upper bound on the total chromatic number in terms of maximum degree.

The total coloring version of maximum degree upper bound is a difficult problem that has eluded mathematicians for 50 years. A trivial lower bound for $\chi''(G)$ is $\Delta(G) + 1$. Some graphs such as cycles of length $n \not \equiv 0 \bmod 3$ and complete bipartite graphs of the form $K_{n,n}$ need $\Delta(G) + 2$ colors but no graph has been found that requires more colors. This leads to the speculation that every graph needs either $\Delta(G) + 1$ or $\Delta(G) + 2$ colors, but never more:

**Total coloring conjecture** ([Behzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Behzad), Vizing). $\chi''(G) \le \Delta(G)+2.$

Apparently, the term "total coloring" and the statement of total coloring conjecture were independently introduced by [Behzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Behzad) and [Vizing](wikipedia://en/21749361) in numerous occasions between 1964 and 1968 (see Jensen & Toft). The conjecture is known to hold for a few important classes of graphs, such as all [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431) and most [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) except those with maximum degree 6. The planar case can be completed if [Vizing's planar graph conjecture](wikipedia://en/690647) is true. Also, if the [list coloring conjecture](wikipedia://en/690686) is true, then $\chi''(G) \le \Delta(G) +3.$

Results related to total coloring have been obtained. For example, Kilakos and Reed (1993) proved that the [fractional chromatic number](wikipedia://en/692369) of the total graph of a graph $G$ is at most $\Delta(G) + 2$.
