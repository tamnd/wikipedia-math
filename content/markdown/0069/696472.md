# Axiom of constructibility

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Axiom of constructibility"}

The **axiom of constructibility** is a possible [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) for [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) in mathematics that asserts that every set is [constructible](wikipedia://en/343335). The axiom is usually written as ***V* = *L***, where $V$ represents the [von Neumann universe](wikipedia://en/445980) of all [well-founded sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-founded_set), and $L$ represents the constructible sets. In [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) (ZF), the property of being constructible is expressible as a single formula $\mathrm{Constructible}(x)$, and every set is in $V$, so the axiom can be written in the language of ZF in the form $\forall x\;\mathrm{Constructible}(x)$.

:::

The axiom of constructibility, first investigated by [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), is inconsistent with the proposition that [zero sharp](wikipedia://en/248085) exists and stronger [large cardinal axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinal_axiom) (see [list of large cardinal properties](wikipedia://en/3096183)). Generalizations (i.e. weaker versions) of this axiom are explored in [inner model theory](wikipedia://en/3796621).[^o2]

## Implications {#Implications}

The axiom of constructibility implies the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) (AC), given [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) without the axiom of choice (ZF). It also settles many natural mathematical questions that are independent of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of choice (ZFC); for example, the axiom of constructibility implies the [generalized continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705#Generalized_continuum_hypothesis),[^o4] the negation of [Suslin's hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin's_hypothesis), and the existence of an [analytical](wikipedia://en/634233) (in fact, $\Delta^1_2$) [non-measurable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-measurable) set of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), all of which are independent of ZFC.

The axiom of constructibility implies the non-existence of those [large cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinals) with [consistency strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_strength) greater or equal to [0<sup>\#</sup>](wikipedia://en/248085), which includes some "relatively small" large cardinals. For example, no cardinal can be $\omega_1$-[Erdős](wikipedia://en/326540) in $L$. While $L$ does contain the [initial ordinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_ordinal) of those large cardinals (when they exist in a supermodel of $L$), and they are still initial ordinals in $L$, it excludes the auxiliary structures (e.g. [measures](wikipedia://en/248102)) that endow those cardinals with their large cardinal properties.

Although the axiom of constructibility does resolve many set-theoretic questions, it is not typically accepted as an axiom for set theory in the same way as the ZFC axioms. Among set theorists of a [realist](wikipedia://en/46439#Mathematical_realism) bent, who believe that the axiom of constructibility is either true or false, most believe that it is false.[^o5] This is in part because it seems unnecessarily "restrictive", as it allows only certain subsets of a given set (for example, $0^\sharp\subseteq \omega$ can't exist), with no clear reason to believe that these are all of them. In part it is because the axiom is contradicted by sufficiently strong [large cardinal axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinal_axiom). This point of view is especially associated with the [Cabal](wikipedia://en/2275193), or the "California school" as [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) would have it.

## In arithmetic {#In_arithmetic}

Especially from the 1950s to the 1970s, there have been some investigations into formulating an analogue of the axiom of constructibility for [subsystems of second-order arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3542454#Subsystems). A few results stand out in the study of such analogues:

- John Addison's $\Sigma_2^1$ formula $\textrm{Constr}(X)$ with the property that $\mathcal P(\omega)\vDash\textrm{Constr}(X)$ if and only if $X\in\mathcal P(\omega)\cap L$, i.e., $X$ is a constructible real.[^o7][^o8]
- There is a $\Pi_3^1$ formula known as the "analytical form of the axiom of constructibility" that has some associations to the set-theoretic axiom $V=L$.[^o9] For example, some cases where $M\vDash V=L$ if and only if $M\cap\mathcal P(\omega)\vDash\text{analytical form of }V=L$ have been given.[^o9]

## Significance {#Significance}

The major significance of the axiom of constructibility is in [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)'s 1938 proof of the relative [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) of the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) and the [generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis) to [Von Neumann–Bernays–Gödel set theory](wikipedia://en/528491). (The proof carries over to [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214), which has become more prevalent in recent years.)

Namely Gödel proved that $V=L$ is relatively consistent (i.e., if $\mathrm{ZFC} + (V=L)$ can prove a contradiction, then so can $\mathrm{ZF}$), and that in $\mathrm{ZF}$

$$V=L\implies \mathrm{AC}\land \mathrm{GCH},$$

thereby establishing that AC and GCH are also relatively consistent.[^o4]

Gödel's proof was complemented in 1962 by [Paul Cohen](wikipedia://en/22994)'s result that both AC and GCH are *independent*, i.e., that the negations of these axioms ($\lnot AC$ and $\lnot GCH$) are also relatively consistent to ZF set theory.

## Statements true in *L* {#Statements_true_in_L}

Here is a list of propositions that hold in the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) (denoted by $L$):

- The existence of a definable [well-order](wikipedia://en/33456) of all sets (the formula for which can be given explicitly), and as consequences
  - The existence of a definable [global choice function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_choice_function), and thus the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840)
  - Every set is an [ordinal definable set](wikipedia://en/14566906) (V=HOD)
- The [generalized continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705#The_generalized_continuum_hypothesis)[^o4]
- Jensen's [diamond principles](wikipedia://en/691736) $\lozenge$, $\lozenge^{*}$, and $\lozenge^{+}$ (and hence also Ostaszewski's [club principle](wikipedia://en/691634) $\text{♣}$)
  - The negation of the [Suslin hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suslin_hypothesis)[^o13]
- [Global square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_square)
- The existence of [morasses](wikipedia://en/20194751)
- The non-existence of [0<sup>\#</sup>](wikipedia://en/248085) and as a consequence
  - The non existence of all [large cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinals) that imply the existence of a [measurable cardinal](wikipedia://en/248102)
- The existence of a $\Delta_2^1$ set of reals (in the [analytical hierarchy](wikipedia://en/634233)) that is not [measurable](wikipedia://en/18198).
- The truth of [Whitehead's conjecture](wikipedia://en/212619) that every [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $A$ with $\mathrm{Ext}^1(A,\mathbb{Z})=0$ is a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296), where $\mathrm{Ext}$ is the [Ext functor](wikipedia://en/840758).
- The existence of a [primitive recursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_recursive) [class](wikipedia://en/20696) surjection $F:\mathrm{Ord}\to V$, i.e., a class function from $\mathrm{Ord}$ whose range contains all sets. [^o14]

Accepting the axiom of constructibility (which asserts that every set is [constructible](wikipedia://en/343335)) these propositions also hold in the [von Neumann universe](wikipedia://en/445980), resolving many propositions in set theory and some interesting questions in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).
