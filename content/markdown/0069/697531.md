# Geometric–harmonic mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric–harmonic mean"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **geometric–harmonic mean** M(*x*, *y*) of two positive [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) *x* and *y* is defined as follows: we form the [geometric mean](wikipedia://en/13046) of *g*<sub>0</sub> = *x* and *h*<sub>0</sub> = *y* and call it *g*<sub>1</sub>, i.e. *g*<sub>1</sub> is the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of *xy*. We also form the [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) of *x* and *y* and call it *h*<sub>1</sub>, i.e. *h*<sub>1</sub> is the [reciprocal](wikipedia://en/229940) of the [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of the reciprocals of *x* and *y*. These may be done sequentially (in any order) or simultaneously.

:::

Now we can iterate this operation with *g*<sub>1</sub> taking the place of *x* and *h*<sub>1</sub> taking the place of *y*. In this way, two interdependent [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) (*g*<sub>*n*</sub>) and (*h*<sub>*n*</sub>) are defined:

$$g_{n+1} = \sqrt{g_n h_n}$$

and

$$h_{n+1} = \frac{2{g_n}{h_n}}{g_n + h_n}$$

Both of these sequences [converge](wikipedia://en/26551602) to the same number, which we call the **geometric–harmonic mean** M(*x*, *y*) of *x* and *y*. The geometric–harmonic mean is also designated as the **harmonic–geometric mean**. (cf. Wolfram MathWorld below.)

The existence of the limit can be proved by the means of [Bolzano&ndash;Weierstrass theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano&ndash;Weierstrass_theorem) in a manner almost identical to the proof of existence of [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

M(*x*, *y*) is a number between the geometric and harmonic mean of *x* and *y*; in particular it is between *x* and *y*. M(*x*, *y*) is also [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/622844), i.e. if *r* > 0, then M(*rx*, *ry*) = *r* M(*x*, *y*).

If AG(*x*, *y*) is the [arithmetic–geometric mean](wikipedia://en/3090), then we also have

$$M(x,y) = \frac{1}{AG(\frac{1}{x},\frac{1}{y})}$$

:::

## Inequalities {#Inequalities}

We have the following inequality involving the Pythagorean means {*H*, *G*, *A*} and iterated Pythagorean means {*HG*, *HA*, *GA*}:

$$\min(x,y) \leq H(x,y) \leq HG(x,y) \leq G(x,y) \leq GA(x,y) \leq A(x,y) \leq \max(x,y)$$

where the iterated Pythagorean means have been identified with their parts {*H*, *G*, *A*} in progressing order:

- *H*(*x*, *y*) is the harmonic mean,
- *HG*(*x*, *y*) is the harmonic–geometric mean,
- *G*(*x*, *y*) = *HA*(*x*, *y*) is the geometric mean (which is also the harmonic–arithmetic mean),
- *GA*(*x*, *y*) is the geometric–arithmetic mean,
- *A*(*x*, *y*) is the arithmetic mean.
