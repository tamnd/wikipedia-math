# Probability of error

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Probability of error"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the term "error" arises in two ways. Firstly, it arises in the context of [decision making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_making), where the **probability of error** may be considered as being the probability of making a wrong decision and which would have a different value for each type of error. Secondly, it arises in the context of [statistical modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_modelling) (for example regression) where the model's predicted value may be in error regarding the observed outcome and where the term **probability of error** may refer to the probabilities of various amounts of error occurring.

:::

## Hypothesis testing {#Hypothesis_testing}

In [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), two types of *[error](wikipedia://en/41118)* are distinguished.

- [Type I errors](wikipedia://en/5657877) which consist of rejecting a [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) that is true; this amounts to a false positive result.
- [Type II errors](wikipedia://en/5657877) which consist of failing to reject a null hypothesis that is false; this amounts to a false negative result.[^o4]

The **probability of error** is similarly distinguished.

- For a Type I error, it is shown as α (alpha) and is known as the *size* of the test and is 1 minus the [specificity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specificity_%28tests%29) of the test. This quantity is sometimes referred to as the confidence of the test, or the level of significance (LOS) of the test.
- For a Type II error, it is shown as β (beta) and is 1 minus the [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_power) or 1 minus the [sensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_%28tests%29) of the test.

:::

## Statistical and econometric modelling {#Statistical_and_econometric_modelling}

The fitting of many [models](wikipedia://en/27576) in statistics and [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) usually seeks to minimise the difference between observed and predicted or theoretical values. This difference is known as an *error*, though when observed it would be better described as a *[residual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics)*.

The error is taken to be a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) and as such has a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543). Thus distribution can be used to calculate the probabilities of errors with values within any given range.
