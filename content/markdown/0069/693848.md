# Hypergeometric identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypergeometric identity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **hypergeometric identities** are equalities involving sums over hypergeometric terms, i.e. the coefficients occurring in [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series). These [identities](wikipedia://en/298428) occur frequently in solutions to [combinatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial) problems, and also in the [analysis of algorithms](wikipedia://en/2230).

:::

These identities were traditionally found 'by hand'. There exist now several algorithms which can find and *prove* all hypergeometric identities.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

$$\sum_{i=0}^{n} {n \choose i} = 2^{n}$$

$$\sum_{i=0}^{n} {n \choose i}^2 = {2n \choose n}$$

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} k {n \choose k} = n2^{n-1}$$

$$\sum_{i=n}^{N} i{i \choose n} = (n+1){N+2\choose n+2}-{N+1\choose n+1}$$

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are two definitions of hypergeometric terms, both used in different cases as explained below. See also [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series).

A term *t<sub>k</sub>* is a hypergeometric term if

$$\frac{t_{k+1}}{t_k}$$

is a [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210) in *k*.

A term *F(n,k)* is a hypergeometric term if

$$\frac{F(n,k+1)}{F(n,k)}$$

is a rational function in *k*.

There exist two types of sums over hypergeometric terms, the definite and indefinite sums. A definite sum is of the form

$$\sum_{k} t_k.$$

The indefinite sum is of the form

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n} F(n,k).$$

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

Although in the past proofs have been found for many specific identities, there exist several general algorithms to find and prove identities. These algorithms first find a *simple expression* for a sum over hypergeometric terms and then provide a certificate which anyone can use to check and prove the correctness of the identity.

For each of the hypergeometric sum types there exist one or more methods to find a *simple expression*. These methods also provide the certificate to check the identity's proof:

- *Definite sums*: [Sister Celine](wikipedia://en/7409868)'s Method, [Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825)'s algorithm
- *Indefinite sums*: [Gosper's algorithm](wikipedia://en/19590493)

The book **A = B** by [Marko Petkovšek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Petkovšek), [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688) and [Doron Zeilberger](wikipedia://en/1724825) describes the three main approaches mentioned above.
