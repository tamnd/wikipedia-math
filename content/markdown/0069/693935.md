# Trinomial expansion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trinomial expansion"}

*Image: Pascal\_pyramid\_trinomial.svg.* Layers of [Pascal's pyramid](wikipedia://en/708349) derived from coefficients in an upside-down [ternary plot](wikipedia://en/1861608) of the terms in the expansions of the powers of a trinomial – the number of terms is clearly a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915)

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **trinomial expansion** is the expansion of a power of a sum of three terms into [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416). The expansion is given by

:::

$$(a+b+c)^n = \sum_{{i,j,k}\atop{i+j+k=n}} {n \choose i,j,k}\, a^i \, b^{\;\! j} \;\! c^k,$$

where $n$ is a nonnegative integer and the sum is taken over all combinations of nonnegative indices $i, j,$ and $k$ such that $i + j + k = n$.[^o2] The **trinomial coefficients** are given by

$${n \choose i,j,k} = \frac{n!}{i!\,j!\,k!} \,.$$

This formula is a special case of the [multinomial formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinomial_formula) for $m = 3$. The coefficients can be defined with a generalization of [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) to three dimensions, called [Pascal's pyramid](wikipedia://en/708349) or Pascal's tetrahedron.[^o3]

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The trinomial expansion can be calculated by applying the [binomial expansion](wikipedia://en/4677) twice, setting $d = b+c$, which leads to

$$\begin{aligned} (a+b+c)^n &= (a+d)^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n} {n \choose r}\, a^{n-r}\, d^{r} \\ &= \sum_{r=0}^{n} {n \choose r}\, a^{n-r}\, (b+c)^{r} \\ &= \sum_{r=0}^{n} {n \choose r}\, a^{n-r}\, \sum_{s=0}^{r} {r \choose s}\, b^{r-s}\,c^{s}. \end{aligned}$$

Above, the resulting $(b+c)^{r}$ in the second line is evaluated by the second application of the binomial expansion, introducing another summation over the index $s$.

The product of the two binomial coefficients is simplified by shortening $r!$,

$${n \choose r}\,{r \choose s} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} \frac{r!}{s!(r-s)!} = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!(r-s)!s!},$$

and comparing the index combinations here with the ones in the exponents, they can be relabelled to $i=n-r, ~ j=r-s, ~ k = s$, which provides the expression given in the first paragraph.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The number of terms of an expanded trinomial is the [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915)

$$t_{n+1} = \frac{(n+2)(n+1)}{2},$$

where $n$ is the exponent to which the trinomial is raised.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Examples of trinomial expansions with $n=2,3,4$ are:

$$(a+b+c)^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+2(ab+bc+ac)$$

$$(a+b+c)^3=a^3+b^3+c^3+3(a^2b+ab^2+b^2c+bc^2+ac^2+a^2c) + 6abc$$

$$(a+b+c)^4=a^4+b^4+c^4+4(a^3b+ab^3+b^3c+bc^3+a^3c+ac^3) + 6(a^2b^2+b^2c^2+a^2c^2) + 12(a^2bc+ab^2c+abc^2)$$

:::
