# Exotic R4

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exotic R4"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **exotic** $\mathbb{R}^4$ is a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) that is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) (i.e. shape preserving) but not [diffeomorphic](wikipedia://en/8564) (i.e. non smooth) to the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^4.$ The first examples were found in 1982 by [Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) and others, by using the contrast between Freedman's theorems about topological 4-manifolds, and [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305)'s theorems about smooth 4-manifolds.[^o2][^o3] There is a [continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901) of non-diffeomorphic [differentiable structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_structure) $\mathbb{R}^4,$ as was shown first by [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920).[^o4]

:::

Prior to this construction, non-diffeomorphic [smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1542238) on spheres – [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931) – were already known to exist, although the question of the existence of such structures for the particular case of the [4-sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-sphere) remains open as of 2026. For any positive integer *n* other than 4, there are no exotic smooth structures $\mathbb{R}^n;$ in other words, if *n* ≠ 4 then any smooth manifold homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n$ is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^n.$[^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

### Construction from the failure of smooth surgery theory {#Construction_from_the_failure_of_smooth_surgery_theory}

Exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ arise from the failure of smooth [surgery theory](wikipedia://en/5961115), in particular glueing a disc $D^4$ to its boundary sphere $S^3$ not resulting in a smooth 4-manifold. Exotic 7-spheres called [Milnor spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_sphere) arise from a fully analogous failure of glueing a disc $D^8$ to its boundary sphere $S^7$ not resulting in a smooth 8-manifold. But the conclusions are different: In the latter case, $D^8\cong\mathbb{R}^8$ has no exotic smooth structure, so $S^7$ has to in order to explain the failure. In the former case, $S^3$ has no exotic smooth structure due to [Moise's theorem](wikipedia://en/25824647),[^o9] so $D^4\cong\mathbb{R}^4$ has to in order to explain the failure.

#### Construction from the K3 surface {#Construction_from_the_K3_surface}

A concrete construction is the [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116) $\mathrm{K3}$, described by the homogenous equation $x^4+y^4+z^4+w^4=0$ in [twistor space](wikipedia://en/2688710) $\mathbb{C}P^3$.[^o11][^o12] According to [Freedman's classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman_classification),[^o13][^o14] it is homeomorphic to the [connected sum](wikipedia://en/490134) $2\overline{M_{E_8}}\#3(S^2\times S^2)$ of twice the orientation-reversed [E8 manifold](wikipedia://en/6091657) $M_{E_8}$ and thrice the [complex surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_surface) $S^2\times S^2\cong\mathbb{C}P^1\times\mathbb{C}P^1$ gained from the product of the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) with itself. Although $\mathrm{K3}$ is a smooth 4-manifold, $2\overline{M_{E_8}}\#k(S^2\times S^2)$ isn't for $k=0,1,2$. For $k=0$, this follows from [Donaldson's theorem](wikipedia://en/2180754),[^o13][^o15] and for $k=1,2$, it was also shown by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305),[^o16] although Donaldson's theorem itself no longer applies. (A simpler argument was later obtained with [Seiberg–Witten invariants](wikipedia://en/14407762).) Hence a smooth surgery along the connecting sphere $S^3$ to remove one, two or three $S^2\times S^2$ from the smooth K3 surface $\mathrm{K3}$ must fail.[^o17] (It is an open question whether this also holds for any connected sum of the K3 surface $\mathrm{K3}$, which is known as [11/8 conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11/8_conjecture).) In the simplest surgery of removing three $S^2\times S^2$, the failure spawns an exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ in $\#3(S^2\times S^2)$.

#### Construction from a nine-fold blow-up {#Construction_from_a_nine-fold_blow-up}

The [intersection form](wikipedia://en/19057150) of the smooth [complex surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_surface) $\mathbb{C}P^2\#9\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$, the simplest example of an [elliptic surface](wikipedia://en/3073462) known as [Dolgachev surface](wikipedia://en/22729603), is $[+1]\oplus 9[-1] \cong -E_8\oplus[+1]\oplus[-1]$[^o22] with the isomorphism coming from the [Hesse–Minkowski theorem](wikipedia://en/1109126). It is therefore the same as for the topological 4-manifold $\overline{M_{E_8}}\#\mathbb{C}P^2\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$ using the [fake second complex projective space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_second_complex_projective_space), so Freedman's classification[^o13][^o14] claims both to be homeomorphic. In particular, that makes $\overline{M_{E_8}}\#\mathbb{C}P^2\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$ smoothable. But a smooth surgery to remove $\mathbb{C}P^2$ results in the non-smoothable manifold $\overline{M_{E_8}}\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$,[^o14] which follows from its intersection form $-E_8\oplus[-1]$ being negative definite and not diagonalizable, which Donaldson's theorem forbids if it was smoothable. The failure of this smooth surgery spawns an exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ in $\mathbb{C}P^2$.

#### Construction from a ten-fold blow-up {#Construction_from_a_ten-fold_blow-up}

The previous construction can be expanded: The intersection form of the smooth complex surface $\mathbb{C}P^2\# 10\overline{\mathbb{C}P^9}$ is $[+1]\oplus 10[-1] \cong -E_8\oplus H\oplus[-1]$,[^o22] the same as for the topological 4-manifold $\overline{M_{E_8}}\#(S^2\times S^2)\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$, so Freedman's classification[^o13][^o14] claims both to be homeomorphic and in particular the latter to be smoothable. But a smooth surgery to remove $S^2\times S^2$ again results in the non-smoothable manifold $\overline{M_{E_8}}\#*\overline{\mathbb{C}P^2}$. The failure of this smooth surgery spawns an exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ in $S^2\times S^2$.

::: {#o21 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Construction from the failure of the smooth h-cobordism theorem"}

### Construction from the failure of the smooth h-cobordism theorem {#Construction_from_the_failure_of_the_smooth_h-cobordism_theorem}

According to Michael Freedman, the four-dimensional topological [h-cobordism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-cobordism_theorem) holds, while according to Simon Donaldson, the four-dimensional smooth h-cobordism theorem doesn't hold. Let $W$ be a smooth h-cobordism between smooth 4-manifolds $M$ and $N$, for which there is no diffeomorphism $W\cong M\times[0,1]$, although there must be a homeomorphism $W\cong M\times[0,1]$. In this case there exists an open sub-cobordism $U\subset W$ with a homeomorphism $U\cong D^4\times[0,1]\cong\mathbb{R}^4\times[0,1]$ and a compact contractible sub-cobordism $K\subset U$, so that $W$ and $U$ are indeed smoothly trivial cobordisms outside of $K$, hence there are diffeomorphisms:

$$W\setminus K \cong(M\setminus K)\times[0,1];$$

$$U\setminus K \cong(U\cap M\setminus K)\times[0,1].$$

Now $U$ connects the two open subsets $U\cap M\setminus K$ and $U\cap N\setminus K$, which are homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^4$, but cannot diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^4$ as this would cause a diffeomorphism $W\cong M\times[0,1]$.[^o22]

## Small exotic R<sup>4</sup>s {#Small_exotic_R4s}

An exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ is called **small** if it can be smoothly embedded as an open subset of the standard $\mathbb{R}^4.$

Small exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ can be constructed by starting with a non-trivial smooth 5-dimensional *h*-[cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) (which exists by Donaldson's proof that the [*h*-cobordism theorem](wikipedia://en/681049) fails in this dimension) and using Freedman's theorem that the topological *h*-cobordism theorem holds in this dimension.

## Large exotic R<sup>4</sup>s {#Large_exotic_R4s}

An exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ is called **large** if it cannot be smoothly embedded as an open subset of the standard $\mathbb{R}^4.$

Examples of large exotic $\mathbb{R}^4$ can be constructed using the fact that compact 4-manifolds can often be split as a topological sum (by Freedman's work), but cannot be split as a smooth sum (by Donaldson's work).

(txt, 69–78) showed that there is a maximal exotic $\mathbb{R}^4,$ into which all other $\mathbb{R}^4$ can be smoothly embedded as open subsets.

## Related exotic structures {#Related_exotic_structures}

[Casson handles](wikipedia://en/7024176) are homeomorphic to $\mathbb{D}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2$ by Freedman's theorem (where $\mathbb{D}^2$ is the closed unit disc) but it follows from Donaldson's theorem that they are not all diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{D}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2.$ In other words, some Casson handles are exotic $\mathbb{D}^2 \times \mathbb{R}^2.$

It is not known (as of 2024) whether or not there are any exotic 4-spheres; such an exotic 4-sphere would be a counterexample to the smooth [generalized Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/8536216) in dimension 4. Some plausible candidates are given by [Gluck twists](wikipedia://en/1114931#4-dimensional_exotic_spheres_and_Gluck_twists).
