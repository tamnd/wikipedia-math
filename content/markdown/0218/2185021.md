# Serre's property FA

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Serre's property FA"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Property FA** is a property of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) first defined by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483).

:::

A group *G* is said to have property FA if every [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *G* on a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) has a global [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738).

Serre shows that if a group has property FA, then it cannot split as an [amalgamated product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamated_product) or [HNN extension](wikipedia://en/2823319); indeed, if *G* is contained in an amalgamated product then it is contained in one of the factors. In particular, a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/99945) group with property FA has finite [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_group).

Property FA is equivalent for [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) *G* to the three properties: *G* is not an amalgamated product; *G* does not have **Z** as a [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526); *G* is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042). For general groups *G* the third condition may be replaced by requiring that *G* not be the union of a strictly increasing sequence of subgroup.

Examples of groups with property FA include SL<sub>3</sub>(**Z**) and more generally *G*(**Z**) where *G* is a simply-connected simple [Chevalley group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley_group) of rank at least 2. The group SL<sub>2</sub>(**Z**) is an exception, since it is isomorphic to the amalgamated product of the cyclic groups *C*<sub>4</sub> and *C*<sub>6</sub> along *C*<sub>2</sub>.

Any [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) of a group with property FA has property FA. If some subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in *G* has property FA then so does *G*, but the converse does not hold in general. If *N* is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G* and both *N* and *G*/*N* have property FA, then so does *G*.

It is a theorem of Watatani that [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724) implies property FA, but not conversely. Indeed, any subgroup of finite index in a T-group has property FA.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following groups have property FA:

- A finitely generated torsion group;
- SL<sub>3</sub>(**Z**);
- The Schwarz group $\left\langle{ a,b : a^A = b^B = (ab)^C = 1 }\right\rangle$ for integers *A*,*B*,*C* ≥ 2;
- SL<sub>2</sub>(*R*) where *R* is the ring of integers of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) which is not **Q** or an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field).

The following groups do not have property FA:

- SL<sub>2</sub>(**Z**);
- SL<sub>2</sub>(*R*<sub>*D*</sub>) where *R*<sub>*D*</sub> is the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant not −3 or −4.
