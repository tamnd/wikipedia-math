# Donaldson's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donaldson's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and especially [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562) and [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/64324611), **Donaldson's theorem** states that a [definite](wikipedia://en/20437320) [intersection form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_form_%284-manifold%29) of a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475), [oriented](wikipedia://en/187446), [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) of [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) 4 is [diagonalizable](wikipedia://en/246325). If the intersection form is positive (negative) definite, it can be diagonalized to the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) (negative identity matrix) over the integers. The original version[^o2] of the theorem required the manifold to be [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected), but it was later improved to apply to 4-manifolds with any [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

The theorem was proved by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305). This was a contribution cited for his [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1986.[^o5]

## Idea of proof {#Idea_of_proof}

Donaldson's proof utilizes the [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) $\mathcal{M}_P$ of solutions to the [anti-self-duality equations](wikipedia://en/35544664#Anti-self-duality_equations) on a [principal $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-bundle](wikipedia://en/80364907) $P$ over the four-manifold $X$. By the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752), the dimension of the moduli space is given by

$$\dim \mathcal{M} = 8k - 3(1-b_1(X) + b_+(X)),$$

where $k=c_2(P)$ is a [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349), $b_1(X)$ is the first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of $X$, and $b_+(X)$ is the dimension of the positive-definite subspace of $H_2(X,\mathbb{R})$ with respect to the intersection form. When $X$ is simply-connected with definite intersection form, possibly after changing orientation, one always has $b_1(X) = 0$ and $b_+(X)=0$. Thus taking any principal $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-bundle with $k=1$, one obtains a moduli space $\mathcal{M}$ of dimension five.

*Image: Donaldson's Theorem cobordism.png.* Vissualisation[^o7][^o8] of the cobordism given by the [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) in Donaldson's theorem

This moduli space is non-compact and generically smooth, with singularities occurring only at the points corresponding to reducible connections, of which there are exactly $b_2(X)$ many.[^o9] Results of [Clifford Taubes](wikipedia://en/6893920) and [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796) show that whilst $\mathcal{M}$ is non-compact, its structure at infinity can be readily described.[^o10][^o11][^o12] Namely, there is an open subset of $\mathcal{M}$, say $\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}$, such that for sufficiently small choices of parameter $\varepsilon$, there is a diffeomorphism

$$\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon} \xrightarrow{\quad \cong\quad} X\times (0,\varepsilon).$$

The work of Taubes and Uhlenbeck essentially concerns constructing sequences of ASD connections on the four-manifold $X$ with curvature becoming infinitely concentrated at any given single point $x\in X$. For each such point, in the limit one obtains a unique singular ASD connection, which becomes a well-defined smooth ASD connection at that point using [Uhlenbeck's singularity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhlenbeck's_singularity_theorem).[^o12][^o9]

Donaldson observed that the singular points in the interior of $\mathcal{M}$ corresponding to reducible connections could also be described: they looked like [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28linear_algebra%29) over the [complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/737155) $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Furthermore, we can count the number of such singular points. Let $E$ be the $\mathbb{C}^2$-bundle over $X$ associated to $P$ by the standard representation of $SU(2)$. Then, reducible connections modulo gauge are in a 1-1 correspondence with splittings $E = L\oplus L^{-1}$ where $L$ is a complex line bundle over $X$.[^o9] Whenever $E = L\oplus L^{-1}$ we may compute:[^o13]

$$1 = k = c_2(E) = c_2(L\oplus L^{-1}) = - Q(c_1(L), c_1(L)),$$

where $Q$ is the intersection form on the second cohomology of $X$. Since line bundles over $X$ are classified by their first Chern class $c_1(L)\in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$, we get that reducible connections modulo gauge are in a 1-1 correspondence with pairs $\pm\alpha\in H^2(X; \mathbb{Z})$ such that $Q(\alpha, \alpha) = -1$. Let the number of pairs be $n(Q)$. An elementary argument that applies to any negative definite quadratic form over the integers tells us that $n(Q)\leq\text{rank}(Q)$, with equality if and only if $Q$ is diagonalizable.[^o9]

It is thus possible to compactify the moduli space as follows: First, cut off each cone at a reducible singularity and glue in a copy of $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Secondly, glue in a copy of $X$ itself at infinity. The resulting space is a [cobordism](wikipedia://en/391832) between $X$ and a disjoint union of $n(Q)$ copies of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (of unknown orientations). The signature $\sigma$ of a four-manifold is a cobordism invariant. Thus, because $X$ is definite:

$$\text{rank}(Q) = b_2(X) = \sigma(X) = \sigma(\bigsqcup n(Q) \mathbb{CP}^2) \leq n(Q),$$

from which one concludes the intersection form of $X$ is diagonalizable.

## Extensions {#Extensions}

[Michael Freedman](wikipedia://en/658970) had previously shown that any [unimodular symmetric bilinear form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodular_symmetric_bilinear_form) is realized as the intersection form of some closed, oriented [four-manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-manifold). Combining this result with the [Serre classification theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_classification_theorem) and Donaldson's theorem, several interesting results can be seen:

\1) Any definite non-diagonalizable intersection form gives rise to a four-dimensional [topological manifold](wikipedia://en/2119193) with no [differentiable structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_structure) (so cannot be smoothed).

\2) Two smooth simply-connected 4-manifolds are [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic), if and only if, their intersection forms have the same [rank](wikipedia://en/26561), [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_quadratic_form), and parity.

:::
