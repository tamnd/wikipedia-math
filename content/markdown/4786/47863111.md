# Susan Brown (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Susan Brown"}

**Susan North Brown** (22 December 1937 – 11 August 2017) was a professor of mathematics at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London)[^o2] and a leading researcher in the field of fluid mechanics.[^o3]

:::

## Background and employment {#Background_and_employment}

An exact timeline for Susan Brown's career has been difficult to pin down, but a newsletter published by Department of Mathematical and Physical Sciences at [UCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) shortly after her death offers a framework for her career achievements and highlights the esteem in which she was held by colleagues and students.[^o2] Her undergraduate degree in mathematics was from [St Hilda's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hilda's_College,_Oxford). For about two years more years she continued studies at Oxford, in theoretical [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988)—and she then moved to the [University of Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Durham) to complete her DPhil in 1964. During this time she held temporary Lectureships at both [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) and [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University) and in 1964 began a Lectureship that started her long association with [UCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). From her Lectureship she advanced to a Readership in 1971 and was appointed to a Professorship in 1986. The [afore-mentioned departmental newsletter](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/maths/news/2017/sep/dept-news-september-2017) that recapped her accomplishments after her death expresses the belief that Brown was the first female in the UK to be appointed to a professorship in Mathematics, but [Joan E. Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_E._Walsh) was promoted to a professorship in mathematics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) in 1974.[^o7]

## Research {#Research}

Brown's department described her as an outstanding teacher and someone with an international reputation for her research.[^o2] She had a productive partnership in fluid dynamics with [UCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) colleague [Keith Stewartson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Stewartson)—who also arrived at [UCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1964. Quoting from the afore-mentioned departmental newsletter, " Together they published 29 papers and pioneered early developments of 'triple-deck' theory, which, in turn, enabled resolution of long-standing questions in steady and unsteady trailing-edge flows, and addressed associated important aerodynamic applications. Another area for which Brown was especially renowned was a series of discussions of critical layers, especially effects of viscosity and nonlinearity and applications to geophysical flows such as atmospheric jets."[^o2][^o7] Google-[Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) lists numerous papers for the pair "SN Brown, K Stewartson" and several of these are listed below.

## Death {#Death}

Brown died on 11 August 2017, aged 79, in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London).[^o10]
