# Pietro Paoli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pietro Paoli"}

*Image: Pietro Paoli.png.* Frontpage of *Memorie sul calcolo integrale* (1793)

**Pietro Paoli** (2 March 1759 – 21 February 1839) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Paoli studied in the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) college of his birthplace. In 1774 he went to [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) to study law. In Pisa he graduated in 1778, but he was more interested in physical and mathematical issues.

In 1780 he became professor in the High School of [Mantua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantua) and in 1782 he was appointed professor in the [University of Pavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pavia), where he learned mathematics from [Gregorio Fontana](wikipedia://en/6942423).

In 1784 Paoli was appointed to the chair of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) in the University of Pisa, where he was until 1814. In the meantime he was appointed, also, *Regio Consultore Idraulico* of the [Grand Duchy of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Tuscany). During his docent period he had as students some important scientists: [Gabrio Piola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrio_Piola), [Vincenzo Brunacci](wikipedia://en/40294765), Pietro Franchini, Giuliano Frullani, [Giovanni Santini](wikipedia://en/15100928)[^o4] or [Antonio Bordoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Bordoni).[^o5]

In 1814 he became *Auditor* (Governor) of the University of Pisa and was in charge of the redaction of the new rules of the university.

From 1816 he lived in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) because he was named secretary of education of the Grand Duchy. Besides this function, he was named president of the commission in charge of drafting the new [cadastre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastre) of Tuscany.

He was member of the *[Società Italiana dei Quaranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Società_Italiana_dei_Quaranta)* from its foundation (1782) and of other Italian scientific academies.

Paoli worked mainly in [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and his applications to optics and mechanics. In spite of his duties, there are only two works on hydraulics.[^o6] His main work was *Elementi di Algebra* in two volumes (1794) which was widely distributed in Italy for many years.[^o7]
