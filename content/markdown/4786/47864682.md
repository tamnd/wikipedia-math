# Rikitaro Fujisawa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rikitaro Fujisawa"}

*Image: Dr. Rikitaro Fujisawa LCCN2014714935.jpg.* Fujisawa in 1922

**Rikitarō Fujisawa** (Japanese: 藤沢 利喜太郎, *Fujisawa Rikitarō*; 12 October 1861 – 23 December 1933) was a Japanese mathematician. During the [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era) he was instrumental in reforming mathematics education in Japan and establishing the ideas of European mathematics in Japan.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Sado Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sado_Province) as the eldest son of Oyano Fujisawa, vassal of the shōgun, Rikitarō Fujisawa graduated in 1882 from the Faculty of Sciences of the University of Tokyo. From 1883 to 1887 he studied mathematics in Europe. After study at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) and the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin), he studied at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) (then a part of Germany) and in 1886[^o4] attained his doctorate with a dissertation on partial differential equations under the direction of [Elwin Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Christoffel). In 1887 Fujisawa was appointed the second titular professor of mathematics at the University of Tokyo.[^o5]

He was Japan's delegate to the [Second International Conference on Mathematics, Paris, 1900](wikipedia://en/627842#History), and also to the International Commission on the Teaching of Mathematics, 1910. Although a mathematician by training, Dr. Fujisawa has written many articles on international politics.[^o6]

Fujisawa, who himself attended the seminary of [Theodor Reye](wikipedia://en/27942014) in Strasbourg, introduced the institution of the research seminary based on the German model early on.[^o7] He was a teacher and mentor of several Japanese mathematicians who gained international reputations. His most famous student was [Teiji Takagi](wikipedia://en/174754).[^o8] In 1921, Fujisawa retired from the University of Tokyo and, beginning in 1925, was twice appointed to the [Japanese House of Peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Peers_%28Japan%29) but died in the early part of his second term.

Two of his sons achieved prominence in Japanese society. One of his brothers, Iwao Fujisawa, was a [rear admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral) in the Imperial Japanese Navy.
