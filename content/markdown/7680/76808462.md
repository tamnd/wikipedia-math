# Supersilver ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supersilver ratio"}

In mathematics, the **supersilver ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](wikipedia://en/1021), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation $x3 = 2x2 + 1$. Its decimal expansion begins with $2.2055694304005903$ .

:::

The name *supersilver ratio* is by analogy with the [silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526), the positive solution of the equation $x2 = 2x + 1$, and the [supergolden ratio](wikipedia://en/58128086).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Supersilver cuboid.svg.* ς = $\tfrac{a+d}{a}$ = $\tfrac{a}{b}$ = $\tfrac{b}{d-a}$. For b = 1 the boxes have volumes $\varsigma3 = \varsigma2 + 1 (\operatorname{red}) + \varsigma2 (\operatorname{green}).$

Three quantities $a > b > c > 0$ are in the supersilver ratio if

$$\frac{2a+c}{a} =\frac{a}{b} =\frac{b}{c}\,.$$

This ratio is commonly denoted $\varsigma$.

Substituting $a=\varsigma \,b = \varsigma^2 c$ in the first fraction gives

$$\varsigma =\frac{2\varsigma^2 c + c}{\varsigma^2 c}.$$

It follows that the supersilver ratio is the unique real solution of the [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) $\varsigma^3 -2\varsigma^2 -1 =0.$

The [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) for the reciprocal root is the depressed cubic $x^3 +2x -1,$ thus the simplest solution with [Cardano's formula](wikipedia://en/180787#Cardano's_formula),

$$\begin{aligned} w_{1,2} &=\left( 1 \pm \frac{1}{3} \sqrt{ \frac{59}{3}} \right) /2 \\ 1 /\varsigma &=\sqrt[3]{w_1} +\sqrt[3]{w_2} \end{aligned}$$

or, using the [hyperbolic sine](wikipedia://en/180787#Trigonometric_and_hyperbolic_solutions),

$$1 /\varsigma =-2 \sqrt\frac{2}{3} \sinh \left( \frac{1}{3} \operatorname{arsinh} \left( -\frac{3}{4} \sqrt\frac{3}{2} \right) \right).$$

$1 /\varsigma$ is the superstable [fixed point](wikipedia://en/7344320) of the iteration $x \gets (2x^3 +1) /(3x^2 +2).$

Dividing the defining trinomial $x^3 -2x^2 -1$ by $x -\varsigma$ one obtains $x^2 +x /\varsigma^2 +1 /\varsigma ,$ and the [conjugate elements](wikipedia://en/458467) of $\varsigma$ are

$$x_{1,2} = \left( -1 \pm i \sqrt{8\varsigma^2 +3} \right) /2 \varsigma^2,$$

with $x_1 +x_2 = 2 -\varsigma \;$ and $\; x_1 x_2 =1 /\varsigma.$

Multiply the minimal polynomial by $x$, and rearrange the relation as $(x^2 +1)^2 =x+1.$ This results in the iteration $x_{n+1} \gets \sqrt{-1 +\sqrt{\phantom{|} 1+x_n}}$, with initial value $x_0 >0$, and the [continued radical](wikipedia://en/2503693#Infinitely_nested_radicals)

$$1/\varsigma =\sqrt{-1 +\sqrt{1 +\sqrt{-1 +\sqrt{\phantom{|} 1 +\cdots}}}}$$

Its counterpart is found by using polynomial $f:z^3 +z^2 -z -2$, which has real zero $\varsigma -1$. Multiply $f$ by $z-1$, then $(z^2 -1)^2 =z-1,$ and the corresponding iteration with $z_0 >1$ gives

$$\varsigma -1 =\sqrt{1 +\sqrt{-1 +\sqrt{1 +\sqrt{\phantom{|} -1 +\cdots}}}}$$

### Supersilver Julia sets {#Supersilver_Julia_sets}

Both systems have linear [convergence rate](wikipedia://en/999701) $\,\log_{10} \tfrac{4(\varsigma -1)}{\varsigma} =0.340...$ In order to improve this constant, divide both sides of $z^3 =z(1-z) +2 \,\text{ by } z,$ and substitute $1 /\varsigma =1 /(z+1) \,$ for $\, 2/z -z$, resulting in the iteration $z_{n+1} \gets \sqrt{1 +\frac{1}{1 +z_n}},$ and the continued reciprocal square root

$$\varsigma -1 =\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{1+\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{1+\sqrt{1 +\cfrac{1}{1 +\ddots}}}}}}$$

For complex initial points $z_0$ other than $-1$ this method converges with linear rate $\,\log_{10} 2(\varsigma^2 +1) =1.069...,$ provided the [principal root](wikipedia://en/29208#Square_roots_of_negative_and_complex_numbers) is chosen at each step. If randomly either the principal root or its negative is picked, the [orbit](wikipedia://en/252250) of $z_0$ is [attracted](wikipedia://en/191094) to a [simple closed curve](wikipedia://en/89246#Topological_curve), which is the [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) $J$ of the backward iteration $z_{n-1} \gets \frac{1}{z^2_n -1} -1.$

The [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951424) $z_c$ for which the derivative $-2z /(z^2 -1)^2$ vanishes are $0$ and $-\infty$. The latter is mapped into the right neighborhood of the [pole](wikipedia://en/81560) $z_p =-1$ and *vice versa*, so $\{-\infty,-1\}$ is the single attracting [limit set](wikipedia://en/5492198).

On $J$, the repelling [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) $z_f$ are the zeros of $f$, namely $\varsigma -1$, and $\left( -\varsigma \pm i\sqrt{\tfrac{7 -\varsigma^2}{\varsigma -1}} \right) /2\,$ (the centers of the largest spirals in the left half of the image) with [divergence rate](wikipedia://en/5493795#Stability_of_fixed_points) $\tfrac12 \log_{10} \tfrac{8}{\varsigma^2 +1} =0.067...$ The first [preimages](wikipedia://en/579311#Inverse_image) of $\varsigma -1 \,$ are $1 -\varsigma$, and the purely imaginary conjugates $\pm i\sqrt{ \varsigma^2 -1}.$

**The counterpart** of this system, with equal rate of convergence, is derived from $r^3 =r(2-r) +4 \,$ with real root $\frac{2}{\varsigma -1},$ numerically $1.658967...$ Divide both sides by $r$, and substitute $2 /\,\varsigma(\varsigma -1) =2 /(r+1) \,$ for $\, 4/r -r$, resulting in the iterations $r_{n+1} \gets \sqrt{2 +\frac{2}{1 +r_n}} \,$ and $\, r_{n-1} \gets \frac{2}{r^2_n -2} -1.$ The backward iteration has derivative $-4r /(r^2 -2)^2$, thus the critical points $-\infty$ and $0$ remain unchanged, and the attracting limit set is $\{-2,0\}$.

On the corresponding Julia set, the repelling fixed points $\left( -\varsigma -\tfrac{1}{\varsigma} \pm i\sqrt{(7 -\varsigma^2)(\varsigma -1)} \right) /2\,$ have divergence rate as above. The preimages of $\frac{2}{\varsigma -1} \,$ are $\frac{2}{1 -\varsigma}$, and the conjugates $\frac{\pm 2i}{\sqrt{ \varsigma^2 -1}}.$

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: SupersilverSquare\_6.svg.* Rectangles with aspect ratios $\varsigma-1$, $\tfrac{\varsigma}{\varsigma-1}$, and $\varsigma$ tile the square.

The growth rate of the average value of the n-th term of a random Fibonacci sequence is $\varsigma - 1$.

The defining equation can be written

$$\begin{aligned} 1 &=\frac{1}{\varsigma -1} +\frac{1}{\varsigma^2 +1} \\ &=\frac{1}{\varsigma} +\frac{\varsigma -1}{\varsigma +1} +\frac{\varsigma -2}{\varsigma -1}.\end{aligned}$$

The supersilver ratio can be expressed in terms of itself as fractions

$$\begin{aligned} (\varsigma -1)^2 &=\frac{\varsigma +1}{\varsigma} \\ \varsigma &=\frac{\varsigma}{\varsigma -1} +\frac{\varsigma -1}{\varsigma +1} \\ \varsigma(\varsigma -1) &=\varsigma +\tfrac{1}{\varsigma} =\frac{\varsigma +1}{\varsigma -1} \\ \varsigma^2 -1 &=\varsigma +\frac{2}{\varsigma -1} =\frac{1}{\varsigma -2} -1.\end{aligned}$$

Similarly as the infinite [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630)

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\varsigma}{\varsigma -1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varsigma^{-n} \\ \frac{\varsigma}{(\varsigma -1)^3} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varsigma^{-2n} \\ \frac{\varsigma}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \varsigma^{-3n},\end{aligned}$$

in comparison to the [silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526) identities

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\sigma}{\sigma -1} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-n} \\ \frac{\sigma}{2} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-2n} \\ \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma +3} &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \sigma^{-3n}.\end{aligned}$$

For every integer $n$ one has

$$\begin{aligned} \varsigma^n &=2\varsigma^{n-1} +\varsigma^{n-3} \\ &=4\varsigma^{n-2} +\varsigma^{n-3} +2\varsigma^{n-4} \\ &=\varsigma^{n-1} +2\varsigma^{n-2} +\varsigma^{n-3} +\varsigma^{n-4} \end{aligned}$$

from this an infinite number of further relations can be found.

[Continued fraction](wikipedia://en/46802) pattern of a few low powers

$$\begin{aligned} \varsigma^{-2} &=[0;4,1,6,2,1,1,1,1,1,1,...] \approx 0.2056 \;(\tfrac{5}{24}) \\ \varsigma^{-1} &=[0;2,4,1,6,2,1,1,1,1,1,...] \approx 0.4534 \;(\tfrac{5}{11}) \\ \varsigma^0 &=[1] \\ \varsigma^1 &=[2;4,1,6,2,1,1,1,1,1,1,...] \approx 2.2056 \;(\tfrac{53}{24}) \\ \varsigma^2 &=[4;1,6,2,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,...] \approx 4.8645 \;(\tfrac{73}{15}) \\ \varsigma^3 &=[10;1,2,1,2,4,4,2,2,6,2,...] \approx 10.729 \;(\tfrac{118}{11}) \end{aligned}$$

As derived from its continued fraction expansion, the simplest rational approximations of $\varsigma$ are:

$$\tfrac{9}{4},\tfrac{11}{5},\tfrac{53}{24},\tfrac{75}{34},\tfrac{161}{73},\tfrac{236}{107},\tfrac{397}{180},\tfrac{633}{287},\tfrac{1030}{467},\tfrac{1663}{754},\tfrac{2693}{1221},\tfrac{7049}{3196}, \ldots$$

*Image: Supersilver Newton map.svg.* [Newton's method](wikipedia://en/22145) for $p(z) = z3 - 2z2 - 1:$ $\varsigma$ (right) and its complex conjugates at the nuclei of their [basins of attraction](wikipedia://en/191094#Basins_of_attraction). [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434) of the Newton map in orange, with unit circle and real curve for reference.

The supersilver ratio is a [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number).[^o13] By definition of these numbers, the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991#Complex_numbers) $1 /\sqrt{\varsigma}$ of the algebraic conjugates is smaller than 1, so powers of $\varsigma$ generate [almost integers](wikipedia://en/14041933). For example: $\varsigma^{10} =2724.00146856...$ $\approx 2724 +1/681$. After ten rotation steps the [phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_%28waves%29) of the inward spiraling conjugate pair – initially close to $\pm 45 \pi/82$ – nearly align with the imaginary axis.

The [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of the supersilver ratio $m(x) =x^3 -2x^2 -1$ has [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $\Delta=-59$ and factors into $(x -21)^{2}(x -19) \pmod{59};\;$ the imaginary [quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781) $K = \mathbb{Q}( \sqrt{\Delta})$ has [class number](wikipedia://en/1867129#Reduction_and_class_numbers) $h=3$. Thus, the [Hilbert class field](wikipedia://en/3021036) of $K$ can be formed by adjoining $\varsigma$.[^o15] With argument $\tau=(1 +\sqrt{\Delta})/2\,$ a generator for the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of $K$, the real root [$j(\tau)$](wikipedia://en/451988) of the Hilbert class polynomial is given by $(\varsigma^{-6} -27\varsigma^{6} -6)^{3}.$[^o16][^o17]

The [Weber-Ramanujan class invariant](wikipedia://en/33559472) is approximated with error $< 3.5 \bullet 10^{-20}$ by

$$\sqrt{2}\,\mathfrak{f}( \sqrt{ \Delta} ) = \sqrt[4]{2}\,G_{59} \approx (e^{\pi \sqrt{- \Delta}} + 24)^{1/24},$$

while its true value is the single real root of the polynomial

$$W_{59}(x) = x^9 -4x^8 +4x^7 -2x^6 +4x^5 -8x^4 +4x^3 -8x^2 +16x -8.$$

The [elliptic integral singular value](wikipedia://en/27903678#Lambda-star) $k_{r} =\lambda^{*}(r) \text{ for } r =59$ has closed form expression

$$\lambda^{*}(59) =\sin ( \arcsin \left( G_{59}^{-12} \right) /2)$$

(which is less than 1/294 the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472#Ellipses) of the orbit of Venus).

## Third-order Pell sequences {#Third-order_Pell_sequences}

These numbers are related to the supersilver ratio as the [Pell numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482) and [Pell-Lucas numbers](wikipedia://en/1355482#Pell–Lucas_numbers) are to the [silver ratio](wikipedia://en/920526).

The fundamental sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$S_n =2S_{n-1} +S_{n-3} \text{ for } n > 2,$$

with initial values

$$S_0 =1, S_1 =2, S_2 =4.$$

The first few terms are 1, 2, 4, 9, 20, 44, 97, 214, 472, 1041, 2296, 5064,... . The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the supersilver ratio:$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} S_{n+1}/S_n =\varsigma.$

The first 8 indices n for which $S_n$ is prime are n = 1, 6, 21, 114, 117, 849, 2418, 6144. The last number has 2111 decimal digits.

The sequence is extended to negative indices using

$$S_n =S_{n+3} -2S_{n+2},$$

obtaining (1), 0, 0, 1, 0,−2, 1, 4,−4,−7,12,10,...

Powers of the supersilver ratio can be written with third-order Pell numbers as quadratic coefficients

$$\varsigma^n =\varsigma^2 S_{n-2} +\varsigma S_{n-4} +S_{n-3},$$

which is proved by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) on $n$. This relation also holds for $n < 0$. The order of the coefficients corresponds to the bottom row of matrix $Q$ below.

The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) of the sequence for non-negative $n$ is given by

$$\frac{1}{1 -2x -x^3} =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} S_n x^n \text{ for } x <\tfrac{1}{\varsigma}$$

The third-order Pell numbers are related to sums of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) by [^o21]

$$S_n =\sum_{k =0}^{\lfloor n /3 \rfloor}{n -2k \choose k} \cdot 2^{n -3k}$$

The [characteristic equation](wikipedia://en/31050469) of the recurrence is $x^3 -2x^2 -1 =0 .$ If the three solutions are real root $\varsigma$ and conjugate pair $\beta$ and $\gamma$, the supersilver numbers are given by the [Binet formula](wikipedia://en/10918#Binet's_formula)

$$S_{n-1} =a \varsigma^n +b \beta^n +c \gamma^n ,$$

with real $a$ and conjugates $b$ and $c$ the roots of $59x^3 -6x -1 =0.$

Since $\left\vert b \beta^n +c \gamma^n \right\vert < \varsigma^{-n/2}$, the number $S_n$ is the [nearest integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function) to $\,a\,\varsigma^{n+1}$, with $n \ge 0$ and coefficient $a =\varsigma^2 /(\varsigma^3 +2) =$ 0.38215

Coefficients $a =b =c =1$ result in the Binet formula for the related sequence $A_n =S_n +2S_{n-3}.$

The first few terms are 3, 2, 4, 11, 24, 52, 115, 254, 560, 1235, 2724, 6008,... .

This third-order Pell-Lucas sequence has the [Fermat property](wikipedia://en/53453): if p is prime, $A_{p} \equiv A_1 \bmod p.$ The converse does not hold, but the small number of odd [pseudoprimes](wikipedia://en/28787420) $\,n \mid (A_n -2)$ makes the sequence special. The 14 odd composite numbers below $10^{8}$ to pass the test are n = 3${}^{2}$, 5${}^{2}$, 5${}^{3}$, 315, 99297, 222443, 418625, 9122185, 3257${}^{2}$, 11889745, 20909625, 24299681, 64036831, 76917325.[^o22]

*Image: Supersilver\_Rauzy\_aba.png.* *The Pilgrim*: a supersilver Rauzy fractal of type a ↦ aba. The three subtiles have areas in ratio $\varsigma.$

The third-order Pell numbers are obtained as integral powers $n > 3$ of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) with real [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) $\varsigma$

$$Q = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,$$

$$Q^n = \begin{pmatrix} S_n & S_{n-2} & S_{n-1} \\ S_{n-1} & S_{n-3} & S_{n-2} \\ S_{n-2} & S_{n-4} & S_{n-3} \end{pmatrix}$$

The [trace](wikipedia://en/43270#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of $Q^n$ gives the above $A_n$.

Alternatively, $Q$ can be interpreted as [incidence matrix](wikipedia://en/420919) for a [D0L](wikipedia://en/37432570#D0L_system) [Lindenmayer system](wikipedia://en/46565) on the alphabet $\{a,b,c\}$ with corresponding [substitution rule](wikipedia://en/2452154)

$$\begin{cases} a \;\mapsto \;aab \\ b \;\mapsto \;c \\ c \;\mapsto \;a \end{cases}$$

and initiator $w_0=b$. The series of words $w_n$ produced by iterating the substitution have lengths $l(w_n) =S_{n-2} +S_{n-3} +S_{n-4}.$[^o23] The number of $c, b\text{ and } a's$ in each word is equal to successive third-order Pell numbers.

Associated to this string rewriting process is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) set composed of [self-similar](wikipedia://en/28782) tiles called the [Rauzy fractal](wikipedia://en/29344658), that visualizes the [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/20913490) information contained in a multiple-generation three-letter sequence.[^o24]

## Supersilver rectangle {#Supersilver_rectangle}

*Image: Supersilver ratio.svg.* Powers of $\varsigma$ within a supersilver rectangle.

Given a rectangle of height $1$, length $\varsigma$ and diagonal length $\varsigma \sqrt{\varsigma -1}.$ The triangles on the diagonal have [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) $1 /\sqrt{\varsigma -1}\,;$ each perpendicular foot divides the diagonal in ratio $\varsigma^2$.

On the right-hand side, cut off a square of side length $1$ and mark the intersection with the falling diagonal. The remaining rectangle now has [aspect ratio](wikipedia://en/1021) $1 +1/ \varsigma^2:1$ (according to $\varsigma =2 +1/ \varsigma^2$). Divide the original rectangle into four parts by a second, horizontal cut passing through the intersection point.[^o26]

The parent supersilver rectangle and the two scaled copies along the diagonal have linear sizes in the ratios $\varsigma:\varsigma -1:1.$ The areas of the rectangles opposite the diagonal are both equal to $(\varsigma -1)/ \varsigma,$ with aspect ratios $\varsigma(\varsigma -1)$ (below) and $\varsigma /(\varsigma -1)$ (above).

If the diagram is further subdivided by perpendicular lines through the feet of the altitudes, the lengths of the diagonal and its seven distinct subsections are in ratios $\varsigma^2 +1:\varsigma^2:\varsigma^2 -1:\varsigma +1:$ $\, \varsigma(\varsigma -1):\varsigma:2/(\varsigma -1):1.$

### Supersilver spiral {#Supersilver_spiral}

*Image: Supersilver\_spiral.svg.* Supersilver spirals with different initial angles on a $\varsigma$− rectangle.

A supersilver spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](wikipedia://en/42373) that gets wider by a factor of $\varsigma$ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) $r( \theta) =a \exp(k \theta),$ with initial radius $a$ and parameter $k =\frac{2}{\pi} \ln( \varsigma).$ If drawn on a supersilver rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio $\varsigma(\varsigma -1)$ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor $\varsigma^{-1}$. The pole of the spiral divides the diagonal of the lower right rectangle in ratio $\varsigma$.
