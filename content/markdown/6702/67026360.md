# Deborah J. Bennett

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Deborah J. Bennett"}

**Deborah Jo Bennett** (born 1950) is an American mathematician, [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471) scholar, and book author. She is a professor of mathematics at [New Jersey City University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_City_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bennett is originally from [Tuscaloosa, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscaloosa,_Alabama); her father was a military officer and her mother worked as a computer systems analyst. She majored in mathematics at the [University of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama), graduating in 1972, and worked as a researcher at the [Institute for Defense Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analysis) and as an [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476) for the US [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) before returning to graduate school for a master's degree in operations research at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) in 1980.

After a year in [Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana) teaching mathematics through the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps), she became a mathematics instructor at [Pace University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pace_University) from 1981 to 1987, and at [Farmingdale State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmingdale_State_College) from 1984 to 1993. While doing this, she also completed a Ph.D. in mathematics education at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in 1993. Her dissertation, *The Development of the Mathematical Concept of Randomness: Educational Implications*, was supervised by Kenneth P. Goldberg.

She joined New Jersey City University as an assistant professor of mathematics in 1993, adding a concurrent appointment in education in 1999. She has since become a full professor, and served two terms as president of the University Senate.

## Books {#Books}

Bennett is the coauthor of the textbook *Algebra for All* (with Phillip Aikey and Julio Guillen, McGraw-Hill, 1997). She is also the author of two [popular mathematics](wikipedia://en/2069923) books, *Randomness* (Harvard University Press, 1998), and *[Logic Made Easy: How to Know When Language Deceives You](wikipedia://en/1290586)* (W. W. Norton, 2004). Her book *Logic Made Easy* was listed as an Outstanding Academic Title in 2004 by [Choice Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choice_Reviews).
