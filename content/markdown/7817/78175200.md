# Premonoidal category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Premonoidal category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **premonoidal category[^o2]** is a generalisation of a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) where the monoidal product need not be a [bifunctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifunctor), but only to be functorial in its two arguments separately. This is in analogy with the concept of [separate continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_continuity) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

Premonoidal categories naturally arise in theoretical [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) as the [Kleisli categories](wikipedia://en/3957200) of [strong monads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_monad).[^o3] They also have a graphical language given by [string diagrams](wikipedia://en/3967296) with an extra wire going through each box so that they cannot be reordered.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Funny tensor product {#Funny_tensor_product}

The [category of small categories](wikipedia://en/2072741) $\mathbf{Cat}$ is a [closed monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1396924) in exactly two ways: with the usual [categorical product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_product) and with the **funny tensor product**.[^o8] Given two categories $C$ and $D$, let $C \Rightarrow D$ be the category with [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) $F, G : C \to D$ as objects and **unnatural transformations** $\alpha : F \Rightarrow G$ as arrows, i.e. families of morphisms $\{ \alpha_X : F(X) \to G(X) \}_{X \in C}$ which do not necessarily satisfy the condition for a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022).

The funny tensor product is the [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28category_theory%29) of unnatural transformations, i.e. there is a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) $\mathbf{Cat}(C \ \Box \ D, D') \simeq \mathbf{Cat}(C, D \Rightarrow D' )$ for [currying](wikipedia://en/6600). It can be defined explicitly as the [pushout](wikipedia://en/582024) of the [span](wikipedia://en/10318351) $(C_0 \times D) \to (C \times D) \leftarrow (C \times D_0)$ where $C_0, D_0$ are the [discrete categories](wikipedia://en/808519) of objects of $C, D$ and the two functors are inclusions. In the case of groups seen as one-object categories, this is called the [free product](wikipedia://en/360036).

## Sesquicategories {#Sesquicategories}

The same way we can define a monoidal category as a one-object [2-category](wikipedia://en/2740708), i.e. an [enriched category](wikipedia://en/142622) over $(\mathbf{Cat}, \times)$ with the Cartesian product as monoidal structure, we can define a premonoidal category as a one-object **sesquicategory**,[^o10] i.e. a category enriched over $(\mathbf{Cat}, \Box)$ with the funny tensor product as monoidal structure. This is called a sesquicategory (literally, "one-and-a-half category") because it is like a 2-category without the interchange law $(\alpha\circ_0\beta)\circ_1(\gamma\circ_0\delta) = (\alpha\circ_1\gamma)\circ_0(\beta\circ_1\delta)$.
