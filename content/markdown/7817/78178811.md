# Hefer's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hefer's theorem"}

In [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), **Hefer's theorem** is a result that represents the difference at two points of a holomorphic function as the sum of the products of the coordinate differences of these two points with other holomorphic functions defined in the Cartesian product of the function's domain.

:::

The theorem bears the name of [Hans Hefer](wikipedia://en/77913411). The result was published by [Karl Stein](wikipedia://en/7218529) and [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678) under the name Hans Hefer.[^o2] In a footnote in the same article, it is written that Hans Hefer died on the [eastern front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_%28World_War_II%29) and that the work was an excerpt from Hefer's dissertation which he defended in 1940.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb{C}^n$ be a [domain of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377) and $f:\Omega\mapsto \mathbb{C}$ be a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110). Then, there exist holomorphic functions $g_1, \cdots, g_n$ defined on $\Omega \times \Omega$ so that

$$f(z)-f(w)=\sum_{j=1}^n (z_j-w_j)g_j(w,z)$$

holds for every $z, w\in \Omega$.

The decomposition in the theorem is feasible also on many non-pseudoconvex domains.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Hefer's lemma"}

## Hefer's lemma {#Hefer's_lemma}

The proof of the theorem follows from Hefer's lemma.[^o7][^o8]

Let $\Omega\subset \mathbb{C}^n$ be a [domain of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377) and $f:\Omega\mapsto \mathbb{C}$ be a [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110). Suppose that $f$ is identically zero on the intersection of $\Omega$ with the $(N-k)$-dimensional complex coordinate space; i.e.

$$f(0,\cdots, 0, z_{k+1}, z_k, \cdots, z_n)\equiv 0.$$

Then, there exist holomorphic functions $g_1, \cdots, g_n$ defined on $\Omega$ so that

$$f(z)=\sum_{j=1}^n z_jg_j(z)$$

holds for every $z\in \Omega$.

:::
