# Unit tangent bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Unit tangent bundle"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), the **unit tangent bundle** of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) (*M*, *g*), denoted by T<sup>1</sup>*M*, UT(*M*), UT*M*, or S*M* is the unit sphere bundle for the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) T(*M*). It is a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) over *M* whose fiber at each point is the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) in the tangent space:

:::

$$\mathrm{UT} (M) := \coprod_{x \in M} \left\{ v \in \mathrm{T}_{x} (M) \left| g_x(v,v) = 1 \right. \right\},$$

where T<sub>*x*</sub>(*M*) denotes the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) to *M* at *x*. Thus, elements of UT(*M*) are pairs (*x*, *v*), where *x* is some point of the manifold and *v* is some tangent direction (of unit length) to the manifold at *x*. The unit tangent bundle is equipped with a natural [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097)

$$\pi : \mathrm{UT} (M) \to M,$$

$$\pi : (x, v) \mapsto x,$$

which takes each point of the bundle to its base point. The fiber *π*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*) over each point *x* ∈ *M* is an (*n*−1)-[sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere) **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup>, where *n* is the dimension of *M*. The unit tangent bundle is therefore a [sphere bundle](wikipedia://en/276526#Sphere_bundles) over *M* with fiber **S**<sup>*n*−1</sup>.

The definition of unit sphere bundle can easily accommodate [Finsler manifolds](wikipedia://en/385510) as well. Specifically, if *M* is a manifold equipped with a Finsler metric *F* : T*M* → **R**, then the unit sphere bundle is the subbundle of the tangent bundle whose fiber at *x* is the indicatrix of *F*:

$$\mathrm{UT}_x (M) = \left\{ v \in \mathrm{T}_{x} (M) \left| F(v) = 1 \right. \right\}.$$

If *M* is an infinite-dimensional manifold (for example, a [Banach](wikipedia://en/2504397), [Fréchet](wikipedia://en/3873038) or [Hilbert manifold](wikipedia://en/2504412)), then UT(*M*) can still be thought of as the unit sphere bundle for the tangent bundle T(*M*), but the fiber *π*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*) over *x* is then the infinite-dimensional unit sphere in the tangent space.

## Structures {#Structures}

The unit tangent bundle carries a variety of differential geometric structures. The metric on *M* induces a [contact structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_structure) on UT*M*. This is given in terms of a [tautological one-form](wikipedia://en/1855974), defined at a point *u* of UT*M* (a unit tangent vector of *M*) by

$$\theta_u(v) = g(u,\pi_* v)\,$$

where $\pi_*$ is the [pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510) along π of the vector *v* ∈ T<sub>*u*</sub>UT*M*.

Geometrically, this contact structure can be regarded as the distribution of (2*n*−2)-planes which, at the unit vector *u*, is the pullback of the orthogonal complement of *u* in the tangent space of *M*. This is a contact structure, for the fiber of UT*M* is obviously an integral manifold (the vertical bundle is everywhere in the kernel of θ), and the remaining tangent directions are filled out by moving up the fiber of UT*M*. Thus the maximal integral manifold of θ is (an open set of) *M* itself.

On a Finsler manifold, the contact form is defined by the analogous formula

$$\theta_u(v) = g_u(u,\pi_*v)\,$$

where *g*<sub>*u*</sub> is the fundamental tensor (the [hessian](wikipedia://en/412108) of the Finsler metric). Geometrically, the associated distribution of hyperplanes at the point *u* ∈ UT<sub>*x*</sub>*M* is the inverse image under π<sub>\*</sub> of the tangent hyperplane to the unit sphere in T<sub>*x*</sub>*M* at *u*.

The [volume form](wikipedia://en/1855811) θ∧*d*θ<sup>*n*−1</sup> defines a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) on *M*, known as the **kinematic measure**, or **Liouville measure**, that is invariant under the [geodesic flow](wikipedia://en/91096#Geodesic_flow) of *M*. As a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933), the kinematic measure μ is defined on compactly supported continuous functions *ƒ* on UT*M* by

$$\int_{UTM} f\,d\mu = \int_M dV(p) \int_{UT_pM} \left.f\right|_{UT_pM}\,d\mu_p$$

where d*V* is the [volume element](wikipedia://en/1920694) on *M*, and μ<sub>*p*</sub> is the standard rotationally-invariant [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on the Euclidean sphere UT<sub>*p*</sub>*M*.

The [Levi-Civita connection](wikipedia://en/249176) of *M* gives rise to a splitting of the tangent bundle

$$T(UTM) = H\oplus V$$

into a vertical space *V* = kerπ<sub>\*</sub> and horizontal space *H* on which π<sub>\*</sub> is a [linear isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_isomorphism) at each point of UT*M*. This splitting induces a metric on UT*M* by declaring that this splitting be an orthogonal direct sum, and defining the metric on *H* by the pullback:

$$g_H(v,w) = g(v,w),\quad v,w\in H$$

and defining the metric on *V* as the induced metric from the embedding of the fiber UT<sub>*x*</sub>*M* into the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) T<sub>*x*</sub>*M*. Equipped with this metric and contact form, UT*M* becomes a [Sasakian manifold](wikipedia://en/4733638).
