# Infinite-dimensional sphere

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinite-dimensional sphere"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the **infinite-dimensional sphere** is the [inductive limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) of all [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859). Although no sphere is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), the infinite-dimensional sphere is contractible[^o2][^o3] and hence appears as the total space of multiple [universal principal bundles](wikipedia://en/2179570).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

With the usual definition $S^n=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}|\|x\|_2=1\}$ of the sphere with the [2-norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-norm), the canonical inclusion $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+2},x\mapsto(x,0)$ restricts to a canonical inclusion $S^n\hookrightarrow S^{n+1}$. Hence the spheres form an inductive system, whose inductive limit:[^o6][^o7]

$$S^\infty :=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}S^n$$

is the *infinite-dimensional sphere*.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The most important property of the infinite-dimensional sphere is that it is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414).[^o2][^o3] Since the infinite-dimensional sphere inherits a [CW structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW_structure) from the spheres,[^o6][^o10] [Whitehead's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead's_theorem) claims that it is sufficient to show that it is [weakly contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weakly_contractible). Intuitively, the homotopy groups of the spheres disappear one by one, hence all do for the infinite-dimensional sphere. Concretely, any map $S^k\rightarrow S^\infty$, due to the compactness of the former sphere, factors over a canonical inclusion $S^n\hookrightarrow S^\infty$ with $k<n$ [without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000). Since $\pi_k(S^n)$ is trivial, $\pi_k(S^\infty)$ is also trivial.

:::

::: {#o12 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

- $S^\infty\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{R}P^\infty$ is the universal principal $\operatorname{O}(1)$-bundle, hence $\operatorname{EO}(1)\cong S^\infty$. The principal $\operatorname{O}(1)$-bundle $S^n\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{R}P^n$ is then the canonical inclusion $i\colon\mathbb{R}P^n \hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}P^\infty$, hence $S^n\cong i^*S^\infty$.
- $S^\infty\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^\infty$ is the universal [principal U(1)-bundle](wikipedia://en/80364893), hence $\operatorname{EU}(1)\cong\operatorname{ESO}(2)\cong S^\infty$. The principal $\operatorname{U}(1)$-bundle $S^{2n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^n$ is then the canonical inclusion $j\colon\mathbb{C}P^n \hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^\infty$, hence $S^{2n+1}\cong j^*S^\infty$.
- $S^\infty\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{H}P^\infty$ is the universal [principal SU(2)-bundle](wikipedia://en/80364907), hence $\operatorname{ESU}(2)\cong\operatorname{ESp}(1)\cong S^\infty$. The principal $\operatorname{SU}(2)$-bundle $S^{4n+3}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{H}P^n$ is then the canonical inclusion $k\colon\mathbb{H}P^n \hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}P^\infty$, hence $S^{4n+3}\cong k^*S^\infty$.

:::
