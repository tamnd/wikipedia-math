# Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture"}

In [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture** proposed a weighted [$L^2$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L^2_space) inequality for the [Fourier extension operator](wikipedia://en/80687023) associated with a [smooth hypersurface](wikipedia://en/525101#Smooth_hypersurface) in Euclidean space. It asserted that the $L^2$ norm of the extension of a function $f$ from the hypersurface to $\mathbb{R}^n$ could be bounded, for any nonnegative [weight function](wikipedia://en/245523), by a constant multiple of the $L^2$ norm of $f$, with the constant depending only on the supremum of the weight over certain tube-shaped regions. The conjecture was disproved in 2025 by [Hannah Cairo](wikipedia://en/80417092).[^o2][^o3]

:::

The [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138)[^o4] originally arose in the study of well-posedness for dispersive [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). In the 1970s and 1980s Jiro Takeuchi was studying the initial value problem associated with a perturbed version of the linear [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874). He at one point claimed[^o5] a well-posed condition in $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$ that was both necessary and sufficient for the associated [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027). [Sigeru Mizohata](wikipedia://en/53225329) noticed[^o6] that Takeuchi’s argument was not compelling and showed that Takeuchi’s condition is necessary, but whether it is also sufficient remained open.
