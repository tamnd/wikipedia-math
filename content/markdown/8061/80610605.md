# Fadil Santosa

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fadil Santosa"}

**Fadil Santosa** is a professor of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), where he is currently the Yu Wu and Chaomei Chen Department Head.[^o2] Before, he was a professor at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota).

:::

At the University of Minnesota he was director of the [Minnesota Center for Industrial Mathematics](https://cse.umn.edu/math/minnesota-center-industrial-mathematics), and later director of the [Institute for Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/10324088) (IMA) from 2008 to 2017.[^o3]

Santosa is a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o4] and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM).[^o5]

Santosa has been recognized with the 2023 SIAM Prize for Distinguished Service to the Profession.[^o6] He also received the 2023 Johns Hopkins Diversity Award.[^o7]

Santosa is known for his work in applied mathematics (detailed below) and his efforts to connect academia and industry, which, in addition to the leadership roles mentioned earlier, include co-authoring a guide for mathematicians on finding careers in business, industry, and government.[^o15]

Santosa holds two patents: for multifocal optical device design and for symbol-based decoding of optical codes.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Santosa's research interests include [inverse problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_problems), wave phenomena in complex media, [photonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photonics) devices, and [optimal design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design). His research includes foundational work on using [L<sup>1</sup>](wikipedia://en/45194) [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) to find sparse solutions to inverse problems, with a 1986 paper[^o10] now seen as a precursor to methods like compressed sensing and LASSO.[^o11]

Santosa was also one of the first to apply the [level-set method](wikipedia://en/1203063) to structural optimization and inverse scattering problems.[^o19]

His PhD advisor was Robert W. Carroll.[^o12] Santosa was born in Bandung, Indonesia.[^o13]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

**Books**

- *BIG Jobs Guide: Business, Industry, and Government Careers for Mathematical Scientists, Statisticians, and Operations Researchers* (2018), with Rachel Levy and Richard Laugesen.[^o15]
- *The Princeton Companion to Applied Mathematics* (2015), co-edited with Nicholas J. Higham, Mark R. Dennis, Paul Glendinning, Paul A. Martin, and Jared Tanner.[^o16]
- *Analysis of Least-Squares Velocity Inversion* (1989), with William W. Symes and Raymon L. Brown.[^o17]

**Journal articles**

- Santosa, Fadil; Symes, William W. (1986). "Linear inversion of band-limited reflection seismograms". *SIAM Journal on Scientific and Statistical Computing*. 7 (4): 1307–1330. doi:10.1137/0907087.[^o10]
- Osher, Stanley J.; Santosa, Fadil (2001). "Level set methods for optimization problems involving geometry and constraints: I. Frequencies of a two-density inhomogeneous drum". *Journal of Computational Physics*. 171 (1): 272–288.[^o19]
- Rondi, Luca; Santosa, Fadil (2001). "Enhanced electrical impedance tomography via the Mumford–Shah functional". *ESAIM: Control, Optimisation and Calculus of Variations*. 6: 517–538. doi:10.1051/cocv:2001121.[^o20]
