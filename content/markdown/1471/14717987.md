# Global element

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Global element"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **global element** of an [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) *A* from a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) is a [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891)

:::

$$h\colon 1 \to A,$$

where $1$ is a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) of the category.[^o2] Roughly speaking, global elements are a generalization of the notion of "elements" from the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), and they can be used to import [set-theoretic](wikipedia://en/27553) concepts into category theory. However, unlike a set, an object of a general category need not be determined by its global elements (not even [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), the terminal objects are the singletons, so a global element of $A$ can be assimilated to an element of $A$ in the usual (set-theoretic) sense. More precisely, there is a [natural isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) $(1 \to A) \cong A$.
- To illustrate that the notion of global elements can sometimes recover the actual elements of the objects in a [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001), in the [category of partially ordered sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_partially_ordered_sets), the terminal objects are again the singletons, so the global elements of a poset $P$ can be identified with the elements of $P$. Precisely, there is a natural isomorphism $(1 \to P) \cong \operatorname{Forget}(P)$ where $\operatorname{Forget}$ is the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) from the category of posets to the category of sets. The same holds in the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051).
- Similarly, in the [category of (small) categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_categories), terminals objects are unit categories (having a single object and a single morphism which is the identity of that object). Consequently, a global element of a category is simply an object of that category. More precisely, there is a natural isomorphism $(1 \to \mathcal{C}) \cong \operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ (where $\operatorname{Ob}$ is the [objects functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objects_functor)).
- As an example where global elements do *not* recover elements of sets, in the [category of groups](wikipedia://en/800092), the terminal objects are [zero groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_group). For any group $G$, there is a unique morphism $1 \to G$ (mapping the identity to the identity of $G$). More generally, in any category with a [zero object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) (such as the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748) or the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) on a field), each object has a unique global element.
- In the [category of graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_graphs), the terminal objects are graphs with a single vertex and a single [self-loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-loop) on that vertex,[^o5] whence the global elements of a graph are its self-loops.
- In an [overcategory](wikipedia://en/65601334) $\mathcal{C}/B$, the object $B \overset{\operatorname{id}}{\to} B$ is terminal. The global elements of an object $A \overset{f}{\to} B$ are the [sections](wikipedia://en/10630303) of $f$.

:::

## In topos theory {#In_topos_theory}

In an [elementary topos](wikipedia://en/815802) the global elements of the [subobject classifier](wikipedia://en/366909) form a [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343) when ordered by inclusion of the corresponding subobjects of the terminal object.[^o7] For example, **Grph** happens to be a topos, whose subobject classifier $\Omega$ is a two-vertex directed [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) with an additional self-loop (so five edges, three of which are self-loops and hence the global elements of $\Omega$). The internal logic of **Grph** is therefore based on the three-element Heyting algebra as its [truth values](wikipedia://en/161711).
