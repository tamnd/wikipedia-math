# Thomas Archer Hirst

*Image: Thomas Archer Hirst.jpg.* Photograph of Thomas Hirst.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Archer Hirst"}

**Thomas Archer Hirst** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (22 April 1830 – 16 February 1892) was a 19th-century English mathematician, specialising in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). He was awarded the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)'s [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) in 1883.

:::

## Life {#Life}

*Image: Grave of Thomas Archer Hirst in Highgate Cemetery.jpg.* Grave of Thomas Archer Hirst in [Highgate Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgate_Cemetery)

Thomas Hirst was born in [Heckmondwike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckmondwike), Yorkshire, England, where both his parents came from families in the [wool trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool_trade). He was the youngest of four sons. The family moved to [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield) so that the boys could attend a better school. Thomas attended [Wakefield Proprietary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield_Proprietary_School) for four years from 1841.[^o3] Of these days, he said:

> "... I could obtain the most rudimentary and necessary instruction. I remember, however, that here mathematics was my favourite study ..."[^o4]

He left the school at 15 to work as an apprentice engineer in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire), surveying for proposed railway lines. It was there that he met [John Tyndall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tyndall), ten years older than Hirst and working as an engineer in the same firm.

In his late teens, at the instigation of Tyndall, Hirst decided to go to Germany for education, initially in [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry). He eventually received a doctorate in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) in 1852 (tutor: Friedrich Ludwig Stegmann). In 1853, he attended geometry lectures by [Jakob Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445) at [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin). Hirst married Anna Martin in 1854, and spent much of the decade of the 1850s on the European continent, where he socialised with many mathematicians, and used his inherited wealth to support himself.

From 1860 to 1864, Hirst taught at [University College School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_School), but resigned because he wanted more time for his mathematical research. He was appointed Professor of Physics at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1865, and he succeeded [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289) to the Chair of Mathematics at UCL in 1867. In 1873 he was appointed as the first Director of Studies at the new [Royal Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_College,_Greenwich), Greenwich. He retired from that post in 1882, to be succeeded by [William Davidson Niven](wikipedia://en/40756335).

From the 1860s onwards, Hirst also allocated much of his time in England to the administrative committees of British science. He was an active member of the governing councils of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society), the [British Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), and the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). He was the founding president of an association to reform school mathematics curricula and also worked to promote the education of women. Alongside his old friend Tyndall, Hirst was a member of [T. H. Huxley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._H._Huxley)'s London [X-Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-Club). He died in London in 1892, four weeks after he had made the last entry in his journal, and was buried on the eastern side of [Highgate Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgate_Cemetery).[^o5][^o6]

### Vestiges {#Vestiges}

In his early days, Hirst wrote extensively in his notebooks ("the Journal"), recording what he read and much of what he was thinking about. This record of about 50 years is preserved in the library of the [Royal Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institution). We know from it, for example, what the effect was of his reading the *[Vestiges of the Natural History of Creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestiges_of_the_Natural_History_of_Creation)*, which promoted ideas of evolution in 1844. According to Secord:

"*Almost no-one reads like this anymore. It is the reading practice of a self-improving [autodidact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodidact), shaped by Bible-reading amongst denominations of learned liberal [Dissent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissenter)... Hirst copied large chunks into his journal... the journal shows that Hirst moved between Vestiges and other related works such as [Paley's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paley) [Natural Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Theology) and [John Arthur Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Arthur_Phillips)' Geology of Yorkshire...*"[^o8]

Both Hirst and Tyndall found that *Vestiges*, especially its geological evidence, made a good case against the story of *[Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis)*, and came to the conclusion that parts of the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament) were allegorical.

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Hirst was a [projective geometer](wikipedia://en/243849) in the style of [Poncelet](wikipedia://en/764818) and [Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445). He was not an adherent of the [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) approach of [Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) and [Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861), despite being a friend of theirs. His speciality was [Cremona transformations](wikipedia://en/7252026).
