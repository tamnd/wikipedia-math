# Rick Durrett

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rick Durrett"}

**Richard Timothy Durrett** (born 1951) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose research concerns [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes), and their applications to [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), including [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268), [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), and [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) modeling. He is the James B. Duke Emeritus Professor of Mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), having previously held faculty positions at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA) and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).

:::

Durrett was elected to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2007 and to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2002. He is also a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), and the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science). His textbook *[Probability: Theory and Examples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability:_Theory_and_Examples)* was described in a review for the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) as one of the two most popular graduate-level texts on probability, alongside [Billingsley](wikipedia://en/31735067)'s *Probability and Measure*.[^o3]

## Education {#Education}

Durrett received his Bachelor of Science (1972) and Master of Science (1973) degrees in mathematics from [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University). He then entered the [Operations Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research) program at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), where he completed his Ph.D. in 1976 under the supervision of [Donald Iglehart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Iglehart).[^o5] His doctoral dissertation, titled *Conditioned Limit Theorems for Some Null Recurrent Markov Processes*, appeared in the *Annals of Probability* in 1976.[^o6]

Durrett has said that he initially enrolled at Emory as a [pre-medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-medical) student during the [Vietnam War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_War) era but switched to mathematics after finding the laboratory component of pre-med unappealing, while still wanting to apply mathematics to real-world problems; this led him to operations research at Stanford.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Durrett began his academic career at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA) in 1976 as an E. R. Hedrick Assistant Professor and was promoted through the ranks to full professor, remaining at UCLA until 1985.[^o8] In 1985 he joined the faculty of [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), where he remained for 25 years.[^o8]

In 2010 Durrett moved to [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) as the James B. Duke Professor of Mathematics, a position he held until his retirement in 2023, simultaneously serving as associate director of the [Statistical and Applied Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/41998171) (SAMSI) in the Research Triangle from 2010.[^o5] He was drawn to Duke partly by the milder climate of North Carolina and partly by the opportunities for collaboration with biostatisticians, computational biologists, and applied mathematicians at Duke, the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), and [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University).[^o5] Upon retirement he became the James B. Duke Emeritus Professor of Mathematics.

Durrett has supervised more than fifty doctoral students, including [Rodrigo Bañuelos](wikipedia://en/66385621) (UCLA, 1984), Claudia Neuhauser (Cornell, 1990), Nathaniel Berestycki (Cornell, 2005), Daniel Remenik (Cornell, 2009), and Shirshendu Chatterjee (Duke, 2011).[^o9] He has also mentored postdoctoral fellows including Vlada Limic, Lea Popovic, Soumik Pal, Jon Peterson, David Sivakoff, and Matt Junge.

He served as an editor of the *[Annals of Applied Probability](wikipedia://en/31018340)* from 1997 to 1999 and has been involved in organizing numerous conferences, including the Southeastern Probability Conference, the Workshop for Women in Probability, and programs at the [Mathematical Biosciences Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biosciences_Institute) and the [Banff International Research Station](wikipedia://en/39151817) (BIRS).[^o9]

## Research {#Research}

Durrett's research spans probability theory and its applications. His early work focused on [interacting particle systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interacting_particle_systems), [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805), and [contact processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_process), building on the frameworks developed by [Thomas M. Liggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Liggett) and [Frank Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Spitzer).[^o11] His 1980 paper with [T. M. Liggett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Liggett) determined the shape of the limit set in [Richardson's model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardson's_model) for the spread of an infection.[^o12]

### Stochastic spatial models in ecology {#Stochastic_spatial_models_in_ecology}

In the late 1980s Durrett began a long-running collaboration with the ecologist [Simon Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_A._Levin) of Princeton University on stochastic spatial models in ecology.[^o5] Their 1994 paper *The Importance of Being Discrete (and Spatial)* argued that spatial structure can qualitatively change the dynamics of ecological interactions and is among the most cited papers in mathematical ecology.[^o14] Their subsequent papers studied species–area curves, allelopathy, interspecific competition, and the spread of epidemics using interacting particle systems and stochastic spatial models.[^o15][^o16]

### Population genetics and cancer {#Population_genetics_and_cancer}

Beginning in the late 1990s Durrett turned to [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268), working with [C. F. Aquadro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_Aquadro) and others on the evolutionary dynamics of microsatellite repeat length distributions[^o18] and, with Jason Schweinsberg, on approximating [selective sweeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selective_sweep) by random partitions.[^o19] His 2008 paper with Deena Schmidt, *Waiting for two mutations*, studied the limits of Darwinian evolution in regulatory sequence evolution.[^o20]

Durrett's later research has applied [branching processes](wikipedia://en/377202) and stochastic models to [cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer) biology, including models of tumor heterogeneity, the evolution of resistance to therapy, and the dynamics of clonal expansion.[^o21][^o22]

### Random graphs {#Random_graphs}

A third strand of Durrett's work concerns stochastic processes on [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477). He showed, with Shirshendu Chatterjee, that [contact processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_process) on random graphs with [power-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-law) degree distributions have critical value zero—meaning infection can survive for any positive infection rate.[^o24] His 2010 Inaugural Article as a member of the National Academy of Sciences, published in the *[Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences)*, examined the spread of epidemics and opinions on random graphs.[^o25] This work is collected in part in his monograph *Random Graph Dynamics* (Cambridge University Press, 2007; second edition Springer, 2025).[^o26]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Durrett has received a number of honors recognizing his contributions to probability theory and mathematical biology. He was awarded a [Sloan Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship) from the [Alfred P. Sloan Memorial Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation) for 1981–1983, and an [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) Centennial Fellowship for 1984–1986.[^o9] In 1981 he was elected a fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o9] He received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) for 1988–1989.[^o5]

In 1990 he delivered a 45-minute invited lecture at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Kyoto.[^o9] He gave the Coxeter Lectures at the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768) in 1999 and the [IMS Wald Lectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wald_Lectures) at the World Congress of Probability and Statistics in Singapore in 2008.[^o9]

Durrett was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 2002[^o8] and to the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2007.[^o28] He was elected a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 2009[^o9] and was named a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the inaugural class of 2012.[^o29]
