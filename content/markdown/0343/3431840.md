# Sparsity-of-effects principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sparsity-of-effects principle"}

In the [statistical analysis](wikipedia://en/26685) of the results from [factorial experiments](wikipedia://en/1827237), the **sparsity-of-effects principle** states that a system is usually dominated by [main effects](wikipedia://en/7042478) and low-order [interactions](wikipedia://en/443083). Thus it is most likely that main (single factor) effects and two-factor interactions are the most significant responses in a factorial experiment. In other words, higher order interactions such as three-factor interactions are very rare. This is sometimes referred to as the *hierarchical ordering principle*.[^o4] The sparsity-of-effects principle actually refers to the idea that only a few effects in a factorial experiment will be statistically significant.[^o4]

:::

This principle is only valid on the assumption of a factor space far from a stationary point.[^o5]
