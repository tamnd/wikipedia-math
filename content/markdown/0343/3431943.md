# Joseph Diez Gergonne

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph Diez Gergonne"}

**Joseph Diez Gergonne** (19 June 1771 at [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy,_France), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) – 4 May 1859 at [Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier), France) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician).

:::

## Life {#Life}

In 1791, Gergonne enlisted in the French army as a captain. That army was undergoing rapid expansion because the French government feared a foreign invasion intended to undo the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) and restore [Louis XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XVI) to the throne of France. He saw action in the major [battle of Valmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Valmy) on 20 September 1792. He then returned to civilian life but soon was called up again and took part in the French invasion of Spain in 1794.

In 1795, Gergonne and his regiment were sent to [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nîmes). At this point, he made a definitive transition to civilian life by taking up the chair of "transcendental mathematics" at the new École centrale. He came under the influence of [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840), the Director of the new [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).

In 1810, in response to difficulties he encountered in trying to publish his work, Gergonne founded his own mathematics journal, officially named the *[Annales de mathématiques pures et appliquées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_de_mathématiques_pures_et_appliquées)* but generally referred to as the *[Annales de Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_de_Gergonne)*. The most common subject of articles in his journal was [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), Gergonne's specialty. Over a period of 22 years, the *Annales de Gergonne* published about 200 articles by Gergonne himself, and other articles by many distinguished mathematicians, including [Poncelet](wikipedia://en/764818), [Servois](wikipedia://en/42809386), [Bobillier](wikipedia://en/2537274), [Steiner](wikipedia://en/567445), [Plücker](wikipedia://en/52146), [Chasles](wikipedia://en/2252716), [Brianchon](wikipedia://en/1402609), [Dupin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dupin), [Lamé](wikipedia://en/52145), even [Galois](wikipedia://en/9815).

Gergonne was appointed to the chair of astronomy at the University of Montpellier in 1816. In 1830, he was appointed [Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of the University of Montpellier, at which time he ceased publishing his journal. He retired in 1844.

## Work {#Work}

Gergonne was among the first mathematicians to employ the word [*polar*](wikipedia://en/25120). In a series of papers beginning in 1810, he contributed to elaborating the [principle of duality](wikipedia://en/539925) in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), by noticing that every [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) in the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) connecting points and lines corresponds to another theorem in which points and lines are interchanged, provided that the theorem embodied no metrical notions. Gergonne was an early proponent of the techniques of [analytical geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_geometry) and in 1814, he devised an elegant coordinate solution to the classical [problem of Apollonius](wikipedia://en/2642185): to find a circle which touches three given circles, thus demonstrating the power of the new methods.

In 1813, Gergonne wrote the prize-winning essay for the Bordeaux Academy, *Methods of synthesis and analysis in mathematics*, unpublished to this day and known only via a summary. The essay is very revealing of Gergonne's philosophical ideas. He called for the abandonment of the words *analysis* and *synthesis*, claiming they lacked clear meanings. Surprisingly for a geometer, he suggested that algebra is more important than geometry at a time when [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) consisted almost entirely of the elementary algebra of the [real field](wikipedia://en/20646438). He predicted that one day quasi-mechanical methods would be used to discover new results.

In 1815, Gergonne wrote the first paper on the [optimal design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design) of [experiments](wikipedia://en/9541) for [polynomial regression](wikipedia://en/21893202). According to [S. M. Stigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_M._Stigler), Gergonne is the pioneer of [optimal design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design) as well as [response surface methodology](wikipedia://en/4481194).

He published his "Essai sur la théorie des définitions" (An essay on the theory of definition) in his *Annales* in 1818. This essay is generally credited for first recognizing and naming the construct of *implicit definition*.[^o5][^o6]
