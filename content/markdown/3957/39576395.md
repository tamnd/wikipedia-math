# Ribbon category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ribbon category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **ribbon category**, also called a **tortile category**, is a particular type of [braided monoidal category](wikipedia://en/1470466).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $\mathcal C$ is, loosely speaking, a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) equipped with a notion resembling the tensor product (of vector spaces, say). That is, for any two objects $C_1, C_2 \in \mathcal C$, there is an object $C_1 \otimes C_2 \in \mathcal C$. The assignment $C_1, C_2 \mapsto C_1 \otimes C_2$ is supposed to be [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial) and needs to require a number of further properties such as a unit object 1 and an [associativity isomorphism](wikipedia://en/80909607). Such a category is called braided if there are [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828)

$$c_{C_1, C_2}: C_1 \otimes C_2 \stackrel \cong \rightarrow C_2 \otimes C_1.$$

A braided monoidal category is called a ribbon category if the category is left [rigid](wikipedia://en/25383437) and has a family of *twists*. The former means that for each object $C$ there is another object (called the left [dual](wikipedia://en/609737)), $C^*$, with maps

$$1 \rightarrow C \otimes C^*, C^* \otimes C \rightarrow 1$$

such that the compositions

$$C^* \cong C^* \otimes 1 \rightarrow C^* \otimes (C \otimes C^*) \cong (C^* \otimes C) \otimes C^* \rightarrow 1 \otimes C^* \cong C^*$$

equals the identity of $C^*$, and similarly with $C$. The twists are maps

$C \in \mathcal C$, $\theta_C : C \rightarrow C$

such that

$$\begin{aligned} \theta_{C_1 \otimes C_2} &= c_{C_2, C_1} c_{C_1, C_2} (\theta_{C_1} \otimes \theta_{C_2})\\ \theta_1 &= \mathrm{id}\\ \theta_{C^*} & = (\theta_C)^*. \end{aligned}$$

To be a ribbon category, the duals have to be thus compatible with the braiding and the twists.

### Concrete Example {#Concrete_Example}

Consider the category $\mathbf{FdVect}(\mathbb{C})$ of finite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb{C}$. Suppose that $C$ is such a vector space, spanned by the basis vectors $\hat{e_1}, \hat{e_2},\cdots,\hat{e_n}$. We assign to $C$ the dual object $C^\dagger$ spanned by the basis vectors $\hat{e}^1, \hat{e}^2,\cdots,\hat{e}^n$. Then let us define

$$\begin{aligned} \cdot: \ C^\dagger\otimes C &\to 1\\ \hat{e}^i\cdot\hat{e_j} &\mapsto \begin{cases} 1 & i=j \\ 0 & i\neq j \end{cases} \end{aligned}$$

and its dual

$$\begin{aligned} k I_n: 1& \to C\otimes C^\dagger\\ k &\mapsto k \sum_{i=1}^n \hat{e_i}\otimes \hat{e}^i\\ &= \begin{pmatrix}k & 0 & \cdots & 0\\ 0 & k & &\vdots \\ &&\ddots&\\0&\cdots &&k\end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}$$

(which largely amounts to assigning a given $\hat{e_i}$ the dual $\hat{e}^i$).

Then indeed we find that (for example)

$$\begin{aligned} \hat{e}^i &\cong \hat{e}^i\otimes 1 \\ &\underset{I_n}{\to} \hat{e}^i \otimes \sum_{j=1}^n \hat{e_j}\otimes \hat{e}^j\\ &\cong \sum_{j=1}^n \left(\hat{e}^i \otimes \hat{e_j}\right)\otimes \hat{e}^j\\ &\underset{\cdot}{\to} \sum_{j=1}^n \begin{cases} 1 \otimes \hat{e}^j & i=j \\ 0 \otimes \hat{e}^j & i\neq j \end{cases}\\ &= 1\otimes \hat{e}^i \cong \hat{e}^i \end{aligned}$$

and similarly for $\hat{e_i}$. Since this proof applies to any [finite-dimensional vector space](wikipedia://en/38267), we have shown that our structure over $\mathbf{FdVect}$ defines a (left) rigid monoidal category.

Then, we must define braids and twists in such a way that they are compatible. In this case, this largely makes one determined given the other on the reals. For example, if we take the trivial braiding

$$\begin{aligned} c_{C_1,C_2}: C_1 \otimes C_2 &\to C_2 \otimes C_1\\ c_{C_1, C_2} (a, b) &\mapsto (b, a) \end{aligned}$$

then $c_{C_1, C_2} c_{C_2, C_1}=\mathrm{id}_{C_1\otimes C_2}$, so our twist must obey $\theta_{C_1 \otimes C_2} = \theta_{C_1} \otimes \theta_{C_2}$. In other words it must operate elementwise across tensor products. But any object $C\in\mathbf{FdVect}$ can be written in the form $C=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n 1$ for some $n$, $\theta_C=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n \theta_1 = \bigotimes_{i=1}^n \mathrm{id} = \mathrm{id}_C$, so our twists must also be trivial.

On the other hand, we can introduce any nonzero multiplicative factor into the above braiding rule without breaking isomorphism (at least in $\mathbb{C}$). Let us for example take the braiding

$$\begin{aligned} c_{C_1,C_2}: C_1 \otimes C_2 &\to C_2 \otimes C_1\\ c_{C_1, C_2} (a, b) &\mapsto i (b, a) \end{aligned}$$

Then $c_{C_1, C_2} c_{C_2, C_1}=-\mathrm{id}_{C_1\otimes C_2}$. Since $\theta_1 = \mathrm{id}$, then $\theta_{1 \otimes 1} = -\mathrm{id}_{1\otimes 1}$; by induction, if $C$ is $n$-dimensional, then $\theta_C = (-1)^{n+1} \mathrm{id}_C$.

### Other Examples {#Other_Examples}

- The category of [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346). In this category, the monoidal structure is the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844), the dual object is the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28linear_algebra%29) in the sense of (linear) algebra, which is again projective. The twists in this case are the [identity maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_map).
- A more sophisticated example of a ribbon category are finite-dimensional representations of a [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678).[^o6]

The name ribbon category is motivated by a graphical depiction of morphisms.[^o7]

## Variant {#Variant}

A **strongly ribbon category** is a ribbon category ***C*** equipped with a [dagger structure](wikipedia://en/7152070) such that the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) †: ***C***<sup>[op](wikipedia://en/372399)</sup> → ***C*** coherently preserves the ribbon structure.

:::
