# Chromatic homotopy theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chromatic homotopy theory"}

In mathematics, **chromatic homotopy theory** is a subfield of [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655) that studies [complex-oriented cohomology theories](wikipedia://en/40770350) from the "chromatic" point of view, which is based on [Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519)'s work relating cohomology theories to [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group). In this picture, theories are classified in terms of their "chromatic levels"; i.e., the heights of the formal groups that define the theories via the [Landweber exact functor theorem](wikipedia://en/28686098). Typical theories it studies include: [complex K-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_K-theory), [elliptic cohomology](wikipedia://en/10295395), [Morava K-theory](wikipedia://en/1033664) and [tmf](wikipedia://en/10274436).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Chromatic convergence theorem"}

## Chromatic convergence theorem {#Chromatic_convergence_theorem}

In algebraic topology, the **chromatic convergence theorem** states the [homotopy limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_limit) of the chromatic tower (defined below) of a finite [*p*-local spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_spectrum) $X$ is $X$ itself. The theorem was proved by [Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569) and [Ravenel](wikipedia://en/27877012).

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

Let $L_{E(n)}$ denotes the [Bousfield localization](wikipedia://en/40776006) with respect to the [Morava E-theory](wikipedia://en/84030397) and let $X$ be a finite, $p$-local spectrum. Then there is a tower associated to the localizations

$$\cdots \rightarrow L_{E(2)} X \rightarrow L_{E(1)} X \rightarrow L_{E(0)} X$$

called the **chromatic tower**, such that its homotopy limit is homotopic to the original spectrum $X$.

The stages in the tower above are often simplifications of the original spectrum. For example, $L_{E(0)} X$ is the rational localization and $L_{E(1)} X$ is the localization with respect to [*p*-local *K*-theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_K-theory).

#### Stable homotopy groups {#Stable_homotopy_groups}

In particular, if the $p$-local spectrum $X$ is the stable $p$-local [sphere spectrum](wikipedia://en/21118593) $\mathbb{S}_{(p)}$, then the homotopy limit of this sequence is the original $p$-local sphere spectrum. This is a key observation for studying stable homotopy groups of spheres using chromatic homotopy theory.
