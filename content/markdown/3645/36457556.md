# I. Michael Ross

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="I. Michael Ross"}

**Isaac Michael Ross** is a Distinguished Professor and Program Director of Control and Optimization at the [Naval Postgraduate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Postgraduate_School) in Monterey, CA. He has published a highly-regarded textbook on optimal control theory[^o2] and seminal papers in [pseudospectral optimal control](wikipedia://en/15983837) theory,[^o3] energy-sink theory,[^o4] the [optimization and deflection of near-Earth asteroids and comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_impact_avoidance),[^o5] [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics),[^o6][^o7] [attitude dynamics and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_dynamics_and_control), orbital mechanics,[^o8] real-time optimal control,[^o9][^o10] unscented optimal control,[^o11] continuous optimization[^o12][^o13][^o14] and aeromaneuvering guidance and control.[^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18] The Kang–Ross–Gong theorem,[^o47][^o35] [Ross' $\pi$ lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross'_π_lemma), Ross' time constant, the [Ross–Fahroo lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross–Fahroo_lemma), and the [Ross–Fahroo pseudospectral method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross–Fahroo_pseudospectral_method) are all named after him.[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o22][^o23] According to a report published by [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University),[^o24] Ross is one of the world's top 2% of scientists.

:::

## Theoretical contributions {#Theoretical_contributions}

Although Ross has made contributions to energy-sink theory, [attitude dynamics and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_dynamics_and_control)[^o26][^o27][^o28] and [planetary defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_impact_avoidance),[^o29] he is best known[^o19][^o20][^o21][^o23][^o30] for work on [pseudospectral optimal control](wikipedia://en/15983837).[^o31][^o32][^o33][^o34] In 2001, Ross and Fahroo announced[^o3] the [covector mapping principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covector_mapping_principle), first, as a special result in [pseudospectral optimal control](wikipedia://en/15983837), and later[^o31] as a general result in [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565). This principle was based on the [Ross–Fahroo lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross–Fahroo_lemma) which proves[^o20] that dualization and [discretization](wikipedia://en/330017) are not necessarily [commutative operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_operation) and that certain steps must be taken to promote commutation. When [discretization](wikipedia://en/330017) is commutative with dualization, then, under appropriate conditions, [Pontryagin's minimum principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontryagin's_minimum_principle) emerges as a consequence of the [convergence of the discretization](wikipedia://en/999701). Together with [F. Fahroo](wikipedia://en/36477771), W. Kang and Q. Gong, Ross proved a series of results on the convergence of pseudospectral discretizations of optimal control problems.[^o35] Ross and his coworkers showed that the [Legendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendre_pseudospectral_method) and [Chebyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_pseudospectral_method) pseudospectral discretizations converge to an optimal solution of a problem under the mild condition of boundedness of variations.[^o35]

## Software contributions {#Software_contributions}

In 2001, Ross created [DIDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIDO_%28optimal_control%29), a software package for solving [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) problems.[^o37][^o38][^o39] Powered by [pseudospectral methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross–Fahroo_pseudospectral_method), Ross created a user-friendly set of objects that required no knowledge of his theory to run DIDO. This work was used in on pseudospectral methods for solving optimal control problems.[^o40] DIDO is used for solving optimal control problems in aerospace applications,[^o41][^o42] search theory,[^o43] and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics). Ross' constructs have been licensed to other software products, and have been used by [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) to solve flight-critical problems on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station).[^o44]

## Flight contributions {#Flight_contributions}

In 2006, NASA used [DIDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DIDO_%28optimal_control%29) to implement zero propellant maneuvering[^o46] of the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station). In 2007, *[SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) News* printed a page 1 article[^o44] announcing the use of Ross' theory. This led other researchers[^o40] to explore the mathematics of [pseudospectral optimal control](wikipedia://en/15983837) theory. DIDO is also used to maneuver the Space Station and operate various ground and flight equipment to incorporate [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomy) and performance efficiency for [nonlinear control systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_control_system).[^o47]

## Awards and distinctions {#Awards_and_distinctions}

In 2010, Ross was elected a Fellow of the [American Astronautical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronautical_Society) for "his pioneering contributions to the theory, software and flight demonstration of [pseudospectral optimal control](wikipedia://en/15983837)." He also received (jointly with [Fariba Fahroo](wikipedia://en/36477771)), the [AIAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIAA) Mechanics and Control of Flight Award for "fundamentally changing the landscape of flight mechanics". His research has made headlines in *SIAM News*,[^o44] *[IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE) Control Systems Magazine*,[^o49] *[IEEE Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Spectrum)*,[^o22] and *Space Daily*.[^o50]
