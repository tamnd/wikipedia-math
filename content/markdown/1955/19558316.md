# Vertex enumeration problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vertex enumeration problem"}

In mathematics, the **vertex enumeration problem** for a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), a polyhedral [cell complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_complex), a [hyperplane arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplane_arrangement), or some other object of [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), is the problem of determination of the object's [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) given some formal representation of the object. A classical example is the problem of enumeration of the vertices of a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) specified by a [set of linear inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_of_linear_inequalities):[^o2]

:::

$$Ax \leq b$$

where *A* is an *m*×*n* matrix, *x* is an *n*×1 column vector of variables, and *b* is an *m*×1 column vector of constants. The inverse ([dual](wikipedia://en/609737)) problem of finding the bounding inequalities given the vertices is called *[facet enumeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_enumeration)* (see [convex hull algorithms](wikipedia://en/11700432)).

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543) of the problem is a subject of research in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). For unbounded polyhedra, the problem is known to be NP-hard, more precisely, there is no algorithm that runs in polynomial time in the combined input-output size, unless P=NP.[^o4]

A 1992 article by [David Avis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Avis) and [Komei Fukuda](wikipedia://en/67628503)[^o5] presents a [reverse-search algorithm](wikipedia://en/71470682) which finds the *v* vertices of a polytope defined by a nondegenerate system of *n* inequalities in *d* dimensions (or, dually, the *v* [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet) of the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of *n* points in *d* dimensions, where each facet contains exactly *d* given points) in time [O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Oh_notation)(*ndv*) and [space](wikipedia://en/338946) O(*nd*). The *v* vertices in a simple arrangement of *n* [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) in *d* dimensions can be found in O(*n*<sup>2</sup>*dv*) time and O(*nd*) space complexity. The Avis–Fukuda algorithm adapted the [criss-cross algorithm](wikipedia://en/31255067) for oriented matroids.

A 2025 article by Zelin Dong, Fenglei Fan, Huan Xiong, and Tieyong Zeng[^o6] introduced the *Zero rule* into an optimized reverse-search algorithm. This pivot rule is proven to terminate within *d* steps. Through a formal analysis of its properties, the rule was integrated into an efficient algorithm, achieving a time complexity O(*n<sup>2</sup>d<sup>2</sup>(v-v<sub>d</sub>) + ndv<sub>d</sub>*) where *v<sub>d</sub>* denotes the number of dictionaries that reach the terminal state in exactly *d* pivots under the *Zero rule*. This becomes O(*nd<sup>4</sup>v*) for simple arrangements, improving upon the O(*n<sup>2</sup>dv*) complexity of its predecessor.
