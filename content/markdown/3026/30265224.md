# Enzo Martinelli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Enzo Martinelli"}

**Enzo Martinelli** (11 November 1911 – 27 August 1999[^o4]) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in the [theory of functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables): he is best known for his work on the theory of [integral representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_representation) for [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) of several variables, notably for discovering the [Bochner–Martinelli formula](wikipedia://en/30280089) in 1938, and for his work in the theory of multi-dimensional residues.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Life {#Life}

He was born in [Pescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pescia) on 11 November 1911, where his father was the Director of the local [agricultural school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_school).[^o7] His family later went to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), where his father ended his working career as the [Director-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-general) of the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [Ministry of Public Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Education_%28Italy%29). Enzo Martilnelli lived in Rome almost all of his life: the only exception was a period of nearly eight years, from 1947 to 1954, when he was in [Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova), working at the local [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_degli_Studi_di_Genova).[^o8] In 1946 he married in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) Luigia Panella, also her a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who later become an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) at the [faculty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_%28university%29) of [Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) of the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), and who was his loving companion for the rest of his life. They had a son, Roberto, and a daughter, Maria Renata, who later followed her parents footsteps becoming also her a mathematician:[^o9] four grandchildren completed their family.[^o10]

### Academic career {#Academic_career}

According to (Rizza 2002, 165), Enzo's talent for [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) was already evident when he was only a [lyceum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyceum) [student](wikipedia://en/155526). In 1933 he earned his [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) from the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome): the title of his [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) was "*Sulle funzioni poligene di una e di due variabili complesse*",[^o13] and his thesis [supervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervisor#Science) was [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716).[^o14] From 1934 to 1946 he worked as an assistant professor first to the chair of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) held by [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) and then to the chair of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) held by [Enrico Bompiani](wikipedia://en/48385220). In 1939 he became "Libero Docente" (free professor) of [Mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396): he taught also [courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_%28education%29) on [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) as [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor).[^o15] In 1946 he won a competitive examination by a judging commission for the chair of "*Geometria analitica con elementi di Geometria Proiettiva e Geometria Descrittiva con Disegno*",[^o16] awarded by the [University of Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genova): the second place and the third place went respectively to [Giovanni Dantoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Dantoni) and [Guido Zappa](wikipedia://en/23748418).[^o17] Martinelli held that chair from 1946 to 1954, teaching also [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [function theory](wikipedia://en/5759), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625)[^o10] and algebraic analysis[^o18] as [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor). In 1954 he went back in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) to the chair of [Geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), holding that chair up to his [retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement), in 1982: he also taught courses on [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [higher mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_mathematics), [higher geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_geometry) upon charge. In the years 1968–1969, during a very difficult period for the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome),[^o19] he served the university as the director of the [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891) Institute of Mathematics.

He attended various [conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_conference) and [meetings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meeting). In 1943 and in 1946 he was invited in Zurich by [Rudolf Fueter](wikipedia://en/42010211), in order to present his researches: later and during all his career he lectured in almost all [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia) and foreign [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University).[^o20]

He was also a member of the [UMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Matematica_Italiana) Scientific Commission (from 1967 to 1972), of the [editorial boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editorial_board) of the [Rendiconti di Matematica e delle sue Applicazioni](wikipedia://en/51097313) (from 1955 to 1992) and of the [Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annali_di_Matematica_Pura_ed_Applicata) (from 1965 to 1999).[^o21]

### Personality traits {#Personality_traits}

Unanimously remembered as a real [gentleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentleman),[^o23] gifted by a caring attention, politeness, generosity and the rare ability to listen to [colleagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comrade) and [students](wikipedia://en/155526) alike,[^o24] (Gallarati 2000, 45) and (Rizza 1984, 6) remember long conversations with him on various mathematical research topics,[^o27] and his disposability to give help and advice to whoever asked for it. In particular (Rizza 1984, 7) recalls the time when he was his doctoral student at the [University of Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genova): they meet every Sunday in the afternoon at Martinelli's home, since Martinelli was not able to meet him during the week. During one of their meetings, lasting a little more than two hours, Martinelli taught him [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274)'s theory of [exterior differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_differential_form), and Rizza used successfully this tool in his first research works.[^o29] Another episode illustrating this aspect of Martinelli's personality is recalled by [Gaetano Fichera](wikipedia://en/22175811).[^o30] When he was back in Rome in 1945, at the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he exposed to Martinelli a theory identical to the theory of differential form: he developed it while being prisoner of the [nazists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazist) in [Teramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teramo) during wartime. Martinelli, very tactfully,[^o31] told him that the idea was already being developed by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) and [Georges de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699).

An excellent teacher himself, capable to arose curiosity and enthusiasm by his lessons,[^o24][^o32] he admired and much respected his own ones:[^o33] nevertheless, this was quite common for the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist) of the same and the preceding generations, who were advised by some of the best Italian scientists ever in the early days of their scientific career.[^o34] His [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) was [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716): other great [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) where among his teachers. [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891), [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661), [Enrico Bompiani](wikipedia://en/48385220), [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180) [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) and [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) were all working at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) when Enzo Martinelli was a student there, following their lessons: (Zappa 1984) describes the activity of the institute of mathematics during that period as extremely stimulating.[^o36]

Another central aspect of his personality was a deep sense of [justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice) and [legality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality):[^o10] Martinelli was very careful in performing his [citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen) and [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) duties, and he was also ready to fight for his own rights and for the needs of higher education. Concerned by the growing interference of [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy) in university education, already in the 1950s he was heard by (Rizza 1984, 6) complaining that: "*In Italia mancano le menti semplificatrici*".[^o38] Martinelli was also free from every kind of [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) to the point that when, during the [protests of 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_of_1968) in Italy, many [newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper) accused the Italian university scientific community of being so, all the [assistant professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) and [students](wikipedia://en/155526) of Martinelli (and perhaps Martinelli himself) were perplexed.[^o39] In the same period, while performing his duties as the director of the [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891) Institute of Mathematics at the Sapienza university of Rome, his rare [intellectual honesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_honesty)[^o40] and rigorous rationality, according to [Rizza](wikipedia://en/31431018), caused him troubles when dealing with many who "*believed in everything except the cold light of [reason](wikipedia://en/42446)*".[^o41]

### Honors {#Honors}

While still attending at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) he won the [Cotronei Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotronei_Foundation) prize, and after earning his [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea), he was awarded the [Beltrami Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltrami_Foundation) prize, the [Fubini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubini_prize) and [Torelli prizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli_prize),[^o3] and the Prize for Mathematical Sciences of the [Ministry of National Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Education_%28Italy%29): this last one was awarded him in 1943, and the judging commission consisted of [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) (as the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_%28corporate_title%29) of the commission), [Ugo Amaldi](wikipedia://en/56015853)[^o43] and [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) (as the [supervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervisor#Science) of the commission).[^o44] In 1948 he was elected [Corresponding Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_Member) of the [Accademia Ligure di Scienze e Lettere](https://web.archive.org/web/20110722040238/http://www.istituticulturali.it/moduli/istcult/istituto.jsp?s=2&idIstituto=312):[^o45] in 1961 and in 1977 he was elected respectively [Corresponding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_Member) and [Full Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician) of the [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei), and from 1982 to 1985 he was "*Professore Linceo*".[^o46] Finally, in 1980 he was elected [Corresponding Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_Member) of the [Accademia delle Scienze di Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino) and then, in 1994, [Full Member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician). Also, in 1986, the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome), to which Enzo Martinelli was particularly tied for all his life,[^o10] awarded him the title of [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).

## Work {#Work}

### Research activity {#Research_activity}

He is the author of more than 50 research works, the first of which was published when Martinelli still was an undergraduate student:[^o51] precisely, his research production consist of 47 papers and 30 between [treatises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise), [textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) and various other publications.[^o52] According to (Rizza 1984, 2), his research personality can be described by two words: "*[enthusiasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enthusiasm)*" and "*[dissatisfaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissatisfaction)*":[^o54] enthusiasm is meant as his steady interest in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at all levels, while dissatisfaction is meant as the desire to going deeper into all mathematical problems investigated, without stopping at first success and expressing all the results in a simple, elegant and essential form.

### Teaching activity {#Teaching_activity}

The aspects of his personality described before and his deep professional commitment also made him a well-liked teacher:[^o56] At least fifteen [textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759)[^o52] testify his didactic activity.[^o39] Those books appear as models of clarity and [mathematical rigour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigour),[^o57] and offer insights on more complex theories and problems to the clever student: indeed, it was one of Martinelli's concerns to teach mathematics by showing its lively development and its attractiveness in terms of interesting, difficult problems offered, in order that no gifted student would abandon the idea to do mathematical research.[^o39]
