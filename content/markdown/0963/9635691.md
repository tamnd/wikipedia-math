# Davies attack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Davies attack"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), the **Davies attack** is a dedicated statistical [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715) method for attacking the [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) (DES). The attack was originally created in 1987 by [Donald Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Davies). In 1994, [Eli Biham](wikipedia://en/348920) and [Alex Biryukov](wikipedia://en/9189491) made significant improvements to the technique. It is a [known-plaintext attack](wikipedia://en/517019) based on the non-uniform [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of the outputs of pairs of adjacent [S-boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_box). It works by collecting many known plaintext/ciphertext pairs and calculating the [empirical distribution](wikipedia://en/2690455) of certain characteristics. Bits of the [key](wikipedia://en/53039) can be deduced given sufficiently many known plaintexts, leaving the remaining bits to be found through [brute force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brute_force_attack). There are tradeoffs between the number of required plaintexts, the number of key bits found, and the probability of success; the attack can find 24 bits of the key with 2<sup>52</sup> known plaintexts and 53% success rate.

:::

The Davies attack can be adapted to other [Feistel ciphers](wikipedia://en/352709) besides DES. In 1998, Pornin developed techniques for analyzing and maximizing a cipher's resistance to this kind of cryptanalysis.
