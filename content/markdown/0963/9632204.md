# De Vaucouleurs's law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="De Vaucouleurs's law"}

**de Vaucouleurs's law** (also known as the **de Vaucouleurs profile** or the **de Vaucouleurs model**) is a mathematical function that describes how the [surface brightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_brightness) of an [elliptical galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptical_galaxy) varies as a function of apparent distance from the center of the galaxy. The model is named after [Gérard de Vaucouleurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_de_Vaucouleurs) who first formulated it in 1948.[^o2][^o3] Other notable density profiles which approximately reproduce de Vaucouleurs's law include the [Jaffe profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffe_profile) and the [Dehnen profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehnen_profile).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

de Vaucouleurs's law is a special case of the [Sérsic profile](wikipedia://en/19744097), with an [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sersic_index) of $n=4$, and has the functional form

$$\ln I(R) = \ln I_{0} - k R^{1/4},$$

or

$$I(R) = I_0 \exp{\!\left(-k R^{1/4}\right)},$$

where $I_{0}$ is the [intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_brightness) at $R = 0$.[^o6] Defining $R_e$ as the [half-light radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_effective_radius) (the [isophote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isophote) containing half of the galaxy's total [luminosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity)), the de Vaucoulers profile may also be expressed as

$$I(R) = I_0 \exp \left( -7.66925 \left[ \left( \frac{R}{R_e}\right)^{1/4} -1\right] \right)$$

where the intensity at $R_e$ is defined as $I_e = I(0) \exp(-7.66925)$.[^o7] This can be confirmed by noting that

$$\int^{R_e}_0 I(r)2\pi r \, dr = \frac{1}{2} \int^{\infty}_0 I(r)2\pi r \, dr .$$

holds in general.

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

de Vaucouleurs's law has multiple applications in [galactic astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extragalactic_astronomy). It is primarily used for the modeling the surface brightness of elliptical galaxies and [galactic bulges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_bulge), where it may be used as a tool in bulge-disk decompositions. de Vaucouleurs profiles may also be mathematically de-projected to yield expressions for mass distributions, [gravitational potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_potential), and internal stellar [velocity dispersions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity_dispersion), which may be used in [dynamical modeling](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o7][^o10][^o11] Notably, however, though the de Vaucouleurs profile fits many elliptical galaxies very well, the specific Sérsic index of $n=4$ likely does not hold significant physical significance as many elliptical galaxies are also well-fit by Sérsic profiles of index $3<n<10$.[^o12] As a result, recent galaxy light profile modeling studies employ the more general Sérsic profile as the de Vaucouleurs profile may introduce systematic errors under certain conditions.[^o13][^o14]

:::
