# Proofs from THE BOOK

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Proofs from THE BOOK"}

***Proofs from THE BOOK*** is a book of [mathematical proofs](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Martin Aigner](wikipedia://en/24448177) and [Günter M. Ziegler](wikipedia://en/14628647), first published in 1998. The book is inspired by and named after an expression used by the [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), who often referred to "The Book" in which [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God) kept the best proof of each mathematical [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977). During a lecture in 1985, Erdős said, "You don't have to believe in God, but you should believe in The Book."[^n1] The greatest praise Erdős gave to mathematical work was to proclaim it "straight from the Book".[^n2]

:::

Aigner and Zeigler proposed to Erdős a real book that would be "a first (and very modest) approximation to 'The Book'". Erdős had many suggestions for proofs that should be included, and would have been a co-author except that he died in 1996. *Proofs from THE BOOK* is instead dedicated to his memory.[^n3]

Including its original publication, *Proofs from THE BOOK* has had six editions in English, and has been translated into Persian, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Japanese, Chinese, Polish, Portuguese, Korean, Turkish, Russian, Spanish and Greek.

The American Mathematical Society awarded the 2018 [Leroy P. Steele Prize for Mathematical Exposition](wikipedia://en/1252550) to Aigner and Ziegler for this book.[^n4]

## Content {#Content}

In its most recent sixth edition, *Proofs from THE BOOK* contains 45 chapters grouped into five parts: [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). In most cases, each chapter is devoted to a particular theorem, sometimes with multiple proofs and related results. In a few cases, a chapter explores proofs related to a particular theme.

Aigner and Ziegler stated that they had no definite criteria for what counted as a proof from "The Book", but selected only those that would be accessible to someone with knowledge of basic undergraduate mathematics.[^n3] Nevertheless some background in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), analysis, and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) is required to understand certain parts.[^n5] Ziegler accepted that different proofs would be "perfect" for different readers.[^n1]

There are differences between the various editions of the book. These are mostly additions of chapters, but some involved adding new results or new proofs for already-present results, and there was one complete deletion of a chapter.[^n6] The first edition included [John Leech](wikipedia://en/4548516)'s proof that it is impossible for thirteen unit spheres to touch a given sphere. It was removed for later editions because Aigner and Ziegler could not fill in details in a way that was "brief and elegant".[^n7]

Because the book concerns beauty in mathematics, Aigner and Ziegler thought that it should have a correspondingly attractive appearance, and thus devoted a lot of time to the text and [typography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography), and to selecting appropriate photographs and other illustrations.[^n8]

The book is illustrated with drawings by [Karl H. Hofmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Heinrich_Hofmann).

## Outline {#Outline}

The outline below relates to the sixth edition. Previous editions contained fewer chapters, in some cases differently arranged.

### Number theory {#Number_theory}

- Chapter 1: Six proofs of the infinity of the [primes](wikipedia://en/23666), including [Euclid](wikipedia://en/3143591)'s and [Furstenberg's](wikipedia://en/13230920).
- Chapter 2: Paul Erdős's 1932 [proof of Bertrand's postulate](wikipedia://en/254237).
- Chapter 3: [Binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients) are almost never powers.
- Chapter 4: [Fermat's theorem on sums of two squares](wikipedia://en/1850216).
- Chapter 5: Two proofs of the [law of quadratic reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_quadratic_reciprocity).
- Chapter 6: Proof of [Wedderburn's little theorem](wikipedia://en/7132002) asserting that every finite [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603).
- Chapter 7: The [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495), [Hadamard's maximal determinant problem](wikipedia://en/30556829) and [Hadamard's inequality](wikipedia://en/612000).
- Chapter 8: Some irrational numbers, including [Ivan Niven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_M._Niven)'s proof that $\pi$ ([pi](wikipedia://en/23601)) is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689).
- Chapter 9: Four proofs of the solution to [Basel problem](wikipedia://en/490303), namely that $\sum_{n \geq 1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

### Geometry {#Geometry}

- Chapter 10: [Hilbert's third problem](wikipedia://en/152703).
- Chapter 11: Lines in the plane, including the [Sylvester–Gallai theorem](wikipedia://en/1052632) and the [De Bruijn–Erdős theorem](wikipedia://en/26825148).
- Chapter 12: The slope problem.
- Chapter 13: Applications of [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/9613).
- Chapter 14: [Cauchy's rigidity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy's_rigidity_theorem).
- Chapter 15: The non-existence of the [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118).
- Chapter 16: Touching [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781).
- Chapter 17: Large angles in point sets.
- Chapter 18: [Borsuk's conjecture](wikipedia://en/7765817).
- Chapter 19: The [Schröder–Bernstein theorem](wikipedia://en/44218028) and [Wetzel's problem](wikipedia://en/46062204) on families of analytic functions with few distinct values.

### Analysis {#Analysis}

- Chapter 21: [The fundamental theorem of algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_fundamental_theorem_of_algebra).
- Chapter 22: [Monsky's theorem](wikipedia://en/35098575).
- Chapter 23: A theorem of [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) on polynomials.
- Chapter 24: [Van der Waerden's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_der_Waerden's_conjecture).
- Chapter 25: [Littlewood–Offord lemma](wikipedia://en/1976420).
- Chapter 26: The cotangent and [Herglotz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Herglotz)'s trick.
- Chapter 27: [Buffon's needle problem](wikipedia://en/667479).

### Combinatorics {#Combinatorics}

- Chapter 28: [Pigeonhole principle](wikipedia://en/54217) and [double counting](wikipedia://en/296942), [Sperner's lemma](wikipedia://en/465067).
- Chapter 29: Results on tiling rectangles due to [Nicolaas Govert de Bruijn](wikipedia://en/3166399) and [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602).
- Chapter 30: [Sperner's theorem](wikipedia://en/748844), [Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem](wikipedia://en/296188) and [Hall's theorem](wikipedia://en/186969)
- Chapter 31: [Shuffling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuffling) cards.
- Chapter 32: [Lattice](wikipedia://en/42123338) paths and determinants, including [Lindström–Gessel–Viennot lemma](wikipedia://en/31847498) and the [Cauchy–Binet formula](wikipedia://en/357354).
- Chapter 33: Four proofs of [Cayley's formula](wikipedia://en/1266713).
- Chapter 34: [Identities](wikipedia://en/298428) versus [bijections](wikipedia://en/3942).
- Chapter 35: [Kakeya sets in vector spaces over finite fields](wikipedia://en/588260#Kakeya_sets_in_vector_spaces_over_finite_fields).

### Graph Theory {#Graph_Theory}

- Chapter 37: The [Bregman–Minc inequality](wikipedia://en/48413286).
- Chapter 38: The [Dinitz problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinitz_problem).
- Chapter 39: The [five color theorem](wikipedia://en/1982872).
- Chapter 40: Steve Fisk's proof of the [art gallery theorem](wikipedia://en/1448859#Fisk's_short_proof).
- Chapter 41: Five proofs of [Turán's theorem](wikipedia://en/360601).
- Chapter 42: [Shannon capacity](wikipedia://en/39932177) and [Lovász number](wikipedia://en/28111101).
- Chapter 43: [Chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of [Kneser graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kneser_graphs).
- Chapter 44: [Friendship theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_theorem).
- Chapter 45: Some proofs using the [probabilistic method](wikipedia://en/173525).
