# (g,K)-module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="(g,K)-module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in the [representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_theory_of_Lie_groups) of [reductive Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_Lie_group), a $(\mathfrak{g},K)$**\-module** is an algebraic object, first introduced by [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190),[^o2] used to deal with continuous infinite-dimensional representations using algebraic techniques. Harish-Chandra showed that the study of [irreducible unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) of a real reductive [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), *G*, could be reduced to the study of irreducible $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-modules, where $\mathfrak{g}$ is the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of *G* and *K* is a [maximal compact subgroup](wikipedia://en/1496726) of *G*.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *G* be a real Lie group. Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be its Lie algebra, and *K* a maximal compact subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{k}$. A $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-module is defined as follows:[^o6] it is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V* that is both a [Lie algebra representation](wikipedia://en/318712) of $\mathfrak{g}$ and a [group representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of *K* (without regard to the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) of *K*) satisfying the following three conditions

\1. for any *v* ∈ *V*, *k* ∈ *K*, and *X* ∈ $\mathfrak{g}$

$$k\cdot (X\cdot v)=(\operatorname{Ad}(k)X)\cdot (k\cdot v)$$

\2. for any *v* ∈ *V*, *Kv* spans a *finite-dimensional* subspace of *V* on which the action of *K* is continuous

\3. for any *v* ∈ *V* and *Y* ∈ $\mathfrak{k}$

$$\left.\left(\frac{d}{dt}\exp(tY)\cdot v\right)\right|_{t=0}=Y\cdot v.$$

In the above, the dot, $\cdot$, denotes both the action of $\mathfrak{g}$ on *V* and that of *K*. The notation Ad(*k*) denotes the [adjoint action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group) of *G* on $\mathfrak{g}$, and *Kv* is the set of vectors $k\cdot v$ as *k* varies over all of *K*.

The first condition can be understood as follows: if *G* is the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(*n*, **R**), then $\mathfrak{g}$ is the algebra of all *n* by *n* matrices, and the adjoint action of *k* on *X* is *kXk*<sup>−1</sup>; condition 1 can then be read as

$$kXv=kXk^{-1}kv=\left(kXk^{-1}\right)kv.$$

In other words, it is a compatibility requirement among the actions of *K* on *V*, $\mathfrak{g}$ on *V*, and *K* on $\mathfrak{g}$. The third condition is also a compatibility condition, this time between the action of $\mathfrak{k}$ on *V* viewed as a sub-Lie algebra of $\mathfrak{g}$ and its action viewed as the differential of the action of *K* on *V*.
