# Soliton distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Soliton distribution"}

A **soliton distribution** is a type of [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) that arises in the theory of [erasure correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasure_correcting_code), which use information redundancy to compensate for transmission errors manifesting as missing (erased) data. A paper by Luby[^o2] introduced two forms of such distributions, the **ideal soliton distribution** and the **robust soliton distribution**.

:::

## Ideal distribution {#Ideal_distribution}

The **ideal soliton distribution** is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on the integers from 1 to *K*, where *K* is the single parameter of the distribution. The [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) is given by[^o4]

$$p(1)= \frac{1}{K},$$

$$p(i)= \frac{1}{i(i-1)} \qquad (i=2,3,\dots,K). \,$$

## Robust distribution {#Robust_distribution}

The **robust** form of distribution is defined by adding an extra set of values *t(i)* to the elements of mass function of the ideal soliton distribution and then normalizing so that the values add up to 1. The extra set of values, *t(i)*, are defined in terms of an additional real-valued parameter *δ* (which is interpreted as a failure probability) and *c*, a constant parameter. Define *R* as *R*=*c* *ln*(*K*/*δ*)$\sqrt{K}$. Then the values added to *p*(*i*), before the final normalization, are[^o4]

$$t(i)= \frac{R}{iK}, \qquad \qquad (i=1,2,\dots,K/R-1), \,$$

$$t(i)= \frac{R\ln(R/\delta)}{K}, \qquad (i=K/R), \,$$

$$t(i)= 0, \qquad \qquad (i=K/R+1,\dots,K). \,$$

While the ideal soliton distribution has a [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127) (or spike) at 2, the effect of the extra component in the robust distribution is to add an additional spike at the value *K/R*.
