# Detlef Laugwitz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Detlef Laugwitz"}

*Image: Detlef Laugwitz.jpg.* Detlef Laugwitz.

**Detlef Laugwitz** (1932–2000) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and historian, who worked in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born on 11 May 1932 in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic). Starting in 1949, he studied mathematics, [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [Georg-August-University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), where he received his doctorate in 1954.[^o3] Until 1956 he worked in the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach). In 1958 he became a lecturer at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), where he obtained his Habilitation. In 1958 he moved to the [Technical University of Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Darmstadt), where in 1962 he became a professor, and remained until his retirement. From 1976 to 1984 he was a visiting professor at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).

## Work {#Work}

Laugwitz worked in differential geometry of infinite dimensional vector spaces (his dissertation) and in [Finsler geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finsler_geometry). In 1958 he and [Curt Schmieden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Schmieden) developed their own approach to [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) through field extensions, independently of [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024). They described this as "infinitesimal mathematics" and leading back to the historical roots in [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281). In 1996 he published the standard biography of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980).
