::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Rectified 6-cubes"}

In six-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rectified 6-cube** is a convex [uniform 6-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821728), being a [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of the regular [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462).

:::

There are unique 6 degrees of rectifications, the zeroth being the [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462), and the 6th and last being the [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518). Vertices of the rectified 6-cube are located at the edge-centers of the 6-cube. Vertices of the birectified 6-cube are located in the square face centers of the 6-cube.

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- Rectified hexeract (acronym: rax) (Jonathan Bowers)[^n1]

::: {#o15 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The rectified 6-cube may be constructed from the [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) by [truncating](wikipedia://en/2192366) its vertices at the midpoints of its edges.

:::

### Coordinates {#Coordinates}

The [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) of the vertices of the rectified 6-cube with edge length $\sqrt{2}$ are all permutations of:

$$(0,\ \pm1,\ \pm1,\ \pm1,\ \pm1,\ \pm1)$$

## Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

These polytopes are part of a set of 63 [uniform 6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821728) generated from the B<sub>6</sub> [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane), including the regular [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) and [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518).
