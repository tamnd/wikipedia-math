# Darboux cyclide

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Darboux cyclide"}

*Image: Darboux cyclide.png.* A smooth Darboux cyclide

A **Darboux cyclide** is an [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) at most 4 that contains multiple families of [circles](wikipedia://en/6220).[^o2][^o3][^o4] Named after [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux) who studied these surfaces in 1880, Darboux cyclides are a [superset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superset) of [Dupin cyclides](wikipedia://en/705128) and [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570). These surfaces have applications in [architectural geometry](wikipedia://en/19315523) and [computer-aided geometric design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer-aided_geometric_design) (CAGD).[^o2][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Darboux cyclide is defined as a surface whose equation in a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) has the form

$$\lambda(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)^2 + (x^2 + y^2 + z^2)L(x, y, z) + Q(x, y, z) = 0$$

where $\lambda$ is a constant, $L(x, y, z) = \mu x + \nu y + \kappa z$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) 1, and $Q$ is a polynomial of degree at most 2 with coefficients that do not [vanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanish_%28mathematics%29) simultaneously. If the left-hand side of this equation factors into non-constant polynomials with [complex coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_polynomial), the Darboux cyclide is called a *reducible* cyclide. A reducible cyclide either splits into a union of spheres/planes or [degenerates](wikipedia://en/232952) to a curve in [$\mathbb R^3$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R^3).[^o2][^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

The mathematical study of Darboux cyclides began with [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117)'s work in 1865, followed by significant contributions from [Gaston Darboux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_Darboux) in 1880.[^o3] [Julian Coolidge](wikipedia://en/4379392) provided a comprehensive discussion of these surfaces in his 1916 monograph. After a period of reduced interest, geometers rediscovered these surfaces in the late 20th century, particularly due to their remarkable property of carrying multiple families of circles.[^o2]

## Properties and applications {#Properties_and_applications}

Darboux cyclides can carry up to six [families of real circles](wikipedia://en/5726601). That is, these circles lie entirely within the surface—they are contained within it as part of its geometric structure. These circle families manifest in two distinct types. The first type consists of paired families, where two families of circles are related such that any sphere through a circle of one family intersects the cyclide in a circle of the other family. The second type comprises single families, which arise when the cyclide is generated as a canal surface (the envelope of a one-parameter family of spheres).[^o2]

A **Möbius sphere** (also known as an ***M*-sphere**) $S$ is the set given by the equation $\lambda \mathbf x^2 + ax + by + cz + d = 0$, where $\lambda, a, b, c, d$ do not vanish simultaneously. Darboux cyclides can exhibit [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) with respect to up to five pairwise [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) Möbius spheres, though at least one of these spheres must be imaginary; that is, one *M*-sphere has no real points at all.[^o2]

Smooth Darboux cyclides can be classified topologically into three distinct categories: [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859)-like surfaces, [toruslike](wikipedia://en/74800) surfaces, and configurations consisting of two spheres.[^o2]

In [architectural geometry](wikipedia://en/19315523), Darboux cyclides have been applied in the *rationalization* of [freeform structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeform_surface_modelling)–the process of taking a complex freeform architectural design and breaking it down into parts that can be manufactured and built while maintaining the designer's artistic intent. Their ability to carry multiple families of circles makes Darboux cyclides particularly useful in the design of circular arc structures and the creation of panels and supporting elements in architectural surfaces. The geometric properties of Darboux cyclides allow for efficient manufacturing processes and [structural stability](wikipedia://en/2515807) in architectural designs.[^o2]
