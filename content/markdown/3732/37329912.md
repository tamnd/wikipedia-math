# James Eells

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Eells"}

**James Eells** (October 25, 1926 – February 14, 2007) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who specialized in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Eells was born on 25 October 1926, in Cleveland, Ohio.[^o4] Eells studied mathematics at [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College) in [Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine) and earned his undergraduate degree in 1947. After graduation he spent one year teaching mathematics at [Robert College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_College) in [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul) and starting in 1948 was for two years an instructor at [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College) in [Amherst, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst,_Massachusetts). Next he undertook graduate study at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where in 1954 he received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) under [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) with thesis *Geometric Aspects of Integration Theory*.

In the academic year 1955–1956 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (and subsequently in 1962–1963, 1972–1973, 1977, and 1982).[^o5] He taught at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) for several years. In 1964 he became a full professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). In 1963 and in 1966–1967 he was at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and after a visit to the new mathematics department developed by [Erik Christopher Zeeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Christopher_Zeeman) at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) Eells became a professor of mathematical analysis there in 1969. Eells organized many of the University of Warwick Symposia in mathematics.

In 1986 he became the first director of the mathematics section of the [Abdus Salam International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdus_Salam_International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste); for six years he served as director in addition to his appointment at the University of Warwick. In 1992 he retired and lived in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge).

Eells did research on [global analysis](wikipedia://en/42027498), especially, [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484) on [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652), which are important in the theory of [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028). His doctoral students included [John C. Wood](wikipedia://en/45284500).

In 1970 he was an invited speaker at the [International Mathematical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematical_Congress) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) (*On Fredholm manifolds* with K. D. Elworthy).

He was co-editor of the collected works of [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794). Eells's doctoral students include Luc Lemaire, [Peter Štefan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Štefan) (1941–1978), Giorgio Valli (1960–1999) and Pierre de la Harpe. Eells was married since 1950 and had a son and three daughters.
