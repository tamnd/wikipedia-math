# Hans Maass

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Maass"}

*Image: Hans Maass.jpg.* Hans Maass (1969)

**Hans Maass** (Hans Maaß; June 17, 1911, in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg) – April 15, 1992) was a German mathematician who introduced [Maass wave forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maass_wave_forms) (Maass 1949) and [Koecher–Maass series](wikipedia://en/30235068) (Maass 1950) and [Maass–Selberg relations](wikipedia://en/33188525) and who proved most of the [Saito–Kurokawa conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saito–Kurokawa_conjecture). Maass was a student of [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990).

:::

Maaß was primarily concerned with the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), being influenced in particular by [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602) (according to Maaß in his inaugural address for admission to the Heidelberg Academy, he met him in the early 1950s), whose *Gesammelte Werke* he also co-edited with [K. S. Chandrasekharan](wikipedia://en/22907213), in addition to Hecke and [Hans Petersson](wikipedia://en/9079432), Hecke's assistant, who suggested the topic of his dissertation. He became known for his introduction of non-[analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) in the 1940s (Maaß waveforms). Instead of satisfying [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941) (as analytic functions do), they are [eigenfunctions](wikipedia://en/228601) of the invariant [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706); Maaß therefore called them waveforms. Internationally, these forms are known by his name. The motivation for the introduction came in part from Maaß's interest in connections of the theory of modular forms to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). Maaß was also concerned with automorphic functions in several variables, Siegel modular functions, and associated zeta functions.
