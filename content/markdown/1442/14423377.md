# Binary cyclic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binary cyclic group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **binary cyclic group** of the *n*-gon is the cyclic group of order 2*n*, $C_{2n}$, thought of as an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of the cyclic group $C_n$ by a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 2. Coxeter writes the *binary cyclic group* with angle-brackets, ⟨*n*⟩, and the index 2 subgroup as (*n*) or \[*n*\]<sup>\+</sup>.

:::

It is the [binary polyhedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_polyhedral_group) corresponding to the cyclic group.[^o2]

In terms of binary polyhedral groups, the binary cyclic group is the preimage of the cyclic group of rotations ($C_n < \operatorname{SO}(3)$) under the 2:1 [covering homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_homomorphism)

$$\operatorname{Spin}(3) \to \operatorname{SO}(3)\,$$

of the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) by the [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231).

As a subgroup of the spin group, the binary cyclic group can be described concretely as a discrete subgroup of the unit [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), under the isomorphism $\operatorname{Spin}(3) \cong \operatorname{Sp}(1)$ where [Sp(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp%281%29) is the multiplicative group of unit quaternions. (For a description of this homomorphism see the article on [quaternions and spatial rotations](wikipedia://en/186057).)

## Presentation {#Presentation}

The *binary cyclic group* can be defined as the set of $2n$<sup>th</sup> roots of unity—that is, the set $\left\{\omega_n^k \; | \; k \in \{0,1,2,...,2n-1\}\right\}$, where

$$\omega_n = e^{i\pi/n} = \cos\frac{\pi}{n} + i\sin\frac{\pi}{n},$$

using multiplication as the group operation.
