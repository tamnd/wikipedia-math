# Sargan–Hansen test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sargan–Hansen test"}

The **Sargan–Hansen test** or **Sargan's $J$ test** is a [statistical test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_test) used for testing [over-identifying restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-identifying_restriction) in a [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576). It was proposed by [John Denis Sargan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Denis_Sargan) in 1958,[^o2] and several variants were derived by him in 1975.[^o3] [Lars Peter Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Peter_Hansen) re-worked through the derivations and showed that it can be extended to general non-linear [GMM](wikipedia://en/1975550) in a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) context.[^o4]

:::

The Sargan test is based on the assumption that model parameters are identified via a priori restrictions on the coefficients, and tests the validity of over-identifying restrictions. The test statistic can be computed from [residuals](wikipedia://en/461509) from [instrumental variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_variable) regression by constructing a quadratic form based on the cross-product of the residuals and exogenous variables.[^o5] Under the null hypothesis that the over-identifying restrictions are valid, the statistic is asymptotically distributed as a [chi-square](wikipedia://en/113424) variable with $(m - k)$ degrees of freedom (where $m$ is the number of instruments and $k$ is the number of endogenous variables).
