# Stanisława Nikodym

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisława Nikodym"}

**Stanisława Nikodym** (née Liliental; 2 July 1897 – 25 March 1988) was a Polish mathematician and artist. She is known for her results in [continuum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_theory), especially on [Jordanian](wikipedia://en/355931) continuums.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Stanisława Dorota Liliental was born in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) to [Regina Lilientalowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regina_Lilientalowa), an ethnographer, and Nathan Liliental. Stanisława had a younger brother, Antoni (born 1908). She attended [Helena Skłodowska-Szalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Skłodowska-Szalay)'s primary school, and went to Warsaw's private school for women for 7 years. She joined the [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University) in 1916, reading mathematics under [Stefan Mazurkiewicz](wikipedia://en/629203), [Zygmunt Janiszewski](wikipedia://en/362471), and [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) [^n1]

She married [Otto M. Nikodym](wikipedia://en/5471556), a mathematician, in 1924, and joined him at [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków). Supervised by Mazurkiewicz, she was awarded a doctoral degree from the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in 1925. She was the first woman in Poland to obtain a PhD in mathematics.[^n2]

Receiving government funding to study in Paris, she and Otto attended the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) for two years from 1926. In 1930, they returned to Warsaw. She took up a job at the [Warsaw Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Polytechnic), working with [Franciszek Leja](wikipedia://en/1983106) till 1936, when he left for Kraków.[^n3]

Her brother, Antoni, a chemist and officer in the Polish army, was murdered by Russians during the [Katyn Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_Massacre) in 1940.[^n1] Under the German Nazi occupation of Poland, *unnecessary* occupations, including higher education, were suppressed. The Nikodyms conducted clandestine classes in mathematics despite the danger of punishment.[^n4] In 1944's [Warsaw Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Uprising), she and her husband lost their possessions, including several unpublished mathematical works. They moved to Belgium for a congress of mathematicians in 1946, and Otto gave lectures in various European cities, before they emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), settling in [Gambier, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambier,_Ohio).[^n5]

After her husband's death in 1974, she donated their papers and her paintings to the Briscoe Center for American History at the University of Texas, Austin.[^n6] Stanisława Nikodym died in Warsaw in 1988.[^n5]

## Career {#Career}

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

While on leave from university in 1918–1919, Stanisława taught mathematics to soldiers in the Polish army.[^n2]

Her doctoral thesis was titled *On disconnecting the plane by connected sets and continua*.[^n1] She published three books and several articles before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) broke out.[^n3]

Among her findings were necessary and sufficient conditions for a subcontinuum of a Jordanian continuum to be Jordanian. She also established that if the intersection and union of two closed sets are Jordanian continua, then so are the sets themselves.[^n3]

In the 1940s, she taught mathematics at [Kenyon College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyon_College) in Gambier, Ohio, where her husband was also a member of the faculty.[^n7]

### Art {#Art}

As a student, Liliental participated in open-air painting in [Sandomierz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandomierz). She painted the cityscape several times in [watercolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolor_painting) over many years in the inter-war period from 1922. The resulting works were presented at an exhibition in 1933, after which she donated them to the District Museum in Sandomierz, where they remain to this day.[^n8]
