# Paul Althaus Smith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Althaus Smith"}

*Image: SmithPaul MailletEdmond Zurich1932.tif.* Smith (left, chin obscured), and Edmond Maillet at [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) 1932

**Paul Althaus Smith** (May 18, 1900 – June 13, 1980) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). His name occurs in two significant conjectures in [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732): the [Smith conjecture](wikipedia://en/4476828), which is now a theorem, and the [Hilbert–Smith conjecture](wikipedia://en/671711), which was proved in dimension 3 in 2013. *Smith theory* is a theory about [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) of finite order of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), particularly [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859).

:::

Smith was a student of [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384) at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), moving to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with Lefschetz in the mid-1920s. He finished his doctorate at Princeton, in 1926. His [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) thesis was published in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* that same year. He also worked with [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906), with whom he wrote a 1928 paper in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), entitled *Structure analysis of surface transformations*, which appeared in the *[Journal des Mathématiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_des_Mathématiques)*.

He subsequently became a professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College). His students at Columbia included [Sherman K. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_K._Stein) and [Moses Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Richardson). He has many academic descendants through Richardson and his student [Louis Billera](wikipedia://en/22444348).

## Family {#Family}

Smith was married to the Swiss–American [early music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_music) pioneer [Suzanne Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Bloch). They had two children.
