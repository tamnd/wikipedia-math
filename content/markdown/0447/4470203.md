# Inscribed figure

*Image: Inscribed circles.svg.* Inscribed circles of various polygons (the circles from inside are passing through each of the other objects)

*Image: Circumcentre.svg.* An inscribed triangle of a circle

*Image: Rhombic tricontahedron cube tetrahedron.png.* A red [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) inscribed in a yellow [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) which is inscribed in a grey [rhombic triacontahedron](wikipedia://en/665025). (A rotating model is here)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inscribed figure"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **inscribed** [planar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) or [solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_%28geometry%29) is one that is enclosed by and fits snugly inside another geometric shape or solid.[^o2] To say that "figure F is inscribed in figure G" means precisely the same thing as "figure G is circumscribed about figure F". A [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) or [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) inscribed in a [convex polygon](wikipedia://en/660019) (or a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) or [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381) inscribed in a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron)) is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to every [side](wikipedia://en/12158034) or [face](wikipedia://en/149926) of the outer figure (but see [Inscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991786) for semantic variants). A polygon inscribed in a circle, ellipse, or polygon (or a polyhedron inscribed in a sphere, ellipsoid, or polyhedron) has each [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) on the outer figure; if the outer figure is a polygon or polyhedron, there must be a vertex of the inscribed polygon or polyhedron on each side of the outer figure. An inscribed figure is not necessarily unique in orientation; this can easily be seen, for example, when the given outer figure is a circle, in which case a rotation of an inscribed figure gives another inscribed figure that is [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to the original one.

:::

Familiar examples of inscribed figures include circles inscribed in [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) or [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), and triangles or regular polygons inscribed in circles. A circle inscribed in any polygon is called its [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle), in which case the polygon is said to be a [tangential polygon](wikipedia://en/35520654). A polygon inscribed in a circle is said to be a [cyclic polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon), and the circle is said to be its circumscribed circle or [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432).

The inradius or [filling radius](wikipedia://en/12868362) of a given outer figure is the [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the inscribed circle or sphere, if it exists.

The definition given above assumes that the objects concerned are embedded in two- or three-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), but can easily be generalized to higher dimensions and other [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018).

For an alternative usage of the term "inscribed", see the [inscribed square problem](wikipedia://en/27631070), in which a square is considered to be inscribed in another figure (even a non-convex one) if all four of its vertices are on that figure.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every circle has an inscribed triangle with any three given [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) measures (summing of course to 180°), and every triangle can be inscribed in some circle (which is called its [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262) or circumcircle).
- Every triangle has an inscribed circle, called the [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle_and_excircles_of_a_triangle).
- Every circle has an inscribed regular polygon of *n* sides, for any *n* ≥ 3, and every regular polygon can be inscribed in some circle (called its circumcircle).
- Every regular polygon has an inscribed circle (called its incircle), and every circle can be inscribed in some regular polygon of *n* sides, for any *n* ≥ 3.
- Not every polygon with more than three sides has an inscribed circle; those polygons that do are called [tangential polygons](wikipedia://en/35520654). Not every polygon with more than three sides is an inscribed polygon of a circle; those polygons that are so inscribed are called [cyclic polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_polygon).
- Every triangle can be inscribed in an ellipse, called its [Steiner circumellipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_circumellipse) or simply its Steiner ellipse, whose center is the triangle's [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926).
- Every triangle has an infinitude of inscribed [ellipses](wikipedia://en/9277). One of them is a circle, and one of them is the [Steiner inellipse](wikipedia://en/17185864) which is tangent to the triangle at the midpoints of the sides.
- Every acute triangle has [three inscribed squares](wikipedia://en/30654#Figures_inscribed_in_a_triangle). In a right triangle two of them are merged and coincide with each other, so there are only two distinct inscribed squares. An obtuse triangle has a single inscribed square, with one side coinciding with part of the triangle's longest side.
- A [Reuleaux triangle](wikipedia://en/580252), or more generally any [curve of constant width](wikipedia://en/580238), can be inscribed with any [orientation](wikipedia://en/2544098) inside a square of the appropriate size.
