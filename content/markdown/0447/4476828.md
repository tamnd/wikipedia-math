# Smith conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smith conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Smith conjecture** states that if *f* is a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792) of [finite order](wikipedia://en/493527), then the [fixed point set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_point_set) of *f* cannot be a nontrivial [knot](wikipedia://en/366808).

:::

(txt, remark after theorem 4) showed that a non-trivial [orientation-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation-preserving) diffeomorphism of finite order with fixed points must have a fixed point set equal to a circle, and asked in (Eilenberg 1949, Problem 36) if the fixed point set could be knotted. (txt) proved the Smith conjecture for the special case of diffeomorphisms of order 2 (and hence any even order). The proof of the general case was described by (txt) and depended on several major advances in [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) theory, In particular the work of [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190) on [hyperbolic structures](wikipedia://en/1237700) on 3-manifolds, and results by [William Meeks](wikipedia://en/57967381) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137) on [minimal surfaces](wikipedia://en/276734) in 3-manifolds, with some additional help from Bass, [Cameron Gordon](wikipedia://en/4695115), [Peter Shalen](wikipedia://en/3520910), and Rick Litherland.

(txt) gave an example of a continuous involution of the 3-sphere whose fixed point set is a wildly embedded circle, so the Smith conjecture is false in the topological (rather than the smooth or PL) category. (txt) showed that the analogue of the Smith conjecture in higher dimensions is false: the fixed point set of a periodic diffeomorphism of a sphere of dimension at least 4 can be a knotted sphere of codimension 2.
