# Siegel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegel"}

**Siegel** (also [Segal](wikipedia://en/2783440), [Segali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segali) or [Segel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segel)), is a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) and [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish) surname. Alternate spellings include Sigel, Sigl, Siegl, and others.

:::

It can be traced to 11th century [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria) and was used by people who made wax seals for or sealed official documents (each such male being described as a *Siegelbeamter*). "Siegel" is also the modern German word for [seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_%28emblem%29). The name ultimately derives from the Latin *sigillum*, meaning "seal". The Germanicized derivative of the name was given to professional seal makers and engravers.

Some researchers have attributed the surname to [Sigel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sowilo_rune), referring to [Sól (Sun)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sól_%28Sun%29), the goddess of the sun in Germanic mythology (Siȝel or sigel in Old English / Anglo-Saxon), but that is highly speculative.

## Variants, and false cognates {#Variants,_and_false_cognates}

Other variants may routinely include Siegelman, Siegle, Sigl, and [Sigel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigel_%28disambiguation%29#Surname). Presumably, some bearers of these names are lineal descendants of [ethnic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Jew) who changed the spellings of their surnames in the course of assimilating among other cultures: "[Segal](wikipedia://en/2783440)" and "[Segel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segel)" are [false cognates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_cognate) to Siegel, usually having derived from Latin America, where the double vowel did not translate into pronunciation.

Most sources indicate that it derives from the [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) acronym סגל the abbreviated form of either *Sagan Gadol ha-Leviya*, which means "great assistant to the [Levites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levites)", or *Sagan Gadol L'-Cohen* which means "great assistant to the [Cohanim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohen)" (special group of Levites who tended to more holy tasks within the [Jewish Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_in_Jerusalem)), and was an honorific title bestowed upon a member of the tribe of Levi who performed temple duties faithfully. (Some Rabbis aver that Segal/Segall derives from the Hebrew s'gula, meaning "treasure." However, this would not explain the association of the name only with the tribe of Levi.) Further, the double-L in Segall seems to be a specifically Romanian spelling variant.
