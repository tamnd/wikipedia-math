# Mathemagician

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathemagician"}

A **mathemagician** is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is also a [magician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magician_%28illusion%29). The term "mathemagic" is believed to have been introduced by [Royal Vale Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Vale_Heath) with his 1933 book "Mathemagic".[^o2]

:::

The name "mathemagician" was probably first applied to [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), but has since been used to describe many mathematician/magicians, including [Arthur T. Benjamin](wikipedia://en/12428320),[^o3] [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467),[^o4] and [Colm Mulcahy](wikipedia://en/51053993).[^o5] Diaconis has suggested that the reason so many mathematicians are magicians is that "inventing a magic trick and inventing a theorem are very similar activities."[^o6]

Mathemagician is a [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism), specifically a [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau), that combines mathematician and magician. A great number of self-working [mentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentalism) tricks rely on mathematical principles, such as [Gilbreath's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbreath's_principle). [Max Maven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Maven) often utilized this type of magic in his performance.

[The Mathemagician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Phantom_Tollbooth_characters#Minor_Characters) is the name of a character in the 1961 children's book *[The Phantom Tollbooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phantom_Tollbooth)*. He is the ruler of Digitopolis, the kingdom of mathematics.
