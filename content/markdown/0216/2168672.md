# Gertrude Mary Cox

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gertrude Mary Cox"}

**Gertrude Mary Cox** (January 13, 1900 – October 17, 1978) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685) and founder of the department of Experimental Statistics at [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University). She was later appointed director of both the Institute of Statistics of the [Consolidated University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) and the Statistics Research Division of North Carolina State University. Her most important and influential research dealt with [experimental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_design); In 1950 she published the book *Experimental Designs,* on the subject with [W. G. Cochran](wikipedia://en/2223833), which became the major reference work on the design of experiments for statisticians for years afterwards. In 1949 Cox became the first woman elected into the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute)[^o3] and in 1956 was [President of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gertrude Cox was born in [Dayton, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton,_Iowa) on January 13, 1900.[^o6] She studied at Perry High School in [Perry, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry,_Iowa), graduating in 1918. At this time she decided to become a deaconess in the [Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church) and worked towards that end.[^o7] However, in 1925, she decided to continue her education at [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_College) (which was renamed Iowa State University in 1959) in Ames where she studied mathematics and statistics and was awarded a B.S. in 1929 and a Master's degree in statistics in 1931.[^o7]

From 1931 to 1933 Cox undertook graduate studies in psychological statistics at the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley), then returned to Iowa State College to assist in establishing the new Statistical Laboratory.[^o8] Here she worked on the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541).

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1939 Cox was appointed assistant professor of statistics at [Iowa State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_College).[^o10] In 1940 Cox was appointed professor of statistics at North Carolina State College (now [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University)) at Raleigh. There she headed the new department of Experimental Statistics, the first female head of any department at this institution.[^o8] In 1945 she became director of the Institute of Statistics of the [Consolidated University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina), and the Statistics Research Division of the North Carolina State College which was run by [William Gemmell Cochran](wikipedia://en/2223833). In the same year of 1945 Cox became the editor of *Biometrics Bulletin* and of *[Biometrics](wikipedia://en/11885692)* and she held this editorship for 10 years. When prolific statistician and eugenicist [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) founded the [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society) in 1947, Cox was one of the founding members.[^o10]

In 1960 she took up her final post as Director of Statistics at the [Research Triangle Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Triangle_Institute) in Durham, North Carolina. She held this post until she retired in 1965.[^o10] After retirement, then worked as a consultant to promote the development of statistical programs in Egypt and Thailand.[^o7][^o11]

## Book {#Book}

In 1950 she published a joint work with William Cochran, *Experimental Designs*,[^o13] which became the major reference work on the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541) for statisticians for years afterwards.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Cox has received many honors. In 1949, she became the first woman elected into the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute). In 1956, she was elected President of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) while in 1975 she was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences). She was also a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).[^o15]

The [University of North Carolina system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) named her an O. Max Gardner Award recipient in 1959. [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) honored Cox by naming Cox Hall in her honor in 1970, and awarding her a Watauga Medal in 1977. Iowa State College in 1958 awarded her an honorary Doctorate of Science in statistics. The [Caucus for Women in Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucus_for_Women_in_Statistics) also established a Gertrude M. Cox Scholarship in 1989.[^o16]
