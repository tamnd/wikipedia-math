# Riemannian submanifold

*Image: Sphere filled blue.svg.* The [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) $S^n$ with the round metric is a Riemannian submanifold of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riemannian submanifold"}

A **Riemannian submanifold** $N$ of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $M$ is a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) $N$ of $M$ equipped with the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) inherited ([induced](wikipedia://en/1253303)) from $M$.

:::

Specifically, if $(M,g)$ is a Riemannian manifold (with or without boundary) and $i : N \to M$ is an [immersed submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immersed_submanifold) or an [embedded submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_submanifold) (with or without boundary), the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) $i^* g$ of $g$ is a Riemannian metric on $N$, and $(N, i^*g)$ is said to be a **Riemannian submanifold** of $(M,g)$. On the other hand, if $N$ already has a Riemannian metric $\tilde g$, then the immersion (or embedding) $i : N \to M$ is called an **[isometric immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_immersion)** (or **[isometric embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_embedding)**) if $\tilde g = i^* g$. Hence isometric immersions and isometric embeddings are Riemannian submanifolds.[^o2][^o3]

For example, the [n-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) $S^n = \{ x \in \mathbb R^{n+1} : \lVert x \rVert = 1 \}$ is an embedded Riemannian submanifold of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ via the inclusion map $S^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb R^{n+1}$ that takes a point in $S^n$ to the corresponding point in the superset $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. The induced metric on $S^n$ is called the round metric.
