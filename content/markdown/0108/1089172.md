# Chaplygin's equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chaplygin's equation"}

In [gas dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_dynamics), **Chaplygin's equation**, named after [Sergei Alekseevich Chaplygin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Alekseevich_Chaplygin) (1902), is a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) useful in the study of [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) [flow](wikipedia://en/2684988).[^o2] It is

:::

$$\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial \theta^2} + \frac{v^2}{1-v^2/c^2}\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial v^2}+v \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}=0.$$

Here, $c=c(v)$ is the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853), determined by the [equation of state](wikipedia://en/9908) of the fluid and [conservation of energy](wikipedia://en/67088). For polytropic gases, we have $c^2/(\gamma-1) = h_0- v^2/2$, where $\gamma$ is the specific heat ratio and $h_0$ is the [stagnation enthalpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagnation_enthalpy), in which case the Chaplygin's equation reduces to

$$\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial \theta^2} + v^2\frac{2h_0-v^2}{2h_0-(\gamma+1)v^2/(\gamma-1)}\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial v^2}+v \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}=0.$$

The Bernoulli equation (see the derivation below) states that maximum velocity occurs when specific enthalpy is at the smallest value possible; one can take the specific enthalpy to be zero corresponding to [absolute zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_zero) temperature as the reference value, in which case $2h_0$ is the maximum attainable velocity. The particular integrals of above equation can be expressed in terms of [hypergeometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_functions).[^o3][^o4]

## Derivation {#Derivation}

For two-dimensional [potential flow](wikipedia://en/58320), the continuity equation and the [Euler equations](wikipedia://en/396022) (in fact, the [compressible Bernoulli's equation](wikipedia://en/64219#Compressible_flow_equation) due to irrotationality) in Cartesian coordinates $(x,y)$ involving the variables fluid velocity $(v_x,v_y)$, [specific enthalpy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_enthalpy) $h$ and density $\rho$ are

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial }{\partial x}(\rho v_x) + \frac{\partial }{\partial y}(\rho v_y) &=0,\\ h + \frac{1}{2}v^2 &= h_o. \end{aligned}$$

with the [equation of state](wikipedia://en/9908) $\rho=\rho(s,h)$ acting as third equation. Here $h_o$ is the stagnation enthalpy, $v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2$ is the magnitude of the velocity vector and $s$ is the entropy. For [isentropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isentropic) flow, density can be expressed as a function only of enthalpy $\rho=\rho(h)$, which in turn using Bernoulli's equation can be written as $\rho=\rho(v)$.

Since the flow is irrotational, a velocity potential $\phi$ exists and its differential is simply $d\phi = v_x dx + v_y dy$. Instead of treating $v_x=v_x(x,y)$ and $v_y=v_y(x,y)$ as dependent variables, we use a coordinate transform such that $x=x(v_x,v_y)$ and $y=y(v_x,v_y)$ become new dependent variables. Similarly the [velocity potential](wikipedia://en/2448955) is replaced by a new function ([Legendre transformation](wikipedia://en/390273))[^o6]

$$\Phi = xv_x + yv_y - \phi$$

such then its differential is $d\Phi = xdv_x + y dv_y$, therefore

$$x = \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v_x}, \quad y = \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v_y}.$$

Introducing another coordinate transformation for the independent variables from $(v_x,v_y)$ to $(v,\theta)$ according to the relation $v_x = v\cos\theta$ and $v_y = v\sin\theta$, where $v$ is the magnitude of the velocity vector and $\theta$ is the angle that the velocity vector makes with the $v_x$-axis, the dependent variables become

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \cos\theta \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}-\frac{\sin\theta}{v}\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \theta},\\ y &= \sin\theta \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}+\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \theta},\\ \phi & = -\Phi + v\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}. \end{aligned}$$

The continuity equation in the new coordinates become

$$\frac{d(\rho v)}{dv} \left(\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v} + \frac{1}{v} \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial \theta^2}\right) + \rho v \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial v^2} =0.$$

For isentropic flow, $dh=\rho^{-1}c^2 d\rho$, where $c$ is the speed of sound. Using the Bernoulli's equation we find

$$\frac{d(\rho v)}{d v} = \rho \left(1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right)$$

where $c=c(v)$. Hence, we have

$$\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial \theta^2} + \frac{v^2}{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial v^2}+v \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial v}=0.$$
