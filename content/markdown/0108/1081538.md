# Complex polygon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex polygon"}

The term ***complex polygon*** can mean two different things:

:::

- In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a polygon in the [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_space) plane, which has two [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) dimensions.
- In [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics), a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) whose boundary is not [simple](wikipedia://en/1059530).

## Geometry {#Geometry}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a complex polygon is a polygon in the complex [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/20598932) plane, which has two [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) dimensions.[^o3]

A [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) may be represented in the form $(a + ib)$, where $a$ and $b$ are [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), and $i$ is the square root of $-1$. Multiples of $i$ such as $ib$ are called *[imaginary numbers](wikipedia://en/14554)*. A complex number lies in a [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) having one real and one imaginary dimension, which may be represented as an [Argand diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argand_diagram). So a single complex dimension comprises two spatial dimensions, but of different kinds - one real and the other imaginary.

The [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_space) plane comprises two such complex planes, which are [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) to each other. Thus it has two real dimensions and two imaginary dimensions.

A **complex polygon** is a (complex) two-dimensional (i.e. four spatial dimensions) analogue of a real polygon. As such it is an example of the more general [complex polytope](wikipedia://en/8581934) in any number of complex dimensions.

In a *real* plane, a visible figure can be constructed as the *real conjugate* of some complex polygon.

## Computer graphics {#Computer_graphics}

*Image: pentagram\_with\_vertices.svg.* A complex (self-intersecting) pentagon with vertices indicated

*Image: regular\_star\_polygons.svg.* All regular [star polygons](wikipedia://en/313303) (with fractional [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394)) are complex

In computer graphics, a complex polygon is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) which has a boundary comprising discrete circuits, such as a polygon with a hole in it.[^o5]

Self-intersecting polygons are also sometimes included among the complex polygons.[^o6] Vertices are only counted at the ends of edges, not where edges intersect in space.

A formula relating an integral over a bounded region to a closed [line integral](wikipedia://en/21245414) may still apply when the "inside-out" parts of the region are counted negatively.

Moving around the polygon, the total amount one "turns" at the vertices can be any integer times 360°, e.g. 720° for a [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) and 0° for an [angular "eight"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_rectangle).
