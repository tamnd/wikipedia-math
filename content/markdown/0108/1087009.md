# Space form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Space form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **space form** is a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) *M* of [constant](wikipedia://en/3638803) [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) *K*. The three most fundamental examples are [Euclidean *n*-space](wikipedia://en/9697), the [*n*-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39782), and [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742), although a space form need not be [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879).

:::

## Reduction to generalized crystallography {#Reduction_to_generalized_crystallography}

The [Killing–Hopf theorem](wikipedia://en/37711150) of Riemannian geometry states that the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of an *n*-dimensional space form $M^n$ with curvature $K = -1$ is isometric to $H^n$, [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742); with curvature $K = 0$ is isometric to $R^n$, [Euclidean *n*-space](wikipedia://en/9697); and with curvature $K = +1$ is isometric to $S^n$, the [*n*-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) of points distance 1 from the origin in $R^{n+1}$.

By rescaling the [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on $H^n$, we may create a space $M_K$ of constant curvature $K$ for any $K < 0$. Similarly, by rescaling the Riemannian metric on $S^n$, we may create a space $M_K$ of constant curvature $K$ for any $K > 0$. Thus the universal cover of a space form $M$ with constant curvature $K$ is isometric to $M_K$.

This reduces the problem of studying space forms to studying [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) $\Gamma$ of $M_K$ which act [properly discontinuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous). Note that the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of $M$, $\pi_1(M)$, will be isomorphic to $\Gamma$. Groups acting in this manner on $R^n$ are called [crystallographic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_group). Groups acting in this manner on $H^2$ and $H^3$ are called [Fuchsian groups](wikipedia://en/471365) and [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652), respectively.
