# Chisini mean

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chisini mean"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a function *f* of *n* variables *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub> leads to a **Chisini mean** *M* if, for every vector ⟨*x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>⟩, there exists a unique *M* such that

:::

*f*(*M*,*M*, ..., *M*) = *f*(*x*<sub>1</sub>,*x*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>).

The [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/612), [harmonic](wikipedia://en/14463), [geometric](wikipedia://en/13046), [generalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalised_mean), [Heronian](wikipedia://en/5747450) and [quadratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_mean) means are all Chisini means, as are their weighted variants.

While [Oscar Chisini](wikipedia://en/1081345) was arguably the first to deal with "substitution means" in some depth in 1929, the idea of defining a mean as above is quite old, appearing (for example) in early works of [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289).[^o2]
