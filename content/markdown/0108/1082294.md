# Compound Poisson process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compound Poisson process"}

A **compound Poisson process** is a continuous-time [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with jumps. The jumps arrive randomly according to a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) and the size of the jumps is also random, with a specified probability distribution. To be precise, a compound Poisson process, parameterised by a rate $\lambda > 0$ and jump size distribution *G*, is a process $\{\,Y(t) : t \geq 0 \,\}$ given by

:::

$$Y(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N(t)} D_i$$

where, $\{\,N(t) : t \geq 0\,\}$ is the counting variable of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with rate $\lambda$, and $\{\,D_i : i \geq 1\,\}$ are independent and identically distributed random variables, with distribution function *G*, which are also independent of $\{\,N(t) : t \geq 0\,\}.\,$[^o2]

When $D_i$ are non-negative integer-valued random variables, then this compound Poisson process is known as a **stuttering Poisson process.**

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties of the compound Poisson process"}

## Properties of the compound Poisson process {#Properties_of_the_compound_Poisson_process}

The [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of a compound Poisson process can be calculated using a result known as [Wald's equation](wikipedia://en/1512013) as:

$$\operatorname E(Y(t)) = \operatorname E(D_1 + \cdots + D_{N(t)}) = \operatorname E(N(t))\operatorname E(D_1) = \operatorname E(N(t)) \operatorname E(D) = \lambda t \operatorname E(D).$$

Making similar use of the [law of total variance](wikipedia://en/312408), the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) can be calculated as:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{var}(Y(t)) &= \operatorname E(\operatorname{var}(Y(t)\mid N(t))) + \operatorname{var}(\operatorname E(Y(t)\mid N(t))) \\[5pt] &= \operatorname E(N(t)\operatorname{var}(D)) + \operatorname{var}(N(t) \operatorname E(D)) \\[5pt] &= \operatorname{var}(D) \operatorname E(N(t)) + \operatorname E(D)^2 \operatorname{var}(N(t)) \\[5pt] &= \operatorname{var}(D)\lambda t + \operatorname E(D)^2\lambda t \\[5pt] &= \lambda t(\operatorname{var}(D) + \operatorname E(D)^2) \\[5pt] &= \lambda t \operatorname E(D^2). \end{aligned}$$

Lastly, using the [law of total probability](wikipedia://en/312383), the [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) can be given as follows:

$$\Pr(Y(t)=i) = \sum_n \Pr(Y(t)=i\mid N(t)=n)\Pr(N(t)=n)$$

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname E(e^{sY}) & = \sum_i e^{si} \Pr(Y(t)=i) \\[5pt] & = \sum_i e^{si} \sum_{n} \Pr(Y(t)=i\mid N(t)=n)\Pr(N(t)=n) \\[5pt] & = \sum_n \Pr(N(t)=n) \sum_i e^{si} \Pr(Y(t)=i\mid N(t)=n) \\[5pt] & = \sum_n \Pr(N(t)=n) \sum_i e^{si}\Pr(D_1 + D_2 + \cdots + D_n=i) \\[5pt] & = \sum_n \Pr(N(t)=n) M_D(s)^n \\[5pt] & = \sum_n \Pr(N(t)=n) e^{n\ln(M_D(s))} \\[5pt] & = M_{N(t)}(\ln(M_D(s))) \\[5pt] & = e^{\lambda t \left( M_D(s) - 1 \right) }. \end{aligned}$$

:::

## Exponentiation of measures {#Exponentiation_of_measures}

Let *N*, *Y*, and *D* be as above. Let *μ* be the probability measure according to which *D* is distributed, i.e.

$$\mu(A) = \Pr(D \in A).\,$$

Let *δ*<sub>0</sub> be the trivial probability distribution putting all of the mass at zero. Then the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of *Y*(*t*) is the measure

$$\exp(\lambda t(\mu - \delta_0))\,$$

where the exponential exp(*ν*) of a finite measure *ν* on [Borel subsets](wikipedia://en/43327) of the [real line](wikipedia://en/20646438) is defined by

$$\exp(\nu) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty {\nu^{*n} \over n!}$$

and

$$\nu^{*n} = \underbrace{\nu * \cdots *\nu}_{n \text{ factors}}$$

is a [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) of measures, and the series converges [weakly](wikipedia://en/50723).
