# Identity component

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Identity component"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **identity component** of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* (also known as its **unity component**) refers to several closely related notions of the largest [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) subgroup of *G* containing the identity element.

:::

In [point set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_set_topology), the **identity component of a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315)** *G* is the [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28topology%29) *G*<sup>0</sup> of *G* that contains the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of the group. The **identity path component of a topological group** *G* is the [path component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_component) of *G* that contains the identity element of the group.

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **identity component of an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924)** *G* over a field *k* is the identity component of the underlying topological space. The **identity component of a [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523)** *G* over a base [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) *S* is, roughly speaking, the group scheme *G*<sup>0</sup> whose [fiber](wikipedia://en/6603892) over the point *s* of *S* is the connected component *G*<sub>*s*</sub><sup>0</sup> of the fiber *G<sub>s</sub>*, an algebraic group.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The identity component *G*<sup>0</sup> of a topological or algebraic group *G* is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G*. It is closed since components are always closed. It is a subgroup since multiplication and inversion in a topological or algebraic group are [continuous maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map_%28topology%29) by definition. Moreover, for any continuous [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) *a* of *G* we have

*a*(*G*<sup>0</sup>) = *G*<sup>0</sup>.

Thus, *G*<sup>0</sup> is a [characteristic](wikipedia://en/7198) (topological or algebraic) subgroup of *G*, so it is normal.

By the same argument as above, the identity path component of a topological group is also a normal subgroup (characteristic as a topological subgroup). It may in general be smaller than the identity component (since path connectedness is a stronger condition than connectedness), but these agree if *G* is locally path-connected.

The identity component *G*<sup>0</sup> of a topological group *G* need not be [open](wikipedia://en/39358) in *G*. In fact, we may have *G*<sup>0</sup> = {*e*}, in which case *G* is [totally disconnected](wikipedia://en/4947490). However, the identity component of a [locally path-connected space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_path-connected_space) (for instance a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945)) is always open, since it contains a [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected) neighbourhood of {*e*}; and therefore is a [clopen set](wikipedia://en/233398).

:::

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Component group"}

## Component group {#Component_group}

The [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) *G*/*G*<sup>0</sup> is called the **group of components** or **component group** of *G*. Its elements are just the connected components of *G*. The component group *G*/*G*<sup>0</sup> is a [discrete group](wikipedia://en/468536) if and only if *G*<sup>0</sup> is open. If *G* is an algebraic group of [finite type](wikipedia://en/35457783), such as an [affine algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_group), then *G*/*G*<sup>0</sup> is actually a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707).

One may similarly define the path component group as the group of path components (quotient of *G* by the identity path component), and in general the component group is a quotient of the path component group, but if *G* is locally path connected these groups agree. The path component group can also be characterized as the zeroth [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506), $\pi_0(G,e).$

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- The group of non-zero real numbers with multiplication (**R**\*,•) has two components and the group of components is ({1,−1},•).
- Consider the [group of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_units) *U* in the ring of [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559). In the ordinary topology of the plane {*z* = *x* + j *y* : *x*, *y* ∈ **R**}, *U* is divided into four components by the lines *y* = *x* and *y* = − *x* where *z* has no inverse. Then *U*<sup>0</sup> = { *z* : |*y*| \< *x* } . In this case the group of components of *U* is isomorphic to the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928).
- The identity component of the additive group (**Z**<sub>p</sub>,+) of [p-adic integers](wikipedia://en/51423) is the singleton set {0}, since **Z**<sub>p</sub> is totally disconnected.
- The [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of a [reductive algebraic group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* is the components group of the [normalizer group](wikipedia://en/145661) of a [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) of *G*.
- Consider the group scheme μ<sub>*2*</sub> = Spec(**Z**\[*x*\]/(*x*<sup>2</sup> - 1)) of second [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) defined over the base scheme Spec(**Z**). Topologically, μ<sub>*n*</sub> consists of two copies of the curve Spec(**Z**) glued together at the point (that is, [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928)) 2. Therefore, μ<sub>*n*</sub> is connected as a topological space, hence as a scheme. However, μ<sub>*2*</sub> does not equal its identity component because the fiber over every point of Spec(**Z**) except 2 consists of two discrete points.

An algebraic group *G* over a [topological field](wikipedia://en/60097) *K* admits two natural topologies, the [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744) and the topology inherited from *K*. The identity component of *G* often changes depending on the topology. For instance, the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**R**) is connected as an algebraic group but has two path components as a Lie group, the matrices of positive determinant and the matrices of negative determinant. Any connected algebraic group over a non-Archimedean [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *K* is totally disconnected in the *K*-topology and thus has trivial identity component in that topology.
