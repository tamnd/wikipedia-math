# Hájek–Le Cam convolution theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hájek–Le Cam convolution theorem"}

article In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Hájek–Le Cam convolution theorem** states that any [regular estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_estimator) in a [parametric model](wikipedia://en/2883137) is asymptotically equivalent to a sum of two [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) random variables, one of which is [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) with asymptotic variance equal to the inverse of [Fisher information](wikipedia://en/598971), and the other having arbitrary distribution.

:::

The obvious corollary from this theorem is that the "best" among regular estimators are those with the second component identically equal to zero. Such estimators are called **efficient** and are known to always exist for [regular parametric models](wikipedia://en/2883137#Regular_parametric_model).

The theorem is named after [Jaroslav Hájek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Hájek) and [Lucien Le Cam](wikipedia://en/2629104).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let ℘ = {*P<sub>θ</sub>* | *θ* ∈ Θ ⊂ ℝ<sup>*k*</sup>} be a [regular parametric model](wikipedia://en/2883137#Regular_parametric_model), and *q*(*θ*): Θ → ℝ<sup>*m*</sup> be a parameter in this model (typically a parameter is just one of the components of vector *θ*). Assume that function *q* is differentiable on Θ, with the *m × k* matrix of derivatives denoted as *q̇<sub>θ</sub>*. Define

$I_{q(\theta)}^{-1} = \dot{q}(\theta)I^{-1}(\theta)\dot{q}(\theta)'$ — the *information bound* for *q*,

$\psi_{q(\theta)} = \dot{q}(\theta)I^{-1}(\theta)\dot\ell(\theta)$ — the *efficient influence function* for *q*,

where *I*(*θ*) is the [Fisher information](wikipedia://en/598971) matrix for model ℘, $\scriptstyle\dot\ell(\theta)$ is the [score function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Score_%28statistics%29), and ′ denotes [matrix transpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_transpose).

**Theorem** (Bickel 1998, Th.2.3.1). Suppose *T<sub>n</sub>* is a uniformly (locally) [regular estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_estimator) of the parameter *q*. Then

There exist independent random *m*-vectors $\scriptstyle Z_\theta\,\sim\,\mathcal{N}(0,\,I^{-1}_{q(\theta)})$ and *Δ<sub>θ</sub>* such that

$\sqrt{n}(T_n - q(\theta)) \ \xrightarrow{d}\ Z_\theta + \Delta_\theta,$ where <sup>*d*</sup> denotes [convergence in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution). More specifically,

$\begin{pmatrix} \sqrt{n}(T_n - q(\theta)) - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi_{q(\theta)}(x_i) \\ \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n \psi_{q(\theta)}(x_i) \end{pmatrix} \ \xrightarrow{d}\ \begin{pmatrix} \Delta_\theta \\ Z_\theta \end{pmatrix}.$ If the map *θ* → *q̇<sub>θ</sub>* is continuous, then the convergence in (A) holds uniformly on compact subsets of Θ. Moreover, in that case Δ<sub>*θ*</sub> = 0 for all *θ* if and only if *T<sub>n</sub>* is uniformly (locally) asymptotically linear with influence function *ψ*<sub>*q*(*θ*)</sub>

:::
