# Fieller's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fieller's theorem"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Fieller's theorem** allows the calculation of a [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911) for the ratio of two [means](wikipedia://en/612).

:::

## Approximate confidence interval {#Approximate_confidence_interval}

Variables *a* and *b* may be measured in different units, so there is no way to directly combine the [standard errors](wikipedia://en/552520) as they may also be in different units. The most complete discussion of this is given by Fieller (1954).[^o3]

Fieller showed that if *a* and *b* are (possibly [correlated](wikipedia://en/157057)) [means of two samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_mean) with [expectations](wikipedia://en/9653) $\mu_a$ and $\mu_b$, and variances $\nu_{11}\sigma^2$ and $\nu_{22}\sigma^2$ and covariance $\nu_{12}\sigma^2$, and if $\nu_{11}, \nu_{12}, \nu_{22}$ are all known, then a (1 − *α*) confidence interval (*m*<sub>L</sub>, *m*<sub>U</sub>) for $\mu_a/\mu_b$ is given by

$$(m_L, m_{U}) = \frac{1}{(1-g)} \left[\frac{a}{b} - \frac{g\nu_{12}}{\nu_{22}} \mp \frac{t_{r,\alpha}s}{b} \sqrt{\nu_{11} - 2\frac{a}{b}\nu_{12} + \frac{a^2}{b^2} \nu_{22} - g\left(\nu_{11} - \frac{\nu_{12}^2}{\nu_{22}}\right)} \right]$$

where

$$g=\frac{t^{2}_{r,\alpha}s^2\nu_{22}}{b^2}.$$

Here $s^2$ is an [unbiased estimator](wikipedia://en/8450479) of $\sigma^2$ based on r degrees of freedom, and $t_{r,\alpha}$ is the $\alpha$-level deviate from the [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375) based on *r* degrees of freedom.

Three features of this formula are important in this context:

a) The expression inside the square root has to be positive, or else the resulting interval will be imaginary.

b) When *g* is very close to 1, the confidence interval is infinite.

c) When *g* is greater than 1, the overall divisor outside the square brackets is negative and the confidence interval is exclusive.

## Other methods {#Other_methods}

One problem is that, when *g* is not small, the confidence interval can blow up when using Fieller's theorem. Andy Grieve has provided a Bayesian solution where the CIs are still sensible, albeit wide.[^o5] [Bootstrapping](wikipedia://en/6885770) provides another alternative that does not require the assumption of normality.[^o6]

## History {#History}

Edgar C. Fieller (1907–1960) first started working on this problem while in [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695)'s group at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), where he was employed for five years after graduating in Mathematics from [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge). He then worked for the [Boots Pure Drug Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boots_UK) as a statistician and [operational researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_research) before becoming deputy head of operational research at [RAF Fighter Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fighter_Command) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), after which he was appointed the first head of the Statistics Section at the [National Physical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Physical_Laboratory_%28United_Kingdom%29).[^o8]
