# Brocard points

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brocard points"}

*Image: Brocard point.svg.* The Brocard point of a triangle, constructed at the intersection point of three circles

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Brocard points** are special points within a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). They are named after [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496) (1845–1922), a French mathematician.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In a triangle $\triangle ABC$ with sides $a, b, c$, where the vertices are labeled $A, B, C$ in counterclockwise order, there is exactly one point $P$ such that the line segments $\overline{AP}, \overline{BP}, \overline{CP}$ form the same angle, $\omega$, with the respective sides $c, a, b$, namely that

$$\angle PAB = \angle PBC = \angle PCA =\omega.\,$$

Point $P$ is called the **first Brocard point** of the triangle $\triangle ABC$, and the angle $\omega$ is called the **Brocard angle** of the triangle. This angle has the property that

$$\cot\omega = \cot\!\bigl(\angle CAB\bigr) + \cot\!\bigl(\angle ABC\bigr) + \cot\!\bigl(\angle BCA\bigr).$$

There is also a **second Brocard point**, $Q$, in triangle $\triangle ABC$ such that line segments $\overline{AQ}, \overline{BQ}, \overline{CQ}$ form equal angles with sides $b, c, a$ respectively. In other words, the equations

$$\angle QCB = \angle QBA = \angle QAC$$

apply. Remarkably, this second Brocard point has the same Brocard angle as the first Brocard point. In other words, angle

$$\angle PBC = \angle PCA = \angle PAB$$

is the same as

$$\angle QCB = \angle QBA = \angle QAC.$$

The two Brocard points are closely related to one another; in fact, the difference between the first and the second depends on the order in which the angles of triangle $\triangle ABC$ are taken. So for example, the first Brocard point of $\triangle ABC$ is the same as the second Brocard point of $\triangle ACB$.

The two Brocard points of a triangle $\triangle ABC$ are [isogonal conjugates](wikipedia://en/2083100) of each other.

:::

## Construction {#Construction}

The most elegant construction of the Brocard points goes as follows. In the following example the first Brocard point is presented, but the construction for the second Brocard point is very similar.

As in the diagram above, form a circle through points $A$ and $B$, tangent to edge $\overline{BC}$ of the triangle (the center of this circle is at the point where the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{AB}$ meets the line through point $B$ that is perpendicular to $\overline{BC}$). Symmetrically, form a circle through points $B$ and $C$, tangent to edge $\overline{AC}$, and a circle through points $A$ and $C$, tangent to edge $\overline{AB}$. These three circles have a common point, the first Brocard point of $\triangle ABC$. See also [Tangent lines to circles](wikipedia://en/19491492).

The three circles just constructed are also designated as **epicycles** of $\triangle ABC$. The second Brocard point is constructed in similar fashion.

:::

## Trilinears and barycentrics of the first two Brocard points {#Trilinears_and_barycentrics_of_the_first_two_Brocard_points}

Homogeneous [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) for the first and second Brocard points are:

$$\begin{array}{rccccc} P= & \frac{c}{b} &:& \frac{a}{c} &:& \frac{b}{a} \\ Q= & \frac{b}{c} &:& \frac{c}{a} &:& \frac{a}{b} \end{array}$$

Thus their [barycentric coordinates](wikipedia://en/762954) are:[^o7]

$$\begin{array}{rccccc} P = & c^2a^2 &:& a^2b^2 &:& b^2c^2 \\ Q = & a^2b^2 &:& b^2c^2 &:& c^2a^2 \end{array}$$

## The segment between the first two Brocard points {#The_segment_between_the_first_two_Brocard_points}

The Brocard points are an example of a bicentric pair of points, but they are not [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488) because neither Brocard point is invariant under [similarity transformations](wikipedia://en/39377): reflecting a scalene triangle, a special case of a similarity, turns one Brocard point into the other. However, the [unordered pair](wikipedia://en/1112432) formed by both points is invariant under similarities. The midpoint of the two Brocard points, called the **Brocard midpoint**, has [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790)[^o9]

$$\sin(A +\omega ) : \sin(B+\omega) : \sin(C+\omega)=a(b^2+c^2):b(c^2+a^2):c(a^2+b^2),$$

and is a triangle center; it is center X(39) in the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593). The **third Brocard point**, given in trilinear coordinates as[^o10]

$$\csc (A-\omega ) : \csc(B-\omega):\csc(C-\omega)=a^{-3}:b^{-3}:c^{-3},$$

is the Brocard midpoint of the [anticomplementary triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticomplementary_triangle). It is center X(76) in the [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593). Further:

- The third Brocard point is the [isotomic conjugate](wikipedia://en/9492657) of the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point).
- The third Brocard point is the [perspector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspector) of the [Brocard triangle](wikipedia://en/14884335) and $\triangle ABC$.
- The third Brocard point lies on the diameter of the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) joining the [Steiner point](wikipedia://en/35857329) and the [Tarry point](wikipedia://en/14662924).

The distance between the first two Brocard points $P$ and $Q$ is always less than or equal to half the radius $R$ of the triangle's circumcircle:[^o7][^o11]

$$\overline{PQ} = 2R\sin \omega \sqrt{1-4\sin ^2\omega} \le \frac{R}{2}.$$

The segment between the first two Brocard points is [perpendicularly bisected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpendicular_bisector) at the Brocard midpoint by the line connecting the triangle's [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and its symmedian point. Moreover, the circumcenter, the Lemoine point, and the first two Brocard points are [concyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concyclic)—they all fall on the **[Brocard circle](wikipedia://en/14663012)**, of which the segment connecting the circumcenter and the Lemoine point is a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007).[^o7] The tangents to the Brocard circle at the first two Brocard points concur at the isogonal conjugate of the third Brocard point.[^o9][^o10]

## Distance from circumcenter {#Distance_from_circumcenter}

The Brocard points $P$ and $Q$ are equidistant from the triangle's [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) $O$:[^o11]

$$\overline{PO} = \overline{QO} = R\sqrt{\frac{a^4+b^4+c^4}{a^2b^2+b^2c^2+c^2a^2}-1} = R\sqrt{1-4\sin^2 \omega }.$$

## Similarities and congruences {#Similarities_and_congruences}

The [pedal triangles](wikipedia://en/152983) of the first and second Brocard points are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to each other and [similar](wikipedia://en/39377) to the original triangle.[^o11]

If the lines $AP, BP, CP$, each through one of a triangle's vertices and its first Brocard point, intersect the triangle's [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) at points $L, M, N$, then the triangle $\triangle LMN$ is congruent with the original triangle $\triangle ABC$. The same is true if the first Brocard point $P$ is replaced by the second Brocard point $Q$.[^o11]
