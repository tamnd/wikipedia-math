# Klara Löbenstein

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Klara Löbenstein"}

**Klara Löbenstein** (15 February 1883 – 10 June 1968) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). She was among the first women to obtain a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). Her doctoral work was on the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of [algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curves).[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Löbenstein was born in [Hildesheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildesheim), [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) on 15 February 1883 to merchant Lehmann Löbenstein and his wife Sofie (née Schönfeld).[^o7]

In 1904, Löbenstein was given permission to take her *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)* at the Realgymnasium I *[Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29)* in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover).[^o7] Thus she belonged to a small group of talented young women in Germany at the beginning of the 20th century who were allowed to take the *Abitur* externally at boys' schools.

Since [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) began to allow women to formally attend university only from the winter semester of 1908–09, Löbenstein and her friend [Margarete Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarete_Kahn) first attended the universities of [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) as guest students. They studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [propaedeutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaedeutics) at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). Löbenstein's field of expertise was [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Together with Kahn she made a contribution to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750).[^o5] Hilbert's sixteenth problem concerned the topology of [algebraic curves in the complex projective plane](wikipedia://en/253260#Complex_curves_and_realsurfaces); as a difficult special case in his formulation of the problem Hilbert proposed that there are no [algebraic curves of degree 6](wikipedia://en/468884#Degree_6) consisting of 11 separate ovals. Löbenstein and Kahn developed methods to address this problem.

Löbenstein obtained a doctorate in 1910 under [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) in Göttingen, with a dissertation titled *Über den Satz, daß eine ebene, algebraische Kurve 6. Ordnung mit 11 sich einander ausschließenden Ovalen nicht existiert* \[On the proposition that no plane algebraic curve of degree 6 with 11 mutually exclusive ovals exists\], and was therefore one of the first German women to obtain a doctorate in mathematics (the mathematics division was part of the faculty of philosophy then). She took her oral examination – again, along with Kahn – on 30 June 1909. Afterwards Löbenstein worked as a high school teacher in [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz) and [Landsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorzów_Wielkopolski). She was dismissed on 1 January 1936 due to the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) [racial laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws).[^o7] In 1941 she emigrated to [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina). She died on 10 June 1968 in [Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires), where she rests in the [Cementerio alemán](http://cementerioaleman.org.ar).
