# Nancy Crooker

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nancy Crooker"}

**Nancy U. Crooker** (born April 1, 1944) is an American physicist and professor emerita of [space physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_physics) at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University), Massachusetts.[^o3] She has made major contributions to the understanding of [geomagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomagnetism) in the Earth's [magnetosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_magnetosphere) and the [heliosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliosphere), particularly through the study of interplanetary [electrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron) and [magnetic reconnection](wikipedia://en/1166647).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Crooker was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago) in 1944. Her father, Michael Uss, a Lithuanian who emigrated to America as a child, was a foreman at the freight yards of the Chicago & Northwestern Railroad, and her mother, Helen Narovec, was a housewife.

Crooker holds a BA in physics from [Knox College, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_College_%28Illinois%29), and an MSc in [Meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904) from the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA).[^o3][^o5] In 1972 Crooker was awarded her PhD in [Atmospheric Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_science), also from UCLA, with her doctoral dissertation entitled, "*The Low-Latitude Asymmetric Disturbance in the Geomagnetic Field*".[^o6]

## Research career {#Research_career}

Crooker has published 207 peer-reviewed articles (as of 8 October 2019)[^o8] across a range of topics within space physics. Her early career was as a postdoctoral researcher at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and then the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in the 1970s. There, together with [Joan Feynman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Feynman) in their seminal [Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29) paper,[^o9] she was one of the first physicists to use geomagnetic data as a way to reconstruct solar activity prior to the space age.[^o10] Crooker then developed the concept of anti-parallel merging of magnetic field lines in Earth's magnetosphere published in *[Journal of Geophysical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Geophysical_Research)* in 1979.[^o11]

In 1990, she returned to UCLA as an adjunct professor before making her final move to Boston University as a research professor in 1994.[^o5] Around this time, Crooker switched focus from the magnetosphere to the heliosphere, in particular the interplanetary manifestations of [coronal mass ejections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronal_mass_ejections). In 1997, she co-edited a monograph on [coronal mass ejections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronal_mass_ejections).[^o12] In 2002, she coined the term "interchange reconnection" for describing the dynamic process by which [heliospheric magnetic flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interplanetary_magnetic_field) introduced by coronal mass ejections is subsequently removed,[^o13][^o14] a term which has been comprehensively adopted in the field.

Crooker was president of the [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union) (AGU) Space Physics & Aeronomy Section from 2004 to 2006.[^o15][^o16] She is a [fellow of the American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Geophysical_Union),[^o17] where the fellowship program recognizes AGU members who have made exceptional contributions to Earth and space science through a breakthrough, discovery, or innovation in their field.[^o18] She also received the [Eugene Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Parker) Lecture award from the AGU in 2013, only the third woman to do so.[^o19]

She has worked closely over decades with several other prominent space physicists, including [John T. Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_T._Gosling), [Marcia Neugebauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_Neugebauer), [Mike Lockwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lockwood_%28physicist%29), [Chris Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_T._Russell) and [Thomas Zurbuchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Zurbuchen).[^o5]

## Scientific Citizenship {#Scientific_Citizenship}

Crooker has served on numerous committees, panels and taskforces throughout her career, including:

- AGU Fellows Program Review Task Force (2015)[^o21][^o22]
- Founding member of the new executive board for the American Geophysical Union (2010)[^o23]
- Media panel member for the [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA) [Ulysses spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_%28spacecraft%29) media telecon (2008)[^o24]
- Working group leader, [International Space Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Science_Institute) (ISSI) workshop on Co-rotating Interaction Regions (1998)[^o25]
- Interviewed by [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN) about [solar storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronal_mass_ejection) (1997)[^o26]
- Chair, AGU Awards Committee for Solar-Planetary Relations Section (1988-1990)[^o27]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- The Eugene Parker Lecture is presented two out of every three years to a space scientist who has made significant contributions to the fields of solar and heliospheric science by the American Geophysical Union.[^o19] Crooker received this honor in 2013.[^o19]
- President of the AGU Space Physics & Aeronomy Section (2004 to 2006) [^o16]
- Member of the Solar Heliospheric and Interplanetary Environment (SHINE) Steering Committee (1995-2002)[^o29]
- Solar Heliospheric Secretary for Space Physics & Aeronomy Section of AGU (2000-2002)
- Fellow of the American Geophysical Union (2000) [^o30][^o17]
- Member of the NASA Magnetospheric Management Operations Working Group (1995-6)[^o31]
- Editor's Citation for Excellence in Refereeing for the *[Journal of Geophysical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Geophysical_Research)* (1993)[^o32] and [Geophysical Research Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysical_Research_Letters) (1996) [^o33]
