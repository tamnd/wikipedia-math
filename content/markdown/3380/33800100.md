# Hilbert–Burch theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Burch theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hilbert–Burch theorem** describes the structure of some [free resolutions](wikipedia://en/19745659) of a [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) of a [local](wikipedia://en/153391) or [graded](wikipedia://en/103094) [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) in the case that the quotient has [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension) 2. (txt) proved a version of this theorem for [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065), and (txt, p. 944) proved a more general version. Several other authors later rediscovered and published variations of this theorem. (Eisenbud 1995, theorem 20.15) gives a statement and proof.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If *R* is a local ring with an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* and

$$0 \rightarrow R^m\stackrel{f}{\rightarrow} R^n \rightarrow R \rightarrow R/I\rightarrow 0$$

is a free resolution of the *R*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) *R*/*I*, then *m* = *n* – 1 and the ideal *I* is *aJ* where *a* is a [regular](wikipedia://en/51441) element of *R* and *J*, a depth-2 ideal, is the first [Fitting ideal](wikipedia://en/28034758) $\operatorname{Fitt}_1 I$ of *I*, i.e., the ideal generated by the [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468) of the minors of size *m* of the matrix of *f*.

:::
