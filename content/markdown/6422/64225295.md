# Linda Zhao

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Linda Zhao"}

**Linda Hong Zhao** is a Chinese-American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). She is a Professor of Statistics and at the [Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania). She is a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics). Zhao specializes in modern [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) methods.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

In 1982, Zhao obtained her [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) from the Department of Mathematics at [Nankai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nankai_University). She later emigrated to the United States and attended [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), where she obtained her [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the Department of Statistics in 1993.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Zhao became an assistant professor statistics at [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1993,[^o6] before joining the [Wharton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharton_School_of_the_University_of_Pennsylvania) in 1994, where she is currently a Professor of Statistics.[^o7]

Her specialty falls in modern [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) methods, replicability in science, high dimensional data, housing price prediction, and [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/404412) methods.[^o6] Current projects include equity ownership network, and its relationship to firm performance and innovation activities; identify signals from noisy data using non-parametric Bayesian scheme; and model-free data analysis.[^o8] Her work has won [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) support for over 20 years.[^o9]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Zhao was married to [Lawrence D. Brown](wikipedia://en/7886903) (1940–2018), a fellow statistician at the Wharton School.[^o11][^o12]
