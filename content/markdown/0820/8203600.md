# Cotlar–Stein lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cotlar–Stein lemma"}

The **Cotlar–Stein almost orthogonality lemma** is a [mathematical lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_lemma) in the field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). It may be used to obtain information on the [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722) on an [operator](wikipedia://en/22213), acting from one [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) into another, when the operator can be decomposed into *almost orthogonal* pieces.

:::

The original version of this lemma (for [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) and mutually [commuting](wikipedia://en/294390) operators) was proved by [Mischa Cotlar](wikipedia://en/35234149) in 1955[^o2] and allowed him to conclude that the [Hilbert transform](wikipedia://en/574024) is a [continuous linear operator](wikipedia://en/1802169) in [$L^2$](wikipedia://en/28680558) without using the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247). A more general version was proved by [Elias Stein](wikipedia://en/418073).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

## Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

Let $E,\,F$ be two [Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_spaces). Consider a family of operators $T_j$, $j\geq 1$, with each $T_j$ a [bounded linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) from $E$ to $F$.

Denote

$$a_{jk}=\Vert T_j T_k^\ast\Vert, \qquad b_{jk}=\Vert T_j^\ast T_k\Vert.$$

The family of operators $T_j:\;E\to F$, $j\ge 1,$ is *almost orthogonal* if

$$A=\sup_{j}\sum_{k}\sqrt{a_{jk}}<\infty, \qquad B=\sup_{j}\sum_{k}\sqrt{b_{jk}}<\infty.$$

The Cotlar–Stein lemma states that if $T_j$ are almost orthogonal, then the series $\sum_{j}T_j$ converges in the [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699), and

$$\Vert \sum_{j}T_j\Vert \le\sqrt{AB}.$$

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

If $T_1,\ldots,T_n$ is a finite collection of bounded operators, then[^o8] (as will be proven below)

$$\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j} |(T_i v,T_jv)| \le \left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_i^*T_j\|^{1\over 2}\right)\left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_iT_j^*\|^{1\over 2}\right)\|v\|^2.}$$

So under the hypotheses of the lemma,

$$\displaystyle{\sum_{i,j} |(T_i v,T_jv)| \le AB\|v\|^2.}$$

It follows that

$$\displaystyle{\|\sum_{i=1}^n T_iv\|^2 \le AB \|v\|^2,}$$

and that

$$\displaystyle{\|\sum_{i=m}^n T_iv\|^2 \le \sum_{i,j\ge m} |(T_iv,T_jv)|.}$$

Hence, the partial sums

$$\displaystyle{s_n=\sum_{i=1}^n T_iv}$$

form a [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085).

The sum is therefore [absolutely convergent](wikipedia://en/61532) with the limit satisfying the stated inequality.

To prove the inequality above set

$$\displaystyle{R=\sum a_{ij}T_i^*T_j}$$

with |*a*<sub>*ij*</sub>| ≤ 1 chosen so that

$$\displaystyle{(Rv,v)=|(Rv,v)|=\sum |(T_iv,T_jv)|.}$$

Then

$$\displaystyle{\|R\|^{2m} =\|(R^*R)^m\|\le \sum \|T_{i_1}^* T_{i_2} T_{i_3}^* T_{i_4} \cdots T_{i_{4m}}\| \le \sum \left(\|T_{i_1}^*\|\|T_{i_1}^*T_{i_2}\|\|T_{i_2}T_{i_3}^*\|\cdots \|T_{i_{4m-1}}^* T_{i_{4m}}\|\|T_{i_{4m}}\|\right)^{1\over 2}.}$$

Hence

$$\displaystyle{\|R\|^{2m} \le n \cdot \max \|T_i\| \left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_i^*T_j\|^{1\over 2}\right)^{2m}\left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_iT_j^*\|^{1\over 2}\right)^{2m-1}.}$$

Taking 2*m*th roots and letting *m* tend to ∞,

$$\displaystyle{\|R\|\le \left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_i^*T_j\|^{1\over 2}\right)\left(\max_i \sum_j \|T_iT_j^*\|^{1\over 2}\right),}$$

which immediately implies the inequality.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

The Cotlar-Stein lemma has been generalized, with sums being replaced by integrals.[^o11][^o12] Let *X* be a [locally compact space](wikipedia://en/48632) and μ a [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on *X*. Let *T*(*x*) be a map from *X* into bounded operators from *E* to *F* which is uniformly bounded and continuous in the strong operator topology. If

$$\displaystyle{A= \sup_x \int_X \|T(x)^*T(y)\|^{1\over 2} \, d\mu(y),\,\,\, B= \sup_x \int_X \|T(y)T(x)^*\|^{1\over 2}\, d\mu(y),}$$

are finite, then the function *T*(*x*)*v* is integrable for each *v* in *E* with

$$\displaystyle{\|\int_X T(x)v\, d\mu(x)\| \le \sqrt{AB} \cdot \|v\|.}$$

The result can be proven by replacing sums with integrals in the previous proof, or by utilizing Riemann sums to approximate the integrals.

:::

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Here is an example of an orthogonal family of operators. Consider the infinite-dimensional matrices.

$$T=\left[ \begin{array}{cccc} 1&0&0&\vdots\\0&1&0&\vdots\\0&0&1&\vdots\\\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\ddots\end{array} \right]$$

and also

$$\qquad T_1=\left[ \begin{array}{cccc} 1&0&0&\vdots\\0&0&0&\vdots\\0&0&0&\vdots\\\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\ddots\end{array} \right], \qquad T_2=\left[ \begin{array}{cccc} 0&0&0&\vdots\\0&1&0&\vdots\\0&0&0&\vdots\\\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\ddots\end{array} \right], \qquad T_3=\left[ \begin{array}{cccc} 0&0&0&\vdots\\0&0&0&\vdots\\0&0&1&\vdots\\\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\ddots\end{array} \right], \qquad \dots.$$

Then $\Vert T_j\Vert=1$ for each $j$, hence the series $\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}T_j$ does not converge in the [uniform operator topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_operator_topology).

Yet, since $\Vert T_j T_k^\ast\Vert=0$ and $\Vert T_j^\ast T_k\Vert=0$ for $j\ne k$, the Cotlar–Stein almost orthogonality lemma tells us that

$$T=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}T_j$$

converges in the [strong operator topology](wikipedia://en/458699) and is bounded by 1.

:::
