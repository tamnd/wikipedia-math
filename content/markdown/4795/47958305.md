# Robert L. Devaney

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Robert L. Devaney"}

**Robert Luke Devaney** (April 9, 1948 – November 16, 2025) was an American mathematician known for his research in [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632). He was the Feld Family Professor of Teaching Excellence at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) and served as the president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) from 2013 to 2015.

:::

Devaney's research spanned [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182), and [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518). He was among the first mathematicians to systematically study the dynamics of transcendental entire functions, and the fractal structures he described in their [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434) became known as *Devaney hairs*.[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Devaney was born in [Lawrence, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Massachusetts), on April 9, 1948.[^o7] He was raised in nearby [Methuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methuen,_Massachusetts), and attended [Central Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Catholic_High_School_%28Massachusetts%29).[^o8]

After high school, Devaney graduated from the [College of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_the_Holy_Cross) in [Worcester, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Massachusetts), with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics with highest honors in 1969.[^o9] As an undergraduate at Holy Cross, he was named the Fenwick Scholar, the college's highest academic honor.[^o10] His senior undergraduate thesis, "Lens Spaces as Coset Spaces", was published in the fifth volume of the *Pi Mu Epsilon Journal*.[^o11] He then earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics in 1973 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), under Fields medalist [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294). His doctoral dissertation was titled, "Reversible diffeomorphisms and flows".[^o13]

### Academic career {#Academic_career}

After completing his doctorate, Devaney was a [postdoctoral fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) from 1974 to 1976 and an assistant professor of mathematics at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University) from 1976 to 1980.[^o7] During the spring semester of 1980, he was a visiting assistant professor at the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park), and that year he joined [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) as an associate professor of mathematics.[^o7] He served as chair of Boston University's Department of Mathematics from 1983 to 1986 and became a full professor there in 1986.[^o7] During his career at Boston University, he also held visiting appointments at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in 1984 and 1989, [Paris-Sud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris-Sud_University) and the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) in 1988, the [Centre de Recerca Matemàtica](wikipedia://en/21193525) in 2001, and the [University of Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Barcelona) in 2008.[^o7]

Beginning in 1989, Devaney directed the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation)-sponsored Dynamical Systems and Technology Project at Boston University.[^o15] The project was designed to help secondary-school and college mathematics teachers introduce contemporary subjects including [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295), [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913), and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) into their classrooms and to use technology in teaching those subjects.[^o16] He also co-directed the Boston University Ordinary Differential Equations Project with mathematicians Paul Blanchard and Glen R. Hall; originally funded by two NSF grants, the project sought to incorporate modern dynamical-systems theory into the traditional sophomore-level course in [ordinary differential equations](wikipedia://en/32742753) and produced a textbook based on that approach.[^o17] Outside academia, he served as a "chaos consultant" for theatrical productions of [*Arcadia*](wikipedia://en/58379) and as a mathematical consultant for the 2007 production of the film [*21*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_%282008_film%29).[^o8]

In 2010, Boston University named Devaney its inaugural Feld Family Professor of Teaching Excellence.[^o18] He then served as president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) from 2013 to 2015.[^o19] He retired from Boston University in 2018, becoming a professor emeritus of mathematics there.[^o8]

Devaney died on November 16, 2025, at the age of 77.[^o20]

## Mathematical research {#Mathematical_research}

Devaney is known for formulating a simple and widely used definition of [chaotic systems](wikipedia://en/6295), one that does not need advanced concepts such as [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory).[^o22] In his 1989 book *An Introduction to Chaotic Dynamical Systems*, Devaney defined a system to be chaotic if it has [sensitive dependence on initial conditions](wikipedia://en/4024), it is [topologically transitive](wikipedia://en/1308185#Topological_mixing) (for any two [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358), some points from one set will eventually hit the other set), and its [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) form a [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594).[^o23] Later, it was observed that this definition is redundant: sensitive dependence on initial conditions follows automatically as a mathematical consequence of the other two properties.[^o24]

Devaney hairs, a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) structure in certain [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434), are named after Devaney, who was the first to investigate them.[^o30][^o25]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1995, Devaney won the [Deborah and Franklin Tepper Haimo Award for Distinguished University Teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).[^o27] In 2002, Devaney won the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) Director's Award for Distinguished Teaching Scholars.[^o28][^o29]

In 2008, a conference in honor of Devaney's 60th birthday was held in [Tossa de Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tossa_de_Mar), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain). The papers from the conference were published in a special issue of the *Journal of Difference Equations and Applications* in 2010, also honoring Devaney.[^o30]

In 2012, he became one of the inaugurals [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o31]

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

**Books**

Devaney is the author of books on [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) and [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) including:

- *An Introduction to Chaotic Dynamical Systems* (Benjamin/Cummings 1986; 2nd ed., Addison-Wesley, 1989; reprinted by Westview Press, 2003)[^o33][^o34][^o35]
- *The Science of Fractal Images* (with [Barnsley](wikipedia://en/1014175), [Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999), [Peitgen](wikipedia://en/1562451), [Saupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietmar_Saupe), and Voss, Springer-Verlag, 1988)[^o36]
- *Chaos, Fractals, and Dynamics: Computer Experiments in Mathematics* (Addison-Wesley, 1990)[^o37]
- *A First Course in Chaotic Dynamical Systems: Theory and Experiment* (Addison-Wesley, 1992)[^o38]
- *Fractals: A Tool Kit of Dynamics Activities* (with J. Choate and A. Foster, Key Curriculum Press, 1999)
- *Iteration: A Tool Kit of Dynamics Activities* (with J. Choate and A. Foster, Key Curriculum Press, 1999)
- *Chaos: A Tool Kit of Dynamics Activities* (with J. Choate, Key Curriculum Press, 2000)
- *The Mandelbrot and Julia Sets: A Tool Kit of Dynamics Activities* (Key Curriculum Press, 2000)
- *Differential Equations* (with P. Blanchard and G. R. Hall, 3rd ed., Brooks/Cole, 2005)
- *Differential Equations, Dynamical Systems, and an Introduction to Chaos* (with [Morris Hirsch](wikipedia://en/32523108) and [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294), 2nd ed., Academic Press, 2004; 3rd ed., Academic Press, 2013)[^o39]
