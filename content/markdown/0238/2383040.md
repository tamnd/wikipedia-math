# Matrix congruence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matrix congruence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), two [square matrices](wikipedia://en/166022) $A$ and $B$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) are called **congruent** if there exists an [invertible matrix](wikipedia://en/217122) $P$ over the same field such that

:::

$$P^{\mathsf{T}} AP = B$$

Matrix congruence arises when considering the effect of [change of basis](wikipedia://en/1179451) on the [Gram matrix](wikipedia://en/987959) attached to a [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) or [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on a [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370): two matrices are congruent if and only if they represent the same bilinear form with respect to different [bases](wikipedia://en/18420).

[Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598) defines congruence in terms of [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) (with respect to a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856)) rather than transpose,[^o2] but this definition has not been adopted by most other authors.

## Congruence over the reals {#Congruence_over_the_reals}

[Sylvester's law of inertia](wikipedia://en/1127460) states that two congruent [symmetric matrices](wikipedia://en/126474) with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) entries have the same numbers of positive, negative, and zero [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue). That is, the number of eigenvalues of each sign is an invariant of the associated quadratic form.[^o4]
