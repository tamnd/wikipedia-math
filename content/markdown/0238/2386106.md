# Jerzy Neyman

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Jerzy Neyman"}

**Jerzy Spława-Neyman** (also known as **Jerzy Neyman**; ; April 16, 1894 – August 5, 1981) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He made major contributions to the development of modern [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579).[^o4] Neyman introduced the modern concept of a [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911) into [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing)[^o5] and, with [Egon Pearson](wikipedia://en/1643759), revised [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824)'s [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) testing. [Neyman allocation](wikipedia://en/79165633), an optimal strategy for choosing sample sizes in [stratified sampling](wikipedia://en/27596), is named for him.

:::

He spent the first part of his professional career at various institutions in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), and then at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), and the second part at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) where he founded and developed one of the world’s leading centres of [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711).

Neyman was the recipient of many awards during his career including the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269), the [Newcomb Cleveland Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomb_Cleveland_Prize), and the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science). He was a member of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Polish Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2121791), the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences), and [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Neyman was born on 16 April 1894 into a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) family in [Bendery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendery), in the [Bessarabia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (present-day [Bender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bender,_Moldova), Moldova), the fourth of four children of Czesław Spława-Neyman, a lawyer,[^o8][^o9] and Kazimiera ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Née) Lutosławska).[^o10] His family was [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic) and descended from a long line of Polish nobles and military heroes. Neyman served as an [altar boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_server) during his early childhood, though he would become an agnostic in later life. By the time he was 10, he could speak five languages: [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language).[^o8]

He graduated from the [Kamieniec Podolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianets-Podilskyi) gubernial [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) for boys in 1909 under the name *Yuri Cheslavovich Neyman*.[^o11] Neyman's father died of [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack) when he was 12[^o12] and his family subsequently relocated to [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv).[^o13] He began studies at [Kharkiv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_University) in 1912, where he was taught by Ukrainian probabilist [Sergei Natanovich Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Natanovich_Bernstein), who exerted an enduring influence on him. Neyman was not drafted into the army following the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) due to poor eyesight, which enabled him to stay at the university and continue his education.[^o8]

After having read *Lessons on the integration and the research of the primitive functions* by [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514), he was fascinated with [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and integration. In 1915, he completed a paper on Lebesgue integration for which he was awarded Gold Medal.[^o8] In 1917–21, he worked as an assiatant of Antoni Przeborski at the Technological Institute in Kharkiv.[^o17]

In the period of the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), Neyman's health began to deteriorate. Doctors diagnosed [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis), advising him to travel south to recuperate. In 1919, he went to the [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea), where he met a Russian woman, Olga Solodovnikova, who was a painter.[^o13] Soon after he returned to Kharkov to begin teaching in the autumn of that year, Olga also came back, and their friendship eventually led to their marriage in 1920.[^o8]

### Professional career {#Professional_career}

In 1921, Neyman returned to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) in a program of repatriation of [POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW) after the [Polish-Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Soviet_War).[^o12] He briefly worked at the Institute of Agricultural Reseach in [Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz).[^o17] His first scientific paper *Sur un théorème métrique, concernant les ensembles fermés* was published in 1923 in *[Fundamenta Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/1576701)*.[^o13] During his student years, Neyman devoted much of his limited free time to teaching mathematics and statistics, including to college students, in order to support himself.[^o12] In 1924, he earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) for a dissertation titled *Justification of Applications of Calculus of Probability to the Solution of Certain Questions of Agricultural Experimentation* written under the supervision of [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122).[^o15].

After having been granted the [Fund for National Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fund_for_National_Culture_%28Poland%29) and [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) scholarships, in 1924–26, he stayed in London, where he collaborated with [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695), his son [Egon](wikipedia://en/1643759), and [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) and in Paris, where he attended lectures by [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) and [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161), and a seminar by [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France).[^o17][^o13][^o12]

His work with Egon Pearson proved especially fruitful as the two authored six joint papers between 1926–34 including "On the Problem of Two Samples" published by the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) (1930)[^o8] and "On the Problem of the Most Efficient Tests of Statistical Hypotheses" (1933), which contained the [Neyman-Pearson lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyman-Pearson_lemma).[^o16]

*Image: Jerzy Neyman.jpg.* Neyman in Warsaw, 1973

After his return to Poland, he established the Biometric Laboratory at the [Nencki Institute of Experimental Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nencki_Institute_of_Experimental_Biology) in Warsaw. In 1923–34, he served as director of the Statistical Laboratory of the [Warsaw University of Life Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University_of_Life_Sciences) (SGGW).[^o17] In 1928, Neyman obtained [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the SGGW. In 1934, at Egon Pearson’s invitation, he travelled to Britain, where he lectured at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London). During his tenure at University College, Neyman wrote a paper on [stratified sampling](wikipedia://en/27596) that helped establish the methodological basis for what became the [Gallup Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_Poll).[^o27]

Neyman proposed and studied randomized experiments in 1923.[^o18] His paper "On the Two Different Aspects of the Representative Method: The Method of [Stratified Sampling](wikipedia://en/27596) and the Method of Purposive Selection", given at the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) on 19 June 1934,[^o19] was the groundbreaking event leading to modern scientific sampling. He introduced the [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911) in his paper in 1937.[^o20]

He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in 1928 in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna)[^o21] and a Plenary Speaker of the ICM in 1954 in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam).[^o22] In 1929, at the 18th session of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) held in Warsaw, Neyman delivered a talk entitled "A Contribution to the Theory of the Credibility of Statistical Hypotheses."[^o13]

In 1937, he visited the United States and spent six weeks giving lectures at a number of univeristies, including the Graduate School in the U.S. Department of Agriculture in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.),[^o12] in which he spoke about the importance of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) to [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), developing his theories and presenting his research findings to support it. Based on these lectures, Neyman published a book in 1938 entitled *Lectures and Conferences on Mathematical Statistics*.[^o17] He also published books dealing with experiments and statistics, and devised the way which the [Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) tests medicines today.

### Move to the United States {#Move_to_the_United_States}

*Image: Wheeler Hall, University of California, Berkeley.jpg.* Neyman spent his academic career in the United States at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).

In 1938, Neyman moved to [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), where he worked for the rest of his life at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o8] After the outbreak of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he briefly turned to military research focusing in particular on bomb‑sight design and targeting problems. During his time at Berkeley, he sought to establish a world‑class centre for mathematical statistics at this institution, working relentlessly toward this aim[^o8] advancing concepts in estimation and hypothesis‑testing theory.[^o27] He established there the Statistical Laboratory, a scientific center which worked for many institutions including the [U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States).[^o17] His efforts culminated in the creation of a separate statistics department at the university on 1 July 1955.[^o8]

Throughout his academic career, 45 students obtained their PhD degrees under his advisorship including [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111), [Lucien Le Cam](wikipedia://en/2629104), [Evelyn Fix](wikipedia://en/54382640), [Erich Leo Lehmann](wikipedia://en/16388476), [Joseph Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lawson_Hodges,_Jr.), [Pao-Lu Hsu](wikipedia://en/42380697), and [Frank J. Massey](wikipedia://en/80980182).[^o15]

### Recognition and death {#Recognition_and_death}

Neyman was a recipient of many prestigious awards for his contributions to science. In 1958, he was awarded the [Newcomb Cleveland Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomb_Cleveland_Prize) of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) (AAAS). In 1966, he received the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) and, three years later, he accepted the U.S. [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science), the highest science award in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States),[^o25][^o26] from [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) "for laying the foundations of modern statistics and devising tests and procedure that have become essential parts of the knowledge of every statistician."[^o27] He died in [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California), [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California), in 1981.[^o8]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Neyman’s scholarly output comprises more than 240 publications, covering an exceptionally broad range of topics – from pure [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711), the theory of [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processe), and [sampling theory](wikipedia://en/160361) to applications in [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997), [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [medical diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_diagnosis), [social insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_insurance), and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy).[^o17][^o4][^o8]

Neyman played a central role in shaping modern [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing). In 1933, together with Egon Pearson, he introduced the concept in statistics known as the [Neyman-Pearson lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyman-Pearson_lemma), which asserts the existence and uniqueness of the likelihood ratio as a [uniformly most powerful test](wikipedia://en/15433591) in specified settings.[^o29] It forms a central component of the broader Neyman–Pearson theory for statistical testing, which also developed key notions such as [Type II error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_II_error), [power function](wikipedia://en/238695), and inductive behavior.[^o30][^o31]

A key part of Neyman's collaboration with Pearson was a systematic revision of [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824)’s formulation of the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis). Fisher had emphasized significance testing as a measure of evidence against a null model, but Neyman and Pearson reframed the problem: instead of treating the null as something to be “rejected” or “not rejected,” they defined hypothesis testing as a decision procedure with controlled long‑run error probabilities.[^o32]

Another significant contribution by Neyman was introduced in 1934, and is now known as the [Neyman allocation](wikipedia://en/79165633). It is a [stratified‑sampling method](wikipedia://en/27596) which specifies the optimal sample size for each stratum so as to minimize the variance of an estimated population parameter given a fixed overall sample size and cost.

In 1937, Neyman developed the concept of the [confidence interval](wikipedia://en/280911) into a general framework for [statistical hypothesis tests](wikipedia://en/30284).[^o20] In [frequentist statistics](wikipedia://en/15537745), it denotes a range of values that, under repeated sampling, is expected to contain the true value of an unknown parameter, for example, a population mean.[^o33] Other notalbe concepts named after Neyman include the [Neyman construction](wikipedia://en/9164216), [Neyman Type A distribution](wikipedia://en/75712890), [Neyman–Scott process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyman–Scott_process), the [Neyman-Rubin causal model](wikipedia://en/5700418), and the [Fisher–Neyman factorization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher–Neyman_factorization_theorem).
