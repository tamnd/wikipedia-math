# Thomas Callister Hales

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Callister Hales"}

**Thomas Callister Hales** (born June 4, 1958) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the areas of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), and [formal verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_verification). In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) he is known for his work on the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) and the proof of the [fundamental lemma](wikipedia://en/25431009) over the group [Sp(4)](wikipedia://en/173977) (many of his ideas were incorporated into the final proof of the fundamental lemma, due to [Ngô Bảo Châu](wikipedia://en/11133659)). In [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), he settled the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311) on the density of [sphere packings](wikipedia://en/368621), the [honeycomb conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb_conjecture), and the [dodecahedral conjecture](wikipedia://en/7033091). In 2014, he announced the completion of the Flyspeck Project, which formally verified the correctness of his proof of the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He received his Ph.D. from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1986 with a dissertation titled *The Subregular Germ of Orbital Integrals*. Hales taught at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago),[^o4] and from 1993 and 2002 he worked at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).

In 1998, Hales submitted his paper on the computer-aided [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311), a centuries-old problem in [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468) which states that the most space-efficient way to [pack spheres](wikipedia://en/368621) is in a tetrahedron shape. He was aided by graduate student Samuel Ferguson. In 1999, Hales proved the [honeycomb conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb_conjecture), and also stated that the conjecture may have been in the minds of mathematicians before [Marcus Terentius Varro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Terentius_Varro). The conjecture is mentioned by [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) in his Book V.

After 2002, Hales became the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh)'s Mellon Professor of Mathematics. In 2003, Hales started work on Flyspeck to vindicate his proof of the Kepler conjecture. His proof relied on computer calculation to verify conjectures. The project used two [proof assistants](wikipedia://en/1258607), [HOL Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOL_Light) and [Isabelle](wikipedia://en/161886). *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* accepted the proof in 2005; but was only 99% sure of the proof. In August 2014, the Flyspeck team's software finally verified the proof to be correct.

In 2017, he initiated the Formal Abstracts project which aims to provide formalised statements of the main results of each mathematical research paper in the language of an [interactive theorem prover](wikipedia://en/1258607). The goal of this project is to benefit from the increased precision and interoperability that computer formalisation provides while circumventing the effort that a full-scale formalisation of all published proofs currently entails. In the long term, the project hopes to build a corpus of mathematical facts which would allow for the application of machine learning techniques in interactive and automated theorem proving.[^o5]

Hales worked on a conjecture of [Karl Reinhardt](wikipedia://en/35727144) with Koundinya Vajjha, that the [smoothed octagon](wikipedia://en/22058210) has the lowest maximum packing density of all centrally symmetric convex shapes in the plane. Although they failed to prove Reinhardt's conjecture, in 2024 they claim to have proved[^o6] a related conjecture of [Kurt Mahler](wikipedia://en/4421496):

> It seems highly probable from the convexity condition, that the boundary of an extreme convex domain consists of line segments and arcs of hyperbolae. [^o7]

Hales retired in May of 2025.[^o8]

## Awards {#Awards}

Hales was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 2002.[^o10] He won the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) in 2003,[^o11] the [R. E. Moore Prize](wikipedia://en/58131299) in 2004,[^o12] a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 2008,[^o13] and a [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) in 2009.[^o14] He was awarded the inaugural [Robbins Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_P._Robbins_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2007.[^o15] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16] He was invited to give the [Tarski Lectures](wikipedia://en/54104355) in 2019. His three lectures were titled "A formal proof of the Kepler conjecture", "Formalizing mathematics", and "Integrating with Logic".[^o17][^o18] He was awarded the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2020.[^o19]
