# Rational surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rational surface"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **rational surface** is a surface [birationally equivalent](wikipedia://en/382733) to the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), or in other words a [rational variety](wikipedia://en/865939) of dimension two. Rational surfaces are the simplest of the 10 or so classes of surface in the [Enriques–Kodaira classification](wikipedia://en/3029260) of complex surfaces, and were the first surfaces to be investigated.

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Minimal rational surface"}

## Structure {#Structure}

Every non-singular rational surface can be obtained by repeatedly [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) a **minimal rational surface**. The minimal rational surfaces are the projective plane and the [Hirzebruch surfaces](wikipedia://en/3212617) Σ<sub>*r*</sub> for *r* = 0 or *r* ≥ 2.

**Invariants:** The [plurigenera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurigenera) are all 0 and the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) is trivial.

**[Hodge diamond](wikipedia://en/2164886#Hodge_diamond):** 1

where *n* is 0 for the projective plane, and 1 for [Hirzebruch surfaces](wikipedia://en/3212617) and greater than 1 for other rational surfaces.

The [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) is the odd [unimodular lattice](wikipedia://en/1648605) I<sub>1,*n*</sub>, except for the [Hirzebruch surfaces](wikipedia://en/3212617) Σ<sub>2*m*</sub> when it is the even unimodular lattice II<sub>1,1</sub>.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Castelnuovo's theorem"}

## Castelnuovo's theorem {#Castelnuovo's_theorem}

[Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891) proved that any complex surface such that *q* and *P*<sub>2</sub> (the irregularity and second plurigenus) both vanish is rational. This is used in the Enriques–Kodaira classification to identify the rational surfaces. (Zariski 1958) proved that Castelnuovo's theorem also holds over fields of positive characteristic.

Castelnuovo's theorem also implies that any [unirational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unirational) complex surface is rational, because if a complex surface is unirational then its irregularity and plurigenera are bounded by those of a rational surface and are therefore all 0, so the surface is rational. Most unirational complex varieties of dimension 3 or larger are not rational. In characteristic *p* > 0 (Zariski 1958) found examples of unirational surfaces ([Zariski surfaces](wikipedia://en/3096979)) that are not rational.

At one time it was unclear whether a complex surface such that *q* and *P*<sub>1</sub> both vanish is rational, but a counterexample (an [Enriques surface](wikipedia://en/3094760)) was found by [Federigo Enriques](wikipedia://en/1732661).

:::

## Examples of rational surfaces {#Examples_of_rational_surfaces}

- [Bordiga surfaces](wikipedia://en/23333699): A degree 6 embedding of the projective plane into *P*<sup>4</sup> defined by the quartics through 10 points in general position.
- [Châtelet surfaces](wikipedia://en/23342300)
- [Coble surfaces](wikipedia://en/23425751)
- [Cubic surfaces](wikipedia://en/526126) Nonsingular cubic surfaces are isomorphic to the projective plane blown up in 6 points, and are Fano surfaces. Named examples include the [Fermat cubic](wikipedia://en/4058828), the [Cayley cubic surface](wikipedia://en/5202189), and the [Clebsch diagonal surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clebsch_diagonal_surface).
- [del Pezzo surfaces](wikipedia://en/732323) (Fano surfaces)
- [Enneper surface](wikipedia://en/4688830)
- [Hirzebruch surfaces](wikipedia://en/3212617) Σ<sub>*n*</sub>
- *P*<sup>1</sup>×*P*<sup>1</sup> The product of two projective lines is the Hirzebruch surface Σ<sub>0</sub>. It is the only surface with two different [rulings](wikipedia://en/705158).
- The [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350)
- [Segre surface](wikipedia://en/23341517) An intersection of two quadrics, isomorphic to the projective plane blown up in 5 points.
- [Steiner surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_surface) A surface in *P*<sup>4</sup> with singularities which is birational to the projective plane.
- [White surfaces](wikipedia://en/23333947), a generalization of Bordiga surfaces.
- [Veronese surface](wikipedia://en/1569217) An embedding of the projective plane into *P*<sup>5</sup>.
