# Rational normal curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rational normal curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **rational normal curve** is a smooth, [rational curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve) $C$ of [degree](wikipedia://en/2776558) $n$ in [projective n-space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbf{P}^{n}$. It is a simple example of a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469); formally, it is the [Veronese variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronese_variety) when the domain is the projective line. For $n = 2$ it is the [plane conic](wikipedia://en/19008673) $Z_{0}Z_{2} = Z^{2}_{1},$ and for $n = 3$ it is the [twisted cubic](wikipedia://en/1547121). The term "normal" refers to [projective normality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_normality), not [normal schemes](wikipedia://en/2629423). The intersection of the rational normal curve with an [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) is called the [moment curve](wikipedia://en/29536414).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The rational normal curve may be given [parametrically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametrically) as the image of the map

$$\nu:\mathbf{P}^1\to\mathbf{P}^n$$

which assigns to the [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) $[S : T]$ the value

$$\nu:[S:T] \mapsto \left [S^n:S^{n-1}T:S^{n-2}T^2:\cdots:T^n \right ].$$

In the [affine coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_coordinates) of the chart $x_{0} \ne 0$ the map is simply

$$\nu:x \mapsto \left (x, x^2, \ldots, x^n \right ).$$

That is, the rational normal curve is the closure by a single [point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139) of the [affine curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_curve)

$$\left (x, x^2, \ldots, x^n \right ).$$

Equivalently, rational normal curve may be understood to be a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469), defined as the common zero locus of the [homogeneous polynomials](wikipedia://en/1698977)

$$F_{i,j} \left (X_0, \ldots, X_n \right ) = X_iX_j - X_{i+1}X_{j-1}$$

where $[X_0: \cdots: X_n]$ are the [homogeneous coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_coordinate) on $\mathbf{P}^{n}$. The full set of these polynomials is not needed; it is sufficient to pick $n$ of these to specify the curve.

:::

## Alternate parameterization {#Alternate_parameterization}

Let $[a_i:b_i]$ be $n + 1$ distinct points in $\mathbf{P}^{1}$. Then the polynomial

$$G(S,T) = \prod_{i=0}^n \left (a_iS -b_iT \right )$$

is a [homogeneous polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) of degree $n + 1$ with distinct roots. The polynomials

$$H_i(S,T) = \frac{G(S,T)} {(a_iS-b_iT)}$$

are then a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $n$. The map

$$[S:T] \mapsto \left [H_0(S,T) : H_1(S,T) : \cdots : H_n (S,T) \right ]$$

or, equivalently, dividing by $G(S, T)$

$$[S:T] \mapsto \left[\frac{1}{(a_0S-b_0T)} : \cdots : \frac{1}{(a_nS-b_nT)}\right]$$

is a rational normal curve. That this is a rational normal curve may be understood by noting that the [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416)

$$S^n,S^{n-1}T,S^{n-2}T^2,\cdots,T^n,$$

are just one possible [basis](wikipedia://en/59220) for the space of degree $n$ homogeneous polynomials. In fact, any [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) will do. This is just an application of the statement that any two projective varieties are projectively equivalent if they are [congruent](wikipedia://en/48167) modulo the [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667) $\operatorname{PGL}_{n + 1}(K)$ (with $K$ the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) over which the projective space is defined).

This rational curve sends the zeros of $G$ to each of the coordinate points of $\mathbf{P}^{n}$; that is, all but one of the $H_{i}$ vanish for a zero of $G$. Conversely, any rational normal curve passing through the $n + 1$ coordinate points may be written parametrically in this way.

## Properties {#Properties}

The rational normal curve has an assortment of nice properties:

- Any $n + 1$ points on $C$ are linearly independent, and span $\mathbf{P}^{n}$. This property distinguishes the rational normal curve from all other curves.
- Given $n + 3$ points in $\mathbf{P}^{n}$ in linear [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) (that is, with no $n + 1$ lying in a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862)), there is a unique rational normal curve passing through them. The curve may be explicitly specified using the parametric representation, by arranging $n + 1$ of the points to lie on the coordinate axes, and then mapping the other two points to $[S : T] = [0 : 1]$ and $[S : T] = [1 : 0]$.
- The tangent and secant lines of a rational normal curve are pairwise disjoint, except at points of the curve itself. This is a property shared by sufficiently positive embeddings of any projective variety.
- There are

$$\binom{n+2}{2}-2n-1$$

independent [quadrics](wikipedia://en/64926134) that generate the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of the curve.

- The curve is not a [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116835), for $n > 2$. That is, it cannot be defined (as a [subscheme](wikipedia://en/364754) of projective space) by only $n - 1$ equations, that being the [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149) of the curve in $\mathbf{P}^n$.
- The [canonical mapping](wikipedia://en/734893#Canonical_maps) for a [hyperelliptic curve](wikipedia://en/499361) has image a rational normal curve, and is 2-to-1.
- Every irreducible non-degenerate curve $C \subset \mathbf{P}^{n}$ of degree $n$ is a rational normal curve.
