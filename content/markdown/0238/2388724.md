# Artin approximation theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin approximation theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Artin approximation theorem** is a fundamental result of (txt) in [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) which implies that [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) with coefficients in a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *k* are well-approximated by the [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) on *k*.

:::

More precisely, Artin proved two such theorems: one, in 1968, on approximation of complex analytic solutions by formal solutions (in the case $k = \mathbb{C}$); and an algebraic version of this theorem in 1969.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\mathbf{x} = x_1, \dots, x_n$ denote a collection of *n* [indeterminates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_%28variable%29), $k[[\mathbf{x}]]$ the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) with indeterminates $\mathbf{x}$ over a field *k*, and $\mathbf{y} = y_1, \dots, y_n$ a different set of indeterminates. Let

$$f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = 0$$

be a system of [polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation) in $k[\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}]$, and *c* a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). Then given a formal power series solution $\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}) \in k[[\mathbf{x}]]$, there is an algebraic solution $\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})$ consisting of [algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/974169) (more precisely, algebraic power series) such that

$$\hat{\mathbf{y}}(\mathbf{x}) \equiv \mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}) \bmod (\mathbf{x})^c.$$

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Given any desired positive integer *c*, this theorem shows that one can find an algebraic solution approximating a formal power series solution up to the degree specified by *c*. This leads to theorems that deduce the existence of certain [formal moduli spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_moduli_space) of deformations as [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754). See also: [Artin's criterion](wikipedia://en/41952275).

## Alternative statement {#Alternative_statement}

The following alternative statement is given in Theorem 1.12 of (txt).

Let $R$ be a field or an excellent discrete valuation ring, let $A$ be the [henselization](wikipedia://en/14623383) at a prime ideal of an $R$-algebra of finite type, let *m* be a proper ideal of $A$, let $\hat{A}$ be the *m*-adic completion of $A$, and let

$$F\colon (A\text{-algebras}) \to (\text{sets}),$$

be a functor sending filtered colimits to filtered colimits (Artin calls such a functor locally of finite presentation). Then for any integer *c* and any $\overline{\xi} \in F(\hat{A})$, there is a $\xi \in F(A)$ such that

$$\overline{\xi} \equiv \xi \bmod m^c.$$
