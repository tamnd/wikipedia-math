# Pierre Remond de Montmort

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre Remond de Montmort"}

*Image: Montmort - Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard, 1713 - 276.tif.* *Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard*, 1713.

**Pierre Remond de Montmort** (27 October 1678 – 7 October 1719) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life {#Life}

His name was originally just Pierre Remond. He was born on 27 October 1678 in Paris. His father pressured him to study law, but he rebelled and travelled to England and Germany, returning to France in 1699 when, upon receiving a large inheritance from his father, he bought an estate and took the name de Montmort. He was friendly with several other notable mathematicians, and especially [Nicholas Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/1151030), who collaborated with him while visiting his estate. He was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1715, while traveling again to England, and became a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1716.

De Montmort is known for his book[^o4] on [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and games of chance, *[Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard](wikipedia://en/48314912)*, which was also the first to introduce the [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) study of [derangements](wikipedia://en/162132). He is also known for naming [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497) after [Blaise Pascal](wikipedia://en/4068), calling it "Table de M. Pascal pour les combinaisons."

Another of de Montmort's interests was the subject of [finite differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_differences). He determined in 1713 the sum of *n* terms of a finite series of the form

$$na + \frac{n(n-1)}{1\cdot 2} \, \Delta a + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3} \, \Delta^2 a +\cdots,$$

where Δ is the forward [difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator), a theorem which seems to have been independently rediscovered by [Goldbach](wikipedia://en/6290) in 1718.

He died on 7 October 1719 in Paris.
