# Sylvia Wiegand

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Sylvia Wiegand"}

**Sylvia Margaret Wiegand** (born March 8, 1945) is an American mathematician.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Wiegand was born in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town), [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa). She is the daughter of mathematician [Laurence Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/9620408) and through him the granddaughter of mathematicians [Grace Chisholm Young](wikipedia://en/239997) and [William Henry Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Young).[^o5] Her family moved to Wisconsin in 1949, and she graduated from [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) in 1966 after three years of study.[^o3] In 1971, Wiegand earned her Ph.D. from the University of Wisconsin-Madison. Her dissertation was titled *Galois Theory of Essential Expansions of Modules and Vanishing Tensor Powers.*[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

In 1987, she was named full professor at the [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln); at the time Wiegand was the only female professor in the department.[^o3] In 1988, Sylvia headed a search committee for two new jobs in the math department, for which two women were hired, although one stayed only a year and another left after four years.[^o8] In 1996, Sylvia and her husband, [Roger Wiegand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Wiegand), established a fellowship for graduate student research at the university in honor of Sylvia's grandparents.[^o9]

From 1997 until 2000, Wiegand was president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o10][^o11]

Wiegand has been an editor for [Communications in Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_in_Algebra) and the [Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountain_Journal_of_Mathematics).[^o5] She was on the board of directors of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) from 1997 to 2000.[^o5]

Wiegand was an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Council member at large.[^o12]

## Awards and recognition {#Awards_and_recognition}

Wiegand is featured in the book *Notable Women in Mathematics: A Biographical Dictionary*, edited by Charlene Morrow and Teri Perl, published in 1998.[^o3] For her work in improving the status of women in mathematics, she was awarded the University of Nebraska–Lincoln's Outstanding Contribution to the Status of Women Award in 2000.[^o8] In May 2005, the University of Nebraska–Lincoln hosted the Nebraska Commutative Algebra Conference: WiegandFest "in celebration of the many important contributions of Sylvia and her husband Roger Wiegand."[^o3]

In 2012, she became a fellow of the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14]

In 2017, she was selected as a fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o15]
