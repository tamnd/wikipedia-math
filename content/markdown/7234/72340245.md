# Method of virtual quanta

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Method of virtual quanta"}

The **method of virtual quanta** is a method used to calculate radiation produced by interactions of electromagnetic particles, particularly in the case of [bremsstrahlung](wikipedia://en/54147). It can also be applied in the context of [gravitational radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_radiation), and more recently to other field theories by [Carl Friedrich von Weizsäcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_von_Weizsäcker) and [Evan James Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_James_Williams) in 1934.

:::

## Background {#Background}

In problems of collision between charged particles or systems, the incident particle is often travelling at relativistic speeds when impacting the struck system, producing the field of a moving charge as follows:[^o3]

$$E_1 = -\frac{q \gamma v t}{(b^2 + \gamma^2 v^2 t^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

$$E_2 = \frac{q \gamma b}{(b^2 + \gamma^2 v^2 t^2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}$$

$$B_3 = \frac{v}{c} E_2$$

where $E_1$ indicates the component of the [electric field](wikipedia://en/41092) in the direction of travel of the particle, $E_2$ indicates the E-field in the direction perpendicular to $E_1$ and in the plane of the collision, $b$ is the [impact parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_parameter), $\gamma$ is the [Lorentz factor](wikipedia://en/732446), $q$ the [charge](wikipedia://en/9804) and $v$ the velocity of the incident particle.

In the [ultrarelativistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrarelativistic) limit, $E_2$ and $B_3$ have the form of a pulse of radiation travelling in the $\overrightarrow{e_1}$ direction. This creates the virtual radiation pulse (virtual quanta) denoted by $P_1$. Moreover, an additional [magnetic field](wikipedia://en/36563) may be added in order to turn $E_1$ into a radiation pulse travelling along $\overrightarrow{e_2}$, denoted $P_2$. This virtual magnetic field will turn out to be much smaller than $B_3$, hence its contribution to the motion of particles is minimal.[^o4]

By taking this point of view, the problem of the collision can be treated as a scattering of radiation. Similar analogies can be made for other processes (e.g. the [ionisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionisation) of an atom by a fast electron can be treated as [photoexcitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoexcitation)).

## Bremsstrahlung {#Bremsstrahlung}

In the case of bremsstrahlung, the problem becomes one of the scattering of the virtual quanta in the nuclear [Coulomb potential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb's_law#Coulomb_potential). This is a standard problem and the [cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_section_%28physics%29) of the scattering is known as the [Thomson cross section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_cross_section):

$\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z^2 e^2}{mc^2}\right)^2(1+\cos^2{\theta})$[^o6]

The differential radiation cross section per unit frequency is hence:

$$\frac{d\chi}{d\omega d\Omega} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{z^2 e^2}{mc^2}\right)^2(1+\cos^2{\theta})\frac{dI}{d\omega}$$

where $\frac{dI}{d\omega}$ is the frequency spectrum of virtual quanta produced by the incident particle over all possible impact parameters.[^o7]

## Other applications {#Other_applications}

### Synchrotron radiation {#Synchrotron_radiation}

Synchrotron radiation

In the [rest frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rest_frame) of the charged accelerating particle, the emission of [synchrotron radiation](wikipedia://en/170808) can be treated as a Thomson scattering problem. This enables the introduction of various corrections into the classical calculation of the power lost by particles while accelerated, such as the quantum correction through the [Klein-Nishina formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein-Nishina_formula).[^o10]

### Gravitational radiation {#Gravitational_radiation}

Gravitational waves

When transforming the gravitational field described by the [Schwarzschild metric](wikipedia://en/264606) into the rest frame of a passing, relativistic test particle, an effect similar to the relativistic transformation of electric and magnetic fields is observed, and virtual pulses of gravitational radiation are produced. Through this, the cross section of close gravitational encounters and radiative power loss caused by such collisions can be calculated.[^o12]
