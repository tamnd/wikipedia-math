# Priestley space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Priestley space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Priestley space** is an [ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with special properties. Priestley spaces are named after [Hilary Priestley](wikipedia://en/43027438) who introduced and investigated them.[^o2] Priestley spaces play a fundamental role in the study of [distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/310883). In particular, there is a [duality](wikipedia://en/26599926) ("**Priestley duality**"[^o3]) between the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of Priestley spaces and the category of bounded distributive lattices.[^o4][^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *Priestley space* is an *ordered topological space* $(X,\tau,\le)$, i.e. a set $X$ equipped with a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) $\le$ and a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450#Definitions) $\tau$, satisfying the following two conditions:

1. $(X,\tau)$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).
1. If $\scriptstyle x\,\not\le\, y$, then there exists a [clopen](wikipedia://en/233398) [up-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up-set) $U$ of $X$ such that $x\in U$ and $y\notin U$. (This condition is known as the *Priestley separation axiom*.)

:::

## Properties of Priestley spaces {#Properties_of_Priestley_spaces}

- Each Priestley space is [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637). Indeed, given two points $x,y$ of a Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$, if $x\ne y$, then as $\le$ is a partial order, either $\scriptstyle x\,\not\le\, y$ or $\scriptstyle y\,\not\le\, x$. Assuming, without loss of generality, that $\scriptstyle x\,\not\le\, y$, (ii) provides a clopen up-set $U$ of $X$ such that $x\in U$ and $y\notin U$. Therefore, $U$ and $V = X - U$ are disjoint open subsets of $X$ separating $x$ and $y$.
- Each Priestley space is also [zero-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-dimensional); that is, each [open neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighborhood) $U$ of a point $x$ of a Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$ contains a clopen neighborhood $C$ of $x$. To see this, one proceeds as follows. For each $y \in X - U$, either $\scriptstyle x\,\not\le\, y$ or $\scriptstyle y\,\not\le\, x$. By the Priestley separation axiom, there exists a clopen up-set or a clopen [down-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down-set) containing $x$ and missing $y$. The intersection of these clopen neighborhoods of $x$ does not meet $X - U$. Therefore, as $X$ is compact, there exists a finite intersection of these clopen neighborhoods of $x$ missing $X - U$. This finite intersection is the desired clopen neighborhood $C$ of $x$ contained in $U$.

It follows that for each Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$, the topological space $(X,\tau)$ is a [Stone space](wikipedia://en/380219); that is, it is a compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional space.

Some further useful properties of Priestley spaces are listed below.

Let $(X,\tau,\le)$ be a Priestley space.

(a) For each closed subset $F$ of $X$, both $\uparrow F = \{x \in X : y \le x\text{ for }\text{ some } y \in F\}$ and $\downarrow F = \{ x \in X : x \le y\text{ for }\text{ some } y \in F\}$ are closed subsets of $X$.

(b) Each open up-set of $X$ is a union of clopen up-sets of $X$ and each open down-set of $X$ is a union of clopen down-sets of $X$.

(c) Each closed up-set of $X$ is an intersection of clopen up-sets of $X$ and each closed down-set of $X$ is an intersection of clopen down-sets of $X$.

(d) Clopen up-sets and clopen down-sets of $X$ form a [subbasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbasis) for $(X,\tau)$.

(e) For each pair of closed subsets $F$ and $G$ of $X$, if $\uparrow F \cap \downarrow G = \emptyset$, then there exists a clopen up-set $U$ such that $F \subseteq U$ and $U \cap G = \emptyset$.

A **Priestley morphism** from a Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$ to another Priestley space $(X',\tau',\le')$ is a map $f : X \to X'$ which is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) and [order-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-preserving).

Let **Pries** denote the category of Priestley spaces and Priestley morphisms.

## Connection with spectral spaces {#Connection_with_spectral_spaces}

Priestley spaces are closely related to [spectral spaces](wikipedia://en/544273). For a Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$, let $\tau^{u}$ denote the collection of all open up-sets of $X$. Similarly, let $\tau^{d}$ denote the collection of all open down-sets of $X$.

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:**[^o11] If $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, then both $(X,\tau^{u})$ and $(X,\tau^{d})$ are spectral spaces.

Conversely, given a spectral space $(X,\tau)$, let $\tau^{\#}$ denote the [patch topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patch_topology) on $X$; that is, the topology generated by the subbasis consisting of compact open subsets of $(X,\tau)$ and their [complements](wikipedia://en/54347). Let also $\le$ denote the [specialization order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialization_order) of $(X,\tau)$.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:**[^o13] If $(X,\tau)$ is a spectral space, then $(X,\tau^{\#},\le)$ is a Priestley space.

In fact, this correspondence between Priestley spaces and spectral spaces is [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial) and yields an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) between **Pries** and the category **Spec** of spectral spaces and [spectral maps](wikipedia://en/544273).

:::

## Connection with bitopological spaces {#Connection_with_bitopological_spaces}

Priestley spaces are also closely related to [bitopological spaces](wikipedia://en/5089733).

::: {#o15 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:**[^o16] If $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, then $(X,\tau^{u},\tau^{d})$ is a [pairwise Stone space](wikipedia://en/26598031). Conversely, if $(X,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})$ is a pairwise Stone space, then $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a Priestley space, where $\tau$ is the join of $\tau_{1}$ and $\tau_{2}$ and $\le$ is the specialization order of $(X,\tau_{1})$.

The correspondence between Priestley spaces and pairwise Stone spaces is functorial and yields an isomorphism between the category **Pries** of Priestley spaces and Priestley morphisms and the category **PStone** of pairwise Stone spaces and [bi-continuous maps](wikipedia://en/5089733#Bi-continuity).

Thus, one has the following isomorphisms of categories:

$$\mathbf{Spec}\cong \mathbf{Pries}\cong \mathbf{PStone}$$

One of the main consequences of the [duality theory for distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/26599926) is that each of these categories is dually equivalent to the category of bounded [distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/310883).

:::
