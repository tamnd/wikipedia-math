# Duality theory for distributive lattices

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Duality theory for distributive lattices"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **duality theory for distributive lattices** provides three different (but closely related) representations of [bounded distributive lattices](wikipedia://en/310883) via [Priestley spaces](wikipedia://en/26599382), [spectral spaces](wikipedia://en/544273), and [pairwise Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/26598031). This duality, which is originally also due to [Marshall H. Stone](wikipedia://en/758331),[^o2] generalizes the well-known [Stone duality](wikipedia://en/379619) between [Stone spaces](wikipedia://en/380219) and [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959).

:::

Let $L$ be a bounded distributive lattice, and let $X$ denote the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [prime filters](wikipedia://en/530559) of $L$. For each $a \in L$, let $\varphi_{+}(a) = \{x\in X : a \in x$. Then $(X,\tau_{+})$ is a spectral space,[^o3] where the [topology](wikipedia://en/30450#Definitions) $\tau_{+}$ on $X$ is generated by $\{\varphi_{+}(a) : a \in L$. The spectral space $(X, \tau_{+})$ is called the *prime spectrum* of $L$.

The [map](wikipedia://en/516931) $\varphi_{+}$ is a lattice [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) from $L$ onto the lattice of all [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) [open](wikipedia://en/39358) subsets of $(X,\tau_{+})$. In fact, each spectral space is [homeomorphic](wikipedia://en/13660) to the prime spectrum of some bounded distributive lattice.[^o4]

Similarly, if $\varphi_{-}(a) = \{x\in X : a \notin x\}$ and $\tau_{-}$ denotes the topology generated by $\{\varphi_{-}(a) : a\in L$, then $(X,\tau_{-})$ is also a spectral space. Moreover, $(X,\tau_{+},\tau_{-})$ is a [pairwise Stone space](wikipedia://en/26598031). The pairwise Stone space $(X,\tau_{+},\tau_{-})$ is called the *[bitopological](wikipedia://en/5089733) dual* of $L$. Each pairwise Stone space is bi-homeomorphic to the bitopological dual of some bounded distributive lattice.[^o5]

Finally, let $\le$ be set-theoretic inclusion on the set of prime filters of $L$ and let $\tau = \tau_{+}\lor \tau_{-}$. Then $(X,\tau,\le)$ is a [Priestley space](wikipedia://en/26599382). Moreover, $\varphi_{+}$ is a lattice isomorphism from $L$ onto the lattice of all [clopen](wikipedia://en/233398) [up-sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up-set) of $(X,\tau,\le)$. The Priestley space $(X,\tau,\le)$ is called the *Priestley dual* of $L$. Each Priestley space is isomorphic to the Priestley dual of some bounded distributive lattice.[^o6]

Let **Dist** denote the category of bounded distributive lattices and bounded lattice [homomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homomorphisms). Then the above three representations of bounded distributive lattices can be extended to [dual equivalences](wikipedia://en/379051)[^o7] between **Dist** and the categories **Spec**, **PStone**, and **Pries** of spectral spaces with spectral maps, of pairwise Stone spaces with bi-continuous maps, and of Priestley spaces with Priestley morphisms, respectively:

*Image: DL Duality.png.* Duality for bounded distributive lattices

Thus, there are three equivalent ways of representing bounded distributive lattices. Each one has its own motivation and advantages, but ultimately they all serve the same purpose of providing better understanding of bounded distributive lattices.
