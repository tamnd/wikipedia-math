# CN-group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CN-group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the area of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) known as [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a more than fifty-year effort was made to answer a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of (Burnside 1911): are all [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) [order](wikipedia://en/493527) [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012)? Progress was made by showing that [CA-groups](wikipedia://en/5745198), groups in which the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of a non-[identity](wikipedia://en/14962) element is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974), of odd order are solvable (Suzuki 1957). Further progress was made showing that **CN-groups**, groups in which the centralizer of a non-identity element is [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/144569), of odd order are solvable (Feit Thompson Hall 1960). The complete solution was given in (Feit Thompson 1963), but further work on CN-groups was done in (Suzuki 1961), giving more detailed information about the structure of these groups. For instance, a non-solvable CN-group *G* is such that its [largest solvable normal subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_%28group%29#Solvable_radicals) *O*<sub>∞</sub>(*G*) is a [2-group](wikipedia://en/24664), and the [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) is a group of [even](wikipedia://en/143135) order.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

Solvable CN groups include

- Nilpotent groups
- [Frobenius groups](wikipedia://en/1716123) whose Frobenius complement is nilpotent
- [3-step groups](wikipedia://en/36011891), such as the symmetric group *S*<sub>4</sub>

Non-solvable CN groups include:

- The [Suzuki simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_simple_group)
- The groups PSL<sub>2</sub>(**F**<sub>2<sup>*n*</sup></sub>) for *n*>1
- The group PSL<sub>2</sub>(**F**<sub>*p*</sub>) for *p*>3 a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime) or [Mersenne prime](wikipedia://en/18908).
- The group PSL<sub>2</sub>(**F**<sub>9</sub>)
- The group PSL<sub>3</sub>(**F**<sub>4</sub>)
