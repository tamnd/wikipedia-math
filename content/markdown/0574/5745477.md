# Centrally closed subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centrally closed subgroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **centrally closed** (commonly abbreviated as CC-subgroup)[^o2] if the [centralizer](wikipedia://en/145661) of any non [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of the subgroup lies inside the subgroup. This property is useful in understanding group structures, normalizers, and [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). Centrally closed subgroups are related to commutators and play a role in analyzing group symmetries and [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634).

:::

Some facts about centrally closed subgroups:

- Every [malnormal subgroup](wikipedia://en/3593817) is centrally closed.
- Every [Frobenius kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_kernel) is centrally closed.
- [SA subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA_subgroup) are precisely the centrally closed Abelian subgroups.
- The [trivial subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_subgroup) and the whole group are centrally closed
