# Judith Tanur

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Judith Tanur"}

**Judith M. Tanur** is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [sociologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociologist) who is Distinguished Teaching Professor Emerita of Sociology at [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^o2]

:::

Judith Tanur was born to Edward Mark and Libbie Berman Mark on August 12, 1935, in Jersey City, New Jersey.[^o3] When Tanur was young, her family moved from [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey), where she was born, to [Great Neck, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Neck,_New_York). She graduated from Great Neck High School in 1953 and entered [Antioch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch_College), studying psychology and statistics there, but in 1955 she transferred to [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), in part because it was closer to the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) where her future husband was studying dentistry. At the same time, she took a job at the New York State Psychiatric Institute. Tanur completed a bachelor's degree in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) in 1957 and began graduate studies at Penn but became pregnant and dropped out. Eventually, she returned to graduate school, completed a master's degree in [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1963, and took a new job as an editor for [William Kruskal](wikipedia://en/3613880) at the *International Encyclopedia of Social Sciences*. She became a lecturer at Stony Brook in 1968, still only holding a master's degree,[^o4] and later completed her [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in sociology at Stony Brook.[^o2]

With S. James Press, she is the author of *The Subjectivity of Scientists and the Bayesian Approach* (Wiley, 2001; Dover, 2016).[^o5]

In 1980 she was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o6] She is also a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), a winner of the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224),[^o2] and the 2006 winner of the Geoffrey Marshall Mentoring Award of the Northeastern Association of Graduate Schools.[^o4]
