# Ruth Moufang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Moufang"}

**Ruth Moufang** (10 January 1905 – 26 November 1977) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

She was born to German chemist Eduard Moufang and Else Fecht Moufang. Eduard Moufang was the son of Friedrich Carl Moufang (1848-1885) from Mainz, and Elisabeth von Moers from Mainz. Ruth Moufang's mother was Else Fecht, who was the daughter of Alexander Fecht (1848-1913) from Kehl and Ella Scholtz (1847-1921). Ruth was the younger of her parents' two daughters, having an elder sister named Erica.

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

She studied mathematics at the [University of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe_University_Frankfurt). In 1931 she received her Ph.D. on [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) under the direction of [Max Dehn](wikipedia://en/570602), and in 1932 spent a fellowship year in Rome. After her year in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), she returned to Germany to lecture at the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) and the University of Frankfurt.[^o4]

Denied permission to teach by the minister of education of [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany), she worked at Research and Development of [Krupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krupp) (battleships, U-boats, tanks, howitzers, guns, etc.), where she became the first German woman with a doctorate to be employed as an industrial mathematician. At the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) she was leading the Department of [Applied Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_Mathematics) at the arms industry of Krupp.

In 1946 she was finally allowed to accept a teaching position at the University of Frankfurt, and in 1957 she became the first woman professor at the university.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Moufang's research in projective geometry built upon the work of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). She was responsible for ground-breaking work on [non-associative](wikipedia://en/6533841) [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364), including the [Moufang loops](wikipedia://en/755329) named after her.[^o4]

In 1933, Moufang showed [Desargues's theorem](wikipedia://en/358488) does not hold in the [Cayley plane](wikipedia://en/9410951). The Cayley plane uses [octonion](wikipedia://en/51436) coordinates which do not satisfy the [associative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_law). Such connections between geometry and algebra had been previously noted by [Karl von Staudt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Staudt) and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302).[^o4] Ruth Moufang thus initiated a new branch of geometry called [Moufang planes](wikipedia://en/14860374).[^o6]

She published 7 papers on this topic, these are

- *Zur Struktur der projectiven Geometrie der Ebene* (1931);
- *Die Einführung in der ebenen Geometrie mit Hilfe des Satzes vom vollständigen Vierseit* (1931);
- *Die Schnittpunktssätze des projektiven speziellen Fünfecksnetzes in ihrer Abhängigkeit voneinander* (1932);
- *Ein Satz über die Schnittpunktsätze des allgemeinen Fünfecksnetzes* (1932);
- *Die Desarguesschen Sätze von Rang 10* (1933);
- *Alternativkörper und der Satz vom vollständigen Vierseit (*D<sub>9</sub>*)* (1934); and
- *Zur Struktur von Alternativkörpern* (1934).

Moufang published only one paper on group theory, *Einige Untersuchungen über geordnete Schiefkörper*, which appeared in print in 1937.[^o4]
