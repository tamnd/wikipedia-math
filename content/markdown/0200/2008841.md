# George Blakley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Blakley"}

**George Robert (Bob) Blakley Jr.** was an American [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University), best known for inventing a [secret sharing](wikipedia://en/458253) scheme in 1979 (see [^o3]).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Blakley did his undergraduate studies in physics at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University), and received his Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) in 1960. After postdoctoral studies at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), he held faculty positions at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) and the [State University of New York at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo,_The_State_University_of_New_York) before joining Texas A&M in 1970. At Texas A&M, he was chairman of the mathematics department from 1970 to 1978.[^o5]

Blakley served on the board of directors of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043) from 1993 to 1995.[^o5] He co-founded the *[International Journal of Information Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Information_Security)*, published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag), in 2000,[^o5][^o6] and then served on its advisory board.[^o7]

His son, George Robert (Bob) Blakley III, is also a [computer security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security) researcher.[^o8]

## Secret-sharing scheme {#Secret-sharing_scheme}

In order to split a secret into several shares, Blakley's scheme specifies the secret as a point in *n*-dimensional space, and gives out shares that correspond to [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) that intersect the secret point. Any *n* such hyperplanes will specify the point, while fewer than *n* hyperplanes will leave at least one degree of freedom, and thus leave the point unspecified.[^o10]

In contrast, [Shamir](wikipedia://en/57314)'s [secret sharing scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamir's_Secret_Sharing) represents the secret as the *y*-intercept of an *n*-[degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) polynomial, and shares correspond to points on the polynomial.[^o11]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2001 Blakley received an honorary doctorate from [Queensland University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_University_of_Technology).[^o5][^o13]

In 2009 he was named a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [International Association for Cryptologic Research](wikipedia://en/87043).[^o5][^o14][^o15]
