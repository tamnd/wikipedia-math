# Tommaso Ceva

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tommaso Ceva"}

**Tommaso Ceva** (December 20, 1648 – February 3, 1737) was an Italian [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) from [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan). He was the brother of [Giovanni Ceva](wikipedia://en/152426). His work aided in spreading a knowledge of [Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s discovery of the [law of gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_law_of_universal_gravitation).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Tommaso Ceva was born into a wealthy Milanese family in 1648. After studying at the Collegio di Brera, a Jesuit college in Milan, on 24 March 1663 he entered the [Society of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits). He taught mathematics and [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447) at the Jesuit College of Brera in Milan for thirty-eight years.[^o4] His most famous student was [Giovanni Girolamo Saccheri](wikipedia://en/30875270). Under the tutelage of Ceva, Saccheri wrote his first work, titled *Quaesita geometrica* (Geometric Investigations, 1693). Ceva was one of the main representatives of [Clelia Grillo Borromeo](wikipedia://en/29215444)'s Academia Vigilantium.[^n1] [Joseph I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) named him Caesarian Theologian early in the 18th century.[^n1]

His first scientific work, *De natura gravium* (The Nature of Gravity, 1699), dealt with physical subjects - such as [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) and [free fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_fall) - in a philosophical way. His only mathematical work, published in 1699 was the *Opuscula Mathematica* (Mathematical Essays), which dealt with [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), gravity and [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118). Ceva designed an instrument to divide a [right angle](wikipedia://en/76956) into a specified number of equal parts.[^o7] His device, described in the *[Acta Eruditorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Eruditorum)* in 1695, won him the attention of [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281).[^o8] This same instrument was described in 1704 by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Guillaume de l'Hôpital](wikipedia://en/1728613).[^n2]

In his *Philosophia novo-antiqua* (New-Ancient Philosophy, 1704) Ceva defended [scholasticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism) against the systems [Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) and [Gassendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gassendi) and tried to reconcile the best of ancient and modern [natural philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_philosophy).[^n3] The work comprises six dissertations, dealing with topics ranging from mathematics to [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) and [mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanics), and engages with live issues for the science of the time ([Copernican theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernican_heliocentrism); Descartes's physics and denial of animal souls; Gassendi's [atomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomism)). Ceva accepted Galileo's theory of motion but not his cosmology. As far as [Cartesian physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_physics) is concerned, he especially criticized the identification of the [essence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essence) of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) with [extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_extensa).[^o11] Ceva's *Philosophia novo-antiqua* was reissued in Wien in 1719, in Florence in 1723 and in Venice in 1732.

Ceva was also a noted [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet) and dedicated a significant amount of his time to this task. In the literary field Ceva shared the Arcadian reaction against [marinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinism), and summed it up in his oft-quoted definition of poetry as ‘un sogno che si fa in presenza della ragione’ (“a dream made in the presence of reason”).[^n3] His Latin poem *Jesus Puer*, dedicated to the Holy Roman emperor [Joseph I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), was translated into many languages including [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language). Two other collections of Latin verses, *Sylvae* (1699; “Woods”) and *Carmina* (1704; “Poems”), range over philosophic, scientific, religious, and literary subjects. Ceva was made a fellow of the [Arcadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Arcadia) in 1718 and was in correspondence with [Vincenzo Viviani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Viviani) and [Luigi Guido Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Guido_Grandi).[^n1] He was a close friend of the mathematician [Pietro Paolo Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Paolo_Caravaggio) and his son Pietro Paolo Caravaggio junior.[^n4]

His *Opuscula mathematica* brought him international fame and his *Carmina* were favourably reviewed in *[Acta Eruditorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Eruditorum)*.[^o15] His work was highly praised by [Gotthold Ephraim Lessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthold_Ephraim_Lessing) and [Christian Friedrich Daniel Schubart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Friedrich_Daniel_Schubart).[^n5]

In his latter years, Ceva suffered from [paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralysis). He died in Milan on 3 February 1737.[^n1] The 6,5-kilometer sized main-belt asteroid [12579 Ceva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12579_Ceva), discovered at the [San Vittore Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vittore_Observatory) in 1999, was named in honor of him and his brother, Giovanni.[^o18]

## The Cycloid of Ceva {#The_Cycloid_of_Ceva}

*Image: Cycloid of ceva.gif.* Cycloid of Ceva

Prompted by the familiar "insertion" method of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844), Ceva devised in 1699 a curve for [trisection](wikipedia://en/91111) which was called the "Cycloidum anomalarum". The principle involved is that of doubling angles. The cycloid of Ceva has the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation)

$$r=1+2\cos(2\theta).$$

In [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) the equation of this curve is

$$(x^2+y^2)^3=(3x^2-y^2)^2.$$

To trisect the angle $\angle ABC$, construct a line parallel to the polar axis (the positive $x$ axis). Let $D$ be the point of intersection of the cycloid and the line. Then the angle $\angle ABD$ is one-third of the angle $\angle ABC$.

Proof: let angle $\angle ABD$ be $\theta$ and let the point $F$ on the $x$ axis be such that $|BE| = |EF| = 1$. Let $G$ be the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of $F$ on the line $BD$. The angle $\angle DEF = 2\theta$, so $|BG| = 1 +\cos(2\theta)$. Since $|BD| = 1 + 2\cos(2\theta)$, $|EG| = |GD|$, $|DF| = |EF| = 1$. So angle $\angle DFH$ equals $4\theta-\angle EFA=3\theta$, but $\angle DFH=\angle CBA$.
