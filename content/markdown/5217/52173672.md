# Al-Mubashshir ibn Fatik

*Image: Meister des al-Mubashshir-Manuskripts 002.jpg.* [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates) and two disciples from an [illuminated manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript) of Mukhtar al-ḥikam by Al-Mubaššir ibn Fatik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Al-Mubashshir ibn Fatik"}

**Abu al-Wafa' al-Mubashshir ibn Fatik** (ابو الوفاء المبشّر بن فاتك ar) was an [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and scholar well versed in the mathematical sciences and also wrote on logic and medicine. He was born in [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus) but lived mainly in Egypt during the 11th century [Fatimid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid) Caliphate. He also wrote an historical chronicle of the reign of al-Mustansir Billah. However, the book he is famed for and the only one extant, *[Kitāb mukhtār al-ḥikam wa-maḥāsin al-kalim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictes_and_Sayings_of_the_Philosophers)* (مختار الحكم ومحاسن الكلم), the "Selected Maxims and Aphorisms", is a collection of sayings attributed to the ancient sages (mainly Greeks) translated into Arabic. The date of composition given by the author is 1048–1049.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

The biographical details we have come from [Ibn Abi Usaibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Abi_Usaibia)'s *Uyūn ul-Anbāʾ fī Ṭabaqāt ul-Aṭibbāʾ* (عيون الأنباء في طبقات الأطباء, "the History of Physicians").[^o3] According to Usaibia, Ibn Fatik was from a noble family and held the position of "[emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir)" at the court of the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) in the reign of al-Mustansir Billah. He was a passionate [bibliophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliophile), acquired a great collection of books and enjoyed the company of scholars, and above all, he devoted himself to study. He trained in mathematics and astronomy under the philosopher, mathematician and astronomer [Ibn al-Haytham](wikipedia://en/1645) (965-1040). He also associated with Ibn al-Amidi and the physician, astrologer, and astronomer [Ali ibn Ridwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_ibn_Ridwan) (988–1061). When he died, many heads of state attended his funeral. According to this biography, such was his wife's disaffection through want of attention, she threw most of his books into the pool at the center of the house, and so they were lost by drowning.

## Works {#Works}

*Kitāb mukhtār al-ḥikam wa-maḥāsin al-kalim* (مختار الحكم ومحاسن الكلم), the “Book of Selected Maxims and Aphorisms”, can be described as a collection of biographies of twenty-one "sages", mainly Greeks (e.g. [Seth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth), ([Zedekiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zedekiah)),[^o5] [Hermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermes), [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer), [Solon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon), [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275), [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates), [Diogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes), [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954), [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), [Galen](wikipedia://en/12326), [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great)), accompanied by the maxims and sayings attributed to them. The biographies are largely legendary and most attributions highly dubious.

## Influences {#Influences}

His *al-Mukhtar* was a great success in the centuries that followed, first in the Arab-Muslim world where it provided source material for later scholars, such as for Muhammad [al-Shahrastani](wikipedia://en/7790479) in his book *Kitab al-wa-l-Milal Nihal* and Shams [al-Din al-Shahrazuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Din_al-Shahrazuri) for his *[Nuzhat al-Arwah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuzhat_al-Arwah)*.

## Translations {#Translations}

**Spanish**

- *Los Bocados de Oro*; translated in the reign of [Alfonso X of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile) (1252–1284) was the earliest translation into a Western European vernacular.[^o8]

**Latin**

- *Liber Philosophorum Moralium Antiquorum* by the Italian [John of Procida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Procida) († 1298), friend and doctor of [Emperor Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Frederick_II). Several early Latin translations appeared as [florilegia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florilegia) and excerpts integrated into larger works.

**French**

- *Les Dits Moraulx des Philosophes* by Guillaume de Tignonville, chamberlain to [King Charles VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France); [Middle French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_French) from the Latin translation. Of the fifty manuscripts extant the oldest dates from 1402. The first printed editions were made in [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges) by [Colard Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colard_Mansion) (no date, perhaps 1477), in Paris by [Antoine Vérard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Vérard) in 1486, by [Jean Trepperel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Trepperel) in 1502, by [Galliot du Pré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galliot_du_Pré) in 1531, etc. (Nine reported editions by 1533).

**Occitan**

- *Los Dichs dels Philosophes* from the Tignonville's French translation.

**English**

- *[The Dicts or Sayings of the Philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dicts_or_Sayings_of_the_Philosophers)* (1450) by [Stephen Scrope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Scrope_%28writer%29) for his stepfather, [John Fastolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Fastolf); [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English) translation.[^o9][^o10]
- *[The Dictes or Sayengis of the Philosophhres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictes_and_Sayings_of_the_Philosophers)* (1473) by [Anthony Woodville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Woodville).[^o11] William Worcester amended Woodville's translation and it appears this was the version printed by [William Caxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Caxton) in his Westminster workshop on November 18, 1477, the first book printed in England, that is discounting [Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas)'s [Apostles' Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles'_Creed), (*Expositio in Symbolum Apostolorum*) printed December 17, 1468.
