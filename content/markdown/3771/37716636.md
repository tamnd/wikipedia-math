# Lelong number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lelong number"}

In mathematics, the **Lelong number** is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) of a point of a [complex analytic variety](wikipedia://en/18748850) that in some sense measures the local density at that point. It was introduced by (txt). More generally a closed positive (*p*,*p*) [current](wikipedia://en/3021207) *u* on a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) has a Lelong number *n*(*u*,*x*) for each point *x* of the manifold. Similarly a [plurisubharmonic function](wikipedia://en/2519085) also has a Lelong number at a point.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The Lelong number of a plurisubharmonic function $\phi$ at a point *x* of **C**<sup>*n*</sup> is

$$\liminf_{z\rightarrow x}\frac{\phi(z)}{\log |z-x|}.$$

For a point *x* of an analytic subset *A* of pure dimension *k*, the Lelong number ν(*A*,*x*) is the limit of the ratio of the areas of *A* ∩ *B*(*r*,*x*) and a ball of radius *r* in **C**<sup>*k*</sup> as the radius tends to zero. (Here *B*(*r*,*x*) is a ball of radius *r* centered at *x*.) In other words the Lelong number is a sort of measure of the local density of *A* near *x*. If *x* is not in the subvariety *A* the Lelong number is 0, and if *x* is a regular point the Lelong number is 1. It can be proved that the Lelong number ν(*A*,*x*) is always an integer.

:::
