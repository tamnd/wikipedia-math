# Walter Gottschalk

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Gottschalk"}

**Walter Helbig Gottschalk** (November 3, 1918 – February 15, 2004) was an American mathematician, one of the founders of [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gottschalk was born in [Lynchburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia), on November 3, 1918, and moved to [Salem, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Virginia) as a child.[^o3][^o4] His father, Carl Gottschalk, was a German immigrant who worked as a machinist and later owned several small businesses in Salem; his younger brother, [Carl W. Gottschalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_W._Gottschalk), became a notable medical researcher.[^o5]

Gottschalk did both his undergraduate studies and graduate studies at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), finishing with a Ph.D. in 1944 under the supervision of [Gustav A. Hedlund](wikipedia://en/18794778).[^o3][^o4] After graduating, he joined the faculty of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), and was chair of the Pennsylvania mathematics department from 1954 to 1958.[^o3][^o4][^o7] In the academic year 1947/1948 he was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o8] At Pennsylvania, his doctoral students included [Philip Rabinowitz](wikipedia://en/15046429), who became known for his work in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), and [Robert Ellis](wikipedia://en/58470189), who became known for his work on topological dynamics.[^o6] Gottschalk moved to [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) in 1963; at Wesleyan, he also served two terms as chair before retiring in 1982.[^o3][^o4] He died on February 15, 2004, in [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island), where he had lived since his retirement.[^o3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Gottschalk and his advisor Gustav Hedlund wrote the 1955 monograph *Topological Dynamics*.[^o3][^o10][^o11] Other research contributions of Gottschalk include the first study of [surjunctive groups](wikipedia://en/37698505)[^n1] and a short proof of the [De Bruijn–Erdős theorem](wikipedia://en/22728378) on [coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) infinite graphs.[^n2]

As well as being a research mathematician, Gottschalk also put on two exhibits of mathematical sculptures in the 1960s.[^o3]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Gottschalk was a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o3]
