# Order of accuracy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order of accuracy"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **order of accuracy** quantifies the [rate of convergence](wikipedia://en/999701) of a numerical approximation of a [differential equation](wikipedia://en/1424309) to the exact solution. Consider $u$, the exact solution to a differential equation in an appropriate [normed space](wikipedia://en/21538) $(V,||\ ||)$. Consider a numerical approximation $u_h$, where $h$ is a parameter characterizing the approximation, such as the step size in a finite difference scheme or the diameter of the cells in a [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581). The numerical solution $u_h$ is said to be **$\mathbf{n}$th-order accurate** if the error $E(h):= ||u-u_h||$ is proportional to the step-size $h$ to the $n$th power:[^o2]

:::

$$E(h) = ||u-u_h|| \leq Ch^n$$

where the constant $C$ is independent of $h$ and usually depends on the solution $u$.[^o3] Using the [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578) an $n$th-order accurate numerical method is notated as

$$||u-u_h|| = O(h^n)$$

This definition is strictly dependent on the norm used in the space; the choice of such norm is fundamental to estimate the rate of convergence and, in general, all numerical errors correctly.

The size of the error of a first-order accurate approximation is directly proportional to $h$. [Partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) which vary over both time and space are said to be accurate to order $n$ in time and to order $m$ in space.[^o4]
