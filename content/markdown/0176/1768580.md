# Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi"}

**Sharaf al-Dīn al-Muẓaffar ibn Muḥammad ibn al-Muẓaffar al-Ṭūsī** (شرف‌الدین مظفر بن محمد بن مظفر توسی; c. 1135 – c. 1213)[^o3] usually shortened to **Sharaf al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī**,[^o4] was a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia) [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy) of the [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age).[^n1][^n2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Al-Tusi was presumably born in the region of [Tus, Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tus,_Iran). Little is known about his life, except what is found in the biographies of other scientists[^n3] and that most mathematicians today can trace their lineage back to him.[^o9]

Around 1165, he moved to [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus) and taught mathematics there. He then lived in [Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo) for three years, before moving to [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul), where he met his most famous disciple [Kamal al-Din ibn Yunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_al-Din_ibn_Yunus) (1156-1242). Kamal al-Din would later become the teacher of another famous mathematician from Tus, [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424).[^n3]

According to [Ibn Abi Usaibi'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Abi_Usaibi'a), Sharaf al-Din was "outstanding in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and the mathematical sciences, having no equal in his time".[^n4]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Al-Tusi has been credited with proposing the idea of a function, however his approach being not very explicit, algebra's decisive move to the dynamic function was made 5 centuries after him, by German polymath Gottfried Leibniz.[^o13] Sharaf al-Din used what would later be known as the "[Ruffini](wikipedia://en/766014)-[Horner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horner_scheme) method" to [numerically](wikipedia://en/21506) approximate the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) of a [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/243709). He also developed a novel method for determining the conditions under which certain types of cubic equations would have two, one, or no solutions.[^n3] To al-Tusi, "solution" meant "positive solution", since the possibility of zero or negative numbers being considered genuine solutions had yet to be recognised at the time.[^n5][^n6][^n7] The equations in question can be written, using modern notation, in the form $f(x) = c$, where $f(x)$ is a cubic polynomial in which the [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) of the cubic term $x^{3}$ is $-1$, and $c$ is positive. The Muslim mathematicians of the time divided the potentially solvable cases of these equations into five different types, determined by the signs of the other coefficients of $f(x)$. For each of these five types, al-Tusi wrote down an expression $m$ for the point where the function $f(x)$ attained its [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima), and gave a geometric proof that $f(x) < f(m)$ for any positive $x$ different from $m$. He then concluded that the equation would have two solutions if $c < f(m)$, one solution if $c = f(m)$, or none if $f(m) < c$.[^n8]

Al-Tusi gave no indication of how he discovered the expressions $m$ for the maxima of the functions $f(x)$.[^n9] Some scholars have concluded that al-Tusi obtained his expressions for these maxima by "systematically" taking the derivative of the function $f(x)$, and setting it equal to zero.[^n10][^n11] This conclusion has been challenged, however, by others, who point out that al-Tusi nowhere wrote down an expression for the derivative, and suggest other plausible methods by which he could have discovered his expressions for the maxima.[^n12][^n13]

The quantities $D = f(m) - c$ which can be obtained from al-Tusi's conditions for the numbers of roots of cubic equations by subtracting one side of these conditions from the other is today called the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) of the cubic polynomials obtained by subtracting one side of the corresponding cubic equations from the other. Although al-Tusi always writes these conditions in the forms $c < f(m)$, $c = f(m)$, or $f(m) < c$, rather than the corresponding forms $D > 0$, $D = 0$, or $D < 0$,[^n13] [Roshdi Rashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshdi_Rashed) nevertheless considers that his discovery of these conditions demonstrated an understanding of the importance of the discriminant for investigating the solutions of cubic equations.[^n14]

Sharaf al-Din analyzed the equation *x*<sup>3</sup> + *d* = *b*⋅*x*<sup>2</sup> in the form *x*<sup>2</sup> ⋅ (*b* - *x*) = *d*, stating that the left hand side must at least equal the value of *d* for the equation to have a solution. He then determined the maximum value of this expression. A value less than *d* means no positive solution; a value equal to *d* corresponds to one solution, while a value greater than *d* corresponds to two solutions. Sharaf al-Din's analysis of this equation was a notable development in [Islamic mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics), but his work was not pursued any further at that time, neither in the Muslim or European world.[^o26]

Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi's "Treatise on equations" has been described by Roshdi Rashed as inaugurating the beginning of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^n15] This was criticized by Jeffrey Oaks who claims that Al-Tusi did not study curves by means of equations, but rather equations by means of curves (just as [al-Khayyam](wikipedia://en/92550) had done before him) and that the study of curves by means of equations originated with Descartes in the seventeenth century.[^o28][^o29]

## Astronomy {#Astronomy}

Sharaf al-Din invented a linear [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664), sometimes called the "Staff of Tusi". While it was easier to construct and was known in [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus), it did not gain much popularity.[^n4]

## Honours {#Honours}

The main-belt asteroid [7058 Al-Ṭūsī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7058_Al-Ṭūsī), discovered by [Henry E. Holt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_E._Holt) at [Palomar Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palomar_Observatory) in 1990, was named in his honor.[^o34]
