# Magic series

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magic series"}

A **magic series** is a set of distinct positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) which add up to the [magic constant](wikipedia://en/546969) of a [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) and a [magic cube](wikipedia://en/372356), thus potentially making up lines in [magic tesseracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_tesseract).

:::

So, in an *n* × *n* magic square using the numbers from 1 to *n*<sup>2</sup>, a magic series is a set of *n* distinct numbers adding up to *n*(*n*<sup>2</sup> + 1)/2. For *n* = 2, there are just two magic series, 1+4 and 2+3. The eight magic series when *n* = 3 all appear in the rows, columns and diagonals of a 3 × 3 magic square.

[Maurice Kraitchik](wikipedia://en/6730105) gave the number of magic series up to *n* = 7 in *Mathematical Recreations* in 1942 . In 2002, [Henry Bottomley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bottomley) extended this up to *n* = 36 and independently [Walter Trump](wikipedia://en/2885379) up to *n* = 32. In 2005, Trump extended this to *n* = 54 (over 2 × 10<sup>111</sup>) while Bottomley gave an experimental approximation for the numbers of magic series:

$$\frac{1}{\pi} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{3}{e}} \cdot \frac{(e n)^n}{n^3-\frac{3}{5}n^2+\frac{2}{7}n}$$

In July 2006, [Robert Gerbicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gerbicz) extended this sequence up to *n* = 150.

In 2013 [Dirk Kinnaes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Kinnaes) was able to exploit his insight that the magic series could be related to the volume of a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471). Trump used this new approach to extend the sequence up to *n* = 1000.[^o2]

[Mike Quist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Quist) showed that the exact second-order count has a multiplicative factor of $\tfrac{1}{n^3}\!\left(1+\tfrac{3}{5n}+\tfrac{31}{420n^2}+\cdots\right)$ equivalent to a denominator of $n^3-\tfrac{3}{5}n^2+\left(\tfrac{2}{7} + \tfrac{1}{2100} \right)\!n+ \cdots.$[^o3]

[Richard Schroeppel](wikipedia://en/1649527) in 1973 published the complete enumeration of the order 5 magic squares at 275,305,224. This recent magic series work gives hope that the relationship between the magic series and the magic square may provide an exact count for order 6 or order 7 magic squares. Consider an intermediate structure that lies in complexity between the magic series and the magic square. It might be described as an amalgamation of 4 magic series that have only one unique common integer. This structure forms the two major diagonals and the central row and column for an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) order magic square. Building blocks such as these could be the way forward.
