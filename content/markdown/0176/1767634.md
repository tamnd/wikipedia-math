# Egorov's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Egorov's theorem"}

In [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), an area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Egorov's theorem** establishes a condition for the [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) of a [pointwise convergent](wikipedia://en/340198) [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055). It is also named **Severini–Egoroff theorem** or **Severini–Egorov theorem**, after [Carlo Severini](wikipedia://en/26527105), an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), and [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov), a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician and [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer), who published independent proofs respectively in 1910 and 1911.

:::

Egorov's theorem can be used along with [compactly supported](wikipedia://en/381013) [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) to prove [Lusin's theorem](wikipedia://en/2257262) for [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function).

## Historical note {#Historical_note}

The first proof of the theorem was given by [Carlo Severini](wikipedia://en/26527105) in 1910:[^o3][^o4] he used the result as a tool in his research on [series](wikipedia://en/15287) of [orthogonal functions](wikipedia://en/453041). His work remained apparently unnoticed outside [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), probably due to the fact that it is written in [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language), appeared in a scientific journal with limited diffusion and was considered only as a means to obtain other theorems. A year later [Dmitri Egorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Egorov) published his independently proved results,[^o5] and the theorem became widely known under his name: however, it is not uncommon to find references to this theorem as the Severini–Egoroff theorem. The first mathematicians to prove independently the theorem in the nowadays common abstract [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) setting were (txt), and in (txt):[^o8] an earlier generalization is due to [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228), who succeeded in slightly relaxing the requirement of finiteness of measure of the [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of convergence of the [pointwise converging functions](wikipedia://en/340198) in the long (242 pages) paper (Luzin 1916).[^o10] Further generalizations were given much later by [Pavel Korovkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Korovkin), in the paper (Korovkin 1947), and by [Gabriel Mokobodzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Mokobodzki) in the paper (Mokobodzki 1970): in particular Korovkin extended the result to a class of non–negative [set functions](wikipedia://en/25911217) more general than measures.[^o13]

## Formal statement and proof {#Formal_statement_and_proof}

::: {#o16 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of $M$-valued measurable functions, where $M$ is a separable metric space, on some [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) $(X,\sigma,\mu)$, and suppose there is a [measurable subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set) $A \subseteq X$, with finite $\mu$-measure, such that $(f_n)$ [converges](wikipedia://en/285773) $\mu$-[almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) on $A$ to a limit function $f$. The following result holds: for every $\varepsilon > 0$, there exists a measurable [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $B$ of $A$ such that $\mu (B)<\varepsilon$, and $(f_n)$ [converges to $f$ uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on $A\setminus B$.

Here, $\mu (B)$ denotes the $\mu$-measure of $B$. In words, the theorem says that pointwise convergence almost everywhere on $A$ implies the apparently much stronger uniform convergence everywhere except on some subset $B$ of arbitrarily small measure. This type of convergence is called *almost uniform convergence*.

:::

### Discussion of assumptions and a counterexample {#Discussion_of_assumptions_and_a_counterexample}

- The hypothesis $\mu (A)<\infty$ is necessary. To see this, it is simple to construct a counterexample when μ is the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198): consider the sequence of real-valued [indicator functions](wikipedia://en/240790) $$f_n(x) = 1_{[n,n+1]}(x), \qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\ x\in\mathbb{R},$$ defined on the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line). This sequence converges pointwise to the zero function everywhere but does not converge uniformly on $\mathbb{R}\setminus B$ for any set $B$ of finite measure: a counterexample in the general [$n$-dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [real vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_vector_space) $\mathbb{R}^n$ can be constructed as shown by (Cafiero 1959, 302).
- The separability of the metric space is needed to make sure that for $M$-valued, measurable functions $f$ and $g$, the distance $d(f(x), g(x))$ is again a measurable real-valued function of $x$.

::: {#o20 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Fix $\varepsilon > 0$. For natural numbers *n* and *k*, define the set *E<sub>n,k</sub>* by the [union](wikipedia://en/47949)

$$E_{n,k} = \bigcup_{m\ge n} \left\{ x\in A \,\Big|\, |f_m(x) - f(x)| \ge \frac1k \right\}.$$

These sets get smaller as *n* increases, meaning that *E*<sub>*n*+1,*k*</sub> is always a subset of *E<sub>n,k</sub>*, because the first union involves fewer sets. A point *x*, for which the sequence (*f<sub>m</sub>*(*x*)) converges to *f*(*x*), cannot be in every *E<sub>n,k</sub>* for a fixed *k*, because *f<sub>m</sub>*(*x*) has to stay closer to *f*(*x*) than 1/*k* eventually. Hence by the assumption of μ-almost everywhere pointwise convergence on *A*,

$$\mu\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}E_{n,k}\right)=0$$

for every *k*. Since *A* is of finite measure, we have [continuity from above](wikipedia://en/19873#Continuity_from_above); hence there exists, for each *k*, some natural number *n<sub>k</sub>* such that

$$\mu(E_{n_k,k}) < \frac\varepsilon{2^k}.$$

For *x* in this set we consider the speed of approach into the 1/*k*-[neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of *f*(*x*) as too slow. Define

$$B = \bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}} E_{n_k,k}$$

as the set of all those points *x* in *A*, for which the speed of approach into *at least one* of these 1/*k*-neighbourhoods of *f*(*x*) is too slow. On the set difference $A\setminus B$ we therefore have uniform convergence. Explicitly, for any $\epsilon$, let $\frac 1 k < \epsilon$, then for any $n > n_k$, we have $|f_n - f| < \epsilon$ on all of $A\setminus B$.

Appealing to the [sigma additivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_additivity) of μ and using the [geometric series](wikipedia://en/12630), we get

$$\mu(B) \le \sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}} \mu(E_{n_k,k}) < \sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac\varepsilon{2^k}=\varepsilon.$$

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

### Luzin's version {#Luzin's_version}

[Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228)'s generalization of the Severini–Egorov theorem is presented here according to (Saks 1937, 19).

::: {#o26 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

#### Statement {#Statement}

Under the same hypothesis of the abstract Severini–Egorov theorem suppose that *A* is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [measurable sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set) of finite μ-measure, and (*f<sub>n</sub>*) is a given sequence of *M*-valued measurable functions on some [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) (*X*,Σ,μ), such that (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>) [converges](wikipedia://en/285773) μ-[almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) on *A* to a limit function *f*, then *A* can be expressed as the union of a sequence of measurable sets *H*, *A<sub>1</sub>*, *A<sub>2</sub>*,... such that μ(*H*) = 0 and (*f<sub>n</sub>*) converges to *f* uniformly on each set *A<sub>k</sub>*.

:::

::: {#o28 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

#### Proof {#Proof}

It is sufficient to consider the case in which the set *A* is itself of finite μ-measure: using this hypothesis and the standard Severini–Egorov theorem, it is possible to define by [mathematical induction](wikipedia://en/18881) a sequence of sets {*A<sub>k</sub>*}<sub>k=1,2,...</sub> such that

$$\mu\left (A \setminus \bigcup_{k=1}^{N} A_k \right)\leq\frac{1}{N}$$

and such that (*f<sub>n</sub>*) converges to *f* uniformly on each set *A<sub>k</sub>* for each *k*. Choosing

$$H=A\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty} A_k$$

then obviously μ(*H*) = 0 and the theorem is proved.

:::

### Korovkin's version {#Korovkin's_version}

The proof of the Korovkin version follows closely the version on (Kharazishvili 2000, 183–184), which however generalizes it to some extent by considering [admissible functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admissible_functional) instead of [non-negative measures](wikipedia://en/19873) and [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) $\leq$ and $\geq$ respectively in conditions 1 and 2.

::: {#o32 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

#### Statement {#Statement}

Let $(M,d)$ denote a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) and $(X,\Sigma )$ a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333): consider a [measurable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set) $A$ and a [class](wikipedia://en/26691) $\mathfrak{A}$ containing $A$ and its measurable [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) such that their [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) in [unions](wikipedia://en/47949) and [intersections](wikipedia://en/23476429) belong to the same class. Suppose there exists a [non-negative measure](wikipedia://en/19873) $\mu$ such that $\mu(A)$ exists and

1. $\mu(\cap A_n) = \lim \mu(A_n)$ if $A_1 \supset A_2 \supset \cdots$ with $A_n\in\mathfrak{A}$ for all $n$
1. $\mu(\cup A_n) = \lim \mu(A_n)$ if $A_1 \subset A_2 \subset \cdots$ with $\cup A_n\in\mathfrak{A}$.

If $f_n$ is a sequence of $M$-valued measurable functions [converging](wikipedia://en/285773) $\mu$-[almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) on $A\in\mathfrak{A}$ to a limit function $f$, then there exists a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $A^\prime$ of $A$ such that $0<\mu (A)-\mu(A^\prime)<\varepsilon$0 and where the convergence is also uniform.

:::

::: {#o34 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

#### Proof {#Proof}

Consider the [indexed family of sets](wikipedia://en/23624339) whose [index set](wikipedia://en/393055) is the set of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) $m\in\mathbb{N},$ defined as follows:

$$A_{0,m}=\left\{x\in A|d(f_n(x),f(x)) \le 1\ \forall n\geq m\right\}$$

Obviously

$$A_{0,1}\subseteq A_{0,2}\subseteq A_{0,3}\subseteq\dots$$

and

$$A=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}A_{0,m}$$

therefore there is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *m<sub>0</sub>* such that putting *A<sub>0,m<sub>0</sub></sub>*=*A<sub>0</sub>* the following relation holds true:

$$0\leq\mu(A)-\mu(A_0)\leq\varepsilon$$

Using *A<sub>0</sub>* it is possible to define the following indexed family

$$A_{1,m}=\left\{x\in A_0\left| d(f_m(x),f(x)) \le \frac12 \ \forall n\geq m\right.\right\}$$

satisfying the following two relationships, analogous to the previously found ones, i.e.

$$A_{1,1}\subseteq A_{1,2}\subseteq A_{1,3}\subseteq\dots$$

and

$$A_0=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}A_{1,m}$$

This fact enable us to define the set *A<sub>1,m<sub>1</sub></sub>*=*A<sub>1</sub>*, where *m<sub>1</sub>* is a surely existing natural number such that

$$0\leq\mu(A)-\mu(A_1)\leq\varepsilon$$

By iterating the shown construction, another indexed family of set {*A<sub>n</sub>*} is defined such that it has the following properties:

- $$A_0\supseteq A_1\supseteq A_2\supseteq\cdots$$
- $0\leq\mu(A)-\mu(A_m)\leq\varepsilon$ for all $m\in\mathbb{N}$
- for each $m\in\mathbb{N}$ there exists *k<sub>m</sub>* such that for all $n \geq k_m$ then $d(f_n(x),f(x)) \le 2^{-m}$ for all $x \in A_m$

and finally putting

$$A'=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_n$$

the thesis is easily proved.

:::

:::
