# Fraction of variance unexplained

Explained variation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fraction of variance unexplained"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **fraction of variance unexplained** (**FVU**) in the context of a [regression task](wikipedia://en/826997) is the fraction of variance of the [regressand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regressand) (dependent variable) *Y* which cannot be explained, i.e., which is not correctly predicted, by the [explanatory variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explanatory_variable) *X*.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Suppose we are given a regression function $f$ yielding for each $y_i$ an estimate $\widehat{y}_i = f(x_i)$ where $x_i$ is the vector of the *i*<sup>th</sup> observations on all the explanatory variables.[^o4] We define the fraction of variance unexplained (FVU) as:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{FVU} & = {\text{VAR}_\text{err} \over \text{VAR}_\text{tot}} = {\text{SS}_\text{err}/N \over \text{SS}_\text{tot}/N} = {\text{SS}_\text{err} \over \text{SS}_\text{tot}} \left( = 1-{\text{SS}_\text{reg} \over \text{SS}_\text{tot}} , \text{ only true in some cases such as linear regression}\right) \\[6pt] & = 1 - R^2 \end{aligned}$$

where *R*<sup>2</sup> is the [coefficient of determination](wikipedia://en/1500869) and *VAR*<sub>err</sub> and *VAR*<sub>tot</sub> are the variance of the residuals and the sample variance of the dependent variable. *SS*<sub>*err*</sub> (the sum of squared predictions errors, equivalently the [residual sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473303)), *SS*<sub>*tot*</sub> (the [total sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473449)), and *SS*<sub>*reg*</sub> (the sum of squares of the regression, equivalently the [explained sum of squares](wikipedia://en/2473431)) are given by

$$\begin{aligned} \text{SS}_\text{err} & = \sum_{i=1}^N\;(y_i - \widehat{y}_i)^2\\ \text{SS}_\text{tot} & = \sum_{i=1}^N\;(y_i-\bar{y})^2 \\ \text{SS}_\text{reg} & = \sum_{i=1}^N\;(\widehat{y}_i-\bar{y})^2 \text{ and} \\ \bar{y} & = \frac 1 N \sum_{i=1}^N\;y_i. \end{aligned}$$

Alternatively, the fraction of variance unexplained can be defined as follows:

$$\text{FVU} = \frac{\operatorname{MSE}(f)}{\operatorname{var}[Y]}$$

where MSE(*f*) is the [mean squared error](wikipedia://en/201816) of the regression function *ƒ*.

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

It is useful to consider the second definition to understand FVU. When trying to predict *Y*, the most naive regression function that we can think of is the constant function predicting the mean of *Y*, i.e., $f(x_i)=\bar{y}$. It follows that the MSE of this function equals the variance of *Y*; that is, *SS*<sub>err</sub> = *SS*<sub>tot</sub>, and *SS*<sub>reg</sub> = 0. In this case, no variation in *Y* can be accounted for, and the FVU then has its maximum value of 1.

More generally, the FVU will be 1 if the explanatory variables *X* tell us nothing about *Y* in the sense that the predicted values of *Y* do not [covary](wikipedia://en/157059) with *Y*. But as prediction gets better and the MSE can be reduced, the FVU goes down. In the case of perfect prediction where $\hat{y}_i = y_i$ for all *i*, the MSE is 0, *SS*<sub>err</sub> = 0, *SS*<sub>reg</sub> = *SS*<sub>tot</sub>, and the FVU is 0.
