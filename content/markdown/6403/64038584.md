# Saturated family

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Saturated family"}

In mathematics, specifically in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a [family](wikipedia://en/723043) $\mathcal{G}$ of subsets a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) $X$ is said to be **saturated** if $\mathcal{G}$ contains a non-empty subset of $X$ and if for every $G \in \mathcal{G},$ the following conditions all hold:

:::

1. $\mathcal{G}$ contains every subset of $G$;
1. the union of any finite collection of elements of $\mathcal{G}$ is an element of $\mathcal{G}$;
1. for every scalar $a,$ $\mathcal{G}$ contains $aG$;
1. the closed [convex balanced hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_balanced_hull) of $G$ belongs to $\mathcal{G}.$[^n1]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

If $\mathcal{S}$ is any collection of subsets of $X$ then the smallest saturated family containing $\mathcal{S}$ is called the saturated hull of $\mathcal{S}.$[^n1]

The family $\mathcal{G}$ is said to cover $X$ if the union $\bigcup_{G \in \mathcal{G}} G$ is equal to $X$; it is total if the linear span of this set is a dense subset of $X.$[^n1]

:::

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The intersection of an arbitrary family of saturated families is a saturated family.[^n1] Since the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $X$ is saturated, any given non-empty family $\mathcal{G}$ of subsets of $X$ containing at least one non-empty set, the saturated hull of $\mathcal{G}$ is well-defined.[^n2] Note that a saturated family of subsets of $X$ that covers $X$ is a [bornology](wikipedia://en/9584635) on $X.$

The set of all [bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_bounded) subsets of a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) is a saturated family.

:::
