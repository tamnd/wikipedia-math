# Noncommutative torus

Irrationale Rotationsalgebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noncommutative torus"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically in the theory of [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184), the **noncommutative tori** *A*<sub>θ</sub>, also known as **irrational rotation algebras** for [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) values of θ, form a family of noncommutative C\*-algebras which generalize the [algebra of continuous functions](wikipedia://en/7184#Commutative_C.2A-algebras) on the [2-torus](wikipedia://en/74800). Many topological and geometric properties of the classical 2-torus have algebraic analogues for the noncommutative tori, and as such they are fundamental examples of a [noncommutative space](wikipedia://en/295917) in the sense of [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For any irrational real number *θ*, the noncommutative torus $A_\theta$ is the C\*-subalgebra of $B(L^2(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}))$, the algebra of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) on [square-integrable functions](wikipedia://en/28680558) on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) $S^1 \subset \mathbb{C}$, generated by two [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) operators $U, V$ defined as

> $$\begin{aligned} U(f)(z) &= z f(z) \\ V(f)(z) &= f(ze^{-2\pi i \theta}). \end{aligned}$$

A quick calculation shows that *VU* = *e*<sup>−2π *i* θ</sup>*UV*.[^o4]

:::

## Alternative characterizations {#Alternative_characterizations}

- **Universal property:** *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> can be defined (up to isomorphism) as the [universal C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/668934) generated by two unitary elements *U* and *V* satisfying the relation *VU* = e<sup>2π *i* θ</sup>*UV*.[^o4] This definition extends to the case when *θ* is rational. In particular when *θ* = 0, *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> is isomorphic to continuous functions on the [2-torus](wikipedia://en/74800) by the [Gelfand transform](wikipedia://en/364372#The_C.2A-algebra_case).
- **Irrational rotation algebra:** Let the infinite cyclic group **Z** act on the circle **S**<sup>1</sup> by the [rotation action](wikipedia://en/9445837) by angle 2$\pi$*iθ*. This induces an action of **Z** by automorphisms on the algebra of continuous functions *C*(**S**<sup>1</sup>). The resulting C\*-[crossed product](wikipedia://en/2749495) *C*(**S**<sup>1</sup>) ⋊ **Z** is isomorphic to *A*<sub>*θ*</sub>. The generating unitaries are the generator of the group **Z** and the identity function on the circle *z* : **S**<sup>1</sup> → **C**.[^o4]
- **Twisted group algebra:** The function σ : **Z**<sup>2</sup> × **Z**<sup>2</sup> → **C**; σ((*m*,*n*), (*p*,*q*)) = *e*<sup>2π*inpθ*</sup> is a [group 2-cocycle](wikipedia://en/248635) on **Z**<sup>2</sup>, and the corresponding twisted [group algebra](wikipedia://en/381010) *C\**(**Z**<sup>2</sup>; *σ*) is isomorphic to *A*<sub>*θ*</sub>.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every irrational rotation algebra *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> is simple, that is, it does not contain any proper closed two-sided ideals other than $\{0\}$ and itself.[^o4]
- Every irrational rotation algebra has a unique [tracial state](wikipedia://en/454959#Some_important_classes_of_states).[^o4]
- The irrational rotation algebras are [nuclear](wikipedia://en/25351068).

:::

## Classification and K-theory {#Classification_and_K-theory}

The [K-theory](wikipedia://en/13314265) of *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> is **Z**<sup>2</sup> in both even dimension and odd dimension, and so does not distinguish the irrational rotation algebras. But as an [ordered group](wikipedia://en/172048), *K*<sub>0</sub> ≃ **Z** + *θ***Z**. Therefore, two noncommutative tori *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> and *A*<sub>*η*</sub> are isomorphic if and only if either *θ* + *η* or *θ* − *η* is an integer.[^o4][^o9]

Two irrational rotation algebras *A*<sub>*θ*</sub> and *A*<sub>*η*</sub> are [strongly Morita equivalent](wikipedia://en/3296134#Further_directions) if and only if *θ* and *η* are in the same orbit of the action of SL(2, **Z**) on **R** by [linear fractional transformations](wikipedia://en/401769). In particular, the noncommutative tori with θ rational are Morita equivalent to the classical torus. On the other hand, the noncommutative tori with θ irrational are simple C\*-algebras.[^o9]
