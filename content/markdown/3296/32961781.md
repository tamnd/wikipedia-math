# Category O

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category O"}

In the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [semisimple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587), **Category O** (or **category $\mathcal{O}$**) is a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose objects are certain [representations](wikipedia://en/318712) of a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587), and whose morphisms are homomorphisms of representations.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

Assume that $\mathfrak{g}$ is a (usually [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)) semisimple Lie algebra with a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) $\mathfrak{h}$. Let $\Phi$ be its [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) and let $\Phi^+$ be a choice of [positive roots](wikipedia://en/277087#Positive_roots_and_simple_roots). Denote by $\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$ the [root space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_space) corresponding to a root $\alpha\in\Phi$, and set $\mathfrak{n}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, a [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/3358867) subalgebra.

If $M$ is a $\mathfrak{g}$-module and $\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^*$, then the $\lambda$-[weight space](wikipedia://en/277179#Weight_space_of_a_representation) of $M$ is

$$M_\lambda=\{v\in M:\forall h\in\mathfrak{h},\; h\cdot v=\lambda(h)v\}.$$

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition of category O"}

## Definition of category O {#Definition_of_category_O}

The objects of category $\mathcal O$ are $\mathfrak{g}$-modules $M$ such that:

1. $M$ is finitely generated;
1. $$M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^*} M_\lambda;$$
1. $M$ is locally $\mathfrak{n}$-finite, i.e. for each $v\in M$, the $\mathfrak{n}$-submodule generated by $v$ is finite-dimensional.

Morphisms in this category are the $\mathfrak{g}$-module homomorphisms.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Basic properties"}

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

- Each module in category $\mathcal O$ has finite-dimensional weight spaces.
- Each module in category $\mathcal O$ is a [Noetherian module](wikipedia://en/542431).
- $\mathcal O$ is an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063).
- $\mathcal O$ has [enough projectives](wikipedia://en/3017382) and [enough injectives](wikipedia://en/473779).
- $\mathcal O$ is closed under taking [submodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule), quotients, and finite direct sums.
- Objects in $\mathcal O$ are $Z(\mathfrak{g})$-finite: if $M$ is an object and $v\in M$, then the subspace $Z(\mathfrak{g})v\subseteq M$ generated by $v$ under the action of the [center](wikipedia://en/342453) of the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) is finite-dimensional.

:::

## Koszul duality {#Koszul_duality}

A homological feature of category $\mathcal O$ is that, after choosing graded lifts of blocks, certain blocks can be described by [Koszul algebras](wikipedia://en/7987653). In particular, Beilinson, Ginzburg, and Soergel showed that (for suitable graded realizations) the endomorphism algebra of a projective generator of a block (notably the principal block) is a Koszul algebra $A$.[^o8] Equivalently, the corresponding graded block of $\mathcal O$ is (via a projective generator) equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional graded modules over $A$.

In this setting, Koszul duality relates two graded blocks: one block is equivalent to $\mathrm{gr}\text{-}A$, while a second (dual) graded block is equivalent to $\mathrm{gr}\text{-}A^{!}$, where $A^{!}$ is the Koszul dual algebra of $A$.[^o8] The associated Koszul duality functors induce a triangulated equivalence between the bounded derived categories of these graded realizations, i.e. an equivalence of the form

$$D^{b}(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{block}}^{\mathrm{gr}})\;\simeq\;D^{b}(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{block}^\vee}^{\mathrm{gr}}),$$

where $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{block}}^{\mathrm{gr}}\simeq \mathrm{gr}\text{-}A$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{block}^\vee}^{\mathrm{gr}}\simeq \mathrm{gr}\text{-}A^{!}$.[^o8]

Koszul duality for category $\mathcal O$ is closely connected with geometric and combinatorial structures such as the geometry of the flag variety, [perverse sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_sheaves), and [Kazhdan–Lusztig theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazhdan–Lusztig_theory).[^o9]

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- All finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{g}$-modules and their $\mathfrak{g}$-homomorphisms are in category $\mathcal O$.
- [Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234) and [generalized Verma modules](wikipedia://en/17114413) and their $\mathfrak{g}$-homomorphisms are in category $\mathcal O$.

:::
