# Radha Charan Gupta

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Radha Charan Gupta"}

**Radha Charan Gupta** (14 August 1935 – 5 September 2024) was an Indian [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), specialising in the history of [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Gupta was born on 14 August 1935 in [Jhansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhansi), in what is now [Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh). He attended secondary school in Jhansi, but there were few opportunities locally to pursue higher education, so he continued his education at the [University of Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lucknow) with a merit scholarship. He married Savitri Devi in 1953, urged by his family to marry young so the [dowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowry) received could help pay his sister's dowry. He completed his bachelor's degree in 1955 and his master's degree in 1957.[^o4]

To support his family, Gupta took a teaching job instead of immediately continuing doctoral studies. He was a lecturer at [Lucknow Christian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow_Christian_College) from 1957 to 1958. In 1958 he joined [Birla Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birla_Institute_of_Technology) (BIT) in [Ranchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchi).

In 1963 he read [Datta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibhutibhushan_Datta) and [Singh](wikipedia://en/74502719)'s *[History of Hindu Mathematics](wikipedia://en/49794476)*, became interested in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), and got to know the mathematical historian [T. A. Sarasvati Amma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._A._Sarasvati_Amma). She later supervised his Ph.D. at [Ranchi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchi_University), which he finished in 1971 with the dissertation *Trigonometry in Ancient and Medieval India*.[^o5]

Starting in 1979, he was in charge of BIT's Research Center for the History of Science.[^o6] In 1982 he was awarded a full professorship at BIT, where he continued teaching until mandatory retirement in 1995. After retiring, Gupta continued his research in mathematical history.[^o7]

Gupta died in Jhansi on 5 September 2024, at the age of 89.[^o8]

## Works {#Works}

In 1969 Gupta addressed[^o10] [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) in [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052). He wrote on [Govindasvamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govindasvamin) and his interpolation of [sine tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine_tables). Furthermore, he contributed an article on the work of [Paramesvara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramesvara): "Paramesvara's rule for the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) of a [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131)".[^o11]

In 2019, a collection of Gupta's papers was published as a book:

- Gupta, Radha Charan. (2019). "Gaṇitānanda: Selected Works of Radha Charan Gupta on History of Mathematics". Springer. doi:10.1007/978-981-13-1229-8. isbn:978-981-13-1228-1

## Awards and other professional activities {#Awards_and_other_professional_activities}

Gupta was one of the founders of the [Indian Society for History of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Society_for_History_of_Mathematics), and in 1979 was the founding editor of its journal, *[Gaṇita Bhāratī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaṇita_Bhāratī)*, which he edited for over 25 years.[^o14]

In 1991 he was elected a Fellow of the [National Academy of Sciences, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences,_India), and in 1994 he became President of the Association of Mathematics Teachers of India.[^o5] He became a corresponding member of the [International Academy of the History of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_the_History_of_Science) in February 1995.[^o15]

In 2009 he was awarded the [Kenneth O. May Prize](wikipedia://en/25758309) alongside the British mathematician [Ivor Grattan-Guinness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Grattan-Guinness).[^o16] He was the first Indian to receive this prize.[^o17]

In 2023, he was awarded the [Padma Shri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Shri) by the [Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India) for his contributions in the field of literature and education.[^o18]
