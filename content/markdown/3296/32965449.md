# Pseudo-polyomino

*Image: Tetrakings.png.* The 22 free tetrakings

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudo-polyomino"}

A **pseudo-polyomino**, also called a **polyking**, **polyplet** or **hinged polyomino**, is a [plane geometric figure](wikipedia://en/169191) formed by joining one or more equal [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) edge-to-edge or corner-to-corner at 90°. It is a [polyform](wikipedia://en/691765) with [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) cells. The [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634) are a subset of the polykings.

:::

The name "polyking" refers to the [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_%28chess%29) in [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess). The *n*-kings are the *n*-square shapes which could be occupied by a king on an infinite chessboard in the course of legal moves.

[Golomb](wikipedia://en/1860707) uses the term *pseudo-polyomino* referring to kingwise-connected sets of squares.[^o7]

### Free, one-sided, and fixed polykings {#Free,_one-sided,_and_fixed_polykings}

There are three common ways of distinguishing polyominoes and polykings for enumeration:[^o7]

- *free* polykings are distinct when none is a rigid transformation ([translation](wikipedia://en/204682), [rotation](wikipedia://en/39789), [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829) or [glide reflection](wikipedia://en/412984)) of another (pieces that can be picked up and flipped over).
- *one-sided* polykings are distinct when none is a translation or rotation of another (pieces that cannot be flipped over).
- *fixed* polykings are distinct when none is a translation of another (pieces that can be neither flipped nor rotated).
