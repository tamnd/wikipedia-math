# Jacques Frédéric Français

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacques Frédéric Français"}

**Jacques Frédéric Français**(20 June 1775 – 9 March 1833) was a French engineer and mathematician.[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born on 20 June 1775, Jacques Frédéric Français was the son of a grocer of [Saverne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saverne). He attended the Royal College of Strasbourg and enrolled voluntarily in the [Army of the Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Rhine) in 1793. In September 1794 he was transferred to the military engineering corps. Admitted to the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in the autumn of 1797, he moved in the spring of 1798 to the École du Génie de Metz and graduated with the brevet of first lieutenant. It was at this time that he wrote about the integration of differential equations. In January 1801 he took part in the [Egyptian Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Campaign) of the French Army. On his return in 1801 he was assigned to the port of [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon). Named captain of the sappers in December 1801, he became second in command of the headquarters of the corps of engineers in November 1802.[^o5]

Under the orders of Admiral [Pierre-Charles de Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Charles_Villeneuve), he took part in the naval battles of [Cape Finisterre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cape_Finisterre_%281805%29) and [Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar). In 1807, he was transferred to the regiment of the Colonel [Étienne Louis Malus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne-Louis_Malus) in Strasbourg. He became in 1811 professor of military art at the École d’Application du Génie et de l’Artillerie in [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz) until his death in 1833.[^o2][^o5]

His mathematical work focused on the study of the changes of Cartesian coordinates applied to systems of planes and lines[^o6] and the geometric representation of [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) based on original ideas of his uncle [Louis François Antoine Arbogast](wikipedia://en/91156). They have been published for the most part in the *[Annales de Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annales_de_Gergonne)*. After the death of his brother [François](wikipedia://en/59496858);in 1810, he published his treatise *Recherches sur la poussée des terres* (1817).[^o2]
