# Samuel Marolois

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel Marolois"}

*Image: Marolois, Samuel – Opera mathematica, 1633 – BEIC 195898.jpg.* Title page of Marolois' *Opera mathematica*, 1633

**Samuel Marolois** (1572 – before 1627) was a Dutch mathematician and military engineer who is best known for his work on [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Marolois (or Marlois) was born 1572 in the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) (possibly in [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague)) as son of Nicolas Marolois, a Protestant native of [Valenciennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenciennes) who had been exiled from France and served the [Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Orange).[^n1][^n2][^n3] Marolois became a mathematician and earthworks engineer in the employ of [Maurice, Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice,_Prince_of_Orange)[^n2][^n3] He was married to Hester le Maire, which made him a brother-in-law of the Amsterdam merchants Thomas le Maire and Pieter le Fevre. In March 1611, he bought a house in The Hague.[^n1] After the death of [Ludolph van Ceulen](wikipedia://en/362612), Marolois attempted unsuccessfully to succeed him as Chair of Mathematics in Leiden.[^n1][^n2]

Marolois wrote a book on [perspective](wikipedia://en/380405), La perspective contenant la theorie et la practique d'icelle, which was published in 1614 and printed many times in other languages including Dutch, German and Latin.[^n3] The book had both theoretical and practical elements.[^n4] The theoretical parts were mostly taken from the works of [Guidobaldo del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_del_Monte), while the practical parts included many examples. In total, 275 figures are printed in the book.[^n5] While Marolois' work contributed little to the mathematical theory of perspective, his book was influential in spreading awareness of the ideas. The artist [Joshua Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Kirby) later claimed it was one of the most important early books on perspective.[^n6]

Marolois was a military adviser to the Dutch Republic 1612–1619. His Fortification ou architecture militaire described the cheapest way to build fortifications.[^n7] It was the first systematic treatment of the Dutch system of fortifications, using geometric operations to draw polygonal plans, and is famous for the drawing of the citadel of [Coevorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevorden).[^n2]

Marolois died in The Hague before 1627.[^n1][^n2] [Albert Girard](wikipedia://en/3615216) prepared an edition of his mathematical works that was published in 1628.[^n8]
