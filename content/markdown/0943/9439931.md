# Claus P. Schnorr

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Claus P. Schnorr"}

**Claus Peter Schnorr** (4 August 1943 – 8 June 2025) was a German mathematician and [cryptographer](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Schnorr received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [Saarland University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_University) in 1966, and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1970. Schnorr's contributions to cryptography include his study of [Schnorr groups](wikipedia://en/1190979), which are used in the [digital signature algorithm](wikipedia://en/450536) bearing his name. Besides this, Schnorr is known for his contributions to [algorithmic information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_information_theory) and for creating an approach to the definition of an [algorithmically random sequence](wikipedia://en/4257548) which is alternative to the concept of Martin-Löf randomness.[^o4]

Schnorr was a professor of mathematics and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at the [Johann Wolfgang Goethe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_Goethe_University_of_Frankfurt_am_Main) at [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt). He retired in 2011 after working there for 40 years. He was also a Distinguished Associate of [RSA Laboratories](wikipedia://en/301240), and a joint recipient of the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize) together with [Johannes Buchmann](wikipedia://en/62210304) in 1993. He received, with [Jean-Jacques Quisquater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Quisquater), the [RSA Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics) in 2013.

Schnorr held a patent on [Schnorr signatures](wikipedia://en/450536) until February 2010.[^o5] He died on 8 June 2025, at the age of 81.[^o6]
