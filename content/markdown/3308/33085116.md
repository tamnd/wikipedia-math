# Radical extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radical extension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and more specifically in [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), a **radical extension** of a field $K$ is a field extension obtained by a tower of field extensions, each generated by adjoining an nth root of an element from the previous field.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **simple radical extension** is a [simple extension](wikipedia://en/4771726) *F*/*K* generated by a single element $\alpha$ satisfying $\alpha^n = b$ for an element *b* of *K*. In [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*, we also take an extension by a root of an [Artin–Schreier polynomial](wikipedia://en/2672057) to be a simple radical extension. A **radical series** is a [tower](wikipedia://en/28194169) $K = F_0 < F_1 < \cdots < F_k$ where each extension $F_i / F_{i-1}$ is a simple radical extension. In this case, the field extension $F_k/K$ is called a **radical extension**.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

1. If *E* is a radical extension of *F* and *F* is a radical extension of *K,* then *E* is a radical extension of *K*.
1. If *E* and *F* are radical extensions of *K* in an [extension field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_field) *C* of *K*, then the [compositum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositum) *EF* (the smallest subfield of *C* that contains both *E* and *F*) is a radical extension of *K*.
1. If *E* is a radical extension of *F* and *E* > *K* > *F* then *E* is a radical extension of *K*.

:::

## Solvability by radicals {#Solvability_by_radicals}

Radical extensions occur naturally when solving [polynomial equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation) in [radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_solution). In fact a [solution in radicals](wikipedia://en/1601833) is the expression of the solution as an element of a radical series: a polynomial *f* over a field *K* is said to be solvable by radicals if there is a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) of *f* over *K* contained in a radical extension of *K*.

The [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518) states that such a solution by radicals does not exist, in general, for equations of degree at least five. [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) showed that an equation is solvable in radicals if and only if its [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012). The proof is based on the [fundamental theorem of Galois theory](wikipedia://en/1947635) and the following theorem.

Let *K* be a field containing *n* distinct [$n$th roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity). An extension of *K* of [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_an_extension) *n* is a radical extension generated by an *n*th root of an element of *K* if and only if it is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) whose Galois group is a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *n*.

The proof is related to [Lagrange resolvents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_resolvent). Let $\omega$ be a [primitive *n*th root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) (belonging to *K*). If the extension is generated by $\alpha$ with $x^n-a$ as a [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106), the mapping $\alpha\mapsto \omega\alpha$ induces a *K*-automorphism of the extension that generates the Galois group, showing the "only if" implication. Conversely, if $\phi$ is a *K*-automorphism generating the Galois group, and $\beta$ is a generator of the extension, let

$$\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\omega^{-i}\phi^i(\beta).$$

The relation $\phi(\alpha) = \omega\alpha$ implies that the product of the [conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_conjugate) of $\alpha$ (that is the images of $\alpha$ by the *K*-automorphisms) belongs to *K*, and is equal to the product of $\alpha^n$ by the product of the *n*th roots of unit. As the product of the *n*th roots of units is $\pm 1$, this implies that $\alpha^n\in K,$ and thus that the extension is a radical extension.

It follows from this theorem that a Galois extension may be extended to a radical extension if and only if its Galois group is solvable (but there are non-radical Galois extensions whose Galois group is solvable, for example $\mathbb{Q}(\cos(2\pi/7))/\mathbb{Q}$). This is, in modern terminology, the criterion of solvability by radicals that was provided by Galois. The proof uses the fact that the [Galois closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_closure) of a simple radical extension of degree *n* is the extension of it by a primitive *n*th root of unity, and that the Galois group of the *n*th roots of unity is cyclic.
