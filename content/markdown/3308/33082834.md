# Eugène Dupréel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eugène Dupréel"}

**Eugène Dupréel** (February 8, 1879 – February 14, 1967) was a [Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgians) [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher). He had been professor at the [Université libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_libre_de_Bruxelles) from 1907 to 1950, teaching [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), [Greek philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_philosophy), [moral philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) and [sociological theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociological_theory). He developed an ethical theory and a theory of knowledge deeply influenced by [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), and worked closely with the [Institut de Sociologie Solvay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvay_Institute_of_Sociology). Leader of the "École de Bruxelles", he had a major influence on the argumentation theorist [Chaïm Perelman](wikipedia://en/3784254) and thus has been instrumental in the renewal of [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447).

:::
