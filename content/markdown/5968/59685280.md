# Steven Dillingham

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steven Dillingham"}

**Steven Dillingham** (born May 12, 1952) is an American lawyer who served as the 25th [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_United_States_Census_Bureau) of the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) from January 7, 2019, to January 20, 2021.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Dillingham graduated from [Rock Hill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Hill_High_School_%28South_Carolina%29) in [Rock Hill, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Hill,_South_Carolina), in 1970. From 1970 to 1972 he attended the [US Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy). Dillingham graduated from [Winthrop University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winthrop_University) in 1973 with a bachelor's degree in political science. He received his [J.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor), [M.P.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Administration), and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina), and completed his [M.B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) at the [George Washington University School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University_School_of_Business) and [LL.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Laws) at [Georgetown Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University_Law_Center).[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

Dillingham is a career member of the U.S. federal government's [Senior Executive Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Executive_Service_%28United_States%29), with more than 25 years of statistical, research, senior management, and legal experience. Dillingham's prior experience heading federal statistical service agencies includes a stint as Director of the [Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Justice_Statistics) and the [Bureau of Transportation Statistics](wikipedia://en/1704636). Dillingham also served as Director of the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps) Office of Strategic Information, Research, and Planning. Other federal service includes directing research and planning for the [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice)'s [United States Trustee Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Trustee_Program), and directing surveys for the [Office of Personnel Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Personnel_Management).[^o8]

In addition, Dillingham was the deputy director for the National District Attorneys Association and administrator of the American Prosecutors Research Institute. Dillingham has served on the faculties of the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina) and [George Mason University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mason_University). He is a senior certified professional with the [Society for Human Resource Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Human_Resource_Management).[^o8]

### U.S. Census {#U.S._Census}

Dillingham was sworn in as the 25th [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_United_States_Census_Bureau) of the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) on January 7, 2019.[^o8] He was nominated by President [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump) in 2018 and was confirmed by the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate) in a unanimous vote on January 2, 2019.[^o9][^o10] On January 18, 2021, Dillingham announced his retirement effective January 20, 2021;[^o11] his appointment was set to last until the end of 2021.[^o12] His departure from the office was marked by accusations that he supported a partisan effort to try to use potentially inaccurate data on undocumented immigrants to exclude them from congressional apportionment.[^o12][^o13]
