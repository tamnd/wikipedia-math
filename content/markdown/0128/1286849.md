# Correspondence theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Correspondence theorem"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **correspondence theorem**[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9] (also the **lattice theorem**,[^o10] and [variously and ambiguously the **third** and **fourth isomorphism theorem**](wikipedia://en/45241#Note_on_numbers_and_names)[^o7][^o11]) states that if $N$ is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$, then there exists a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) from the set of all [subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroups) $A$ of $G$ containing $N$, onto the set of all subgroups of the [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) $G/N$. Loosely speaking, the structure of the subgroups of $G/N$ is exactly the same as the structure of the subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, with $N$ collapsed to the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962).

:::

Specifically, if

$G$ is a group,

$N \triangleleft G$, a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $G$,

$\mathcal{G} = \{ A \mid N \subseteq A \leq G \}$, the set of all subgroups $A$ of $G$ that contain $N$, and

$\mathcal{N} = \{ S \mid S \leq G/N \}$, the set of all subgroups of $G/N$,

then there is a bijective map $\phi: \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{N}$ such that

$\phi(A) = A/N$ for all $A \in \mathcal{G}.$

One further has that if $A$ and $B$ are in $\mathcal{G}$ then

- $A \subseteq B$ if and only if $A/N \subseteq B/N$;
- if $A \subseteq B$ then $|B:A| = |B/N:A/N|$, where $|B:A|$ is the [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29) of $A$ in $B$ (the number of [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) $bA$ of $A$ in $B$);
- $\langle A,B \rangle / N = \left\langle A/N, B/N \right\rangle,$ where $\langle A, B \rangle$ is the subgroup of $G$ [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by $A\cup B;$
- $(A \cap B)/N = A/N \cap B/N$, and
- $A$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ if and only if $A/N$ is a normal subgroup of $G/N$.

This list is far from exhaustive. In fact, most properties of subgroups are preserved in their images under the bijection onto subgroups of a quotient group.

More generally, there is a [monotone Galois connection](wikipedia://en/156411) $(f^*, f_*)$ between the [lattice of subgroups](wikipedia://en/3983172) of $G$ (not necessarily containing $N$) and the lattice of subgroups of $G/N$: the lower adjoint of a subgroup $H$ of $G$ is given by $f^*(H) = HN/N$ and the upper adjoint of a subgroup $K/N$ of $G/N$ is a given by $f_*(K/N) = K$. The associated [closure operator](wikipedia://en/483120) on subgroups of $G$ is $\bar H = HN$; the associated [kernel operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_operator) on subgroups of $G/N$ is the identity. A proof of the correspondence theorem can be found [here](https://proofwiki.org/wiki/Correspondence_Theorem_%28Group_Theory%29).

Similar results hold for [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), [modules](wikipedia://en/276410), [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), and [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788). More generally an [analogous result](wikipedia://en/45241#Theorem_D_%28universal_algebra%29) that concerns [congruence relations](wikipedia://en/48167) instead of normal subgroups holds for any [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364).
