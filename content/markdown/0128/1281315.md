# Kryha

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kryha"}

*Image: Kryha-1.jpg.* The standard Kryha machine weighed around five kilograms and was totally mechanical. While the machine achieved a measure of popularity, its security was relatively weak; US cryptanalyst [William Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Friedman) reported that he solved the device within 2 hours and 41 minutes.

In the [history of cryptography](wikipedia://en/520066), the **Kryha** machine was a device for encryption and decryption, appearing in the early 1920s and used until the 1950s. The machine was the invention of Alexander von Kryha (born 31.10.1891 in [Charkow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charkow), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), committed suicide in [Baden-Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Baden) in 1955).[^o2] During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Kryha worked as an officer for the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht).[^o2] There were several versions; the standard Kryha machine weighed around five kilograms, and was totally mechanical. A scaled down pocket version was introduced later on, termed the "Lilliput" model.[^o3] There was also a more bulky electrical version.[^o5]

:::

The machine was used for a time by the German Diplomatic Corps, and was adopted by [Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marconi_plc) in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o5]

## Operation {#Operation}

*Image: Kryha-2.jpg.* The inner ring stepped an irregular number of places with each lever press, changing the encryption in a complex manner.

The machine consisted of two [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric) rings each containing an [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet).[^o5] The inner alphabet was stepped a variable number of places by pushing a lever. In operation, the user would encrypt by finding the [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935) letter on one ring (usually the outer ring), and reading the corresponding letter on the other ring; this was then used as the ciphertext letter.[^o5] When the lever was pressed, the inner ring would step, causing the relationship between the two alphabets to change. The stepping was irregular and governed by the use of a disk with a number of sectors, each containing a number of teeth.[^o5]

## Cryptanalysis {#Cryptanalysis}

*Image: Kryha-patent.png.* The Kryha machine was patented in the US in 1930; the design shows two concentric discs labelled with the alphabet.

The security of the machine was evaluated by the mathematician [Georg Hamel](wikipedia://en/1509021), who calculated the size of the [key](wikipedia://en/53039) space.[^o3] The US Army was also contacted to see if they would be interested in using the machine, and were persuaded to accept a challenge message to evaluate the security of the device. The challenge message, 1135 characters long, was solved by [William Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Friedman), assisted by [Solomon Kullback](wikipedia://en/506420), [Frank Rowlett](wikipedia://en/477018) and [Abraham Sinkov](wikipedia://en/477032), in 2 hours and 41 minutes.[^o2]
