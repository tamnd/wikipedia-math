# Orthant

*Image: Cartesian coordinates 2D.svg.* In two dimensions, there are four orthants (called quadrants)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthant"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **orthant**[^o2] or **hyperoctant** is the analogue in *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) of a [quadrant](wikipedia://en/18315778) in the plane or an [octant](wikipedia://en/36160226) in three dimensions.

:::

In general an orthant in *n*-dimensions can be considered the intersection of *n* mutually orthogonal [half-spaces](wikipedia://en/647297). By independent selections of half-space signs, there are 2<sup>*n*</sup> orthants in *n*-dimensional space.

More specifically, a **closed orthant** in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is a subset defined by constraining each [Cartesian coordinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinate) to be nonnegative or nonpositive. Such a subset is defined by a system of inequalities:

ε<sub>1</sub>*x*<sub>1</sub> ≥ 0 ε<sub>2</sub>*x*<sub>2</sub> ≥ 0 · · · ε<sub>*n*</sub>*x*<sub>*n*</sub> ≥ 0,

where each ε<sub>*i*</sub> is +1 or −1.

Similarly, an **open orthant** in **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is a subset defined by a system of strict inequalities

ε<sub>1</sub>*x*<sub>1</sub> > 0 ε<sub>2</sub>*x*<sub>2</sub> > 0 · · · ε<sub>*n*</sub>*x*<sub>*n*</sub> > 0,

where each ε<sub>*i*</sub> is +1 or −1.

By dimension:

- In one dimension, an orthant is a [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray).
- In two dimensions, an orthant is a [quadrant](wikipedia://en/7706#Quadrants_and_octants).
- In three dimensions, an orthant is an [octant](wikipedia://en/36160226).

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Neil Sloane](wikipedia://en/400062) defined the term *n*-[orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex) from **orthant complex** as a [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) in *n*-dimensions with 2<sup>*n*</sup> [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647), one per orthant.[^o4]

The **nonnegative orthant** is the generalization of the first [quadrant](wikipedia://en/18315778) to *n*-dimensions and is important in many [constrained optimization](wikipedia://en/4171950) problems.

## See also {#See_also}

- [Cross polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_polytope) (or orthoplex) – a family of [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) in *n*-dimensions which can be constructed with one [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) in each orthant space.
- [Measure polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_polytope) (or hypercube) – a family of regular polytopes in *n*-dimensions which can be constructed with one [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) in each orthant space.
- [Orthotope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthotope) – generalization of a rectangle in *n*-dimensions, with one vertex in each orthant.
