# Mental Calculation World Cup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mental Calculation World Cup"}

The **Mental Calculation World Cup** (German: Weltmeisterschaften im Kopfrechnen, or World Championship in Mental Calculation) is an international competition for [mental calculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_calculator), held every two years in Germany.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2004 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2004}

The first Mental Calculation World Cup was held in [Annaberg-Buchholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaberg-Buchholz), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) on 30 October 2004. There were 17 participants from 10 countries.

The World Cup involved the following contests (and two surprise tasks):

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto_García) (Spain); 10 correct results; 5:50 minutes, **world record**
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto_García) (Spain), 8 correct results
- Calendar Calculations, two series one minute each, dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: Matthias Kesselschläger (Germany), 33 correct results, **world record**
- Square Root from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) (Germany)

In the overall ranking the first place is taken by [Robert Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fountain_%28mental_calculator%29) (Great Britain), the runner-up was [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) (Germany).

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2006 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2006}

The second Mental Calculation World Cup was held on 4 November 2006 in the [Mathematikum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematikum) museum in [Gießen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gießen), Germany. 26 Calculators from 11 countries took part. The World Cup involved the following contests (and two surprise tasks):

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Jorge Arturo Mendoza Huertas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Arturo_Mendoza_Huertas) (Peru); 10 correct results
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto_García) (Spain)
- Calendar Calculations, two series one minute each, dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: Matthias Kesselschläger (Germany)
- Square Root from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Robert Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fountain_%28mental_calculator%29) (Great Britain)

[Robert Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fountain_%28mental_calculator%29) (Great Britain) defended his title in the overall competition, the places 2 to 4 have been won by [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) (Netherlands), [Gert Mittring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gert_Mittring) (Germany) and [Yusnier Viera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusnier_Viera) Romero (Cuba).

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2008 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2008}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2008 was held at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University), Germany on 1 July 2008. It attracted 28 calculators from 12 countries.

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto_García) (Spain), 10 correct results in 4:26 minutes. **world record**
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto_García) (Spain), 10 correct results in 8:25 minutes, **world record**
- Calendar Calculations, dates from the years 1600–2100, one minute
  - Winner: [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) (Germany), 40 correct results
- Square Root from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) (Germany)

[Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto) (Spain) won the overall title. [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld), starting for Germany this time, became second, and [Jorge Arturo Mendoza Huertas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Arturo_Mendoza_Huertas) (Peru) finished third. [Robert Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fountain_%28mental_calculator%29), who won the title in 2004 and 2006, achieved a fourth rank.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2010 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2010}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2010 was held at the [University of Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_von_Guericke_University_Magdeburg), Germany[^o9] on 6–7 June 2010. It attracted 33 calculators from 13 countries.

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto) (Spain), 10 correct results in 3:42 minutes, world record
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 15 minutes
  - Winner: Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain), 10 correct results in 4:56 minutes, world record
- Calendar Calculations, dates from the years 1600–2100, one minute
  - Winner: [Yusnier Viera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusnier_Viera) (Cuba), 48 correct results, world cup record
- Square Root from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 6:51 minutes,' world cup record
  - Winner: [Priyanshi Somani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priyanshi_Somani) (India)
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving six unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Jerry Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_and_Mary_Newport) (U.S.)[^o10]

[Priyanshi Somani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priyanshi_Somani) (India) won the overall title. Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain) came second, and [Alberto Coto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Coto) finished third.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2012 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2012}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2012 was held from 30/9/2012 to 01/10/2012 in the [Mathematikum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematikum) museum in [Gießen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gießen), Germany. 32 calculators from 16 countries participated. The world cup involved the following 5 main contests :

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Naofumi Ogasawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naofumi_Ogasawara) (Japan); 10 correct results in 191 seconds, **world record**
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Freddis Reyes Hernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddis_Reyes_Hernández) (Cuba), 10 correct results in 361 seconds
- Calendar Calculations, One minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: [Myagmarsuren Tuuruul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myagmarsuren_Tuuruul) (Mongolia), 57 correct results, **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Naofumi Ogasawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naofumi_Ogasawara) (Japan), 8 correct results (each solved, up to all the first 8 digits: 3 integers and 5 decimals)
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Naofumi Ogasawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naofumi_Ogasawara) (Japan), 500, perfect score.

Naofumi Ogasawara (Japan) won the title in the overall competition[^o12] (combination of all 10 categories). Hua Wei Chan (Malaysia) was 2nd overall,[^o13] and [Jan van Koningsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Koningsveld) was 3rd.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2014 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2014}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2014 was held from 10/10/2014 to 12/10/2014 in the Faculty of Mathematics in [Dresden University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden_University_of_Technology), Germany.

39 mental calculators from 17 countries participated.

The World Cup involved the following 5 main contests:

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Granth Thakkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granth_Thakkar) (India); 10 correct results in 242 seconds,
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain), 10 correct results in 295 seconds, **world cup record**
- Calendar Calculations, one minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain), 64 correct results, **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 tasks in 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Rhea Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhea_Shah) (India), 10 correct results in 135 seconds (each solved to eight [significant figures](wikipedia://en/317062)), **world cup record**
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Andreas Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Berger_%28Mental_Calculation%29) (Germany), 365/500

[Granth Thakkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granth_Thakkar) (India),won the title in the overall competition (combination of all 10 categories). Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain) was 2nd overall, and [Chie Ishikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chie_Ishikawa) (Japan) was 3rd.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2016 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2016}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2016 was held from 23 to 25 September 2016, in Bielefeld Germany. 31 mental calculators from 16 countries participated.

The World Cup involved the following 5 main contests:

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Yuki Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Kimura_%28mental_calculation%29) (Japan)
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Lee Jeonghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jeonghee) (South Korea)
- Calendar Calculations, one minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: Georgi Georgiev (Bulgaria), 66 correct results, **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Yuki Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Kimura_%28mental_calculation%29) (Japan)
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Lee Jeonghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jeonghee) (South Korea)
- Memoriad Trophy
  - Winner: [Yuki Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Kimura_%28mental_calculation%29) (Japan)

[Yuki Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Kimura_%28mental_calculation%29) (Japan) won the title in the overall competition (combination of all 10 categories). [Tetsuya Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuya_Ono) (Japan) was 2nd overall, and [Lee Jeonghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jeonghee) (South Korea) was 3rd.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2018 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2018}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2018 was held on 28–30 September 2018 at the [Phaeno Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phaeno_Science_Center) in Wolfsburg, Germany. 82 calculators from 24 countries applied for the qualification; 33 calculators from 17 countries were qualified and took part at the contest in Wolfsburg.

The World Cup involved the following 5 main contests:

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Lee Jeonghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Jeonghee) (South Korea)[^o17]
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: Tomohiro Iseda (Japan)
- Calendar Calculations, one minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: Marc Jornet Sanz (Spain), 71 correct results, **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: Tomohiro Iseda (Japan)
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: Wenzel Grüß (Germany)

Tomohiro Iseda (Japan) won the title in the overall competition[^o18] (combination of all 10 categories). Hiroto Ihara (Japan) finished second and Wenzel Grüß (Germany) third.

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2020/22 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2020/22}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2020 was planned to take place on 21–23 August 2020. Due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic), it had to be postponed. It was held on 15-17 July 2022 at [Heinz Nixdorf MuseumsForum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Nixdorf_MuseumsForum) (world's largest computer museum) in [Paderborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn), Germany.[^o20] 66 calculators applied for the qualification; 35 calculators from 17 countries were qualified and took part at the contest in Paderborn.

[Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), won the title in the overall competition (combination of all 10 categories). [Tetsuya Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuya_Ono) (Japan) finished second and [Mohammad El-Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_El-Mir) (Lebanon) third.

The World Cup involved the following 5 main contests:

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Tetsuya Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuya_Ono) (Japan), 32 points (34 correct, 2 incorrect results)
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), 21 points (23 correct, 2 incorrect results), **world cup record**
- Calendar Calculations, one minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: [Akshita Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akshita_Shah) (India), 80 correct results, **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), 74 points (79 correct, 5 incorrect results), **world cup record**
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Tetsuya Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuya_Ono) (Japan)

## Mental Calculation World Cup 2024 {#Mental_Calculation_World_Cup_2024}

The Mental Calculation World Cup 2024 was held on 13-15 September 2024 at the [Heinz Nixdorf MuseumsForum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Nixdorf_MuseumsForum) in Paderborn, Germany. 69 calculators from 21 countries applied for the qualification; 35 calculators from 16 countries were qualified and took part at the contest in Paderborn.

The World Cup involved the following 5 main contests:

- Adding ten 10-digit numbers, 7 minutes
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India)
- Multiplying two 8-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), 25 points (28 correct, 3 incorrect), **world cup record**
- Calendar Calculations, one minute, random dates from the years 1600–2100
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), 100 correct results (out of 100 dates given), **world cup record**
- Square Roots from 6-digit numbers, 10 minutes
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), 82 points (86 correct, 4 incorrect, 90 total questions), **world cup record**
- Most Versatile Calculator (the best score for solving another 5 unknown "surprise tasks")
  - Winner: [Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India)

[Aaryan Nitin Shukla](wikipedia://en/77683133) (India), won the title in the overall competition for the second consecutive time[^o22] (combination of all 9 categories). Kaloyan Geshev (Bulgaria) finished second and Hiko Chiba (Japan) third.
