# Quartic graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quartic graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **quartic graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) where all [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) have [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 4. In other words, a quartic graph is a 4-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Chvatal Lombardi.svg.* The [Chvátal graph](wikipedia://en/22258988)

Several well-known graphs are quartic. They include:

- The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_5$, a quartic graph with 5 vertices, is the smallest possible quartic graph.
- The [octahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_graph) $K_{2,2,2}$, the graph of the regular octahedron, has 6 vertices.[^o5]
- The [Chvátal graph](wikipedia://en/22258988), another quartic graph with 12 vertices, is the smallest quartic graph that both has no triangles and cannot be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with three colors.[^o6]
- The [Folkman graph](wikipedia://en/23761072), a quartic graph with 20 vertices, is the smallest [semi-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/2554240). This means that it has symmetries taking every two edges to each other, but does not have symmetries taking every two vertices to each other.[^o7]
- The [Meredith graph](wikipedia://en/24128464), a quartic graph with 70 vertices, is [4-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) but has no [Hamiltonian cycle](wikipedia://en/244437), disproving a conjecture of [Crispin Nash-Williams](wikipedia://en/16550517).[^o8]

Every [medial graph](wikipedia://en/28280731) is a quartic [plane graph](wikipedia://en/24314), and every quartic plane graph is the medial graph of a pair of dual plane graphs or multigraphs.[^o9] [Knot diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram) and link diagrams are also quartic plane [multigraphs](wikipedia://en/1774970), in which the vertices represent the crossings of the diagram and are marked with additional information concerning which of the two branches of the knot crosses the other branch at that point.[^o10] The [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of any [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508) is quartic; the [cuboctahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuboctahedral_graph) is an example, as the line graph of a [cube graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_graph).

The [subdivided double](wikipedia://en/83918691) of a quartic graph is another, larger, quartic graph. It is obtained by subdividing each edge of the given graph into a two-edge path, and then replacing each vertex that came from the given graph by a pair of vertices, both adjacent to the same neighborhood of subdivision vertices. For instance, the Folkman graph is the subdivided double of the complete graph $K_5$.[^o11]

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Because the [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) of every vertex in a quartic graph is even, every [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) quartic graph has an [Euler tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_tour). And as with regular bipartite graphs more generally, every [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431) quartic graph has a [perfect matching](wikipedia://en/44031). In this case, a much simpler and faster [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for finding such a matching is possible than for irregular graphs: by selecting every other edge of an Euler tour, one may find a [2-factor](wikipedia://en/3298854), which in this case must be a collection of cycles, each of even length, with each vertex of the graph appearing in exactly one cycle. By selecting every other edge again in these cycles, one obtains a perfect matching in [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time). The same method can also be used to [color the edges of the graph](wikipedia://en/690647) with four colors in linear time.[^o14]

Quartic graphs have an even number of [Hamiltonian decompositions](wikipedia://en/24004195).[^o15]

:::

::: {#o17 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Open problems"}

## Open problems {#Open_problems}

It is an open conjecture whether all quartic Hamiltonian graphs have an even number of [Hamiltonian circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_circuit), or have more than one Hamiltonian circuit. The answer is known to be false for quartic [multigraphs](wikipedia://en/1774970).[^o18]
