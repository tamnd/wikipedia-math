# Kannan Soundararajan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kannan Soundararajan"}

**Kannan Soundararajan** (born December 27, 1973)[^o3] is an [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India)-born American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a professor of mathematics at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University). Before moving to Stanford in 2006, he was a faculty member at [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), where he had also pursued his undergraduate studies. His main research interest is in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), particularly in the subfields of [automorphic](wikipedia://en/782146) [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283), and [multiplicative number theory](wikipedia://en/18103572).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Soundararajan grew up in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) and was a student at [Padma Seshadri High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Seshadri_Bala_Bhavan) in Nungambakkam in Chennai. In 1989, he attended the [Research Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Science_Institute). He represented India at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 1991 and won a Silver Medal.[^o5]

## Education {#Education}

Soundararajan joined the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan), Ann Arbor, in 1991 for undergraduate studies, and graduated with highest honours in 1995. Soundararajan won the inaugural [Morgan Prize](wikipedia://en/4180157) in 1995 for his work in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) while an undergraduate at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan),[^o7] where he later served as professor. He joined [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1995 and did his Ph.D under the guidance of Professor [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak).

## Career {#Career}

After his Ph.D. he received the first five-year fellowship from the American Institute of Mathematics, and held positions at Princeton University, the Institute for Advanced Study, and the University of Michigan. He moved to Stanford University in 2006 where he is, as of November 2022,[^o9] the Anne T. and [Robert M. Bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bass_%28businessman%29) Professor of Mathematics.

He provided a proof[^o10] of a conjecture of [Ron Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Graham_%28mathematician%29) in combinatorial number theory jointly with [Ramachandran Balasubramanian](wikipedia://en/18734196). He made contributions in settling the arithmetic Quantum Unique Ergodicity conjecture[^o11] for [Maass wave forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maass_wave_forms) and [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms).

## Awards {#Awards}

He received the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 2003 "for contributions to the area of [Dirichlet L-functions](wikipedia://en/400092) and related character sums". In 2005, he won the \$10,000 [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize), shared with [Manjul Bhargava](wikipedia://en/5102088), awarded by [SASTRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA) in [Thanjavur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanjavur), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India), for his outstanding contributions to [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o13] In 2011, he was awarded the Infosys science foundation prize.[^o14] He was awarded the Ostrowski prize[^o15] in 2011, shared with [Ib Madsen](wikipedia://en/38416618) and [David Preiss](wikipedia://en/19372849), for a cornucopia of fundamental results in the last five years to go along with his earlier work.

He gave an [invited talk at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010, on the topic of "Number Theory".[^o16] In July 2017, Soundararajan was a plenary lecturer in the Mathematical Congress of the Americas.[^o17] He was elected to the 2018 class of [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o18] Kannan Soundararajan was invited as a plenary speaker[^o19] of the 2022 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), scheduled to take place in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), but moved to Helsinki and online because of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).
