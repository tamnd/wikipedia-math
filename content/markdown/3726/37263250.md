# Right kite

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Right kite"}

*Image: Bicentric kite 001.svg.* A right kite with its circumcircle and incircle. The leftmost and rightmost vertices have right angles.

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), a **right kite** is a [kite](wikipedia://en/84840) (a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) whose four sides can be grouped into two pairs of equal-length sides that are adjacent to each other) that can be inscribed in a circle.[^o2] That is, it is a kite with a [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/161262) (i.e., a [cyclic](wikipedia://en/194131) kite). Thus the right kite is a [convex](wikipedia://en/660019) quadrilateral and has two opposite [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956).[^o3] If there are exactly two right angles, each must be between sides of different lengths. All right kites are [bicentric quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/23335118) (quadrilaterals with both a circumcircle and an incircle), since all kites have an [incircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incircle). One of the diagonals (the one that is a line of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741)) divides the right kite into two [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) and is also a [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the circumcircle. All right kites are [harmonic quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/61186948) since they have a circumcircle and each pair of opposite sides has the same two lengths.

:::

In a [tangential quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/9176412) (one with an incircle), the four line segments between the center of the incircle and the points where it is tangent to the quadrilateral partition the quadrilateral into four right kites.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special case"}

## Special case {#Special_case}

A special case of right kites are [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), where the diagonals have equal lengths, and the incircle and circumcircle are [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Characterizations"}

## Characterizations {#Characterizations}

A kite is a right kite [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it has a circumcircle (by definition). This is equivalent to its being a kite with two opposite right angles.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Metric formulas"}

## Metric formulas {#Metric_formulas}

Since a right kite can be divided into two right triangles, the following metric formulas easily follow from well known properties of right triangles. In a right kite *ABCD* where the opposite angles *B* and *D* are right angles, the other two angles can be calculated from

$$\tan{\frac{A}{2}}=\frac{b}{a},\qquad \tan{\frac{C}{2}}=\frac{a}{b}$$

where *a* = *AB* = *AD* and *b* = *BC* = *CD*. The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a right kite is

$$\displaystyle K=ab.$$

The [diagonal](wikipedia://en/349251) *AC* that is a line of symmetry has the length

$$p=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$$

and, since the diagonals are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) (so a right kite is an [orthodiagonal quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/30425383) with area $K=\frac{pq}{2}$), the other diagonal *BD* has the length

$$q=\frac{2ab}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}.$$

The [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the circumcircle is (according to the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034))

$$R=\tfrac12\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$$

and, since all kites are [tangential quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/9176412), the radius of the incircle is given by

$$r=\frac{K}{s}=\frac{ab}{a+b}$$

where *s* is the semiperimeter.

The area is given in terms of the circumradius *R* and the inradius *r* as[^o10]

$$K=r(r+\sqrt{4R^2+r^2}).$$

If we take the segments extending from the intersection of the diagonals to the vertices in clockwise order to be $d_1$, $d_2$,$d_3$, and $d_4$, then,

$$d_1 d_3=d_2 d_4$$

This is a direct result of the [geometric mean theorem](wikipedia://en/37981515).

:::

## Duality {#Duality}

The [dual polygon](wikipedia://en/19865549) to a right kite is an [isosceles tangential trapezoid](wikipedia://en/34853912#Isosceles_tangential_trapezoid).[^o2]

::: {#o13 .definition cue=heading name="Alternative definition"}

## Alternative definition {#Alternative_definition}

Sometimes a right kite is defined as a kite with at least one right angle.[^o14] If there is only one right angle, it must be between two sides of equal length; in this case, the formulas given above do not apply.

:::
