# Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion"}

The **Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion** is a [condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stability_criterion) for [linear stability](wikipedia://en/3652079) (sometimes called *spectral stability*) of [solitary wave solutions](wikipedia://en/51654) to a wide class of [**U**(1)-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_invariance) [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531), named after Soviet scientists Aleksandr Kolokolov (Александр Александрович Колоколов) and Nazib Vakhitov (Назиб Галиевич Вахитов). The condition for linear stability of a [solitary wave](wikipedia://en/51654) $u(x,t) = \phi_\omega(x)e^{-i\omega t}$ with frequency $\omega$ has the form

:::

$$\frac{d}{d\omega}Q(\omega)<0,$$

where $Q(\omega)\,$ is the [charge](wikipedia://en/9804) (or [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431)) of the solitary wave $\phi_\omega(x)e^{-i\omega t}$, conserved by [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159) due to **U**(1)-invariance of the system.

## Original formulation {#Original_formulation}

Originally, this criterion was obtained for the [nonlinear Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/1422748),

$$i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,t)= -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} u(x,t) +g(|u(x,t)|^2)u(x,t),$$

where $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$, and $g \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ is a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued [function](wikipedia://en/185427). The solution $u(x,t)$ is assumed to be [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued. Since the equation is **U**(1)-invariant, by [Noether's theorem](wikipedia://en/150159), it has an [integral of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_of_motion), $Q(u) = \frac{1}{2} \int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x,t)|^2\,dx$, which is called [charge](wikipedia://en/9804) or [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431), depending on the model under consideration. For a wide class of functions $g$, the nonlinear Schrödinger equation admits solitary wave solutions of the form $u(x,t) = \phi_\omega(x)e^{-i\omega t}$, where $\omega \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\phi_\omega(x)$ decays for large $x$ (one often requires that $\phi_\omega(x)$ belongs to the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^1(\mathbb{R}^n)$). Usually such solutions exist for $\omega$ from an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) or collection of intervals of a real line. The Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion,[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6]

$$\frac{d}{d\omega}Q(\phi_\omega)<0,$$

is a condition of spectral stability of a solitary wave solution. Namely, if this condition is satisfied at a particular value of $\omega$, then the linearization at the solitary wave with this $\omega$ has no spectrum in the right half-plane.

This result is based on an earlier work[^o7] by [Vladimir Zakharov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_E._Zakharov).

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

This result has been generalized to abstract [Hamiltonian systems](wikipedia://en/1197531) with **U**(1)-invariance.[^o10] It was shown that under rather general conditions the Vakhitov–Kolokolov stability criterion guarantees not only spectral stability but also [orbital stability](wikipedia://en/28842205) of solitary waves.

The stability condition has been generalized[^o11] to traveling wave solutions to the [generalized Korteweg–de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Korteweg–de_Vries_equation) of the form

$$\partial_t u + \partial_x^3 u + \partial_x f(u) = 0\,.$$

The stability condition has also been generalized to Hamiltonian systems with a more general [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186).[^o12]
