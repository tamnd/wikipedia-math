# Vector algebra relations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector algebra relations"}

The following are important identities in [vector algebra](wikipedia://en/32370). Identities that only involve the magnitude of a vector $\|\mathbf A\|$ and the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) (scalar product) of two vectors **A**·**B**, apply to vectors in any dimension, while identities that use the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) (vector product) **A**×**B** only apply in three dimensions, since the cross product is only defined there.[^o2] Most of these relations can be dated to founder of vector calculus [Josiah Willard Gibbs](wikipedia://en/37332), if not earlier.[^o18]

:::

## Magnitudes {#Magnitudes}

The magnitude of a vector **A** can be expressed using the dot product:

$$\|\mathbf A \|^2 = \mathbf {A \cdot A}$$

In three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the magnitude of a vector is determined from its three components using [Pythagoras' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoras'_theorem):

$$\|\mathbf A \|^2 = A_1^2 + A_2^2 +A_3^2$$

## Inequalities {#Inequalities}

- The [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128): $\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} \le \left\|\mathbf A \right\| \left\|\mathbf B \right\|$
- The [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941): $\|\mathbf{A + B}\| \le \| \mathbf{A}\| + \|\mathbf{B}\|$
- The [reverse triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941#Reverse_triangle_inequality): $\|\mathbf{A - B}\| \ge \Bigl| \| \mathbf{A}\| - \|\mathbf{B}\| \Bigr|$

## Angles {#Angles}

The vector product and the scalar product of two vectors define the angle between them, say *θ*:[^o2][^o6]

$$\sin \theta =\frac{\|\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}\|}{\left\|\mathbf A \right\| \left\|\mathbf B \right\|} \quad ( -\pi < \theta \le \pi )$$

To satisfy the [right-hand rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-hand_rule), for positive *θ*, vector **B** is counter-clockwise from **A**, and for negative *θ* it is clockwise.

$$\cos \theta = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{\left\|\mathbf A \right\| \left\|\mathbf B \right\|} \quad ( -\pi < \theta \le \pi )$$

The [Pythagorean trigonometric identity](wikipedia://en/1050741) then provides:

$$\left\|\mathbf{A \times B}\right\|^2 +(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B})^2 = \left\|\mathbf A \right\|^2 \left\|\mathbf B \right\|^2$$

(This may also be seen by identifying a vector $\mathbf{V}=V_x \mathbf{i}+V_y \mathbf{j}+V_z \mathbf{k}$ with the corresponding imaginary [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440), so that the quaternion product is $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B} = -\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}\,+\,\mathbf{A}{\times}\mathbf{B}$ ; then the above identity follows from the multiplicative property $\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}\| = \|\mathbf{A}\|\,\|\mathbf{B}\|$.)

If a vector **A** = (*A<sub>x</sub>, A<sub>y</sub>, A<sub>z</sub>*) makes angles *α*, *β*, *γ* with an orthogonal set of *x-*, *y-* and *z-*axes, then:

$$\cos \alpha = \frac{ A_x }{ \sqrt {A_x^2 +A_y^2 +A_z^2} } = \frac {A_x} {\| \mathbf A \|} \ ,$$

and analogously for angles β, γ. Consequently:

$$\mathbf A = \left\|\mathbf A \right\|\left( \cos (\alpha) \, \mathbf i + \cos (\beta)\, \mathbf j + \cos (\gamma) \, \mathbf k \right) ,$$

where $\mathbf i, \mathbf j, \mathbf k$ are the coordinate unit vectors.

## Areas and volumes {#Areas_and_volumes}

The area Σ of a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) with sides *A* and *B* containing the angle *θ* is:

$$\Sigma = AB \sin \theta ,$$

which will be recognized as the magnitude of the vector cross product of the vectors **A** and **B** lying along the sides of the parallelogram. That is:

$$\Sigma = \left\|\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B} \right\| = \sqrt{ \left\|\mathbf A\right\|^2 \left\|\mathbf B\right\|^2 - \left(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} \right)^2} \ .$$

(If **A**, **B** are two-dimensional vectors, this is equal to the determinant of the 2 × 2 matrix with rows **A**, **B**.) The square of this expression is:[^o8]

$$\Sigma^2 = (\mathbf{A \cdot A })(\mathbf{B \cdot B })-(\mathbf{A \cdot B })(\mathbf{B \cdot A })=\Gamma(\mathbf A,\ \mathbf B ) \ ,$$

where Γ(**A**, **B**) is the [Gram determinant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_determinant) of **A** and **B** defined by:

$$\Gamma(\mathbf A,\ \mathbf B )=\begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{A\cdot A} & \mathbf{A\cdot B} \\ \mathbf{B\cdot A} & \mathbf{B\cdot B} \end{vmatrix} \ .$$

In a similar fashion, the squared volume *V* of a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) spanned by the three vectors **A**, **B**, **C** is given by the Gram determinant of the three vectors:[^o8]

$$V^2 =\Gamma ( \mathbf A ,\ \mathbf B ,\ \mathbf C ) = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{A\cdot A} & \mathbf{A\cdot B} & \mathbf{A\cdot C} \\\mathbf{B\cdot A} & \mathbf{B\cdot B} & \mathbf{B\cdot C}\\ \mathbf{C\cdot A} & \mathbf{C\cdot B} & \mathbf{C\cdot C} \end{vmatrix} \ ,$$

Since **A**, **B, C** are three-dimensional vectors, this is equal to the square of the [scalar triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_triple_product) $\det[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}] = |\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}|$ below.

This process can be extended to *n*-dimensions. Indeed, letting $M=\left[\begin{smallmatrix} \mathbf{A}_1\\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{A}_n\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ be the $n\times n$ matrix whose rows are the vectors $\mathbf{A}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{A}_n$, the Gram matrix $G$ is product of $M$ with its transpose, $\Gamma = M M^T$, so that $\Gamma =\det G = (\det M)^2 = V^2$.

## Addition and multiplication of vectors {#Addition_and_multiplication_of_vectors}

- [Commutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity) of addition: $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{A}$.
- Commutativity of scalar product: $\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{A}$.
- [Anticommutativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) of cross product: $\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{-}(\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{A})$.
- [Distributivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributivity) of multiplication by a scalar over addition: $c (\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}) = c\mathbf{A}+c\mathbf{B}$.
- Distributivity of scalar product over addition: $\left(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}\right)\cdot\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{C}$.
- Distributivity of vector product over addition: $(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C}$.
- [Scalar triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_triple_product): $$\mathbf{A}\cdot (\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C})=\mathbf{B}\cdot (\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{A})=\mathbf{C}\cdot (\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}) = |\mathbf{A}\, \mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{C}|= \begin{vmatrix} A_{x} & B_{x} & C_{x}\\ A_{y} & B_{y} & C_{y}\\ A_{z} & B_{z} & C_{z}\end{vmatrix}.$$
- [Vector triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_triple_product): $\mathbf{A}\times (\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C}) = (\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{C} )\mathbf{B}- (\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B})\mathbf{C}$.
- [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370): $$\mathbf{A}\times (\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C} )+\mathbf{C}\times (\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B} )+ \mathbf{B}\times (\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{A} )= \mathbf 0 .$$
- [Lagrange's identity](wikipedia://en/2557590): $|\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B}|^2 = (\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{A}) (\mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{B})-(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B})^2$.

### Quadruple product {#Quadruple_product}

The name "quadruple product" is used for two different products,[^o12] the scalar-valued **scalar quadruple product** and the vector-valued **vector quadruple product** or **vector product of four vectors**.

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Scalar quadruple product"}

#### Scalar quadruple product {#Scalar_quadruple_product}

The **scalar quadruple product** is defined as the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) of two [cross products](wikipedia://en/157092):

$$(\mathbf{a \times b})\cdot(\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d}) \ ,$$

where **a, b, c, d** are vectors in three-dimensional Euclidean space.[^o15] It can be evaluated using the [Binet-Cauchy identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binet-Cauchy_identity):[^o15]

$$(\mathbf{a \times b})\cdot(\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d}) = (\mathbf{a \cdot c})(\mathbf{b \cdot d}) - (\mathbf{a \cdot d})(\mathbf{b \cdot c}) \ .$$

or using the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468):

$$(\mathbf{a \times b})\cdot(\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d}) =\begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{a\cdot c} & \mathbf{a\cdot d} \\ \mathbf{b\cdot c} & \mathbf{b\cdot d} \end{vmatrix} \ .$$

:::

::: {#o17 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Vector quadruple product"}

#### Vector quadruple product {#Vector_quadruple_product}

The **vector quadruple product** is defined as the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) of two cross products:

$$(\mathbf{a \times b}) \mathbf{\times} (\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d}) \ ,$$

where **a, b, c, d** are vectors in three-dimensional Euclidean space.[^o18] It can be evaluated using the identity:[^o19]

$$(\mathbf{a \times b} )\mathbf{\times} (\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d}) = (\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{d})) \mathbf c - (\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c})) \mathbf d \ .$$

Equivalent forms can be obtained using the identity:[^o20][^o21][^o22]

$$(\mathbf{b} \cdot (\mathbf{c} \times \mathbf{d}))\mathbf a - (\mathbf{c} \cdot (\mathbf{d} \times \mathbf{a}))\mathbf b+(\mathbf{d} \cdot (\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}))\mathbf{c} -(\mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}))\mathbf d = 0 \ .$$

This identity can also be written using [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) notation and the [Einstein summation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation) convention as follows:

$$(\mathbf{a \times b} )\mathbf{\times} (\mathbf{c}\times \mathbf{d})=\varepsilon_{ijk} a^i c^j d^k b^l - \varepsilon_{ijk} b^i c^j d^k a^l=\varepsilon_{ijk} a^i b^j d^k c^l - \varepsilon_{ijk} a^i b^j c^k d^l$$

where $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is the [Levi-Civita symbol](wikipedia://en/195982).

Related relationships:

- A consequence of the previous equation:[^o23] $$|\mathbf{A}\, \mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{C}|\,\mathbf{D}= (\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{D} )\left(\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C}\right)+\left(\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{D}\right)\left(\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{A}\right)+\left(\mathbf{C}\cdot\mathbf{D}\right)\left(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{B}\right).$$
- In 3 dimensions, a vector **D** can be expressed in terms of [basis vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_vectors) {**A**,**B**,**C**} as:[^o24] $$\mathbf D \ =\ \frac{\mathbf{D} \cdot (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{C})}{|\mathbf{A}\, \mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{C}|}\ \mathbf A +\frac{\mathbf{D} \cdot (\mathbf{C} \times \mathbf{A})}{|\mathbf{A}\, \mathbf{B}\, \mathbf{C}|}\ \mathbf B + \frac{\mathbf{D} \cdot (\mathbf{A} \times \mathbf{B})}{|\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}\, \mathbf{C}|}\ \mathbf C.$$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

These relations are useful for deriving various formulas in spherical and Euclidean geometry. For example, if four points are chosen on the unit sphere, *A, B, C, D*, and unit vectors drawn from the center of the sphere to the four points, **a, b, c, d** respectively, the identity:

$$(\mathbf{a \times b})\mathbf{\cdot}(\mathbf{c \times d}) = (\mathbf {a\cdot c })(\mathbf {b\cdot d })-(\mathbf{ a\cdot d })(\mathbf {b\cdot c }) \ ,$$

in conjunction with the relation for the magnitude of the cross product:

$$\|\mathbf{a \times b}\| = a b \sin \theta_{ab} \ ,$$

and the dot product:

$$\mathbf{a \cdot b} = a b \cos \theta_{ab} \ ,$$

where *a = b* = 1 for the unit sphere, results in the identity among the angles attributed to Gauss:

$$\sin \theta_{ab}\sin \theta_{cd}\cos x = \cos\theta_{ac}\cos\theta_{bd} - \cos\theta_{ad} \cos \theta_{bc} \ ,$$

where *x* is the angle between **a** × **b** and **c** × **d**, or equivalently, between the planes defined by these vectors.[^o18]
