# Analytic combinatorics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analytic combinatorics"}

**Analytic combinatorics** uses techniques from [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) to solve problems in [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533), specifically to find [asymptotic estimates](wikipedia://en/641995) for the coefficients of [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993).[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

## History {#History}

One of the earliest uses of analytic techniques for an enumeration problem came from [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) and [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599)'s work on [integer partitions](wikipedia://en/312250),[^o6][^o7] starting in 1918, first using a Tauberian theorem and later the [circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method).[^o8]

[Walter Hayman](wikipedia://en/34191584)'s 1956 paper "A Generalisation of Stirling's Formula" is considered one of the earliest examples of the saddle-point method.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

In 1990, [Philippe Flajolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Flajolet) and [Andrew Odlyzko](wikipedia://en/1986042) developed the theory of singularity analysis.[^o12]

In 2009, [Philippe Flajolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Flajolet) and [Robert Sedgewick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sedgewick_%28computer_scientist%29) wrote the book *[Analytic Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/51136652)*, which presents analytic combinatorics with their viewpoint and notation.

Some of the earliest work on [multivariate generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993#Bivariate_and_multivariate_generating_functions) started in the 1970s using probabilistic methods.[^o13][^o14]

Development of further multivariate techniques started in the early 2000s.[^o15]

## Techniques {#Techniques}

### Meromorphic functions {#Meromorphic_functions}

If $h(z) = \frac{f(z)}{g(z)}$ is a [meromorphic function](wikipedia://en/80857) and $a$ is its [pole](wikipedia://en/81560) closest to the origin with order $m$, then[^o18]

$[z^n] h(z) \sim \frac{(-1)^m m f(a)}{a^m g^{(m)}(a)} \left( \frac{1}{a} \right)^n n^{m-1} \quad$ as $n \to \infty$

where $[z^n] h(z)$ is the coefficient of $z^n$ in the [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) of $h(z)$ around $z=0$.

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Tauberian theorem"}

### Tauberian theorem {#Tauberian_theorem}

If

$f(z) \sim \frac{1}{(1 - z)^\sigma} L(\frac{1}{1 - z}) \quad$ as $z \to 1$

where $\sigma > 0$ and $L$ is a [slowly varying function](wikipedia://en/17053182), then[^o21]

$[z^n]f(z) \sim \frac{n^{\sigma-1}}{\Gamma(\sigma)} L(n) \quad$ as $n \to \infty$

See also the [Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian theorem](wikipedia://en/22846993).

:::

### Circle Method {#Circle_Method}

For generating functions with [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860) or [roots](wikipedia://en/235029), which have [branch singularities](wikipedia://en/54240#Branch_points).[^o23]

#### Darboux's method {#Darboux's_method}

If we have a function $(1 - z)^\beta f(z)$ where $\beta \notin \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ and $f(z)$ has a [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) greater than $1$ and a [Taylor expansion](wikipedia://en/30448) near 1 of $\sum_{j\geq0} f_j (1 - z)^j$, then[^o25]

$$[z^n](1 - z)^\beta f(z) = \sum_{j=0}^m f_j \frac{n^{-\beta-j-1}}{\Gamma(-\beta-j)} + O(n^{-m-\beta-2})$$

See Szegő (1975) for a similar theorem dealing with multiple singularities.

#### Singularity analysis {#Singularity_analysis}

If $f(z)$ has a singularity at $\zeta$ and

$f(z) \sim \left(1 - \frac{z}{\zeta}\right)^\alpha \left(\frac{1}{\frac{z}{\zeta}}\log\frac{1}{1 - \frac{z}{\zeta}}\right)^\gamma \left(\frac{1}{\frac{z}{\zeta}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\frac{z}{\zeta}}\log\frac{1}{1 - \frac{z}{\zeta}}\right)\right)^\delta \quad$ as $z \to \zeta$

where $\alpha \notin \{0, 1, 2, \cdots\}, \gamma, \delta \notin \{1, 2, \cdots\}$ then[^o27]

$[z^n]f(z) \sim \zeta^{-n} \frac{n^{-\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(-\alpha)} (\log n)^\gamma (\log\log n)^\delta \quad$ as $n \to \infty$

### Saddle-point method {#Saddle-point_method}

For generating functions including [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372).[^o29][^o30]

Intuitively, the biggest contribution to the [contour integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integral) is around the [saddle point](wikipedia://en/640249) and estimating near the saddle-point gives us an estimate for the whole contour.

If $F(z)$ is an admissible function,[^o31] then[^o32][^o33]

$[z^n] F(z) \sim \frac{F(\zeta)}{\zeta^{n+1} \sqrt{2 \pi f^{''}(\zeta)}} \quad$ as $n \to \infty$

where $F^'(\zeta) = 0$.

See also the [method of steepest descent](wikipedia://en/27082137).
