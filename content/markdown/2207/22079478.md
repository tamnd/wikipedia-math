# Erich Kamke

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Erich Kamke"}

**Erich Kamke** (18 August 1890 – 28 September 1961) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), who specialized in the theory of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). Also, his book on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553)[^o3] became a standard introduction to the field.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kamke was born in [Marienburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malbork), [West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Prussia), [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) (modern Malbork, Poland).

After attending school in [Stettin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stettin), Kamke studied mathematics and physics from 1909 at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). He was a volunteer in the signals force in World War I. In 1919, he married Dora Heimowitch, who was the daughter of a Jewish businessman. He earned his doctorate in 1919 at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) under [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) with thesis *Verallgemeinerungen des Waring-Hilbertschen Satzes* (Generalizations of the [Waring-Hilbert theorem](wikipedia://en/33569)). While teaching between 1920 and 1926, Kamke earned his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) in 1922. He was appointed as a professor at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) in 1926.

Because of his marriage and his opposition to [National Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialism), he was denounced by fellow mathematician [Erich Schönhardt](wikipedia://en/25330786) and eventually forced to retire in 1937.[^o6]

Following World War II, he was reappointed as a professor at the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen), and was instrumental in the organisation of a mathematical congress in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen) in autumn 1946, the first scientific congress in Germany after the war. In 1948, he re-established the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973), and was the chairman until 1952, when he became vice-president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868), which he remained until 1954.

He died in [Rottenburg am Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rottenburg_am_Neckar) from a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack).

## Works {#Works}

- *[Theory of Sets](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.6109/page/n3/mode/2up)*, Dover Publications, Inc., New York, 1950
- *Mengenlehre*, Sammlung Göschen/Walter de Gruyter, Berlin 1928
- *Das Lebesguesche Integral. Eine Einführung in die neuere Theorie der reellen Funktionen*, B. G. Teubner, Leipzig 1925
- *Differentialgleichungen reeller Funktionen*, [Akademische Verlagsgesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akademische_Verlagsgesellschaft), Leipzig 1930; ab der 4. (überarbeiteten) Auflage 1962 in zwei Bänden:
  - *Band 1: Gewöhnliche Differentialgleichungen*
  - *Band 2: Partielle Differentialgleichungen*
- *Einführung in die Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie*, S. Hirzel, Leipzig 1932[^o8]
- *Differentialgleichungen. Lösungsmethoden und Lösungen I. Gewöhnliche Differentialgleichungen*, Leipzig 1942
- *Differentialgleichungen. Lösungsmethoden und Lösungen II. Partielle Differentialgleichungen 1. Ordnung für eine gesuchte Funktion*, Leipzig 1944
- *Das Lebesgue-Stieltjes-Integral*, B. G. Teubner, Leipzig 1956
