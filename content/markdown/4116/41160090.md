# Gromov boundary

*Image: F2 Cayley Graph.svg.* The [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) with two generators. This is a [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) whose Gromov boundary is a [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172). Hyperbolic groups and their boundaries are important topics in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), as are Cayley graphs.

*Image: Hyperbolic domains 642.png.* The (6,4,2) triangular hyperbolic tiling. The [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342) corresponding to this tiling has a circle as its Gromov boundary.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gromov boundary"}

In mathematics, the **Gromov boundary** of a [δ-hyperbolic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) (especially a [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817)) is an abstract concept generalizing the boundary sphere of [hyperbolic space](wikipedia://en/318742). Conceptually, the Gromov boundary is the set of all [points at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139). For instance, the Gromov boundary of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line) is two points, corresponding to positive and negative infinity.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are several equivalent definitions of the Gromov boundary of a geodesic and proper δ-hyperbolic space. One of the most common uses equivalence classes of [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096#Metric_geometry) rays.[^o4]

Pick some point $O$ of a hyperbolic metric space $X$ to be the origin. A **geodesic ray** is a path given by an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) $\gamma:[0,\infty)\rightarrow X$ such that each segment $\gamma([0,t])$ is a path of shortest length from $O$ to $\gamma(t)$.

Two geodesics $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ are defined to be equivalent if there is a constant $K$ such that $d(\gamma_1(t),\gamma_2(t))\leq K$ for all $t$. The [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of $\gamma$ is denoted $[\gamma]$.

The **Gromov boundary** of a geodesic and proper hyperbolic metric space $X$ is the set $\partial X=\{[\gamma]|\gamma$ is a geodesic ray in $X\}$.

### Topology {#Topology}

It is useful to use the **Gromov product** of three points. The Gromov product of three points $x,y,z$ in a metric space is $(x,y)_z=1/2(d(x,z)+d(y,z)-d(x,y))$. In a [tree (graph theory)](wikipedia://en/48560), this measures how long the paths from $z$ to $x$ and $y$ stay together before diverging. Since hyperbolic spaces are tree-like, the Gromov product measures how long geodesics from $z$ to $x$ and $y$ stay close before diverging.

Given a point $p$ in the Gromov boundary, we define the sets $V(p,r)=\{q\in \partial X|$ there are geodesic rays $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ with $[\gamma_1]=p, [\gamma_2]=q$ and $\lim \inf_{s,t\rightarrow \infty}(\gamma_1(s),\gamma_2(t))_O\geq r\}$. These open sets form a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) for the topology of the Gromov boundary.

These open sets are just the set of geodesic rays which follow one fixed geodesic ray up to a distance $r$ before diverging.

This topology makes the Gromov boundary into a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem) space.

The number of [ends](wikipedia://en/6862105) of a hyperbolic group is the number of [components](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_set) of the Gromov boundary.

:::

## Gromov boundary of a group {#Gromov_boundary_of_a_group}

The Gromov boundary is a [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) invariant; that is, if two Gromov-hyperbolic metric spaces are quasi-isometric, then the quasi-isometry between them induces a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) between their boundaries.[^o7][^o8] This is important because homeomorphisms of compact spaces are much easier to understand than quasi-isometries of spaces.

This invariance allows to define the Gromov boundary of a [Gromov-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov-hyperbolic_group): if $G$ is such a group, its Gromov boundary is by definition that of any proper geodesic space on which $G$ acts [properly discontinuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous_action) and cocompactly (for instance its [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277)). This is well-defined as a topological space by the invariance under quasi-isometry and the [Milnor-Schwarz lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor-Schwarz_lemma).

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The Gromov boundary of a regular [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) of degree *d≥3* is a [Cantor space](wikipedia://en/352376).
- The Gromov boundary of [hyperbolic n-space](wikipedia://en/318742) is an *(n-1)*-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859).
- The Gromov boundary of the fundamental group of a compact [hyperbolic Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181#Hyperbolic_Riemann_surfaces) is the unit circle.
- The Gromov boundary of *most* hyperbolic groups is a [Menger sponge](wikipedia://en/208811).[^o11]

:::

## Variations {#Variations}

### Visual boundary of CAT(0) space {#Visual_boundary_of_CAT(0)_space}

For a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) [CAT(0) space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space) *X*, the visual boundary of *X*, like the Gromov boundary of δ-hyperbolic space, consists of equivalence class of asymptotic geodesic rays. However, the Gromov product cannot be used to define a topology on it. For example, in the case of a flat plane, any two geodesic rays issuing from a point not heading in opposite directions will have infinite Gromov product with respect to that point. The visual boundary is instead endowed with the **cone topology**. Fix a point *o* in *X*. Any boundary point can be represented by a unique geodesic ray issuing from *o*. Given a ray $\gamma$ issuing from *o*, and positive numbers *t* > 0 and *r* > 0, a [neighborhood basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_basis) at the boundary point $[\gamma]$ is given by sets of the form

$$U(\gamma, t, r) = \{[\gamma_1]\in\partial X | \gamma_1(0)=o, d( \gamma_1(t),\gamma(t))< r\}.$$

The cone topology as defined above is independent of the choice of *o*.

If *X* is [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space), then the visual boundary with the cone topology is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29). When *X* is both CAT(0) and proper geodesic δ-hyperbolic space, the cone topology coincides with the topology of Gromov boundary.[^o15]

:::

## Cannon's Conjecture {#Cannon's_Conjecture}

Cannon's conjecture concerns the classification of groups with a 2-sphere at infinity:

**Cannon's conjecture**: Every [Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29) [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817) with a 2-sphere at infinity [acts geometrically](wikipedia://en/12746935) on [hyperbolic 3-space](wikipedia://en/318742).[^o18]

The analog to this conjecture is known to be true for 1-spheres and false for spheres of all dimension greater than 2.

:::
