# Anomaly (natural sciences)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anomaly"}

In the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences), especially in [atmospheric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_sciences) and [Earth sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_sciences) involving [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics), an **anomaly** is a persisting [deviation](wikipedia://en/9252911) in a [physical quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_quantity) from its expected value, e.g., the [systematic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_effect) difference between a measurement and a trend or a model prediction.[^o2] Similarly, a **standardized anomaly** equals an anomaly divided by a [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590).[^o2] A group of anomalies can be analyzed spatially, as a map, or temporally, as a [time series](wikipedia://en/406624). It should not be confused for an isolated [outlier](wikipedia://en/160951). There are examples in atmospheric sciences and in geophysics.

:::

## Calculation {#Calculation}

The [location and scale measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_tendency) used in forming an anomaly time-series may either be constant or may themselves be a time series or a map. For example, if the original time series consisted of daily mean temperatures, the effect of seasonal cycles might be removed using a [deseasonalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deseasonalization) filter.

[Robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691), resistant to the effects of [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951), are sometimes used as the basis of the [transformation](wikipedia://en/10056274).[^o2]

### Atmospheric sciences {#Atmospheric_sciences}

In the atmospheric sciences, the climatological [annual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_cycle) is often used as the expected value. Famous atmospheric anomalies are for instance the [Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Oscillation) index (SOI) and the [North Atlantic oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_oscillation) index. SOI is the atmospheric component of [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Niño), while [NAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_oscillation) plays an important role for [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) [weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather) by modification of the exit of the Atlantic [storm track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_track). A [climate normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_normal) can also be used to derive a climate anomaly.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Anomaly (geophysics)"}

### Geophysics {#Geophysics}

- [Gravity anomaly](wikipedia://en/937535), difference between the observed gravity and a value predicted from a model
  - [Bouguer anomaly](wikipedia://en/743441), anomaly in gravimetry
  - [Free-air anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-air_anomaly), gravity anomaly that has been computed for latitude and corrected for elevation of the station
- [Iridium anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iridium_anomaly), an unusual abundance of what is normally a very rare element in the Earth's crust
- [Magnetic anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_anomaly), local variation in the Earth's magnetic field
  - [Bangui magnetic anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangui_magnetic_anomaly), in central Africa
  - [Kursk Magnetic Anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Magnetic_Anomaly), territory rich in iron ores located within Kursk Oblast, Belgorod Oblast, and Oryol Oblast
  - [Temagami Magnetic Anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temagami_Magnetic_Anomaly), large buried geologic structure in the Temagami region of Ontario, Canada
