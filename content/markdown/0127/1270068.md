# Pierre-Louis Lions

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre-Louis Lions"}

**Pierre-Louis Lions** (;[^o3] born 11 August 1956) is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is known for a number of contributions to the fields of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). He was a recipient of the 1994 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lions entered the [École normale supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29) in 1975, and received his doctorate from the [University of Pierre and Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pierre_and_Marie_Curie) in 1979.[^o5] He holds the position of Professor of *[Partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) and their applications* at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) in Paris as well as a position at [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique).[^o6][^o7] Since 2014, he has also been a visiting professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o8]

In 1979, Lions married Lila Laurenti, with whom he has one son. Lions' parents were Andrée Olivier and the renowned mathematician [Jacques-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/3735500), at the time a professor at the [University of Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nancy).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1994, while working at the [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University), Lions received the International Mathematical Union's prestigious [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859). He was cited for his contributions to [viscosity solutions](wikipedia://en/2300687), the [Boltzmann equation](wikipedia://en/1026522), and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). He has also received the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences)' [Prix Paul Doistau–Émile Blutet](wikipedia://en/63735602) (in 1986) and [Ampère Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampère_Prize) (in 1992).

He was an invited professor at the [Conservatoire national des arts et métiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatoire_national_des_arts_et_métiers) (2000).[^o10] He is a doctor honoris causa of [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University)[^o11] ([Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh)), [EPFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (2010),[^o12] [Narvik University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narvik_University_College) (2014), and of the [City University of Hong-Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_University_of_Hong_Kong) and is listed as an [ISI highly cited researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_highly_cited_researcher).[^o13]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

### Operator theory {#Operator_theory}

Lions' earliest work dealt with the [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) of [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932). His first published article, in 1977, was a contribution to the vast literature on convergence of certain iterative algorithms to [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) of a given [nonexpansive self-map](wikipedia://en/469760) of a closed convex subset of Hilbert space.L77[^o16] In collaboration with his thesis advisor [Haïm Brézis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haïm_Brézis), Lions gave new results about [maximal monotone operators](wikipedia://en/48260#Monotonicity_in_functional_analysis) in Hilbert space, proving one of the first convergence results for Bernard Martinet and [R. Tyrrell Rockafellar](wikipedia://en/26300644)'s [proximal point algorithm](wikipedia://en/51026323).BL78[^o17] In the time since, there have been a large number of modifications and improvements of such results.[^o18]

With Bertrand Mercier, Lions proposed a "forward-backward splitting algorithm" for finding a zero of the sum of two maximal monotone operators.LM79 Their algorithm can be viewed as an abstract version of the well-known Douglas−Rachford and Peaceman−Rachford numerical algorithms for computation of solutions to [parabolic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/7466971). The Lions−Mercier algorithms and their proof of convergence have been particularly influential in the literature on [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and its applications to [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). A similar method was studied at the same time by Gregory Passty.[^o19][^o17]

### Calculus of variations {#Calculus_of_variations}

The mathematical study of the steady-state [Schrödinger–Newton equation](wikipedia://en/1905371), also called the *Choquard equation*, was initiated in a seminal article of [Elliott Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb).[^o21] It is inspired by [plasma physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_physics) via a [standard approximation technique](wikipedia://en/475393) in [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry). Lions showed that one could apply standard methods such as the [mountain pass theorem](wikipedia://en/4408952), together with some technical work of [Walter Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Alexander_Strauss), in order to show that a generalized steady-state Schrödinger–Newton equation with a radially symmetric generalization of the gravitational potential is necessarily solvable by a radially symmetric function.L80

The partial differential equation

$$\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x_1^2}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x_n^2}=f(u)$$

has received a great deal of attention in the mathematical literature. Lions' extensive work on this equation is concerned with the existence of rotationally symmetric solutions as well as estimates and existence for boundary value problems of various type.L82a In the interest of studying solutions on all of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), where standard compactness theory does not apply, Lions established a number of compactness results for functions with symmetry.L82b With [Henri Berestycki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Berestycki) and Lambertus Peletier, Lions used standard ODE [shooting methods](wikipedia://en/498427) to directly study the existence of rotationally symmetric solutions.BLP81 However, sharper results were obtained two years later by Berestycki and Lions by variational methods. They considered the solutions of the equation as rescalings of minima of a constrained optimization problem, based upon a modified [Dirichlet energy](wikipedia://en/14377820). Making use of the Schwarz symmetrization, there exists a minimizing sequence for the infimization problem which consists of positive and rotationally symmetric functions. So they were able to show that there is a minimum which is also rotationally symmetric and nonnegative.BL83a By adapting the critical point methods of [Felix Browder](wikipedia://en/10201445), [Paul Rabinowitz](wikipedia://en/22335165), and others, Berestycki and Lions also demonstrated the existence of infinitely many (not always positive) radially symmetric solutions to the PDE.BL83b [Maria Esteban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/María_J._Esteban) and Lions investigated the nonexistence of solutions in a number of unbounded domains with Dirichlet boundary data.EL82 Their basic tool is a Pohozaev-type identity, as previously reworked by Berestycki and Lions.BL83a They showed that such identities can be effectively used with [Nachman Aronszajn](wikipedia://en/13496987)'s unique continuation theorem to obtain the triviality of solutions under some general conditions.[^o22] Significant ["a priori" estimates](wikipedia://en/30669047) for solutions were found by Lions in collaboration with [Djairo Guedes de Figueiredo](wikipedia://en/46612953) and [Roger Nussbaum](wikipedia://en/61891313).FLN82

In more general settings, Lions introduced the "concentration-compactness principle", which characterizes when minimizing sequences of functionals may fail to subsequentially converge. His first work dealt with the case of translation-invariance, with applications to several problems of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), including the Choquard equation.L84a He was also able to extend parts of his work with Berestycki to settings without any rotational symmetry.L84b By making use of [Abbas Bahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Bahri)'s topological methods and min-max theory, Bahri and Lions were able to establish multiplicity results for these problems.BL88 Lions also considered the problem of dilation invariance, with natural applications to optimizing functions for dilation-invariant functional inequalities such as the [Sobolev inequality](wikipedia://en/4665038).L85a He was able to apply his methods to give a new perspective on previous works on geometric problems such as the [Yamabe problem](wikipedia://en/11757994) and [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484).L85b With Thierry Cazenave, Lions applied his concentration-compactness results to establish [orbital stability](wikipedia://en/28842205) of certain symmetric solutions of [nonlinear Schrödinger equations](wikipedia://en/1422748) which admit variational interpretations and energy-conserving solutions.CL82

### Transport and Boltzmann equations {#Transport_and_Boltzmann_equations}

In 1988, [François Golse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Golse), Lions, [Benoît Perthame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benoit_Perthame), and Rémi Sentis studied the [transport equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_equation), which is a first-order linear partial differential equation.GLPS88 They showed that if the first-order coefficients are randomly chosen according to some [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543), then the corresponding function values are distributed with regularity which is enhanced from the original probability distribution. These results were later extended by DiPerna, Lions, and Meyer.DLM91 In the physical sense, such results, known as *velocity-averaging lemmas*, correspond to the fact that macroscopic observables have greater smoothness than their microscopic rules directly indicate. According to [Cédric Villani](wikipedia://en/27172341), it is unknown if it is possible to instead use the explicit representation of solutions of the transport equation to derive these properties.[^o24]

The classical [Picard–Lindelöf theorem](wikipedia://en/666177) deals with integral curves of [Lipschitz-continuous](wikipedia://en/51731) [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641). By viewing integral curves as [characteristic curves](wikipedia://en/751933) for a transport equation in multiple dimensions, Lions and [Ronald DiPerna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_DiPerna) initiated the broader study of integral curves of [Sobolev](wikipedia://en/611964) vector fields.DL89a DiPerna and Lions' results on the transport equation were later extended by [Luigi Ambrosio](wikipedia://en/25337146) to the setting of [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673), and by [Alessio Figalli](wikipedia://en/38609892) to the context of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895).[^o25]

DiPerna and Lions were able to prove the global existence of solutions to the [Boltzmann equation](wikipedia://en/1026522).DL89b Later, by applying the methods of [Fourier integral operators](wikipedia://en/10138003), Lions established estimates for the Boltzmann collision operator, thereby finding compactness results for solutions of the Boltzmann equation.L94 As a particular application of his compactness theory, he was able to show that solutions subsequentially converge at infinite time to Maxwell distributions.[^o24] DiPerna and Lions also established a similar result for the [Maxwell−Vlasov equations](wikipedia://en/1991528).DL89c[^o26]

### Viscosity solutions {#Viscosity_solutions}

[Michael Crandall](wikipedia://en/35265777) and Lions introduced the notion of [viscosity solution](wikipedia://en/2300687), which is a kind of generalized solution of [Hamilton–Jacobi equations](wikipedia://en/897539). Their definition is significant since they were able to establish a [well-posedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-posedness) theory in such a generalized context.CL83 The basic theory of viscosity solutions was further worked out in collaboration with [Lawrence Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Evans).CEL84 Using a min-max quantity, Lions and Jean-Michel Lasry considered mollification of functions on [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) which preserve analytic phenomena.LL86 Their approximations are naturally applicable to Hamilton-Jacobi equations, by regularizing sub- or super-solutions. Using such techniques, Crandall and Lions extended their analysis of Hamilton-Jacobi equations to the infinite-dimensional case, proving a comparison principle and a corresponding uniqueness theorem.CL85

Crandall and Lions investigated the numerical analysis of their viscosity solutions, proving convergence results both for a [finite difference](wikipedia://en/39147) scheme and [artificial viscosity](wikipedia://en/2785898).CL84

The comparison principle underlying Crandall and Lions' notion of viscosity solution makes their definition naturally applicable to second-order [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787), given the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104).[^o28]IL90 Crandall, Ishii, and Lions' survey article on viscosity solutions for such equations has become a standard reference work.CIL92

### Mean field games {#Mean_field_games}

With Jean-Michel Lasry, Lions has contributed to the development of [mean-field game theory](wikipedia://en/38306273).LL07
