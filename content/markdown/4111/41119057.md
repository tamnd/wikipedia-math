# Assouad dimension

*Image: Assouad dimension.svg.* The Assouad dimension of the [Sierpiński triangle](wikipedia://en/29638) is equal to its [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294), $\alpha = \frac{\log(3)}{\log(2)}$. In the illustration, we see that for a particular choice of $r$, $R$, and $x$, $N_{r}(B_{R}(x) \cap E) = 3 = 2^\alpha = \left( \frac{R}{r} \right)^{\alpha}.$ For other choices, the constant $C$ may be greater than 1, but is still bounded.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Assouad dimension"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [fractal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_geometry) — the **Assouad dimension** is a definition of [fractal dimension](wikipedia://en/285907) for subsets of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). It was introduced by [Patrice Assouad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Assouad) in his 1977 [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) and later published in 1979,[^o2] although the same notion had been studied in 1928 by [Georges Bouligand](wikipedia://en/34789355).[^o3] As well as being used to study fractals, the Assouad dimension has also been used to study [quasiconformal mappings](wikipedia://en/2749226) and [embeddability problems](wikipedia://en/52119).

:::

## Definition {#Definition}

The *Assouad dimension* of $X, d_A(X)$, is the infimum of all $s$ such that $(X, \varsigma)$ is $(M, s)$-homogeneous for some $M \ge 1$.[^o6]

Let $(X, d)$ be a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), and let $E$ be a non-empty subset of $X$. For $r > 0$, let $N_{r}(E)$ denote the least number of metric [open balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) of radius less than or equal to $r$ with which it is possible to [cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) the set $E$. The Assouad dimension of $E$ is defined to be the [infimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) $\alpha \ge 0$ for which there exist positive constants $C$ and $\rho$ so that, whenever

$$0 < r < R \leq \rho,$$

the following bound holds:

$$\sup_{x \in E} N_{r}(B_{R}(x) \cap E) \leq C \left( \frac{R}{r} \right)^{\alpha}.$$

The intuition underlying this definition is that, for a set $E$ with "ordinary" integer dimension $n$, the number of small balls of radius $r$ needed to cover the intersection of a larger ball of radius $R$ with $E$ will scale like $(R/r)^{n}$.

:::

## Relationships to other notions of dimension {#Relationships_to_other_notions_of_dimension}

- The Assouad dimension of a metric space is always greater than or equal to its [Assouad–Nagata dimension](wikipedia://en/68496655).[^o8]
- The Assouad dimension of a metric space is always greater than or equal to its [upper box dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-counting_dimension), which in turn is greater than or equal to the [Hausdorff dimension](wikipedia://en/14294).[^o9]
- The [Lebesgue covering dimension](wikipedia://en/398886) of a [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738) $X$ is the minimal Assouad dimension of any metric on $X$. In particular, for every metrizable space there is a metric for which the Assouad dimension is equal to the Lebesgue covering dimension.[^o9]
