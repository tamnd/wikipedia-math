# Jason Rosenhouse

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jason Rosenhouse"}

**Jason Rosenhouse** is an American author and professor of mathematics at [James Madison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_University). His research focuses on [algebraic graph theory](wikipedia://en/1620216), as well as [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513).[^o3] He ran the **Evolution Blog** at [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_%28magazine%29)'s [ScienceBlogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScienceBlogs),[^o4] where he was a frequent critic of [creationism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creationism),[^o5] he has contributed to the pro-evolution blog *[The Panda's Thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Panda's_Thumb_%28blog%29)*,[^o6] and he has written for the *[HuffPost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* on topics such as the [Higgs boson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_boson).[^o7] He is also the author and editor of several books on [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446).

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Jason grew up in New Jersey. While in middle school and high school, he was a prolific chess player, attending both tournaments and chess camp.[^o9][^o10]

## Education {#Education}

Rosenhouse has a bachelor's degree from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in mathematics (1995), and an M.A. (1997) and PhD in mathematics (2000), both from [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College).[^o3] His PhD thesis was entitled "Isoperimetric numbers of certain Cayley graphs associated to PSL (2, \[zeta subscript n\])".[^o12]

## Career {#Career}

In 2000, Rosenhouse accepted a position at [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University)'s mathematics department, at a time when the state school board was embroiled in a dispute over teaching creationism in schools. The school board's elimination of evolution from science textbooks introduced him to the creationist community, and he says that his time spent with them has convinced him that "the task of reconciling science with faith is far more difficult than is sometimes pretended."[^o14] He was appointed assistant professor at [James Madison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_University) in 2003 and full professor in 2014.
