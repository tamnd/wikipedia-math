# Tannakian formalism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tannakian formalism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Tannakian category** is a particular kind of [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) *C*, equipped with some extra structure relative to a given [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K*. The role of such [categories](wikipedia://en/53759) *C* is to generalise the category of [linear representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of an [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *G* defined over *K*. A number of major applications of the theory have been made, or might be made in pursuit of some of the central [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) of contemporary [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

The name is taken from [Tadao Tannaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadao_Tannaka) and [Tannaka–Krein duality](wikipedia://en/2588624), a theory about [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326) *G* and their [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200). The theory was developed first in the school of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). It was later reconsidered by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), and some simplifications made. The pattern of the theory is that of [Grothendieck's Galois theory](wikipedia://en/372470), which is a theory about finite [permutation representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_representation_%28symmetric_group%29) of groups *G* which are [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266).

The gist of the theory is that the [fiber functor](wikipedia://en/41040671) Φ of the Galois theory is replaced by an exact and faithful **tensor functor** *F* from *C* to the category of [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over *K*. The group of [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) of Φ to itself, which turns out to be a profinite group in the Galois theory, is replaced by the group *G* of natural transformations of *F* into itself, that respect the tensor structure. This is in general not an algebraic group but a more general [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) that is an [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) of algebraic groups ([pro-algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-algebraic_group)), and *C* is then found to be [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of finite-dimensional linear representations of *G*.

More generally, it may be that fiber functors *F* as above only exists to categories of finite-dimensional vector spaces over non-trivial [extension fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_field) *L*/*K*. In such cases the group scheme *G* is replaced by a [gerbe](wikipedia://en/822043) $\mathcal G$ on the [fpqc site](wikipedia://en/10538912) of Spec(*K*), and *C* is then equivalent to the category of (finite-dimensional) representations of $\mathcal G$.

## Formal definition of Tannakian categories {#Formal_definition_of_Tannakian_categories}

Let *K* be a field and *C* a *K*-linear [abelian](wikipedia://en/45063) [rigid](wikipedia://en/25383437) tensor (i.e., a [symmetric monoidal](wikipedia://en/3865249)) category such that $\mathrm{End}(\mathbf{1})\cong K$. Then *C* is a **Tannakian category** (over *K*) if there is an extension field *L* of *K* such that there exists a *K*-linear [exact](wikipedia://en/453945) and [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor) tensor functor (i.e., a [strong monoidal functor](wikipedia://en/4278182)) *F* from *C* to the [category of finite dimensional *L*-vector spaces](wikipedia://en/72243857). A Tannakian category over *K* is **neutral** if such exact faithful tensor functor *F* exists with *L=K*.[^o3]

## Applications {#Applications}

The tannakian construction is used in relations between [Hodge structure](wikipedia://en/10297305) and [l-adic representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-adic_representation). Morally, the philosophy of motives tells us that the Hodge structure and the Galois representation associated to an algebraic variety are related to each other. The closely related algebraic groups [Mumford–Tate group](wikipedia://en/27137708) and [motivic Galois group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_Galois_group) arise from categories of Hodge structures, category of Galois representations and motives through Tannakian categories. Mumford-Tate conjecture proposes that the algebraic groups arising from the Hodge strucuture and the Galois representation by means of Tannakian categories are isomorphic to one another up to connected components.

Those areas of application are closely connected to the theory of [motives](wikipedia://en/430680). Another place in which Tannakian categories have been used is in connection with the [Grothendieck–Katz p-curvature conjecture](wikipedia://en/3135542); in other words, in bounding [monodromy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monodromy_group).

The [Geometric Satake equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Satake_equivalence) establishes an equivalence between representations of the [Langlands dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_dual) group ${}^L G$ of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) *G* and certain equivariant [perverse sheaves](wikipedia://en/1714012) on the [affine Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/16896827) associated to *G*. This equivalence provides a non-combinatorial construction of the Langlands dual group. It is proved by showing that the mentioned category of perverse sheaves is a Tannakian category and identifying its Tannaka dual group with ${}^L G$.

:::

## Extensions {#Extensions}

(Wedhorn 2004) has established partial Tannaka duality results in the situation where the category is *R*-linear, where *R* is no longer a field (as in classical Tannakian duality), but certain [valuation rings](wikipedia://en/966856). (Iwanari 2018) has initiated and developed Tannaka duality in the context of [infinity-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity-categories).

:::
