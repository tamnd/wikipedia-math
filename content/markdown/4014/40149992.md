# Burstiness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Burstiness"}

In [temporal networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporal_network), **burstiness** is the intermittent increases and decreases in activity or [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) of an event.[^o2][^o3] One measure of burstiness is the [Fano factor](wikipedia://en/3671126)—a ratio between the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) and [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) of counts.

:::

Burstiness is observable in natural phenomena, such as [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disaster), or other phenomena, such as [network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network)/[data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data)/[email](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email) network traffic[^o4][^o5] or [vehicular traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_flow).[^o6] Burstiness is, in part, due to changes in the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of inter-event times.[^o7] Distributions of bursty processes or events are characterised by [heavy, or fat, tails](wikipedia://en/4357461).[^o2]

Burstiness of inter-contact time between nodes in a [time-varying network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-varying_network) can decidedly slow spreading processes over the network. This is of great interest for studying the spread of information and disease. [^o8]

## Burstiness score {#Burstiness_score}

One relatively simple measure of burstiness is burstiness score. The burstiness score of a subset $t$ of time period $T$ relative to an event $e$ is a measure of how often $e$ appears in $t$ compared to its occurrences in $T$. It is defined by

$$\mathrm{Burst}(e, t) = \left (\frac{E_t}{E} - \frac{1}{T}\right )$$

Where $E_t$ is the total number of occurrences of event $e$ in subset $t$ and $E$ is the total number of occurrences of $e$ in $T$.

Burstiness score can be used to determine if $t$ is a "bursty period" relative to $e$. A positive score says that $e$ occurs more often during subset $t$ than over total time $T$, making $t$ a bursty period. A negative score implies otherwise. [^o10]
