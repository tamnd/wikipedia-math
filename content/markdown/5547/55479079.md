# Howson property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Howson property"}

In the mathematical subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **Howson property**, also known as the **finitely generated intersection property (FGIP)**, is the property of a group saying that the intersection of any two finitely generated subgroups of this group is again finitely generated. The property is named after [Albert G. Howson](wikipedia://en/72427614) who in a 1954 paper established that [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735) have this property.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A [group](wikipedia://en/19447) $G$ is said to have the **Howson property** if for every [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) $H,K$ of $G$ their intersection $H\cap K$ is again a finitely generated subgroup of $G$.[^o5]

:::

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

- Every finite group has the Howson property.
- The group $G=F(a,b)\times \mathbb Z$ does not have the Howson property. Specifically, if $t$ is the generator of the $\mathbb Z$ factor of $G$, then for $H=F(a,b)$ and $K=\langle a,tb\rangle \le G$, one has $H\cap K=\operatorname{ncl}_{F(a,b)}(a)$. Therefore, $H\cap K$ is not finitely generated.[^o7]
- If $\Sigma$ is a compact surface then the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ of $\Sigma$ has the Howson property.[^o8]
- A [free-by-(infinite cyclic group)](wikipedia://en/10377468) $F_n\rtimes \mathbb Z$, where $n\ge 2$, never has the Howson property.[^o9]
- In view of the recent proof of the [Virtually Haken conjecture](wikipedia://en/4692183) and the [Virtually fibered conjecture](wikipedia://en/2823145) for 3-manifolds, previously established results imply that if *M* is a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold then $\pi_1(M)$ does not have the Howson property.[^o10]
- Among 3-manifold groups, there are many examples that do and do not have the Howson property. 3-manifold groups with the Howson property include fundamental groups of hyperbolic 3-manifolds of infinite volume, 3-manifold groups based on [Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_geometry) and [Nil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_geometry) geometries, as well as 3-manifold groups obtained by some connected sum and [JSJ decomposition](wikipedia://en/228599) constructions.[^o10]
- For every $n\ge 1$ the [Baumslag–Solitar group](wikipedia://en/11485892) $BS(1,n)=\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}at=a^n\rangle$ has the Howson property.[^o7]
- If *G* is group where every finitely generated subgroup is [Noetherian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_group) then *G* has the Howson property. In particular, all [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) and all [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569) have the Howson property.
- Every polycyclic-by-finite group has the Howson property.[^o11]
- If $A,B$ are groups with the Howson property then their free product $A\ast B$ also has the Howson property.[^o12] More generally, the Howson property is preserved under taking amalgamated free products and [HNN-extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNN-extension) of groups with the Howson property over finite subgroups.[^o13]
- In general, the Howson property is rather sensitive to amalgamated products and HNN extensions over infinite subgroups. In particular, for free groups $F,F'$ and an infinite cyclic group $C$, the amalgamated free product $F\ast_C F'$ has the Howson property if and only if $C$ is a maximal cyclic subgroup in both $F$ and $F'$.[^o14]
- A [right-angled Artin group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-angled_Artin_group) $A(\Gamma)$ has the Howson property if and only if every connected component of $\Gamma$ is a complete graph.[^o15]
- [Limit groups](wikipedia://en/78938535) have the Howson property.[^o16]
- It is not known whether $SL(3,\mathbb Z)$ has the Howson property.[^o17]
- For $n\ge 4$ the group $SL(n,\mathbb Z)$ contains a subgroup isomorphic to $F(a,b)\times F(a,b)$ and does not have the Howson property.[^o17]
- Many [small cancellation groups](wikipedia://en/19882883) and [Coxeter groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_groups), satisfying the "perimeter reduction" condition on their presentation, are locally quasiconvex [word-hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817) and therefore have the Howson property.[^o18][^o19]
- One-relator groups $G=\langle x_1,\dots, x_k \mid r^n=1\rangle$, where $n\ge |r|$ are also locally quasiconvex [word-hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817) and therefore have the Howson property.[^o20]
- The [Grigorchuk group](wikipedia://en/18707371) *G* of intermediate growth does not have the Howson property.[^o21]
- The Howson property is not a [first-order](wikipedia://en/10983) property, that is the Howson property cannot be characterized by a collection of first order [group language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_language) formulas.[^o22]
- A free [pro-p group](wikipedia://en/1842075) $F$ satisfies a topological version of the Howson property: If $H,K$ are topologically finitely generated closed subgroups of $F$ then their intersection $H\cap K$ is topologically finitely generated.[^o23]
- For any fixed integers $m\ge 2,n\ge 1,d\ge 1,$ a \`\`generic" $m$-generator $n$-relator group $G=\langle x_1,\dots x_m|r_1,\dots, r_n\rangle$ has the property that for any $d$-generated subgroups $H,K\le G$ their intersection $H\cap K$ is again finitely generated.[^o24]
- The [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) $\mathbb Z\ wr\ \mathbb Z$ does not have the Howson property.[^o25]
- [Thompson's group](wikipedia://en/612245) $F$ does not have the Howson property, since it contains $\mathbb Z\ wr\ \mathbb Z$.[^o26]
