# Pierre François Verhulst

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre François Verhulst"}

*Image: Pierre Francois Verhulst.jpg.* Pierre François Verhulst

**Pierre François Verhulst** (28 October 1804 – 15 February 1849) was a Belgian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a doctor in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) from the [University of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghent) in 1825. He is best known for the [logistic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistic_growth) model.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pierre François Verhulst was born in the French Empire, now Brussels. His family was a wealthy family, being able to give him a high-quality education and promoting his later career. He could obtain his doctorate on August 3rd in 1825.[^o3]

## Logistic equation {#Logistic_equation}

Verhulst developed the logistic function in a series of three papers between 1838 and 1847, based on research on modeling [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) that he conducted in the mid-1830s, under the guidance of [Adolphe Quetelet](wikipedia://en/184215); see Logistic function for details.[^n1]

Verhulst published in (Verhulst 1838) the equation:

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = rN - \alpha N^2$$

where *N*(*t*) represents number of individuals at time *t*, *r* the intrinsic growth rate, and *$\alpha$* is the density-dependent crowding effect (also known as intraspecific competition). In this equation, the population equilibrium (sometimes referred to as the [carrying capacity](wikipedia://en/47544), *K*), $N^*$, is

$$N^* = \frac{r}{\alpha}.$$

In (Verhulst 1845) he named the solution the [logistic curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistic_curve).

Later, [Raymond Pearl](wikipedia://en/1732409) and [Lowell Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_Reed) popularized the equation, but with a presumed equilibrium, *K*, as

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = r N \left(1 - \frac {N}{K} \right)$$

where *K* sometimes represents the maximum number of individuals that the environment can support. In relation to the density-dependent crowding effect, $\alpha = \frac{r}{K}$. The Pearl-Reed logistic equation can be integrated exactly, and has solution

$$N(t) = \frac{K}{1+ C K e^{-rt}}$$

where *C* = 1/*N*(0) − 1/*K* is determined by the initial condition *N*(0). The solution can also be written as a weighted [harmonic mean](wikipedia://en/14463) of the initial condition and the carrying capacity,

$$\frac{1}{N(t)} = \frac{1-e^{-rt}}{K}+ \frac{e^{-rt}}{N(0)}.$$

Although the continuous-time logistic equation is often compared to the [logistic map](wikipedia://en/18137) because of similarity of form, it is actually more closely related to the [Beverton–Holt model](wikipedia://en/11185796) of fisheries recruitment.

The concept of [R/K selection theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R/K_selection_theory) derives its name from the competing dynamics of [exponential growth](wikipedia://en/191933) and [carrying capacity](wikipedia://en/47544) introduced by the equations above.
