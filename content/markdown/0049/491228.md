# Alfréd Haar

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alfréd Haar"}

**Alfréd Haar** (Haar Alfréd; 11 October 1885, [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest) – 16 March 1933, [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged)) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). In 1904 he began to study at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). His doctorate was supervised by [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). The [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645), [Haar wavelet](wikipedia://en/50905), and [Haar transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haar_transform) are named in his honor. Between 1912 and 1919 he taught at [Franz Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_University) in Kolozsvár (now [Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca), Romania). Together with [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126), he made the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged) a centre of mathematics. He also founded the *[Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Scientiarum_Mathematicarum)* journal together with Riesz.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Matematikai konferencia Szegeden, 1928.jpg.* Conference at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged), 1928. *Left to right, standing:* [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126), [Béla Kerékjártó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Kerékjártó), Alfréd Haar, [Gyula Kőnig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Kőnig), Rudolf Ortvay, *on chairs:* [József Kürschák](wikipedia://en/6023762), [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906), O.D. Kellog, [Leopold Fejér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Fejér), *sitting on the floor:* [Tibor Radó](wikipedia://en/1490393), István Lipka, [László Kalmár](wikipedia://en/1988240), Pál Szász

Haar was born to a [Hungarian-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian-Jewish)[^o4] family in Budapest on 11 October 1885 to parents Ignác Haar and Emma Fuchs. He graduated in 1903 from the secondary school [Fasori Evangélikus Gimnázium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasori_Gimnázium) where he was a student of [László Rátz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/László_Rátz). He started his university studies in Budapest, later moving on to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) reading mathematics and sciences. Among the many famous professors he was taught by, he could count [Loránd Eötvös](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loránd_Eötvös), [József Kürschák](wikipedia://en/6023762), [Constantin Carathéodory](wikipedia://en/470625), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302), [Felix Christian Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Christian_Klein) and [Ernst Zermelo](wikipedia://en/178655).

During years of the secondary school, he collaborated with the mathematical journal for secondary school students [Középiskolai Matematikai Lapok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Középiskolai_Matematikai_Lapok), and won the national Eötvös Loránd Mathematical Competition. He enrolled to the [Technical University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_University_of_Technology_and_Economics) as a student of chemical engineering, but in the same year he moved on to the [University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University), and after a year to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). His doctoral research was supervised by Hilbert graduating in June 1909. His 49-page thesis studies systems of [Sturm–Liouville functions](wikipedia://en/490990) and [spherical functions](wikipedia://en/17708915), introducing the now widely used Haar orthogonal systems. In the same year he habilitated to become a private professor of the university.

In 1912, the [Franz Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_University) in Kolozsvár, [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (now [Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca) in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania)) invited him along with [Gyula Farkas](wikipedia://en/6107158) and [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126) to join as faculty, and he became the professor of 'Quatitics'. A number of his lecture notes from the time became established books later. After the [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon), which ceded [Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transylvania) to Romania, the university had to move to [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged), the closest city within the new boundaries, where he with Riesz established the Centre of Mathematics, and the first internationally recognised Hungarian mathematical journal, the *Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum*.

One of his doctoral students at the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged) was [Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Béla_Szőkefalvi-Nagy).

Haar died of stomach cancer on 16 March 1933.

## Fields of research {#Fields_of_research}

His results are from the fields of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315), in particular he researched [orthogonal systems of functions](wikipedia://en/453041), [singular integrals](wikipedia://en/12791220), [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_functions), [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), [function approximation](wikipedia://en/336897) and [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).
