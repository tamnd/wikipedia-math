# Cancellation property

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cancellation property"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the notion of **cancellativity** (or *cancellability*) is a generalization of the notion of [invertibility](wikipedia://en/45199) that does not rely on an inverse element.

:::

An element $a$ in a [magma](wikipedia://en/141916) $(M,*)$ has the **left cancellation property** (or is **left-cancellative**) if for all $b$ and $c$ in $M$ , $a*b=a*c$ always implies that $b=c$ .

An element $a$ in a magma $(M,*)$ has the **right cancellation property** (or is **right-cancellative**) if for all $b$ and $c$ in $M$ , $b*a=c*a$ always implies that $b=c$ .

An element $a$ in a magma $(M,*)$ has the **two-sided cancellation property** (or is **cancellative**) if it is both left- and right-cancellative.

A magma $(M,*)$ is left-cancellative if all $a$ in the magma are left cancellative, and similar definitions apply for the right cancellative or two-sided cancellative properties.

In a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799), a left-invertible element is left-cancellative, and analogously for right and two-sided. If $a^{-1}$ is the left inverse of $a$ , then $a*b=a*c$ implies $a^{-1}*(a*b)=a^{-1}*(a*c)$, which implies $b=c$ by associativity.

For example, every [quasigroup](wikipedia://en/25223), and thus every [group](wikipedia://en/19447), is cancellative.

## Interpretation {#Interpretation}

To say that an element $a$ in a magma $(M,*)$ is left-cancellative, is to say that the function $g:x\mapsto a*x$ is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) where $x$ is also an element of $M$ .[^o3] That the function $g$ is injective implies that given some equality of the form $a*x=b$, where the only unknown is $x$ , there is only one possible value of $x$ satisfying the equality. More precisely, we are able to define some function $f$ , the inverse of $g$ , such that for all $x$ , $f(g(x))=f(a*x)=x$. Put another way, for all $x$ and *y* in $M$ , if $a*x=a*y$, then $x=y$ .[^o4]

Similarly, to say that the element $a$ is right-cancellative, is to say that the function $h:x\mapsto x*a$ is injective and that for all $x$ and *y* in $M$ , if $x*a=y*a$, then $x=y$ .

## Examples of cancellative monoids and semigroups {#Examples_of_cancellative_monoids_and_semigroups}

The positive (equally non-negative) integers form a cancellative [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) under addition. The non-negative integers form a cancellative [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652) under addition. Each of these is an example of a cancellative magma that is not a quasigroup.

Any free semigroup or monoid obeys the cancellative law, and in general, any semigroup or monoid that embeds into a group (as the above examples clearly do) will obey the cancellative law.

In a different vein, (a subsemigroup of) the multiplicative semigroup of elements of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that are not zero divisors (which is just the set of all nonzero elements if the ring in question is a [domain](wikipedia://en/487627), like the integers) has the cancellation property. This remains valid even if the ring in question is noncommutative and/or nonunital.

## Non-cancellative algebraic structures {#Non-cancellative_algebraic_structures}

Although the cancellation property holds for addition and subtraction of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), it does not hold for multiplication due to exception of multiplication by [zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29). The cancellation property does not hold for any nontrivial structure that has an [absorbing element](wikipedia://en/14206224) (such as $0$).

Whereas the integers and real numbers are not cancellative under multiplication, with the removal of $0$, they each form a cancellative structure under multiplication.
