# Homological conjectures in commutative algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Homological conjectures in commutative algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **homological conjectures** have been a focus of research activity in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) since the early 1960s. They concern a number of interrelated (sometimes surprisingly so) conjectures relating various [homological](wikipedia://en/139410) properties of a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) to its internal ring structure, particularly its [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) and [depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depth_%28algebra%29).

:::

The following list given by [Melvin Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster) is considered definitive for this area. In the sequel, $A, R$, and $S$ refer to [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346); $R$ will be a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) with maximal ideal $m_R$, and $M$ and $N$ are [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) $R$-modules.

1. **The Zero Divisor Theorem.** If $M \ne 0$ has finite [projective dimension](wikipedia://en/364488#Projective_resolutions) and $r \in R$ is not a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441) on $M$, then $r$ is not a zero divisor on $R$.
1. **Bass's Question.** If $M \ne 0$ has a finite [injective resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_resolution), then $R$ is a [Cohen–Macaulay ring](wikipedia://en/497407).
1. **The Intersection Theorem.** If $M \otimes_R N \ne 0$ has finite length, then the [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of *N* (i.e., the dimension of *R* modulo the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of *N*) is at most the [projective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_dimension) of *M*.
1. **The New Intersection Theorem.** Let $0 \to G_n\to\cdots \to G_0\to 0$ denote a finite complex of free *R*-modules such that $\bigoplus\nolimits_i H_i(G_{\bullet})$ has finite length but is not 0. Then the (Krull dimension) $\dim R \le n$.
1. **The Improved New Intersection Conjecture.** Let $0 \to G_n\to\cdots \to G_0\to 0$ denote a finite complex of free *R*-modules such that $H_i(G_{\bullet})$ has finite length for $i > 0$ and $H_0(G_{\bullet})$ has a minimal generator that is killed by a power of the maximal ideal of *R*. Then $\dim R \le n$.
1. **The Direct Summand Conjecture.** If $R \subseteq S$ is a module-finite ring extension with *R* regular (here, *R* need not be local but the problem reduces at once to the local case), then *R* is a direct summand of *S* as an *R*-module. The conjecture was proven by [Yves André](wikipedia://en/58118010) using a theory of [perfectoid spaces](wikipedia://en/39197973).[^o2]
1. **The Canonical Element Conjecture.** Let $x_1, \ldots, x_d$ be a [system of parameters](wikipedia://en/30823935) for *R*, let $F_\bullet$ be a free *R*-resolution of the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) of *R* with $F_0 = R$, and let $K_\bullet$ denote the [Koszul complex](wikipedia://en/519992) of *R* with respect to $x_1, \ldots, x_d$. Lift the identity map $R = K_0 \to F_0 = R$ to a map of complexes. Then no matter what the choice of system of parameters or lifting, the last map from $R = K_d \to F_d$ is not 0.
1. **Existence of Balanced Big Cohen–Macaulay Modules Conjecture.** There exists a (not necessarily finitely generated) *R*-module *W* such that *m<sub>R</sub>W ≠ W* and every system of parameters for *R* is a regular sequence on *W*.
1. **Cohen-Macaulayness of Direct Summands Conjecture.** If *R* is a direct summand of a regular ring *S* as an *R*-module, then *R* is Cohen–Macaulay (*R* need not be local, but the result reduces at once to the case where *R* is local).
1. **The Vanishing Conjecture for Maps of Tor.** Let $A \subseteq R \to S$ be homomorphisms where *R* is not necessarily local (one can reduce to that case however), with *A, S* regular and *R* finitely generated as an *A*-module. Let *W* be any *A*-module. Then the map $\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(W,R) \to \operatorname{Tor}_i^A(W,S)$ is zero for all $i \ge 1$.
1. **The Strong Direct Summand Conjecture.** Let $R \subseteq S$ be a map of complete local domains, and let *Q* be a height one prime ideal of *S* lying over $xR$, where *R* and $R/xR$ are both regular. Then $xR$ is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of *Q* considered as *R*-modules.
1. **Existence of Weakly Functorial Big Cohen-Macaulay Algebras Conjecture.** Let $R \to S$ be a local homomorphism of complete local domains. Then there exists an *R*-algebra *B<sub>R</sub>* that is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra for *R*, an *S*-algebra $B_S$ that is a balanced big Cohen-Macaulay algebra for *S*, and a homomorphism *B<sub>R</sub> → B<sub>S</sub>* such that the natural square given by these maps commutes.
1. **Serre's Conjecture on Multiplicities.** (cf. **[Serre's multiplicity conjectures](wikipedia://en/472700).**) Suppose that *R* is regular of dimension *d* and that $M \otimes_R N$ has finite length. Then $\chi(M, N)$, defined as the alternating sum of the lengths of the modules $\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(M, N)$ is 0 if $\dim M + \dim N < d$, and is positive if the sum is equal to *d*. (N.B. [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) proved that the sum cannot exceed *d*.)
1. **Small Cohen–Macaulay Modules Conjecture.** If *R* is complete, then there exists a finitely-generated *R*-module $M \ne 0$ such that some (equivalently every) system of parameters for *R* is a [regular sequence](wikipedia://en/505119) on *M*.
