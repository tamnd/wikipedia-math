# Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro"}

**Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro** (; 12 January 1853 – 6 August 1925) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3] He is most famous as the discoverer of [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus).

:::

With his former student [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), he wrote his most famous single publication,[^o4] a pioneering work on the calculus of [tensors](wikipedia://en/29965), signing it as Gregorio Ricci. This appears to be the only time that Ricci-Curbastro used the shortened form of his name in a publication, and continues to cause confusion.

Ricci-Curbastro also published important works in other fields, including a book on higher [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and infinitesimal analysis,[^o5] and papers on the theory of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), an area in which he extended the research begun by [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106).[^o6]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Completing his high school studies privately at only 16 years of age, he enrolled on the course of philosophy-mathematics at [Rome University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) (1869). The following year, the [Papal State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_State) fell, and so Gregorio was called by his father to the city of his birth, [Lugo di Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugo,_Emilia-Romagna). Subsequently, he attended courses at [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) during the year 1872 - 1873, then transferred to the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa).[^o8][^o9]

In 1875, he graduated in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) in physical sciences and mathematics with a thesis on [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309), entitled "On Fuchs's Research Concerning Linear Differential Equations". During his various travels, he was a student of the mathematicians [Enrico Betti](wikipedia://en/979158), [Eugenio Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760), [Ulisse Dini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulisse_Dini) and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887).

## Studies on absolute differential calculus {#Studies_on_absolute_differential_calculus}

In 1877 Ricci-Curbastro obtained a scholarship at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich), Bavaria, and he later worked as an assistant of Ulisse Dini, his teacher.

In 1880, he became a lecturer of mathematics at the University of Padua, where he dealt with [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) and differential quadratic forms.

He formed a research group in which Tullio Levi-Civita worked, with whom he wrote the fundamental treatise on absolute differential calculus (also known as Ricci calculus) with coordinates or tensor calculus on [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), which then became the [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) of the subsequent theory of [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736)'s general relativity. In fact, absolute differential calculus had a crucial role in developing the theory, as is shown in a letter written by Albert Einstein to Ricci-Curbastro's nephew. In this context, Ricci-Curbastro identified the so-called [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor), which would have a crucial role within that theory.

### Influences {#Influences}

The advent of tensor calculus in dynamics goes back to [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange), who originated the general treatment of a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), and to [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980), who was the first to think about geometry in an arbitrary number of dimensions. He was also influenced by the works of [Elwin Bruno Christoffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwin_Bruno_Christoffel) and of [Rudolf Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/339965) on the quadratic forms. In fact, it was essentially Christoffel's idea of [covariant differentiation](wikipedia://en/431848)[^o12] that allowed Ricci-Curbastro to make the greatest progress.[^o13]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Ricci-Curbastro received many honours for his contributions.

He is honoured by mentions in various Academies, amongst which are:

- The Veneto Institute of Science - Istituto veneto di scienze - letters and articles (from 1892), of which he was then president from 1916 to 1919.
- The Lincei Academy - [Accademia dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_dei_Lincei) - of which he was a member from 1899.
- The Academy of Padua - Accademia di Padova - from 1905.
- The Science Academy of Turin - Accademia delle Scienze di Torino - from 1918.
- The Galileian Academy of Science - Accademia Galileiana di Scienze, Lettere ed Arti - letters and articles, of which he was then president from 1920 to 1922.
- The [Academy of Sciences of the Institute of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Institute_of_Bologna) - Reale Accademia di Bologna - from 1922.
- The [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences) - Accademia Pontificia delle Scienze - from 1925.

He participated actively in political life, both in his native town and in Padua, and contributed with his projects to the Ravenna-area land drainage and the Lugo aqueduct.

An [asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid), [13642 Ricci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minor_planets:_13001–14000), is named after him.
