# James Ivory (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Ivory"}

**James Ivory**, [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) [KH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Guelphic_Order) LLD (17 February 1765 – 21 September 1842) was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He stated and proved [Ivory's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory's_lemma).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Ivory was born in [Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee), son of watchmaker [James Ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ivory_%28watchmaker%29). The family lived and worked on the High Street in Dundee.[^o5]

He was educated at [Dundee Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundee_Grammar_School). In 1779 he entered the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews), distinguishing himself especially in mathematics. He then studied [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology); but, after two sessions at St Andrews and one at [Edinburgh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh_University), he abandoned all idea of the church, and in 1786 he became an assistant-teacher of mathematics and natural philosophy in the newly established [Dundee Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_School_of_Dundee#The_English_School_and_Dundee_Academy). Three years later he became partner in, and manager of, a flax spinning company at Douglastown in [Forfarshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forfarshire), while continuing mathematical research as a hobby. He was essentially a self-trained mathematician, and was not only deeply versed in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), but also kept up with contemporary developments in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o6]

He published a memoir with an [analytical expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_expression) for the rectification of the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) in the *[Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh)* (1796), and two others on [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equations) (1799) and the [Kepler problem](wikipedia://en/2410571) (1802). In 1804 the flax-spinning company he managed was dissolved, and he obtained one of the mathematical chairs in the [Royal Military College, Great Marlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College,_Great_Marlow) (later moved to Sandhurst), at which he remained until 1816, when failing health obliged him to resign.

During this period he published several important memoirs in the *[Philosophical Transactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society)*, which earned for him the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) in 1814 and ensured his election as a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1815. Of special importance in the history of attractions is the first of these (1809), in which the attraction of a homogeneous ellipsoid upon an external point is reduced to the simpler case of the attraction of a related ellipsoid upon a corresponding point interior to it. This theorem is known as Ivory's theorem, though one of its [lemmas](wikipedia://en/18634), [Ivory's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory's_lemma), is better known today and is itself sometimes called Ivory's theorem. He also anonymously published an edition of [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107), which was described as having brought the difficult problems "more within the reach of ordinary understandings".[^o7] His later papers in the *Philosophical Transactions* discuss astronomical refractions, planetary perturbations, equilibrium of fluid masses, etc. For his astronomical investigations he received a royal medal in 1826 and again in 1839.

In 1831, on the recommendation of Lord Brougham, [King William IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom) granted him a yearly pension of £300 and appointed him Knight of the [Royal Guelphic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Guelphic_Order). He was directly connected with the Royal Society of Edinburgh and the Royal Irish Academy, and was corresponding member of the Royal Academies of Sciences of [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) and [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), and of the Royal Society of [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen).

In 1839, the University of St. Andrews conferred on him an honorary degree as a Doctor of Laws (LLD).[^o8]

He died in [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead) in north London on 21 September 1842.[^o9]
