# Bimodule

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bimodule"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **bimodule** is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) that is both a left and a right [module](wikipedia://en/276410), such that the left and right multiplications are compatible. Besides appearing naturally in many parts of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), bimodules play a clarifying role, in the sense that many of the relationships between left and right modules become simpler when they are expressed in terms of bimodules.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *R* and *S* are two [rings](wikipedia://en/48404), then an *R*-*S*-**bimodule** is an abelian group (*M*, +) such that:

1. *M* is a left *R*-module with an operation **·** and a right *S*-module with an operation $*$.
1. For all *r* in *R*, *s* in *S* and *m* in *M*: $$(r\cdot m)*s = r\cdot (m*s) .$$

An *R*-*R*-bimodule is also known as an *R*-bimodule.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- For positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) *n* and *m*, the set *M*<sub>*n*,*m*</sub>(**R**) of *n* × *m* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is an *R*-*S*-bimodule, where *R* is the ring *M*<sub>*n*</sub>(**R**) of *n* × *n* matrices, and *S* is the ring *M*<sub>*m*</sub>(**R**) of *m* × *m* matrices. Addition and multiplication are carried out using the usual rules of [matrix addition](wikipedia://en/125276) and [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280); the heights and widths of the matrices have been chosen so that multiplication is defined. Note that *M*<sub>*n*,*m*</sub>(**R**) itself is not a ring (unless *n* = *m*), because multiplying an *n* × *m* matrix by another *n* × *m* matrix is not defined. The crucial bimodule property, that (*r*.*x*).*s* = *r*.(*x*.*s*), is the statement that multiplication of matrices is associative (which, in the case of a [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541), corresponds to [associativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity)).
- Any algebra *A* over a ring *R* has the natural structure of an *R*-bimodule, with left and right multiplication defined by *r*.*a* = *φ*(*r*)*a* and *a*.*r* = *aφ*(*r*) respectively, where *φ* : *R* → *A* is the canonical embedding of *R* into *A*.
- If *R* is a ring, then *R* itself can be considered to be an *R*-*R*-bimodule by taking the left and right actions to be multiplication – the actions commute by associativity. This can be extended to *R*<sup>*n*</sup> (the *n*-fold [direct product](wikipedia://en/25063) of *R*).
- Any two-sided [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of a ring *R* is an *R*-*R*-bimodule, with the ring multiplication both as the left and as the right multiplication.
- Any module over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R* has the natural structure of a bimodule. For example, if *M* is a left module, we can define multiplication on the right to be the same as multiplication on the left. (However, not all *R*-bimodules arise this way: other compatible right multiplications may exist.)
- If *M* is a left *R*-module, then *M* is an *R*-**Z**-bimodule, where **Z** is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/14563#Algebraic_properties). Similarly, right *R*-modules may be interpreted as **Z**-*R*-bimodules. Any abelian group may be treated as a **Z**-**Z**-bimodule.
- If *M* is a right *R*-module, then the set End<sub>*R*</sub>(*M*) of *R*-module [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) is a ring with the multiplication given by composition. The endomorphism ring End<sub>*R*</sub>(*M*) acts on *M* by left multiplication defined by *f*.*x* = *f*(*x*). The bimodule property, that (*f*.*x*).*r* = *f*.(*x*.*r*), restates that *f* is a *R*-module homomorphism from *M* to itself. Therefore any right *R*-module *M* is an End<sub>*R*</sub>(*M*)-*R*-bimodule. Similarly any left *R*-module *N* is an *R*-End<sub>*R*</sub>(*N*)<sup>op</sup>-bimodule.
- If *R* is a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of *S*, then *S* is an *R*-*R*-bimodule. It is also an *R*-*S*- and an *S*-*R*-bimodule.
- If *M* is an *S*-*R*-bimodule and *N* is an *R*-*T*-bimodule, then *M* ⊗<sub>*R*</sub> *N* is an *S*-*T*-bimodule.

:::

## Further notions and facts {#Further_notions_and_facts}

If *M* and *N* are *R*-*S*-bimodules, then a map *f* : *M* → *N* is a *bimodule homomorphism* if it is both a homomorphism of left *R*-modules and of right *S*-modules.

An *R*-*S*-bimodule is actually the same thing as a left module over the ring *R* ⊗<sub>**Z**</sub> *S*<sup>op</sup>, where *S*<sup>op</sup> is the [opposite ring](wikipedia://en/3129323) of *S* (where the multiplication is defined with the arguments exchanged). Bimodule homomorphisms are the same as homomorphisms of left *R* ⊗<sub>**Z**</sub> *S*<sup>op</sup> modules. Using these facts, many definitions and statements about modules can be immediately translated into definitions and statements about bimodules. For example, the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of all *R*-*S*-bimodules is [abelian](wikipedia://en/45063), and the standard [isomorphism theorems](wikipedia://en/45241) are valid for bimodules.

There are however some new effects in the world of bimodules, especially when it comes to the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836): if *M* is an *R*-*S*-bimodule and *N* is an *S*-*T*-bimodule, then the tensor product of *M* and *N* (taken over the ring *S*) is an *R*-*T*-bimodule in a natural fashion. This tensor product of bimodules is [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) ([up to](wikipedia://en/44787) a unique canonical [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)), and one can hence construct a category whose objects are the rings and whose morphisms are the bimodules. This is in fact a [2-category](wikipedia://en/2740708), in a canonical way – 2 morphisms between *R*-*S*-bimodules *M* and *N* are exactly bimodule homomorphisms, i.e. functions

$$f: M \rightarrow N$$

that satisfy

1. $$f(m+m') = f(m)+ f(m')$$
1. $$f(r.m.s) = r.f(m).s,$$

for *m* ∈ *M*, *r* ∈ *R*, and *s* ∈ *S*. One immediately verifies the interchange law for bimodule homomorphisms, i.e.

$$(f'\otimes g')\circ (f\otimes g) = (f'\circ f)\otimes(g'\circ g)$$

holds whenever either (and hence the other) side of the equation is defined, and where $\circ$ is the usual composition of homomorphisms. In this interpretation, the category **End**(*R*) = **Bimod**(*R*, *R*) is exactly the [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) of *R*-*R*-bimodules with the usual [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) over *R* the tensor product of the category. In particular, if *R* is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), every left or right *R*-module is canonically an *R*-*R*-bimodule, which gives a monoidal embedding of the category *R*-**Mod** into **Bimod**(*R*, *R*). The case that *R* is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is a motivating example of a symmetric monoidal category, in which case *R*-**Mod** = *K*-**Vect**, the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over *K*, with the usual tensor product ⊗ = ⊗<sub>*K*</sub> giving the monoidal structure, and with unit *K*. We also see that a [monoid](wikipedia://en/3955446) in **Bimod**(*R*, *R*) is exactly an *R*-algebra.[^o8] Furthermore, if *M* is an *R*-*S*-bimodule and *L* is an *T*-*S*-bimodule, then the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) Hom<sub>*S*</sub>(*M*, *L*) of all *S*-module homomorphisms from *M* to *L* becomes a *T*-*R*-bimodule in a natural fashion. These statements extend to the [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875) [Ext](wikipedia://en/840758) and [Tor](wikipedia://en/856347).

[Profunctors](wikipedia://en/4403842) can be seen as a categorical generalization of bimodules.

Note that bimodules are not at all related to [bialgebras](wikipedia://en/310887).
