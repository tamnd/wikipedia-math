# 99 Bottles of Beer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="99 Bottles of Beer"}

"**99 Bottles of Beer**" is a traditional reverse counting song from the United States and Canada. It is popular to sing on road trips, as it has a very [repetitive format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repetitive_song) which is easy to memorize and can take a long time when sung in full. In particular, the song is often sung by children on long [school bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_bus) trips, such as class field trips, family road trips, or on [Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_%28Scouting%29) or [Girl Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girl_Guiding_and_Girl_Scouting) outings. In computer science, printing the lyrics of "99 Bottles of Beer" is a commonly used task to demonstrate [esoteric programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esoteric_programming_language).

:::

## History {#History}

Variations of the song include "ninety nine blue bottles a-hanging on the wall",[^o3] "Forty-nine Blue Bottles", and "Forty-nine 'Possums" (an "old game song" with lyrics such as "Take one away and there will be / Forty-eight 'possums hanging on a tree."[^o4]).[^o5] The "blue bottles" may refer to [blue bottle flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calliphoridae).[^o5][^o6] Some variant of the song has existed since at least "shortly after [the Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)", estimated as 1860s or early 1870s.[^o5][^o7]

## Lyrics {#Lyrics}

The song's lyrics are as follows, beginning with n=99:[^o14][^o15] (n) bottle(s) of beer on the wall. (n) bottle(s) of beer. Take one down, pass it around, (n−1) bottle(s) of beer on the wall.

[^o16]

The same verse is repeated, each time with one bottle fewer, until there are none left. Variations on the last verse following the last bottle going down include lines such as:

No more bottles of beer on the wall, no more bottles of beer. Go to the store and buy some more, 99 bottles of beer on the wall...

Or:

No more bottles of beer on the wall, no more bottles of beer. We've taken them down and passed them around; now we're drunk and passed out!

Other alternate lines read:

No more bottles of beer on the wall, no more bottles of beer. There's nothing else to fall, because there's no more bottles of beer on the wall.

Alternatively, the verse for the last one can be modified instead, such as:

If that one bottle should happen to fall, what a waste of alcohol!

Or:[^o17][^o18][^o19]

And if that green bottle should accidentally fall, There'd be nothing but the smell a-hanging on the wall.

Or the song does not stop at the last "1" or "0" bottles of beer but continues counting with

−1 (Negative one) bottles of beer on the wall Take one down, pass it around, −2 (negative 2) bottles of beer on the wall...

continuing onward through the negative numbers.

Another option is to count back up by saying "Add one bottle to them all."[^o8][^o9]

## Full-length recitals {#Full-length_recitals}

Singing all verses takes an extraordinarily long time. The American comedian [Andy Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Kaufman) used this for comedic effect early in his career when he actually sang all 100 verses.[^o21]

[Atticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atticus_%28band%29), a band from Knoxville, Tennessee, recorded a thirteen and a half minute live version of the song in its entirety at the [Glasgow Cathouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_Cathouse) in Scotland. It was included in the 2001 album *Figment*. Rich Stewart *aka* Homebrew Stew listed it as the number one [drinking song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_song) out of 86 in an article for *[Modern Drunkard Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Drunkard_Magazine)* the following year.[^o22]

## Mathematically inspired variants {#Mathematically_inspired_variants}

Donald Byrd has collected dozens of variants inspired by [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) concepts and written by himself and others.[^o24] (A subset of his collection has been published.[^o25]) Byrd argues that the collection has pedagogic as well as amusement value. Among his variants are:

- "Infinity bottles of beer on the wall". If one bottle is taken down, there are still [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) bottles of beer on the wall (thus creating an unending sequence much like "[The Song That Doesn't End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_That_Doesn't_End)").
  - "Aleph-null bottles of beer on the wall". [Aleph-null](wikipedia://en/293961#Aleph-null) is the size of the set of all [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), and is the smallest infinity and the only [countable](wikipedia://en/32248) one; therefore, even if an infinite aleph-null of bottles fall, it could still be true that the same amount remains.
  - "Aleph-one/two/three/etc. bottles of beer on the wall". [Aleph-one, two, three, etc.](wikipedia://en/293961) are uncountable infinite sets, which are larger than countable ones; therefore, if only a countable infinity of bottles fall, an uncountable number remains.

Other versions in Byrd's collection involve concepts including [geometric progressions](wikipedia://en/23474670), [differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_%28infinitesimal%29), [Euler's identity](wikipedia://en/46740), [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), [summation notation](wikipedia://en/246160#Capital-sigma_notation), the [Cantor set](wikipedia://en/6172), the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918), and the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705), among others.

## References in computer science {#References_in_computer_science}

The [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) [Donald Knuth](wikipedia://en/8095) proved that the song has a [complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) of $O(\log N)$ in his [in-joke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-joke)-article "[The Complexity of Songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Complexity_of_Songs)".[^o27]

Numerous computer programs exist to output the lyrics to the song. This is analogous to ["Hello, World!" programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/"Hello,_World!"_program), with the addition of a [loop](wikipedia://en/45459#Loops). As with "Hello, World!", this can be a practice exercise for those studying computer programming, and a demonstration of different [programming paradigms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_paradigm) dealing with looping constructs and syntactic differences between programming languages within a paradigm.

The program has been written in over 1,500 different programming languages.[^o28]

### Example {#Example}

#### [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) {#C}

```c
# include <stdio.h>
# include <stdbool.h>

int main(void) {
  for (size_t i = 99; i > 0; i--) {
    printf("%zu bottle%s of beer on the wall, %zu bottle%s of beer.\nTake one down & pass it around, now there's ",
           i, (i == 1 ? "" : "s"), i, (i == 1 ? "" : "s"));

    printf((i > 1) ? "%zu bottle%s of beer on the wall\n"
                   : "no more bottles of beer on the wall!\n",
           i - 1, i==2?"":"s");
  }
}
```

#### [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_%28programming_language%29) {#Rust}

```rust
fn main() {
    for i in (3..100).rev() {
        println!(
            "{i} bottles of beer on the wall,\n\
             {i} bottles of beer.\n\
             Take one down and pass it around,\n\
             now there's {} more bottles of beer on the wall!\n",
            i - 1
        );
    }
    println!(
        "2 bottles of beer on the wall,\n\
         2 bottles of beer.\n\
         Take one down and pass it around,\n\
         now there's 1 more bottle of beer on the wall!\n",
    );
    println!(
        "1 bottle of beer on the wall,\n\
         1 bottle of beer.\n\
         Take one down and pass it around,\n\
         there's no more bottles of beer on the wall!"
    );
}
```

```haskell
verses :: [String]
verses =
    "1 bottle of beer on the wall, 1 bottle of beer.\nTake one down and pass it around, there's no more bottles of beer on the wall!"
    : "2 bottles of beer on the wall, 2 bottles of beer.\nTake one down and pass it around, now there's 1 more bottle of beer on the wall!"
    : map (\n -> show n
        ++ " bottles of beer on the wall, "
        ++ show n
        ++ " bottles of beer.\nTake one down and pass it around, now there's "
        ++ show (n-1)
        ++ " more bottles of beer on the wall!") [3..]

main :: IO ()
main = mapM_ putStrLn (reverse $ take 99 verses)
```

#### [R](wikipedia://en/376707) {#R}

```r
positive_bottles <- function(n) {
  s  <- ifelse(n > 1, "s", "")
  "{n} bottle{s} of beer on the wall.
{n} bottle{s} of beer.
If one of the bottles just happen to fall,
{n-1} bottles of beer on the wall." |> glue::glue()
}

zero_bottles <- "No more bottles of beer on the wall,
no more bottles of beer.
There's nothing else to fall,
because there's no more bottles of beer on the wall."

for (i in 99:1)
  positive_bottles(i) |> cat("\n\n")
cat(zero_bottles)
```
