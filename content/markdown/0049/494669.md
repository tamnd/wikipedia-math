# Free algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) known as [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), a **free algebra** is the noncommutative analogue of a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) since its elements may be described as "polynomials" with non-commuting variables. Likewise, the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) may be regarded as a **free commutative algebra**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For *R* a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), the free ([associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative), [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra)) [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) on *n* [indeterminates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_%28variable%29) {*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*} is the [free *R*-module](wikipedia://en/326454) with a basis consisting of all [words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28mathematics%29) over the alphabet {*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*} (including the empty word, which is the unit of the free algebra). This *R*-module becomes an [*R*-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) by defining a multiplication as follows: the product of two basis elements is the [concatenation](wikipedia://en/64474) of the corresponding words:

$$\left(X_{i_1}X_{i_2} \cdots X_{i_l}\right) \cdot \left(X_{j_1}X_{j_2} \cdots X_{j_m}\right) = X_{i_1}X_{i_2} \cdots X_{i_l}X_{j_1}X_{j_2} \cdots X_{j_m},$$

and the product of two arbitrary *R*-module elements is thus uniquely determined (because the multiplication in an *R*-algebra must be *R*-bilinear). This *R*-algebra is denoted *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*⟩. This construction can easily be generalized to an arbitrary set *X* of indeterminates.

In short, for an arbitrary set $X=\{X_i\,;\; i\in I\}$, the **free ([associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative), [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra)) *R*-[algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) on *X*** is

$$R\langle X\rangle:=\bigoplus_{w\in X^\ast}R w$$

with the *R*-bilinear multiplication that is concatenation on words, where *X*\* denotes the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) on *X* (i.e. words on the letters *X*<sub>i</sub>), $\oplus$ denotes the external [direct sum](wikipedia://en/58899), and *Rw* denotes the [free *R*-module](wikipedia://en/326454) on 1 element, the word *w*.

For example, in *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>,*X*<sub>2</sub>,*X*<sub>3</sub>,*X*<sub>4</sub>⟩, for scalars *α, β, γ, δ* ∈ *R*, a concrete example of a product of two elements is

$$(\alpha X_1X_2^2 + \beta X_2X_3)\cdot(\gamma X_2X_1 + \delta X_1^4X_4) = \alpha\gamma X_1X_2^3X_1 + \alpha\delta X_1X_2^2X_1^4X_4 + \beta\gamma X_2X_3X_2X_1 + \beta\delta X_2X_3X_1^4X_4.$$

The non-commutative polynomial ring may be identified with the [monoid ring](wikipedia://en/20781) over *R* of the [free monoid](wikipedia://en/523166) of all finite words in the *X*<sub>*i*</sub>.

:::

## Contrast with polynomials {#Contrast_with_polynomials}

Since the words over the alphabet {*X*<sub>1</sub>, ...,*X<sub>n</sub>*} form a basis of *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*⟩, it is clear that any element of *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>, ...,*X<sub>n</sub>*⟩ can be written uniquely in the form:

$$\sum\limits_{k = 0}^\infty \, \, \, \sum\limits_{i_1,i_2, \cdots ,i_k\in\left\lbrace 1,2, \cdots ,n\right\rbrace} a_{i_1,i_2, \cdots ,i_k} X_{i_1} X_{i_2} \cdots X_{i_k},$$

where $a_{i_1,i_2,...,i_k}$ are elements of *R* and all but finitely many of these elements are zero. This explains why the elements of *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*⟩ are often denoted as "non-commutative polynomials" in the "variables" (or "indeterminates") *X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*; the elements $a_{i_1,i_2,...,i_k}$ are said to be "coefficients" of these polynomials, and the *R*-algebra *R*⟨*X*<sub>1</sub>,...,*X<sub>n</sub>*⟩ is called the "non-commutative polynomial algebra over *R* in *n* indeterminates". Note that unlike in an actual [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065), the variables do not [commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_operation). For example, *X*<sub>1</sub>*X*<sub>2</sub> does not equal *X*<sub>2</sub>*X*<sub>1</sub>.

More generally, one can construct the free algebra *R*⟨*E*⟩ on any set *E* of [generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generating_set). Since rings may be regarded as **Z**-algebras, a **free ring** on *E* can be defined as the free algebra **Z**⟨*E*⟩.

Over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), the free algebra on *n* indeterminates can be constructed as the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) on an *n*-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). For a more general coefficient ring, the same construction works if we take the [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) on *n* [generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generating_set).

The construction of the free algebra on *E* is [functorial](wikipedia://en/10987) in nature and satisfies an appropriate [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245). The free algebra functor is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) to the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) from the category of *R*-algebras to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829).

Free algebras over [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067) are [free ideal rings](wikipedia://en/14943400).
