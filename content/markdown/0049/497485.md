# Centered hexagonal number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centered hexagonal number"}

*Image: Catan\_Universe\_fixed\_setup.svg.* Centered hexagonal numbers appearing in the [Catan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catan) board game:19 land tiles,37 total tiles

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), a **centered hexagonal number**, or **centered** **hexagon number**,[^o2][^o3] is a [centered](wikipedia://en/860507) [figurate number](wikipedia://en/325260) that represents a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) with a dot in the center and all other dots surrounding the center dot in a [hexagonal lattice](wikipedia://en/2868762). ## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: visual\_proof\_centered\_hexagonal\_numbers\_sum.svg.* [Proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) of the sum of the first *n* hex numbers by arranging *n*<sup>3</sup> semitransparent balls in a cube and viewing along a [space diagonal](wikipedia://en/1337505) – colour denotes cube layer and line style denotes hex number

In [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10) one can notice that the hexagonal numbers' rightmost (least significant) digits follow the pattern 1–7–9–7–1 (repeating with [period](wikipedia://en/810250) 5). This follows from the [last digit of the triangle numbers](wikipedia://en/213915#Other_properties) which repeat 0-1-3-1-0 when taken modulo 5. In [base 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_6) the rightmost digit is always 1: 1<sub>6</sub>, 11<sub>6</sub>, 31<sub>6</sub>, 101<sub>6</sub>, 141<sub>6</sub>, 231<sub>6</sub>, 331<sub>6</sub>, 441<sub>6</sub>... This follows from the fact that every centered hexagonal number modulo 6 (=10<sub>6</sub>) equals 1.

The sum of the first $n$ centered hexagonal numbers is $n^{3}$. That is, centered hexagonal [pyramidal numbers](wikipedia://en/509081) and [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_number) are the same numbers, but they represent different shapes. Viewed from the opposite perspective, centered hexagonal numbers are differences of two consecutive cubes, so that the centered hexagonal numbers are the [gnomon](wikipedia://en/325260#Gnomon) of the cubes. (This can be seen geometrically from the diagram.) In particular, [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) centered hexagonal numbers are [cuban primes](wikipedia://en/524503).

The difference between $(2n)^{2}$ and the $n$th centered hexagonal number is a number of the form $3n^{2} + 3n - 1$, while the difference between $(2n - 1)^{2}$ and the $n$th centered hexagonal number is a [pronic number](wikipedia://en/652733).

## Applications {#Applications}

### Telescopes {#Telescopes}

*Image: comparison\_optical\_telescope\_primary\_mirrors.svg.* Ignoring central holes, the number of mirror segments in several [segmented mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segmented_mirror) [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope) are centered hexagonal numbers

Many [segmented mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segmented_mirror) [reflecting telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflecting_telescope) have primary mirrors comprising a centered hexagonal number of segments (neglecting the central segment removed to allow passage of light) to simplify the control system.[^o12] Some examples:

::: {#o13 .table cue=table}

| Telescope | Number of segments | Number missing | Total | *n*-th centered hexagonal number |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Giant Magellan Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Magellan_Telescope) | 7 | 0 | **7** | 2 |
| [James Webb Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Webb_Space_Telescope) | 18 | 1 | **19** | 3 |
| [Gran Telescopio Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Telescopio_Canarias) | 36 | 1 | **37** | 4 |
| [Guido Horn d'Arturo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Horn_d'Arturo)'s prototype | 61 | 0 | **61** | 5 |
| [Southern African Large Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Large_Telescope) | 91 | 0 | **91** | 6 |

:::

### Rocket pods {#Rocket_pods}

*Image: Hydra 70 03.jpg.* The M261 rocket pod has a capacity of 19 [Hydra 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_70) rockets, arranged hexagonally.

Many rocket pods in service currently use centered hexagonal numbers of rocket tubes.

|---|---|---|
