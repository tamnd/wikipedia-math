# Lars Gårding

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Lars Gårding"}

**Lars Gårding** (7 March 1919 – 7 July 2014)[^o4] was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He made notable contributions to the study of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [partial differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_operator). He was a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of mathematics at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) in Sweden from 1952 to 1984. Together with [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz), he was a thesis advisor for [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958). In physics, he is known for postulating the [Gårding–Wightman axioms](wikipedia://en/293622) of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gårding was born in [Hedemora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedemora), Sweden but grew up in [Motala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motala), where his father was an engineer at the plant. He began to study mathematics in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) in 1937, first with the intention of becoming an [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405).

His doctorate thesis, which was written under the supervision of [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz), was first on [group representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representations) in 1944, but in the following years he changed his research focus to the theory of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations). He held the professorship of mathematics at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) from 1952 until retirement in 1984.

Gårding's interest was not limited to mathematics, but also in art, literature and music. He played the violin and the piano. He published a book on bird songs and calls in 1987, a result of his interest in birdwatching.

He was elected a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1953 and of the [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters) in 1985.[^o6]

Gårding died on 7 July 2014, aged 95.
