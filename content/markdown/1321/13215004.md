# Moreau's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moreau's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Moreau's theorem** is a result in [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201) named after French mathematician [Jean-Jacques Moreau](wikipedia://en/34674927). It shows that sufficiently [well-behaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-behaved) [convex functionals](wikipedia://en/245568) on [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) are differentiable and the derivative is well-approximated by the so-called [Yosida approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yosida_approximation), which is defined in terms of the [resolvent operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolvent_operator).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *H* be a Hilbert space and let *φ* : *H* → **R** ∪ {+∞} be a [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_function), convex and [lower semi-continuous](wikipedia://en/57824) [extended real-valued functional](wikipedia://en/51698) on *H*. Let *A* stand for ∂*φ*, the [subderivative](wikipedia://en/3462862) of *φ*; for *α* > 0 let *J*<sub>*α*</sub> denote the resolvent:

$$J_{\alpha} = (\mathrm{id} + \alpha A)^{-1};$$

and let *A*<sub>*α*</sub> denote the **Yosida approximation** to *A*:

$$A_{\alpha} = \frac1{\alpha} ( \mathrm{id} - J_{\alpha} ).$$

For each *α* > 0 and *x* ∈ *H*, let

$$\varphi_{\alpha} (x) = \inf_{y \in H} \frac1{2 \alpha} \| y - x \|^{2} + \varphi (y).$$

Then

$$\varphi_{\alpha} (x) = \frac{\alpha}{2} \| A_{\alpha} x \|^{2} + \varphi (J_{\alpha} (x))$$

and *φ*<sub>*α*</sub> is convex and [Fréchet differentiable](wikipedia://en/2735727) with derivative d*φ*<sub>*α*</sub> = *A*<sub>*α*</sub>. Also, for each *x* ∈ *H* (pointwise), *φ*<sub>*α*</sub>(*x*) converges upwards to *φ*(*x*) as *α* → 0.

:::
