# Parallel redrawing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Parallel redrawing"}

In [geometric graph theory](wikipedia://en/3313527), and the theory of [structural rigidity](wikipedia://en/23547165), a **parallel redrawing** of a [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) with straight edges in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) or higher-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is another drawing of the same graph such that all edges of the second drawing are parallel to their corresponding edges in the first drawing. A **parallel morph** of a graph is a continuous family of drawings, all parallel redrawings of each other.

:::

Parallel redrawings include [translations](wikipedia://en/204682), [scaling](wikipedia://en/651752), and other modifications of the drawing that change it more locally. For instance, for graphs drawn as the vertices or edges of a [simple polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_polyhedron), a parallel drawing can be obtained by translating the plane of one of the polyhedron's face, and adjusting the positions of the vertices and edges that border that face.[^o2] A polyhedron is said to be **tight** if its only parallel redrawings are similarities (combinations of translation and scaling); among the [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905), the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) and [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) are not tight (because of the possibility of translating one face while keeping the others fixed), but the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), and [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) are tight.[^o3]

In three dimensions, even for drawings where all edges are axis-parallel and the drawing forms the boundary of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), there may exist parallel redrawings that cannot be connected by a parallel morph.[^o4] For two-dimensional planar drawings, with parallel edges required to preserve their orientation, a morph always exists when the [slope number](wikipedia://en/36545943) is two, but it is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to determine the existence of a morph for three or more slopes.[^o5][^o6] Any parallel morph can be parameterized so that the each point moves with constant speed along a line. The graphs that remain planar throughout such a motion can be derived from [pseudotriangulations](wikipedia://en/10609147).[^o7]

In [structural rigidity](wikipedia://en/23547165), the existence of ([infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990)) parallel redrawings of a structural framework is dual to the existence of an infinitesimal motion, one that preserves its edge lengths but not their orientations. Thus, a framework has one kind of motion if it has the other kind, but detecting the existence of a parallel redrawing may be easier than detecting the existence of an infinitesimal motion.[^o8]
