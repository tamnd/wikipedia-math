# Chenyang Xu

Xu Chenyang

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Chenyang Xu"}

**Chenyang Xu** (; born 1981) is a Chinese mathematician in the area of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and a professor at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Xu is known for his work in [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), the [minimal model program](wikipedia://en/15148924), and the [K-stability of Fano varieties](wikipedia://en/70175623).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Xu graduated from [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) with a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) and [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/25750436) in 2003 and 2004, respectively. He then earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár)'s supervision,[^o4] Xu joined [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) as a [CLE Moore Instructor](wikipedia://en/21957255) between 2008-2011. After a promotion to assistant professor at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), Xu returned to [Peking University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_University) in 2012 to join the Beijing International Center of Mathematical Research, subsequently promoted to professor in 2013.

In 2018, Xu joined the mathematics faculty at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) as Professor.

In 2020, Xu moved to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as Professor.

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2016, he was announced as a winner of the [ICTP Ramanujan Prize](wikipedia://en/13595525) for that year, "in recognition of Xu's outstanding works in algebraic geometry, notably in the area of birational geometry, including works both on log canonical pairs and on Q-Fano varieties, and on the topology of singularities and their dual complexes."[^o6] He won the 2019 New Horizons Prize for Early-Career Achievement in Mathematics, associated with the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) for his research in the minimal model program and applications to the moduli of algebraic varieties.[^o7] In 2021, he received the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in Algebra from the AMS.[^o8] In 2022, he was awarded the [Simons Investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Investigator).

He was elected as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the 2020 Class, for "contributions to algebraic geometry, in particular the minimal model program and the K-stability of Fano varieties".[^o9]

In 2018, he was an invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM).

In 2018, he became a laureate of the [Asian Scientist 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist_100) by the *[Asian Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Scientist)*.[^o10]
