# Masamichi Takesaki

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Masamichi Takesaki"}

**Masamichi Takesaki** (竹崎 正道; born July 18, 1933, in [Sendai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendai)[^o2]) is a Japanese mathematician working in the theory of [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987).

:::

Takesaki studied at [Tohoku University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_University), earning a bachelor's degree in 1956, a master's degree in 1958 and a doctorate in 1965. Beginning in 1958 he was a research assistant at the [Tokyo Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Institute_of_Technology) and from 1965 to 1968 he was an associate professor at Tohoku University. From 1968 to 1969 he was a visiting associate professor at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania). In 1970, he became a professor at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles). He was also a visiting professor at [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University) (1973–74) and [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University) (1975–76).

He is known for the [Tomita–Takesaki theory](wikipedia://en/2898625), which is about [modular automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_automorphism) of [von Neumann algebras](wikipedia://en/294185). This theory was initially developed by [Minoru Tomita](wikipedia://en/57837484) until 1967, but his work was published only partially (in Japanese) and was quite difficult to understand, drawing little notice, before being presented by Takesaki in 1970 in a book.[^o3][^o4]

In 1970, he was an invited speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice); his talk was about one parameter automorphism groups and states of operator algebras. In 1990 he was awarded the [Fujiwara Science Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujiwara_Science_Prize). He is a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]
