# Lisa Sauermann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lisa Sauermann"}

**Lisa Sauermann** (born 25 September 1992) is a German mathematician known for her performance in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208), where in 2011 she had the single highest (and perfect) score. She won four gold medals (2008–2011) and one silver medal (2007) at the olympiad, representing Germany.[^o4][^o5]

:::

Sauermann attended [Martin-Andersen-Nexö-Gymnasium Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Andersen-Nexö-Gymnasium_Dresden) when she was in 12th grade. She won the Franz Ludwig Gehe Prize in 2011 and the gold medal in the age group III, the 11th–12th grade competition. As a result, she won a trip to the [Royal Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in Stockholm. To achieve this, she presented a new mathematical theorem with a proof in a work entitled "Forests with Hypergraphs".[^o6]

In 2011 she began studying mathematics at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn).[^o7] In 2014, she completed her bachelor thesis on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) under [Michael Rapoport](wikipedia://en/26629548).[^o8] She became a graduate student studying with [Jacob Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Fox) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) where she obtained her PhD in 2019, receiving two prizes for her dissertation titled "Modern Methods in Extremal Combinatorics".[^o9][^o10] After her graduation she worked as an assistant professor at Stanford before spending a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton). In 2021 became an assistant professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) and received the [European Prize in Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Prize_in_Combinatorics) at [Eurocomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocomb) for her work in combinatorics.[^o11] In 2023 she accepted a tenured professorship at [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), where she currently works,[^o12] her main field of work being [probabilistic combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_combinatorics).[^o13] In 2022, she was awarded a [Sloan fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship),[^o14] and in 2023, she received the von Kaven Award.[^o15]

Her sister, Anne, two years her junior, was a successful participant in math and science Olympiads at the national level.[^o16]
