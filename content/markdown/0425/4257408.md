# Denjoy's theorem on rotation number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Denjoy's theorem on rotation number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Denjoy theorem** gives a sufficient condition for a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of the circle to be [topologically conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically_conjugate) to a diffeomorphism of a special kind, namely an [irrational rotation](wikipedia://en/9445837). (txt) proved the theorem in the course of his topological classification of [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) of the circle. He also gave an example of a *C*<sup>1</sup> diffeomorphism with an irrational [rotation number](wikipedia://en/3928569) that is not conjugate to a rotation.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let *ƒ*: *S*<sup>1</sup> → *S*<sup>1</sup> be an [orientation-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation-preserving) diffeomorphism of the circle whose rotation number *θ* = *ρ*(*ƒ*) is [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689). Assume that it has positive derivative *ƒ*(*x*) > 0 that is a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) with [bounded variation](wikipedia://en/330673) on the interval \[0,1). Then *ƒ* is topologically conjugate to the irrational rotation by *θ*. Moreover, every orbit is [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) and every nontrivial interval *I* of the circle intersects its forward image *ƒ*°<sup>*q*</sup>(*I*), for some *q* > 0 (this means that the [non-wandering set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-wandering_set) of *ƒ* is the whole circle).

:::

## Complements {#Complements}

If *ƒ* is a *C*<sup>2</sup> map, then the hypothesis on the derivative holds; however, for any irrational rotation number Denjoy constructed an example showing that this condition cannot be relaxed to *C*<sup>1</sup>, [continuous differentiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_differentiable) of *ƒ*.

[Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) showed that the conjugating map need not be [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function), even for an [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) diffeomorphism of the circle. Later Michel Herman proved that nonetheless, the conjugating map of an analytic diffeomorphism is itself analytic for "most" rotation numbers, forming a set of full [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), namely, for those that are [badly approximable](wikipedia://en/251558) by rational numbers. His results are even more general and specify differentiability class of the conjugating map for *C*<sup>*r*</sup> diffeomorphisms with any *r* ≥ 3.
