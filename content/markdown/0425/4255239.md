# Athanassios Fokas

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Athanassios Fokas"}

**Athanassios Spyridon Fokas** (Αθανάσιος Σπυρίδων Φωκάς; born 30 June 1952) is a [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom)–based [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people) academic, educator and scientist, with degrees in Aeronautical Engineering and Medicine. Since 2002, he is Professor of Nonlinear Mathematical Science[^o5] in the [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](wikipedia://en/3254271#DAMTP) (DAMTP) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Fokas earned a BS in [Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautics) from [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College) in 1975 and a PhD in [Applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1979. His dissertation, *Invariants, Lie-Backlund Operators and Backlund Transformations*, was written under the direction of [Paco Axel Lagerstrom](wikipedia://en/24426227).[^o2] He subsequently attended the [Miller School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_School_of_Medicine) at the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami), earning his medical degree in 1986.

## Career {#Career}

After medical school, Fokas was appointed Professor and Chair of the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science at [Clarkson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_University) in 1986. From there, he moved to [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College) in 1996 to a Chair of Applied Mathematics. Since 2002, he holds the Professorship of Nonlinear Mathematical Science (2000) in the [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](wikipedia://en/3254271#DAMTP) at the University of Cambridge, a professorship established in the year 2000 for a single tenure.[^o8] He was elected a Member of the [Academy of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Athens_%28modern%29) in 2004 and a professorial fellow of [Clare Hall, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Hall,_Cambridge) in 2005.

### Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

Fokas has written about symmetries, integrable nonlinear PDEs, Painlevé equations and random matrices, models for leukemia and protein folding, electro-magneto-enchephalography, nuclear imaging, and relativistic gravity. Also, he has introduced a new method for solving boundary value problems known as the [Fokas method](wikipedia://en/52147450).

I. M. Gelfand, a mathematician, who has also written about biology, in the citation for the Aristeion prize, wrote ''Fokas is now a very rare example of a scientist in the style of the Renaissance".[^o10]

## Awards {#Awards}

Fokas received the [Naylor Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naylor_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2000.[^o12] He was awarded a Guggenheim Fellowship (2009). He was awarded a Blaise Pascal Medal and elected to the Mathematics section of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2023.[^o13] He was elected as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the 2024 class of fellows.[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Fokas is married to Regina Karousou-Fokas with whom he has two children, Anastasia and Ioanna.
