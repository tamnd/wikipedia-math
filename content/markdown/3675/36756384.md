# Fredrik Lange-Nielsen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Fredrik Lange-Nielsen"}

**Fredrik Lange-Nielsen** (13 May 1891 – 16 May 1980) was a Norwegian mathematician and insurance company manager. He chaired the [Norwegian Students' Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Students'_Society), edited *Norsk matematisk Tidsskrift*, and lectured at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo). He was chief executive of the insurance company *Norske Liv* for nearly twenty years, was elected member of several governmental commissions, and a member of the [Norwegian Academy for Language and Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_for_Language_and_Literature) from its establishment in 1953.

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Lange-Nielsen was born in [Eivindvik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eivindvik) in [Gulen Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulen_Municipality); the son of physician Johan Fredrik Nielsen and Christine Lange. He married Laura Stang Lund in 1918.[^o4] He was the father of judge [Trygve Lange-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trygve_Lange-Nielsen),[^o5] and father-in-law of novelist [Sissel Lange-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sissel_Lange-Nielsen). He died in Oslo in 1980.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Lange-Nielsen finished his secondary education in 1908, and attended the [Norwegian Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Military_Academy) in 1909. He actively took part in social academic life.[^o4] He was among the editors of the [Norwegian Students' Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Students'_Society)'s magazine *Samfundsbladet*, and he was a board member of the society in the autumn of 1913, at the society's Centennial Anniversary.[^o7] He chaired the [Norwegian Students' Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Students'_Society) in 1916. He graduated [candidatus realium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidatus_realium) in 1917, and subsequently studied mathematics in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund) and in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o4]

He was manager of the statistics department of De norske Livsforsikringsselskaper from 1920 to 1938, and edited the journal *Norsk matematisk Tidsskrift* from 1924 to 1929. He also lectured in mathematical subjects at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo). From 1938 to 1945 he worked for the [Norwegian Public Service Pension Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Public_Service_Pension_Fund), but was arrested by the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Norway_by_Nazi_Germany) in December 1941 because of his participation in the [Norwegian resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_resistance_movement), and did not return to the Pension Fund until 1945.[^o4][^o8] He was imprisoned at [Møllergata 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Møllergata_19), and then incarcerated at the [Grini concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grini_concentration_camp) from January 1942 to May 1943.[^o9] While at Grini, he took part in what has been described as the first actual political discussions in the camp, along with [Erling Bühring-Dehli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erling_Bühring-Dehli), [Olaf Solumsmoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Solumsmoen) and others.[^o10]

From 1945 to 1964 he was chief executive of the insurance company [Norske Liv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norske_Liv). He was elected to several governmental commissions, including *Pensjonslovkomiteen* of 1935, *Krigpensjoneringsutvalget* of 1940, and *Livsforsikringskomiteen* of 1947.[^o4] He was among the first members of the [Norwegian Academy for Language and Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_for_Language_and_Literature) from its foundation in 1953. He was awarded the Grand Cross of Den Gyldne Gris, and was decorated as a Knight, First Class of the [Order of St. Olav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Olav), Knight of the Danish [Order of Dannebrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Dannebrog), and Knight of the Swedish [Order of the Polar Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Polar_Star).[^o4]
