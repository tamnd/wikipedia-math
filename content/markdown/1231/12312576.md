# Analytic Fredholm theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Analytic Fredholm theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **analytic Fredholm theorem** is a result concerning the existence of [bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) [inverses](wikipedia://en/14907) for a family of bounded linear operators on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932). It is the basis of two classical and important theorems, the [Fredholm alternative](wikipedia://en/3050954) and the [Hilbert–Schmidt theorem](wikipedia://en/12312868). The result is named after the [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Erik Ivar Fredholm](wikipedia://en/669963).

:::

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $G \subseteq \mathbf{C}$ be a domain (an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) and [connected set](wikipedia://en/6233)). Let $(H, \langle , \rangle)$ be a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) Hilbert space and let Lin(*H*) denote the space of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) from *H* into itself; let **I** denote the [identity operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_operator). Let $B : G \to \operatorname{Lin}(H)$ be a mapping such that

- *B* is analytic on *G* in the sense that the [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602) $$\lim_{\lambda \to \lambda_{0}} \frac{B(\lambda) - B(\lambda_{0})}{\lambda - \lambda_{0}}$$ exists for all $\lambda_{0} \in G$; and
- the operator *B*(*λ*) is a [compact operator](wikipedia://en/577441) for each $\lambda \in G$.

Then either

- $(\mathbf{I} - B(\lambda))^{-1}$ does not exist for any $\lambda \in G$; or
- $(\mathbf{I} - B(\lambda))^{-1}$ exists for every $\lambda \in G \backslash S$, where *S* is a [discrete subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_set) of *G* (i.e., *S* has no [limit points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) in *G*). In this case, the function taking *λ* to $(\mathbf{I} - B(\lambda))^{-1}$ is analytic on $G \backslash S$ and, if $\lambda \in S$, then the equation $$B(\lambda) \psi = \psi$$ has a finite-dimensional family of solutions.

:::
