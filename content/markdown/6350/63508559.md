# Chair tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chair tiling"}

*Image: L\_substitution\_tiling.svg.* The chair substitution (left) and a portion of a chair tiling (right)

In geometry, a **chair tiling** (or L tiling) is a [nonperiodic](wikipedia://en/868145) [substitution tiling](wikipedia://en/5558956) created from [L-tromino](wikipedia://en/1090244) [prototiles](wikipedia://en/39372). These prototiles are examples of [rep-tiles](wikipedia://en/33478101) and so an iterative process of decomposing the L tiles into smaller copies and then rescaling them to their original size can be used to cover patches of the plane.[^o2] Chair tilings do not possess [translational symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry), i.e., they are examples of *nonperiodic tilings*, but the chair tiles are not [aperiodic tiles](wikipedia://en/41287104) since they are not forced to tile nonperiodically by themselves.[^o3] The *trilobite* and *cross* tiles are aperiodic tiles that enforce the chair tiling substitution structure[^o4] and these tiles have been modified to a simple aperiodic set of tiles using matching rules enforcing the same structure.[^o5] Barge et al. have computed the [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614) of the chair tiling[^o6] and it has been shown that chair tilings can also be obtained via a [cut-and-project scheme](wikipedia://en/868145#Cut-and-project_method).[^o7]

:::
