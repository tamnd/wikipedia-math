# Boothby-Wang construction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Boothby-Wang construction"}

In [contact geometry](wikipedia://en/476398), a field in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Boothby-Wang** **construction** (or **Boothby-Wang** **theorem**) characterises a special class of [contact manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold) $(P,\alpha)$ as principal $S^1$-bundles over [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356) $(M,\omega)$ where $\omega$ defines an *integral* cohomology class. The proof employs the theory of [connection](wikipedia://en/706412) and [curvature](wikipedia://en/489440) forms.

:::

The name **Boothby-Wang construction** is used sometimes only for the construction of the contact manifold starting from the symplectic form (as part of [contactisation](wikipedia://en/476398#Contactification) procedures), sometimes for the entire statement (i.e. also the converse construction of the symplectic manifold starting from the contact form). The resulting [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) $(P,\alpha) \to (M,\omega)$ is sometimes also called **Boothby-Wang** **Fibration**.**[^o2][^o3]**

This result was proved in 1959 by [Boothby](wikipedia://en/69073815) and [Wang](wikipedia://en/50742441)[^o4] and has been subject to several generalisations.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

## Preliminaries {#Preliminaries}

### Regular contact manifolds {#Regular_contact_manifolds}

Consider any contact manifold $(P,\alpha)$; since its [Reeb vector field](wikipedia://en/10761147) $R_\alpha \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$ is nowhere zero, the orbits of the [flow](wikipedia://en/2620168) of $R_\alpha$ (also called the Reeb orbits) define a [regular foliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_foliation) $\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ of rank 1. The contact form $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P)$ is called **regular** if such foliation $\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ is simple, i.e. if the leaf space $P/\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ is a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) such that the projection $P \to P/\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ is a surjective [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413).

Assuming that $P$ is [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) and $R_\alpha$ is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_vector_field), this regularity condition implies that $P \to P/\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ is either a principal $\mathbb{R}$-bundle or a principal $S^1$-bundle (since the Reeb orbits are either not periodic, or [periodic](wikipedia://en/76848) with the same minimal period).

In the case when $P$ is furthermore [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) (which automatically implies that $R_\alpha$ is complete), since the fibres of $P \to P/\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ are [closed sets](wikipedia://en/47279) of $P$, they must be compact as well, hence they must be [circles](wikipedia://en/6220); this means that the Reeb orbits are periodic with the same minimal period and $P \to P/\mathcal{F}_{R_\alpha}$ is a principal $S^1$-bundle.

For these reasons, in the complete setting, other authors define a **regular** (or **Boothby-Wang)** contact form $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P)$ with a stronger condition: all Reeb orbits are required to be circles with the same minimal periods. This last condition is equivalent to saying that the Reeb vector field $R_\alpha \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$ generates a free $S^1$-action.**[^o2][^o3]**

### Integral symplectic manifolds {#Integral_symplectic_manifolds}

A [closed](wikipedia://en/404181) [differential 2-form](wikipedia://en/221519) $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$ is called **integral** if its induced [de Rham cohomology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_cohomology_class) $[\omega] \in H^2_{dR} (M)$ lies in the image of $H^2 (M, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2 (M,\mathbb{R}) \cong H^2_{dR} (M)$. Equivalently, its period group

$$\mathrm{Per}(\omega):= \left\{ \int_\gamma \omega \ | \ \gamma: S^2 \to M \text{ homology 2-cycle } \right\} \subset \mathbb{R}$$

is of the kind $k\mathbb{Z}$ for some integer $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Notice that, depending on the normalisation conventions, one might have to add a multiplicative factor $\frac{1}{2\pi}$.

### Principal bundles and connections {#Principal_bundles_and_connections}

A crucial step in the proof relies on the following fact: given any principal $G$-bundle $\pi: P \to M$, there is a 1-1 correspondence between $k$-forms on $M$ and $k$-forms on $P$ which are **basic**, i.e. $G$-invariant and $\pi$-horizontal (vanishing on any $\pi$-[vertical vector field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_vector_field)). In particular, given $\beta \in \Omega^k (P)$, there is a unique $\omega \in \Omega^k (M)$ such that $\beta = \pi^*\omega$.

In general, a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) on $\pi: P \to M$ is a form $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P,\mathfrak{g})$ with values in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g} := \mathrm{Lie} (G)$ satisfying the $G$-[equivariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariance)

$$(R_g)^*\alpha = g^{-1} \cdot \alpha \quad \forall g \in G$$

and the condition

$$\alpha (x^\dagger) = x \quad \forall x \in \mathfrak{g}$$

where $x^\dagger \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$ denotes the [fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232) associated to $x \in \mathfrak{g}$, i.e. the image by the infinitesimal action $\mathfrak{g} \to \mathfrak{X}(P)$.

In the case of the [abelian Lie group](wikipedia://en/62891333) $G = S^1$, we have the [abelian Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_Lie_algebra) $\mathfrak{g} = (\mathbb{R},+)$, so that the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) $g^{-1} \cdot$ is trivial and the $G$-equivariance boils down to the $G$-invariance $(R_g)^*\alpha = \alpha$ for every $g \in G$; in this case, this is equivalent to $\mathcal{L}_{x^\dagger} (\alpha) = 0$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

## From regular contact manifolds to integral symplectic manifolds {#From_regular_contact_manifolds_to_integral_symplectic_manifolds}

Let $(P,\alpha)$ be a compact regular [contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold). Then $\pi: P \to M:= P/S^1$ is a [principal $S^1$-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) and there is a unique integral [symplectic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symplectic_2-form) $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$ such that $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$.**[^o2][^o3]** If one adopts the stronger condition of regularity, i.e. requires that the Reeb vector field generates a free $S^1$-action, the compactness hypothesis is not needed.[^o7]

This follows from the more complete statement below (using the extra fact that, since $S^1$ is compact, any $S^1$-action is automatically proper; then $M:= P/S^1$ is a smooth manifold and $\pi$ is a principal $S^1$-bundle).

Let $\pi: P \to M$ be a principal $S^1$-bundle over a $2n$-dimensional manifold $M$, and let $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P,\mathbb{R}) = \Omega^1 (P)$ be a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) (where the coefficient space $\mathbb{R}$ is identified with the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the [abelian Lie group](wikipedia://en/62891333) $S^1$). Then

- there is a unique closed differential 2-form $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$ such that $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$;
- the class $[\omega] \in H^2_{dR} (M)$ is the image of the first Chern class $c_1 (P) \in H^2 (M,\mathbb{Z})$ via $H^2 (M, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2 (M,\mathbb{R}) \cong H^2_{dR} (M)$;
- $\omega$ is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_form) (i.e. it is a symplectic form) if and only if $\alpha$ is a [contact form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_form).

Moreover, if this happens,

- the [fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232) associated to $1 \in \mathbb{R} \cong \mathrm{Lie}(S^1)$ is the Reeb vector field of $\alpha$;
- the Reeb orbits are circles and the contact form is regular.

::: {#o14 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

One checks first that $d\alpha \in \Omega^2 (P)$ is basic. Indeed, it is $S^1$-equivariant since $\alpha$ is so; moreover, it is horizontal since $\pi$-vertical vector field are fundamental vector field of the kind $x^\dagger \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$, for $x \in \mathbb{R} = \mathrm{Lie}(S^1)$, hence, by the properties of a connection form,

$$d\alpha (x^\dagger, \cdot) = \iota_{x^\dagger} (d\alpha) = \cancel{\mathcal{L}_{x^\dagger} \alpha} - d (\iota_{x^\dagger} \alpha) = - d (\alpha (x^\dagger) ) = d (x) = 0.$$

Then, by the lemma mentioned above, there is a unique $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$ such that $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$; since [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and [pullbacks](wikipedia://en/494995) commute, $\omega$ is automatically closed.

Using [Chern-Weil theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern-Weil_theory), the image via $H^2 (M, \mathbb{Z}) \to H^2 (M,\mathbb{R}) \cong H^2_{dR} (M)$ of the first Chern class of $P$ can be computed precisely by choosing a connection $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P)$ and considering the induced $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$, so that $c_1 (P) = [\omega]$.

Furthermore, $\alpha \wedge (d\alpha)^n = \alpha \wedge \pi^* (\omega^n) \neq 0$ (i.e. $\alpha$ is a contact form) if and only if $\omega^n \neq 0$ (i.e. $\omega$ is nondegenerate).

Last, consider the fundamental vector field $R:= a(1) = 1^\dagger \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$, where $a: \mathrm{Lie}(S^1) \cong \mathbb{R} \to \mathfrak{X}(P)$ is the infinitesimal action. To prove that it coincides with the Reeb vector field $R_\alpha \in \mathfrak{X}(P)$, one checks that it satisfies $\alpha(R) = 1$ and $\iota_R (d\alpha) = 0$. But the first condition follows from $\alpha (x^\dagger) = x$ for $x = 1$, while the second condition follows from the fact that fundamental vector fields are $\pi$-vertical, hence

$$\iota_R (d\alpha) = (\pi^*\omega) (R, \cdot) = \omega (\cancel{d\pi(R)},d\pi(\cdot)) = 0.$$

The Reeb orbits coincide therefore with the fibres of $\pi: P \to M$, i.e. they are circles, and the contact form is regular.

:::

## From integral symplectic manifolds to regular contact manifolds {#From_integral_symplectic_manifolds_to_regular_contact_manifolds}

Let $(M,\omega)$ be an integral [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356). Then there is a [principal $S^1$-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) $\pi: P \to M$ and a regular [contact form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_form) $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P)$ such that $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$.**[^o2][^o3]**

This follows from the more complete statement below.

Let $M$ be a $2n$-dimensional manifold and $\omega \in \Omega^2 (M)$ a [closed](wikipedia://en/404181) [differential 2-form](wikipedia://en/221519) which is integral. Then

- there is a principal $S^1$-bundle $\pi: P \to M$ with [first Chern class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chern_class) $[\omega] \in H^2 (M,\mathbb{Z})$;
- there is a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P,\mathbb{R}) = \Omega^1 (P)$ such that $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$;
- $\omega$ is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_form) (i.e. it is a symplectic form) if and only if $\alpha$ is a [contact form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_form).

Moreover, if this happens,

- the [fundamental vector field](wikipedia://en/3946232) associated to $1 \in \mathbb{R} \cong \mathrm{Lie}(S^1)$ is the Reeb vector field of $\alpha$;
- the Reeb orbits are circles and the contact form is regular.

::: {#o17 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The existence of a principal $S^1$-bundle $\pi: P \to M$ with first Chern class $[\omega] \in H^2 (M)$ follows from the general classification of principal $S^1$-bundles via $H^2(M,\mathbb{Z})$.

Picking any connection form $\alpha_0 \in \Omega^1 (P,\mathbb{R}) = \Omega^1 (P)$, the differential $d\alpha_0 \in \Omega^2 (P)$ is basic by the same arguments as in the previous proof. Then, by the lemma mentioned above there is an induced closed 2-form $\omega_0 \in \Omega^2 (M)$ such that $d\alpha_0 = \pi^*\omega_0$. By definition of Chern class, one has $[\omega] = [\omega_0]$, i.e. $\omega - \omega_0 = d\beta$ for some $\beta \in \Omega^1(M)$. Then $\alpha := \alpha_0 + \pi^*\beta \in \Omega^1 (P)$ is by construction a connection form satisfying $d\alpha = \pi^*\omega$.

Last, again with the same arguments as in the previous proof, $\alpha$ is a contact form if and only if $\omega$ is nondegenerate, and the further two points hold.

:::

## Relation to geometric quantisation {#Relation_to_geometric_quantisation}

A **prequantum** **(or prequantisation) line bundle** over a symplectic manifold $(M,\omega)$ is a [complex](wikipedia://en/1538109) [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) $L \to M$ together with a fibrewise [Hermitian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_metric) $h$ and a [Hermitian connection](wikipedia://en/19454065) $\nabla$ such that its curvature $F_\nabla$ satisfies $F_\nabla = \pm i \omega$ (the sign depends on the conventions). The existence of this object constitutes the first step (before choosing a polarisation) to perform [geometric quantisation](wikipedia://en/1146267), and it is obstructed by the integrability condition of $(M,\omega)$.

The principal bundle $(P,\alpha) \to (M,\omega)$ from the Boothby-Wang construction is equivalent to the data of a prequantisation line bundle. Indeed, given $P \to M$ one defines $L \to M$ as the vector bundle $L:= (P \times \mathbb{C})/S^1$ associated to the natural [representation](wikipedia://en/19378200) $\mathbb{C} \in \mathrm{Rep} (S^1)$. Conversely, given $(L,h) \to M$, one defines $P \to M$ as the principal bundle of [unitary frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_frame_bundle) $\mathrm{Fr}(L,h) \subset \mathrm{Fr}(L)$, with structure group the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) $U(1) \cong S^1$. The rest of the equivalence follows from the standard equivalence between vector bundle connections and principal connections, and the fact that the [curvature of a connection form](wikipedia://en/489440) $\alpha \in \Omega^1 (P, \mathbb{R}) = \Omega^1 (P)$ is given by the Maurer-Cartan expression $d\alpha + \frac{1}{2} \cancel{[\alpha,\alpha]} = d\alpha \in \Omega^2 (P)$ (since the Lie algebra $\mathrm{Lie} (S^1) = \mathbb{R}$ is abelian).

The Boothby-Wang construction yield therefore an equivalence between regular contact manifolds and prequantisation bundles (over a given integral symplectic manifold).

::: {#o20 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider the [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) and [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action) [action](wikipedia://en/39904936) of the Lie group $S^1 \subset \mathbb{C}$ on the [sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) $S^{2n+1} \subset \mathbb{C}^{n+1}$ by multiplication on each complex coordinate; its quotient can be identified with the [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) $\mathbb{CP}^n$. Then the resulting principal $S^1$-bundle $(S^{2n+1},\alpha_{\mathrm{std}}) \to (\mathbb{CP}^n,\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})$ is a Boothby-Wang fibration, where $S^{2n+1}$ is endowed with the [standard contact form](wikipedia://en/476398#The_standard_contact_structure_on_the_sphere) $\alpha_{\mathrm{std}}$, which is automatically regular, and $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is endowed with the Fubini-Study symplectic form $\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}$, which is automatically integral.

In the case $n=1$ one recovers the classical [Hopf](wikipedia://en/580384) [fibration](wikipedia://en/580384) $S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1 \cong S^2$.

:::
