# Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis"}

*Image: Leibniz-Acta-1684-NovaMethodus.jpg.* First page of the "Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis", *Acta Eruditorum*, October 1684

"**Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis**" is the first published work on the subject of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), although Newton's earliest manuscript works on calculus date from 1665-1666 [^o2], [De analysi per aequationes numero terminorum infinitas](wikipedia://en/34514568) from 1669 and [Method of Fluxions](wikipedia://en/252953) from 1671 were created earlier. It was published by [Gottfried Leibniz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Leibniz) in the *[Acta Eruditorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Eruditorum)* in October 1684.[^o3]

:::

## Full title {#Full_title}

The full title of the published work is "Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis, itemque tangentibus, quae nec fractas nec irrationales quantitates moratur, et singulare pro illis calculi genus." In English, the full title can be translated as "A new method for maxima and minima, and for tangents, that is not hindered by fractional or irrational quantities, and a singular kind of calculus for the above mentioned."[^o5] It is from this title that this branch of mathematics takes the name calculus.

## Influence {#Influence}

Although calculus was developed earlier by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), most of the notation in modern calculus is from Leibniz.[^o7] Leibniz's careful attention to his notation makes some believe that "his contribution to calculus was much more influential than Newton's."[^o8]
