# Flower snark

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Flower snark"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **flower snarks** form an infinite family of [snarks](wikipedia://en/727811) introduced by [Rufus Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Isaacs_%28game_theorist%29) in 1975.[^o4]

:::

As snarks, the flower snarks are [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492), [bridgeless](wikipedia://en/1305071) [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [graphs](wikipedia://en/325806) with [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) equal to 4. The flower snarks are [non-planar](wikipedia://en/24314) and [non-Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph), though they are [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632).[^o5] The flower snarks J<sub>5</sub> and J<sub>7</sub> have [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3 and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The flower snark J<sub>*n*</sub> can be constructed with the following process :

- Build *n* copies of the [star graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_graph) on 4 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899). Denote the central vertex of each star A<sub>*i*</sub> and the outer vertices B<sub>*i*</sub>, C<sub>*i*</sub> and D<sub>*i*</sub>. This results in a disconnected graph on 4*n* vertices with 3*n* edges (A<sub>*i*</sub> − B<sub>*i*</sub>, A<sub>*i*</sub> − C<sub>*i*</sub> and A<sub>*i*</sub> − D<sub>*i*</sub> for 1 ≤ *i* ≤ *n*).
- Construct the *n*-cycle (B<sub>1</sub>... B<sub>*n*</sub>). This adds *n* edges.
- Finally construct the 2*n*-cycle (C<sub>1</sub>... C<sub>*n*</sub>D<sub>1</sub>... D<sub>*n*</sub>). This adds *2n* edges.

By construction, the Flower snark J<sub>*n*</sub> is a cubic graph with 4*n* vertices and 6*n* edges. For it to have the required properties, *n* should be [odd](wikipedia://en/143135).

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

The name flower snark is sometimes used for J<sub>5</sub>, a flower snark with 20 vertices and 30 edges. It is one of 6 snarks on 20 vertices . The flower snark J<sub>5</sub> is [hypohamiltonian](wikipedia://en/18301304).

J<sub>3</sub> is a trivial variation of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) formed by replacing one of its vertices by a triangle. This graph is also known as the [Tietze's graph](wikipedia://en/20058152).[^o13] In order to avoid trivial cases, snarks are generally restricted to have [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) at least 5. With that restriction, J<sub>3</sub> is not a snark.

:::
