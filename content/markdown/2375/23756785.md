# Right conoid

*Image: Right concoid.svg.* A right [conoid](wikipedia://en/6932317) as a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Right conoid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **right conoid** is a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158) generated by a family of [straight lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) that all intersect [perpendicularly](wikipedia://en/76944) to a fixed straight line, called the *axis* of the right [conoid](wikipedia://en/6932317).

:::

Using a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853), if we take the $z$-axis to be the axis of a right conoid, then the right conoid can be represented by the [parametric equations](wikipedia://en/576108):

$$x=v\cos u$$

$$y=v\sin u$$

$$z=h(u)$$

where $h(u)$ is some [function](wikipedia://en/185427) for representing the *height* of the moving line.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Conoid.gif.* Generation of a typical right conoid

A typical example of right conoids is given by the parametric equations

$$x=v\cos u, y=v\sin u, z=2\sin u$$

The image on the right shows how the coplanar lines generate the right conoid.

Other right conoids include:

- [Helicoid](wikipedia://en/684207): $x=v\cos u, y=v\sin u, z=cu.$
- [Whitney umbrella](wikipedia://en/1964554): $x=vu, y=v, z=u^2.$
- [Wallis's conical edge](wikipedia://en/23760380): $x=v\cos u, y=v \sin u, z=c\sqrt{a^2-b^2\cos^2u}.$
- [Plücker's conoid](wikipedia://en/23787036): $x=v\cos u, y=v\sin u, z=c\sin nu.$
- [hyperbolic paraboloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_paraboloid): $x=v, y=u, z=uv$ (with x-axis and y-axis as its axes).
