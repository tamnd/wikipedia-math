# Hosoya index

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hosoya index"}

*Image: K4 matchings.svg.* The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>4</sub> has the ten matchings shown, so its Hosoya index is ten, the maximum for any four-vertex graph.

The **Hosoya index**, also known as the **Z index**, of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is the total number of [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797) in it. The Hosoya index is always at least one, because the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) of edges is counted as a matching for this purpose. Equivalently, the Hosoya index is the number of non-empty matchings plus one. The index is named after [Haruo Hosoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruo_Hosoya). It is used as a [topological index](wikipedia://en/14221614) in [chemical graph theory](wikipedia://en/14222708).

:::

[Complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) have the largest Hosoya index for any given number of vertices; their Hosoya indices are the [telephone numbers](wikipedia://en/35258497).

## History {#History}

This [graph invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_invariant) was introduced by [Haruo Hosoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruo_Hosoya) in 1971.[^o3] It is often used in [chemoinformatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemoinformatics) for investigations of [organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_compound).[^o4][^o5]

In his article, "The Topological Index Z Before and After 1971," on the history of the notion and the associated inside stories, Hosoya writes that he introduced the Z index to report a good correlation of the [boiling points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_point) of [alkane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkane) [isomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomers) and their Z indices, basing on his unpublished 1957 work carried out while he was an undergraduate student at the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo).[^o4]

## Example {#Example}

A linear [alkane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkane), for the purposes of the Hosoya index, may be represented as a [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) without any branching. A path with one vertex and no edges (corresponding to the [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane) molecule) has one (empty) matching, so its Hosoya index is one; a path with one edge ([ethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethane)) has two matchings (one with zero edges and one with one edges), so its Hosoya index is two. [Propane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propane) (a length-two path) has three matchings: either of its edges, or the empty matching. *n*-[butane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butane) (a length-three path) has five matchings, distinguishing it from [isobutane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isobutane) which has four. More generally, a matching in a path with $k$ edges either forms a matching in the first $k-1$ edges, or it forms a matching in the first $k-2$ edges together with the final edge of the path. This case analysis shows that the Hosoya indices of linear alkanes obey the recurrence governing the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number), and because they also have the same base case they must equal the Fibonacci numbers. The structure of the matchings in these graphs may be visualized using a [Fibonacci cube](wikipedia://en/21616365).

The largest possible value of the Hosoya index, on a graph with $n$ vertices, is given by the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_n$. The Hosoya indices for the complete graphs are the [telephone numbers](wikipedia://en/35258497)

These numbers can be expressed by a [summation](wikipedia://en/246160) formula involving [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606), as

$$\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor} \frac{n!}{2^k \cdot k! \cdot \left(n - 2k\right)! }.$$

Every graph that is not complete has a smaller Hosoya index than this [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound).[^o8]

## Algorithms {#Algorithms}

The Hosoya index is [\#P-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp-P-complete) to compute, even for [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314).[^o11] However, it may be calculated by evaluating the [matching polynomial](wikipedia://en/24956783) *m<sub>G</sub>* at the argument 1.[^o12] Based on this evaluation, the calculation of the Hosoya index is [fixed-parameter tractable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-parameter_tractability) for graphs of bounded [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086)[^o13] and [polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) (with an exponent that depends linearly on the width) for graphs of bounded [clique-width](wikipedia://en/16795502).[^o14] The Hosoya index can be efficiently approximated to any desired constant [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio) using a [fully-polynomial randomized approximation scheme](wikipedia://en/666431#Randomized).[^o15]
