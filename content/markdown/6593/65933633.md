# Andrea Saltelli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Andrea Saltelli"}

**Andrea Saltelli** (born August 26, 1953, in [Rome, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome)) is an Italian scholar specializing in [quantification](wikipedia://en/43507282) using [statistical](wikipedia://en/26685) and [sociological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) tools. He has extended the theory of [sensitivity analysis](wikipedia://en/620083) to [sensitivity auditing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_auditing), focusing on [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry), [environmental statistics](wikipedia://en/8919339), impact assessment and science for policy. He is currently Counsellor at the [UPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University) (Universitat Pompeu Fabra) ca:UPF Barcelona School of Management.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Saltelli earned his degrees in [inorganic chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inorganic_chemistry) from [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) in the summer of 1976. He then worked at the Italian Nuclear Authority [ENEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENEA_%28Italy%29) and, for one year, at the [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory) in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). From 1992 to 2015, he worked at the [Joint Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Research_Centre) of the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission), leading a team dedicated to [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) and [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics) from 2005. Between 2016 and 2020, he held the position of associate [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the Centre for the Study of the Sciences and the Humanities (Senter for vitenskapsteori) at the [University of Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bergen).[^o4][^o5]

## Works {#Works}

Andrea Saltelli has significantly contributed to the field of [uncertainty](wikipedia://en/5987648) and [sensitivity analysis](wikipedia://en/620083),[^o7][^o8][^o9] founding the SAMO (Sensitivity Analysis of Model Output) international conference series in 1995.[^o10] Saltelli has authored two handbooks on global sensitivity analysis,[^o11][^o12] the most recent of which has been translated into Chinese.[^o13] His works introduced the concepts of global sensitivity analysis[^o14] and total sensitivity indices,[^o15] helping to popularize[^o16] the [variance-based sensitivity analysis](wikipedia://en/37456405) work of the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician [Ilya M. Sobol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_M._Sobol).[^o17] His formulae for efficiently computing variance-based sensitivity indices[^o14] have been widely adopted by many practitioners.[^o18][^o19] Saltelli has applied his expertise to diverse fields, including [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change),[^o20] ranking of higher education,[^o21] [ecological footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_footprint),[^o22] and composite indicators.[^o23][^o24] His recent research focuses on the reproducibility of scientific results,[^o25][^o26] principles for mathematical [modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modelling),[^o27][^o28] the [ethics of quantification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_of_quantification),[^o29][^o30] and the politics of modelling.[^o31]

Andrea Saltelli has collaborated with [Silvio Funtowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Funtowicz), [Jerome R. Ravetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_R._Ravetz) and Jeroen van der Sluijs on the theories and applications of [post-normal science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-normal_science).[^o32] He has also worked with the Belgian sociologist [Paul-Marie Boulanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul-Marie_Boulanger) on the application of [Niklas Luhmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niklas_Luhmann)'s theories to the [reproducibility crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replication_crisis) in scientific research[^o33] and to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic).[^o34] Furthermore, Saltelli has collaborated with the Norwegian economist [Erik Reinert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Reinert) on themes related to quantification in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics),[^o35][^o36] and with Daniel Sarewitz on the post-truth debate.[^o37]

## Sensitivity auditing {#Sensitivity_auditing}

In an interview for 'The Corbet Report',[^o39] Saltelli shared his early fascination with generating quantified evidence through statistical or mathematical [modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model), highlighting his concern over how easy it is to produce poor-quality evidence, if not to manipulate data or [deceive with numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_dishonesty). This concern drove his engagement with issues in the fields of [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), and science for policy[^o39] surrounding the responsible production of data.

This same concern led him to extend the theory of [sensitivity analysis](wikipedia://en/620083) to [sensitivity auditing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_auditing),[^o40] which aims to assess the entire process of knowledge and model generation, including explicit or implicit assumptions, interests, stakes and motivations on part of the developers.[^o41] According to existing guidelines[^o42] including those from the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission),[^o43] sensitivity auditing becomes particularly relevant when modelling results influence political decision-making processes.
