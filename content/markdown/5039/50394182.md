# Cone (algebraic geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cone"}

In algebraic geometry, a **cone** is a generalization of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524). Specifically, given a scheme *X*, the [relative Spec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_Spec)

:::

$$C = \operatorname{Spec}_X R$$

of a quasi-coherent graded [*O*<sub>*X*</sub>-algebra](wikipedia://en/56371096) *R* is called the **cone** or **affine cone** of *R*. Similarly, the [relative Proj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_Proj)

$$\mathbb{P}(C) = \operatorname{Proj}_X R$$

is called the **projective cone** of *C* or *R*.

**Note**: The cone comes with the $\mathbb{G}_m$-action due to the [grading](wikipedia://en/103094) of *R*; this action is a part of the data of a cone (whence the terminology).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If *X* = Spec *k* is a point and *R* is a [homogeneous coordinate ring](wikipedia://en/23833909), then the affine cone of *R* is the (usual) [affine cone](wikipedia://en/3558266#Affine_convex_cones) over the [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) corresponding to *R*.
- If $R = \bigoplus_0^\infty I^n/I^{n+1}$ for some [ideal sheaf](wikipedia://en/9508138) *I*, then $\operatorname{Spec}_X R$ is the [normal cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_cone) to the closed scheme determined by *I*.
- If $R = \bigoplus_0^\infty L^{\otimes n}$ for some line bundle *L*, then $\operatorname{Spec}_X R$ is the total space of the dual of *L*.
- More generally, given a vector bundle (finite-rank locally free sheaf) *E* on *X*, if *R*=Sym(*E*<sup>\*</sup>) is the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) generated by the dual of *E*, then the cone $\operatorname{Spec}_X R$ is the total space of *E*, often written just as *E*, and the projective cone $\operatorname{Proj}_X R$ is the [projective bundle](wikipedia://en/35183882) of *E*, which is written as $\mathbb{P}(E)$.
- Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent [sheaf](wikipedia://en/56408134) on a [Deligne–Mumford stack](wikipedia://en/40639179) *X*. Then let $C(\mathcal{F}) := \operatorname{Spec}_X(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{F})).$[^o4] For any $f: T \to X$, since global Spec is a right adjoint to the direct image functor, we have: $C(\mathcal{F})(T) = \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{F}), f_* \mathcal{O}_T)$; in particular, $C(\mathcal{F})$ is a commutative [group scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) over *X*.
- Let *R* be a graded $\mathcal{O}_X$-algebra such that $R_0 = \mathcal{O}_X$ and $R_1$ is coherent and locally generates *R* as $R_0$-algebra. Then there is a [closed immersion](wikipedia://en/1623986)

$$\operatorname{Spec}_X R \hookrightarrow C(R_1)$$

given by $\operatorname{Sym}(R_1) \to R$. Because of this, $C(R_1)$ is called the abelian hull of the cone $\operatorname{Spec}_X R.$ For example, if $R = \oplus_0^{\infty} I^n/I^{n+1}$ for some ideal sheaf *I*, then this embedding is the embedding of the normal cone into the normal bundle.

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Computations"}

### Computations {#Computations}

Consider the complete intersection ideal $(f,g_1,g_2,g_3) \subset \mathbb{C}[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ and let $X$ be the projective scheme defined by the ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I} = (f)(g_1,g_2,g_3)$. Then, we have the isomorphism of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}$-algebras given by

$$\bigoplus_{n\geq 0 } \frac{\mathcal{I}^n}{\mathcal{I}^{n+1}} \cong \frac{\mathcal{O}_X[a,b,c]}{(g_2a - g_1b, g_3a - g_1c, g_3b - g_2c)}$$

## Properties {#Properties}

If $S \to R$ is a graded homomorphism of graded *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-algebras, then one gets an induced morphism between the cones:

$$C_R = \operatorname{Spec}_X R \to C_S = \operatorname{Spec}_X S.$$

If the homomorphism is surjective, then one gets closed immersions $C_R \hookrightarrow C_S,\, \mathbb{P}(C_R) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}(C_S).$

In particular, assuming *R*<sub>0</sub> = *O*<sub>*X*</sub>, the construction applies to the projection $R = R_0 \oplus R_1 \oplus \cdots \to R_0$ (which is an [augmentation map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_map)) and gives

$$\sigma: X \hookrightarrow C_R.$$

It is a section; i.e., $X \overset{\sigma}\to C_R \to X$ is the identity and is called the zero-section embedding.

Consider the graded algebra *R*\[*t*\] with variable *t* having degree one: explicitly, the *n*-th degree piece is

$$R_n \oplus R_{n-1} t \oplus R_{n-2} t^2 \oplus \cdots \oplus R_0 t^n.$$

Then the affine cone of it is denoted by $C_{R[t]} = C_R \oplus 1$. The projective cone $\mathbb{P}(C_R \oplus 1)$ is called the **projective completion** of *C*<sub>*R*</sub>. Indeed, the zero-locus *t* = 0 is exactly $\mathbb{P}(C_R)$ and the complement is the open subscheme *C*<sub>*R*</sub>. The locus *t* = 0 is called the hyperplane at infinity.

:::

## *O*(1) {#O(1)}

Let *R* be a quasi-coherent graded *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-algebra such that *R*<sub>0</sub> = *O*<sub>*X*</sub> and *R* is locally generated as *O*<sub>*X*</sub>-algebra by *R*<sub>1</sub>. Then, by definition, the projective cone of *R* is:

$$\mathbb{P}(C) = \operatorname{Proj}_X R = \varinjlim \operatorname{Proj}(R(U))$$

where the [colimit](wikipedia://en/211566) runs over open affine subsets *U* of *X*. By assumption *R*(*U*) has finitely many degree-one generators *x*<sub>*i*</sub>'s. Thus,

$$\operatorname{Proj}(R(U)) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}^r \times U.$$

Then $\operatorname{Proj}(R(U))$ has the line bundle *O*(1) given by the [hyperplane bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplane_bundle) $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}(1)$ of $\mathbb{P}^r$; gluing such local *O*(1)'s, which agree locally, gives the line bundle *O*(1) on $\mathbb{P}(C)$.

For any integer *n*, one also writes *O*(*n*) for the *n*-th tensor power of *O*(1). If the cone *C*=Spec<sub>*X*</sub>*R* is the total space of a vector bundle *E*, then *O*(-1) is the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) on the [projective bundle](wikipedia://en/35183882) **P**(*E*).

::: {#o10 .remark cue=inline-head name="Remark"}

**Remark**: When the (local) generators of *R* have degree other than one, the construction of *O*(1) still goes through but with a [weighted projective space](wikipedia://en/42783108) in place of a projective space; so the resulting *O*(1) is not necessarily a line bundle. In the language of [divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055), this *O*(1) corresponds to a **Q**-Cartier divisor.

:::
