# Residence time (statistics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residence time"}

In statistics, the **residence time** is the average amount of time it takes for a [random process](wikipedia://en/47895) to reach a certain boundary value, usually a boundary far from the mean.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose $y(t)$ is a real, scalar [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with initial value $y(t_{0}) = y_{0}$, mean $y_{\operatorname{avg}}$ and two critical values $\{y_{\operatorname{avg}} - y_{\min}, y_{\operatorname{avg}} + y_{\max}$}, where $y_{\min} > 0$ and $y_{\max} > 0$. Define the first [passage time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passage_time) of $y(t)$ from within the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $(-y_{\min}, y_{\max})$ as

$$\tau(y_0) = \inf\{t \ge t_0 : y(t) \in \{y_{\operatorname{avg}}-y_{\min},\ y_{\operatorname{avg}}+y_{\max}\} \},$$

where "inf" is the [infimum](wikipedia://en/39382). This is the smallest time after the initial time $t_{0}$ that $y(t)$ is equal to one of the critical values forming the boundary of the interval, assuming $y_{0}$ is within the interval.

Because $y(t)$ proceeds randomly from its initial value to the boundary, $\tau(y_{0})$ is itself a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685). The mean of $\tau(y_{0})$ is the **residence time**,[^n1][^n2]

$$\bar{\tau}(y_0) = E[\tau(y_0)\mid y_0].$$

For a [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944) and a boundary far from the mean, the residence time equals the inverse of the [frequency of exceedance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_of_exceedance) of the smaller critical value,[^n2]

$$\bar{\tau} = N^{-1}(\min(y_{\min},\ y_{\max})),$$

where the frequency of exceedance $N$ is

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$N(y_{\max}) = N_0 e^{-y_{\max}^2/2\sigma_y^2}, \tag{1}$$

:::

$\sigma_{y}^{2}$ is the variance of the Gaussian distribution,

$$N_0 = \sqrt{\frac{\int_0^\infty{f^2 \Phi_y(f) \, df}}{\int_0^\infty{\Phi_y(f) \, df}}},$$

and $\Phi_{y}(f)$ is the [power spectral density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectral_density) of the Gaussian distribution over a frequency $f$.

### Generalization to multiple dimensions {#Generalization_to_multiple_dimensions}

Suppose that instead of being scalar, $y(t)$ has dimension $p$, or $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}^p$. Define a domain $\Psi \subset \mathbb{R}^p$ that contains $y_{\operatorname{avg}}$ and has a smooth boundary $\partial\Psi$. In this case, define the first passage time of $y(t)$ from within the domain $\Psi$ as

$$\tau(y_0) = \inf\{t \ge t_0 : y(t) \in \partial \Psi \mid y_0 \in \Psi \}.$$

In this case, this infimum is the smallest time at which $y(t)$ is on the boundary of $\Psi$ rather than being equal to one of two discrete values, assuming $y_{0}$ is within $\Psi$. The mean of this time is the **residence time**,[^n3][^n4]

$$\bar{\tau}(y_0) = \operatorname{E}[\tau(y_0)\mid y_0].$$

### Logarithmic residence time {#Logarithmic_residence_time}

The logarithmic residence time is a [dimensionless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_quantity) variation of the residence time. It is proportional to the natural log of a normalized residence time. Noting the exponential in Equation 1, the **logarithmic residence time** of a Gaussian process is defined as[^n5][^n6]

$$\hat{\mu} = \ln \left(N_0 \bar{\tau} \right) = \frac{\min(y_{\min},\ y_{\max})^2}{2 \sigma_y^2}.$$

This is closely related to another dimensionless descriptor of this system, the number of standard deviations between the boundary and the mean, $\min(y_{\min}, y_{\max})/\sigma_{y}$.

In general, the normalization factor $N_{0}$ can be difficult or impossible to compute, so the dimensionless quantities can be more useful in applications.
