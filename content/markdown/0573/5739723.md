# Critical group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Critical group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **critical** if it is not in the [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) generated by all its proper [subquotients](wikipedia://en/1639294), which includes all its [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) and all its [quotients](wikipedia://en/332640).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A factor of a group $G$ is a group of the form $H/N$, where $H$ is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $G$, and $N$ is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $G$, and is called a proper factor when $N$ is non-trivial or $H$ is a proper subgroup. A group $G$ is critical when it is [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) as well as not within the [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) generated by the group's proper factors.[^o5] Critical groups were introduced by [D. C. Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._C._Cross)[^o9]

### Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

Every [finite simple group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_simple_group) is critical.[^o2] On the other hand, if a group is generated by a subgroup with multiple normal subgroups of that group, but not generated from any proper subset of those normal subgroups with the subgroup, and if the commutator subgroup generated by the normal subgroups is trivial for every permutation involved in generating the commutator subgroup, then the group is not critical.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every critical group $G$ has a unique minimal normal subgroup called the monolith, and this subgroup is denoted $\sigma G$.[^o5] Such groups are called [monolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_group), which are a necessary yet insufficient condition for being critical.[^o10]

- Any [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [monolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_group) [A-group](wikipedia://en/4938898) is critical. This result is due to Kovacs and Newman.[^o9] But not every monolithic group is critical.[^o10]
- The variety generated by a finite group has a finite number of nonisomorphic critical groups.[^o2]

:::

## Cross variety {#Cross_variety}

A Cross variety is a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) of groups that satisfies:[^o2]

- The variety "has a finite basis for its identical relations"
- All [finitely generated groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_generated_groups) in the variety are necessarily finite.
- There are only a finite amount of critical groups in the variety.

[Sheila Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Oates) and [M.B.Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.B.Powell) proved using Cross varieties that every finite group has a finite basis for the identical relations holding in the group.[^o2] They also proved that "\[a\] variety of groups is Cross if and only if it is generated by a finite group," which can be shown inductively from the fact that any variety generated by a Cross variety and a finite group is also a Cross variety.[^o2]
