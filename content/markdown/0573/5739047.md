# Slender group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Slender group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **slender group** is a [torsion-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) that is "small" in a sense that is made precise in the definition below.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathbb Z^{\mathbb{N}}$ denote the [Baer–Specker group](wikipedia://en/19200651), that is, the [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of all [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421), with termwise addition. For each [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n$, let $e_n$ be the sequence with $n$-th term equal to 1 and all other terms 0.

A torsion-free abelian group $G$ is said to be **slender** if every [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) from $\mathbb Z^{\mathbb{N}}$ into $G$ maps all but finitely many of the $e_n$ to the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962).

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) is slender.

The additive group of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) $\mathbb Q$ is not slender: any mapping of the $e_n$ into $\mathbb Q$ extends to a homomorphism from the free [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) generated by the $e_n$, and as $\mathbb Q$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/473775) this homomorphism extends over the whole of $\mathbb Z^{\mathbb{N}}$. Therefore, a slender group must be [reduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_abelian_group).

Every [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) reduced torsion-free abelian group is slender, so every proper subgroup of $\mathbb Q$ is slender.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A torsion-free abelian group is slender [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is reduced and contains no copy of the Baer–Specker group and no copy of the [$p$-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integers) for any $p$.
- Direct sums of slender groups are also slender.
- Subgroups of slender groups are slender.
- Every homomorphism from $\mathbb Z^{\mathbb{N}}$ into a slender group factors through $\mathbb Z^n$ for some natural number $n$.

:::
