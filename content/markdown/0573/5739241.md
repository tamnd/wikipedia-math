# Cotorsion group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cotorsion group"}

In [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an abelian group is said to be **cotorsion** if any [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of it by a [torsion-free abelian group](wikipedia://en/11630973) splits. If the group is $M$, this says that the [Ext-group](wikipedia://en/840758) $\operatorname{Ext}_\mathbb{Z}(F,M)$ is zero for all torsion-free groups $F$. Since any such $F$ embeds into a direct sum of copies of $\mathbb{Q}$, it suffices to check the condition on the group of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024).[^o2]

:::

Every [divisible group](wikipedia://en/472147) or [injective group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_group) (in particular the group of rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$) is cotorsion. For any two abelian groups $A$ and $B$, the group of extensions $\operatorname{Ext}_\mathbb{Z}(A,B)$ is cotorsion.[^o3]

## Properties of cotorsion groups {#Properties_of_cotorsion_groups}

The class of cotorsion groups is [closed under](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_under) extensions, [direct products](wikipedia://en/3706015), and [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526).[^o2]

A [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) cotorsion group is a direct sum of a divisible group and a bounded group, that is a group of bounded [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent_of_a_group).[^o2]

The **Baer--Fomin Theorem** states that a torsion group is cotorsion if and only if it is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/106341) of a divisible group and a bounded group.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

A torsion-free abelian group is cotorsion if and only if it is [algebraically compact](wikipedia://en/5739636).[^o8][^o9] Such groups are precisely the [direct summands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of direct products of [$p$-adic integers.](wikipedia://en/51423)[^o8]

[Ulm subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_subgroup) of cotorsion groups are cotorsion and [Ulm factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_factor) of cotorsion groups are algebraically compact.[^o10]

## In ring theory {#In_ring_theory}

A [right module](wikipedia://en/276410) *M* over a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is said to be a **cotorsion module** if $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(F,M)=0$ for all [flat (right) modules](wikipedia://en/492616) *F*.[^o12] When $R$ is the ring of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z}$, this reduces to the previous definition of cotorsion abelian groups.

The ring $R$ is said to be (right) cotorsion if the [regular module](wikipedia://en/360011) $R_R$ is cotorsion. [^o13]
