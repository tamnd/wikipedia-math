# Algebraically compact group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraically compact group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **algebraically compact** if it is a [direct summand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_summand) of every abelian group containing it as a [pure subgroup](wikipedia://en/5739585).

:::

Equivalent characterizations of algebraic compactness:

- The reduced part of the group is Hausdorff and complete in the $\mathbb{Z}$ adic topology.
- The group is *pure injective*, that is, injective with respect to exact sequences where the embedding is as a pure subgroup.

Relations with other properties:

- A [torsion-free group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) is [cotorsion](wikipedia://en/5739241) if and only if it is algebraically compact.
- Every [injective group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_group) is algebraically compact.
- [Ulm factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_factor) of cotorsion groups are algebraically compact.
