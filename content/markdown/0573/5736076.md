# Brauer–Nesbitt theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer–Nesbitt theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Brauer–Nesbitt theorem** can refer to several different theorems proved by [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) and [Cecil J. Nesbitt](wikipedia://en/14373679) in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707).

:::

In [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685), the **Brauer–Nesbitt theorem on blocks of defect zero** states that a character whose order is divisible by the highest power of a prime *p* dividing the order of a finite group remains irreducible when reduced mod *p* and vanishes on all elements whose order is divisible by *p*. Moreover, it belongs to a [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_%28representation_theory%29) of [defect zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defect_zero). A block of defect zero contains only one [ordinary character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_character) and only one [modular character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_character).

Another version states that if *k* is a field of characteristic zero, *A* is a *k*-algebra, *V*, *W* are semisimple *A*-modules which are finite dimensional over *k*, and Tr<sub>*V*</sub> = Tr<sub>*W*</sub> as elements of Hom<sub>k</sub>(*A*,k), then *V* and *W* are isomorphic as *A*-modules.

Let $G$ be a group and $E$ be some field. If $\rho_i:G\to GL_n(E),i=1,2$ are two finite-dimensional semisimple representations such that the characteristic polynomials of $\rho_1(g)$ and $\rho_2(g)$ coincide for all $g\in G$, then $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are isomorphic representations. If $char(E)=0$ or $char(E)>n$, then the condition on the characteristic polynomials can be changed to the condition that Tr$\rho_1(g)$=Tr$\rho_2(g)$ for all $g\in G$.

As a consequence, let $\rho:Gal(K^{\rm{sep}}/K)\to GL_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$ be a semisimple (continuous) $l$-adic representations of the absolute Galois group of some field $K$, unramified outside some finite set of primes $S\subset M_K$. Then the representation is uniquely determined by the values of the traces of $\rho(Frob_p)$ for $p\in M_K^0-S$ (also using the Chebotarev density theorem).
