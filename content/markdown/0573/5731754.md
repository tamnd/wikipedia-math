# Ehresmann connection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ehresmann connection"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), an **Ehresmann connection** (after the French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Charles Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108) who first formalized this concept) is a version of the notion of a [connection](wikipedia://en/308815), which makes sense on any smooth [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526). In particular, it does not rely on the possible vector bundle structure of the underlying fiber bundle, but nevertheless, [linear connections](wikipedia://en/391816) may be viewed as a special case. Another important special case of Ehresmann connections are [principal connections](wikipedia://en/470632) on [principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950), which are required to be [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) in the principal [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) action.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

A [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) in differential geometry is a [linear differential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_operator) which takes the [directional derivative](wikipedia://en/487859) of a section of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) in a [covariant](wikipedia://en/443235) manner. It also allows one to formulate a notion of a [parallel](wikipedia://en/285595) section of a bundle in the direction of a vector: a section *s* is parallel along a vector $X$ if $\nabla_X s = 0$. So a covariant derivative provides at least two things: a differential operator, *and* a notion of what it means to be parallel in each direction. An **Ehresmann connection** drops the differential operator completely and defines a connection axiomatically in terms of the sections parallel in each direction (Ehresmann 1950). Specifically, an Ehresmann connection singles out a [vector subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_subspace) of each [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) to the total space of the fiber bundle, called the *horizontal space*. A section $s$ is then horizontal (i.e., parallel) in the direction *$X$* if ${\rm d}s(X)$ lies in a horizontal space. Here we are regarding *$s$* as a function $s\colon M\to E$ from the base *$M$* to the fiber bundle *$E$*, so that ${\rm d}s\colon TM\to TE$ is then the [pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510) of tangent vectors. The horizontal spaces together form a vector subbundle of $TE$.

This has the immediate benefit of being definable on a much broader class of structures than mere vector bundles. In particular, it is well-defined on a general [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526). Furthermore, many of the features of the covariant derivative still remain: parallel transport, [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770), and [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921).

The missing ingredient of the connection, apart from linearity, is *covariance*. With the classical covariant derivatives, covariance is an *a posteriori* feature of the derivative. In their construction one specifies the transformation law of the [Christoffel symbols](wikipedia://en/1401020) – which is not covariant – and then [general covariance](wikipedia://en/681875) of the *derivative* follows as a result. For an Ehresmann connection, it is possible to impose a generalized covariance principle from the beginning by introducing a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) acting on the fibers of the fiber bundle. The appropriate condition is to require that the horizontal spaces be, in a certain sense, [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) with respect to the group action.

The finishing touch for an Ehresmann connection is that it can be represented as a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519), in much the same way as the case of a [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412). If the group acts on the fibers and the connection is equivariant, then the form will also be equivariant. Furthermore, the connection form allows for a definition of curvature as a [curvature form](wikipedia://en/489440) as well.

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

*Image: Ehresmann connection.png.* An Ehresmann connection is a choice of horizontal subspace $H_p\subset T_pP$ for every $p\in P$, where $P$ is some fiber bundle, typically a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950).

Let $\pi\colon E\to M$ be a smooth [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526).[^o6] Let

$$V= \ker (\operatorname{d} \pi \colon TE\to TM)$$

be the **[vertical bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_bundle)** consisting of the vectors "tangent to the fibers" of *E*, i.e. the fiber of *V* at $e\in E$ is $V_e =T_e(E_{\pi(e)})$. This subbundle of $TE$ is canonically defined even when there is no canonical subspace tangent to the base space *M*. (Of course, this asymmetry comes from the very definition of a fiber bundle, which "only has one projection" $\pi\colon E\to M$ while a product $E=M\times F$ would have two.)

### Definition via horizontal subspaces {#Definition_via_horizontal_subspaces}

An **Ehresmann connection** on *$E$* is a smooth subbundle *$H$* of $TE$, called the **[horizontal bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_bundle)** of the connection, which is complementary to *V*, in the sense that it defines a [direct sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_sum_of_vector_bundles) decomposition $TE=H\oplus V$.[^n1] In more detail, the horizontal bundle has the following properties.

- For each point $e\in E$, $H_e$ is a [vector subspace](wikipedia://en/32370) of the tangent space $T_e E$ to *$E$* at *$e$*, called the *horizontal subspace* of the connection at *$e$*.
- $H_e$ depends [smoothly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function#Smoothness) on *$e$*.
- For each $e\in E$, $H_e \cap V_e = \{0\}$.
- Any tangent vector in *$T_e E$* (for any $e\in E$) is the sum of a horizontal and vertical component, so that *$T_eE =H_e+V_e$*.

In more sophisticated terms, such an assignment of horizontal spaces satisfying these properties corresponds precisely to a smooth section of the [jet bundle](wikipedia://en/928060) $J^1E\rightarrow E$.

### Definition via a connection form {#Definition_via_a_connection_form}

Equivalently, let $\Phi$ be the projection onto the vertical bundle *V* along *H* (so that *H* = ker $\Phi$). This is determined by the above *direct sum* decomposition of *TE* into horizontal and vertical parts and is sometimes called the [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412) of the Ehresmann connection. Thus $\Phi$ is a [vector bundle homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_bundle_homomorphism) from *TE* to itself with the following properties (of projections in general):

- $\Phi$<sup>2</sup> = $\Phi$;
- $\Phi$ is the identity on *V* =Im $\Phi$.

Conversely, if $\Phi$ is a vector bundle [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of *TE* satisfying these two properties, then *H* = ker $\Phi$ is the horizontal subbundle of an Ehresmann connection.

Finally, note that $\Phi$, being a linear mapping of each tangent space into itself, may also be regarded as a *TE*-valued 1-form on *E*. This will be a useful perspective in sections to come.

### Parallel transport via horizontal lifts {#Parallel_transport_via_horizontal_lifts}

An Ehresmann connection also prescribes a manner for lifting curves from the base manifold *M* into the total space of the fiber bundle *E* so that the tangents to the curve are horizontal.[^n1][^n2] These **horizontal lifts** are a direct analogue of [parallel transport](wikipedia://en/285595) for other versions of the connection formalism.

Specifically, suppose that *γ*(*t*) is a smooth curve in *M* through the point *x* = *γ*(0). Let *e* ∈ *E*<sub>*x*</sub> be a point in the fiber over *x*. A **lift** of *γ* through *e* is a curve $\tilde{\gamma}(t)$ in the total space *E* such that

$\tilde{\gamma}(0) = e$, and $\pi(\tilde{\gamma}(t)) = \gamma(t).$

A lift is **horizontal** if, in addition, every tangent of the curve lies in the horizontal subbundle of *TE*:

$$\tilde{\gamma}'(t) \in H_{\tilde{\gamma}(t)}.$$

It can be shown using the [rank–nullity theorem](wikipedia://en/330310) applied to *π* and $\Phi$ that each vector *X*∈*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M* has a unique horizontal lift to a vector $\tilde{X} \in T_e E$. In particular, the tangent field to *γ* generates a horizontal vector field in the total space of the [pullback bundle](wikipedia://en/1665392) *γ*\**E*. By the [Picard–Lindelöf theorem](wikipedia://en/666177), this vector field is [integrable](wikipedia://en/62641#Flow_curves). Thus, for any curve *γ* and point *e* over *x* = *γ*(0), there exists a *unique horizontal lift* of *γ* through *e* for small time *t*.

Note that, for general Ehresmann connections, the horizontal lift is path-dependent. When two smooth curves in *M*, coinciding at *γ*<sub>1</sub>(0) = *γ*<sub>2</sub>(0) = *x*<sub>0</sub> and also intersecting at another point *x*<sub>1</sub> ∈ *M*, are lifted horizontally to *E* through the same *e* ∈ *π*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*<sub>0</sub>), they will generally pass through different points of *π*<sup>−1</sup>(*x*<sub>1</sub>). This has important consequences for the differential geometry of fiber bundles: the space of sections of *H* is not a [Lie subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_subalgebra) of the space of vector fields on *E*, because it is not (in general) closed under the [Lie bracket of vector fields](wikipedia://en/294437). This failure of closure under Lie bracket is measured by the *curvature*.

## Properties {#Properties}

### Curvature {#Curvature}

Let $\Phi$ be an Ehresmann connection. Then the curvature of $\Phi$ is given by[^n3]

$$R = \tfrac{1}{2}[\varPhi,\varPhi]$$

where \[-,-\] denotes the [Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frölicher-Nijenhuis_bracket) of $\Phi$ ∈ Ω<sup>1</sup>(*E*,*TE*) with itself. Thus *R* ∈ Ω<sup>2</sup>(*E*,*TE*) is the two-form on *E* with values in *TE* defined by

$$R(X,Y) = \varPhi\left([(\mathrm{id} - \varPhi)X,(\mathrm{id} - \varPhi)Y]\right),$$

or, in other terms,

$$R\left(X,Y\right) = \left[X_H,Y_H\right]_V,$$

where *X* = *X*<sub>H</sub> + *X*<sub>V</sub> denotes the direct sum decomposition into *H* and *V* components, respectively. From this last expression for the curvature, it is seen to vanish identically if, and only if, the horizontal subbundle is [Frobenius integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_integration_theorem). Thus the curvature is the [integrability condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition) for the horizontal subbundle to yield transverse sections of the fiber bundle *E* → *M*.

The curvature of an Ehresmann connection also satisfies a version of the [Bianchi identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianchi_identity):

$$\left[\varPhi, R\right] = 0$$

where again \[-,-\] is the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket of $\Phi$ ∈ Ω<sup>1</sup>(*E*,*TE*) and *R* ∈ Ω<sup>2</sup>(*E*,*TE*).

### Completeness {#Completeness}

An Ehresmann connection allows curves to have unique horizontal lifts [locally](wikipedia://en/1462457). For a **complete** Ehresmann connection, a curve can be horizontally lifted over its entire domain.

### Holonomy {#Holonomy}

Flatness of the connection corresponds locally to the [Frobenius integrability](wikipedia://en/602480) of the horizontal spaces. At the other extreme, non-vanishing curvature implies the presence of [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) of the connection.[^o20]

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

### Principal bundles and principal connections {#Principal_bundles_and_principal_connections}

*Image: Principal bundle connection form projection.png.* A principal bundle connection form $\omega$ may be thought of as a projection operator on the tangent bundle $TP$ of the principal bundle $P$. The kernel of the connection form is given by the horizontal subspaces for the associated Ehresmann connection.

Suppose that *E* is a smooth [principal *G*-bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over *M*. Then an Ehresmann connection *H* on *E* is said to be a **principal (Ehresmann) connection**[^n2] if it is invariant with respect to the *G* action on *E* in the sense that

$H_{eg}=\mathrm d(R_g)_e (H_{e})$ for any *e*∈*E* and *g*∈*G*; here $\mathrm d(R_g)_e$ denotes the differential of the [right action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of *g* on *E* at *e*.

The one-parameter subgroups of *G* act vertically on *E*. The differential of this action allows one to identify the subspace $V_e$ with the Lie algebra **g** of group *G*, say by map $\iota\colon V_e\to \mathfrak g$. The connection form *$\Phi$* of the Ehresmann connection may then be viewed as a 1-form *ω* on *E* with values in **g** defined by *ω*(*X*)=*ι*(*$\Phi$*(*X*)).

Thus reinterpreted, the connection form *ω* satisfies the following two properties:

- It transforms [equivariantly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) under the *G* action: $R_h^*\omega=\hbox{Ad}(h^{-1})\omega$ for all *h*∈*G*, where *R*<sub>*h*</sub><sup>\*</sup> is the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) under the right action and *Ad* is the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of *G* on its Lie algebra.
- It maps [vertical vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertical_vector_field) to their associated elements of the Lie algebra: *ω*(*X*)=*ι*(*X*) for all *X*∈*V*.

Conversely, it can be shown that such a **g**-valued 1-form on a principal bundle generates a horizontal distribution satisfying the aforementioned properties.

Given a local trivialization one can reduce *ω* to the horizontal vector fields (in this trivialization). It defines a 1-form *ω'* on *M* via [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995). The form *ω**determines*****ω*completely, but it depends on the choice of trivialization. (This form is often also called a****[connection form](wikipedia://en/706412)**and denoted simply by*****ω*.)***

### Vector bundles and covariant derivatives {#Vector_bundles_and_covariant_derivatives}

Suppose that *E* is a smooth [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over *M*. Then an Ehresmann connection *H* on *E* is said to be a **linear (Ehresmann) connection** if *H*<sub>*e*</sub> depends linearly on *e* ∈ *E*<sub>*x*</sub> for each *x* ∈ *M*. To make this precise, let *S*<sub>*λ*</sub> denote scalar multiplication by *λ* on *E*. Then *H* is linear if and only if $H_{\lambda e} = \mathrm d(S_{\lambda})_e (H_{e})$for any *e* ∈ *E* and scalar λ.

Since *E* is a vector bundle, its vertical bundle *V* is isomorphic to *π*\**E*. Therefore if *s* is a section of *E*, then *$\Phi$*(d*s*):*TM*→*s*\**V*=*s*\**π*\**E*=*E*. It is a vector bundle morphism, and is therefore given by a section ∇*s* of the vector bundle Hom(*TM*,*E*). The fact that the Ehresmann connection is linear implies that in addition it verifies for every function $f$ on $M$ the Leibniz rule, i.e. $\nabla(f s) = f\nabla (s) + d(f)\otimes s$, and therefore is a [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/391816) of *s*.

Conversely a [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/391816) *∇* on a vector bundle defines a linear Ehresmann connection by defining *H*<sub>*e*</sub>, for *e* ∈ *E* with *x*=*π*(*e*), to be the image d*s*<sub>*x*</sub>(*T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M*) where *s* is a section of *E* with *s*(*x*) = *e* and ∇<sub>*X*</sub>*s* = 0 for all *X* ∈ *T*<sub>*x*</sub>*M*.

Note that (for historical reasons) the term *linear* when applied to connections, is sometimes used (like the word *affine* – see [Affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690)) to refer to connections defined on the tangent bundle or [frame bundle](wikipedia://en/366550).

### Associated bundles {#Associated_bundles}

An Ehresmann connection on a [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) (endowed with a structure group) sometimes gives rise to an Ehresmann connection on an [associated bundle](wikipedia://en/398874). For instance, a (linear) [connection in a vector bundle](wikipedia://en/391816) *E*, thought of giving a parallelism of *E* as above, induces a connection on the associated bundle of frames P*E* of *E*. Conversely, a connection in P*E* gives rise to a (linear) connection in *E* provided that the connection in P*E* is equivariant with respect to the action of the general linear group on the frames (and thus a [principal connection](wikipedia://en/470632)). It is *not always* possible for an Ehresmann connection to induce, in a natural way, a connection on an associated bundle. For example, a non-equivariant Ehresmann connection on a bundle of frames of a vector bundle may not induce a connection on the vector bundle.

Suppose that *E* is an associated bundle of *P*, so that *E* = *P* ×<sub>G</sub> *F*. A ***G*-connection** on *E* is an Ehresmann connection such that the parallel transport map τ : *F*<sub>x</sub> → *F*<sub>x′</sub> is given by a *G*-transformation of the fibers (over sufficiently nearby points *x* and *x*′ in *M* joined by a curve).[^o27]

Given a principal connection on *P*, one obtains a *G*-connection on the associated fiber bundle *E* = *P* ×<sub>G</sub> *F* via [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995).

Conversely, given a *G*-connection on *E* it is possible to recover the principal connection on the associated principal bundle *P*. To recover this principal connection, one introduces the notion of a *frame* on the typical fiber *F*. Since *G* is a finite-dimensional[^o28] Lie group acting effectively on *F*, there must exist a finite configuration of points (*y*<sub>1</sub>,...,*y*<sub>m</sub>) within *F* such that the *G*-orbit *R* = {(*gy*<sub>1</sub>,...,*gy*<sub>m</sub>) | *g* ∈ *G*} is a principal homogeneous space of *G*. One can think of *R* as giving a generalization of the notion of a frame for the *G*-action on *F*. Note that, since *R* is a principal homogeneous space for *G*, the fiber bundle *E*(*R*) associated to *E* with typical fiber *R* is (equivalent to) the principal bundle associated to *E*. But it is also a subbundle of the *m*-fold product bundle of *E* with itself. The distribution of horizontal spaces on *E* induces a distribution of spaces on this product bundle. Since the parallel transport maps associated to the connection are *G*-maps, they preserve the subspace *E*(*R*), and so the *G*-connection descends to a principal *G*-connection on *E*(*R*).

In summary, there is a one-to-one correspondence (up to equivalence) between the descents of principal connections to associated fiber bundles, and *G*-connections on associated fiber bundles. For this reason, in the category of fiber bundles with a structure group *G*, the principal connection contains all relevant information for *G*-connections on the associated bundles. Hence, unless there is an overriding reason to consider connections on associated bundles (as there is, for instance, in the case of [Cartan connections](wikipedia://en/412272)) one usually works directly with the principal connection.
