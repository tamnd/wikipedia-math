# Thin group (combinatorial group theory)

*Image: Thin group.svg.* [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277) of the group C<sub>4</sub> with generators {1} and {1,2} respectively. Out of all possible generators, {1} is the one resulting in the largest girth, meaning its upper bound is finite, and the group is thin. All finite groups have this limitation naturally, as their Cayley graph has a finite number of vertices. Additionally, all cyclic groups have a girth equal to their order.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thin group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) is said to be **thin** if there is a finite upper bound on the [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) of the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) induced by any finite [generating set](wikipedia://en/99945). The group is called **fat** if it is not thin.

:::

Given any generating set of the group, we can consider a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose vertices are elements of the group with two vertices adjacent if their ratio is in the generating set. The graph is [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) and vertex transitive. [Paths](wikipedia://en/638889) in the graph correspond to [words](wikipedia://en/12792326) in the generators.

If the graph has a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of a given length, it has a cycle of the same length containing the identity element. Thus, the girth of the graph corresponds to the minimum length of a nontrivial word that reduces to the identity. A nontrivial word is a word that, if viewed as a word in the free group, does not reduce to the identity.

If the graph has no cycles, its girth is set to be infinity.

The girth depends on the choice of generating set. A *thin* group is a group where the girth has an upper bound for all finite generating sets.

Some facts about thin and fat groups and about girths:

- Every [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is thin.
- Every [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is fat.
- The girth of a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) equals its order.
- The girth of a noncyclic [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is at most 4, because any two elements commute and the commutation relation gives a nontrivial word.
- The girth of the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) is 2.
- Every [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569), and more generally, every [solvable group](wikipedia://en/105012), is thin.
