# Frederick Rowbottom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick Rowbottom"}

**Frederick Rowbottom** (16 January 1938 – 12 October 2009) was a British [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). The [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) notion of [Rowbottom cardinals](wikipedia://en/3317885) is named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Rowbottom was educated at Glossop Grammar School (now [Glossopdale School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossopdale_School)) in [High Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Peak,_Derbyshire), [Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire), and [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated with a degree in mathematics in 1960.[^o3] Upon leaving Cambridge, he studied under [Howard Jerome Keisler](wikipedia://en/10962250) at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), earning his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) degree in 1964, with a thesis entitled *Large Cardinals and Small Constructible Sets*, under the supervision of [Jerome Keisler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Keisler).[^o4] With a recommendation from [Georg Kreisel](wikipedia://en/11761785), he took a position at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol) in 1965, where he spent the rest of his professional career.

He published a paper called "Some strong axioms of infinity incompatible with the [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472)" in the *Annals of Mathematical Logic*, 3 1971. This paper, together with his thesis, "showed that [Ramsey cardinals](wikipedia://en/326533) were weaker than [measurable cardinals](wikipedia://en/248102), and that their existence implied the constructible real continuum was [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable); he further proved that this followed also from weaker partition and two cardinal properties."[^o5] The [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) notion of [Rowbottom cardinals](wikipedia://en/3317885) is named after him,[^o6] as is the notion of a Rowbottom [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911).[^o7]

[Keith Devlin](wikipedia://en/574895) studied [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) under Rowbottom. In 1992 he and a student, Jonathan Chapman, wrote a textbook on [topos theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topos_theory), *Relative Category Theory and Geometric Morphisms: A Logical Approach*, published in *Oxford Logic Guides*, No. 16.[^o8][^o9][^o10] Rowbottom retired in 1993 at the age of 55.

Rowbottom died of heart failure in [Hadfield, Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadfield,_Derbyshire), England, on 12 October 2009, aged 71.[^o5]
