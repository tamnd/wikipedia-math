# Maura Mast

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maura Mast"}

**Maura B. Mast** is an Irish-American mathematician, [mathematics educator](wikipedia://en/326471), and academic administrator who is the current president of [Seattle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_University). She assumed office as the school's 23rd president on September 1, 2026. Her research specialties are in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [quantitative reasoning](wikipedia://en/397245). With Ethan D. Bolker, she is the author of the textbook *Common Sense Mathematics*.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Mast is the daughter of Cecil B. Mast (1927–2008), a mathematics professor at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) in [South Bend, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Bend,_Indiana). Her mother was Irish, and Mast has dual Irish and American citizenship. She grew up in South Bend and did her undergraduate studies at Notre Dame, with a double major in mathematics and anthropology.

She completed her doctorate in mathematics in 1992 at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill). Her dissertation, *Closed Geodesics in 2-step Nilmanifolds*, concerned the [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) of [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) on curved surfaces, and was supervised by Pat Eberlein.

## Career {#Career}

Mast became a faculty member at the [University of Northern Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Northern_Iowa) in 1992. After visiting professorships at [Northeastern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_University) and [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College), she moved to the [University of Massachusetts Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Boston) in 1998. There, in 2009, she became associate vice provost for undergraduate studies. In 2015, she came to [Fordham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University) as the first female dean of [Fordham College at Rose Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University#Rose_Hill). In 2022, she was promoted to the rank of full professor at Fordham University. In June 2026, she was named the 23rd president of [Seattle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_University), becoming the first woman to serve in the role.[^o6]

## Activism {#Activism}

Mast has been an active member of the Clavius Group, a group of [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) and lay mathematicians, and is a strong supporter of the Jesuit vision of [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) spirituality.

She has also been a passionate advocate for the advancement of women in mathematics and science, which she writes is "crucial for the future of the country and for women". She has participated in the governance of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) as Clerk and Executive Committee member of the association.

Mast was chair of the Special Interest Group on Quantitative Literacy of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for 2006–2007.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2017 Mast was given the Association for Women in Mathematics Service Award. The [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) has included Mast in the 2020 class of AWM Fellows for "her sustained and deep contributions to promoting and encouraging the participation of women in the mathematical sciences through AWM, the Joint Committee on Women, the MAA, and through leadership in academia".
