# Truncated order-4 octagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated order-4 octagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **truncated order-4 octagonal tiling** is a uniform tiling of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of t<sub>0,1</sub>{8,4}. A secondary construction t<sub>0,1,2</sub>{8,8} is called a **truncated octaoctagonal tiling** with two colors of [hexakaidecagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexakaidecagon).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Constructions"}

## Constructions {#Constructions}

There are two uniform constructions of this tiling, first by the \[8,4\] [kaleidoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaleidoscope), and second by removing the last mirror, \[8,4,1<sup>\+</sup>\], gives \[8,8\], (\*882).

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Truncated\_order-4\_octagonal\_tiling\_with\_882\_mirrors.png.* Truncated order-4 octagonal tiling with \*882 mirror lines

The dual of the tiling represents the fundamental domains of (\*882) [orbifold](wikipedia://en/2915678) symmetry. From \[8,8\] symmetry, there are 15 small index subgroup by mirror removal and [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operators. Mirrors can be removed if its branch orders are all even, and cuts neighboring branch orders in half. Removing two mirrors leaves a half-order gyration point where the removed mirrors met. In these images unique mirrors are colored red, green, and blue, and alternatively colored triangles show the location of gyration points. The \[8<sup>\+</sup>,8<sup>\+</sup>\], (44×) subgroup has narrow lines representing glide reflections. The [subgroup index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup_index)-8 group, \[1<sup>\+</sup>,8,1<sup>\+</sup>,8,1<sup>\+</sup>\] (4444) is the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) of \[8,8\].

One larger subgroup is constructed as \[8,8\*\], removing the gyration points of (8\*4), index 16 becomes (\*44444444), and its direct subgroup \[8,8\*\]<sup>\+</sup>, index 32, (44444444).

The \[8,8\] symmetry can be doubled by a mirror bisecting the fundamental domain, and creating [\*884 symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/884_symmetry).
