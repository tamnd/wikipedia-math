# Enrico D'Ovidio

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Enrico D'Ovidio"}

**Enrico D'Ovidio** (1843–1933) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who is known by his works on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

D'Ovidio, son of [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) parents involved in the Italian independence movement, studied at the [University of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples) under his uncle, [Achille Sannia](wikipedia://en/59714572), who prepared him to enter in the School of Bridges and Roads. In 1869, he published with Sannia a very successful textbook to teach geometry in the schools.[^n1]

Encouraged by [Eugenio Beltrami](wikipedia://en/543760), he obtained the chair on Algebra and Analytic Geometry at the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin) in 1872, and he remained there for the remaining 46 years of his life. He was also rector of the university from 1880 to 1885.[^n2]

The research of D'Ovidio was mainly in geometry and the most important works were produced when he was in Turin.[^n3] Specially interesting is his work *[Le funzioni metriche fondamentali negli spazi di quante si vogliono dimensioni e di curvatura costante](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2FBF01444650?LI=true) (The fundamental metrical functions in the n-dimensional spaces of constant curvature)*, published in 1876 and where he stated for first time the [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222) in n-dimensional curved spaces.[^n4]
