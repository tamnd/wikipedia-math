# Henry Vuibert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Vuibert"}

*Image: "Dodécaèdre régulier de 3e espèce à faces étoilées".jpg.* "Dodécaèdre régulier de 3e espèce à faces étoilées" (likely a [great icosahedron](wikipedia://en/3029898)) from Vuibert's Les Anaglyphes geometriques, meant to be viewed with what are now called [3-D glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-D_glasses)

**Désiré-Henry Vuibert** (21 August 1857 – 27 November 1945) was a French mathematician and publisher of technical books and journals, and founder of the French publishing house Vuibert.[^o2][^o3] He was a publisher of the same class as [Louis Hachette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Christophe_François_Hachette) and [Pierre Larousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Larousse), and is said to have begun his company in 1876.[^o4]p. 780

:::

His book *Les Anaglyphes geometriques* described "Vuibert's principle of anaglyphic vision" based on a "procedure invented by [Louis Ducos du Houron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Arthur_Ducos_du_Hauron), which consisted in printing, in superimposition, pairs of stereoscopic views, taken in complementary colors".[^o5] This book and the concepts therein are said to have "inspired [Marcel Duchamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Duchamp)'s interest in [anaglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaglyph_3D)".[^o6] *Les Anaglyphes geometriques* "set the standard" for representation of 3D in two dimensions and offered a "grand tour of shape" that influenced both artists and mathematicians alike.[^o7]

Also, according to one account, it was Vuibert, not [Eutaris](wikipedia://en/76418053), who first worked out what is now called a [Taylor circle](wikipedia://en/76155246).[^o8]
