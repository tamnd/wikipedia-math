# Hurwitz's theorem (number theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurwitz's theorem"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Hurwitz's theorem**, named after [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690), gives a [bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on a [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558). The theorem states that for every [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689) *ξ* there are infinitely many [relatively prime](wikipedia://en/6556) integers *m*, *n* such that

$$\left |\xi-\frac{m}{n}\right | < \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\, n^2}.$$

:::

The condition that *ξ* is irrational cannot be omitted. Moreover, the constant $\sqrt{5}$ is the best possible; if we replace $\sqrt{5}$ by any number $A > \sqrt{5}$ and we let $\xi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$ (the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)) then there exist only *finitely* many relatively prime integers *m*, *n* such that the formula above holds.

The theorem is equivalent to the claim that the [Markov constant](wikipedia://en/62331378) of every number is larger than $\sqrt{5}$.
