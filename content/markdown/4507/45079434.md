# Polar circle (geometry)

*Image: Polar circle2.svg.* solid #4169E1 solid #82c458 dashed red 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polar circle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **polar circle** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) whose center is the triangle's [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) and whose squared radius is

:::

$$\displaystyle \begin{aligned} r^2 & = \overline{HA} \times \overline{HD} = \overline{HB} \times \overline{HE} = \overline{HC} \times \overline{HF} \\[4pt] &= -4R^2 \cos A \cos B \cos C \\[4pt] &= 4R^2 - \frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{2} \end{aligned}$$

where

$A$, $B$, and $C$ denote both the triangle's [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) and the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) measures at those vertices;

$H$ is the [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) (the intersection of the triangle's [altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_%28geometry%29));

$D$, $E$, $F$ are the feet of the altitudes from vertices $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively;

$R$ is the triangle's [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) (the radius of its [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262));

$a$, $b$, $c$ are the lengths of the triangle's sides opposite vertices $A$, $B$, $C$ respectively.[^o2]

The first parts of the radius formula reflect the fact that the orthocenter divides the altitudes into segment pairs of equal products. The [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261) formula for the radius shows that the polar circle has a real existence only if the triangle is [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle), so one of its angles is obtuse and hence has a negative [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Polar circle4.svg.* solid black solid magenta solid lime solid #4169E1 solid red The centers of these circles relating to $\triangle ABC$ are all collinear–they fall on the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622).

Any two polar circles of two triangles in an [orthocentric system](wikipedia://en/161263) are [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/74072119).[^o2]

The polar circles of the triangles of a [complete quadrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_quadrilateral) form a [coaxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxal_circles) system.[^o2]

The most important property of the polar circle is the triangle is self-polar; the [polar](wikipedia://en/19451201) of each side/point is the opposite side/point.

A triangle's circumcircle, its [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243), its polar circle, and the circumcircle of its [tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle) are coaxal.[^o5]
