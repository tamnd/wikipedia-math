# Kozeny–Carman equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kozeny–Carman equation"}

The **Kozeny–Carman equation** (or **Carman–Kozeny equation** or **Kozeny equation**) is a relation used in the field of [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) to calculate the [pressure drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_drop) of a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) flowing through a [packed bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packed_bed) of solids. It is named after [Josef Kozeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Kozeny) and Philip C. Carman. The equation is only valid for [creeping flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creeping_flow), i.e. in the slowest limit of [laminar flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laminar_flow). The equation was derived by Kozeny (1927)[^o9] and Carman (1937, 1956)[^o10][^o11][^o2] from a starting point of (a) modelling fluid flow in a [packed bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packed_bed) as laminar fluid flow in a collection of curving passages/tubes crossing the [packed bed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packed_bed) and (b) [Poiseuille's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poiseuille's_law) describing laminar fluid flow in straight, circular section pipes.

:::

## Equation {#Equation}

The equation is given as:[^o2][^o4]

$$\frac{\Delta P}{L} = \frac{150 \mu}{\mathit{\Phi}_\mathrm{s}^2 d_\mathrm{p}^2}\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^2}{\varepsilon^3}V_\mathrm{0}$$

where:

- $\Delta P$ is the [pressure drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_drop);
- $L$ is the total height of the bed;
- $\mu$ is the [viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754) of the fluid;
- $\varepsilon$ is the [porosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porosity) of the bed ($\simeq 0.37$ for randomly packed spheres); [^o5]
- $\mathit{\Phi}_\mathrm{s}$ is the [sphericity](wikipedia://en/2628049) of the particles in the packed bed ($\mathit{\Phi}_\mathrm{s}$ = 1.0 for spherical particles);
- $d_\mathrm{p}$ is the diameter of the volume equivalent spherical particle;
- $V_\mathrm{0}$ is the [superficial or "empty-tower" velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superficial_velocity) which is directly proportional to the average volumetric fluid flux in the channels (q), and porosity ($\mathbf{\varepsilon}$). [^o6]

This equation holds for flow through packed beds with particle [Reynolds numbers](wikipedia://en/23868856) up to approximately 1.0, after which point frequent shifting of flow channels in the bed causes considerable [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) losses.

This equation is a particular case of [Darcy's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy's_law), with a very specific permeability. [Darcy's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy's_law) states that "*flow is proportional to the pressure gradient and inversely proportional to the fluid viscosity*" and is given as:

**q** $= \frac{\kappa}{\mu} \frac{\Delta P}{L}$

Combining these equations gives the final Kozeny equation for absolute (single phase) permeability:

$$\kappa = \mathit{\Phi}_\mathrm{s}^2 \frac {\varepsilon^3 d_\mathrm{p}^2}{150(1-\varepsilon)^2}$$

where:

- $\kappa$ is the absolute (i.e., single phase) permeability.

## History {#History}

The equation was first[^o8] proposed by Kozeny (1927)[^o9] and later modified by Carman (1937, 1956).[^o10][^o11] A similar equation was derived independently by Fair and Hatch in 1933.[^o12] A comprehensive review of other equations has been published.[^o13]
