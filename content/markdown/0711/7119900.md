# Hall's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hall's conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Hall's conjecture** is an [open question](wikipedia://en/1333335) on the differences between [perfect squares](wikipedia://en/213919) and [perfect cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_cube). It asserts that a perfect square *$y^2$* and a perfect cube *$x^3$* that are not equal must lie a substantial distance apart. This question arose from consideration of the [Mordell equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_equation) in the theory of [integer points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_point) on [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).

:::

The original version of Hall's conjecture, formulated by [Marshall Hall, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Hall,_Jr.) in 1970, says that there is a positive constant *$C$* such that for any [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) *$x$* and *$y$* for which *$y^2 \neq x^3$*,

$$|y^2 - x^3| > C|x|^{1/2}.$$

Hall suggested that perhaps *$C$* could be taken as $1/5$, which was consistent with all the data known at the time the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) was proposed.

In 1982, [Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilov_%28no_wiki_page%29) proved that $|y^2-x^3|$ is less than $433\sqrt{2}\times|x|^{1/2}$ infinitely often,[^o2] thus providing an upper bound on *$C$* and proving that the exponent $1/2$ (that is, the use of $|x|^{1/2}$) is optimal and cannot be replaced by any higher power: for no $\delta > 0$ is there a constant *$C$* such that $|y^2 - x^3| > C|x|^{1/2+\delta}$ whenever $y^2 \neq x^3$.

In 1965, Davenport proved an analogue of the above conjecture in the case of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000): if $f(t)$ and *$g(t)$* are nonzero polynomials over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) **$\mathbb{C}$** such that *$g(t)^3\neq f(t)^2$* in *$\mathbb{C}[t]$*, then

$$\deg(g(t)^2 - f(t)^3) \geq \frac{1}{2}\deg f(t) + 1.$$

The *weak* form of Hall's conjecture, stated by Stark and Trotter around 1980, replaces the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) on the right side of the [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) by any exponent *less* than $1/2$: for any *$\varepsilon > 0$*, there is some constant *$c(\varepsilon)$* depending on *$\varepsilon$* such that for any integers *$x$* and *$y$* for which *$y^2 \neq x^3$*,

$$|y^2 - x^3| > c(\varepsilon) |x|^{1/2 - \varepsilon}.$$

The original, *strong*, form of the conjecture with exponent $1/2$ has never been disproved, although it is no longer believed to be true and the term *Hall's conjecture* now generally means the version with the *$\varepsilon$* in it. For example, in 1998, [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) found the example

$$447884928428402042307918^2 - 5853886516781223^3 = -1641843,$$

for which compatibility with Hall's conjecture would require *$C$* to be less than *$0.0214\approx1/50$*, so roughly 10 times smaller than the original choice of $1/5$ that Hall suggested.

The weak form of Hall's conjecture would follow from the [ABC conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_conjecture).[^o3] A generalization to other perfect powers is [Pillai's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillai's_conjecture), though it is also known that Pillai's conjecture would be true if Hall's conjecture held for any specific *$0<\varepsilon<1/2$*.[^o4]
