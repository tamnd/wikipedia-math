# Jon Folkman

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Jon Folkman"}

**Jon Hal Folkman** (December 8, 1938 – January 23, 1969)[^o5] was an American mathematician, a student of [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), and a researcher at the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation).

:::

## Schooling {#Schooling}

Folkman was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lowell_Putnam_Mathematical_Competition) in 1960.[^o7] He received his Ph.D. in 1964 from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), under the supervision of Milnor, with a thesis entitled *Equivariant Maps of Spheres into the Classical Groups*.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

*Image: Folkman graph alt.svg.* Jon Folkman found the [semi-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/2554240) with the fewest possible vertices, the [Folkman graph](wikipedia://en/23761072).

Jon Folkman contributed important theorems in many areas of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

In [geometric combinatorics](wikipedia://en/20898181), Folkman is known for his pioneering and posthumously-published studies of [oriented matroids](wikipedia://en/24338041); in particular, the [Folkman–Lawrence topological representation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folkman–Lawrence_topological_representation_theorem)[^o10] is "one of the cornerstones of the theory of oriented matroids".[^o11][^o12] In [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) theory, Folkman solved an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335) on the foundations of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533) by proving a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) of [Gian–Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891); in proving Rota's conjecture, Folkman characterized the structure of the [homology groups](wikipedia://en/142432) of ["geometric lattices"](wikipedia://en/244321) in terms of the [free](wikipedia://en/59735) [Abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) of [finite rank](wikipedia://en/18720088).[^o13] In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), he was the first to study [semi-symmetric graphs](wikipedia://en/2554240), and he discovered the semi-symmetric graph with the fewest possible vertices, now known as the [Folkman graph](wikipedia://en/23761072).[^o14] He proved the existence, for every positive *h*, of a finite *K*<sub>*h* + 1</sub>-free graph which has a monocolored *K<sub>h</sub>* in every 2-coloring of the edges, settling a problem previously posed by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [András Hajnal](wikipedia://en/21765779).[^o15] He further proved that if *G* is a finite graph such that every set *S* of vertices contains an independent set of size (|*S*| − *k*)/2 then the chromatic number of *G* is at most *k* + 2.[^o16]

In [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882), Folkman worked with his [RAND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation) colleague [Lloyd Shapley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Shapley) to prove the [Shapley–Folkman lemma and theorem](wikipedia://en/29237460): Their results suggest that [sums of sets](wikipedia://en/504105) are approximately convex; in [mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) their results are used to explain why [economies with many agents](wikipedia://en/45938) have approximate [equilibria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_equilibrium), despite individual nonconvexities.[^o17]

In [additive combinatorics](wikipedia://en/18243392), [Folkman's theorem](wikipedia://en/29265641) states that for each assignment of finitely many colors to the positive integers, there exist arbitrarily large sets of integers all of whose nonempty sums have the same color; the name was chosen as a memorial to Folkman by his friends.[^o18] In [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175), the Rado–Folkman–Sanders theorem describes "[partition regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_regular)" sets.

### The Folkman Number F(p, q; r) {#The_Folkman_Number_F(p,_q;_r)}

For r > max{p, q}, let F(p, q; r) denote the minimum number of vertices in a graph G that has the following properties:

1. G contains no complete subgraph on r vertices,
1. in any green-red coloring of the edges of G there is either a green K<sub>p</sub> or a red K<sub>q</sub> subgraph.

Some results are

- F(3, 3; 5) \< 18 (Martin Erickson)[^o20]
- F(2, 3; 4) \< 1000 ([Vojtěch Rödl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojtěch_Rödl), Andrzej Dudek)[^o21]

## Brain cancer and despair {#Brain_cancer_and_despair}

*Image: Erdos head budapest fall 1992.jpg.* [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) visited Jon Folkman after Folkman awoke from surgery for brain cancer. To restore Folkman's confidence, Erdős immediately challenged him to solve [mathematical problems](wikipedia://en/256700).[^o23]

In the late 1960s, Folkman suffered from [brain cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_cancer); while hospitalized, Folkman was visited repeatedly by [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284). After his brain surgery, Folkman was despairing that he had lost his mathematical skills. As soon as Folkman received Graham and Erdős at the hospital, Erdős challenged Folkman with mathematical problems, helping to rebuild his [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-confidence).

Folkman later purchased a gun and killed himself. Folkman's supervisor at RAND, [Delbert Ray Fulkerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delbert_Ray_Fulkerson), blamed himself for failing to notice suicidal behaviors in Folkman. Several years later Fulkerson also killed himself.[^o23]
