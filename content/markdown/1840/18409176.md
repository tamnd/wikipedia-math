# Legendre–Clebsch condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Legendre–Clebsch condition"}

In the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882) the **Legendre–Clebsch condition** is a second-order condition which a solution of the [Euler–Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/294995) must satisfy in order to be a minimum.

:::

For the problem of minimizing

$$\int_{a}^{b} L(t,x,x')\, dt . \,$$

the condition is

$$L_{x' x'}(t,x(t),x'(t)) \ge 0, \, \forall t \in[a,b]$$

## Generalized Legendre–Clebsch {#Generalized_Legendre–Clebsch}

In [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), the situation is more complicated because of the possibility of a [singular solution](wikipedia://en/5975433). The **generalized Legendre–Clebsch condition**,[^o3] also known as convexity,[^o4] is a sufficient condition for local optimality such that when the linear sensitivity of the [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/5987577) to changes in u is zero, i.e.,

$$\frac{\partial H}{\partial u} = 0,$$

the Hessian of the Hamiltonian is positive definite along the trajectory of the solution:

$$\frac{\partial^2 H}{\partial u^2} > 0$$

In words, the generalized LC condition gives ones more necessary condition for the Hamiltonian be minimized over a singular arc.
