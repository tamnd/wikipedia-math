# Nielsen–Schreier theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nielsen–Schreier theorem"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a branch of mathematics, the **Nielsen–Schreier theorem** states that every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) is itself free.[^o11][^o2][^o22] It is named after [Jakob Nielsen](wikipedia://en/602516) and [Otto Schreier](wikipedia://en/4355201).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

A free group may be defined from a [group presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) consisting of a [set of generators](wikipedia://en/99945) with no relations. That is, every element is a product of some sequence of generators and their inverses, but these elements do not obey any equations except those trivially following from $gg^{-1}$ = 1. The elements of a free group may be described as all possible [reduced words](wikipedia://en/12792326), those [strings](wikipedia://en/27701) of generators and their inverses in which no generator is adjacent to its own inverse. Two reduced words may be multiplied by [concatenating](wikipedia://en/64474) them and then removing any generator-inverse pairs that result from the concatenation.

The **Nielsen–Schreier theorem** states that if *H* is a subgroup of a free group *G*, then *H* is also a free group. That is, there exists a set *S* of elements which generate *H*, with no nontrivial relations among the elements of *S*.

The **Nielsen–Schreier formula**, or **Schreier index formula**, quantifies the result in the case where the subgroup has finite index: if *G* is a free group of rank *n* (free on *n* generators), and *H* is a subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) \[*G* : *H*\] = *e*, then *H* is free of rank $1 + e(n{-}1)$.[^o5]

:::

## Example {#Example}

*Image: Covering-Graph.png.* The free group *G* = π<sub>1</sub>(*X*) has *n* = 2 generators corresponding to loops *a*,*b* from the base point *P* in *X*. The subgroup *H* of even-length words, with index *e* = \[*G* : *H*\] = 2, corresponds to the covering graph *Y* with two vertices corresponding to the cosets *H* and *H**\=*****aH*\=*bH*\=*a*<sup>−1</sup>*H*\=*b<sup>−</sup>*<sup>1</sup>*H*, and two lifted edges for each of the original loop-edges*a*,*b*. Contracting one of the edges of*Y*gives a homotopy equivalence to a bouquet of three circles, so that*H*\= π<sub>1</sub>(*Y*) is a free group on three generators, for example*aa*,*ab*,*ba*.|alt=***

Let *G* be the free group with two generators $a,b$, and let *H* be the subgroup consisting of all reduced words of even length (products of an even number of letters $a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}$). Then *H* is generated by its six elements $p=aa$, $q=ab$, $r=ba$, $s=bb$, $t=ab^{-1}$, $u=a^{-1}b$. A factorization of any reduced word in *H* into these generators and their inverses may be constructed simply by taking consecutive pairs of letters in the reduced word. However, this is not a set of free generators of *H* (not a basis of *H*) because the last three generators can be written in terms of the first three as $s=rp^{-1}q,\ t=pr^{-1},\ u=p^{-1}q$. Rather, *H* is generated as a free group by the three elements $p=aa,\ q=ab,\ r=ba,$ which have no relations among them; or instead by several other triples of the six redundant generators.[^o8] Further, *G* is free on *n* = 2 generators, *H* has index *e* = \[*G* : *H*\] = 2 in *G*, and *H* is free on 1 + *e*(*n*–1) = 3 generators. The Nielsen–Schreier theorem states that like *H*, every subgroup of a free group can be generated as a free group, and if the index of *H* is finite, its rank is given by the index formula.

## Proof {#Proof}

A short proof of the Nielsen–Schreier theorem uses the [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) of [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) and [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323).[^o11] A free group *G* on a set of generators is the fundamental group of a [bouquet of circles](wikipedia://en/3526954), a [topological graph](wikipedia://en/37439184) *X* with a single vertex and with a loop-edge for each generator.[^o13] Any subgroup *H* of the fundamental group is itself the fundamental group of a connected covering space *Y* → *X.* The space *Y* is a (possibly infinite) topological graph, the [Schreier coset graph](wikipedia://en/24673785) having one vertex for each [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) in *G/H*.[^o12] In any connected topological graph, it is possible to shrink the edges of a [spanning tree](wikipedia://en/455770) of the graph, producing a bouquet of circles that has the [same fundamental group](wikipedia://en/212250#Homotopy_equivalence) *H*. Since *H* is the fundamental group of a bouquet of circles, it is itself free.[^o13]

The rank of *H* can be computed using two properties of [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of a graph (the number of vertices minus the number of edges), which remains invariant under contraction of edges (homotopy). The first property is that the Euler characteristic of a [bouquet](wikipedia://en/3526954) of *s* circles is *1 - s*. The second property is [multiplicativity in covering spaces](wikipedia://en/148420#Covering_spaces): If *Y* is a degree-*d* cover of *X*, then

$$\chi(Y) = d \cdot \chi(X).$$

Now suppose *H* is a subgroup of the free group *G*, with index \[*G* : *H*\] *\= e*. As above, consider the covering graph *Y* corresponding to *H*, and contract edges to make it a bouquet of *r* circles, so that *H* is a free group of rank *r*. Applying the two properties of Euler characteristic gives:

$$\chi(Y) = 1 - r$$

and

$$\chi(Y) = e \cdot \chi(X) = e (1 - n).$$

Combining these equations, we obtain $r = 1 - e (1-n) = 1 + e (n - 1).$

This proof appears in [May's](wikipedia://en/14644098) *Concise Course*.[^o14] An equivalent proof using [homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) and the first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of *Y* is due to (txt). The original proof by Schreier forms the Schreier graph in a different way as a quotient of the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of $G$ modulo the action of $H$.[^o16]

According to [Schreier's subgroup lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schreier's_subgroup_lemma), a set of generators for a free presentation of $H$ may be constructed from [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609) in the covering graph formed by concatenating a spanning tree path from a base point (the coset of the identity) to one of the cosets, a single non-tree edge, and an inverse spanning tree path from the other endpoint of the edge back to the base point.[^o17][^o16]

:::

## Axiomatic foundations {#Axiomatic_foundations}

Although several different proofs of the Nielsen–Schreier theorem are known, they all depend on the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840). In the proof based on fundamental groups of bouquets, for instance, the axiom of choice appears in the guise of the statement that every connected graph has a spanning tree. The use of this axiom is necessary, as there exist models of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) in which the axiom of choice and the Nielsen–Schreier theorem are both false. The Nielsen–Schreier theorem in turn implies a weaker version of the axiom of choice, for finite sets.[^o19][^o20]

## History {#History}

The Nielsen–Schreier theorem is a [non-abelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonabelian_group) analogue of an older result of [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), that every subgroup of a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) is free [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974).[^o22]

(txt) originally proved a restricted form of the theorem, stating that any finitely-generated subgroup of a free group is free. His proof involves performing a sequence of [Nielsen transformations](wikipedia://en/18400182) on the subgroup's generating set that reduce their length (as reduced words in the free group from which they are drawn).[^o11][^o24] Otto Schreier proved the Nielsen–Schreier theorem in its full generality in his 1926 [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis), *Die Untergruppen der freien Gruppe*, also published in 1927 in *Abh. math. Sem. Hamburg. Univ.*[^o26]

The topological proof based on fundamental groups of bouquets of circles is due to (txt). Another topological proof, based on the [Bass–Serre theory](wikipedia://en/18673708) of [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on [trees](wikipedia://en/371914), was published by (txt).[^o29]
