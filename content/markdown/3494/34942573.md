# Sara Billey

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sara Billey"}

**Sara Cosette Billey** is an American mathematician working in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582). She is known for her contributions on [Schubert polynomials](wikipedia://en/31925388), singular loci of [Schubert varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_varieties), [Kostant polynomials](wikipedia://en/22810211), and [Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials](wikipedia://en/10161645)[^o3] often using computer verified proofs. She is currently a professor of mathematics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).[^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Billey did her undergraduate studies at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), graduating in 1990.[^o5] She earned her Ph.D. in mathematics in 1994 from the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), under the joint supervision of [Adriano Garsia](wikipedia://en/18857510) and [Mark Haiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Haiman). She returned to MIT as a postdoctoral researcher with [Richard P. Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397), and continued there as an assistant and associate professor until 2003, when she moved to the University of Washington.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2000, Billey received the [Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers), the highest honor given by the United States government to mathematicians, scientists, and engineers near the start of their independent research careers.[^o8][^o9] Billey became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012, in the inaugural class.[^o10]
