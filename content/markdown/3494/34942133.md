# Ian Sneddon

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Ian Sneddon"}

**Ian Naismith Sneddon** (8 December 1919 Glasgow, Scotland – 4 November 2000 Glasgow, Scotland) was a Scottish mathematician who worked on analysis and applied mathematics.[^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Sneddon was born in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow) on 8 December 1919, the son of Mary Ann Cameron and Naismith Sneddon. He was educated at [Hyndland School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyndland_School) in Glasgow.[^o8]

He studied mathematics and physics at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow), graduating with a BSc. He then went to the University of Cambridge, gaining an MA in 1941. From 1942 to 1945, during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he served as a Scientific Officer to the [Ministry of Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Supply). After the war he worked as a Research Officer for H H Wills Laboratory at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol). In 1946, he began lecturing in Natural Philosophy (physics) at the University of Glasgow.[^o9]

In 1950, he received a professorship at [University College of North Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_North_Staffordshire). In 1956, he returned to the University of Glasgow as Professor of Mathematics.

In 1958, he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Robert Alexander Rankin](wikipedia://en/5884314), [Philip Ivor Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Ivor_Dee), [William Marshall Smart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Marshall_Smart) and [Edward Copson](wikipedia://en/8918906). He won the Society's Makdougall-Brisbane Prize for the period 1956–58. In 1983, he was further elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London).[^o10][^o3]

He retired in 1985, and died in Glasgow on 4 November 2000.

## Family {#Family}

In 1943, he married Mary Campbell Macgregor.

## Research {#Research}

Sneddon's research was published widely including:

- with [Nevill Mott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevill_Francis_Mott): Wave mechanics and its applications, 1948
- Fourier transforms, 1951[^o13]
- Special functions of mathematical physics and chemistry, 1956[^o14]
- Elements of partial differential equations, 1957[^o15]
- with James George Defares: An introduction to the mathematics of medicine and biology, 1960[^o16]
- Mixed boundary problems in potential theory, 1966
- Lectures on transform methods, 1967
- with Morton Lowengrub: Crack problems in the classical theory of elasticity, 1969
- The use of integral transforms, 1972
- The linear theory of thermoelasticity, 1974
- Encyclopaedic dictionary of mathematics for engineers and applied scientists, 1976
- The use of operators of fractional integration in applied mathematics, 1979
- with [E. L. Ince](wikipedia://en/33356146): The solution of ordinary differential equations, 1987

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Sneddon received Honorary Doctorates from [Warsaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_University) (1973), [Heriot-Watt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heriot-Watt_University) (1982)[^o18] [University of Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hull) (1983) and [University of Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strathclyde) (1984).
