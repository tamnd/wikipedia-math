# Polyakov action

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polyakov action"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **Polyakov action** is an [action](wikipedia://en/312881) of the [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) describing the [worldsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldsheet) of a string in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305). It was introduced by [Stanley Deser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Deser) and [Bruno Zumino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Zumino) and independently by [L. Brink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Brink), [P. Di Vecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Di_Vecchia) and P. S. Howe in 1976,[^o2][^o3] and has become associated with [Alexander Polyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Markovich_Polyakov) after he made use of it in quantizing the string in 1981.[^o4] The action reads:

:::

$$\mathcal{S} = \frac{T}{2} \int\mathrm{d}^2\sigma\, \sqrt{-h}\,h^{ab} g_{\mu\nu}(X) \partial_a X^\mu(\sigma) \partial_b X^\nu(\sigma),$$

where $T$ is the string [tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tension_%28mechanics%29), $g_{\mu\nu}$ is the metric of the [target manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_manifold), $h_{ab}$ is the worldsheet metric, $h^{ab}$ its inverse, and $h$ is the determinant of $h_{ab}$. The [metric signature](wikipedia://en/463225) is chosen such that timelike directions are + and the spacelike directions are −. The spacelike worldsheet coordinate is called $\sigma$, whereas the timelike worldsheet coordinate is called $\tau$. This is also known as the [nonlinear sigma model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_sigma_model).[^o5]

The Polyakov action must be supplemented by the [Liouville action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liouville_field_theory) to describe string fluctuations.

## Global symmetries {#Global_symmetries}

N.B.: Here, a symmetry is said to be local or global from the two dimensional theory (on the worldsheet) point of view. For example, Lorentz transformations, that are local symmetries of the space-time, are global symmetries of the theory on the worldsheet.

The action is [invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_%28physics%29) under spacetime [translations](wikipedia://en/204682) and [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) [Lorentz transformations](wikipedia://en/18404)$X^\alpha \to X^\alpha + b^\alpha,$; $X^\alpha \to X^\alpha + \omega^\alpha_{\ \beta} X^\beta,$ where $\omega_{\mu \nu} = -\omega_{\nu \mu}$, and $b^\alpha$ is a constant. This forms the [Poincaré symmetry](wikipedia://en/230487) of the target manifold.

The invariance under (i) follows since the action $\mathcal{S}$ depends only on the first derivative of $X^\alpha$. The proof of the invariance under (ii) is as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal{S}' &= {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2\sigma\, \sqrt{-h}\, h^{ab} g_{\mu \nu} \partial_a \left( X^\mu + \omega^\mu_{\ \delta} X^\delta \right) \partial_b \left( X^\nu + \omega^\nu_{\ \delta} X^\delta \right) \\ &= \mathcal{S} + {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2\sigma\, \sqrt{-h}\, h^{ab} \left( \omega_{\mu \delta} \partial_a X^\mu \partial_b X^\delta + \omega_{\nu \delta} \partial_a X^\delta \partial_b X^\nu \right) + \operatorname{O}\left(\omega^2\right) \\ &= \mathcal{S} + {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2\sigma\, \sqrt{-h}\, h^{ab} \left( \omega_{\mu \delta} + \omega_{\delta \mu } \right) \partial_a X^\mu \partial_b X^\delta + \operatorname{O}\left(\omega^2\right) \\ &= \mathcal{S} + \operatorname{O}\left(\omega^2\right). \end{aligned}$$

## Local symmetries {#Local_symmetries}

The action is [invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_%28physics%29) under worldsheet [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) (or coordinates transformations) and [Weyl transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation).

### Diffeomorphisms {#Diffeomorphisms}

Assume the following transformation:

$$\sigma^\alpha \rightarrow \tilde{\sigma}^\alpha\left(\sigma,\tau \right).$$

It transforms the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) in the following way:

$$h^{ab}(\sigma) \rightarrow \tilde{h}^{ab} = h^{cd} (\tilde{\sigma})\frac{\partial {\sigma}^a}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^c} \frac{\partial {\sigma}^b}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^d}.$$

One can see that:

$$\tilde{h}^{ab} \frac{\partial}{\partial {\sigma}^a} X^\mu(\tilde{\sigma}) \frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma^b} X^\nu(\tilde{\sigma}) = h^{cd} \left(\tilde{\sigma}\right)\frac{\partial \sigma^a}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^c} \frac{\partial \sigma^b}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^d} \frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma^a} X^\mu(\tilde{\sigma})\frac{\partial}{\partial {\sigma}^b} X^\nu(\tilde{\sigma}) = h^{ab}\left(\tilde{\sigma}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^a}X^\mu(\tilde{\sigma}) \frac{\partial}{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^b} X^\nu(\tilde{\sigma}).$$

One knows that the [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of this transformation is given by

$$\mathrm{J} = \operatorname{det} \left( \frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}^\alpha}{\partial \sigma^\beta} \right),$$

which leads to

$$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}^2 \tilde{\sigma} &= \mathrm{J} \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \\ h &= \operatorname{det} \left( h_{ab} \right) \\ \Rightarrow \tilde{h} &= \mathrm{J}^2 h, \end{aligned}$$

and one sees that

$$\sqrt{-\tilde{h}} \mathrm{d}^2 {\sigma} = \sqrt{-h \left(\tilde{\sigma}\right)} \mathrm{d}^2 \tilde{\sigma}.$$

Summing up this transformation and relabeling $\tilde{\sigma} = \sigma$, we see that the action is invariant.

### Weyl transformation {#Weyl_transformation}

Assume the [Weyl transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation):

$$h_{ab} \to \tilde{h}_{ab} = \Lambda(\sigma) h_{ab},$$

then

$$\begin{aligned} \tilde{h}^{ab} &= \Lambda^{-1}(\sigma) h^{ab}, \\ \operatorname{det} \left( \tilde{h}_{ab} \right) &= \Lambda^2(\sigma) \operatorname{det} (h_{ab}). \end{aligned}$$

And finally:

{|

\| $\mathcal{S}',$ | $= {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \sqrt{-\tilde{h}} \tilde{h}^{ab} g_{\mu\nu} (X) \partial_a X^\mu (\sigma) \partial_b X^\nu(\sigma),$ |- | |$= {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \sqrt{-h} \left( \Lambda \Lambda^{-1} \right) h^{ab} g_{\mu \nu} (X) \partial_a X^\mu (\sigma) \partial_b X^\nu(\sigma) = \mathcal{S}.$ |} And one can see that the action is invariant under [Weyl transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation). If we consider *n*-dimensional (spatially) extended objects whose action is proportional to their worldsheet area/hyperarea, unless *n* = 1, the corresponding Polyakov action would contain another term breaking Weyl symmetry.

One can define the [stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/70671):

$$T^{ab} = \frac{-2}{\sqrt{-h}} \frac{\delta S}{\delta h_{ab}}.$$

Let's define:

$$\hat{h}_{ab} = \exp\left(\phi(\sigma)\right) h_{ab}.$$

Because of [Weyl symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_symmetry), the action does not depend on $\phi$:

$$\frac{\delta S}{\delta \phi} = \frac{\delta S}{\delta \hat{h}_{ab}} \frac{\delta \hat{h}_{ab}}{\delta \phi} = -\frac12 \sqrt{-h} \,T_{ab}\, e^{\phi}\, h^{ab} = -\frac12 \sqrt{-h} \,T^a_{\ a} \,e^{\phi} = 0 \Rightarrow T^{a}_{\ a} = 0,$$

where we've used the [functional derivative](wikipedia://en/301504) chain rule.

## Relation with Nambu–Goto action {#Relation_with_Nambu–Goto_action}

Writing the [Euler–Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/294995) for the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $h^{ab}$ one obtains that

$$\frac{\delta S}{\delta h^{ab}} = T_{ab} = 0.$$

Knowing also that:

$$\delta \sqrt{-h} = -\frac12 \sqrt{-h} h_{ab} \delta h^{ab}.$$

One can write the variational derivative of the action:

$$\frac{\delta S}{\delta h^{ab}} = \frac{T}{2} \sqrt{-h} \left( G_{ab} - \frac12 h_{ab} h^{cd} G_{cd} \right),$$

where $G_{ab} = g_{\mu \nu} \partial_a X^\mu \partial_b X^\nu$, which leads to

$$\begin{aligned} T_{ab} &= T \left( G_{ab} - \frac12 h_{ab} h^{cd} G_{cd} \right) = 0, \\ G_{ab} &= \frac12 h_{ab} h^{cd} G_{cd}, \\ G &= \operatorname{det} \left( G_{ab} \right) = \frac14 h \left( h^{cd} G_{cd} \right)^2. \end{aligned}$$

If the auxiliary [worldsheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldsheet) [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) $\sqrt{-h}$ is calculated from the equations of motion:

$$\sqrt{-h} = \frac{2 \sqrt{-G}}{h^{cd} G_{cd}}$$

and substituted back to the action, it becomes the [Nambu–Goto action](wikipedia://en/1004401):

$$S = {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \sqrt{-h} h^{ab} G_{ab} = {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \frac{2 \sqrt{-G}}{h^{cd} G_{cd}} h^{ab} G_{ab} = T \int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \sqrt{-G}.$$

However, the Polyakov action is more easily [quantized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29) because it is [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear).

## Equations of motion {#Equations_of_motion}

Using [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) and [Weyl transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl_transformation), with a [Minkowskian target space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), one can make the physically insignificant transformation $\sqrt{-h} h^{ab} \rightarrow \eta^{ab}$, thus writing the action in the *conformal gauge*:

$$\mathcal{S} = {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \sqrt{-\eta} \eta^{ab} g_{\mu \nu} (X) \partial_a X^\mu (\sigma) \partial_b X^\nu(\sigma) = {T \over 2}\int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \left( \dot{X}^2 - X'^2 \right),$$

where $\eta_{ab} = \left( \begin{array}{cc} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{array} \right)$.

Keeping in mind that $T_{ab} = 0$ one can derive the constraints:

$$\begin{aligned} T_{01} &= T_{10} = \dot{X} X' = 0, \\ T_{00} &= T_{11} = \frac12 \left( \dot{X}^2 + X'^2 \right) = 0. \end{aligned}$$

Substituting $X^\mu \to X^\mu + \delta X^\mu$, one obtains

$$\begin{aligned} \delta \mathcal{S} &= T \int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \eta^{ab} \partial_a X^\mu \partial_b \delta X_\mu \\ &= -T \int \mathrm{d}^2 \sigma \eta^{ab} \partial_a \partial_b X^\mu \delta X_\mu + \left( T \int d \tau X' \delta X \right)_{\sigma=\pi} - \left( T \int d \tau X' \delta X \right)_{\sigma=0} \\ &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

And consequently

$$\square X^\mu = \eta^{ab} \partial_a \partial_b X^\mu = 0.$$

The boundary conditions to satisfy the second part of the variation of the action are as follows.

- Closed strings:

[Periodic boundary conditions](wikipedia://en/9667552): $X^\mu(\tau, \sigma + \pi) = X^\mu(\tau, \sigma).$

- Open strings:[Neumann boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumann_boundary_conditions): $\partial_\sigma X^\mu (\tau, 0) = 0, \partial_\sigma X^\mu (\tau, \pi) = 0.$; [Dirichlet boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_boundary_conditions): $X^\mu(\tau, 0) = b^\mu, X^\mu(\tau, \pi) = b'^\mu.$

Working in [light-cone coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-cone_coordinates) $\xi^\pm = \tau \pm \sigma$, we can rewrite the equations of motion as

$$\begin{aligned} \partial_+ \partial_- X^\mu &= 0, \\ (\partial_+ X)^2 = (\partial_- X)^2 &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

Thus, the solution can be written as $X^\mu = X^\mu_+ (\xi^+) + X^\mu_- (\xi^-)$, and the stress-energy tensor is now diagonal. By [Fourier-expanding](wikipedia://en/59038) the solution and imposing [canonical commutation relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_commutation_relations) on the coefficients, applying the second equation of motion motivates the definition of the Virasoro operators and lead to the [Virasoro constraints](wikipedia://en/30441390#Free_field_construction) that vanish when acting on physical states.
