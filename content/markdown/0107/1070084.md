# Jean Dieudonné

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Dieudonné"}

**Jean Alexandre Eugène Dieudonné** (; 1 July 1906 – 29 November 1992) was a French mathematician, notable for research in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), for close involvement with the [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) [pseudonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym) group and the *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)* project of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), and as a historian of mathematics, particularly in the fields of functional analysis and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). His work on the [classical groups](wikipedia://en/10172878) (the book *La Géométrie des groupes classiques* was published in 1955), and on [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group), introducing what now are called [Dieudonné modules](wikipedia://en/27127074), had a major effect on those fields.

:::

He was born and brought up in [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille), with a formative stay in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) where he was introduced to [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). In 1924 he was admitted to the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), where [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) was a classmate.[^o3] He began working in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). In 1934 he was one of the group of *normaliens* convened by Weil, which would become '[Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394)'.

## Education and teaching {#Education_and_teaching}

He served in the [French Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Army) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), and then taught in [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand) until the liberation of France. After holding professorships at the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo) (1946–47), the [University of Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nancy) (1948–1952) and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (1952–53), he joined the Department of Mathematics at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) in 1953, before returning to France as a founding member of the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques). He moved to the [University of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis) to found the Department of Mathematics in 1964, and retired in 1970. He was elected as a member of the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) in 1968.

## Career {#Career}

Dieudonné drafted much of the Bourbaki series of texts, the many volumes of the [EGA](wikipedia://en/471387) [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) series, and nine volumes of his own [Éléments d'Analyse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Éléments_d'Analyse). The first volume of the *Traité* is a French version of the book *Foundations of Modern Analysis* (1960), which had become a graduate textbook on functional analysis.

He also wrote individual monographs on *Infinitesimal Calculus*, *Linear Algebra and Elementary Geometry*, [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and formal groups.

With [Laurent Schwartz](wikipedia://en/317485) he supervised the early research of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). Later from 1959 to 1964 he was at the [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques) alongside Grothendieck, and collaborating on the expository work needed to support the project of refounding [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) on the new basis of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754).
