# Hahn embedding theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hahn embedding theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the area of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) dealing with ordered structures on [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), the **Hahn embedding theorem** gives a simple description of all [linearly ordered abelian groups](wikipedia://en/349014). It is named after [Hans Hahn](wikipedia://en/1075212).[^o2]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The theorem states that every linearly ordered abelian group *G* can be [embedded](wikipedia://en/52119) as an ordered [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of the additive group $\mathbb{R}^\Omega$ endowed with a [lexicographical order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographical_order), where $\mathbb{R}$ is the additive group of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) (with its standard order), $\Omega$ is the set of *Archimedean [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260)* of *G*, and $\mathbb{R}^\Omega$ is the set of all [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) from $\Omega$ to $\mathbb{R}$ which vanish outside a [well-ordered set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-ordered_set).

Let 0 denote the [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) of *G*. For any nonzero element *g* of *G*, exactly one of the elements *g* or −*g* is greater than 0; denote this element by |*g*|. Two nonzero elements *g* and *h* of *G* are *Archimedean equivalent* if there exist [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) *N* and *M* such that *N*|*g*| > |*h*| and *M*|*h*| > |*g*|. Intuitively, this means that neither *g* nor *h* is "infinitesimal" with respect to the other. The group *G* is [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264169) if *all* nonzero elements are Archimedean-equivalent. In this case, $\Omega$ is a [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798), so $\mathbb{R}^\Omega$ is just the group of real numbers. Then Hahn's Embedding Theorem reduces to [Hölder](wikipedia://en/191652)'s theorem, which states that a linearly ordered abelian group is [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264169) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is a subgroup of the ordered additive group of the real numbers.

(Gravett 1956) gives a clear statement and [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the theorem.[^o5] The papers of (Clifford 1954)[^o7] and (Hausner Wendel 1952)[^o9] together provide another proof.[^o7][^o9] See also (Fuchs Salce 2001, 62).
