# Frigyes Riesz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frigyes Riesz"}

Riesz Frigyes **Frigyes Riesz** (Riesz Frigyes, , sometimes known in English and French as **Frederic Riesz**;[^o3] 22 January 1880 – 28 February 1956) was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_people)[^o5] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made fundamental contributions to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), as did his younger brother [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

He was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Hungary) family in [Győr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Győr), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and died in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic). Between 1911 and 1919 he was a professor at the [Franz Joseph University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_University) in [Kolozsvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolozsvár), Austria-Hungary. The post-WW1 [Treaty of Trianon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Trianon) transferred former Austro-Hungarian territory including Kolozsvár to the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania), whereupon Kolozsvár's name changed to [Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca) and the University of Kolozsvár moved to [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged), Hungary, becoming the [University of Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Szeged).[^o10] Then, Riesz was the [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) and a professor at the University of Szeged, as well as a member of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences)[^o7] and the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning). He was the older brother of the mathematician [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz).

Riesz did some of the fundamental work in developing [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and his work has had a number of important applications in physics. He established the [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713) for [bounded](wikipedia://en/455961) [symmetric operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_operator) in a form very much like that now regarded as standard.[^o5] He also made many contributions to other areas including [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954)[^o8] and he gave an elementary proof of the mean ergodic theorem.

Together with [Alfréd Haar](wikipedia://en/491228), Riesz founded the *[Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_Scientiarum_Mathematicarum)* journal.

He had an uncommon method of giving lectures: he entered the lecture hall with an assistant and a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent). The docent then began reading the proper passages from Riesz's handbook and the assistant wrote the appropriate equations on the blackboard—while Riesz himself stood aside, nodding occasionally.[^o9]

The Swiss-American mathematician [Edgar Lorch](wikipedia://en/37579702) spent 1934 in Szeged working under Riesz and wrote a reminiscence about his time there, including his collaboration with Riesz.[^o10]

The corpus of his bibliography was compiled by the mathematician [Pál Medgyessy](wikipedia://en/69590748).[^o11]
