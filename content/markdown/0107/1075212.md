# Hans Hahn (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Hahn"}

**Hans Hahn** (; ; 27 September 1879 – 24 July 1934) was an Austrian mathematician and philosopher who made contributions to [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), and [order theory](wikipedia://en/361924). In philosophy he was among the main [logical positivists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) of the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) as the son of a higher government official of the [K.K. Telegraphen-Korrespondenz Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraphen-Korrespondenz_Bureau), in 1898 Hahn became a student at the [Universität Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) starting with a study of law. In 1899 he switched over to mathematics and spent some time at the universities of [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). In 1902 he took his Ph.D. in Vienna, on the subject "Zur Theorie der zweiten Variation einfacher Integrale". He was a student of [Gustav von Escherich](wikipedia://en/29030428).

He was appointed to the teaching staff ([habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)) in Vienna in 1905. After 1905/1906 as a stand-in for [Otto Stolz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Stolz) at the [University of Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Innsbruck) and some further years as a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in Vienna, he was nominated in 1909 [Professor extraordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_extraordinarius) in [Czernowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernivtsi), at that time a town within the empire of Austria. After joining the Austrian army in 1915, he was badly wounded in 1916 and became again [Professor extraordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_extraordinarius), now in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn). In 1917 he was nominated a regular [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) there and in 1921 he returned to Vienna with this title, where he stayed until his rather early death in 1934 at the age of 54, following cancer surgery.[^o4] He had married Eleonore ("Lilly") Minor in 1909, and they had a daughter, Nora (born 1910).

He was also interested in philosophy, and was part of a discussion group concerning [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach)'s [positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) with [Otto Neurath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Neurath) (who had married Hahn’s sister [Olga Hahn-Neurath](wikipedia://en/10197563) in 1912), and [Phillip Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Frank) prior to the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). In 1922, he helped arrange [Moritz Schlick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Schlick)'s entry into the group, which led to the founding of the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle), the group that was at the center of [logical positivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivist) thought in the 1920s. His most famous student was [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736), whose Ph.D. thesis was completed in 1929. After [Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss) the fact that Hans Hahn had been of partial [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) origin[^o5] caused Gödel's difficulties with getting a position at the University of Vienna.[^o6] Within the Vienna Circle, Hahn was also known (and controversial) for using his mathematical and philosophical work to study [psychic phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasensory_perception); according to [Karl Menger](wikipedia://en/149431) he sometimes openly advocated further research into [extrasensory perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasensory_perception) while lecturing.[^o7]

Politically Hahn was a socialist and was chairman of the Association of Socialist University Teachers.[^o8] He contributed to the Social Democrat magazine *[Der Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Kampf_%28magazine%29)*.[^o9]

Hahn's contributions to mathematics include the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860) and (independently of [Banach](wikipedia://en/29646) and [Steinhaus](wikipedia://en/71735)) the [uniform boundedness principle](wikipedia://en/247392). Other theorems include:

- the [Hahn decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/2985858);
- the [Hahn embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/1075596);
- the [Hahn–Kolmogorov theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahn–Kolmogorov_theorem);
- the [Hahn–Mazurkiewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/352398);
- the [Vitali–Hahn–Saks theorem](wikipedia://en/1075379).

Hahn authored the book (Hahn 1921): according to [Arthur Rosenthal](wikipedia://en/33183847),[^o11] "... (it) formed a great advance in the Theory of Real functions and had a great influence on the further development of this theory." He was also a co-author of the book *Set Functions*,[^o12] published in 1948 by Arthur Rosenthal, fourteen years after his death in Vienna in 1934.

In 1921 he received the [Richard Lieben Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieben_Prize#Richard_Lieben_Prize). In 1926 he was the president of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973). In 1928 he was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Bologna.[^o13]

Hahn died in 1934 following surgery for a newly diagnosed cancer. He was only 54 and his death came as a shock to friends and colleagues.[^o14]
