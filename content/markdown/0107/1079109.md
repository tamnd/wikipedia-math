# ABA digital signature guidelines

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ABA digital signature guidelines"}

The **ABA digital signature guidelines**[^o2] are a set of guidelines published on 1 August 1996 by the [American Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bar_Association) (ABA) Section of Science and Technology Law. The authors are members of the Section's Information Security Committee.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The document was the first overview of principles and a framework for the use of [digital signatures](wikipedia://en/59644) and [authentication](wikipedia://en/47967) in electronic commerce from a legal viewpoint, including technologies such as [certificate authorities](wikipedia://en/296370) and [public key infrastructure](wikipedia://en/194112) (PKI). The guidelines were a product of a four-year collaboration by 70 [lawyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer) and technical experts from a dozen countries, and have been adopted as the model for legislation by some states in the [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), including [Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida) and [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah).

The Digital Signature Guidelines were followed by the Public Key Infrastructure Assessment Guidelines[^o4] published by the ABA in 2003.

A similar effort was undertaken in Slovenia by the Digital Signature Working Group (within the [Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Commerce_and_Industry_of_Slovenia) (CCIS)).
