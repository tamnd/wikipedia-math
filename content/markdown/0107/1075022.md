# Cluster decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cluster decomposition"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the **cluster decomposition property** states that [experiments](wikipedia://en/59861) carried out far away from each other cannot influence each other. Usually applied to [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), it requires that [vacuum expectation values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_expectation_value) of [products](wikipedia://en/24977) of [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_%28physics%29) localized in [bounded regions](wikipedia://en/48258) of [space](wikipedia://en/27667) [factorize](wikipedia://en/82341) whenever these regions becomes sufficiently distant from each other. First formulated by [Eyvind Wichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyvind_Wichmann) and James H. Crichton in 1963 in the context of the [*S*-matrix](wikipedia://en/622053),[^o2] it was conjectured in [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg)'s "[folk theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_theorem_%28physics%29)" that in the [low energy limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_field_theory) the cluster decomposition property, together with [Lorentz invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_covariance) and [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), inevitably lead to quantum field theory. [String theory](wikipedia://en/28305) satisfies all three of the conditions and so provides a counter-example against this being true at all energy scales.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

The *S*-matrix $S_{\beta \alpha}$ describes the [scattering amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude) of a process with an initial state $\alpha$ evolving into a final state $\beta$. If the initial and final states consist of two clusters, with $\alpha_1$ and $\beta_1$ close to each other but far from the pair $\alpha_2$ and $\beta_2$, then the cluster decomposition property requires that the *S*-matrix factorizes

$$S_{\beta \alpha} \rightarrow S_{\beta_1 \alpha_1}S_{\beta_2\alpha_2}$$

as the distance between the two clusters increases. The physical interpretation of this is that any two spatially well separated experiments $\alpha_1 \rightarrow \beta_1$ and $\alpha_2 \rightarrow \beta_2$ cannot influence each other.[^o6] This condition is fundamental to the ability of doing physics without having to know the [state](wikipedia://en/30876419) of the entire [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe). By expanding the *S*-matrix in terms of connected *S*-matrix elements $S_{\beta \alpha}^c$, which at the [perturbative level](wikipedia://en/297069) are equivalent to [connected Feynman diagrams](wikipedia://en/11617#Connected_diagrams:_linked-cluster_theorem), the cluster decomposition property can be restated as demanding that connected *S*-matrix elements must vanish whenever some of its clusters of particles are far apart from each other.

This position space formulation can also be reformulated in terms of the [momentum space](wikipedia://en/36740699) *S*-matrix $\tilde S^c_{\beta \alpha}$.[^o7] Since its [Fourier transformation](wikipedia://en/52247) gives the position space connected *S*-matrix, this only depends on position through exponential terms. Therefore, performing a uniform [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation_operator_%28quantum_mechanics%29) in a direction $\boldsymbol a$ on a subset of particles will effectively change the momentum space *S*-matrix as

$$\tilde S_{\beta \alpha}^c \xrightarrow{\boldsymbol x_i \rightarrow \boldsymbol x_i+\boldsymbol a} e^{i\boldsymbol a\cdot (\sum_i \boldsymbol p_i)} \tilde S_{\beta \alpha}^c.$$

By [translational invariance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translational_symmetry), a translation of all particles cannot change the *S*-matrix, therefore $\tilde S_{\beta \alpha}$ must be proportional to a momentum conserving [delta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_function) $\delta (\Sigma \boldsymbol p)$ to ensure that the translation exponential factor vanishes. If there is an additional delta function of only a subset of momenta corresponding to some cluster of particles, then this cluster can be moved arbitrarily far through a translation without changing the *S*-matrix, which would violate cluster decomposition. This means that in momentum space cluster decomposition requires that the *S*-matrix only has a single delta function.

Cluster decomposition can also be formulated in terms of [correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29), where for any two operators $\mathcal O_1(x)$ and $\mathcal O_2(x)$ localized to some region, the vacuum expectation values factorize as the two operators become distantly separated

$$\lim_{|\boldsymbol x|\rightarrow \infty}\langle \mathcal O_1(\boldsymbol x)\mathcal O_2(0)\rangle \rightarrow \langle \mathcal O_1\rangle \langle \mathcal O_2 \rangle.$$

This formulation allows for the property to be applied to theories that lack an *S*-matrix such as [conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/364774). It is in terms of these [Wightman functions](wikipedia://en/293622) that the property is usually formulated in [axiomatic quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/3559472).[^o8] In some formulations, such as Euclidean [constructive field theory](wikipedia://en/1231204), it is explicitly introduced as an [axiom](wikipedia://en/928).[^o9]

## Properties {#Properties}

If a theory is constructed from [creation and annihilation operators](wikipedia://en/701991), then the cluster decomposition property automatically holds. This can be seen by expanding out the *S*-matrix as a sum of Feynman diagrams which allows for the identification of connected *S*-matrix elements with connected Feynman diagrams. [Vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) arise whenever creation and annihilation operators commute past each other leaving behind a single momentum delta function. In any [connected diagram](wikipedia://en/1614492) with V vertices, I internal lines and L loops, I-L of the delta functions go into fixing internal momenta, leaving V-(I-L) delta functions unfixed. A form of [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/148420) states that any graph with C disjoint connected components satisfies C = V-I+L. Since the connected *S*-matrix elements correspond to C=1 diagrams, these only have a single delta function and thus the cluster decomposition property, as formulated above in momentum space in terms of delta functions, holds.

Microcausality, the [locality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_locality) condition requiring commutation relations of local operators to vanish for [spacelike separations](wikipedia://en/28758#spacetime_interval), is a sufficient condition for the *S*-matrix to satisfy cluster decomposition. In this sense cluster decomposition serves a similar purpose for the *S*-matrix as microcausality does for [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29), preventing [causal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality_%28physics%29) influence from propagating between regions that are distantly separated.[^o12] However, cluster decomposition is weaker than having no [superluminal causation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faster-than-light) since it can be formulated for classical theories as well.[^o13]

One key requirement for cluster decomposition is that it requires a unique [vacuum state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_state), with it failing if the vacuum state is a [mixed state](wikipedia://en/30876419).[^o14] The rate at which the correlation functions factorize depends on the spectrum of the theory, where if it has [mass gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_gap) of mass $m$ then there is an exponential falloff $\langle \phi(x) \phi(0)\rangle \sim e^{-m|x|}$ while if there are [massless particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massless_particle) present then it can be as slow as $1/|x|^2$.[^o15]
