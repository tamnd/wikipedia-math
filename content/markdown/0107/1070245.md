# Szolem Mandelbrojt

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Szolem Mandelbrojt"}

**Szolem Mandelbrojt** (10 January 1899 – 23 September 1983) was a Polish-French mathematician who specialized in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). He was a professor at the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) from 1938 to 1972, where he held the Chair of Analytical Mechanics and Celestial Mechanics.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Szolem Mandelbrojt was born on 10 January 1899 in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw), Poland into a Jewish family of [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Jews) descent. He was initially educated in Warsaw, then in 1919 he moved to [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) (then [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)) and spent a year as a student of the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician [Sergei Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Natanovich_Bernstein). A year later, he emigrated to France and settled in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). In subsequent years, he attended the seminars of [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), [Henri Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514), [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000), and others. In 1923, he received a doctorate from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) on the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448). Hadamard was his Ph.D. advisor.

In 1924 Mandelbrojt was awarded a Rockefeller Fellowship in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). In May 1926 he married Gladys Manuelle Grunwald (born 28 June 1904 in Paris). From 1926 to 1927, he spent a year as an assistant professor at the [Rice Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) (now Rice University) in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston), Texas.

In 1928 he returned to France - having received French citizenship in 1927 – and was appointed an assistant professor at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Lille_Nord_de_France). The following year he became a full professor at the [University of Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Clermont-Ferrand). In December 1934 Mandelbrojt co-founded the [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) group of mathematicians, of which he was a member until [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). He succeeded Hadamard at Collège de France in 1938 and took up the Chair of [Analytical Mechanics](wikipedia://en/454450) and [Celestial Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_Mechanics).[^o4]

Mandelbrojt helped several members of his family emigrate from Poland to France in 1936. One of them, his nephew [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999), was to discover the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) and coin the word [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) in the 1970s.

In 1939 he fought for France when the country was invaded by the Nazis, then in 1940, along with many scientists helped by [Louis Rapkine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Rapkine) and the [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation), Mandelbrojt relocated to the United States, taking up a position at the Rice Institute. In 1944 he joined the scientific committee of the [Free French Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French_Forces) in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London), England.

In 1945 Mandelbrojt moved back to France and resumed his professional activities at Collège de France, where he remained until his retirement in 1972. In his retirement year he was elected a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).

Szolem Mandelbrojt died at the age of 84 in Paris, France, on 23 September 1983.

## Research {#Research}

*Image: Szolem Mandelbrojt 2.jpg.* Szolem Mandelbrojt, 1941

Even though Mandelbrojt was an early member of the [Bourbaki group](wikipedia://en/167394#The_group), and he did take part in a number of Bourbaki gatherings until the breakout of the war, his main research interests were actually quite remote from [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). As evidenced by his publications (see next), he focused on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), with an emphasis on [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258), [lacunary series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacunary_series), and [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372).

Rather than a Bourbakist, he is perhaps more accurately described as a follower of [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599). Together with [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185) and [Torsten Carleman](wikipedia://en/6063370), he can be viewed as a moderate modernizer of classical [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659).

[Shmuel Agmon](wikipedia://en/32542224), [Jean-Pierre Kahane](wikipedia://en/6949961), [Yitzhak Katznelson](wikipedia://en/5643045), [Paul Malliavin](wikipedia://en/10459388) and [George Piranian](wikipedia://en/14393400) are among his students.
