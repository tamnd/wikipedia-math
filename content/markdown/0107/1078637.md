# Herbrand–Ribet theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herbrand–Ribet theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Herbrand–Ribet theorem** is a result on the [class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of certain [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field). It is a strengthening of [Ernst Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117)'s theorem to the effect that the [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p* divides the [class number](wikipedia://en/150907) of the [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) of *p*-th [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *p* divides the numerator of the *n*-th [Bernoulli number](wikipedia://en/4964) *B*<sub>*n*</sub> for some *n*, 0 \< *n* \< *p* − 1. The Herbrand–Ribet theorem specifies what, in particular, it means when *p* divides such an *B<sub>n</sub>*.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) Δ of the [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) of *p*th roots of unity for an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) prime *p*, **Q**(ζ) with ζ<sup>*p*</sup> = 1, consists of the *p* − 1 group elements σ<sub>*a*</sub>, where $\sigma_a(\zeta) = \zeta^a$. As a consequence of [Fermat's little theorem](wikipedia://en/53453), in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer) $\mathbb{Z}_p$ we have *p* − 1 roots of unity, each of which is congruent mod *p* to some number in the range 1 to *p* − 1; we can therefore define a [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) ω (the Teichmüller character) with values in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ by requiring that for *n* relatively prime to *p*, ω(*n*) be congruent to *n* modulo *p*. The *p* part of the class group is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module (since it is *p*-primary), hence a module over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]$. We now define [idempotent elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent_element) of the group ring for each *n* from 1 to *p* − 1, as

$$\epsilon_n = \frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{a=1}^{p-1} \omega(a)^n \sigma_a^{-1}.$$

It is easy to see that $\sum\epsilon_n = 1$ and $\epsilon_i\epsilon_j = \delta_{ij}\epsilon_i$ where $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). This allows us to break up the *p* part of the ideal class group *G* of **Q**(ζ) by means of the idempotents; if *G* is the *p*-primary part of the ideal class group, then, letting *G*<sub>*n*</sub> = ε<sub>*n*</sub>(*G*), we have $G = \oplus G_n$.

The Herbrand–Ribet theorem states that for odd *n*, *G*<sub>*n*</sub> is nontrivial if and only if *p* divides the Bernoulli number *B*<sub>*p*−*n*</sub>.[^o4]

The theorem makes no assertion about even values of *n*, but there is no known *p* for which *G*<sub>*n*</sub> is nontrivial for any even *n*: triviality for all *p* would be a consequence of [Vandiver's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandiver's_conjecture).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

The part saying *p* divides *B*<sub>*p*−*n*</sub> if *G*<sub>*n*</sub> is not trivial is due to [Jacques Herbrand](wikipedia://en/159901).[^o8] The converse, that if *p* divides *B*<sub>*p*−*n*</sub> then *G*<sub>*n*</sub> is not trivial is due to [Kenneth Ribet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Ribet), and is considerably more difficult. By [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695), this can only be true if there is an unramified extension of the field of *p*th roots of unity by a [cyclic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_extension) of degree *p* which behaves in the specified way under the action of Σ; Ribet proves this by actually constructing such an extension using methods in the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). A more elementary proof of Ribet's converse to Herbrand's theorem, a consequence of the theory of [Euler systems](wikipedia://en/1052176), can be found in Washington's book on cyclotomic fields.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Ribet's methods were developed further by [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) and [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) in order to prove the [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332),[^o12] a corollary of which is a strengthening of the Herbrand–Ribet theorem: the power of *p* dividing *B*<sub>*p*−*n*</sub> is exactly the power of *p* dividing the order of *G*<sub>*n*</sub>.
