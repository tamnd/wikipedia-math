# Cotton tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cotton tensor"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Cotton tensor** on a [pseudo-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/287555) of dimension *n* is a third-order [tensor](wikipedia://en/256738) concomitant of the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795). The vanishing of the Cotton tensor for *n* = 3 is [necessary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessary_condition) and [sufficient condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_condition) for the manifold to be locally [conformally flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformally_flat). By contrast, in dimensions *n* ≥ 4, the vanishing of the Cotton tensor is necessary but not sufficient for the metric to be conformally flat; instead, the corresponding necessary and sufficient condition in these higher dimensions is the vanishing of the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848), while the Cotton tensor just becomes a constant times the divergence of the Weyl tensor. For *n* \< 3 the Cotton tensor is identically zero. The concept is named after [Émile Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émile_Cotton).

:::

The proof of the classical result that for *n* = 3 the vanishing of the Cotton tensor is equivalent to the metric being conformally flat is given by [Eisenhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_P._Eisenhart) using a standard [integrability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition) argument. This tensor density is uniquely characterized by its conformal properties coupled with the demand that it be differentiable for arbitrary metric tensors, as shown by (Aldersley 1979). The Cotton tensor is a component of the full [Cartan conformal curvature](wikipedia://en/5804750) in all dimensions $n>2$, the other component being the Weyl curvature.

Recently, the study of three-dimensional spaces is becoming of great interest, because the Cotton tensor restricts the relation between the Ricci tensor and the [energy–momentum tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy–momentum_tensor) in the [Einstein equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_equation) and plays an important role in the [Hamiltonian formalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_formalism) of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In coordinates, and denoting the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) by *R*<sub>*ij*</sub> and the scalar curvature by *R*, the components of the Cotton tensor are

$$C_{ijk} = \nabla_{k} R_{ij} - \nabla_{j} R_{ik} + \frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left( \nabla_{j}Rg_{ik} - \nabla_{k}Rg_{ij}\right).$$

The Cotton tensor can be regarded as a vector valued [2-form](wikipedia://en/221519), and for *n* = 3 the [Hodge star operator](wikipedia://en/310914) converts this into a second-order trace-free tensor density:

$$C_i^j = \nabla_{k} \left( R_{li} - \frac{1}{4} Rg_{li}\right)\epsilon^{klj},$$

sometimes called the *Cotton–[York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_W._York) tensor*.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

### Conformal rescaling {#Conformal_rescaling}

Under conformal rescaling of the metric $\tilde{g} = e^{2\omega} g$ for some scalar function $\omega$, we see that the [Christoffel symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoffel_symbol) transform as

$$\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}=\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}+S^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}$$

where $S^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}$ is the tensor

$$S^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma} = \delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma} \partial_{\beta} \omega + \delta^{\alpha}_{\beta} \partial_{\gamma} \omega - g_{\beta\gamma} \partial^{\alpha} \omega$$

The [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) transforms as

$${\widetilde{R}^{\lambda}}{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}={R^{\lambda}}_{\mu\alpha\beta}+\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\lambda}_{\beta\mu}-\nabla_{\beta}S^{\lambda}_{\alpha\mu}+S^{\lambda}_{\alpha\rho}S^{\rho}_{\beta\mu}-S^{\lambda}_{\beta\rho}S^{\rho}_{\alpha\mu}$$

In $n$-dimensional manifolds, we obtain the [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) by contracting the transformed Riemann tensor to see it transform as

$$\widetilde{R}_{\beta\mu}=R_{\beta\mu}-g_{\beta\mu}\nabla^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\omega-(n-2)\nabla_{\mu}\partial_{\beta}\omega+(n-2)(\partial_{\mu}\omega\partial_{\beta}\omega-g_{\beta\mu}\partial^{\lambda}\omega\partial_{\lambda}\omega)$$

Similarly the [Ricci scalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_scalar) transforms as

$$\widetilde{R}=e^{-2\omega}R-2e^{-2\omega}(n-1)\nabla^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\omega-(n-2)(n-1)e^{-2\omega}\partial^{\lambda}\omega\partial_{\lambda}\omega$$

Combining all these facts together permits us to conclude the Cotton–York tensor transforms as

$$\widetilde{C}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+(n-2)\partial_{\lambda}\omega {W_{\beta\gamma\alpha}}^{\lambda}$$

or using coordinate independent language as

$$\tilde{C} = C \; + (n-2) \; \operatorname{grad} \, \omega \; \lrcorner \; W,$$

where the gradient is contracted with the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) *W*.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetries"}

### Symmetries {#Symmetries}

The Cotton tensor has the following symmetries:

$$C_{ijk} = -C_{ikj}$$

and therefore

$$C_{[ijk]} = 0.$$

In addition the Bianchi formula for the [Weyl tensor](wikipedia://en/1026848) can be rewritten as

$$\delta W = (3-n) C,$$

where $\delta$ is the positive divergence in the first component of *W*.

:::
