# René de Possel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="René de Possel"}

**Lucien Alexandre Charles René de Possel** (7 February 1905 – 1974) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), one of the founders of the [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394) group, and later a pioneer [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), working in particular on [optical character recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_character_recognition).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Possel was born in [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille). He had the conventional background for a member of Bourbaki: the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), [agrégation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrégation), and then study in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). He left Bourbaki at an early stage: there was an obvious personal matter intruding between him and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) who had married De Possel's ex-wife Eveline following her divorce from De Possel in 1937.[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

De Possel published an early book on [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) in 1936 (*Sur la théorie mathématique des jeux de hasard et de réflexion*). His later research work in computer science at the [Institut Blaise Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Blaise_Pascal) was in a position of relative isolation, as the subject strove for independence and to move away from the imposed role of service provider in the field of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506). He became director there in 1960, in succession to [Louis Couffignal](wikipedia://en/1836048) (1902–1966), until an administrative reorganisation under the [C. N. R. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._N._R._S.) in 1969. He was a leading figure in pushing for the later [Institut de Programmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_Programmation). He died in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).
