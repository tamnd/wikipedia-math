# Shadowing lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shadowing lemma"}

In the [theory of dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/990632), the **shadowing lemma** is a [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) describing the behaviour of pseudo-orbits near a [hyperbolic invariant set](wikipedia://en/2658073). Informally, the theory states that every pseudo-orbit (which one can think of as a numerically computed trajectory with rounding errors on every step) stays uniformly close to some true trajectory (with slightly altered initial position)—in other words, a pseudo-trajectory is "shadowed" by a true one.[^o3] This suggests that numerical solutions can be trusted to represent the orbits of the dynamical system. However, caution should be exercised as some shadowing trajectories may not always be [physically realizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physically_realizable).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Given a map *f* : *X* → *X* of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) (*X*, *d*) to itself, define a **ε-pseudo-orbit** (or **ε-orbit**) as a sequence $(x_n)$ of points such that $x_{n+1}$ belongs to a ε-neighborhood of $f(x_n)$.

Then, near a hyperbolic invariant set, the following statement holds:[^o7] Let Λ be a hyperbolic invariant set of a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) f. There exists a neighborhood U of Λ with the following property: for any *δ* > 0 there exists *ε* > 0, such that any (finite or infinite) ε-pseudo-orbit that stays in U also stays in a δ-neighborhood of some true orbit.

$$\forall (x_n),\, x_n\in U, \, d(x_{n+1},f(x_n))<\varepsilon \quad \exists (y_n), \, \, y_{n+1}=f(y_n),\quad \text{such that} \,\, \forall n \,\, x_n\in U_{\delta}(y_n).$$

:::

## Popular culture {#Popular_culture}

In [Men at Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_at_Arms) (1993), a novel by [Terry Pratchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pratchett), the "Shadowing Lemma" appears as a fictional creature that "exists in only two dimensions and eats mathematicians." In the same passage, Pratchett also references the "quantum weather butterfly," a nod to the [butterfly effect](wikipedia://en/4024) and chaos theory.
