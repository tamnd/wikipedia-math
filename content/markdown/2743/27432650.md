# John Law (bishop)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Law"}

**John Law** (1745–1810) was an English mathematician and clergyman who began his career as a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge), and went on to become chaplain to the [Lord Lieutenant of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland) and [Church of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Ireland) bishop of [Clonfert and Kilmacduagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Clonfert_and_Kilmacduagh) (1782–1787), [Killala and Achonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Killala_and_Achonry) (1787–1795), and finally of [Elphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Elphin) (1795–1810).

:::

He was a lifelong friend and correspondent of the philosopher [William Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paley).

## Early life {#Early_life}

The son of [Edmund Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Law), later [Bishop of Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Carlisle), and Mary Christian, Law was born at [Greystoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greystoke,_Cumbria) in [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland), where his father was [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28ecclesiastical%29). He was educated at [Charterhouse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterhouse_School) and [Christ's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Cambridge), where in 1766 he graduated Bachelor of Arts with [first-class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_honours) in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) and was named as [second Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29).[^o4] He proceeded [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxbridge_and_Dublin%29) in 1769.

## Career {#Career}

Law became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of Christ's and an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) clergyman, and spent several years as a [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor) and lecturer at Cambridge. In 1773 his father gave him his first [benefices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefice), as [vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar) of [Warkworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warkworth,_Northumberland), Northumberland, and as [prebendary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prebendary) of [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle,_Cumbria).[^o4] In 1777 he was collated [Archdeacon of Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Carlisle). In April 1782, he went to Ireland as chaplain to the [Lord Lieutenant of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland), [William Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland), who had large estates in [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland).[^o11]

Law was quickly nominated as [Bishop of Clonfert and Kilmacduagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Clonfert_and_Kilmacduagh) on 26 July 1782 and consecrated on 21 September. The same year, he graduated [Doctor of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Divinity).[^o11] The appointment to Clonfert was unexpected. It was later reported that the Duke of Portland, after a long legal battle with [Sir James Lowther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Lowther,_1st_Earl_of_Lonsdale) over estates in Carlisle, was anxious to reward a man who had helped him in that matter with other preferments then held by Law.[^o4]

Law was a lifelong friend of [William Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Paley), with whom he corresponded for decades.[^o7] Paley and Law had been friends at Cambridge, where Law lectured on mathematics and Paley on [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and [morals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics). When Law became Bishop of Clonfert, Paley was his successor as Archdeacon of Carlisle, and it was the urging of Law which led Paley to expand his Cambridge lectures into his book *The Principles of Moral and Political Philosophy* (1785).[^o8][^o9] In that work, the chapter on *Reverencing the Deity* was ascribed to Law.[^o4] Among other matters, they corresponded on the scientific proofs which could be used to support a theological argument. In 1797, for instance, Law wrote to Paley: "In your chapter on divine contrivance, you must have an article on the [solar system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_System)..."[^o7]

On taking up the post in Clonfert, Law hired the mathematician [John Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Howard_%28mathematician%29) as his [steward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steward_%28office%29), but dispensed with Howard's services in 1786 after "an unfortunate marriage".[^o10]

Law was translated to [Killala and Achonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Killala_and_Achonry) on 10 November 1787.[^o11] On learning that almost the whole population of his new see were [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic), he commented: "That as it was a hopeless task to make them [protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants), it would answer every desirable purpose to make them *good catholicks*." At his own expense, Law then distributed throughout the diocese a new edition of the Rev. John Gother's *The Sincere Christian's Guide in the Choice of Religion*.[^o4]

He was translated again to become [Bishop of Elphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Elphin) in 1795, being nominated on 11 March and appointed by [letters patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_patent) on 27 March. He died in [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin) on 18 March 1810 and was buried in the Chapel of [Trinity College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Dublin), where he had founded prizes for mathematics.[^o11]

## Family {#Family}

On 17 January 1782, while living in Carlisle, Law married Anne Thomlinson, a daughter of the Rev. William Plaskett and widow of John Thomlinson of [Blencogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blencogo), [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland), but they had no children.[^o4][^o11][^o13] His wife, born on 4 August 1741, was the half-sister of [James Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wallace_%28British_politician%29), [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister), of [Featherstone Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Featherstone_Castle), Member of Parliament, [Solicitor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_for_England_and_Wales) and [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_for_England_and_Wales), and the aunt of [Thomas Wallace, 1st Baron Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Wallace,_1st_Baron_Wallace).[^o14]

Law was the elder brother of [Ewan Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewan_Law) (1747–1829), of [Edward Law, 1st Baron Ellenborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Law,_1st_Baron_Ellenborough) (1750–1818), who became [Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chief_Justice_of_England_and_Wales), and of [George Henry Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Henry_Law) (1761–1845), [Bishop of Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Chester) and [Bishop of Bath and Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Bath_and_Wells).[^o11] Another brother, [Thomas Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Law_%281756–1834%29) (1756–1834), was a business man who settled first in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) and then in 1793 in the United States, where he married as his second wife [Eliza Parke Custis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Parke_Custis_Law), a granddaughter of [Martha Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Washington).[^o15][^o16] Eliza and Thomas Law divorced in 1811.[^o17]
