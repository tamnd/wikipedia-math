# Non-abelian group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-abelian group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and specifically in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **non-abelian group**, sometimes called a **non-commutative group**, is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) (*G*, ∗) in which there exists at least one pair of elements *a* and *b* of *G*, such that *a* ∗ *b* ≠ *b* ∗ *a*.[^o2][^o3] This class of groups contrasts with the [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), where all pairs of group elements [commute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity).

:::

Non-abelian groups are pervasive in mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). One of the simplest examples of a non-abelian group is the [dihedral group of order 6](wikipedia://en/2375824). It is the smallest finite non-abelian group. A common example from physics is the [rotation group SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_group_SO%283%29) in three dimensions (for example, rotating something 90 degrees along one axis and then 90 degrees along a different axis is not the same as doing them in reverse order).

Both [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536) and [continuous groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_group) may be non-abelian. Most of the interesting [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) are non-abelian, and these play an important role in [gauge theory](wikipedia://en/23912155).
