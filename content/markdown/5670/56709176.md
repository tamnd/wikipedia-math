# Jean Yang

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Yang"}

**Jean Yee Hwa Yang** is an Australian statistician known for her work on [variance reduction](wikipedia://en/5187715) for [microarrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microarray), and for inferring proteins from [mass spectrometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_spectrometry) data. Yang is a Professor in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Yang earned a bachelor's degree with first class honours and a University Medal from the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney) in 1996, in mathematics and statistics.

After working for half a year at the [Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Scientific_and_Industrial_Research_Organisation), Yang then went to the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), for graduate study, completing her Ph.D. in statistics in 2002. Her dissertation, "Statistical methods in the design and analysis of gene expression data from cDNA microarray experiment", was supervised by [Terry Speed](wikipedia://en/11272394).

Yang did postdoctoral research in [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) and [bioinformatics](wikipedia://en/4214) with Mark R. Segal at the [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco), where she became an assistant professor in 2003. In 2005, she returned to the University of Sydney with a faculty position.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2015, Yang was the winner of the [Moran Medal](wikipedia://en/23296035) of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) for her "significant contributions to the development of statistical methodology for analyzing molecular data arising in contemporary biomedical research". She was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_New_South_Wales) in 2025.[^o4]
