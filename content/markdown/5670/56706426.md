# Ermelinda DeLaViña

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ermelinda DeLaViña"}

**Ermelinda DeLaViña** is an American mathematician specializing in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). She is a professor in the Computer and Mathematical Sciences Department of the [University of Houston–Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston–Downtown), where she is also associate dean of the College of Science and Technology.

:::

## Education {#Education}

DeLaViña grew up in a working-class family in Texas, with roots stretching back for five generations there. Her parents came from [Bishop, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop,_Texas), but raised her in [Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston). Inspired by a 9th-grade algebra teacher, she aimed for a college education despite the discouragement of her school counselors. She started her undergraduate studies at the [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston), but dropped out after one term, and after working for two years began again at the [University of Texas–Pan American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas–Pan_American), where she graduated with a bachelor's degree in mathematics and a minor in computer science in 1989, becoming the first in her family with a college degree. She returned to graduate school at the University of Houston and completed a Ph.D. in mathematics there in 1997. Her [doctoral supervisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_supervisor) was [Siemion Fajtlowicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemion_Fajtlowicz), with whom she worked on the [Graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti_%28program%29) computer program for automatically formulating conjectures in graph theory.

## Career {#Career}

After completing her doctorate, DeLaViña became an assistant professor at the University of Houston–Downtown. She was promoted to full professor there in 2010, and became associate dean in 2012.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

One of DeLaViña's results in graph theory is related to an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) showing that every [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) has an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) that is at least as large as its [radius](wikipedia://en/1020021); DeLaViña showed that the graphs with no larger independent set always contain a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437).
