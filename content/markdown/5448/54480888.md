# Heinrich Friedrich Gretschel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Heinrich Friedrich Gretschel"}

**Heinrich Friedrich Gretschel** (21 October 1830, in Prietitz near Elstra, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) – 2 February 1892, in [Freiburg, Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg,_Switzerland)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and scientist.

:::

In 1847, Gretschel became a student at the Technischen Bildungsanstalt (now [TU Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TU_Dresden)). On 28 April 1851 he was enrolled at [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University), where he studied mathematics, natural sciences and ancient languages. In 1854, he passed the examination for the higher school office in the first ranking and became a teacher at the Leipziger Gesamtgymnasium.[^o3]

On 2 January 1873 he was promoted by the Faculty of Philosophy of the University of Leipzig as a result of completing the mathematical dissertation, *Lehrbuch zur Einführung in die organische Geometrie* ("Introduction to organic geometry").[^o4][^o5][^o3]

On 7 January 1873 he became professor of mathematics and geometric construction at the [Bergakademie Freiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergakademie_Freiberg), a position that he held until his death in 1892.[^o3] In 1876, Gretschel became a Bergrat professor.[^o3] During 1875–1880, 1881–1883, and 1886–1889 he belonged to the Bergakademischen Senate.[^o6]

Gretschel wrote and lectured in a wide range of fields, publishing books about [meteorology](wikipedia://en/19904), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography).[^o3] He and [Georg Bornemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Bornemann) wrote about the organization of the [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table), published as *Das Naturliche System der Elemente* (1883).[^o7][^o8] Gretschel also wrote about the construction of stringed instruments in the [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin) family[^o9] and co-wrote a book on the construction of the [pianoforte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pianoforte) with [Julius Blüthner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Blüthner), *Lehrbuch des Pianofortebaues* (1872).[^o10]
