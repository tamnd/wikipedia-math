# Tukey's trend test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tukey's trend test"}

**Tukey's trend test** (or the **Tukey-Ciminera-Heyse trend test**) is a [non-parametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics) or semi-parametric [statistical hypothesis test](wikipedia://en/30284) used to detect a [dose–response relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dose–response_relationship) between a discrete independent variable (such as dosage level) and a continuous dependent variable.

:::

First proposed by [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974), Joseph Ciminera, and John Heyse in 1985, the test was developed to address the limitations of [simple linear regression](wikipedia://en/2593771) when the exact functional form of the dose-response curve is unknown.[^o2] It is frequently applied in [toxicology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxicology) and [pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacology) to determine if increasing exposure to a substance correlates with a monotonic increase or decrease in a biological response.

## Background {#Background}

In clinical and laboratory experiments, researchers often test a [control group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_group) alongside several increasing dose levels (e.g., control, low, medium, and high). While a [one-way ANOVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-way_ANOVA) can identify differences between dose groups, it does not account for the ordered nature of the doses. On the other hand, standard linear regression assumes a strictly linear relationship, which may fail to detect trends that are concave, convex, or [step functions](wikipedia://en/170939).

Tukey's trend test provides a robust alternative by using a "maximum-type" test statistic. It simultaneously considers multiple possible "shapes" of the trend, reducing the risk of a [Type II error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_II_error) when the true dose-response shape is non-linear.[^o4]

## Methodology {#Methodology}

The test typically evaluates a null hypothesis ($H_0$) of no trend against an alternative hypothesis ($H_A$) of a monotonic trend.

### Scoring shapes {#Scoring_shapes}

The original Tukey trend test employs three different sets of scores ($x$) to represent potential dose-response shapes:

1. Arithmetic: assumes equal increments between doses (e.g., 0, 1, 2, 3).
1. Ordinal: ranks the doses regardless of their physical value (e.g., 1, 2, 3, 4)
1. Logarithmic: assumes the effect is proportional to the logarithm of the dose (e.g., $0, \ln(d_{1}+1), \ln(d_{2}+1), \dots$

### Test statistic and correlation {#Test_statistic_and_correlation}

Testing for a trend is treated as a regression problem. For each set of scores, a [t-statistic](wikipedia://en/22474343) is calculated, based on the [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708) ($r$) between the response ($Y$) and the assigned dose scores ($x$). For each score, its corresponding t-statistic is defined as:

$$t=\frac{r\sqrt{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}$$

where $n$ is the total number of observations. The t-statistics are denoted $t_\text{arith}$, $t_\text{ord}$, and $t_\log$ respectively, and quantify the level of "statistical certainty" that the slope of the relationship is non-zero. The final test statistic is formed as the maximum of these individual statistics:

$$T_\max = \max\left(t_\text{arith}, t_\text{ord}, t_\log\right)$$

Because $T_\max$ is the maximum of several correlated statistics, it does not follow a standard [Student's t-distribution](wikipedia://en/105375). Instead, the corresponding [p-value](wikipedia://en/554994) must be adjusted, either via a [multivariate t-distribution](wikipedia://en/7984768) or through [permutation](wikipedia://en/2468117) methods.[^o8]

## Significance and regulatory context {#Significance_and_regulatory_context}

It is often cited as a precursor to the [Multiple Comparison Procedures and Modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Comparison_Procedures_and_Modeling) (MCP-MOD) approach.[^o10] While Tukey's 1985 test used a fixed set of three contrasts, MCP-MOD generalizes this to any number of candidate parametric models.[^o11]

In 2014, the [European Medicines Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Medicines_Agency) (EMA) issued a positive qualification opinion on MCP-MOD as an efficient methodology for [Phase II](wikipedia://en/34382035) dose-finding studies.[^o12] The [Food and Drug Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration) gave a similar endorsement in 2016.[^o13]

## Comparison to other tests {#Comparison_to_other_tests}

Unlike the [Jonckheere's trend test](wikipedia://en/35615250), which is fully non-parametric and based on ranks, Tukey's trend test is more closely related to regression-based approaches.
