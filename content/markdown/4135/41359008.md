# Glen Bredon

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Glen Bredon"}

**Glen Eugene Bredon** (August 24, 1932[^o3] – May 8, 2000) was an American mathematician who worked in the area of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bredon received a bachelor's degree from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1954 and a master's degree from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1955. In 1958, he wrote his PhD thesis at Harvard (*Some theorems on transformation groups*) under the supervision of [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871).[^o6] Starting in 1960, he worked as a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and since 1969 at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), until he retired in 1993, after which he moved to [North Fork, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Fork,_California).[^o9]

From 1958 to 1960 and 1966/67 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

The [Bredon cohomology](wikipedia://en/43619732) of topological spaces under action of a topological group is named after him.[^o7]

In the late 1980s, he wrote the program DOS.MASTER for [Apple II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_II) computers. He is the author of the programs [Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_%28assembler%29) (a [macro assembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macro_assembler)) and [ProSel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProSel) for Apple machines.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1963, while at Berkeley, he married folk singer [Anne Bredon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Bredon) (née Loeb), with whom he had two children, Aaron and Joelle.[^o9]

## Software {#Software}

Bredon is the author of the programs [Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_%28assembler%29) (a [macro assembler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macro_assembler)) and [ProSel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProSel) for Apple machines.

**DOS.MASTER** (also: DOS Master) is a program for Apple II computers which allows [Apple DOS 3.3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_DOS) programs to be placed on a [hard drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disk_drive) or 3½" [floppy disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floppy_disk) and run from [ProDOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_ProDOS). Bredon wrote it as a commercial program during the late 1980s where it experienced widespread success; it was released into the [public domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_domain) by his family after the author's death.[^o11]

DOS.MASTER was created as a result of [Apple Computer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Inc.) abandonment of the DOS 3.3 [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) and its subsequent replacement by ProDOS. Apple provided a program to copy files from DOS 3.3 volumes to new ProDOS volumes; however, programs written for DOS 3.3 did not run on ProDOS volumes. DOS.MASTER enabled a widely installed base of previously ProDOS incompatible programs written for DOS 3.3 to be run under ProDOS. DOS.MASTER took a large ProDOS partition, formatted it as a file, and then created a series of DOS 3.3 volumes within that file. The program allowed the user to create one of four DOS 3.3 volume sizes: 140 KB (the standard capacity of an Apple II 5¼" floppy disk), 160 KB, 200 KB, or 400 KB (the maximum that DOS 3.3 could address). Up to 255 of these volumes could be created on the larger ProDOS partition, space allowing, essentially simulating a very large stack of virtual floppy disk drives.
