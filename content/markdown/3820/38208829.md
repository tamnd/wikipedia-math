# Varignon's theorem (mechanics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Varignon's theorem"}

**Varignon's theorem** is a theorem of French mathematician [Pierre Varignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Varignon) (1654–1722), published in 1687 in his book *Projet d'une nouvelle mécanique*. The theorem states that the [torque](wikipedia://en/30400) of a [resultant](wikipedia://en/32533#Addition_and_subtraction) of two [concurrent forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resultant_force) about any point is equal to the [algebraic sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_sum) of the torques of its components about the same point. In other words, "If many [concurrent](wikipedia://en/2206157) forces are acting on a body, then the algebraic sum of torques of all the forces about a point in the plane of the forces is equal to the torque of their resultant about the same point."[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Consider a set of *$N$* [force vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_vector) $\mathbf{f}_1, \mathbf{f}_2, ..., \mathbf{f}_N$ that concur at a point $\mathbf{O}$ in space. Their resultant is:

*Image: Varignontheoremdiagram.png.*

$$\mathbf{F}=\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{f}_i.$$

The torque of each vector with respect to some other point $\mathbf{O}_1$ is

$$\mathbf{\mathrm{T}}_{O_1}^{\mathbf{f}_i} = (\mathbf{O}-\mathbf{O}_1) \times \mathbf{f}_i.$$

Adding up the torques and pulling out the common factor $(\mathbf{O}-\mathbf{O_{1}})$, one sees that the result may be expressed solely in terms of $\mathbf{F}$, and is in fact the torque of $\mathbf{F}$ with respect to the point $\mathbf{O}_1$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{\mathrm{T}}_{O_1}^{\mathbf{f}_i} = (\mathbf{O}-\mathbf{O}_1) \times \left ( \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{f}_{i} \right ) = (\mathbf{O}-\mathbf{O}_1) \times \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{\mathrm{T}}_{O_1}^{\mathbf{F}}.$$

Proving the theorem, i.e. that the sum of torques about $\mathbf{O}_1$ is the same as the torque of the sum of the forces about the same point.

:::
