# Jeff Rosenthal

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Jeff Rosenthal"}

**Jeffrey Seth Rosenthal** (born October 13, 1967) is a Canadian [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and nonfiction author. He is a professor in the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto)'s department of statistics, cross-appointed with its [department of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto_Department_of_Mathematics).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rosenthal graduated from [Woburn Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woburn_Collegiate_Institute) in 1984, and received his B.Sc. (in mathematics, physics, and computer science) from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1988, and his Ph.D. in mathematics ("Rates of Convergence for Gibbs Sampler and Other Markov Chains") from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1992, supervised by [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467).[^o5] He was an assistant professor in the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) from 1992 to 1993.[^o6] Rosenthal began his career in the Department of Statistics at the University of Toronto as an assistant professor in 1993, became an associate professor in 1997, and has been a full professor since 2000.[^o6] Rosenthal has written numerous research papers about the theory of [Markov chain Monte Carlo](wikipedia://en/236801) and other statistical computation [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms), many joint with [Gareth O. Roberts](wikipedia://en/24723921).[^o7][^o8] He has also written a graduate textbook on probability theory[^o9] and co-authored an undergraduate textbook on probability and statistics,[^o10] and supervised numerous student research projects.[^o11]

## Public engagements {#Public_engagements}

In 2005 Rosenthal wrote a book for the general public, *Struck by Lightning: The Curious World of Probabilities*,[^o13] which was a bestseller in Canada[^o14][^o15] and has been published in ten languages.[^o16] He has been interviewed by the media about such diverse topics as crime statistics,[^o17] pedestrian deaths,[^o18] gambling probabilities,[^o19][^o20] and television game shows,[^o21] and has appeared on *[William Shatner's Weird or What?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shatner's_Weird_or_What?)*. His [Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_%28magazine%29) Q&A video received 2.5 million views.[^o22]

In 2006, Rosenthal did the statistical analysis used by the [Canadian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) television [news magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_magazine) *[The Fifth Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Estate_%28TV_series%29)* to expose the [Ontario lottery retailer fraud scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Lottery_and_Gaming_Corporation#Retailer_fraud),[^o23][^o24] which was debated in the [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario) provincial legislature.[^o25] In 2010 his research with [Albert H. Yoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_H._Yoon) about the [U.S. Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States) was quoted in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*.[^o26] His statistical analysis refuted suspicions by former world [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) champion [Vladimir Kramnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Kramnik) about [Hikaru Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikaru_Nakamura)'s long online chess winning streaks.[^o27][^o28] He has also written about the [Monty Hall problem](wikipedia://en/6026198).[^o29]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Rosenthal received the [CRM](wikipedia://en/3505436)-[SSC](wikipedia://en/15572327) Prize in 2006,[^o32] the [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) in 2007,[^o33] the [Statistical Society of Canada](wikipedia://en/15572327) Gold Medal in 2013,[^o34] and a Faculty of Arts & Science Outstanding Teaching Award in 1998.[^o35] He was elected a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) in 2005,[^o36] and of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 2012.[^o37]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Rosenthal's father [Peter Rosenthal](wikipedia://en/43370076) (1941–2024) and mother Helen Stephanie Rosenthal (1942–2017) were originally from [Queens, New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens,_New_York_City). They emigrated to Canada in 1967 where both of them became math professors at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).[^o2]

Besides his research, Rosenthal performs [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music)[^o39] and [improvisational comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisational_theatre), including at [The Bad Dog Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bad_Dog_Theatre_Company).[^o40] He performed musical accompaniment for the sketch comedy show *Middle Raged* by [Geri Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geri_Hall) and [Gary Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Pearson_%28comedian%29).[^o41][^o42][^o43][^o44]

Jeffrey Rosenthal is married to Margaret Fulford, who is the [University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Toronto) Librarian.[^o45] His father-in-law was journalist [Robert Fulford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fulford_%28journalist%29).[^o46]
