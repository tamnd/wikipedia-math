# Crocco's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Crocco's theorem"}

In [aerodynamics](wikipedia://en/2819), **Crocco's theorem** relates the [flow velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity), [vorticity](wikipedia://en/166010), and [stagnation pressure](wikipedia://en/2953344) (or [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891)) of a [potential flow](wikipedia://en/58320). This [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) gives the relation between the thermodynamics and fluid kinematics. The theorem was first enunciated by [Alexander Friedmann](wikipedia://en/728892) for the particular case of a [perfect gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_gas) and published in 1922:[^o2]

:::

$$\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt}=T \nabla s-\nabla h$$

However, usually this theorem is connected with the name of Italian scientist Luigi Crocco,[^o3] a son of [Gaetano Crocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Crocco).

Consider an element of fluid in the flow field subjected to translational and rotational motion: because stagnation pressure loss and entropy generation can be viewed as essentially the same thing, there are three popular forms for writing Crocco's theorem:

1. Stagnation pressure: $\mathbf u \times \boldsymbol \omega =v \nabla p_0$ [^o4]
1. Entropy (the following form holds for plane steady flows): $T \frac{ds}{dn} = \frac{dh_0}{dn} +u \omega$ [^o5]
1. Momentum: $\frac{\partial \mathbf u}{\partial t} + \nabla \left(\frac{u^2}{2} + h \right) = \mathbf u \times \boldsymbol \omega + T \nabla s + \mathbf{g},$

In the above equations, $\mathbf u$ is the flow velocity vector, $\omega$ is the vorticity, $v$ is the specific volume, $p_0$ is the [stagnation pressure](wikipedia://en/2953344), $T$ is [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050), $s$ is specific [entropy](wikipedia://en/9891), $h$ is specific [enthalpy](wikipedia://en/10274), $\mathbf{g}$ is specific [body force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_force), and $n$ is the direction normal to the streamlines. All quantities considered (entropy, enthalpy, and body force) are *specific*, in the sense of "per unit mass".
