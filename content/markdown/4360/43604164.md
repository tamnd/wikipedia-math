# Conductor of an elliptic curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conductor of an elliptic curve"}

In mathematics, the **conductor of an elliptic curve** over the field of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) (or more generally a [local](wikipedia://en/145375) or [global field](wikipedia://en/288358)) is an integral [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977), which is analogous to the [Artin conductor](wikipedia://en/34170016) of a [Galois representation](wikipedia://en/486929). It is given as a product of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928), together with associated exponents, which encode the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) in the [field extensions](wikipedia://en/11634) generated by the points of finite order in the [group law](wikipedia://en/10225#The_group_law) of the [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225). The primes involved in the conductor are precisely the primes of [bad reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_reduction) of the curve: this is the [Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion](wikipedia://en/35128259).

:::

Ogg's formula expresses the conductor in terms of the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) and the number of components of the special fiber over a local field, which can be computed using [Tate's algorithm](wikipedia://en/14795246).

## History {#History}

The conductor of an elliptic curve over a local field was implicitly studied (but not named) by (Ogg 1967) in the form of an integer invariant ε+δ which later turned out to be the exponent of the conductor.

The conductor of an elliptic curve over the rationals was introduced and named by (Weil 1967) as a constant appearing in the functional equation of its [L-series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-series_of_an_elliptic_curve), analogous to the way the conductor of a global field appears in the functional equation of its zeta function. He showed that it could be written as a product over primes with exponents given by order(Δ) − μ + 1, which by Ogg's formula is equal to ε+δ. A similar definition works for any global field. Weil also suggested that the conductor was equal to the level of a modular form corresponding to the elliptic curve.

(Serre Tate 1968) extended the theory to [conductors of abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/18847878).

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *E* be an elliptic curve defined over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *K* and **p** a prime ideal of the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of *K*. We consider a *minimal equation* for *E*: a generalised [Weierstrass equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_equation) whose coefficients are **p**-integral and with the valuation of the discriminant ν<sub>**p**</sub>(Δ) as small as possible. If the discriminant is a **p**-unit then *E* has *good reduction* at **p** and the exponent of the conductor is zero.

We can write the exponent *f* of the conductor as a sum ε + δ of two terms, corresponding to the tame and wild ramification. The tame ramification part ε is defined in terms of the reduction type: ε=0 for good reduction, ε=1 for multiplicative reduction and ε=2 for additive reduction. The wild ramification term δ is zero unless **p** divides 2 or 3, and in the latter cases it is defined in terms of the [wild ramification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_ramification) of the extensions of *K* by the [division points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_point) of *E* by Serre's formula

$$\delta = \dim_{Z/lZ} \text {Hom}_{Z_l[G]}(P, M).$$

Here *M* is the group of points on the elliptic curve of order *l* for a prime *l*, *P* is the [Swan representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_representation), and *G* the Galois group of a finite extension of *K* such that the points of *M* are defined over it (so that *G* acts on *M*)

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Ogg's formula"}

## Ogg's formula {#Ogg's_formula}

The exponent of the conductor is related to other invariants of the elliptic curve by Ogg's formula:

$$f_\mathbf{p} = \nu_\mathbf{p} ( \Delta ) + 1 - n \ ,$$

where *n* is the number of components (without counting multiplicities) of the singular fibre of the [Néron minimal model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Néron_minimal_model) for E. (This is sometimes used as a definition of the conductor).

Ogg's original proof used a lot of case by case checking, especially in characteristics 2 and 3. (Saito 1988) gave a uniform proof and generalized Ogg's formula to more general arithmetic surfaces.

We can also describe ε in terms of the valuation of the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) ν<sub>**p**</sub>(*j*): it is 0 in the case of good reduction; otherwise it is 1 if ν<sub>**p**</sub>(*j*) \< 0 and 2 if ν<sub>**p**</sub>(*j*) ≥ 0.

:::

## Global conductor {#Global_conductor}

Let *E* be an elliptic curve defined over a number field *K*. The global conductor is the ideal given by the product over primes of *K*

$$f(E) = \prod_\mathbf{p} \mathbf{p}^{f_\mathbf{p}} \ .$$

This is a finite product as the primes of bad reduction are contained in the set of primes divisors of the discriminant of any model for *E* with global integral coefficients.
