# Ruth Charney

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Charney"}

**Ruth Michele Charney** (born 1950)[^o3] is an American mathematician known for her work in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [Artin groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_group). Other areas of research include [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).[^o4] She holds the Theodore and Evelyn G. Berenson Chair in Mathematics at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University). She was in the first group of mathematicians named Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5][^o6] She was in the first group of mathematicians named Fellows of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o20][^o7] She served as president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) during 2013–2015,[^o8] and served as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for the 2021–2023 term.[^o9]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Charney attended [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University), graduating in mathematics in 1972.[^o11] She then attended [Merce Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merce_Cunningham)'s dance studio for a year, studying [modern dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_dance). She received her Ph.D. from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1977 under [Wu-Chung Hsiang](wikipedia://en/50980728).[^o12]

## Work {#Work}

Following her graduation from [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), Charney took a postdoctoral position at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), followed by an NSF postdoctoral appointment/assistant professor position at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o14] She worked for [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) until 2003, when she returned to work at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University).

Charney served as president of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) during 2013–2015.[^o11] She emphasized the importance of encouraging young women in mathematics through summer programs, mentorships, and parental involvement.[^o15]

She has served as an editor of the journal *[Algebraic and Geometric Topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_and_Geometric_Topology)* from 2000 to 2007.[^o16][^o14]

In 2019 she was elected to serve as president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) during 2021–2023.[^o9] She currently serves as the [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) Immediate Past President.[^o17]

Additionally, she was a member at large for the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) from 1992 to 1994.[^o18]

## Honors {#Honors}

- In 2013 Charney was named a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the inaugural class.[^o5][^o6]
- In 2017 she was selected as a Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) in the inaugural class.[^o20][^o7]
