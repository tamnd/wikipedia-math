# Napierian logarithm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Napierian logarithm"}

*Image: NapLog.png.* A plot of the Napierian logarithm for inputs between 0 and 10<sup>8</sup>.

*Image: Napier's Mirici Logarithmorum table for 19 deg.agr.jpg.* The 19 degree pages from Napier's 1614 table of logarithms of trigonometric functions la

The term **Napierian logarithm** or **Naperian logarithm**, named after the Scottish mathematician [John Napier](wikipedia://en/15993) (1550–1617), is often used to mean the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476). Napier did not introduce this *natural* logarithmic function, although it is named after him.[^o2][^o3] However, if it is taken to mean the "[logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860)" as originally produced by Napier, it is a function given by (in terms of the modern [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476)):

$$\operatorname{NapLog}(x) = -10^7 \ln \left(x/10^7\right)$$

:::

The Napierian logarithm satisfies identities quite similar to the modern logarithm, such as[^o4]

$$\operatorname{NapLog}(xy) \approx \operatorname{NapLog}(x)+\operatorname{NapLog}(y)-161180956$$

or

$$\operatorname{NapLog}\left(xy/10^7\right) = \operatorname{NapLog}(x)+\operatorname{NapLog}(y)$$

In Napier's 1614 la, he provides tables of logarithms of sines for 0 to 90°, where the values given (columns 3 and 5) are

$$\operatorname{NapLog}(\theta) = -10^7 \ln (\sin(\theta))$$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Napier's "logarithm" is related to the [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476) by the relation

$$\operatorname{NapLog} (x) \approx 10000000 (16.11809565 - \ln x)$$

and to the [common logarithm](wikipedia://en/174482) by

$$\operatorname{NapLog} (x) \approx 23025851 (7 - \log_{10} x).$$

Note that

$$16.11809565 \approx 7 \ln \left(10\right)$$

and

$$23025851 \approx 10^7 \ln (10).$$

Napierian logarithms are essentially natural logarithms with decimal points shifted 7 places rightward and with sign reversed. For instance the logarithmic values

$$\ln(.5000000) = -0.6931471806$$

$$\ln(.3333333) = -1.0986123887$$

would have the corresponding Napierian logarithms:

$$\operatorname{NapLog}(5000000) = 6931472$$

$$\operatorname{NapLog}(3333333) = 10986124$$

For further detail, see [history of logarithms](wikipedia://en/8185549).

:::
