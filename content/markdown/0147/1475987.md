# Gian Francesco Malfatti

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gian Francesco Malfatti"}

**Giovanni Francesco Giuseppe Malfatti**, also known as **Gian Francesco** or **Gianfrancesco** (26 September 1731 – 9 October 1807) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). Best known for posing the [Malfatti problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malfatti_problem), he was also the first mathematician to “solve” the quintic using a resolvent of sixth degree.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gian Francesco Malfatti was born in [Ala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala,_Trentino), [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino), [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). He studied at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) where his mentors included [Vincenzo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Riccati), [Laura Bassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bassi), [F. M. Zanotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Zanotti) and [Gabriele Manfredi](wikipedia://en/38298712).[^n1] He moved to Ferrara in 1754 and became a professor at the [University of Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ferrara) when it was re-established in 1771. In 1782 he was one of the founders of the *[Società Italiana delle Scienze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Società_Italiana_delle_Scienze)*, founded by [Antonio Maria Lorgna](wikipedia://en/46666197), later to become the [Accademia nazionale delle scienze detta dei XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_delle_scienze_detta_dei_XL). Following [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880)'s invasion of Italy, Malfatti reluctantly collaborated with the French. When the Austro-Russian army occupied Ferrara, he was stripped of his university tenure. He was reinstated in his positions after the [battle of Marengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marengo). Malfatti died in [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara) on 9 October 1807, aged 76.

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

*Image: Malfatti's circles.svg.* [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894)

In 1803, Malfatti posed the problem of carving three circular [columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column) out of a triangular block of marble, using as much of the marble as possible, and conjectured that three mutually tangent circles inscribed within the triangle would provide the optimal solution. These tangent circles are now known as [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894) after his work, despite the earlier work of Japanese mathematician [Ajima Naonobu](wikipedia://en/841517) and of Malfatti's countryman Gilio di Cecco da Montepulciano on the same problem[^o7][^o8] and even though the conjecture was later proven false.[^o9] Several [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488) derived from these circles are also named after both Ajima and Malfatti.[^o10][^o11]

*Image: Lemniscate gravity.gif.* The gravitational property of the lemniscate

*Image: Malfatti - De natura radicum in aequationibus quarti gradus, 1758 - 1402537.jpg.* *De natura radicum in aequationibus quarti gradus*, 1758

Additional topics in Malfatti's research concerned [quintic equations](wikipedia://en/303368) and the property of the [lemniscate of Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/250908) that a ball rolling down an arc of the lemniscate, under the influence of gravity, will take the same time to traverse it as a ball rolling down a straight line segment connecting the endpoints of the arc. In a study published in 1771 Malfatti, using a [method of elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_elimination) derived from Euler and [Bézout](wikipedia://en/96017), arrived by calculation of a 5th degree equation at a resolvent of the 6th degree, the so-called Malfatti resolvent.[^o12] In 1804, he criticized [Paolo Ruffini](wikipedia://en/547426)’s first attempts to prove that equations of degree higher than 4 are not solvable by radicals and thus contributed to Ruffini’s later works.[^o13]
