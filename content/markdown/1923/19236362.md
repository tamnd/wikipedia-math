::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="4 21 polytope"}

In 8-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **4<sub>21</sub>** is a semiregular [uniform 8-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821715), constructed within the symmetry of the [E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994) [group](wikipedia://en/19447). It was discovered by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset), published in his 1900 paper. He called it an *8-ic semi-regular figure*.[^o3]

:::

Its [Coxeter symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_symbol) is **4<sub>21</sub>**, describing its bifurcating [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), with a single ring on the end of the 4-node sequences, .

The **rectified 4<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the mid-edges of the **4<sub>21</sub>**. The **birectified 4<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the triangle face centers of the **4<sub>21</sub>**. The **trirectified 4<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the tetrahedral centers of the **4<sub>21</sub>**.

These polytopes are part of a family of 255 = 2<sup>8</sup> − 1 convex [uniform 8-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821715), made of [uniform 7-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821719) facets and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), defined by all [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of one or more rings in this Coxeter-Dynkin diagram: .

The **4<sub>21</sub>** polytope has 17,280 [7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-simplex) and 2,160 [7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-orthoplex) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647), and 240 vertices. Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is the **[3<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353)** polytope. As its vertices represent the [root vectors](wikipedia://en/277087) of the [simple Lie group](wikipedia://en/292831) [E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994), this polytope is sometimes referred to as the **E<sub>8</sub> root polytope**.

The vertices of this polytope can also be obtained by taking the 240 [integral octonions](wikipedia://en/51436#Integral_octonions) of norm 1. Because the [octonions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octonions) are a nonassociative [normed division algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_division_algebra), these 240 points have a multiplication operation making them not into a group but rather a [loop](wikipedia://en/25223#Loop), in fact a [Moufang loop](wikipedia://en/755329).

For visualization this 8-dimensional polytope is often displayed in a special skewed [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) direction that fits its 240 vertices within a regular [triacontagon](wikipedia://en/927478) (called a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869)). Its 6720 edges are drawn between the 240 vertices. Specific higher elements (faces, cells, etc.) can also be extracted and drawn on this projection.

### Alternative names {#Alternative_names}

- This polytope was discovered by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset), who described it in his 1900 paper as an **8-ic semi-regular figure**.[^o3] It is the [last finite semiregular figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope) in his enumeration, semiregular to him meaning that it contained only regular facets.
- [E. L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Elte) named it V<sub>240</sub> (for its 240 vertices) in his 1912 listing of semiregular polytopes.[^o13]
- [H.S.M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) called it **4<sub>21</sub>** because its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) has three branches of length 4, 2, and 1, with a single node on the terminal node of the 4 branch.
- **Dischiliahectohexaconta-myriaheptachiliadiacosioctaconta-zetton** (Acronym: fy) - 2160-17280 facetted polyzetton (Jonathan Bowers)[^n1]

### Coordinates {#Coordinates}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 8 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 8-dimensional space.

The 240 vertices of the **4<sub>21</sub>** polytope can be constructed in two sets: 112 ($2^{2} \times^{8}C_{2}$) with coordinates obtained from $(\pm 2,\pm 2,0,0,0,0,0,0)\,$ by taking an arbitrary [combination](wikipedia://en/5308) of signs and an arbitrary [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of coordinates, and 128 roots (2<sup>7</sup>) with coordinates obtained from $(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1)\,$ by taking an even number of minus signs (or, equivalently, requiring that the sum of all the eight coordinates be a multiple of 4).

Each vertex has 56 nearest neighbors; for example, the nearest neighbors of the vertex $(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)$ are those whose coordinates sum to 4, namely the 28 obtained by permuting the coordinates of $(2, 2,0,0,0,0,0,0)\,$ and the 28 obtained by permuting the coordinates of $(1,1,1,1,1,1,-1,-1)$. These 56 points are the vertices of a [3<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353) polytope in 7 dimensions.

Each vertex has 126 second nearest neighbors: for example, the nearest neighbors of the vertex $(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)$ are those whose coordinates sum to 0, namely the 56 obtained by permuting the coordinates of $(2, -2,0,0,0,0,0,0)\,$ and the 70 obtained by permuting the coordinates of $(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1)$. These 126 points are the vertices of a [2<sub>31</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070163) polytope in 7 dimensions.

Each vertex also has 56 third nearest neighbors, which are the negatives of its nearest neighbors, and one antipodal vertex, for a total of $1 + 56 + 126 + 56 + 1 = 240$ vertices.

Another construction is by taking signed combination of 14 codewords of [8-bit Extended Hamming code(8,4)](wikipedia://en/41226#Encoding) that give 14 × 2<sup>4</sup> = 224 vertices and adding trivial signed axis $( \pm 2,0,0,0, 0,0,0,0)$ for last 16 vertices. In this case, vertices are distance of $\sqrt{4}$ from origin rather than $\sqrt{8}$.

### Construction and faces {#Construction_and_faces}

The facet information of this polytope can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram):

Removing the node on the short branch leaves the [7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-simplex):

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-orthoplex) in its alternated form (**4<sub>11</sub>**):

Every 7-simplex facet touches only 7-orthoplex facets, while alternate facets of an orthoplex facet touch either a simplex or another orthoplex. There are 17,280 simplex facets and 2160 orthoplex facets.

Since every 7-simplex has 7 6-simplex facets, each incident to no other 6-simplex, the 4<sub>21</sub> polytope has 120,960 (7×17,280) 6-simplex faces that are facets of 7-simplexes. Since every 7-orthoplex has 128 (2<sup>7</sup>) 6-simplex facets, half of which are not incident to 7-simplexes, the 4<sub>21</sub> polytope has 138,240 (2<sup>6</sup>×2160) 6-simplex faces that are not facets of 7-simplexes. The 4<sub>21</sub> polytope thus has two kinds of 6-simplex faces, not interchanged by symmetries of this polytope. The total number of 6-simplex faces is 259200 (120,960+138,240).

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of a single-ring polytope is obtained by removing the ringed node and ringing its neighbor(s). This makes the **[3<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_3_21_polytope)** polytope.

### *k*<sub>21</sub> family {#k21_family}

The **4<sub>21</sub>** polytope is last in a family called the [*k*<sub>21</sub> polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_k_21_polytope). The first polytope in this family is the semiregular [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584) which is constructed from three squares (2-orthoplexes) and two triangles (2-simplexes).

### Geometric folding {#Geometric_folding}

*Image: E8 roots zome.jpg.* The **4<sub>21</sub>** polytope can be projected into 3-space as a physical vertex-edge model. Pictured here as 2 concentric [600-cells](wikipedia://en/501447) (at the golden ratio) using [Zome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zome) tools.[^o32] (Not all of the 3360 edges of length $\sqrt{2}$($\sqrt{5}$-1) are represented.)

The **4<sub>21</sub>** is related to the [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) by a geometric [folding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_%28Dynkin_diagram%29) of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). This can be seen in the E8/H4 [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) projections. The 240 vertices of the **4<sub>21</sub>** polytope are projected into 4-space as two copies of the 120 vertices of the 600-cell, one copy smaller (scaled by the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386)) than the other with the same orientation. Seen as a 2D orthographic projection in the E8/H4 Coxeter plane, the 120 vertices of the 600-cell are projected in the same four rings as seen in the 4<sub>21</sub>. The other 4 rings of the 4<sub>21</sub> graph also match a smaller copy of the four rings of the 600-cell.

### Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

In 4-dimensional complex geometry, the [regular complex polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_polytope) <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) exists with the same vertex arrangement as the 4<sub>21</sub> polytope. It is self-dual. Coxeter called it the [Witting polytope](wikipedia://en/28363938), after [Alexander Witting](wikipedia://en/28363783). Coxeter expresses its [Shephard group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shephard_group) symmetry by <sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>\[3\]<sub>3</sub>.[^o35]

The 4<sub>21</sub> is sixth in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope). Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the previous polytope. [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) identified this series in 1900 as containing all [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) facets, containing all [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).

### Construction {#Construction}

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram).

Removing the node on the end of the 4-length branch leaves the [3<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353):

### Construction {#Construction}

It is created by a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) upon a set of 8 [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) mirrors in 8-dimensional space. It is named for being a [birectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of the 4<sub>21</sub>. Vertices are positioned at the center of all the triangle faces of 4<sub>21</sub>.

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram).

Removing the node on the short branch leaves the [birectified 7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_7-simplex). There are 17280 of these facets.

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [birectified 7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birectified_7-orthoplex) in its alternated form. There are 2160 of these facets.

Removing the node on the end of the 4-length branch leaves the [rectified 3<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_3_21_polytope). There are 240 of these facets.

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is determined by removing the ringed node and adding rings to the neighboring nodes. This makes a [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232)-triangular duoprism.
