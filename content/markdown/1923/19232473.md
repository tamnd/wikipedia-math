::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="2 21 polytope"}

In 6-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **2<sub>21</sub>** polytope is a [uniform 6-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821728), constructed within the symmetry of the [E<sub>6</sub>](wikipedia://en/293725) group. It was discovered by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset), published in his 1900 paper. He called it a [6-ic semi-regular figure](wikipedia://en/11684875).[^o4] It is also called the [Schläfli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli) polytope.

:::

Its [Coxeter symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_symbol) is **2<sub>21</sub>**, describing its bifurcating [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), with a single ring on the end of one of the 2-node sequences. He also studied[^o5] its connection with the 27 lines on the [cubic surface](wikipedia://en/526126), which are naturally in correspondence with the vertices of 2<sub>21</sub>.

The **rectified 2<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the mid-edges of the **2<sub>21</sub>**. The **birectified 2<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the triangle face centers of the **2<sub>21</sub>**, and is the same as the rectified 1<sub>22</sub>.

These polytopes are a part of family of 39 convex [uniform polytopes in 6-dimensions](wikipedia://en/6821728), made of [uniform 5-polytope](wikipedia://en/14415338) facets and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), defined by all permutations of rings in this [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram): .

The **2<sub>21</sub>** has 27 vertices, and 99 facets: 27 [5-orthoplexes](wikipedia://en/7594615) and 72 [5-simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex). Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is a [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232).

For visualization this 6-dimensional polytope is often displayed in a special skewed orthographic projection direction that fits its 27 vertices within a 12-gonal regular polygon (called a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869)). Its 216 edges are drawn between 2 rings of 12 vertices, and 3 vertices projected into the center. Higher elements (faces, cells, etc.) can also be extracted and drawn on this projection.

The [Schläfli graph](wikipedia://en/28200770) is the [1-skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-skeleton) of this polytope.

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615) in its alternated form: (**2<sub>11</sub>**), . ### Geometric folding {#Geometric_folding}

The **2<sub>21</sub>** is related to the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) by a geometric [folding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_%28Dynkin_diagram%29) of the E6/F4 [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). This can be seen in the [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) projections. The 24 vertices of the 24-cell are projected in the same two rings as seen in the 2<sub>21</sub>.

### Related complex polyhedra {#Related_complex_polyhedra}

The [regular complex polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_complex_polytope) <sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>{3}<sub>3</sub>, , in $\mathbb{C}^2$ has a real representation as the *2<sub>21</sub>* polytope, , in 4-dimensional space. ### Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

The 2<sub>21</sub> is fourth in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope). Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the previous polytope. [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) identified this series in 1900 as containing all [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) facets, containing all [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).

The 2<sub>21</sub> polytope is fourth in dimensional series 2<sub>*k*1</sub>.

The 2<sub>21</sub> polytope is second in dimensional series 2<sub>2*k*</sub>.
