::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="3 21 polytope"}

In 7-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **3<sub>21</sub>** polytope is a [uniform 7-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821719), constructed within the symmetry of the [E<sub>7</sub>](wikipedia://en/649115) group. It was discovered by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset), published in his 1900 paper. He called it a [7-ic semi-regular figure](wikipedia://en/11684875).[^o4]

:::

Its [Coxeter symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_symbol) is **3<sub>21</sub>**, describing its bifurcating [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), with a single ring on the end of one of the 3-node sequences.

The **rectified 3<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the mid-edges of the **3<sub>21</sub>**. The **birectified 3<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the triangle face centers of the **3<sub>21</sub>**. The **trirectified 3<sub>21</sub>** is constructed by points at the tetrahedral centers of the **3<sub>21</sub>**, and is the same as the rectified 1<sub>32</sub>.

These polytopes are part of a family of 127 (2<sup>7</sup>−1) convex [uniform polytopes in 7 dimensions](wikipedia://en/6821719), made of [uniform 6-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821728) facets and [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719), defined by all permutations of rings in this [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram): .

In 7-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **3<sub>21</sub>** polytope is a [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392). It has 56 vertices, and 702 facets: 126 [3<sub>11</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexacross) and 576 [6-simplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex).

For visualization this 7-dimensional polytope is often displayed in a special skewed orthographic projection direction that fits its 56 vertices within an 18-gonal regular polygon (called a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869)). Its 756 edges are drawn between 3 rings of 18 vertices, and 2 vertices in the center. Specific higher elements (faces, cells, etc.) can also be extracted and drawn on this projection.

The 1-[skeleton](wikipedia://en/2977910) of the **3<sub>21</sub>** polytope is the [Gosset graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_graph).

This polytope, along with the [7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-simplex), can [tessellate](wikipedia://en/6008654) 7-dimensional space, represented by [**3<sub>31</sub>**](wikipedia://en/19593724) and Coxeter-Dynkin diagram: .

### Alternate names {#Alternate_names}

- It is also called the **Hess polytope** for [Edmund Hess](wikipedia://en/9191575) who first discovered it.
- It was enumerated by [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) in his 1900 paper. He called it a *7-ic semi-regular figure*.[^o4]
- [E. L. Elte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._L._Elte) named it V<sub>56</sub> (for its 56 vertices) in his 1912 listing of semiregular polytopes.[^n1]
- [H.S.M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) called it **3<sub>21</sub>** due to its bifurcating [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), having 3 branches of length 3, 2, and 1, and having a single ring on the final node of the 3 branch.
- Hecatonicosihexa-pentacosiheptacontahexa-exon (acronym: naq) - 126-576 facetted polyexon (Jonathan Bowers)[^n2]

### Construction {#Construction}

Its construction is based on the [E7](wikipedia://en/649115) group. [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) named it as **3<sub>21</sub>** by its bifurcating [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), with a single ring on the end of the 3-node sequence.

The facet information can be extracted from its [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), .

Removing the node on the short branch leaves the [6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex), .

Removing the node on the end of the 2-length branch leaves the [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518) in its alternated form: **3<sub>11</sub>**, .

Every simplex facet touches a 6-orthoplex facet, while alternate facets of the orthoplex touch either a simplex or another orthoplex.

The [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) is determined by removing the ringed node and ringing the neighboring node. This makes **[2<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_2_21_polytope)** polytope, .

Seen in a [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/56419724), the element counts can be derived by mirror removal and ratios of [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004#Properties) orders.[^o19]

### Related polytopes {#Related_polytopes}

The 3<sub>21</sub> is fifth in a dimensional series of [semiregular polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_k21_polytope). Each progressive [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392) is constructed [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of the previous polytope. [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) identified this series in 1900 as containing all [regular polytope](wikipedia://en/518693) facets, containing all [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) and [orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex).

It is in a dimensional series of uniform polytopes and honeycombs, expressed by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) as 3<sub>*k*1</sub> series. (A degenerate 4-dimensional case exists as 3-sphere tiling, a tetrahedral [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192).)
