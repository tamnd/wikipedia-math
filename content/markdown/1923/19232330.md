In 7-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), there are 127 [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821719) with E<sub>7</sub> symmetry. The three simplest forms are the [3<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19236353), [2<sub>31</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070163), and [1<sub>32</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070862) polytopes, composed of 56, 126, and 576 [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) respectively.

:::

They can be visualized as symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) in [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) of the E<sub>7</sub> Coxeter group, and other subgroups.

## Graphs {#Graphs}

Symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) of these 127 polytopes can be made in the E<sub>7</sub>, E<sub>6</sub>, D<sub>6</sub>, D<sub>5</sub>, D<sub>4</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, A<sub>6</sub>, A<sub>5</sub>, A<sub>4</sub>, A<sub>3</sub>, A<sub>2</sub> [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). A<sub>k</sub> has *k+1* symmetry, D<sub>k</sub> has *2(k-1)* symmetry, and E<sub>6</sub> and E<sub>7</sub> have *12*, *18* symmetry respectively.

For 10 of 127 polytopes (7 single rings, and 3 truncations), they are shown in these 8 symmetry planes, with vertices and edges drawn, and vertices colored by the number of overlapping vertices in each projective position in progressive order: red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, purple, magenta, red-violet.
