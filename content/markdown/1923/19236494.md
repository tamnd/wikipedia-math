# Edwin E. Moise

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edwin E. Moise"}

**Edwin Evariste Moïse** (;[^o3] December 22, 1918 – December 18, 1998)[^o3][^o4] was an American mathematician and mathematics education reformer. After his retirement from mathematics he became a [literary critic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_critic) of 19th-century [English poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_poetry) and had several notes published in that field.[^o3][^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Edwin E. Moïse was born December 22, 1918, in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans), [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana).[^o4][^o7] He graduated from [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) in 1940.[^o3] He worked as a [cryptanalyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalyst) and Japanese translator for the [Office of the Chief of Naval Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Chief_of_Naval_Operations) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o3][^o8]

He received his [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics from the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) in 1947.[^o3] His dissertation was titled "An indecomposable continuum which is homeomorphic to each of its nondegenerate subcontinua," a topic in [continuum theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_theory), and was written under the direction of [Robert Lee Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133). In his dissertation Moise coined the term [pseudo-arc](wikipedia://en/3264412).[^o8][^o9]

## Career {#Career}

Moïse taught at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) from 1947 to 1960. He was James B. Conant Professor of education and mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) from 1960 to 1971. He held a Distinguished Professorship at [Queens College, City University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_College,_City_University_of_New_York) from 1971 to 1987.[^o3][^o8]

Moïse started working on the topology of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257) while at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan). During 1949–1951 he held an appointment at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) during which he proved [Moise's theorem](wikipedia://en/25824647) that every 3-manifold can be [triangulated](wikipedia://en/1440976) in an essentially unique way.[^o8]

Moïse joined the [School Mathematics Study Group](wikipedia://en/1700591) when it started in 1958, as a member of the geometry writing team. The team produced several course outlines and sample pages for a 10th grade [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) course, and then Moïse and Floyd L. Downs wrote a geometry textbook, based on the team's approach, that was published in 1964. The textbook used metric postulates instead of [Euclid's postulates](wikipedia://en/9417#Axioms), a controversial approach supported by some mathematicians such as [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) but opposed by others such as Alexander Wittenberg and [Morris Kline](wikipedia://en/826363).[^o8]

Moïse was a president of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772), a vice-president of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and was on the executive committee of the [International Commission on Mathematical Instruction](wikipedia://en/8818410).[^o3][^o8]

Moïse retired from Queens College in 1987 and started a second career studying 19th century [English poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_poetry).[^o3] He had six short notes of [literary criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism) published.[^o5]

Moise died in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on December 18, 1998, aged 79.[^o3][^o4]
