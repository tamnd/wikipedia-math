# James A. Donaldson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James A. Donaldson"}

**James Ashley Donaldson** (April 17, 1941 — October 18, 2019) was an American mathematician. He was a professor at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University), where he was instrumental in establishing a PhD program in mathematics. He also helped found the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Donaldson was born in on April 17, 1941, on a farm near [Tallahassee, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee,_Florida), where he grew up attending [segregated schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregated_school).[^o4][^o5] In the wake of [Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education), he took the advice of his high school mathematics teacher to attend college in the [northern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_United_States), where he attended [Lincoln University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_University_%28Pennsylvania%29) in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania).[^o6] After graduating from Lincoln with a AB in mathematics in 1961, he moved to the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) for his graduate work, where he completed an MS degree in 1963, and a PhD in 1965.[^o4][^o7] His PhD thesis on [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations) was titled *Integral Representations of the Extended Airy Integral Type for the Modified Bessel Function*, and was supervised by Ray Gartner Langebartel.

Following his PhD, Donaldson had short term appointments at [Southern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_University), [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University), the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago), and the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico).[^o7] He returned to Howard University in 1971 as a professor, where he remained for most of the rest of his career.[^o9] He served as chair of the mathematics department at Howard from 1972 until 1990, in which role he was instrumental in developing a PhD program in mathematics. Howard's program, established in 1976, was the first PhD program in mathematics at a [historically black college or university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_college_or_university).[^o4][^o9][^o10] In the 1998—1999 school year, Donaldson took a leave of absence from Howard to serve as the acting president of Lincoln University, his undergraduate alma mater.[^o6][^o11] He returned to Howard in 1999, and from that year until 2012 he served as dean of the College of Arts and Sciences.[^o4][^o9] Donaldson retired and was granted emeritus status in 2013.[^o11]

## Organizational work {#Organizational_work}

In 1969, Donaldson was one of the founding members of the [National Association of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/58449356), the professional society promoting mathematical development of underrepresented minorities. He served as the first editor of the newsletter of that organization, and was a member of its board of directors from 1984 to 1994.[^o4][^o13]

## Death {#Death}

Donaldson died of heart disease on October 18, 2019, at his home in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o4][^o11]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2018, Donaldson received an honorary doctoral degree from his former undergraduate institution, Lincoln University.[^o4][^o6] In 2021, a Special Session in Donaldson's honor was held at the [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143).[^o16]
