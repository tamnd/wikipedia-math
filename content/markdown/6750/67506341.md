# Tetrad (geometry puzzle)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tetrad"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tetrad** is a set of four [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected) disjoint planar regions in the plane, each pair sharing a finite portion of common boundary. It was named by Michael R. W. Buckley in 1975 in the *[Journal of Recreational Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1118492)*. A further question was proposed that became a puzzle, whether the 4 regions could be congruent, with or without *holes*, other enclosed regions.[^o3]

:::

## Fewest sides and vertices {#Fewest_sides_and_vertices}

The solutions with four congruent tiles include some with five sides.[^o5] However, their placement surrounds an uncovered hole in the plane. Among solutions without holes, the ones with the fewest possible sides are given by a hexagon identified by Scott Kim as a student at Stanford University.[^o3] It is not known whether five-sided solutions without holes are possible.[^o5]

*Image: Hexagonal tetrad.svg.*

Kim's solution has 16 vertices, while some of the pentagon solutions have as few as 11 vertices. It is not known whether fewer vertices are possible.[^o5]

## Congruent polyform solutions {#Congruent_polyform_solutions}

Gardner offered a number of [polyform](wikipedia://en/691765) ([polyomino](wikipedia://en/72634), [polyiamond](wikipedia://en/185271), and [polyhex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhex_%28mathematics%29)) solutions, with no holes.[^o3]
