# Nick Trefethen

::: {#o7 .subject cue=lead name="Nick Trefethen"}

**Lloyd Nicholas Trefethen** (born 30 August 1955) is an American mathematician, professor of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) and until 2023 head of the Numerical Analysis Group at the [Mathematical Institute, University of Oxford](wikipedia://en/7905850).[^o9][^o11] He was elected a Member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2025.[^o12]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Trefethen was born 30 August 1955 in [Boston, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts),[^o14] the son of mechanical engineer [Lloyd M. Trefethen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_M._Trefethen) and codebreaker, poet, teacher and editor [Florence Newman Trefethen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Newman_Trefethen).[^o2] Trefethen attended [Phillips Exeter Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Exeter_Academy).

He obtained his bachelor's degree from [Harvard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_College) in 1977 and his master's from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1980. His PhD was on *Wave Propagation and Stability for Finite Difference Schemes* supervised by [Joseph E. Oliger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_E._Oliger) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o4][^o15]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Following his PhD, Trefethen went on to work at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) in New York, [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), before being appointed to a chair at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and a Fellowship of [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford).[^o18]

His publications span a wide range of areas within numerical analysis and applied mathematics, including non-normal [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) problems and applications, spectral methods for differential equations, [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660), [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), computational [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), and approximation theory.[^o19] He is perhaps best known for his work on [pseudospectra](wikipedia://en/3528170) of [non-normal matrices](wikipedia://en/170366) and operators. This work covers theoretical aspects as well as numerical algorithms, and applications including fluid mechanics, numerical solution of partial differential equations, numerical linear algebra, shuffling of cards, random matrices, differential equations and [lasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers). Trefethen is currently an [ISI highly cited researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISI_highly_cited_researcher).[^o20]

Trefethen has written a number of books on numerical analysis including *Numerical Linear Algebra*[^o21] with David Bau, *Spectral Methods in MATLAB*, *Schwarz–Christoffel Mapping* with Tobin Driscoll, and *Spectra and Pseudospectra: The Behavior of Nonnormal Matrices and Operators*[^o22] with [Mark Embree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Embree).[^o5] He is the leader of the [MATLAB](wikipedia://en/20412)-based [Chebfun](wikipedia://en/29095224) software project.

In 2013 he proposed a new formula to calculate the [BMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index) of a person:[^o23][^o24]

$$\text{BMI} = 1.3 \times \frac{\text{weight}\,\,\,\,}{\text{height}^{2.5}}$$

([International System of Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_System_of_Units))

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Trefethen was the first winner of the [Leslie Fox Prize for Numerical Analysis](wikipedia://en/15366214). In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o27] He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o28] and a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in the United States. Trefethen was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_2005),[^o6] and his certificate of election reads:

Nick Trefethen is distinguished for his many seminal contributions to Numerical Analysis and its applications in Applied Mathematics and in Engineering Science. His research spans theory, algorithms, software and physical applications, particularly involving eigenvalues, pseudospectra – a concept which he introduced – and dynamics. He has an international reputation for his work on nonnormal matrices and operators. He has also made major contributions to finite difference and spectral methods for partial differential equations, numerical linear algebra, and complex analysis. His monograph Numerical Linear Algebra (SIAM, 1997) is one of the SIAM's best selling books and has already been through five printings.[^o6]

In 2010 Trefethen was awarded the [Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_Gold_Medal) of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) in recognition of his "outstanding contributions to mathematics and its applications over a period of years".[^o29] In 2013 Trefethen was awarded the [Naylor Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naylor_Prize) and lectureship in Applied Mathematics from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o30] He was awarded the George Pólya Prize for Mathematical Exposition in 2017 and the John von Neumann Prize in 2020 by SIAM.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Trefethen has one son and one daughter from his first marriage to [Anne Elizabeth Trefethen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Trefethen) (née Daman).[^o2] He is currently married to Kate McLoughlin, a professor of English literature at Oxford.
