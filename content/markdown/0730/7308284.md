# Stopped process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stopped process"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **stopped process** is a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) that is forced to assume the same value after a prescribed (possibly random) time.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let

- $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325);
- $(\mathbb{X}, \mathcal{A})$ be a [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333);
- $X : [0, + \infty) \times \Omega \to \mathbb{X}$ be a stochastic process;
- $\tau : \Omega \to [0, + \infty]$ be a [stopping time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stopping_rule) with respect to some [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) $\{ \mathcal{F}_{t} | t \geq 0 \}$ of ${}\mathcal{F}$.

Then the **stopped process** $X^{\tau}$ is defined for $t \geq 0$ and $\omega \in \Omega$ by

$$X_{t}^{\tau} (\omega) := X_{\min \{ t, \tau (\omega) \}} (\omega).$$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Gambling {#Gambling}

Consider a [gambler](wikipedia://en/11921) playing [roulette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roulette). *X*<sub>*t*</sub> denotes the gambler's total holdings in the casino at time *t* ≥ 0, which may or may not be allowed to be negative, depending on whether or not the [casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino) offers credit. Let *Y*<sub>*t*</sub> denote what the gambler's holdings would be if he/she could obtain unlimited credit (so *Y* can attain negative values).

- Stopping at a deterministic time: suppose that the casino is prepared to lend the gambler unlimited credit, and that the gambler resolves to leave the game at a predetermined time *T*, regardless of the state of play. Then *X* is really the stopped process *Y*<sup>*T*</sup>, since the gambler's account remains in the same state after leaving the game as it was in at the moment that the gambler left the game.
- Stopping at a random time: suppose that the gambler has no other sources of revenue, and that the casino will not extend its customers credit. The gambler resolves to play until and unless he/she [goes broke](wikipedia://en/453078). Then the random time $$\tau (\omega) := \inf \{ t \geq 0 | Y_{t} (\omega) = 0 \}$$ is a stopping time for *Y*, and, since the gambler cannot continue to play after he/she has exhausted his/her resources, *X* is the stopped process *Y*<sup>*τ*</sup>.

### Brownian motion {#Brownian_motion}

Let $B : [0, + \infty) \times \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ be one-dimensional standard [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) starting at zero.

- Stopping at a deterministic time $T > 0$: if $\tau (\omega) \equiv T$, then the stopped Brownian motion $B^{\tau}$ will evolve as per usual up until time $T$, and thereafter will stay constant: i.e., $B_{t}^{\tau} (\omega) \equiv B_{T} (\omega)$ for all $t \geq T$.
- Stopping at a random time: define a random stopping time $\tau$ by the first [hitting time](wikipedia://en/7059985) for the region $\{ x \in \mathbb{R} | x \geq a \}$: $$\tau (\omega) := \inf \{ t > 0 | B_{t} (\omega) \geq a \}.$$ Then the stopped Brownian motion $B^{\tau}$ will evolve as per usual up until the random time $\tau$, and will thereafter be constant with value $a$: i.e., $B_{t}^{\tau} (\omega) \equiv a$ for all $t \geq \tau (\omega)$.
