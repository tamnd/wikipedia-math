# Hellmuth Kneser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hellmuth Kneser"}

**Hellmuth Kneser** (16 April 1898 – 23 August 1973) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made notable contributions to [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). His most famous result may be his theorem on the existence of a [prime decomposition for 3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition_%283-manifold%29). His proof originated the concept of [normal surface](wikipedia://en/3141418), a fundamental cornerstone of the theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

:::

He was born in [Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorpat), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (now [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia)) and died in [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tübingen), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). He was the son of the mathematician [Adolf Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368530) and the father of the mathematician [Martin Kneser](wikipedia://en/17620650). He assisted [Wilhelm Süss](wikipedia://en/1368716) in the founding of the [Mathematical Research Institute of Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) and served as the director of the institute from 1958 to 1959. He was an editor of [Mathematische Zeitschrift](wikipedia://en/7457514), [Archiv der Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archiv_der_Mathematik) and [Aequationes Mathematicae](wikipedia://en/54589646).

Kneser formulated the problem of non-integer iteration of functions and proved the existence of the entire [Abel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_function) of the [exponential](wikipedia://en/9678); on the base of this Abel function, he constructed the [functional square root](wikipedia://en/7207519) of the exponential function as a half-iteration of the exponential, i.e. a function $\varphi$ such that $\varphi(\varphi(z)) = \exp(z)$.[^o3]

Kneser was a student of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). He was an advisor of a number of notable mathematicians, including [Reinhold Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114).

Hellmuth Kneser was a member of the [NSDAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSDAP) and also the [SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung).[^o4] In July 1934 he wrote to [Ludwig Bieberbach](wikipedia://en/616186) a short note supporting his antisemitic views and stating: "May God grant German science a unitary, powerful and continued political position."[^o5]
