# Alfred Clebsch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Clebsch"}

**Rudolf Friedrich Alfred Clebsch** (19 January 1833 – 7 November 1872) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made important contributions to [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762). He attended the [University of Königsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Königsberg) and was habilitated at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). He subsequently taught in Berlin and [Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Karlsruhe). His collaboration with [Paul Gordan](wikipedia://en/1059850) in [Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen) led to the introduction of [Clebsch–Gordan coefficients](wikipedia://en/1074990) for [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056), which are now widely used in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).

:::

Together with [Carl Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551) at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), he founded the mathematical research journal *[Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381)* in 1868.

In 1883, [Saint-Venant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhémar_Jean_Claude_Barré_de_Saint-Venant) translated Clebsch's work on [elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_%28physics%29) into French and published it as *Théorie de l'élasticité des Corps Solides*.

His wife was Dorothe Charlote Mathilde Heinel (1838-1866), Alfred and Dorothe Clebsch had four sons: Ernst Friedrich Alfred Clebsch (1859-1945); Arthur Friedrich Alfred Clebsch (1860-1931); Eduard Friedrich Alfred Clebsch (1861-1895), who became a medical doctor in Ems; and Alfred Friedrich Clebsch (1864-).[^o4]
