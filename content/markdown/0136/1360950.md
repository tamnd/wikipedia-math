# Recall bias

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Recall bias"}

In [epidemiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiological) research, **recall bias** is a [systematic error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_error) caused by differences in the accuracy or completeness of the [recollections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recollection) retrieved ("recalled") by study participants regarding events or experiences from the past.[^o2] It is sometimes also referred to as **[response bias](wikipedia://en/1051310)**, **responder bias** or **[reporting bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporting_bias)**.

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

Recall bias is a type of [measurement bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_bias_%28epidemiology%29), and can be a [methodological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodological) issue in research involving [interviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview) or [questionnaires](wikipedia://en/956483). In this case, it could lead to misclassification of various types of [exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposure_assessment).[^o4] Recall bias is of particular concern in [retrospective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrospective) studies that use a [case-control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case-control) design to investigate the [etiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiology) of a disease or psychiatric condition.[^o5][^o6][^o7] For example, in studies of [risk factors](wikipedia://en/909227) for [breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer), women who have had the disease may search their memories more thoroughly than members of the unaffected [control group](wikipedia://en/1765232) for possible causes of their cancer. Those in the case group (those with breast cancer) may be able to recall a greater number of potential risk factors they had been exposed to than those in the control group (women unaffected by breast cancer). This can potentially exaggerate the relation between a potential risk factor and the disease.[^o8]

## Prevention {#Prevention}

To minimize recall bias, some clinical trials have adopted a "[wash out period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washout_period)", i.e., a substantial time period that must elapse between the subject's first observation and their subsequent observation of the same event.[^o10] Use of hospital records rather than patient experience can also help to avoid recall bias.[^o11] Standardising sampling methods can help to avoid needing recall information in the first place.[^o12]

Often, recall bias is difficult to avoid, and many studies change experiment design to avoid recalling information.[^o12]
