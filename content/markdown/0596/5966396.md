# Transpose graph

*Image: Amirali reverse.jpg.* A graph and its transpose

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transpose graph"}

In the mathematical and algorithmic study of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **converse**,[^o2] **transpose**[^o3] or **reverse**[^o4] of a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) $G$ is another directed graph on the same set of [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) with all of the edges reversed compared to the orientation of the corresponding edges in $G$. That is, if $G$ contains an edge $(u, v)$ then the converse/transpose/reverse of $G$ contains an edge $(v, u)$ and vice versa.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

The name converse arises because the reversal of arrows corresponds to taking the [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) of an implication in logic. The name transpose is because the [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) of the transpose directed graph is the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) of the adjacency matrix of the original directed graph.

There is no general agreement on preferred terminology.

The converse is denoted symbolically as $G'$, $G^{T}$, $G^{R}$, or other notations, depending on which terminology is used and which book or article is the source for the notation.

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Although there is little difference mathematically between a graph and its transpose, the difference may be larger in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), depending on [how a given graph is represented](wikipedia://en/557931). For instance, for the [web graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_graph), it is easy to determine the outgoing links of a vertex, but hard to determine the incoming links, while in the reversal of this graph the opposite is true. In [graph algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_algorithm), therefore, it may sometimes be useful to construct an explicit representation of the reversal of a graph, in order to put the graph into a form which is more suitable for the operations being performed on it. An example of this is [Kosaraju's algorithm](wikipedia://en/4072443) for [strongly connected components](wikipedia://en/684680), which applies [depth-first search](wikipedia://en/97034) twice, once to the given graph and a second time to its reversal.

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

A [skew-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/17283742) is a graph that is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to its own transpose graph, via a special kind of isomorphism that pairs up all of the vertices.

The [converse relation](wikipedia://en/3941020) of a [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) is the relation that reverses the ordering of each pair of related objects. If the relation is interpreted as a directed graph, this is the same thing as the transpose of the graph. In particular, the [dual order](wikipedia://en/600618) of a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) can be interpreted in this way as the transposition of a [transitively-closed](wikipedia://en/292239) [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002).
