# Amir Aczel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amir Aczel"}

**Amir Dan Aczel** (עמיר דן עכשאל; ;[^o3] November 6, 1950[^o4] – November 26, 2015) was an Israeli-born American lecturer in mathematics and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science), and an author of [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Amir D. Aczel was born in [Haifa, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa,_Israel). Amir graduated from the [Hebrew Reali School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Reali_School) in Haifa, in 1969.

When Aczel was 21, he studied at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). He graduated with a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1975 and received a Master of Science in 1976. Several years later, Aczel earned a PhD in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) from the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon). He married his wife Debra in 1984, and had one daughter and one stepdaughter.

Aczel taught mathematics at universities in California, [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece). He accepted a professorship at [Bentley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentley_College) in [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts), where he taught classes on statistics, the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science), and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). He authored two textbooks on statistics.

While teaching at Bentley, Aczel wrote several non-technical books on mathematics and science, as well as two textbooks. His book *Fermat's Last Theorem* was a United States bestseller and was nominated for a [Los Angeles Times Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize).

Aczel was a 2004 Fellow of the [John Simon Guggenheim Memorial Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Simon_Guggenheim_Memorial_Foundation), a visiting scholar in the History of Science at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (2007), and was awarded a Sloan Foundation grant to research his book *Finding Zero* (2015). In 2003, he became a research fellow at the [Boston University Center for Philosophy and History of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University_Center_for_Philosophy_and_History_of_Science), and in the fall of 2011, was teaching mathematics courses at [University of Massachusetts Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Boston). He was a speaker at [La Ciudad de las Ideas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ciudad_de_las_Ideas) in, [Puebla, Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puebla,_Mexico), in 2008[^o6] and 2011.

In 2015, Aczel died in [Nîmes, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nîmes,_France) from cancer.[^o4]
