# Central polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central polynomial"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **central polynomial** for *n*-by-*n* [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in non-commuting variables that is non-constant but yields a scalar matrix whenever it is evaluated at *n*-by-*n* matrices. That such polynomials exist for any [square matrices](wikipedia://en/166022) was discovered in 1970 independently by [Formanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_W._Formanek) and Razmyslov. The term "central" is because the evaluation of a central polynomial has the image lying in the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of the [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346). The notion has an application to the theory of [polynomial identity rings](wikipedia://en/25386269).

:::

Example: $(xy - yx)^2$ is a central polynomial for 2-by-2-matrices. Indeed, by the [Cayley–Hamilton theorem](wikipedia://en/173547), one has that $(xy - yx)^2 = -\det(xy - yx)I$ for any 2-by-2-matrices *x* and *y*.
