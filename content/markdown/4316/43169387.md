# RAC drawing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="RAC drawing"}

*Image: RAC\_drawings.svg.* RAC drawings of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>5</sub> and the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>3,4</sub>

In [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813), a **RAC drawing** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is a drawing in which the vertices are represented as points, the edges are represented as straight [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) or [polylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyline), at most two edges cross at any point, and when two edges cross they do so at [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956) to each other. In the name of this drawing style, "RAC" stands for "right angle crossing".

:::

The right-angle crossing style and the name "RAC drawing" for this style were both formulated by (Didimo Eades Liotta 2009),[^o3] motivated by previous user studies showing that crossings with large angles are much less harmful to the readability of drawings than shallow crossings.[^o4] Even for [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), allowing some right-angle crossings in a drawing of the graph can significantly improve measures of the drawing quality such as its [area](wikipedia://en/43176265) or [angular resolution](wikipedia://en/33245450).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>5</sub> has a RAC drawing with straight edges, but *K*<sub>6</sub> does not. Every 6-vertex RAC drawing has at most 14 edges, but *K*<sub>6</sub> has 15 edges, too many to have a RAC drawing.[^o3]

A [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) *K*<sub>*a*,*b*</sub> has a RAC drawing with straight edges if and only if either min(*a*,*b*) ≤ 2 or *a* + *b* ≤ 7. If min(*a*,*b*) ≤ 2, then the graph is a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314), and (by [Fáry's theorem](wikipedia://en/6805386)) every planar graph has a straight-line drawing with no crossings. Such a drawing is automatically a RAC drawing. The only two cases remaining are the graphs *K*<sub>3,3</sub> and *K*<sub>3,4</sub>. A drawing of *K*<sub>3,4</sub> is shown; *K*<sub>3,3</sub> can be formed from it by deleting one vertex. Neither of the next two larger graphs, *K*<sub>4,4</sub> and *K*<sub>3,5</sub>, has a RAC drawing.[^o8]

:::

## Edges and bends {#Edges_and_bends}

If an *n*-vertex graph (*n* ≥ 4) has a RAC drawing with straight edges, it can have at most 4*n* − 10 edges. This is tight: there exist RAC-drawable graphs with exactly 4*n* − 10 edges.[^o3] For drawings with polyline edges, the bound on the number of edges in the graph depends on the [number of bends](wikipedia://en/43176911) that are allowed per edge. The graphs that have RAC drawings with one or two bends per edge have O(*n*) edges; more specifically, with one bend there are at most 5.5*n* edges[^o10] and with two bends there are at most 74.2*n* edges.[^o11] Every graph has a RAC drawing with three bends per edge.[^o3]

## Relation to 1-planarity {#Relation_to_1-planarity}

A graph is [1-planar](wikipedia://en/38249632) if it has a drawing with at most one crossing per edge. Intuitively, this restriction makes it easier to cause this crossing to be at right angles, and the 4*n* − 10 bound on the number of edges of straight-line RAC drawings is close to the bounds of 4*n* − 8 on the number of edges in a [1-planar graph](wikipedia://en/38249632), and of 4*n* − 9 on the number of edges in a straight-line 1-planar graph. Every RAC drawing with 4*n* − 10 edges is 1-planar.[^o15][^o13] Additionally, every outer-1-planar graph (that is, a graph drawn with one crossing per edge with all vertices on the outer face of the drawing) has a RAC drawing.[^o14] However, there exist 1-planar graphs with 4*n* − 10 edges that do not have RAC drawings.[^o15]

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

It is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to determine whether a given graph has a RAC drawing with straight edges,[^o17] even if the input graph is 1-planar and the output RAC drawing must be 1-planar as well.[^o18] More specifically, RAC drawing is complete for the [existential theory of the reals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Existential_theory_of_the_reals).[^o19] The RAC drawing problem remains NP-hard for upward drawing of [directed acyclic graphs](wikipedia://en/204002).[^o20] However, in the special case of outer-1-planar graphs, a RAC drawing can be constructed in linear time.[^o21]
