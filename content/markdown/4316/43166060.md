# Fixed-point subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fixed-point subgroup"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **fixed-point subgroup** $G^f$ of an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) *f* of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* is the [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G*:[^o2]

:::

$$G^f = \{ g \in G \mid f(g) = g \}.$$

More generally, if *S* is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of automorphisms of *G* (i.e., a subset of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of *G*), then the set of the elements of *G* that are left fixed by every automorphism in *S* is a subgroup of *G*, denoted by *G*<sup>*S*</sup>.

For example, take *G* to be the group of [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) *n*-by-*n* [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) and $f(g)=(g^T)^{-1}$ (called the [Cartan involution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_involution)). Then $G^f$ is the group $O(n)$ of *n*-by-*n* [orthogonal matrices](wikipedia://en/105620).

To give an abstract example, let *S* be a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of a group *G*. Then each element *s* of *S* can be associated with the automorphism $g \mapsto sgs^{-1}$, i.e. [conjugation](wikipedia://en/49176) by *s*. Then

$$G^S = \{ g \in G \mid sgs^{-1} = g \text{ for all } s \in S\};$$

that is, the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of *S*.
