# Projectivization

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projectivization"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **projectivization** is a procedure which associates with a non-zero [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbf{P}(V)$, whose elements are one-dimensional [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of $V$. More generally, any subset $S$ of $V$ closed under scalar multiplication defines a subset of $\mathbf{P}(V)$ formed by the lines contained in $S$ and is called the projectivization of $S$.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Projectivization is a special case of the [factorization](wikipedia://en/237213) by a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29): the projective space $\mathbf{P}(V)$ is the quotient of the open set $V \backslash \{0\}$ of nonzero vectors by the action of the multiplicative group of the base field by scalar transformations. The [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_%28mathematics%29) of $\mathbf{P}(V)$ in the sense of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) is one less than the dimension of the vector space $V$.
- Projectivization is [functorial](wikipedia://en/10987) with respect to [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) linear maps: if

$$f: V\to W$$

is a linear map with trivial [kernel](wikipedia://en/1072915) then $f$ defines an algebraic map of the corresponding projective spaces,

$$\mathbf{P}(f): \mathbf{P}(V)\to \mathbf{P}(W).$$

In particular, the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) GL(*V*) acts on the projective space $\mathbf{P}(V)$ by [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160).

:::

## Projective completion {#Projective_completion}

A related procedure embeds a vector space $V$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ into the projective space $\mathbf{P}(V \oplus K)$ of the same dimension. To every vector $v$ of $V$, it associates the line spanned by the vector $(v, 1)$ of $V \oplus K$.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), there is a procedure that associates a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) $\operatorname{Proj} S$ with a [graded commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) $S$ (under some technical restrictions on $S$). If $S$ is the [algebra of polynomials on a vector space](wikipedia://en/38608533) $V$ then $\operatorname{Proj} S$ is $\mathbf{P}(V)$. This [Proj construction](wikipedia://en/2285000) gives rise to a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the category of graded commutative rings and surjective graded maps to the category of projective [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754).

:::
