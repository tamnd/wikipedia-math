# Simple extension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simple extension"}

In [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), a **simple extension** is a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) that is generated by the [adjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28field_theory%29) of a single element, called a *primitive element*. Simple extensions are well understood and can be completely classified.

:::

The [primitive element theorem](wikipedia://en/448933) states that every [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [separable](wikipedia://en/363340) extension is a simple extension. For a field of [characteristic 0](wikipedia://en/1066621) such as the rationals, this means all finite extensions are simple.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A field extension $L/K$ is called a **simple extension** if there exists an element $\theta$ in *L* with

$$L = K(\theta).$$

This means that every element of $L$ can be expressed as a [rational fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_fraction) in $\theta$, with coefficients in $K$; that is, it is produced from $\theta$ and elements of $K$ by the field operations +, −, •, / . Equivalently, $L$ is the smallest field that contains both *$K$* and $\theta$.

There are two different kinds of simple extensions (see below):

1. The element $\theta$ may be [transcendental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_element) over $K$, which means that it is not a [root](wikipedia://en/264210) of any [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) with coefficients in $K$. In this case $K(\theta)$ is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_isomorphism) to the [field of rational functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_rational_functions) $K(X).$
1. Otherwise, $\theta$ is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/186291) over $K$; that is, $\theta$ is a root of a polynomial over $K$. The [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) $p(X)$ of minimal [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) $n$, with $\theta$ as a root, is called the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of $\theta$. Its degree equals the [degree of the field extension](wikipedia://en/4382697), that is, the [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) of $L$ viewed as a $K$-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). In this case, every element of $K(\theta)$ can be uniquely expressed as a polynomial in $\theta$ of degree less than $n$, and $K(\theta)$ is isomorphic to the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) $K[X]/(p(X)).$

In both cases, the element $\theta$ is called a **generating element** or **primitive element** for the extension; one says also $L$ is **generated over** $K$ by $\theta$.

For example, every [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) is a simple extension of the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) of the same [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621). More precisely, if $p$ is a prime number and $q=p^n,$ the field $L=\mathbb{F}_{q}$ of $q$ elements is a simple extension of degree *n* of $K=\mathbb{F}_{p}.$ In fact, *L* is generated as a field by any element $\theta$ that is a root of an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) *n* in $K[X]$.

However, in the case of finite fields, the term *primitive element* is usually reserved for a stronger notion, an element *γ* that [generates](wikipedia://en/99945) $L^\times = L-\{0\}$ as a [multiplicative group](wikipedia://en/1110742), so that every nonzero element of *L* is a power of *γ*, i.e. is produced from *γ* using only the group operation • *.* To distinguish these meanings, one uses the term "generator" or **field primitive element** for the weaker meaning, reserving "primitive element" or **group primitive element** for the stronger meaning.[^o4] (See Finite field#Multiplicative structure and [Primitive element (finite field)](wikipedia://en/19127149)).

:::

## Structure of simple extensions {#Structure_of_simple_extensions}

Let *L* be a simple extension of *K* generated by *θ*. For the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) *K*\[*X*\], one of its main properties is the unique [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411)

$$\begin{aligned} \varphi: K[X] &\rightarrow L\\ f(X) &\mapsto f(\theta)\,. \end{aligned}$$

Two cases may occur:

1. If $\varphi$ is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective), it may be extended injectively to the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) *K*(*X*) of *K*\[*X*\]. Since *L* is generated by *θ*, this implies that $\varphi$ is an isomorphism from *K*(*X*) onto *L*. This implies that every element of *L* is equal to an [irreducible fraction](wikipedia://en/14822) of polynomials in *θ*, and that two such irreducible fractions are equal if and only if one may pass from one to the other by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same non zero element of *K*.
1. If $\varphi$ is not injective, let *p*(*X*) be a generator of its [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240#Ring_homomorphisms), which is thus the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) of *θ*. The [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of $\varphi$ is a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of *L*, and thus an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462). This implies that *p* is an irreducible polynomial, and thus that the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) $K[X]/\langle p(X) \rangle$ is a field. As *L* is generated by *θ*, $\varphi$ is [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective), and $\varphi$ induces an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) from $K[X]/\langle p(X) \rangle$ onto *L*. This implies that every element of *L* is equal to a unique polynomial in *θ* of degree lower than the degree $n = \operatorname{deg}p(X)$. That is, we have a *K-*basis of *L* given by $1,\theta,\theta^2,\ldots,\theta^{n-1}$.

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- **C** / **R** generated by $\theta = i =\sqrt{-1}$.
- **Q**($\sqrt{2}$) / **Q** generated by $\theta=\sqrt{2}$.
- Any [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) (i.e., a finite extension of **Q**) is a simple extension **Q**(*θ*) for some *θ*. For example, $\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{7})$ is generated by $\theta=\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{7}$.
- *F*(*X*) / *F,* a field of rational functions, is generated by the formal variable *X*.

:::
