# Charles E. Hall

*Image: Charles E. Hall.png.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles E. Hall"}

**Charles Edward Hall** (May 22, 1868 – September 29, 1952) was a government official in the United States and an expert statistician.

:::

He was born in [Batavia, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Illinois) the son of Reverend Abraham Thompson Hall, the first licensed African to preach in Chicago, and Joanna Huss Hall, a community activist. He had 11 siblings. He attended public schools and then business college in [Spokane, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane,_Ohio) and at [Wilberforce University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilberforce_University) in Ohio.[^o2]

Hall was the first non white to be appointed to be a senate committee clerk in the [Illinois Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Senate).[^o3] He worked as the managing editor of the *[Illinois Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Record)* from 1897 to 1898, and in 1900 was appointed as a clerk in the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070).[^o3] He worked in the [United States Department of Commerce and Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Commerce_and_Labor) for the census bureau for a period and was known as an expert statistician.[^o3] In 1935 he was promoted to a new position of *Specialist in Negro Statistics* that was created for him.[^o4][^o5] By 1938 he had worked for 38 years in the bureau and was described as an essential part of [Franklin D. Roosevelts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal).[^o6]

Hall authored *The Negro In the United States* in 1930,[^o9] and *Negroes in the United States, 1920-1932* published in 1969.[^o7] He also wrote a 165 page pamphlet in 1905 called "Pottery and the Clay Products of the United States".[^o3]

He died September 29, 1952 at the home of Dr. [Lloyd A. Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_A._Hall), his nephew, in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago).[^o8][^o9]
