# Wolfgang Gaschütz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Gaschütz"}

*Image: Gaschuetz.jpg.* Wolfgang Gaschütz 1973

**Wolfgang Gaschütz** (11 June 1920 – 7 November 2016)[^o2] was a German mathematician, known for his research in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), especially the theory of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gaschütz was born on 11 June 1920 in [Karlshof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlshof), [Oderbruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oderbruch). He moved with his family in 1931 to Berlin, where he completed his *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)* in 1938. He served as an artillery officer in WW II, which ended for him in 1945 near Kiel. There in autumn 1945 he matriculated at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel).[^o4] He was inspired by [Andreas Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser)'s book *Die Theorie der Gruppen von endlicher Ordnung*.[^o6][^o7] Gaschütz received his Ph.D. ([*Promotion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate#Germany)) in 1949 under the supervision of [Karl-Heinrich Weise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Heinrich_Weise) with doctoral dissertation entitled *(Zur $\Phi$-Untergruppe endlicher Gruppen)*. In 1953 Gaschütz completed his habilitation in Kiel.[^o4] At the University of Kiel he held the junior academic appointments *Wissenschaftliche Hilfskraft* from 1949 to 1956 and *Diätendozent* from 1956 to 1959. He was [*Außerplanmäßiger Professor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_ranks_in_Germany) from 1959 to 1962, professor extraordinarius from 1962 to 1964, and professor ordinarius (full professor) from 1964 to 1988. He taught at Kiel until his retirement as professor emeritus in 1988.[^o9] He rejected calls to Karlsruhe and Mainz. He was a visiting professor at various universities in Europe ([Queen Mary College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_University_of_London) 1965 and 1970, [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) 1966, [University of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Florence) 1971, [University of Naples Federico II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Naples_Federico_II) 1974, [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick) 1967, 1973 and 1977); in the USA ([Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University) 1963, [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) 1968); and in Australia ([Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) in Canberra).[^o9]

Gaschütz created a school of group theorists in Kiel, where there had been a gap in mathematical expertise in algebra since the death of [Ernst Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685) in 1928. Gaschütz, influenced by [Helmut Wielandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Wielandt) in the 1950s, is best known for his research on [Frattini subgroups](wikipedia://en/540707), on questions of complementability, on [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635), and on the theory of finite [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012).[^o4] In 1959 he gave a formula for the Eulerian function[^o10] introduced in 1936 by [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033)[^o11] and determined the number of generators of a finite solvable group in terms of structure and embedding of the chief factors of the Eulerian function.[^o4]

In 1962, Gaschütz published his theory of [formations](wikipedia://en/33449138),[^o12] giving a unified theory of [Hall subgroups](wikipedia://en/1735335) and [Carter subgroups](wikipedia://en/5456815). Gaschütz's theory is important for understanding finite solvable groups. He characterized solvable [T-groups](wikipedia://en/10545261).[^o13] He is one of the pioneers of the theory of Fitting classes begun by [Bernd Fischer](wikipedia://en/29628162) in 1966[^o4] and the theory of Schunk classes.[^o14]

Gaschütz organized the [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_Oberwolfach) conferences on group theory for many years with [Bertram Huppert](wikipedia://en/17639456) and [Karl W. Gruenberg](wikipedia://en/57806841). In 2000 Gaschütz received an honorary doctorate from [Francisk Skorina Gomel State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisk_Skorina_Gomel_State_University) in [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus).[^o4] His doctoral students include Joachim Neubüser.

Gaschütz and his wife Gudrun were married in 1943 and became the parents of a son and two daughters.[^o4]

Gaschütz died in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) on 7 November 2016, at the age of 96.
