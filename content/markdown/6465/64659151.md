# Independence complex

*Image: Independence complex.svg.* The **independence complex** of *G*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Independence complex"}

The **independence complex** of a [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_%28graph_theory%29) is a mathematical object describing the [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) of the graph. Formally, the independence complex of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) *G*, denoted by I(*G*), is an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) (that is, a family of finite sets closed under the operation of taking subsets), formed by the sets of vertices in the [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) of *G*. Any subset of an independent set is itself an independent set, so I(*G*) is indeed closed under taking subsets.

:::

Every independent set in a graph is a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) in its [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649), and vice versa. Therefore, the independence complex of a graph equals the [clique complex](wikipedia://en/20751674) of its complement graph, and vice versa.

## Homology groups {#Homology_groups}

Several authors studied the relations between the properties of a graph *G* = (*V*, *E*), and the [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group) of its independence complex I(*G*).[^o3] In particular, several properties related to the [dominating sets](wikipedia://en/1747972) in *G* guarantee that some [reduced homology](wikipedia://en/3110036) groups of I(*G*) are trivial.

\1. The *total* *domination number* of G, denoted $\gamma_0(G)$, is the minimum cardinality of a [total dominating set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_dominating_set) of *G -* a set *S* such that every vertex of V is adjacent to a vertex of *S*. If $\gamma_0(G)>k$ then $\tilde{H}_{k-1}(I(G))=0$.[^o4]

\2. The *total domination number* of a subset *A* of *V* in G, denoted $\gamma_0(G,A)$, is the minimum cardinality of a set *S* such that every vertex of *A* is adjacent to a vertex of *S*. The *independence domination number* of G, denoted $i \gamma(G)$, is the maximum, over all independent sets *A* in *G*, of $\gamma_0(G,A)$. If $i \gamma(G) > k$, then $\tilde{H}_{k-1}(I(G))=0$.[^o3][^o5]

\3. The *domination number* of *G*, denoted $\gamma(G)$, is the minimum cardinality of a [dominating set](wikipedia://en/1747972) of G - a set *S* such that every vertex of V \\ S is adjacent to a vertex of *S*. Note that $\gamma_0(G)\geq \gamma(G)$. If *G* is a [chordal graph](wikipedia://en/744165) and $\gamma(G)>k$ then $\tilde{H}_{k-1}(I(G))=0$.[^o6]

\4. The *induced matching number* of *G*, denoted $\mu(G)$, is the largest cardinality of an [induced matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_matching) in *G* - a matching that includes every edge connecting any two vertices in the subset. If there exists a subset *A* of *V* such that $\gamma_0(G,A)>k+\min[k, \mu(G[A])]$ then $\tilde{H}_{k-1}(I(G))=0$.[^o7] This is a generalization of both properties 1 and 2 above.

\5. The *non-dominating independence complex* of G, denoted I'(*G*), is the abstract simplicial complex of the independent sets that are not [dominating sets](wikipedia://en/1747972) of *G*. Obviously I'(*G*) is contained in I(*G*); denote the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) by $i: I'(G)\to I(G)$. If *G* is a [chordal graph](wikipedia://en/744165) then the induced map $i_*: \tilde{H}_k(I'(G))\to \tilde{H}_k(I(G))$ is zero for all $k\geq -1$.[^o3] This is a generalization of property 3 above.

\6. The *fractional star-domination number* of G, denoted $\gamma^*_s(G)$, is the minimum size of a fractional star-dominating set in *G*. If $\gamma^*_s(G)>k$ then $\tilde{H}_{k-1}(I(G))=0$.[^o3]

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

**[Meshulam's game](wikipedia://en/65171548)** is a game played on a graph *G*, that can be used to calculate a lower bound on the [homological connectivity](wikipedia://en/64688677) of the independence complex of *G*.

The **matching complex** of a graph *G*, denoted M(*G*), is an abstract simplicial complex of the [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797) in *G*. It is the independence complex of the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of *G*.[^o9][^o10]

The **(*m*,*n*)-chessboard complex** is the matching complex on the complete [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) *K<sub>m</sub>,<sub>n</sub>*. It is the abstract simplicial complex of all sets of positions on an *m*-by-*n* [chessboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessboard), on which it is possible to put [rooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rook_%28chess%29) without each of them threatening the other.[^o11][^o12]

The **[clique complex](wikipedia://en/20751674)** of G is the independence complex of the [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) of *G*.
