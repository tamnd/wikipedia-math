# Dominique de Caen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Dominique de Caen"}

**Dominique de Caen** (1956-5-11 – 2002) was a mathematician, Doctor of Mathematics, and professor of mathematics, who specialized in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773). He is renowned for his research on [Turán's](wikipedia://en/614998) extremal problem for [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Career {#Career}

He studied mathematics at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), where he earned a Bachelor of Science degree in 1977.[^o3]

In 1979, he obtained a Master of Science degree from [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) with a thesis on [Prime Boolean](wikipedia://en/4109196) matrices.[^o3]

In 1982, he earned the Doctorate of Mathematics degree from [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) with a thesis entitled *On Turán's Hypergraph Problem* which was supervised by [Eric Mendelsohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Mendelsohn_%28mathematician%29).[^o3]

Most of his academic papers have been published in the journals *[Discrete Mathematics](wikipedia://en/21988115)*, *Designs, Codes and Cryptography*, the *[Journal of Combinatorial Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Combinatorial_Theory)*, and the *[European Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Combinatorics)*, among others.[^o4][^o7]
