# Daniel A. Griffith

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel A. Griffith"}

**Daniel Alva Griffith** (born November 15, 1948) is an American geographer, spatial statistician, and geographic information scientist (GIScientist). He is known for his contributions to quantitative geography and spatial statistics, particularly for developing the Moran eigenvector spatial filtering (MESF) technique and introducing the sui-random variables model, both of which address spatial autocorrelation in regression modeling.[^o3][^o4] His work has applications in geography, economics, epidemiology, environmental sciences, and other disciplines.

:::

Griffith is Ashbel Smith Professor Emeritus of Geospatial Information Sciences at the [University of Texas at Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Dallas).[^o5] Since 2021, bibliometric rankings such as Research.com and ScholarGPS have placed him among the top 0.05% of global scholars in geography, with one ranking listing him as the top spatial analyst worldwide.[^o6][^o7]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Griffith was born in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, and raised in Westmoreland County. He earned a B.S. in mathematics education and an M.A. in geography from [Indiana University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_of_Pennsylvania).[^o9] He completed a Ph.D. in geography at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), followed by an M.S. in statistics at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University).[^o5]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Griffith has held faculty positions at [Toronto Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Metropolitan_University), the [University at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo), [Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_University), the [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami), and the [University of Texas at Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Dallas), where he was Ashbel Smith Professor and later became professor emeritus.[^o5] He also held courtesy appointments at [SUNY College of Environmental Science and Forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY_College_of_Environmental_Science_and_Forestry), the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), and the [University of Central Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Florida).

He has been a visiting scholar at institutions including the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), the [Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Demographic_Research), and [Wuhan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan_University).[^o11]

From 2008 to 2014 he served as editor of *[Geographical Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Analysis_%28journal%29)*.[^o12][^o13]

## Research and contributions {#Research_and_contributions}

Griffith’s research focuses on methods to address [spatial autocorrelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_autocorrelation), a statistical phenomenon in which nearby values are correlated more than expected by chance. His development of the Moran eigenvector spatial filtering (MESF) approach has been widely cited and applied …[^o15] He also introduced the sui-random variables model as a framework for handling self-correlated data.[^o16]

His methods have been implemented in [R](wikipedia://en/376707), MATLAB, SAS, and ArcGIS. Dedicated tools include ESFtool, a .NET-based regression tool,[^o17] and SAAR, an ArcGIS-integrated software with R.[^o18]

In applied work, Griffith’s spatial analyses of pediatric blood-lead levels in Syracuse, New York, using Onondaga County Health Department data, demonstrated neighborhood-level clustering of elevated lead exposure and contributed to public health research on lead-poisoning prevention and remediation.[^o19]
