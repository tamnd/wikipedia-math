# J. W. Hopkins

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="J. W. Hopkins"}

**John William Hopkins** (1908–1989, published as **J. W. Hopkins**) was a Canadian bioscientist and statistician who worked for many years for the [National Research Council Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_Canada).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hopkins initially studied the biochemistry of [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal), especially focusing on [rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_leaf_rust)-resistant [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat). He obtained a master's degree in 1931 from the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), with the thesis *Gluten quality and the effect of dilution of wheat flours with starch*.

His interest in statistics was attracted by a 1925 book by [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), *Statistical Methods for Research Workers*. He worked for two years in England as a statistician at [Rothamsted Research](wikipedia://en/165688) under Fisher, while also holding a position with the [National Research Council Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Council_Canada) as a research assistant and junior research biologist for grain research. In 1933, Fisher moved from Rothamsted to [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) to head a new Department of Eugenics, and in 1934 Hopkins completed a Ph.D. there.

Subsequently, in Canada at the National Research Council, he established a laboratory for [biometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometrics). During World War II, he worked on [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) for the [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force). When the [International Biometric Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biometric_Society) was founded in 1947, he became its first treasurer. He retired in 1973.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Hopkins was made a [Member of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (MBE) for his wartime service.

He was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association), in the 1948 class of fellows. He was also a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada) (FRSC), a [Fellow of the Royal Statistical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Statistical_Society) (FRSS), and an Elected Member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute). In 1981, the [Statistical Society of Canada](wikipedia://en/15572327) named him as an honorary member.

He was one of the inaugural recipients of the Centenary Medal of the Royal Society of Canada, in 1982.
