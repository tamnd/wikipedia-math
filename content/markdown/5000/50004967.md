# Maryna Viazovska

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maryna Viazovska"}

Sergiivna

**Maryna Sergiivna Viazovska** (Марина Сергіївна Вязовська,[^o4] ; born 2 December 1984[^o5]) is a Ukrainian mathematician known for her work in [sphere packing](wikipedia://en/368621). She is a full professor and Chair of [Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_Theory) at the Institute of Mathematics of the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) in Switzerland.[^o13] She was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 2022.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Viazovska was born in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv), the oldest of three sisters. Her father was a chemist who worked at the [Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov) aircraft factory, and her mother was an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).[^o7] She attended a specialized secondary school for high-achieving students in science and technology, [Kyiv Natural Science Lyceum No. 145](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Natural_Science_Lyceum_No._145). An influential teacher there, Andrii Knyazyuk, had previously worked as a professional research mathematician before becoming a secondary school teacher. Viazovska competed in domestic mathematics Olympiads when she was at high school, placing 13th in a national competition where 12 students were selected to a training camp before a six-member team for the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) was chosen.[^o7] As a student at [Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv), she competed at the [International Mathematics Competition for University Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematics_Competition_for_University_Students) in 2002, 2003, 2004, and 2005, and was one of the first-place winners in 2002 and 2005.[^o10] She co-authored her first research paper in 2005.[^o7]

Viazovska earned a master's from the [University of Kaiserslautern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kaiserslautern) in 2007, [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) from the [Institute of Mathematics of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematics_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine) in 2010,[^o4] and a doctorate ([Dr. rer. nat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._rer._nat.)) from the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in 2013. Her doctoral dissertation, *Modular Functions and Special Cycles*, concerns [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and was supervised by [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557) and [Werner Müller](wikipedia://en/24858805).[^o11]

She was a postdoctoral researcher at the [Berlin Mathematical School](wikipedia://en/14614258) and the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin)[^o19] and a Minerva Distinguished Visitor[^o12] at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). Since January 2018 she has held the Chair of Number Theory as a [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor) at the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (EPFL) in Switzerland after a short stint as [tenure-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_tenure) assistant professor.[^o13]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 2016, Viazovska solved the [sphere-packing problem](wikipedia://en/368621) in [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) 8.[^o15][^o16][^o17] Her dimension 8 solution quickly led to collaboration with others, and a solution in dimension 24.[^o18][^o19] Previously, the problem had been solved only for three or fewer dimensions, and the proof of the three-dimensional version (the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311)) involved long computer calculations. In contrast, Viazovska's proof for 8 and 24 dimensions has been described as "stunningly simple".[^o19]

A few years later, in 2018, Viazovska and her collaborators significantly extended[^o20] their sphere packing results in dimensions 8 and 24 to address potential energy minimization.[^o21] In 2019, Viazovska and her team solved a mathematical equation that determines how an infinite number of points repelling each other are placed in 8- and 24-dimensional spaces.[^o22][^o23][^o24]

As well as for her work on sphere packing, Viazovska is also known for her research on [spherical designs](wikipedia://en/9730285) with Bondarenko and Radchenko. With them she proved a conjecture of [Korevaar](wikipedia://en/39389708) and Meyers on the existence of small designs in arbitrary dimensions. This result was one of the contributions for which her co-author Andriy Bondarenko won the Vasil A. Popov Prize for [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) in 2013.[^o25]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2016, Viazovska received the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize)[^o27] and, in 2017, the [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) and the [SASTRA Ramanujan Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SASTRA_Ramanujan_Prize) for her work on sphere packing and [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000).[^o28][^o29] In December 2017, she was awarded a 2018 [New Horizons Prize in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Horizons_Prize_in_Mathematics).[^o30] She was an invited speaker at the 2018 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842).[^o31] For 2019 she was awarded the [Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/37554140)[^o32] and the [Fermat Prize](wikipedia://en/10919195).[^o33] She is one of the 2020 winners of the [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize).[^o34] In 2020, she also received the National Latsis Prize awarded by the [Latsis Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latsis_Foundation).[^o35] She was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2021.[^o36] She was appointed Senior Scholar at the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) in July 2022.[^o37]

She was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in July 2022, making her the second woman (after [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655)), the second person born in the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) (after [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747)) and the first with a degree from a Ukrainian university to ever receive it.[^o38][^o39][^o40] She was honored as one of the [BBC 100 Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_Women_%28BBC%29) in December 2022.[^o41]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Viazovska met her husband, Daniil Evtushinsky, at an after-school physics group for schoolchildren. He is also a researcher at EPFL, in physics. They have two children, a son and a daughter.[^o7][^o43]
