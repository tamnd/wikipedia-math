# Sato–Tate conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sato–Tate conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Sato–Tate conjecture"}

**Sato–Tate conjecture.**

:::

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Sato–Tate conjecture** is a [statistical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical) statement about the family of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) *E<sub>p</sub>* obtained from an elliptic curve *E* over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) by [reduction modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_modulo_a_prime) almost all [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*. [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336) and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) independently posed the conjecture around 1960.

:::

If *N<sub>p</sub>* denotes the number of points on the elliptic curve *E<sub>p</sub>* defined over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with *p* elements, the conjecture gives an answer to the distribution of the second-order term for *N<sub>p</sub>*. By [Hasse's theorem on elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/1108758),

$$N_p/p = 1 + \mathrm{O}(1/\!\sqrt{p})\$$

as $p\to\infty$, and the point of the conjecture is to predict how the [O-term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-O_notation) varies.

The original conjecture and its generalization to all [totally real fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) was proved by [Laurent Clozel](wikipedia://en/51408473), [Michael Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452), [Nicholas Shepherd-Barron](wikipedia://en/7681535), and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931) under mild assumptions in 2008, and completed by [Thomas Barnet-Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Barnet-Lamb), [David Geraghty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Geraghty_%28mathematician%29), Harris, and Taylor in 2011. Several generalizations to other algebraic varieties and fields are open.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *E* be an elliptic curve defined over the rational numbers without [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183). For a prime number *p*, define *θ*<sub>*p*</sub> as the solution to the equation

$$p+1-N_p=2\sqrt{p}\cos\theta_p ~~ (0\leq \theta_p \leq \pi).$$

Then, for every two real numbers $\alpha$ and $\beta$ for which $0\leq \alpha < \beta \leq \pi,$

$$\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{p\leq N:\alpha\leq \theta_p \leq \beta\}} {\#\{p\leq N\}}=\frac{2}{\pi} \int_\alpha^\beta \sin^2 \theta \, d\theta = \frac{1}{\pi}\left(\beta-\alpha+\sin(\alpha)\cos(\alpha)-\sin(\beta)\cos(\beta)\right)$$

:::

## Details {#Details}

By [Hasse's theorem on elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/1108758), the ratio

$$\frac{(p + 1)-N_p}{2\sqrt{p}}=\frac{a_p}{2\sqrt{p}}$$

is between -1 and 1. Thus it can be expressed as cos *θ* for an angle *θ*; in geometric terms there are two [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) accounting for the remainder and with the denominator as given they are [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) and of [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) 1. The *Sato–Tate conjecture*, when *E* doesn't have complex multiplication,[^o6] states that the [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) of *θ* is proportional to

$\sin^2 \theta \, d\theta.$[^o7]

This is due to [Mikio Sato](wikipedia://en/373336) and [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933) (independently, and around 1960, published somewhat later).[^o8]

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

In 2008, Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron, and Taylor published a proof of the Sato–Tate conjecture for elliptic curves over [totally real fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field) satisfying a certain condition: of having multiplicative reduction at some prime,[^o11] in a series of three joint papers.[^o12][^o13][^o14]

Further results are conditional on improved forms of the [Arthur–Selberg trace formula](wikipedia://en/20419621). Harris has a [conditional proof](wikipedia://en/6562) of a result for the product of two elliptic curves (not [isogenous](wikipedia://en/1085176)) following from such a hypothetical trace formula.[^o15] In 2011, Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, and Taylor proved a generalized version of the Sato–Tate conjecture for an arbitrary non-CM holomorphic modular form of weight greater than or equal to two,[^o16] by improving the potential modularity results of previous papers.[^o17] The prior issues involved with the trace formula were solved by [Michael Harris](wikipedia://en/31954452),[^o18] and [Sug Woo Shin](wikipedia://en/63281413).[^o19][^o20]

In 2015, Richard Taylor was awarded the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) "for numerous breakthrough results in (...) the Sato–Tate conjecture."[^o21]

:::

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

There are generalisations, involving the distribution of [Frobenius elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element) in [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673) involved in the [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) on [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231). In particular there is a conjectural theory for curves of genus *n* > 1.

Under the random matrix model developed by [Nick Katz](wikipedia://en/1451427) and [Peter Sarnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Sarnak),[^o23] there is a conjectural correspondence between (unitarized) characteristic polynomials of Frobenius elements and [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) in the [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) USp(2*n*) = [Sp(*n*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp%28n%29). The [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) on USp(2*n*) then gives the conjectured distribution, and the classical case is USp(2) = [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29).

::: {#o25 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Lang–Trotter conjecture"}

## Refinements {#Refinements}

There are also more refined statements. The **Lang–Trotter conjecture** (1976) of [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991) and [Hale Trotter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hale_Trotter) states the asymptotic number of primes *p* with a given value of *a*<sub>*p*</sub>,[^o26] the trace of Frobenius that appears in the formula. For the typical case (no [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183), trace ≠ 0) their formula states that the number of *p* up to *X* is asymptotically

$$c \sqrt{X}/ \log X\$$

with a specified constant *c*. [Neal Koblitz](wikipedia://en/1587628) (1988) provided detailed conjectures for the case of a prime number *q* of points on *E*<sub>*p*</sub>, motivated by [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography).[^o27] In 1999, [Chantal David](wikipedia://en/55372363) and [Francesco Pappalardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Pappalardi) proved an averaged version of the Lang–Trotter conjecture.[^o28][^o29]

:::
