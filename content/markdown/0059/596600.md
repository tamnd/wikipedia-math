# Relatively compact subspace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Relatively compact subspace"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **relatively compact subspace** (or **relatively compact subset**, or **precompact subset**) $Y$ of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is a subset whose [closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_closure) is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every subset of a compact topological space is relatively compact (since a closed subset of a compact space is compact). In an arbitrary topological space every subset of a relatively compact set is relatively compact.

Every compact subset of a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) is relatively compact. In a non-Hausdorff space, such as the [particular point topology](wikipedia://en/5336200) on an infinite set, the closure of a compact subset is *not* necessarily compact; said differently, a compact subset of a non-Hausdorff space is not necessarily relatively compact.

Every compact subset of a (possibly non-Hausdorff) [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) is [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) and relatively compact.

In the case of a [metric topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_topology), or more generally when [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) may be used to test for compactness, the criterion for relative compactness becomes that any sequence in $Y$ has a subsequence convergent in $X$.

Some major theorems characterize relatively compact subsets, in particular in [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630). An example is the [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622). Other cases of interest relate to [uniform integrability](wikipedia://en/9610107), and the concept of [normal family](wikipedia://en/1480325) in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). [Mahler's compactness theorem](wikipedia://en/636427) in the [geometry of numbers](wikipedia://en/250153) characterizes relatively compact subsets in certain non-compact [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325) (specifically spaces of [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246)).

:::

::: {#o5 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="Counterexample"}

## Counterexample {#Counterexample}

As a counterexample take any finite [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) of the particular point of an infinite [particular point space](wikipedia://en/5336200). The neighbourhood itself is compact but is not relatively compact because its closure is the whole non-compact space.

:::

## Almost periodic functions {#Almost_periodic_functions}

The definition of an [almost periodic function](wikipedia://en/405512) $F$ at a conceptual level has to do with the translates of $F$ being a relatively compact set. This needs to be made precise in terms of the topology used, in a particular theory.
