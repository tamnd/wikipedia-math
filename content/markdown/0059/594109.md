# Wolfram Research

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfram Research"}

**Wolfram Research, Inc.** ( ) is an American [multinational company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_corporation) that creates computational technology. Wolfram's flagship product is the [technical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_computing) program [Wolfram Mathematica](wikipedia://en/49024), first released on June 23, 1988. Other products include [WolframAlpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WolframAlpha), [Wolfram System Modeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_SystemModeler), Wolfram Workbench,[^o4] [gridMathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GridMathematica), Wolfram Finance Platform,[^o5] [webMathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebMathematica), the Wolfram Cloud, and the Wolfram Programming Lab.[^o6] Wolfram Research founder [Stephen Wolfram](wikipedia://en/52469) is the [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEO). The company is headquartered in [Champaign, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champaign,_Illinois), United States.

:::

## History {#History}

The company launched [Wolfram Alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Alpha), an [answer engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Answer_engine) on May 16, 2009. It brings a new approach to knowledge generation and acquisition that involves large amounts of [curated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_curation) computable data in addition to semantic indexing of text.[^o8]

Wolfram Research acquired MathCore Engineering AB on March 30, 2011.[^o9][^o10]

On July 21, 2011, Wolfram Research launched the [Computable Document Format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computable_Document_Format) (CDF). CDF is an electronic document format[^o11] designed to allow easy authoring[^o12] of dynamically generated interactive content.

In June 2014, Wolfram Research officially introduced the [Wolfram Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Language) as a new general multi-paradigm programming language.[^o13] It is the primary programming language used in [Mathematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematica).[^o14]

On April 15, 2020, Wolfram Research received \$5,575,000 to help pay its employees during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) as part of the [U.S. government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States)'s [Paycheck Protection Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paycheck_Protection_Program) administered by the [Small Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Business_Administration). The loan was forgiven.[^o15][^o16]

## Products and resources {#Products_and_resources}

### Mathematica {#Mathematica}

Mathematica began as a software program for doing mathematics by computer, and has evolved to cover all domains of [technical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_computing) software, with features for [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_neural_network), [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [image processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904), and [visualizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_visualization). Central to Mathematica's mission is its ability to perform symbolic computation, for example, the ability to solve indefinite integrals symbolically. Mathematica includes a [notebook interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notebook_interface) and can produce slides for presentations. Mathematica is available in a desktop version, a grid computing version, and a cloud version.

### Wolfram Alpha {#Wolfram_Alpha}

Wolfram Alpha is a free online service that [answers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_answering) factual queries directly by computing the answer from externally sourced curated data, rather than providing a list of documents or web pages that might contain the answer as a search engine might. Users submit queries and computation requests via a text field and Wolfram Alpha then computes answers and relevant visualizations. In 2012, a paid "Pro" level with additional features was released.[^o20]

In 2016, Wolfram Alpha Enterprise, a business-focused analytics tool, was launched. The program combines data supplied by a corporation with the algorithms from Wolfram Alpha to answer questions related to that corporation.[^o21]

### Wolfram System Modeler {#Wolfram_System_Modeler}

Wolfram System Modeler is a platform for engineering as well as life-science modeling and simulation based on the [Modelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modelica) language. It provides an interactive graphical modeling and simulation environment and a customizable set of component libraries. The primary interface, [ModelCenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ModelCenter), is an interactive graphical environment including a customizable set of component libraries. The software also provides a tight integration with Mathematica. Users can develop, simulate, document, and analyze their models within Mathematica notebooks.

## Publishing {#Publishing}

Wolfram Research publishes several free websites including the *[MathWorld](wikipedia://en/374235)* and *ScienceWorld* encyclopedias. *ScienceWorld*, which launched in 2002, is divided into sites on chemistry, physics, astronomy and scientific biography.[^o24] In 2005, the physics site was deemed a "valuable resource" by *[American Scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Scientist)* magazine.[^o25] However, by 2009, the astronomy site was said to suffer from outdated information, incomplete articles and [link rot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_rot).[^o26]

[The Wolfram Demonstrations Project](wikipedia://en/13989702) is a collaborative site hosting interactive technical demonstrations powered by a free Mathematica Player runtime.

Wolfram Research publishes *The Mathematica Journal*.[^o27] Wolfram has also published several books via Wolfram Media, Wolfram's publishing arm.[^o28][^o29] In addition, they have experimented with electronic textbook creation.[^o30]

## Media activities {#Media_activities}

Wolfram Research served as the mathematical consultant for the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) television series *[Numb3rs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numb3rs)*, a show about the mathematical aspects of crime-solving.[^o32]
