# Product cipher

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Product cipher"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), a **product cipher** combines two or more transformations in a manner intending that the resulting cipher is more secure than the individual components to make it resistant to [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715).[^o2] The product cipher combines a sequence of simple transformations such as [substitution](wikipedia://en/48356) (S-box), [permutation](wikipedia://en/48358) (P-box), and [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087). The concept of product ciphers is due to [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693), who presented the idea in his foundational paper, *[Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems](wikipedia://en/452085)*. A particular product cipher design where all the constituting transformation functions have the same structure is called an **iterative cipher** with the term "[rounds](wikipedia://en/73297602)" applied to the functions themselves.[^n1]

:::

For transformation involving reasonable number of n message symbols, both of the foregoing cipher systems (the [S-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_box) and [P-box](wikipedia://en/19958773)) are by themselves wanting. Shannon suggested using a combination of S-box and P-box transformation—a product cipher. The combination could yield a cipher system more powerful than either one alone. This approach of alternatively applying substitution and permutation transformation has been used by IBM in the [Lucifer](wikipedia://en/364242) cipher system, and has become the standard for national data encryption standards such as the [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) and the [Advanced Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/1260). A product cipher that uses only substitutions and permutations is called an [SP-network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SP-network). [Feistel ciphers](wikipedia://en/352709) are an important class of product ciphers.
