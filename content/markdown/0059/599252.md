# Fallacy of the single cause

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fallacy of the single cause"}

The **fallacy of the single cause**, also known as **complex cause**, **causal oversimplification**,[^o2] **causal reductionism**, **root cause fallacy**, and **reduction fallacy**,[^o3] is an [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450) of [questionable cause](wikipedia://en/235588) that occurs when it is assumed that there is a single, simple cause of an outcome when in reality it may have been caused by a number of only jointly [sufficient causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufficient_causes).

:::

Fallacy of the single cause can be logically reduced to: "X caused Y; therefore, X was the only cause of Y" (although A,B,C...etc. also contributed to Y.)[^o3]

Causal oversimplification is a specific kind of [false dilemma](wikipedia://en/17687) where conjoint possibilities are ignored. In other words, the possible causes are assumed to be "A xor B xor C" when "A and B and C" or "A and B and not C" (etc.) are not taken into consideration; i.e. the "or" is not [exclusive](wikipedia://en/105979#Exclusive_or_in_natural_language).
