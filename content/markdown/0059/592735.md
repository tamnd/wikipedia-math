# Hypocycloid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hypocycloid"}

*Image: astroid2.gif.* The red path is a hypocycloid traced as the smaller black circle rolls around inside the larger black circle (parameters are R=4.0, r=1.0, and so k=4, giving an [astroid](wikipedia://en/769163)).

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **hypocycloid** is a special [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) generated by the trace of a fixed point on a small [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) that rolls within a larger circle. As the radius of the larger circle is increased, the hypocycloid becomes more like the [cycloid](wikipedia://en/41638) created by rolling a circle on a line.

:::

## History {#History}

The 2-cusped hypocycloid called [Tusi couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi_couple) was first described by the 13th-century [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_astronomy) and [mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics) [Nasir al-Din al-Tusi](wikipedia://en/33642424) in *Tahrir al-Majisti (Commentary on the Almagest)*.[^o3][^o4] German painter and German Renaissance theorist [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Dürer) described [epitrochoids](wikipedia://en/1547292) in 1525, and later Roemer and Bernoulli concentrated on some specific hypocycloids, like the astroid, in 1674 and 1691, respectively.[^o10]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If the rolling circle has radius $r$, and the fixed circle has radius $R = kr$, then the [parametric equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parametric_equations) for the curve can be given by either:

$$\begin{aligned} & x (\theta) = (R - r) \cos \theta + r \cos \left(\frac{R-r}{r} \theta \right) \\ & y (\theta) = (R - r) \sin \theta - r \sin \left( \frac{R - r}{r} \theta \right) \end{aligned}$$

or:

$$\begin{aligned} & x (\theta) = r (k - 1) \cos \theta + r \cos \left( (k - 1) \theta \right) \\ & y (\theta) = r (k - 1) \sin \theta - r \sin \left( (k - 1) \theta \right) \end{aligned}$$

If $k$ is an integer, then the curve is closed, and has $k$ [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758) (i.e., sharp corners, where the curve is not [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206)). Specially for $k = 2$ the curve is a straight line and the circles are called [Tusi couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi_couple). Nasir al-Din al-Tusi was the first to describe these hypocycloids and their applications to high-speed [printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_press).[^o7][^o8]

If $k$ is a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024), say $k = p/q$ expressed as [irreducible fraction](wikipedia://en/14822), then the curve has $p$ cusps.

To close the curve and complete the 1st repeating pattern:

- $\theta$=0 to q rotations
- $\alpha$=0 to p rotations
- total rotations of rolling circle=p-q rotations

If $k$ is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689), then the curve never closes, and fills the space between the larger circle and a circle of radius $R - 2r$.

Each hypocycloid (for any value of $r$) is a [brachistochrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachistochrone) for the gravitational potential inside a homogeneous sphere of radius $R$.[^o9]

The area enclosed by a hypocycloid is given by: [^o10] [^o11]

$$A = \frac {(k - 1)(k - 2)} {k^2} \pi R^2 = (k - 1)(k - 2) \pi r^2$$

The [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of a hypocycloid is given by:[^o11]

$$s = \frac {8(k - 1)} {k} R = 8(k - 1) r$$

## Examples {#Examples}

The hypocycloid is a special kind of [hypotrochoid](wikipedia://en/1547158), which is a particular kind of [roulette](wikipedia://en/846500).

A hypocycloid with three cusps is known as a [deltoid](wikipedia://en/769176).

A hypocycloid curve with four cusps is known as an [astroid](wikipedia://en/769163).

The hypocycloid with two "cusps" is a degenerate but still very interesting case, known as the [Tusi couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi_couple).

:::

## Relationship to group theory {#Relationship_to_group_theory}

*Image: Rolling Hypocycloids.gif.* Hypocycloids "rolling" inside one another. The cusps of each of the smaller curves maintain continuous contact with the next-larger hypocycloid.

Any hypocycloid with an integral value of *k*, and thus *k* cusps, can move snugly inside another hypocycloid with *k*+1 cusps, such that the points of the smaller hypocycloid will always be in contact with the larger. This motion looks like 'rolling', though it is not technically rolling in the sense of classical mechanics, since it involves slipping.

Hypocycloid shapes can be related to [special unitary groups](wikipedia://en/173997), denoted SU(*k*), which consist of *k* × *k* unitary matrices with determinant 1. For example, the allowed values of the sum of diagonal entries for a matrix in SU(3), are precisely the points in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) lying inside a hypocycloid of three cusps (a deltoid). Likewise, summing the diagonal entries of SU(4) matrices gives points inside an astroid, and so on.

Thanks to this result, one can use the fact that SU(*k*) fits inside SU(*k+1*) as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) to prove that an [epicycloid](wikipedia://en/713354) with *k* cusps moves snugly inside one with *k*+1 cusps.[^o15][^o16]

## Derived curves {#Derived_curves}

The [evolute](wikipedia://en/842387) of a hypocycloid is an enlarged version of the hypocycloid itself, while the [involute](wikipedia://en/840704) of a hypocycloid is a reduced copy of itself.[^o18]

The [pedal](wikipedia://en/845864) of a hypocycloid with pole at the center of the hypocycloid is a [rose curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_curve).

The [isoptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isoptic) of a hypocycloid is a hypocycloid.

## Hypocycloids in popular culture {#Hypocycloids_in_popular_culture}

*Image: Steelmark logo.svg.* The Steelmark logo, featuring three hypocycloids

Curves similar to hypocycloids can be drawn with the [Spirograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirograph) toy. Specifically, the Spirograph can draw [hypotrochoids](wikipedia://en/1547158) and [epitrochoids](wikipedia://en/1547292).

The [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers)' logo, which is based on the [Steelmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steelmark), includes three [astroids](wikipedia://en/769163) (hypocycloids of four [cusps](wikipedia://en/6851758)). In his weekly [NFL.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League) column "Tuesday Morning Quarterback," [Gregg Easterbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Easterbrook) often refers to the Steelers as the Hypocycloids. Chilean soccer team [CD Huachipato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Huachipato) based their crest on the Steelers' logo, and as such features hypocycloids.

The first Drew Carey season of *[The Price Is Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Price_is_Right_%28U.S._game_show%29)**s set features astroids on the three main doors, giant price tag, and the turntable area. The astroids on the doors and turntable were removed when the show switched to [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television) broadcasts starting in 2008, and only the giant price tag prop still features them today.[^o20]***
