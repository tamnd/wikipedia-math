# Chowla–Mordell theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chowla–Mordell theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Chowla–Mordell theorem** says that for a Dirichlet character modulo an odd prime, if the argument of its Gaussian sum is a root of unity, then the character must be quadratic. It is a result in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) determining cases where a [Gauss sum](wikipedia://en/6378204) is the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), multiplied by a [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). It was proved and published independently by [Sarvadaman Chowla](wikipedia://en/292142) and [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell), around 1951.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

If $p$ is a prime number, $\chi$ a nontrivial [Dirichlet character](wikipedia://en/174515) [modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_operation) $p$, and

$$G(\chi)=\sum \chi(a) \zeta^a$$

where $\zeta$ is a primitive $p$-th root of unity in the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), then

$$\frac{G(\chi)}{|G(\chi)|}$$

is a root of unity [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $\chi$ is the [quadratic residue symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_residue_symbol) modulo $p$. The 'if' part was known to [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125): the contribution of Chowla and Mordell was the 'only if' direction. The ratio in the theorem occurs in the [functional equation of L-functions](wikipedia://en/595708).[^o4][^o5][^o6]

:::
