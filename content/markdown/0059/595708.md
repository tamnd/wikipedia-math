# Functional equation (L-function)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Functional equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [*L*-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) are expected to have several characteristic properties, one of which is that they satisfy certain **[functional equations](wikipedia://en/364377)**. There is an elaborate theory of what these equations should be, much of which is still conjectural.

:::

## Introduction {#Introduction}

A prototypical example, the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) has a functional equation relating its value at the [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) *s* with its value at 1 − *s*. In every case this relates to some value ζ(*s*) that is only defined by [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) from the [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) definition. That is, writing – as is conventional – σ for the real part of *s*, the functional equation relates the cases

σ > 1 and σ \< 0,

and also changes a case with

0 \< σ \< 1

in the *critical strip* to another such case, reflected in the line σ = ½. Therefore, use of the functional equation is basic, in order to study the zeta-function in the whole [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628).

The functional equation in question for the Riemann zeta function takes the simple form

$$Z(s) = Z(1-s) \,$$

where *Z*(*s*) is ζ(*s*) multiplied by a *gamma-factor*, involving the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316). This is now read as an 'extra' factor in the [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540) for the zeta-function, corresponding to the [infinite prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_prime). Just the same shape of functional equation holds for the [Dedekind zeta function](wikipedia://en/1131243) of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *K*, with an appropriate gamma-factor that depends only on the embeddings of *K* (in algebraic terms, on the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/372240) of *K* with the [real field](wikipedia://en/20646438)).

There is a similar equation for the [Dirichlet L-functions](wikipedia://en/400092), but this time relating them in pairs:[^o3]

$$\Lambda(s,\chi)=\varepsilon\Lambda(1-s,\chi^*)$$

with χ a [primitive Dirichlet character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_Dirichlet_character), χ<sup>\*</sup> its complex conjugate, Λ the L-function multiplied by a gamma-factor, and ε a complex number of [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) 1, of shape

$$G(\chi) \over {\left |G(\chi)\right \vert}$$

where *G*(χ) is a [Gauss sum](wikipedia://en/6378204) formed from χ. This equation has the same function on both sides if and only if χ is a *real character*, taking values in {0,1,−1}. Then ε must be 1 or −1, and the case of the value −1 would imply a zero of *Λ*(*s*) at *s* = ½. According to the theory (of Gauss, in effect) of Gauss sums, the value is always 1, so no such *simple* zero can exist (the function is *even* about the point).

## Theory of functional equations {#Theory_of_functional_equations}

A unified theory of such functional equations was given by [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990), and the theory was taken up again in [Tate's thesis](wikipedia://en/3149490) by [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933). Hecke found generalised characters of number fields, now called [Hecke characters](wikipedia://en/3149443), for which his proof (based on [theta functions](wikipedia://en/449745)) also worked. These characters and their associated L-functions are now understood to be strictly related to [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183), as the Dirichlet characters are to [cyclotomic fields](wikipedia://en/30872462).

There are also functional equations for the [local zeta-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_zeta-function), arising at a fundamental level for the (analogue of) [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732) in [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231). The Euler products of the [Hasse–Weil zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Weil_zeta-function) for an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* over a number field *K*, formed by reducing *modulo* [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) to get local zeta-functions, are conjectured to have a *global* functional equation; but this is currently considered out of reach except in special cases. The definition can be read directly out of étale cohomology theory, again; but in general some assumption coming from [automorphic representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automorphic_representation) theory seems required to get the functional equation. The [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) was a particular case of this as general theory. By relating the gamma-factor aspect to [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), and detailed studies of the expected ε factor, the theory as empirical has been brought to quite a refined state, even if proofs are missing.
