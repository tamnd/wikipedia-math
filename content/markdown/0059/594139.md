# Lucas chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lucas chain"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Lucas chain** is a restricted type of [addition chain](wikipedia://en/578656), named for the French mathematician [Édouard Lucas](wikipedia://en/150102). It is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838)

:::

$$a_0, a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots$$

that satisfies $a_{0}=1$, and, for each $k > 0$,

$$a_k = a_i + a_j,$$

and either

$$a_i = a_j \text{ or } \vert a_i - a_j \vert = a_m$$

for some $i, j, m < k$.[^o2][^o3]

The sequence of powers of 2 (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, ...) and the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918) (with a slight adjustment of the starting point 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ...) are simple examples of Lucas chains.

Lucas chains were introduced by [Peter Montgomery](wikipedia://en/14573146) in 1983.[^o4] They are analogous to [addition chains](wikipedia://en/578656), in that just as an addition chain $A$ that ends with $n$ allows for evaluating $a^{n}$ in $\operatorname{len}(A)$ steps, a Lucas chain $C$ that ends with $n$ allow for evaluating the [Lucas sequence](wikipedia://en/734635) terms $\{U, V\,\}_{n}\,(P, Q)$ in $\operatorname{len}(C)$ steps.[^o5]

Just like the addition chain, a basic method for computing a Lucas chain that ends with $n$ is via the *binary method*, similar to [exponentiation by squaring](wikipedia://en/10237). This gives a chain length of $O(\log n)$. Shorter chains can be obtained using a range of other methods such as Montgomery's PRAC. Because the process of finding a better chain is expensive, good chains are usually precomputed.[^o6] DJ Bernstein mentions a number of Lucas chain-based schemes in 2017.[^o7]

If $L(n)$ is the length of the shortest Lucas chain for $n$, then Kutz has shown that most $n$ do not have $L < (1-\varepsilon) \log_{\varphi}(n)$, where φ is the [Golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^o2]
