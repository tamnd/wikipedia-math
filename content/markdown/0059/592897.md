# Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem** states that an everywhere-defined [symmetric operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_operator) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) with [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) $\langle \cdot | \cdot \rangle$ is [bounded](wikipedia://en/455961). By definition, an operator *A* is *symmetric* if

:::

$$\langle A x | y \rangle = \langle x | A y\rangle$$

for all *x*, *y* in the domain of *A*. Note that symmetric *everywhere-defined* operators are necessarily [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408), so this theorem can also be stated as follows: an everywhere-defined self-adjoint operator is bounded. The theorem is named after [Ernst David Hellinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_David_Hellinger) and [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682).

This theorem can be viewed as an immediate corollary of the [closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/497834), as self-adjoint operators are [closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_operator). Alternatively, it can be argued using the [uniform boundedness principle](wikipedia://en/247392). One relies on the symmetric assumption, therefore the inner product structure, in proving the theorem. Also crucial is the fact that the given operator *A* is defined everywhere (and, in turn, the completeness of Hilbert spaces).

The Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem reveals certain technical difficulties in the [mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/20728). [Observables](wikipedia://en/294248) in quantum mechanics correspond to self-adjoint operators on some Hilbert space, but some observables (like energy) are unbounded. By Hellinger–Toeplitz, such operators cannot be everywhere defined (but they may be defined on a [dense subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_subset)). Take for instance the [quantum harmonic oscillator](wikipedia://en/50719). Here the Hilbert space is [L](wikipedia://en/45194)<sup>2</sup>(**R**), the space of square integrable functions on **R**, and the energy operator *H* is defined by (assuming the units are chosen such that ℏ = *m* = ω = 1)

$$[Hf](x) = - \frac12 \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}x^2} f(x) + \frac12 x^2 f(x).$$

This operator is self-adjoint and unbounded (its [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) are 1/2, 3/2, 5/2, ...), so it cannot be defined on the whole of L<sup>2</sup>(**R**). In other words, it will map some functions in L<sup>2</sup>(**R**) to functions that are no longer square integrable. One such function could be

$$\psi(x) = \frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{1 + x^2}}.$$
