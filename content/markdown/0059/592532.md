# FEAL

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="FEAL"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **FEAL** (the **Fast data Encipherment Algorithm**) is a [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) proposed as an alternative to the [Data Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/7978) (DES), and designed to be much faster in software. The [Feistel](wikipedia://en/352709) based algorithm was first published in 1987 by [Akihiro Shimizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihiro_Shimizu) and [Shoji Miyaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoji_Miyaguchi) from [NTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Telegraph_and_Telephone). The cipher is susceptible to various forms of [cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/5715), and has acted as a catalyst in the discovery of [differential](wikipedia://en/8536) and [linear cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/59469).

:::

There have been several different revisions of FEAL, though all are [Feistel ciphers](wikipedia://en/352709), and make use of the same basic round function and operate on a [64-bit block](wikipedia://en/294065). One of the earliest designs is now termed **FEAL-4**, which has four rounds and a [64-bit key](wikipedia://en/53039).

Problems were found with FEAL-4 from the start: Bert den Boer related a weakness in an unpublished rump session at the same conference where the cipher was first presented. A later paper (den Boer, 1988) describes an attack requiring 100–10000 [chosen plaintexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_plaintext), and Sean Murphy (1990) found an improvement that needs only 20 chosen plaintexts. Murphy and den Boer's methods contain elements similar to those used in [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536).

The designers countered by doubling the number of rounds, **FEAL-8** (Shimizu and Miyaguchi, 1988). However, eight rounds also proved to be insufficient — in 1989, at the Securicom conference, [Eli Biham](wikipedia://en/348920) and [Adi Shamir](wikipedia://en/57314) described a differential attack on the cipher, mentioned in (Miyaguchi, 1989). Gilbert and Chassé (1990) subsequently published a statistical attack similar to differential cryptanalysis which requires 10000 pairs of chosen plaintexts.

In response, the designers introduced a variable-round cipher, **FEAL-N** (Miyaguchi, 1990), where "N" was chosen by the user, together with **FEAL-NX**, which had a larger 128-bit key. Biham and Shamir's differential cryptanalysis (1991) showed that both FEAL-N and FEAL-NX could be broken faster than exhaustive search for N ≤ 31. Later attacks, precursors to linear cryptanalysis, could break versions under the [known plaintext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Known_plaintext) assumption, first (Tardy-Corfdir and Gilbert, 1991) and then (Matsui and Yamagishi, 1992), the latter breaking FEAL-4 with 5 known plaintexts, FEAL-6 with 100, and FEAL-8 with 2<sup>15</sup>.

In 1994, Ohta and Aoki presented a linear cryptanalytic attack against FEAL-8 that required 2<sup>12</sup> known plaintexts.[^o3]
