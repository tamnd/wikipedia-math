# John Allen Paulos

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Allen Paulos"}

**John Allen Paulos** (born July 4, 1945) is an American professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University) in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania), a writer and speaker on mathematics and the importance of [mathematical literacy](wikipedia://en/397245). He writes about the dangers of mathematical [innumeracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innumeracy), ie the layperson's misconceptions about numbers, probability, and logic.[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Paulos was born in [Denver, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Colorado) and grew up in Chicago, [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois) and [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin), where he attended high school. After his Bachelor of Mathematics at [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin) (1967) and his Master of Science at [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) (1968), he received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) (1974). In an interview he described himself as lifelong skeptic.[^o6] He volunteered for the [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps) in the seventies.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

Mathematics is no more computation than literature is typing.

The most amazing coincidence of all would be the complete absence of all coincidences.

Uncertainty is the only certainty there is, and knowing how to live with insecurity is the only security.

His academic work is mainly in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

His book *[Innumeracy: Mathematical Illiteracy and its Consequences](wikipedia://en/4760663)* (1988) was a bestseller and *A Mathematician Reads the Newspaper* (1995) extended the critique. In his books Paulos discusses [innumeracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innumeracy) with quirky anecdotes, scenarios and facts, encouraging readers in the end to look at their world in a more [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_property) way.

He has also written on other subjects often "combining disparate disciplines", such as the mathematical and philosophical basis of humor in *Mathematics and Humor* and *I Think, Therefore I Laugh*, the [stock market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market) in *A Mathematician Plays the Stock Market*, quantitative aspects of narrative in *Once Upon a Number*, the arguments for God in *Irreligion*, and most recently "bringing mathematics to bear on...biography" in *A Numerate Life*.[^o9]

Paulos also wrote a mathematics-tinged column for the UK newspaper *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* and is a [Committee for Skeptical Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_Skeptical_Inquiry) fellow.[^o10]

Paulos has appeared frequently on radio and television, including a four-part [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) adaptation of *A Mathematician Reads the Newspaper* and appearances on the [Lehrer News Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehrer_News_Hour), [20/20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20/20_%28U.S._TV_series%29), [Larry King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_King), and [David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Letterman).[^o11]

In 2001 Paulos taught a course on quantitative literacy for journalists at the Columbia University School of Journalism. The course stimulated further programs at Columbia and elsewhere in precision and data-driven journalism.

His long-running "[ABCNews.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABCNews.com)" monthly column *Who's Counting*[^o12] deals with mathematical aspects of stories in the news. All the columns over a 10- year period are archived here.[^o12]

## Private life {#Private_life}

He is married, father of two, grandfather of four.

## Awards {#Awards}

- 2013: JPBM ([Joint Policy Board for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/4675178)) Award for communicating mathematics on a sustained basis to large audiences[^o15]
- 2003: AAAS ([American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)) Award for promoting the public understanding of science and technology[^o16]
- 2002: University Creativity Award at [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University)[^o17]
- 1994: Folio Award for the article *Counting on Dyscalculia* which appeared in *Discover Magazine* in 1994[^o18]
