# Tschirnhaus transformation

*Image: Tschirnhaus.jpg.* *[Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus](wikipedia://en/26456397)*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tschirnhaus transformation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Tschirnhaus transformation**, also known as **Tschirnhausen transformation**, is a type of mapping on [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) developed by [Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus](wikipedia://en/26456397) in 1683.

:::

Simply, it is a method for transforming a polynomial equation of degree $n\ge2$ with some nonzero intermediate coefficients, $a_1, ..., a_{n-1}$, such that some or all of the transformed intermediate coefficients, $a'_1, ..., a'_{n-1}$, are exactly zero.

For example, finding a substitution

$$y(x)=k_1x^2 + k_2x+k_3$$

for a [cubic equation](wikipedia://en/180787) of degree $n=3$,

$$f(x) = x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0$$

such that substituting $x=x(y)$ yields a new equation

$$f'(y)=y^3+a'_2y^2+a'_1y+a'_0$$

such that $a'_1=0$, $a'_2=0$, or both.

More generally, it may be defined conveniently by means of [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29), as the transformation on [minimal polynomials](wikipedia://en/9667106) implied by a different choice of [primitive element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_element_%28field_theory%29). This is the most general transformation of an [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) that takes a root to some [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210) applied to that root.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For a generic $n^{th}$ degree [reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reducible_polynomial) [monic](wikipedia://en/314730) [polynomial equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation) $f(x)=0$ of the form $f(x) = g(x) / h(x)$, where $g(x)$ and $h(x)$ are polynomials and $h(x)$ does not vanish at $f(x) = 0$,

$$f(x) = x^n+a_1x^{n-1}+a_2x^{n-2}+...+a_{n-1}x+a_n=0$$

the Tschirnhaus transformation is the function:

$$y=k_1x^{n-1} + k_2x^{n-2}+...+k_{n-1}x+k_n$$

Such that the new equation in $y$, $f'(y)$, has certain special properties, most commonly such that some coefficients, $a'_1,...,a'_{n-1}$, are [identically zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identically_zero).[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Example: Tschirnhaus' method for cubic equations {#Example:_Tschirnhaus'_method_for_cubic_equations}

In Tschirnhaus' 1683 paper,[^o11] he found the [roots](wikipedia://en/264210) of the polynomial

$$f(x)=x^3-px^2+qx-r$$

using the [change of variables](wikipedia://en/2146848)

$$y(x;a)=x-a$$

and its inverse

$$x(y;a)=y+a.$$

Replacing $x$ by $y+a$ in $f$, and expanding the powers of $y+a$ in the resulting formula, yields the transformed polynomial

$$\begin{aligned} f'(y;a)&=y^3+(3a-p)y^2+(3a^2-2pa+q) y+(a^3-pa^2+qa-r)\\ &=y^3+a'_1y^2+a'_2y+a'_3 \end{aligned}$$

with the coefficients

$$\begin{aligned} a'_1&=3a-p \\ a'_2&=3a^2-2pa+q \\ a'_3&=a^3-pa^2+qa-r. \end{aligned}$$

The quadratic term in $f'$ may be eliminated by setting $a'_1=0$, and solving for $a'_1=3a-p=0$ determines the parameter $a$ as $a=p/3$. Thus, the Tschirnhaus transformation

$$y=x-\frac{p}{3},$$

may be substituted into $f'(y;a)$ to yield a polynomial of the form

$$f'(y)=y^3-q'y-r'.$$

The roots of the original polynomial $f$ may be obtained from the roots of this transformed polynomial $f'$ by the same transformation. Tschirnhaus went on to describe how a Tschirnhaus transformation of the form

$$x^2=bx+y+a$$

may be used to eliminate two coefficients in a similar way.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

In detail, let $K$ be a field, and $P(t)$ a polynomial over $K$. If $P$ is irreducible, then the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $K[t]$ by the [principal ideal](wikipedia://en/145126) generated by $P$,

$$K[t]/(P(t)) = L,$$

is a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of $K$. We have

$$L = K(\alpha)$$

where $\alpha$ is $t$ modulo $(P)$. That is, any element of $L$ is a polynomial in $\alpha$, which is thus a primitive element of $L$. There will be other choices $\beta$ of primitive element in $L$: for any such choice of $\beta$ we will have by definition:

$$\beta = F(\alpha), \alpha = G(\beta),$$

with polynomials $F$ and $G$ over $K$. Now if $Q$ is the minimal polynomial for $\beta$ over $K$, we can call $Q$ a **Tschirnhaus transformation** of $P$.

Therefore the set of all Tschirnhaus transformations of an irreducible polynomial is to be described as running over all ways of changing $P$, but leaving $L$ the same. This concept is used in reducing quintics to [Bring–Jerrard form](wikipedia://en/3191883#Bring–Jerrard_normal_form), for example. There is a connection with [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316), when $L$ is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of $K$. The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) may then be considered as all the Tschirnhaus transformations of $P$ to itself.

:::

## History {#History}

In 1683, [Ehrenfried Walther von Tschirnhaus](wikipedia://en/26456397) published a method for rewriting a polynomial of degree $n>2$ such that the $x^{n-1}$ and $x^{n-2}$ terms have zero coefficients.[^o10][^o11] In his paper, Tschirnhaus referenced a method by [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) to reduce a quadratic polynomial $(n=2)$ such that the $x$ term has zero coefficient.

In 1786, this work was expanded by [Erland Samuel Bring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erland_Samuel_Bring) who showed that any generic quintic polynomial could be similarly reduced.

In 1834, [George Jerrard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Jerrard) further expanded Tschirnhaus' work by showing a Tschirnhaus transformation may be used to eliminate the $x^{n-1}$, $x^{n-2}$, and $x^{n-3}$ for a general polynomial of degree $n>3$.[^o5]
