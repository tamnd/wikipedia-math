# London Mathematical Society

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="London Mathematical Society"}

The **London Mathematical Society** (**LMS**) is one of the United Kingdom's [learned societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_society) for [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the others being the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) (RSS), the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) (IMA), the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) and the [Operational Research Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Research_Society) (ORS).

:::

## History {#History}

The Society was established on 16 January 1865, the first president being [Augustus De Morgan](wikipedia://en/18949289). The earliest meetings were held in [University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London), but the Society soon moved into [Burlington House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington_House), [Piccadilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccadilly). The initial activities of the Society included talks and publication of a journal.

The LMS was used as a model for the establishment of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 1888.

[Mary Cartwright](wikipedia://en/1529094) was the first woman to be President of the LMS (in 1961–62).[^o4]

The Society was granted a [royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter) in 1965, a century after its foundation. In 1998 the Society moved from rooms in Burlington House into **De Morgan House** (named after the society's first president), at 57–58 [Russell Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Square), [Bloomsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomsbury), to accommodate an expansion of its staff.

In 2015 the Society celebrated its 150th anniversary. During the year the anniversary was celebrated with a wide range of meetings, events, and other activities, highlighting the historical and continuing value and prevalence of mathematics in society, and in everyday life.

## Membership {#Membership}

Membership is open to those who are interested in mathematics. Currently, there are four classes of membership, namely: (a) Ordinary, (b) Reciprocity, (c) Associate, and (d) Associate (undergraduate). In addition, Honorary Members of the Society are distinguished mathematicians who are not normally resident in the UK, who are proposed by the Society's Council for election to Membership at a Society Meeting.[^o6]

## LMS Activities {#LMS_Activities}

The Society publishes books and periodicals; organises mathematical conferences; provides funding to promote mathematics research and education; and awards a number of prizes and fellowships for excellence in mathematical research.

### Grants {#Grants}

The Society supports mathematics in the UK through its grant schemes. These schemes provide support for mathematicians at different stages in their careers. The Society’s grants include [research grants](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/research-grants) for mathematicians, early career researchers and computer scientists working at the interface of mathematics and computer science; [education grants](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/education-grants) for teachers and other educators; travel grants to attend conferences; and grants for those with [caring](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/caring-supplementary-grants) responsibilities.

Awarding grants is one of the primary mechanisms through which the Society achieves its central purpose, namely to 'promote and extend mathematical knowledge’.

### Fellowships {#Fellowships}

The Society also offers a range of Fellowships: [LMS Early Career Fellowships](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/lms-early-career-fellowships); [LMS Atiyah-Lebanon UK Fellowships](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/atiyah-uk-lebanon-fellowships); [LMS Emmy Noether Fellowships](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/lms-emmy-noether-fellowships) and [Grace Chisholm Young Fellowships](https://www.lms.ac.uk/grants/grace-chisholm-young-fellowships).

### Society lectures and meetings {#Society_lectures_and_meetings}

The Society organises an annual programme of events and meetings. The programme provides meetings of interest to undergraduates, through early career researchers to established mathematicians. These include [LMS-Bath Mathematical Symposia](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/mathematical-symposia), [Lecture Series (Aitken/Forder, Hardy, Invited)](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/lectures/lecture-series), [Research Schools](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/lms-research-schools), [LMS Prospects in Mathematics Meeting](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/lms-prospects-mathematics-meeting), [Public Lectures](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/public-lectures), [Society Meetings](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/society-meetings), [LMS Undergraduate Summer Schools](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/lms-summer-schools) and [Women in Mathematics Days](https://www.lms.ac.uk/events/women-mathematics-day).

## Publications {#Publications}

The Society's periodical publications include five [journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_journal):

- *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* (1969–present)[^o13]
- *Journal of the London Mathematical Society* (1926–present)[^o14]
- *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society* (1865–present)[^o15]
- *Transactions of the London Mathematical Society* (2014–present)[^o16]
- *[Journal of Topology](wikipedia://en/34784652)* (2006 – present)

It also publishes the journal *[Compositio Mathematica](wikipedia://en/31882322)* on behalf of its owning foundation, *[Mathematika](wikipedia://en/49068222)* on behalf of [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) and *[Nonlinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity_%28journal%29)* with the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics).

### Books {#Books}

The Society publishes two book series, the [*LMS* *Lecture Notes*](https://www.cambridge.org/gb/academic/subjects/mathematics/recreational-mathematics/series/london-mathematical-society-lecture-note-series) and [*LMS* *Student Texts*](https://www.cambridge.org/gb/academic/subjects/mathematics/recreational-mathematics/series/london-mathematical-society-student-texts).

Previously it published a series of *Monographs* and (jointly with the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)) the *History of Mathematics* series.

An electronic journal, the *[LMS Journal of Computation and Mathematics](wikipedia://en/63370948)*, ceased publication at the end of 2017.

## Prizes {#Prizes}

The named prizes are:

- [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) (triennial) — the most prestigious
- [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) (two years out of three)
- [Fröhlich Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fröhlich_Prize) (biennial)
- [Shephard Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shephard_Prize) (biennial)
- [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) (biennial)
- [Naylor Prize and Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naylor_Prize_and_Lectureship) (biennial)
- [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize)
- [Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prize)
- [Senior Anne Bennett Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Anne_Bennett_Prize)
- [Anne Bennett Prize](wikipedia://en/61347778)
- [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913)
- [Louis Bachelier Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bachelier_Prize) (biennial)
- [Hirst Prize](https://www.lms.ac.uk/prizes/hirstprize)

In addition, the Society jointly with the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) awards the [David Crighton](wikipedia://en/1560570) Medal and [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415) Medal on alternating years.[^o19] The LMS also awards the [Emmy Noether Fellowships](wikipedia://en/66190328).

## Collections {#Collections}

The Society's archive (up until 1994) is held in [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) (UCL) Special Collections.[^o24] The collection primarily contains letters to [Thomas Archer Hirst](wikipedia://en/14718360), but also includes correspondence to other European mathematicians.[^o24] UCL holds around 150 rare books from the Society which focus on the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and were published between 1701 - 1850.[^o25]
