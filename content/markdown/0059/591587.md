# Hurewicz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurewicz theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hurewicz theorem** is a basic result of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), connecting [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) with [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) via a map known as the **Hurewicz homomorphism**. The theorem is named after [Witold Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385), and generalizes earlier results of [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorems"}

## Statement of the theorems {#Statement_of_the_theorems}

The Hurewicz theorems are a key link between [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) and [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group).

### Absolute version {#Absolute_version}

For any [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected) space *X* and strictly positive integer *n* there exists a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396)

$$h_* \colon \pi_n(X) \to H_n(X),$$

called the **Hurewicz homomorphism**, from the *n*-th [homotopy group](wikipedia://en/297506) to the *n*-th [homology group](wikipedia://en/142432) (with integer coefficients). It is given in the following way: choose a canonical generator $u_n \in H_n(S^n)$, then a homotopy class of maps $f \in \pi_n(X)$ is taken to $f_*(u_n) \in H_n(X)$.

The Hurewicz theorem states cases in which the Hurewicz homomorphism is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397).

- For $n\ge 2$, if *X* is [$(n-1)$-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-connected) (that is: $\pi_i(X)= 0$ for all $i < n$), then $\tilde{H_i}(X)= 0$ for all $i < n$, and the Hurewicz map $h_* \colon \pi_n(X) \to H_n(X)$ is an isomorphism.[^o5]p. 366 This implies, in particular, that the [homological connectivity](wikipedia://en/64688677) equals the [homotopical connectivity](wikipedia://en/6519310) when the latter is at least 1. In addition, the Hurewicz map $h_* \colon \pi_{n+1}(X) \to H_{n+1}(X)$ is an [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) in this case.[^o5]p. 388
- For $n=1$, the Hurewicz homomorphism induces an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) $\tilde{h}_* \colon \pi_1(X)/[ \pi_1(X), \pi_1(X)] \to H_1(X)$, between the [abelianization](wikipedia://en/8847) of the first homotopy group (the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004)) and the first homology group.

### Relative version {#Relative_version}

For any [pair of spaces](wikipedia://en/19067071) $(X,A)$ and integer $k>1$ there exists a homomorphism

$$h_* \colon \pi_k(X,A) \to H_k(X,A)$$

from relative homotopy groups to relative homology groups. The Relative Hurewicz Theorem states that if both $X$ and $A$ are connected and the pair is $(n-1)$-connected then $H_k(X,A)=0$ for $k<n$ and $H_n(X,A)$ is obtained from $\pi_n(X,A)$ by factoring out the action of $\pi_1(A)$. This is proved in, for example, (Whitehead 1978) by induction, proving in turn the absolute version and the Homotopy Addition Lemma.

This relative Hurewicz theorem is reformulated by (Brown Higgins 1981) as a statement about the morphism

$$\pi_n(X,A) \to \pi_n(X \cup CA),$$

where $CA$ denotes the [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) of $A$. This statement is a special case of a [homotopical excision theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopical_excision_theorem), involving induced modules for $n>2$ ([crossed modules](wikipedia://en/1977281) if $n=2$), which itself is deduced from a higher homotopy [van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Kampen_theorem) for relative homotopy groups, whose proof requires development of techniques of a cubical higher homotopy groupoid of a filtered space.

### Triadic version {#Triadic_version}

For any triad of spaces $(X;A,B)$ (i.e., a space *X* and subspaces *A*, *B*) and integer $k>2$ there exists a homomorphism

$$h_*\colon \pi_k(X;A,B) \to H_k(X;A,B)$$

from triad homotopy groups to triad homology groups. Note that

$$H_k(X;A,B) \cong H_k(X\cup (C(A\cup B))).$$

The Triadic Hurewicz Theorem states that if *X*, *A*, *B*, and $C=A\cap B$ are connected, the pairs $(A,C)$ and $(B,C)$ are $(p-1)$-connected and $(q-1)$-connected, respectively, and the triad $(X;A,B)$ is $(p+q-2)$-connected, then $H_k(X;A,B)=0$ for $k<p+q-2$ and $H_{p+q-1}(X;A)$ is obtained from $\pi_{p+q-1}(X;A,B)$ by factoring out the action of $\pi_1(A\cap B)$ and the generalised Whitehead products. The proof of this theorem uses a higher homotopy van Kampen type theorem for triadic homotopy groups, which requires a notion of the fundamental $\operatorname{cat}^n$-group of an *n*-cube of spaces.

### Simplicial set version {#Simplicial_set_version}

The Hurewicz theorem for topological spaces can also be stated for *n*-connected [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784) satisfying the Kan condition.[^o11]

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Rational Hurewicz theorem"}

### Rational Hurewicz theorem {#Rational_Hurewicz_theorem}

**Rational Hurewicz theorem:[^o14][^o15]** Let *X* be a simply connected topological space with $\pi_i(X)\otimes \mathbb{Q} = 0$ for $i\leq r$. Then the Hurewicz map

$$h\otimes \mathbb{Q} \colon \pi_i(X)\otimes \mathbb{Q} \longrightarrow H_i(X;\mathbb{Q} )$$

induces an isomorphism for $1\leq i \leq 2r$ and a surjection for $i = 2r+1$.
