# Brill–Noether theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brill–Noether theory"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **Brill–Noether theory**, introduced by (txt), is the study of **special divisors**, certain [divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_on_an_algebraic_curve) on a [curve](wikipedia://en/253260) $C$ that determine more compatible functions than would be predicted. In classical language, special divisors move on the curve in a "larger than expected" [linear system of divisors](wikipedia://en/1186886).

:::

Throughout, we consider a projective smooth curve over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) (or over some other [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018)).

The condition to be a special divisor $D$ can be formulated in [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) terms, as the non-vanishing of the $H^{1}$ [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of the sheaf of sections of the [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) or [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) associated to $D$. This means that, by the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261), the $H^{0}$ cohomology or space of holomorphic sections is larger than expected.

Alternatively, by [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175), the condition is that there exist [holomorphic differentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_differential) with divisor $\ge -D$ on the curve.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Main theorems of Brill–Noether theory"}

## Main theorems of Brill–Noether theory {#Main_theorems_of_Brill–Noether_theory}

For a given genus $g$, the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) for curves $C$ of genus $g$ should contain a dense subset parameterizing those curves with the minimum in the way of special divisors. One goal of the theory is to 'count constants', for those curves: to predict the dimension of the space of special divisors (up to [linear equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence)) of a given degree $d$, as a function of $g$, that *must* be present on a curve of that genus.

The basic statement can be formulated in terms of the [Picard variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_variety) $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$ of a smooth curve $C$, and the subset of $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$ corresponding to [divisor classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class) of divisors $D$, with given values $d$ of $\deg(D)$ and $r$ of $l(D) - 1$ in the notation of the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261). There is a lower bound $\rho$ for the dimension $\dim(d, r, g)$ of this [subscheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscheme) in $\operatorname{Pic}(C)$:

$$\dim(d,r,g) \geq \rho = g-(r+1)(g-d+r)$$

called the **Brill–Noether number**. The formula can be memorized via the mnemonic (using our desired $h^0(D) = r+1$ and Riemann-Roch)

$$g-(r+1)(g-d+r) = g - h^0(D)h^1(D)$$

For smooth curves $C$ and for $d \ge 1$, $r \ge 0$ the basic results about the space $G^r_d$ of linear systems on $C$ of degree $d$ and dimension $r$ are as follows.

- [George Kempf](wikipedia://en/34816743) proved that if $\rho \ge 0$ then $G^r_d$ is not empty, and every component has dimension at least $\rho$.
- [William Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149) and [Robert Lazarsfeld](wikipedia://en/29022720) proved that if $\rho \ge 1$ then $G^r_d$ is connected.
- (Griffiths Harris 1980) showed that if $C$ is generic then $G^r_d$ is reduced and all components have dimension exactly $\rho$ (so in particular $G^r_d$ is empty if $\rho < 0$).
- [David Gieseker](wikipedia://en/56567476) proved that if $C$ is generic then $G^r_d$ is smooth. By the connectedness result this implies it is irreducible if $\rho > 0$.

Other more recent results not necessarily in terms of space $G^r_d$ of linear systems are:

- Eric Larson (2017) proved that if $\rho \ge 0$, $r \ge 3$, and $n \ge 1$, the restriction maps $H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}(n))\rightarrow H^0(\mathcal{O}_{C}(n))$ are of maximal rank, also known as the maximal rank conjecture.[^o6][^o7]
- Eric Larson and Isabel Vogt (2022) proved that if $\rho \ge 0$ then there is a curve $C$ interpolating through $n$ general points in $\mathbb{P}^r$ if and only if $(r-1)n \leq (r + 1)d - (r-3)(g-1),$ except in 4 exceptional cases: $(d, g, r) \in \{(5,2,3),(6,4,3),(7,2,5),(10,6,5)\}.$[^o8][^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Maximal rank conjecture"}

## Maximal rank conjecture {#Maximal_rank_conjecture}

Let $C \subset$ $\mathbb{P}^r$ be a general curve of genus *g* embedded via a general linear series of degree *d*. The Maximal Rank Conjecture asserts that the restriction maps $H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^r}(m)) \to H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}(m))$ are of maximal rank; this determines the Hilbert function of *$C$.*[^o12]

Its first proof was published by Eric Larson on 14 Nov 2017[^o13] whilst a [graduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_student) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT).[^o14]

:::
