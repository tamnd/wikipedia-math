# Rota–Baxter algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rota–Baxter algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Rota–Baxter algebra** is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112), together with a particular [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) $R$ which satisfies the **Rota–Baxter identity**. It appeared first in the work of the American mathematician [Glen E. Baxter](wikipedia://en/21092449)[^o2] in the realm of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). Baxter's work was further explored from different angles by [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891),[^o3][^o4][^o5] [Pierre Cartier](wikipedia://en/1470549),[^o6] and [Frederic V. Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_V._Atkinson),[^o7] among others. Baxter’s derivation of this identity that later bore his name emanated from some of the fundamental results of the famous probabilist [Frank Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Spitzer) in [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) theory.[^o8][^o9]

:::

In the 1980s, the Rota-Baxter operator of weight 0 in the context of Lie algebras was rediscovered as the operator form of the classical [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810),[^o10] named after the well-known physicists [Chen-Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen-Ning_Yang) and [Rodney Baxter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Baxter).

The study of Rota–Baxter algebras experienced a renaissance this century, beginning with several developments, in the algebraic approach to [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453) of perturbative [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267),[^o11] [dendriform algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendriform_algebra), associative analogues of the classical Yang–Baxter equation[^o12] and mixable shuffle product constructions.[^o13]

## Definition and first properties {#Definition_and_first_properties}

Let $k$ be a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) and let $\lambda$ be given. A linear operator $R$ on a $k$-algebra $A$ is called a **Rota–Baxter operator of weight $\lambda$** if it satisfies the **Rota–Baxter relation of weight $\lambda$**:

$$R(x)R(y)=R(R(x)y) + R(xR(y)) + \lambda R(xy)$$

for all $x, y \in A$. Then the pair $(A,R)$ or simply $A$ is called a **Rota–Baxter algebra of weight $\lambda$**. In some literature, $\theta=-\lambda$ is used in which case the above equation becomes

$$R(x)R(y)+\theta R(xy) = R(R(x)y) + R(xR(y)),$$

called the **Rota-Baxter equation of weight $\theta$**. The terms Baxter [operator algebra](wikipedia://en/455987) and Baxter algebra are also used.

Let $R$ be a Rota–Baxter of weight $\lambda$. Then $-\lambda Id - R$ is also a Rota–Baxter operator of weight $\lambda$. Further, for $\mu$ in $k$, $\mu R$ is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight $\mu\lambda$.

## Examples {#Examples}

**Integration by parts**

[Integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) is an example of a Rota–Baxter algebra of weight 0. Let $C(\mathbb R)$ be the algebra of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on the set of reals $\mathbb R$. Define the linear map $I\colon C(\mathbb R)\to C(\mathbb R)$ via [integration](wikipedia://en/15532):

$$I(f)(x) = \int_0^x f(t) dt.$$

for a continuous function $f \in C(\mathbb R)$ and $x\in\mathbb R$. For $f,g\in C(\mathbb R)$, let $G = I(g)$ and $F = I(f)$. Then the formula for integration by parts can be written as

$$F(x)G(x) = \int_0^x f(t) G(t) dt + \int_0^x F(t)g(t) dt.$$

In other words

$$I(f)I(g) = I(fI(g)) + I(I(f)g),$$

which shows that $I$ is a Rota–Baxter algebra of weight 0.

:::

## Spitzer's identity {#Spitzer's_identity}

[Spitzer's identity](wikipedia://en/37986691) appeared is named after the American mathematician [Frank Spitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Spitzer). It is regarded as a remarkable stepping stone in the theory of sums of [independent random variables](wikipedia://en/27593) in fluctuation theory of probability. It can naturally be understood in terms of Rota–Baxter operators.

:::
