# Siegfried Gottwald

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegfried Gottwald"}

**Siegfried Johannes Gottwald** (30 March 1943 – 20 September 2015) was a German mathematician, [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [historian of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science).[^o3]

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Gottwald was born in [Limbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbach-Oberfrohna), Saxony in 1943. From 1961 to 1966, he studied mathematics at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig), where he was awarded his doctor title in 1969 and his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1977.[^o3]

He was [a tenured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure) professor of [non-classical](wikipedia://en/3192556) and [mathematical](wikipedia://en/19636) logic at the University of Leipzig where he taught from 1972 to his retirement in 2008.[^o3] His main research areas are [fuzzy sets](wikipedia://en/56601) and fuzzy methodologies, [many-valued logic](wikipedia://en/38024) and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).[^o3]

He published several books on many-valued logic and on fuzzy sets and their applications, a co-authored textbook on [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), and a reader in the history of logic. He also contributed to the German biographical dictionary of mathematicians, *Lexikon berühmter Mathematiker*.

Gottwald was Deputy Dean of the Faculty of Social Sciences and Philosophy at the University of Leipzig for several years. He was married with three children.
