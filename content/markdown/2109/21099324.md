# Krull–Schmidt category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Krull–Schmidt category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of mathematics, a **Krull–Schmidt category** is a generalization of categories in which the [Krull–Schmidt theorem](wikipedia://en/21100715) holds. They arise, for example, in the study of finite-dimensional [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *C* be an [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854), or more generally an additive [$R$-linear category](wikipedia://en/59521#R-linear_categories) for a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $R$. We call *C* a **Krull–Schmidt category** provided that every object decomposes into a finite direct sum of objects having local endomorphism rings. Equivalently, *C* has [split idempotents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_idempotent) and the endomorphism ring of every object is [semiperfect](wikipedia://en/22658615#semiperfect_ring).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

One has the analogue of the Krull–Schmidt theorem in Krull–Schmidt categories:

An object is called *indecomposable* if it is not isomorphic to a direct sum of two nonzero objects. In a Krull–Schmidt category we have that

- an object is indecomposable if and only if its endomorphism ring is local.
- every object is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects.
- if $X_1 \oplus X_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus X_r \cong Y_1 \oplus Y_2 \oplus \cdots \oplus Y_s$ where the $X_i$ and $Y_j$ are all indecomposable, then $r=s$, and there exists a permutation $\pi$ such that $X_{\pi(i)} \cong Y_i$ for all $i$.

One can define the [Auslander–Reiten quiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auslander–Reiten_quiver) of a Krull–Schmidt category.

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- An [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) in which every object has [finite length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_length).[^o8] This includes as a special case the category of finite-dimensional modules over an algebra.
- The category of finitely-generated modules over a **finite**[^o9] [$R$-algebra](wikipedia://en/2112), where $R$ is a [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [complete local ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_local_ring).[^o10]
- The category of [coherent sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_sheaves) on a [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627) over an [algebraically-closed field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_closed).[^o11]

::: {#o13 .example kind=example/counterexample cue=heading name="A non-example"}

### A non-example {#A_non-example}

The category of finitely-generated [projective modules](wikipedia://en/364488) over the integers has split idempotents, and every module is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of the regular module, the number being given by the [rank](wikipedia://en/3327998). Thus the category has unique decomposition into indecomposables, but is not Krull-Schmidt since the regular module does not have a local endomorphism ring.

:::
