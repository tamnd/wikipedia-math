# HP-67/97

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="HP-67/97"}

The **HP-67** is a magnetic card-programmable [handheld calculator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handheld_calculator), introduced by [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) in 1976 at an [MSRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_price) of \$450.[^o3] A desktop version with built-in [thermal printer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_printer) was sold as the **HP-97** at a price of \$750.[^o4] Collectively, they are known as the **HP-67/97**.[^o5]

:::

Marketed as improved successors to the [HP-65](wikipedia://en/506769), the HP-67/97 were based on the technology of the "20-series" of calculators ([HP-25](wikipedia://en/4402245), [HP-19C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP-19C) etc.) introduced a year earlier. The two models are functionally equivalent, and programs on magnetic cards can be interchanged between them.

## Features {#Features}

The 67/97 provide a complete set of scientific, statistical and engineering operations, including [trigonometric](wikipedia://en/18717261), [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860) and [exponential](wikipedia://en/9678) functions, coordinate conversions, average/[standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) etc.

The HP-67/97 series featured a program memory of 224 eight-bit [words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_word). The two extra bits per word compared to the HP-65's six allowed the designers to store any program instruction in a single memory cell ("fully merged keycodes") even if it required multiple keystrokes to enter. Programs could include 20 labels, subroutines (3 levels deep), four [flag registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_register), 8 [comparison functions](wikipedia://en/4088765), and extended index and loop control functions.

At 15 digits, the display was wider than those of the predecessor models, although the decimal point was displayed on its own digit position. The HP-67 keys carry up to four functions each, accessed through "f", "g" and "h" prefix keys (gold, blue and black labels, respectively). The model 97 had more (and larger) keys, therefore only two functions were assigned to each key. When interchanging magnetic cards between the HP-67 and the HP-97, the calculators' software took care of converting the key codes, and emulated the 97's print functions through the 67's display.

The HP-67 is powered by a pack of three AA-sized [nickel-cadmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel-cadmium_battery) rechargeable batteries. Owing to the power requirements of the built-in thermal printer, the HP-97 employs a larger battery pack and more powerful charger.

## Memory and programming {#Memory_and_programming}

Of the 26-[register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processor_register) data memory, the first ten ("primary registers") could be accessed directly, ten more as an alternate register set, and the remaining six through the user defined keys A-E and as an [index register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_register). Using the latter, a program could access all 26 registers as a single indexed array. Data memory is not permanent as in later models, i.e. register contents and program are lost when powering off. The alternate register set was also used by statistical functions.

The built-in magnetic card reader/writer could be used to save programs and data, with the ability to combine data from multiple cards. The same magnetic card format was later used for the [HP-41C](wikipedia://en/630715) which offered compatibility to the 67/97 through software in the card reader. HP offered a library of programs supplied on packs of pre-recorded magnetic cards for many applications including [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying), medicine, as well as [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) and [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering). Cards could be write protected by cutting off a designated corner.

In addition to software and support from HP, an active user community supported the HP-67/97 as well as the other HP programmables of the era. The group was called PPC and produced the [*PPC Journal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPC_Journal). One of the notable contributions of the group was the development of a "Blackbox" that allowed pseudo-alphanumeric displays.

A version adapted to support an additional backward-facing display manufactured by Educational Calculator Devices named EduCALC 67 GD existed as well.[^o8][^o9]

## HP-97S {#HP-97S}

*Image: Hp-97 front-2.JPG.* HP-97 calculator

In 1977, HP introduced an extended version of the desktop model as the **HP-97S** which featured an extra parallel I/O port (40 lines for 10 4-bit BCD digits, plus 5 control lines) for collecting data from external hardware,[^o11] at a price of \$1,375.
