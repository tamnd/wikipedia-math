# Stein-Rosenberg theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stein-Rosenberg theorem"}

The **Stein-Rosenberg theorem**, proved in 1948, states that under certain premises, the [Jacobi method](wikipedia://en/4047104) and the [Gauss-Seidel method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Seidel_method) are either both convergent, or both divergent. If they are convergent, then the Gauss-Seidel is asymptotically faster than the Jacobi method.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$. Let $\rho(X)$ be the [spectral radius](wikipedia://en/386169) of a matrix $X$. Let $T_J=D^{-1}(L+U)$ and $T_1=(D-L)^{-1}U$ be the [matrix splitting](wikipedia://en/36743227) for the Jacobi method and the Gauss-Seidel method respectively.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** If $a_{ij}\le 0$ for $i\ne j$ and $a_{ii} > 0$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$. Then, one and only one of the following mutually exclusive relations is valid:

1. $$\rho(T_J) = \rho(T_1) = 0.$$
1. $$0 < \rho(T_1) < \rho(T_J) < 1.$$
1. $$1=\rho(T_J)=\rho(T_1).$$
1. $$1 < \rho(T_J) < \rho(T_1).$$

:::

:::

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof and applications"}

## Proof and applications {#Proof_and_applications}

The proof uses the [Perron-Frobenius theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perron-Frobenius_theorem) for non-negative matrices. Its proof can be found in [Richard S. Varga](wikipedia://en/25492358)'s 1962 book *Matrix Iterative Analysis*.[^o7]

In the words of Richard Varga:

> the Stein-Rosenberg theorem gives us our first comparison theorem for two different iterative methods. Interpreted in a more practical way, not only is the point Gauss-Seidel iterative method computationally more convenient to use (because of storage requirements) than the point Jacobi iterative matrix, but it is also asymptotically faster when the Jacobi matrix $T_J$ is non-negative

Employing more hypotheses, on the matrix $A$, one can even give quantitative results. For example, under certain conditions one can state that the Gauss-Seidel method is twice as fast as the Jacobi iteration.[^o8]

:::
