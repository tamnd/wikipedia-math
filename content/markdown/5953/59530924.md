# S-object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="S-object"}

In [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), an **$\mathbb{S}$-object** (also called a **symmetric sequence**) is a sequence $\{ X(n) \}$ of objects such that each $X(n)$ comes with an action[^o2] of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $\mathbb{S}_n$.

:::

The category of [combinatorial species](wikipedia://en/160076) is equivalent to the category of finite $\mathbb{S}$-sets (roughly because the [permutation category](wikipedia://en/59512190) is equivalent to the category of finite sets and bijections.)[^o3]

## S-module {#S-module}

By *$\mathbb{S}$-module*, we mean an $\mathbb{S}$-object in the category $\mathsf{Vect}$ of finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field *k* of characteristic zero (the symmetric groups act from the right by convention). Then each $\mathbb{S}$-module determines a [Schur functor](wikipedia://en/11597641) on $\mathsf{Vect}$.

This definition of $\mathbb{S}$-module shares its name with the considerably better-known model for [highly structured ring spectra](wikipedia://en/27342975) due to Elmendorf, Kriz, Mandell and May.
