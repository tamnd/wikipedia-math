# Charles Tweedie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Tweedie"}

**Charles Tweedie** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (27 June 1868 – 14 September 1925) was a Scottish mathematician and mathematical historian.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Tweedie was born in [Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swinton,_Scottish_Borders) on 27 June 1868, the son of Charlotte Lugton (1836–1909) and George Tweedie (1837–1905) a schoolmaster originally from [Cleish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleish). He was from a large family, and his older brother, [David Tweedie](wikipedia://en/60919715) (b. 1865), was also a mathematician, who worked mainly in Egypt.[^o3][^o4]

He was educated at Swinton Parish School and then [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). He won the Sibbald Scholarship to study mathematics and physics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). Here he was trusted enough by [Professor George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803) to assist with the editing of Chrystal's textbook on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). Tweedie graduated with an MA BSc with honours in 1890. He won the Bruce of Grangehill Bursary which allowed him to undertake postgraduate study at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) 1890/91 and [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) 1891/92.[^o3]

In the autumn of 1892 he became an assistant in the mathematics department at the University of Edinburgh under Professor Chrystal and became an official soon after, as Chrystal's right-hand man.[^o5]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1897. His proposers were [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803), [Peter Guthrie Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait), [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott) and [John Sturgeon Mackay](wikipedia://en/54492344). He was President of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) 1903–1904.[^o6]

He died in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 14 September 1925. He was unmarried and had no children.

## Publications {#Publications}

- *The Geometrica Organica of [Colin Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/7824)* (1916)

- *Notes on [James Gray](wikipedia://en/30666836)* (1924)
