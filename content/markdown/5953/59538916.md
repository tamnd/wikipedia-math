# Dieter Jungnickel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dieter Jungnickel"}

*Image: Blokhuis Hirschfeld Jungnickel Thas.jpg.* *From left:* Aart Blokhuis, [James William Peter Hirschfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_William_Peter_Hirschfeld), Dieter Jungnickel, and [Joseph A. Thas](wikipedia://en/45184583), at the [MFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), 2001

**Dieter Jungnickel** (born 20 March 1952 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin)) is a German mathematician specializing in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

In 1971 Jungnickel entered the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin), graduating in 1975. He studied the [finite difference method](wikipedia://en/6054681) with [Hanfried Lenz](wikipedia://en/14052690) and completed his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1978. He became a professor of mathematics at [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen) in 1980.

The uses of [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061) in [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) led Jungnickel and Thomas Beth to write "Variations of Seven Points"[^o2] which used the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) as a starting point.

With Thomas Beth and Hanfried Lenz he wrote *Design Theory* (1985) on [combinatorial design](wikipedia://en/2761968). [Albrecht Beutelspacher](wikipedia://en/13395792) reviewed it positively as deserving high estimation, and as an impressive work. It was re-published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) in 1986. He updated the work in 1989[^o4] For the second edition *Design Theory* (1999) was split into two volumes, one and two.

In 1990 Jungnickel wrote an article on geometric and graph-theoretical aspects of [latin squares](wikipedia://en/37447) of interest in coding theory.[^o7] In September 1990 [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont) was the site of a conference concerned with the mathematics of coding theory, and commemorating the contributions of [Marshall Hall](wikipedia://en/7758584). Together with [Scott Vanstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Vanstone), Jungnickel edited the *Proceedings*.[^o8]

In 1993 Jungnickel joined the [University of Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Augsburg)[^o9] where he occupies the chair for [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492), [optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimisation), and [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476).

Jungnickel wrote about [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) in 1993: *Finite fields, Structure and Arithmetics*. A reviewer notes that "The author does a beautiful job showing and developing the practical applicability of the fascinating area of finite field theory".

In 1999 his book *Graphs, Networks and Algorithms* appeared as translation of the 1994 German version. A reviewer calls it a "first class textbook" and indispensable for teachers of combinatorial optimization. The second edition appeared in 2005, the third in 2008, and the fourth in 2013.
