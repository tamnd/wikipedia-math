# Newest vertex bisection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Newest vertex bisection"}

**Newest Vertex Bisection** is an [algorithmic](wikipedia://en/775) method to locally refine [triangulations](wikipedia://en/207174).[^o2] It is widely used in [computational science](wikipedia://en/1181008), [numerical simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_simulation), and [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics). The advantage of newest vertex bisection is that it allows local refinement of triangulations without degenerating the shape of the triangles after repeated usage.[^o3]

:::

In newest vertex bisection, whenever a triangle is to be split into smaller triangles, it will be bisected by drawing a line from the newest [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) to the midpoint of the edge opposite to that vertex. That midpoint becomes the newest vertex of the two newer triangles. One can show that repeating this procedure for a given triangulation leads to triangles that belong to only a finite number of [similarity classes](wikipedia://en/39377).

Generalizations of newest vertex bisection to dimension three and higher are known.[^o4] Newest vertex bisection is used in local mesh refinement for adaptive [finite element methods](wikipedia://en/18233581),[^o5] where it is an alternative to [red-green refinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-green_refinement) and [uniform mesh refinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_mesh_refinement).
