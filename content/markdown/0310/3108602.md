# Spanier–Whitehead duality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spanier–Whitehead duality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Spanier–Whitehead duality** is a [duality theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory) in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), based on a geometrical idea that a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* may be considered as dual to its complement in the *n*-[sphere](wikipedia://en/27859), where *n* is large enough. Its origins lie in [Alexander duality](wikipedia://en/3108888) theory, in [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), concerning complements in [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470). The theory is also referred to as ***S-duality***, but this can now cause possible confusion with the [S-duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-duality) of [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305). It is named for [Edwin Spanier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Spanier) and [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179), who developed it in papers from 1955.

:::

The basic point is that sphere complements determine the homology, but not the [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type), in general. What is determined, however, is the [stable homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stable_homotopy_type), which was conceived as a first approximation to homotopy type. Thus Spanier–Whitehead duality fits into [stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/2946655).

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *X* be a compact [neighborhood retract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_retract) in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Then $X^+$ and $\Sigma^{-n}\Sigma'(\mathbb{R}^n \setminus X)$ are [dual objects](wikipedia://en/5560194) in the category of pointed [spectra](wikipedia://en/1033697) with the smash product as a monoidal structure. Here $X^+$ is the union of $X$ and a point, $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma'$ are reduced and unreduced [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) respectively.

Taking homology and cohomology with respect to an [Eilenberg–MacLane spectrum](wikipedia://en/1073230) recovers [Alexander duality](wikipedia://en/3108888) formally.

:::
