# Landauer's principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landauer's principle"}

**Landauer's principle** is a [physical principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle#Principle_as_scientific_law) pertaining to a lower [theoretical](wikipedia://en/19594028) limit of [energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) of [computation](wikipedia://en/5926). It holds that an irreversible change in [information](wikipedia://en/18985062#As_a_property_in_physics) stored in a computer, such as merging two computational paths, dissipates a minimum amount of heat to its surroundings.[^o2] It is hypothesized that energy consumption below this lower bound would require the development of [reversible computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_computing).

:::

The principle was first proposed by [Rolf Landauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Landauer) in 1961.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Landauer's principle states that the minimum energy needed to erase one bit of information is proportional to the temperature at which the system is operating. Specifically, the energy needed for this computational task is given by

$$E \geq k_\text{B} T \ln 2,$$

where $k_\text{B}$ is the [Boltzmann constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_constant) and $T$ is the [absolute temperature](wikipedia://en/41789).[^o5] At [room temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_temperature), the Landauer limit represents an energy of approximately \0.018 eV. , modern computers use about a billion times as much energy per operation.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## History {#History}

Rolf Landauer first proposed the principle in 1961 while working at [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM).[^o9] He justified and stated important limits to an earlier conjecture by [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942). This refinement is sometimes called the Landauer bound, or Landauer limit.

In 2008 and 2009, researchers showed that Landauer's principle can be derived from the [second law of thermodynamics](wikipedia://en/133017) and the entropy change associated with information gain, developing the thermodynamics of quantum and classical feedback-controlled systems.[^o10][^o11]

In 2011, the principle was generalized to show that while information erasure requires an increase in entropy, this increase could theoretically occur at no energy cost.[^o12] Instead, the cost can be taken in another [conserved quantity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_law_%28physics%29), such as [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839).

In a 2012 article published in [*Nature*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29), a team of physicists from the [École normale supérieure de Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Lyon), [University of Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Augsburg) and the [University of Kaiserslautern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kaiserslautern) described that for the first time they have measured the tiny amount of heat released when an individual bit of data is erased.[^o13]

In 2014, physical experiments tested Landauer's principle and confirmed its predictions.[^o14]

In 2016, researchers used a laser probe to measure the amount of energy dissipation that resulted when a [nanomagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanomagnet) bit flipped from off to on. Flipping the bit required about \0.026 eV at 300 K, which is just 44% above the Landauer minimum.[^o15]

A 2018 article published in *[Nature Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Physics)* features a Landauer erasure performed at cryogenic temperatures (*T* = 1 K) on an array of [high-spin](wikipedia://en/19593829#Higher_spins) (*S* = 10) quantum [molecular magnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-molecule_magnet). The array is made to act as a spin register where each nanomagnet encodes a single bit of information.[^o16] The experiment has laid the foundations for the extension of the validity of the Landauer principle to the quantum realm. Owing to the fast dynamics and low "inertia" of the single spins used in the experiment, the researchers also showed how an erasure operation can be carried out at the lowest possible thermodynamic cost—that imposed by the Landauer principle—and at a high speed.[^o16][^o2]

## Challenges {#Challenges}

The principle is widely accepted as [physical law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_law), but it has been challenged for using [circular reasoning](wikipedia://en/313944) and faulty assumptions.[^o18][^o19][^o20][^o21] Others[^o2][^o22][^o23] have defended the principle, and Sagawa and Ueda (2008)[^o10] and Cao and Feito (2009)[^o11] have shown that Landauer's principle is a consequence of the second law of thermodynamics and the entropy reduction associated with information gain.

On the other hand, recent advances in non-equilibrium statistical physics have established that there is no a priori relationship between logical and thermodynamic reversibility.[^o24] It is possible that a physical process is logically reversible but thermodynamically irreversible. It is also possible that a physical process is logically irreversible but thermodynamically reversible. At best, the benefits of implementing a computation with a logically reversible system are nuanced.[^o25]
