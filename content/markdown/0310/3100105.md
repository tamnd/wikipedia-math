# Köthe conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Köthe conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Köthe conjecture** is a problem in [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424). It is formulated in various ways. Suppose that *R* is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404). One way to state the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) is that if *R* has no [nil ideal](wikipedia://en/3100140), other than {0}, then it has no nil [one-sided ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-sided_ideal), other than {0}.

:::

This question was posed in 1930 by [Gottfried Köthe](wikipedia://en/27641787) (1905–1989). The Köthe conjecture has been shown to be true for various classes of rings, such as [polynomial identity rings](wikipedia://en/25386269)[^o2] and right [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129).[^o3] In 2026, the AI model [GPT-6 Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-6_Astra) found an explicit counterexample which disproves the conjecture, after being prompted by Tom Adamczewski of Epoch AI.[^o4][^o5] An exposition of the proof was posted by Adamczewski and ring theorists Bernhard Böhmler and Rene Marczinzik.[^o6] Another counterexample was found by Greenfeld, King and Vendramin, with some assistance from GPT-6 Astra.[^o7]

## Equivalent formulations {#Equivalent_formulations}

The conjecture has several different formulations:[^o9][^o10][^o11]

1. (Köthe conjecture) In any ring, the [sum](wikipedia://en/25977#Ideal_operations) of two nil left ideals is nil.
1. In any ring, the sum of two one-sided nil ideals is nil.
1. In any ring, every nil left or right ideal of the ring is contained in the [upper nil radical](wikipedia://en/372242#The_upper_nil_radical_or_K.C3.B6the_radical) of the ring.
1. For any ring *R* and for any nil ideal *J* of *R*, the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) ideal M<sub>*n*</sub>(*J*) is a nil ideal of M<sub>*n*</sub>(*R*) for every *n*.
1. For any ring *R* and for any nil ideal *J* of *R*, the matrix ideal M<sub>2</sub>(*J*) is a nil ideal of M<sub>2</sub>(*R*).
1. For any ring *R*, the upper nil radical of M<sub>*n*</sub>(*R*) is the set of matrices with entries from the upper nilradical of *R* for every positive integer *n*.
1. For any ring *R* and for any nil ideal *J* of *R*, the [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) with indeterminate *x* and [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) from *J* lie in the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) *R*\[*x*\].
1. For any ring *R*, the Jacobson radical of *R*\[*x*\] consists of the polynomials with coefficients from the upper nilradical of *R*.

Necessary and sufficient conditions under which statement 3 given above does not hold have been given recently.[^o12]

## Related problems {#Related_problems}

A conjecture by [Amitsur](wikipedia://en/19937535) read: "If *J* is a nil ideal in *R*, then *J*\[*x*\] is a nil ideal of the polynomial ring *R*\[*x*\]."[^o14] This conjecture, if true, would have [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) the Köthe conjecture through the equivalent statements above. However, in 2000 a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) was produced by [Agata Smoktunowicz](wikipedia://en/44906201).[^o15] While not a disproof of the Köthe conjecture, this fueled suspicions that the Köthe conjecture may be false.[^o16]

[Kegel](wikipedia://en/80543894) proved a ring which is the direct sum of two nilpotent [subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145) is itself [nilpotent](wikipedia://en/252235).[^o17] The question arose whether or not "nilpotent" could be replaced with "locally nilpotent" or "nil". Partial progress was made when Kelarev produced an example of a ring which isn't nil, but is the direct sum of two locally nilpotent rings. This demonstrates that Kegel's question with "locally nilpotent" replacing "nilpotent" is answered in the negative.[^o18]

The sum of a nilpotent subring and a nil subring is always nil.[^o19]
