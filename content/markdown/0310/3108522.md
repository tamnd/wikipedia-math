# Stunted projective space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stunted projective space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **stunted projective space** is a construction on a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of importance in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), introduced by (txt). Idea includes collapsing a part of conventional projective space to a point.

:::

More concretely, in a [real projective space](wikipedia://en/1014694), [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) or [quaternionic projective space](wikipedia://en/2808931)

$$\mathbb{KP}^n$$

where $\mathbb{K}$ can be either [$\color{blue}{\mathbb{R}}$](wikipedia://en/20646438), [$\color{blue}{\mathbb{C}}$](wikipedia://en/5826) or [$\color{blue}\mathbb{H}$](wikipedia://en/51440). One can find (in many ways) copies of

$$\mathbb{KP}^m$$

where, $m < n$. The corresponding stunted projective space is then

$$\mathbb{KP}^{n,m} = \mathbb{KP}^n / \mathbb{KP}^m$$

where, the notation implies that the $\mathbb{KP}^m$ has been identified to a point. This makes a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) that is no longer a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). The importance of this construction was realised when it was shown that real stunted projective spaces arose as [Spanier–Whitehead duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanier–Whitehead_dual) of spaces of [Ioan James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioan_James), so-called *quasi-projective spaces*, constructed from [Stiefel manifolds](wikipedia://en/1392495). Their properties were therefore linked to the construction of [frame fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_field) on [spheres](wikipedia://en/27859).

In this way the question on [vector fields on spheres](wikipedia://en/3100908) was reduced to a question on stunted projective spaces:

> For $\mathbb{RP}^{n,m}$, is there a [degree one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_mapping) mapping on the 'next cell up' (of the first dimension not collapsed in the stunting) that extends to the whole space?

[Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959) showed that this could not happen, completing the proof.

In later developments spaces $\mathbb{KP}^{\infty,m}$ and stunted [lens spaces](wikipedia://en/885725) have also been used.
