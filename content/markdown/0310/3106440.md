# Rankine vortex

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rankine vortex"}

*Image: RankineVortex.svg.* Velocity distribution in a Rankine vortex.

*Image: Rankine vortex animation.gif.* Animation of a Rankine vortex. Free-floating test particles reveal the velocity and vorticity pattern.

The **Rankine vortex** is a simple mathematical model of a [vortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortex) in a [viscous](wikipedia://en/18963754) fluid. It is named after its discoverer, [William John Macquorn Rankine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_John_Macquorn_Rankine).

:::

The vortices observed in nature are usually modelled with an [irrotational](wikipedia://en/58320#Examples_of_two-dimensional_flows#Line_vortex) (potential or free) vortex. However, in a potential vortex, the velocity becomes infinite at the vortex center. In reality, very close to the origin, the motion resembles a solid body rotation. The Rankine vortex model assumes a solid-body rotation inside a cylinder of radius $a$ and a potential vortex outside the cylinder. The radius $a$ is referred to as the vortex-core radius. The velocity components $(v_r,v_\theta,v_z)$ of the Rankine vortex, expressed in terms of the cylindrical-coordinate system $(r,\theta,z)$ are given by[^o2]

$$v_r=0,\quad v_\theta(r) = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\begin{cases} r/a^2 & r \le a, \\ 1/ r & r > a \end{cases}, \quad v_z = 0$$

where $\Gamma$ is the [circulation strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulation_%28fluid_dynamics%29) of the Rankine vortex. Since solid-body rotation is characterized by an azimuthal velocity $\Omega r$, where $\Omega$ is the constant [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927), the parameter $\Omega =\Gamma/(2\pi a^2)$ can also be used to characterize the vortex.

The [vorticity](wikipedia://en/166010) field $(\omega_r,\omega_\theta,\omega_z)$ associated with the Rankine vortex is

$$\omega_r=0,\quad \omega_\theta=0, \quad \omega_z = \begin{cases} 2\Omega & r \le a, \\ 0 & r > a \end{cases}.$$

At all points inside the core of the Rankine vortex, the vorticity is uniform at twice the angular velocity of the core; whereas vorticity is zero at all points outside the core because the flow there is irrotational.

In reality, vortex cores are not always circular; and vorticity is not exactly uniform throughout the vortex core.
