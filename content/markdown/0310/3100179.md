# Locally nilpotent

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Locally nilpotent"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* in a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *A* is **locally nilpotent** at a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) *p* if *I*<sub>*p*</sub>, the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) of *I* at *p*, is a [nilpotent ideal](wikipedia://en/6389159) in *A*<sub>*p*</sub>.[^o2]

:::

In non-commutative algebra and group theory, an algebra or group is locally nilpotent if and only if every finitely generated subalgebra or subgroup is nilpotent. The subgroup generated by the normal locally nilpotent subgroups is called the [Hirsch–Plotkin radical](wikipedia://en/22468906) and is the generalization of the [Fitting subgroup](wikipedia://en/1416042) to groups without the ascending chain condition on normal subgroups.

A locally nilpotent ring is one in which every finitely generated subring is nilpotent: locally nilpotent rings form a [radical class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_class), giving rise to the [Levitzki radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levitzki_radical).[^o2]
