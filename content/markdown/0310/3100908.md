# Vector fields on spheres

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector fields on spheres"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the discussion of **vector fields on spheres** was a classical problem of [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), beginning with the [hairy ball theorem](wikipedia://en/485168), and early work on the classification of [division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625).

:::

Specifically, the question is how many [linearly independent](wikipedia://en/101863) smooth nowhere-zero vector fields can be constructed on a [sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersphere) in $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). A definitive answer was provided in 1962 by [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959). It was already known,[^o2] by direct construction using [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305), that if $\rho(n)$ is the Radon-Hurwitz number of the n-sphere (definition below), then there were at least $\rho(n)-1$ such fields. Adams applied [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) and [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468)[^o3] to prove that no more independent vector fields could be found. Hence $\rho(n)-1$ is the exact number of pointwise linearly independent vector fields that exist on an ($n-1$)-dimensional sphere.

## Technical details {#Technical_details}

In detail, the question applies to the 'round spheres' and to their [tangent bundles](wikipedia://en/211794): in fact since all [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931) have isomorphic tangent bundles, the *Radon–Hurwitz numbers* $\rho(n)$ determine the maximum number of linearly independent sections of the tangent bundle of any [homotopy sphere](wikipedia://en/1202074). The case of $n$ odd is taken care of by the [Poincaré–Hopf index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré–Hopf_index_theorem) (see [hairy ball theorem](wikipedia://en/485168)), so the case $n$ even is an extension of that. Adams showed that the maximum number of continuous (*smooth* would be no different here) pointwise linearly-independent vector fields on the ($n-1$)-sphere is exactly $\rho(n)-1$.

The construction of the fields is related to the real [Clifford algebras](wikipedia://en/45305), which is a theory with a periodicity *modulo* 8 that also shows up here. By the [Gram–Schmidt process](wikipedia://en/82361), it is the same to ask for (pointwise) linear independence or fields that give an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) at each point.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Radon-Hurwitz number"}

## Radon–Hurwitz numbers {#Radon–Hurwitz_numbers}

The **Radon–Hurwitz numbers** $\rho(n)$ occur in earlier work of [Johann Radon](wikipedia://en/339331) (1922) and [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690) (1923) on the [Hurwitz problem](wikipedia://en/36830673) on [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478).[^o7] When we write

$$n = (2 a + 1) 2^{4d + c},$$

where $a,d,c$ are all integers and $0 \leq c < 4$, then[^o7]

$$\rho(n)=2^c+8d.$$

If $n$ is odd, then $c=d=0$ and $\rho(n) = 1$. The first few values of $\rho(2n)$ are (from ):

2, 4, 2, 8, 2, 4, 2, 9, 2, 4, 2, 8, 2, 4, 2, 10, ...

These numbers occur also in other, related areas. In [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/20556859), the Radon–Hurwitz number counts the maximum size of a linear subspace of the real $n\times n$ matrices, for which each non-zero matrix is a [similarity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarity_transformation_%28geometry%29), i.e. a product of an [orthogonal matrix](wikipedia://en/105620) and a [scalar matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_matrix). In [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478), the [Hurwitz problem](wikipedia://en/36830673) asks for multiplicative identities between quadratic forms. The classical results were revisited in 1952 by [Beno Eckmann](wikipedia://en/19613365). They are now applied in areas including [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028).
