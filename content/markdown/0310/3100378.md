# Ian G. Macdonald

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ian G. Macdonald"}

*Image: Ian Macdonald.jpg.* Macdonald at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach) in 1977

**Ian Grant Macdonald** (11 October 1928 – 8 August 2023) was a British mathematician known for his contributions to [symmetric functions](wikipedia://en/22510359), [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function), [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) theory and other aspects of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in London, he was educated at [Winchester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_College) and [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), graduating in 1952.

## Career {#Career}

He then spent five years as a civil servant. He was offered a position at [Manchester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_University) in 1957 by [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867), on the basis of work he had done while outside academia. In 1960 he moved to the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter), and in 1963 became a Fellow of [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford). Macdonald became [Fielden Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielden_Chair_of_Pure_Mathematics) at Manchester in 1972, and professor at [Queen Mary College, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary,_University_of_London), in 1976.

He worked on [symmetric products of algebraic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_products_of_algebraic_curves), [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371) and the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of groups over [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375). In 1972 he proved the [Macdonald identities](wikipedia://en/5027268), after a pattern known to [Freeman Dyson](wikipedia://en/11397). His 1979 book *Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials* has become a classic. Symmetric functions are an old theory, part of the [theory of equations](wikipedia://en/583600), to which both [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) lead. His was the first text to integrate much classical theory, such as [Hall polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_polynomial), [Schur functions](wikipedia://en/3147062), the [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359), with the [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) approach. It was both an expository work and, in part, a research monograph, and had a major impact in the field. The [Macdonald polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_polynomial) are now named after him. The [Macdonald conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macdonald_conjecture_%28disambiguation%29) from 1982 also proved most influential.

Macdonald was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1979. He was an invited speaker in 1970 at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in Nice[^o4] and a plenary speaker in 1998 at the ICM in Berlin.[^o5] In 1991 he received the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097).[^o6] In 2002 he received an honorary doctorate from the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam).[^o7] He was awarded the 2009 [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) for Mathematical Exposition. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Ian G. Macdonald died on 8 August 2023, at the age of 94.[^o10]
