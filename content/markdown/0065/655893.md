# Elwyn Berlekamp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elwyn Berlekamp"}

**Elwyn Ralph Berlekamp** (September 6, 1940 – April 9, 2019) was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o4][^o5] Berlekamp was widely known for his work in computer science, [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) and [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231).

:::

Berlekamp invented an [algorithm to factor polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlekamp%27s_algorithm) and the [Berlekamp switching game](wikipedia://en/64510569), and was one of the inventors of the [Berlekamp–Welch algorithm](wikipedia://en/31698015) and the [Berlekamp–Massey algorithms](wikipedia://en/514832), which are used to implement [Reed–Solomon error correction](wikipedia://en/45600). He also co-invented the [Berlekamp–Rabin algorithm](wikipedia://en/61379828), [Berlekamp–Zassenhaus algorithm](wikipedia://en/25744542), and the [Berlekamp–Van Lint–Seidel graph](wikipedia://en/59945606).

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Berlekamp was born in 1940, in [Dover, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover,_Ohio), the son of Waldo Berlekamp and Loretta Kimmel Berlekamp. Waldo was of German descent and a reverend in the [United Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ).[^o7]

His family moved to Northern Kentucky, where from 1954 Berlekamp attended [Fort Thomas Highlands High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlands_High_School_%28Fort_Thomas,_Kentucky%29) in [Fort Thomas, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Thomas,_Kentucky). He was elected class president and joined the swim team which practiced naked at the local YMCA pool; Berlekamp was the slowest swimmer but chose swimming because of the low level of competition compared to other sports.[^o8] He decided to attend the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) after learning it did not have an [American football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football) team. At MIT, his freshman professors included [John Forbes Nash Jr.](wikipedia://en/102567) and he was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) during his senior year in 1961.[^o8][^o9] He completed his bachelor's and master's degrees in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) in 1962. Berlekamp did [internships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internships) at Bell Labs in 1960 and 1962, where his boss was [John Larry Kelly Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Larry_Kelly_Jr.)[^o8] Continuing his studies at MIT, he finished his Ph.D. in electrical engineering in 1964; his advisors were [Robert G. Gallager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_G._Gallager), [Peter Elias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Elias), [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693), and [John Wozencraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wozencraft).

Berlekamp met his wife, Jennifer Wilson, in 1964 after juggling in his apartment and having to apologize for causing a noise disturbance.[^o8] They had two daughters and a son. He lived in [Piedmont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont,_California) and died in April 2019 at the age of 78 from complications of [pulmonary fibrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_fibrosis).[^o10]

## Career {#Career}

Berlekamp was a professor of electrical engineering at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) from 1964 until 1966, when he became a mathematics researcher at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs). In 1971, Berlekamp returned to Berkeley as professor of mathematics and computer science, where he served as the advisor for over twenty doctoral students.[^o4][^o5][^o12]

He was a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) (1977)[^o13] and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (1999).[^o14] He was elected a Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1996,[^o15] and became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o16] In 1991, he received the [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal),[^o17] and in 1993, the [Claude E. Shannon Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_E._Shannon_Award). In 1998, he received a Golden Jubilee Award for Technological Innovation from the [IEEE Information Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Information_Theory_Society).[^o18] Along with [Tom M. Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Malin_Rodgers)[^o19] he was one of the founders of [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) and was on its board for many years.[^o20] In the mid-1980s, he was president of [Cyclotomics, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomics,_Inc.), a corporation that developed error-correcting code technology.[^o4]

He studied various games, including [dots and boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dots_and_boxes), [fox and geese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_and_geese), and, especially, [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28game%29). Berlekamp and co-author [David Wolfe](wikipedia://en/58628756) described methods for analyzing certain classes of Go endgames in the book *Mathematical Go.*

## Berlekamp and Martin Gardner {#Berlekamp_and_Martin_Gardner}

Berlekamp was a member of the group of people around the *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* columnist [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), a close friend.[^o22] Berlekamp teamed up with [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) and [Richard K. Guy](wikipedia://en/816308), two other close associates of Gardner, to co-author the book *[Winning Ways for your Mathematical Plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winning_Ways_for_your_Mathematical_Plays)*, leading to his recognition as one of the founders of [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231).[^o23] The dedication of their book says, "To Martin Gardner, who has brought more mathematics to more millions than anyone else."[^o24]

Berlekamp and Gardner both supported [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446).[^o23] Conferences called [Gathering 4 Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gathering_4_Gardner) (G4G) are held every two years to celebrate the Gardner legacy.[^o22] Berlekamp was one of the founders of G4G and was on its board of directors for many years.[^o25]
