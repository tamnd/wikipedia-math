# Tridecagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tridecagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **tridecagon** or **triskaidecagon** or 13-gon is a thirteen-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).

:::

## Regular tridecagon {#Regular_tridecagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) tridecagon* is represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {13}.

The measure of each internal angle of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) tridecagon is approximately 152.308 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294), and the area with side length *a* is given by

$$A = \frac{13}{4}a^2 \cot \frac{\pi}{13} \simeq 13.1858\,a^2.$$

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

As 13 is a [Pierpont prime](wikipedia://en/2595060) but not a [Fermat prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime), the regular tridecagon cannot be [constructed](wikipedia://en/523430) using a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge). However, it is constructible using [neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339), or angle trisection.

The following is an animation from a *neusis construction* of a regular tridecagon with radius of circumcircle $\overline{OA} = 12,$ according to [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871),[^o5] based on the [angle trisection](wikipedia://en/91111) by means of the [Tomahawk](wikipedia://en/1735473) (light blue).

*Image: 01-Triskaidecagon-Animation.gif.* A neusis construction of a regular tridecagon (triskaidecagon) with radius of circumcircle $\overline{OA} = 12$ as an animation (1 min 44 s), angle trisection by means of the Tomahawk (light blue). This construction is derived from the following equation: $\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{13}\right)=\frac{1}{12}\left(2\sqrt{26-2\sqrt{13}}\cos\left(\frac{1}{3}\arctan\left(\frac{26+5\sqrt{13}}{9}\right)\right)+\sqrt{13}-1\right).$

:::

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries of tridecagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular tridecagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices and edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular tridecagon* has [Dih<sub>13</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 26. Since 13 is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) there is one subgroup with dihedral symmetry: Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 2 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>13</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

These 4 symmetries can be seen in 4 distinct symmetries on the tridecagon. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o8] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r26** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g13** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Numismatic use {#Numismatic_use}

The regular tridecagon is used as the shape of the [Czech 20 korun coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_of_the_Czech_koruna).[^o10]

## Related polygons {#Related_polygons}

A **tridecagram** is a 13-sided [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303). There are 5 regular forms given by [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394): {13/2}, {13/3}, {13/4}, {13/5}, and {13/6}. Since 13 is prime, none of the tridecagrams are compound figures.
