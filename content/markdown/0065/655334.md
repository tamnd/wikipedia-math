# Weak interpretability

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weak interpretability"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **weak interpretability** is a notion of translation of [logical theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_%28logic%29), introduced together with [interpretability](wikipedia://en/621176) by [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) in 1953.

:::

Let *T* and *S* be [formal theories](wikipedia://en/3512103). Slightly simplified, *T* is said to be **weakly interpretable** in *S* if, and only if, the language of *T* can be translated into the language of *S* in such a way that the translation of every [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of *T* is consistent with *S*. Of course, there are some natural conditions on admissible translations here, such as the necessity for a translation to preserve the logical structure of [formulas](wikipedia://en/404582).

A generalization of weak interpretability, [tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolerance_%28in_logic%29), was introduced by [Giorgi Japaridze](wikipedia://en/1866981) in 1992.
