# John Hammersley

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Hammersley"}

**John Michael Hammersley**, (21 March 1920 – 2 May 2004)[^o4][^o5] was a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his foundational work in the theory of [self-avoiding walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-avoiding_walks) and [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hammersley was born in [Helensburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helensburgh) in [Dunbartonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbartonshire), and educated at [Sedbergh School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedbergh_School). He started reading [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Emmanuel College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_College,_Cambridge) but was called up to join the [Royal Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Artillery) in 1941. During his time in the army he worked on [ballistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistics). He graduated in mathematics in 1948. He never studied for a PhD but was awarded an ScD by Cambridge University and a DSc by Oxford University in 1959.

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

*Image: Hammersley-Kesten.tif.* John Hammersley (left) with [Harry Kesten](wikipedia://en/19816546) in the Mathematical Institute, [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), 1993

With [Jillian Beardwood](wikipedia://en/63714069) and J.H. Halton, Hammersley is known for the Beardwood-Halton-Hammersley Theorem. Published by the [Cambridge Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Philosophical_Society) in a 1959 article entitled “The Shortest Path Through Many Points,” the theorem provides a practical solution to the “[traveling salesman problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traveling_salesman_problem).”[^o8]

He held a number of positions, both in and outside academia. His book *Monte Carlo Methods* with David Handscomb was published in 1964. He is known for devising an early solution to the [moving sofa problem](wikipedia://en/11413257) in 1968.

He was an advocate of [problem solving](wikipedia://en/1467948), and an opponent of abstraction in mathematics, taking part in the [New Math](wikipedia://en/404988) debate.

He was a fellow (later professorial fellow) of [Trinity College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford), from 1961, [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in mathematical statistics at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) from 1969, and elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 1976.
