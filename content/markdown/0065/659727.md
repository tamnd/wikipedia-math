# William Arveson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Arveson"}

**William B. Arveson** (22 November 1934 – 15 November 2011) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [operator algebras](wikipedia://en/455987) who worked as a professor of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Arveson obtained his Ph.D. from [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) in 1964 with thesis advisor [Henry Dye](wikipedia://en/58105253) and thesis *Prediction theory and group representations*.

Of particular note is Arveson's work on [completely positive maps](wikipedia://en/6083511). One of his earlier results in this area is an extension theorem for completely positive [maps](wikipedia://en/516931) with values in the algebra of all [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).[^o5] This theorem led naturally to the question of injectivity of [von-Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) algebras in general, which culminated in work by [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340) relating injectivity to hyperfiniteness.

One of the major features of Arveson's work was the use of algebras of operators to elucidate single operator theory. In a series of papers in the 1960s and 1970s, Arveson introduced [noncommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative) analogues of several concepts from classical [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) including the Shilov and Choquet boundaries and used them very successfully in single operator theory.[^o6]

In a highly cited paper,[^o7] Arveson made a systematic study of commutative subspace lattices, which yield a large class of nonselfadjoint operator algebras and proved among other results, the theorem that a transitive algebra containing a maximal abelian von Neumann subalgebra in B(H) must be trivial.

In the late 80's and 90's, Arveson played a leading role in developing the theory of one-parameter semigroups of \*-endomorphisms on [von Neumann algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_algebras) - also known as [E-semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-semigroups). Among his achievements, he introduced [product systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_system) and proved that they are complete invariants of E-semigroups up to cocycle conjugacy.
