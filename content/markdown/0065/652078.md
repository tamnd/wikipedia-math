# Second fundamental form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Second fundamental form"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **second fundamental form** (or **shape tensor**) is a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478) on the [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane) of a [smooth surface](wikipedia://en/15513875) in the three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), usually denoted by $\mathrm{I\!I}$ (read "two"). Together with the [first fundamental form](wikipedia://en/510355), it serves to define extrinsic invariants of the surface, its [principal curvatures](wikipedia://en/2072472). More generally, such a quadratic form is defined for a smooth immersed [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) in a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652).

:::

## Surface in R<sup>3</sup> {#Surface_in_R3}

*Image: Second fundamental form.svg.* Definition of second fundamental form

### Motivation {#Motivation}

The second fundamental form of a [parametric surface](wikipedia://en/3400953) $S$ in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ was introduced and studied by [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125). First suppose that the surface is the graph of a twice [continuously differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_differentiable) function, $z = f(x,y)$, and that the plane $z = 0$ is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to the surface at the origin. Then $f$ and its [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) with respect to $x$ and $y$ vanish at (0,0). Therefore, the [Taylor expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_expansion) of *f* at (0,0) starts with quadratic terms:

$$z=L\frac{x^2}{2} + Mxy + N\frac{y^2}{2} + \text{higher order terms}\,,$$

and the second fundamental form at the origin in the coordinates $(x,y)$ is the [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/251478)

$$L \, dx^2 + 2M \, dx \, dy + N \, dy^2 \,.$$

For a smooth point $P$ on $S$, one can choose the coordinate system so that the plane $z = 0$ is tangent to $S$ at $P$, and define the second fundamental form in the same way.

### Classical notation {#Classical_notation}

The second fundamental form of a general parametric surface is defined as follows. Let $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}(u,v)$ be a regular parametrization of a surface in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$, where $\mathbf{r}$ is a smooth [vector-valued function](wikipedia://en/3675281) of two variables. It is common to denote the partial derivatives of $\mathbf{r}$ with respect to $u$ and $v$ by $\mathbf{r}_{u}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{v}$. Regularity of the parametrization means that $\mathbf{r}_{u}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{v}$ are linearly independent for any $(u,v)$ in the domain of $\mathbf{r}$, and hence span the tangent plane to $S$ at each point. Equivalently, the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) $\mathbf{r}_{u} \times \mathbf{r}_{v}$ is a nonzero vector normal to the surface. The parametrization thus defines a field of unit normal vectors $\mathbf{n}$:

$$\mathbf{n} = \frac{\mathbf{r}_u\times\mathbf{r}_v}{|\mathbf{r}_u\times\mathbf{r}_v|} \,.$$

The second fundamental form is usually written as

$$\mathrm{I\!I} = L\, du^2 + 2M\, du\, dv + N\, dv^2 \,;$$

its matrix in the basis $\{\mathbf{r}_{u}, \mathbf{r}_{v}\}$ of the tangent plane is

$$\begin{bmatrix} L&M\\ M&N \end{bmatrix} \,.$$

The coefficients $L, M, N$ at a given point in the parametric $uv$-plane are given by the projections of the second partial derivatives of $\mathbf{r}$ at that point onto the normal line to $S$ and can be computed with the aid of the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) as follows:

$$L = \mathbf{r}_{uu} \cdot \mathbf{n}\,, \quad M = \mathbf{r}_{uv} \cdot \mathbf{n}\,, \quad N = \mathbf{r}_{vv} \cdot \mathbf{n}\,.$$

For a [signed distance field](wikipedia://en/5660713) of [Hessian](wikipedia://en/412108) $\mathbf{H}$, the second fundamental form coefficients can be computed as follows:

$$L = -\mathbf{r}_u \cdot \mathbf{H} \cdot \mathbf{r}_u\,, \quad M = -\mathbf{r}_u \cdot \mathbf{H} \cdot \mathbf{r}_v\,, \quad N = -\mathbf{r}_v \cdot \mathbf{H} \cdot \mathbf{r}_v\,.$$

### Physicist's notation {#Physicist's_notation}

The second fundamental form of a general parametric surface $S$ is defined as follows.

Let $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}(u^{1},u^{2})$ be a regular parametrization of a surface in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$, where $\mathbf{r}$ is a smooth [vector-valued function](wikipedia://en/3675281) of two variables. It is common to denote the partial derivatives of $\mathbf{r}$ with respect to $u^{\alpha}$ by $\mathbf{r}_{\alpha}$, $\alpha = 1, 2$. Regularity of the parametrization means that $\mathbf{r}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{2}$ are linearly independent for any $(u^{1},u^{2})$ in the domain of $\mathbf{r}$, and hence span the tangent plane to $S$ at each point. Equivalently, the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) $\mathbf{r}_{1} \times \mathbf{r}_{2}$ is a nonzero vector normal to the surface. The parametrization thus defines a field of unit normal vectors $\mathbf{n}$:

$$\mathbf{n} = \frac{\mathbf{r}_1\times\mathbf{r}_2}{|\mathbf{r}_1\times\mathbf{r}_2|}\,.$$

The second fundamental form is usually written as

$$\mathrm{I\!I} = b_{\alpha \beta} \, du^{\alpha} \, du^{\beta} \,.$$

The equation above uses the [Einstein summation convention](wikipedia://en/195407).

The coefficients $b_{\alpha\beta}$ at a given point in the parametric $u^{1}u^{2}$-plane are given by the projections of the second partial derivatives of $\mathbf{r}$ at that point onto the normal line to $S$ and can be computed in terms of the normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ as follows:

$$b_{\alpha \beta} = r_{,\alpha \beta}^{\ \ \,\gamma} n_{\gamma}\,.$$

## Hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold {#Hypersurface_in_a_Riemannian_manifold}

In [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the second fundamental form is given by

$$\mathrm{I\!I}(v,w) = -\langle d\nu(v),w\rangle$$

where $\nu$ is the [Gauss map](wikipedia://en/378881), and $d\nu$ the [differential](wikipedia://en/1558510) of $\nu$ regarded as a [vector-valued differential form](wikipedia://en/3772116), and the brackets denote the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) of Euclidean space.

More generally, on a Riemannian manifold, the second fundamental form is an equivalent way to describe the [shape operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_operator) (denoted by $S$) of a hypersurface,

$$\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(v,w)=\langle S(v),w\rangle = -\langle \nabla_v n,w\rangle=\langle n,\nabla_v w\rangle \,,$$

where $\nabla_{v}w$ denotes the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) of the ambient manifold and $n$ a field of normal vectors on the hypersurface. (If the [affine connection](wikipedia://en/607690) is [torsion-free](wikipedia://en/4342484), then the second fundamental form is symmetric.)

The sign of the second fundamental form depends on the choice of direction of $n$ (which is called a co-orientation of the hypersurface - for surfaces in Euclidean space, this is equivalently given by a choice of [orientation](wikipedia://en/187446) of the surface).

### Generalization to arbitrary codimension {#Generalization_to_arbitrary_codimension}

The second fundamental form can be generalized to arbitrary [codimension](wikipedia://en/525149). In that case it is a quadratic form on the tangent space with values in the [normal bundle](wikipedia://en/2370200) and it can be defined by

$$\mathrm{I\!I}(v,w)=(\nabla_v w)^\bot\,,$$

where $(\nabla_v w)^\bot$ denotes the orthogonal projection of [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) $\nabla_v w$ onto the normal bundle.

In [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the [curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) of a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) can be described by the following formula:

$$\langle R(u,v)w,z\rangle =\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(u,z)\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(v,w)-\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(u,w)\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(v,z).$$

This is called the **[Gauss equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Codazzi_equation)**, as it may be viewed as a generalization of Gauss's [Theorema Egregium](wikipedia://en/259906).

For general Riemannian manifolds one has to add the curvature of ambient space; if $N$ is a manifold embedded in a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M,g)$ then the curvature tensor $R_{N}$ of $N$ with induced metric can be expressed using the second fundamental form and $R_{M}$, the curvature tensor of $M$:

$$\langle R_N(u,v)w,z\rangle = \langle R_M(u,v)w,z\rangle+\langle \mathrm I\!\mathrm I(u,z),\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(v,w)\rangle-\langle \mathrm I\!\mathrm I(u,w),\mathrm I\!\mathrm I(v,z)\rangle\,.$$

## Example {#Example}

- The second fundamental form of a plane is identically zero. A plane has a constant unit normal vector $\mathbf{n}$, so the second derivatives $\mathbf{r}_{uu}, \mathbf{r}_{uv}, \mathbf{r}_{vv}$ are tangent to the surface. Consequently, all coefficients $L, M, N$ in the second fundamental form are zero, and $\mathrm{I\!I} = 0$.
- The second fundamental form of the unit sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ in the local coordinates $\mathbf{r}(u,v) = (\sin u \cos v, \sin u \sin v, \cos u)$ is $\mathrm{I\!I} = -du^2 - \sin^2 u dv^2$. The outward unit normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ coincides with $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$. Computing dot products of $\mathbf{n}$ with $\mathbf{r}_{uu}, \mathbf{r}_{uv}, \mathbf{r}_{vv}$ yields $L=-1,\, M=0,\, N=-\sin^2 u$.
