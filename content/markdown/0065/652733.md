# Pronic number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pronic number"}

A **pronic number** is a number that is the product of two consecutive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), that is, a number of the form $n(n+1)$.[^o2] The study of these numbers dates back to [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308). They are also called **oblong numbers**, **heteromecic numbers**,[^o3] or **rectangular numbers**;[^o7] however, the term "rectangular number" has also been applied to the [composite numbers](wikipedia://en/82289).[^o4][^o5]

:::

The first 60 pronic numbers are:

[0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_%28number%29), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), [12](wikipedia://en/208155), [20](wikipedia://en/208259), [30](wikipedia://en/341668), [42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42_%28number%29), [56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/56_%28number%29), [72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72_%28number%29), [90](wikipedia://en/343340), [110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_%28number%29), [132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/132_%28number%29), 156, 182, 210, 240, 272, 306, 342, 380, [420](wikipedia://en/27033107), 462, 506, 552, 600, 650, 702, 756, 812, 870, 930, 992, 1056, 1122, 1190, 1260, 1332, 1406, 1482, 1560, 1640, 1722, 1806, 1892, 1980, 2070, 2162, 2256, 2352, 2450, 2550, 2652, 2756, 2862, 2970, 3080, 3192, 3306, 3422, 3540, 3660... .

Letting $P_n$ denote the pronic number $n(n+1)$, we have $P_{{-}n} = P_{n{-}1}$. Therefore, in discussing pronic numbers, we may assume that $n\geq 0$ [without loss of generality](wikipedia://en/296000), a convention that is adopted in the following sections.

## As figurate numbers {#As_figurate_numbers}

*Image: Illustration of Triangular Number Leading to a Rectangle.svg.* Twice a triangular number is a pronic number

*Image: Illustration of Pronic Number n^2 and nplus1^2.svg.* The $n$th pronic number is $n$ more than the $n$th [square number](wikipedia://en/213919) and $n$+1 less than the ($n$+1)st square

The $n$th pronic number is the sum of the first $n$ [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_and_odd_numbers) integers, and as such is twice the $n$th triangular number[^o2][^o3] and $n$ more than the $n$th [square number](wikipedia://en/213919), as given by the alternative formula $n^{2} + n$ for pronic numbers. Hence the $n$th pronic number and the $n$th square number (the sum of the [first $n$ odd integers](wikipedia://en/213919#Properties)) form a [superparticular ratio](wikipedia://en/834100):

$$\frac{n(n+1)}{n^2} = \frac{n + 1}{n}$$

Due to this ratio, the $n$th pronic number is at a radius of $n$ and $n$ + 1 from a perfect square, and the $n$th perfect square is at a radius of $n$ from a pronic number. The $n$th pronic number is also the difference between the [odd square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_and_odd_numbers) $(2n + 1)^{2}$ and the $(n+1)$st [centered hexagonal number](wikipedia://en/497485).

Since the number of off-diagonal entries in a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) is twice a triangular number, it is a pronic number.[^o8]

## Sum of pronic numbers {#Sum_of_pronic_numbers}

The partial sum of the first $n$ positive pronic numbers is twice the value of the $n$th [tetrahedral number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_number):

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n} k(k+1) =\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{3}= 2T_n.$$

The sum of the reciprocals of the positive pronic numbers (excluding 0) is a [telescoping series](wikipedia://en/508070) that sums to 1:[^o10]

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{i(i+1)}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{20}\cdots=1.$$

The [partial sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sum) of the first $n$ terms in this series is[^o10]

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{i(i+1)} =\frac{n}{n+1}.$$

The alternating sum of the reciprocals of the positive pronic numbers (excluding 0) is a [convergent series](wikipedia://en/1743431):

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{i(i+1)}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{20}\cdots=\log(4)-1.$$

## In quantum mechanics {#In_quantum_mechanics}

In [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), a wave-particle's [spin angular momentum](wikipedia://en/19593829) $L$, specifically its total spin magnitude, is defined by the [reduced Planck constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_Planck_constant) $\hbar=\frac{h}{2\pi}$ times the square root of a half-integer pronic number $\frac{n}{2}$:[^o12]

$$L=\hbar\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{n}{2}+1)}.$$

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

Pronic numbers are even, and 2 is the only [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) pronic number. It is also the only pronic number in the [Fibonacci sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) and the only pronic [Lucas number](wikipedia://en/398404).[^o14][^o15]

The [arithmetic mean](wikipedia://en/612) of two consecutive pronic numbers is a [square number](wikipedia://en/213919):

$$\frac {n(n+1) + (n+1)(n+2)}{2} = (n+1)^2$$

So there is a square between any two consecutive pronic numbers. It is unique, since

$$n^2 \leq n(n+1) < (n+1)^2 < (n+1)(n+2) < (n+2)^2.$$

Another consequence of this chain of inequalities is the following property. If $m$ is a pronic number, then the following holds:

$$\lfloor{\sqrt{m}}\rfloor \cdot \lceil{\sqrt{m}}\rceil = m.$$

The fact that consecutive integers are [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) and that a pronic number is the product of two consecutive integers leads to a number of properties. Each distinct prime factor of a pronic number is present in only one of the factors $n$ or $n + 1$. Thus a pronic number is [squarefree](wikipedia://en/29525) if and only if $n$ and $n + 1$ are also squarefree. The number of distinct prime factors of a pronic number is the sum of the number of distinct prime factors of $n$ and $n + 1$.

If 25 is appended to the [decimal representation](wikipedia://en/3293594) of any pronic number, the result is a square number, the square of a number ending on 5; for example, 625 = 25<sup>2</sup> and 1225 = 35<sup>2</sup>. This is so because

$$100n(n+1) + 25 = 100n^2 + 100n + 25 = (10n+5)^2.$$

The difference between two consecutive [unit fractions](wikipedia://en/491316) is the reciprocal of a pronic number:[^o16]

$$\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{(n+1) - n}{n(n+1)} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)}$$
