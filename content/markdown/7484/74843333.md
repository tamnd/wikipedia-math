# Juan Mario Restrepo

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Juan Mario Restrepo"}

**Juan Mario Restrepo** (born September 4, 1961) is an American mathematician recognized for his contributions to [ocean dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_dynamics), [data assimilation](wikipedia://en/2458875), and computational [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481). In ocean dynamics Restrepo is best known for his work in [wave–current interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave–current_interaction).[^o4] He was first to introduce the use of stochastic processes to capture wave breaking dissipation,[^o5] provided fundamental understanding of transient wave transport[^o6] and predicted the phenomenon of 'sticky waters' [^o7] in nearshore waters. He is the son of the Colombian artist [Pedro Restrepo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Restrepo).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Restrepo majored in music at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University), graduating in 1983. After studying [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), he earned a master's degree in engineering acoustics at the [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) in 1987, where he completed a Ph.D. in physics in 1992.[^o9] His dissertation, *Model for the Formation and Evolution of Sand Ridges on the Continental Shelf*, was supervised by [Jerry L. Bona](wikipedia://en/21528082).[^o2]

After postdoctoral research at the [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory) and [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), he became an assistant professor of mathematics at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) in 1997. He moved up the ranks to full professor in 2009, also adding joint appointments in physics and atmospheric science. From 2014 to 2020 he was a professor of mathematics at [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University), with joint appointments in the Department of Statistics and in the Department of Oceans and Atmospheres. He moved to [Oak Ridge National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge_National_Laboratory) in 2020, to become Distinguished Member of the R&D Staff, and section head for the Mathematics and Computation Section.[^o9]

He has served as vice chair of [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) Geosciences Section, chair of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society) Focus Group on Climate, President of the Nonlinear Geophysics Section at [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union), and many committee assignments in SIAM. He is an associate editor at the International Journal of Uncertainty Quantification, [Nonlinear Processes in Geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_Processes_in_Geophysics), and Foundations of Data Sciences. He is also Joint Faculty Professor in the Mathematics Department at the [University of Tennessee, Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tennessee,_Knoxville).[^o10]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Restrepo's awards include the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) ([SIAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM)), Geosciences Career Prize 2017, and a [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy) Young Investigator Award, 2003. He is a fellow of [SIAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM), and a fellow of the [American Physical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society).
