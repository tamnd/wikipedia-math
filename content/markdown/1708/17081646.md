# Richard Meyer (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Meyer"}

**Richard Ernst Meyer** (23 March 1919 – 16 January 2008) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).

:::

Born in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), Meyer received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [aeronautical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_engineering) from [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich) in 1946. He worked at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) and in 1953, he got a job as a senior lecturer in aeronautical engineering at the [University of Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sydney). He studied turbines, which led him to the research of supersonic aeronautics and the theory of characteristics. Meyer did both theoretical and experimental research; he set up a supersonic laboratory in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia). He was elected into the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 1956.

In 1957, Meyer moved to the department of applied mathematics at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University). By this time, he had also become interested in the theory of [water waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_wave). Additionally, he looked into theoretical aspects of hyperbolic and singular [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [asymptotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotics). Meyer became a professor of mathematics at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1964, where he worked until he was named professor emeritus in 1994.

Meyer died on 16 January 2008.
