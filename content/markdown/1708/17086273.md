# James O. Berger

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James O. Berger"}

**James Orvis Berger** (born April 6, 1950, in [Minneapolis, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis,_Minnesota))[^o3] is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) best known for his work on [Bayesian statistics](wikipedia://en/404412) and [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216). He won the [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483), one of the two highest awards in statistics, in 1985 at the age of 35. He received a Ph.D. in mathematics from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1974. He was a faculty member in the Department of Statistics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) until 1997, at which time he moved to the Institute of Statistics and Decision Sciences (now the Department of Statistical Science) at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), where he is currently the Arts and Sciences Professor of Statistics. He was also director of the [Statistical and Applied Mathematical Sciences Institute](wikipedia://en/41998171) from 2002 to 2010, and has been a visiting professor at the University of Chicago since 2011.[^o3][^o4][^o5] In 2024–25, Berger has been elected as a Hagler Fellow at the [Hagler Institute for Advanced Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagler_Institute_for_Advanced_Study) at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University).[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Contributions to science {#Contributions_to_science}

Berger has worked on the decision theoretic bases of [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571), including advances on the [Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stein_%28statistician%29) phenomenon[^o9][^o10] during and after his thesis. He has also greatly contributed to advances in the so-called [objective Bayes](wikipedia://en/472877#Uninformative_priors) approach where [prior distributions](wikipedia://en/472877) are constructed from the structure of the [sampling distributions](wikipedia://en/160361) and/or of [frequentist properties](wikipedia://en/15537745). He is also recognized for his analysis of the opposition between Bayesian and frequentist visions on [testing statistical hypotheses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), with criticisms of the use of [p-values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-values)[^o11] and critical levels.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Berger has received numerous awards for his work: [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship), the [COPSS Presidents' Award](wikipedia://en/11690483) and the [R. A. Fisher Lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Fisher_Lectureship). He was elected as a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) and to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2003.[^o13] In 2004, he was awarded an honorary Doctor of Science degree by [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University).[^o14]
