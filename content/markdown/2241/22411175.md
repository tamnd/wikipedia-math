# Michael Aizenman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Aizenman"}

**Michael Aizenman** (מיכאל אייזנמן; born 28 August 1945) is an American-Israeli [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), working in the fields of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

:::

The highlights of his work include: the [triviality](wikipedia://en/22003136) of a class of [scalar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_%28physics%29) [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267) in [more than three dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_dimensions); a description of the [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) in the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) in three and more dimensions; the sharpness of the [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) in [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805); a method for the study of spectral and dynamical localization for random [Schrödinger operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator); and insights concerning [conformal invariance](wikipedia://en/173181) in two-dimensional percolation.[^o3]

## Biography {#Biography}

Aizenman is a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish)[^o5] American - Israeli who was born in Russia. He was an undergraduate at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). He was awarded his PhD in 1975 at [Yeshiva University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshiva_University) (Belfer Graduate School of Science), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), with advisor [Joel Lebowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Lebowitz). After [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) appointments at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) (1974–75), and Princeton University (1975–1977), with [Elliott H. Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb), he was appointed [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) at Princeton. In 1982 he moved to [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) as [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) and then [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_professor). In 1987 he moved to the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute) and in 1990 returned to [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as professor of mathematics and physics. He was several times a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), in 1984–85, 1991–92, and 1997–98,[^o6] and is a regular visiting scholar at the [Weizmann Institute of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute_of_Science).

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

- Norbert Wiener Prize (1990) of the [Amer. Math. Soc.](wikipedia://en/198822) and [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) for "his outstanding contribution of original and non-[perturbative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbative) mathematical methods in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) by means of which he was able to solve several long open important problems concerning critical phenomena, phase transitions, and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267)."
- [Brouwer Medal](wikipedia://en/42578624) (2002)[^o8] of the Dutch Math. Soc. and the [Royal Dutch Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences)
- Dannie Heineman Prize in Mathematical Physics (2010), of [APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physics_Society) and [AIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Physics)
- [Henri Poincaré Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincare_Prize) (2018) of IAMP.

Aizenman received [honorary degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_degree) (DHC) from [Université de Cergy-Pontoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cergy-Pontoise_University) (2009) and [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion) (2018), and is a member of [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) (1997), [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (2017), and Academia Europaea (2016).

During 2001-2012 he served as the [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of [Communications in Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/23886833).
