# T. H. C. Stevenson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="T. H. C. Stevenson"}

**Thomas Henry Craig Stevenson** [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBE) (24 November 1870[^o2] – 12 September 1932) was a Northern Irish statistician.

:::

He was born in [Strabane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabane), [County Tyrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Tyrone), and educated at Strabane Academy, [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin), and [University College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_London), before receiving his MB at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). He set up in practice and read for an MD in State Medicine and was later offered a post in the Brighton Public Health Department. After posts in public health elsewhere, he became the School Medical Officer of [Somerset County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerset_County_Council). In 1909, he was appointed Superintendent of Statistics in the General Register Office.[^o3]

Responsible for the 1911 census in the UK which was published in 1913, he originated the idea of social class classification, divided into three basic classes (the upper, middle and working classes) commonly known as the Registrar General's Social Classes.[^o4][^o5]

He was awarded the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold by the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) in 1920, the [Edward Jenner Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jenner_Medal) by the [Royal Society of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Medicine) in 1931, and the [Bisset Hawkins Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisset_Hawkins_Medal) by the [Royal College of Physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians) in 1932. He was appointed a [CBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) in 1919.[^o6]
