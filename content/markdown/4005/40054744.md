# Auxiliary line

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Auxiliary line"}

An **auxiliary line** (or **helping line**) is an extra [line](wikipedia://en/946975) needed to complete a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) in [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry).[^o2] Other common auxiliary constructs in elementary plane synthetic geometry are the helping circles.

:::

*Image: Angles of triangle add up to 180 degrees.png.*

As an example, a proof of the theorem on the [sum of angles of a triangle](wikipedia://en/5407025) can be done by adding a straight line parallel to one of the triangle sides (passing through the opposite vertex).[^o3]

Although the adding of auxiliary constructs can often make a problem obvious, it's not at all obvious to discover the helpful construct among all the possibilities, and for this reason many prefer to use more systematic methods for the solution of geometric problems (such as the coordinate method, which requires much less ingenuity).
