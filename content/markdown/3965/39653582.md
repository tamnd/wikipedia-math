# Spherical basis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical basis"}

In [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), particularly [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) and [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) and their applications, a **spherical basis** is the [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) used to express **[spherical tensors](wikipedia://en/39654522)**. The spherical basis closely relates to the description of [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839) in quantum mechanics and [spherical harmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonic) functions.

:::

While [spherical polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_polar_coordinates) are one [orthogonal coordinate system](wikipedia://en/5006088) for expressing vectors and tensors using polar and azimuthal angles and radial distance, the spherical basis are constructed from the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) and use [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

## In three dimensions {#In_three_dimensions}

A vector **A** in 3D [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ can be expressed in the familiar [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) in the [standard basis](wikipedia://en/462005) **e**<sub>*x*</sub>, **e**<sub>*y*</sub>, **e**<sub>*z*</sub>, and [coordinates](wikipedia://en/879358) *A<sub>x</sub>*, *A<sub>y</sub>*, *A<sub>z</sub>*:

::: {#o3 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\mathbf{A} = A_x \mathbf{e}_x + A_y \mathbf{e}_y + A_z \mathbf{e}_z \tag{1}$$

:::

or any other [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931) with associated [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) set of vectors. From this extend the scalars to allow multiplication by complex numbers, so that we are now working in $\mathbb{C}^3$ rather than $\mathbb{R}^3$.

### Basis definition {#Basis_definition}

In the spherical bases denoted **e**<sub>\+</sub>, **e**<sub>−</sub>, **e**<sub>0</sub>, and associated coordinates with respect to this basis, denoted *A*<sub>\+</sub>, *A*<sub>−</sub>, *A*<sub>0</sub>, the vector **A** is:

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\mathbf{A} = A_+ \mathbf{e}_{+} + A_{-} \mathbf{e}_{-} + A_0 \mathbf{e}_0 \tag{2}$$

:::

where the spherical basis vectors can be defined in terms of the Cartesian basis using [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued coefficients in the *xy* plane:[^o6]

::: {#o7 .equation cue=numblk name="3A"}

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{e}_+ & = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \mathbf{e}_x -\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbf{e}_y \\ \mathbf{e}_{-} & = +\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbf{e}_x - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbf{e}_y \\ \end{aligned} \quad \rightleftharpoons \quad \mathbf{e}_\pm = \mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{e}_x \pm i\mathbf{e}_y\right)\, \tag{3A}$$

:::

in which $i$ denotes the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358), and one normal to the plane in the *z* direction:

$$\mathbf{e}_0 = \mathbf{e}_z$$

The inverse relations are:

::: {#o8 .equation cue=numblk name="3B"}

$$\begin{aligned}\mathbf{e}_x & = - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \mathbf{e}_+ + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbf{e_{-}} \\ \mathbf{e}_y & = + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} \mathbf{e}_+ + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbf{e_{-}} \\ \mathbf{e}_z & = \mathbf{e}_0 \end{aligned} \tag{3B}$$

:::

### Commutator definition {#Commutator_definition}

While giving a basis in a 3-dimensional space is a valid definition for a spherical tensor, it only covers the case for when the rank $k$ is 1. For higher ranks, one may use either the commutator, or rotation definition of a spherical tensor. The commutator definition is given below, any operator $T_q^{(k)}$ that satisfies the following relations is a spherical tensor:

$$[J_\pm, T_q^{(k)}] = \hbar \sqrt{(k\mp q)(k\pm q+1)}T_{q\pm 1}^{(k)}$$

$$[J_z, T_q^{(k)}] = \hbar q T_q^{(k)}$$

### Rotation definition {#Rotation_definition}

Analogously to how the [spherical harmonics](wikipedia://en/203056) transform under a rotation, a general spherical tensor transforms as follows, when the states transform under the [unitary](wikipedia://en/225999) [Wigner D-matrix](wikipedia://en/7739570) $\mathcal{D}(R)$, where $R$ is a (3×3 rotation) group element in [SO(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%283%29). That is, these matrices represent the rotation group elements. With the help of its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), one can show these two definitions are equivalent.

$$\mathcal{D}(R)T_q^{(k)}\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(R) = \sum_{q' = -k}^k T_{q'}^{(k)} \mathcal{D}_{q' q}^{(k)}$$

### Coordinate vectors {#Coordinate_vectors}

For the spherical basis, the [coordinates](wikipedia://en/879358) are complex-valued numbers *A*<sub>\+</sub>, *A*<sub>0</sub>, *A*<sub>−</sub>, and can be found by substitution of ((3B)) into ((1)), or directly calculated from the [inner product](wikipedia://en/157093#Complex_vectors) $\langle$, $\rangle$ ((5)):

::: {#o12 .equation cue=numblk name="4A"}

$$\begin{aligned} A_+ & = \left\langle \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{e}_+ \right\rangle = -\frac{A_x}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{iA_y}{\sqrt{2}} \\ A_{-} & = \left\langle \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{e}_- \right\rangle = +\frac{A_x}{\sqrt{2}} + \frac{iA_y}{\sqrt{2}} \\ \end{aligned} \quad \rightleftharpoons \quad A_\pm = \left\langle \mathbf{e}_\pm, \mathbf{A} \right\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left(\mp A_x + iA_y \right) \tag{4A}$$

:::

$$A_0 = \left\langle \mathbf{e}_0, \mathbf{A} \right\rangle = \left\langle \mathbf{e}_z, \mathbf{A} \right\rangle = A_z$$

with inverse relations:

::: {#o13 .equation cue=numblk name="4B"}

$$\begin{aligned} A_x & = - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} A_+ + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} A_{-} \\ A_y & = - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} A_+ - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} A_{-} \\ A_z & = A_0 \end{aligned} \tag{4B}$$

:::

In general, for two vectors with complex coefficients in the same real-valued orthonormal basis **e**<sub>*i*</sub>, with the property **e**<sub>*i*</sub>·**e**<sub>*j*</sub> = *δ<sub>ij</sub>*, the [inner product](wikipedia://en/157093#Complex_vectors) is:

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$\left\langle \mathbf{a} , \mathbf{b} \right\rangle = \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}^\star = \sum_j a_j b_j^\star \tag{5}$$

:::

where · is the usual [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) and the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) \* must be used to keep the [magnitude (or "norm")](wikipedia://en/990534) of the vector [positive definite](wikipedia://en/3142115).

## Properties (three dimensions) {#Properties_(three_dimensions)}

### Orthonormality {#Orthonormality}

The spherical basis is an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063), since the [inner product](wikipedia://en/157093#Complex_vectors) $\langle$, $\rangle$ ((5)) of every pair vanishes meaning the basis vectors are all mutually [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221):

$$\left\langle \mathbf{e}_+ , \mathbf{e}_{-} \right\rangle = \left\langle \mathbf{e}_{-} , \mathbf{e}_0 \right\rangle = \left\langle \mathbf{e}_0 , \mathbf{e}_+ \right\rangle = 0$$

and each basis vector is a [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053):

$$\left\langle\mathbf{e}_+ , \mathbf{e}_{+} \right\rangle = \left\langle\mathbf{e}_{-} , \mathbf{e}_{-} \right\rangle = \left\langle\mathbf{e}_0 , \mathbf{e}_0 \right\rangle = 1$$

hence the need for the normalizing factors of $1/\!\sqrt{2}$.

### Change of basis matrix {#Change_of_basis_matrix}

The defining relations ((3A)) can be summarized by a [transformation matrix](wikipedia://en/692458) **U**:

$$\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e}_+ \\ \mathbf{e}_{-} \\ \mathbf{e}_0 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{U}\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e}_x \\ \mathbf{e}_y \\ \mathbf{e}_z \end{pmatrix} \,,\quad \mathbf{U} = \begin{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\,,$$

with inverse:

$$\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e}_x \\ \mathbf{e}_y \\ \mathbf{e}_z \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{U}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e}_+ \\ \mathbf{e}_{-} \\ \mathbf{e}_0 \end{pmatrix} \,,\quad \mathbf{U}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\,.$$

It can be seen that **U** is a [unitary matrix](wikipedia://en/129222), in other words its [Hermitian conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_conjugate) **U**<sup>†</sup> ([complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) and [matrix transpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_transpose)) is also the [inverse matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix) **U**<sup>−1</sup>.

For the coordinates:

$$\begin{pmatrix} A_+ \\ A_{-} \\ A_0 \end{pmatrix} =\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{*} \begin{pmatrix} A_x \\ A_y \\ A_z \end{pmatrix} \,,\quad \mathbf{U}^\mathrm{*} = \begin{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\,,$$

and inverse:

$$\begin{pmatrix} A_x \\ A_y \\ A_z \end{pmatrix} = (\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{*})^{-1} \begin{pmatrix} A_+ \\ A_{-} \\ A_0 \end{pmatrix} \,,\quad (\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{*})^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & - \frac{i}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\,.$$

### Cross products {#Cross_products}

Taking [cross products](wikipedia://en/157092) of the spherical basis vectors, we find an obvious relation:

$$\mathbf{e}_{q}\times\mathbf{e}_{q} = \boldsymbol{0}$$

where *q* is a placeholder for +, −, 0, and two less obvious relations:

$$\mathbf{e}_{\pm}\times\mathbf{e}_{\mp} = \pm i \mathbf{e}_0$$

$$\mathbf{e}_{\pm}\times\mathbf{e}_{0} = \pm i \mathbf{e}_{\pm}$$

### Inner product in the spherical basis {#Inner_product_in_the_spherical_basis}

The inner product between two vectors **A** and **B** in the spherical basis follows from the above definition of the inner product:

$$\left\langle \mathbf{A} , \mathbf{B} \right\rangle = A_+ B_+^\star + A_{-}B_{-}^\star + A_0 B_0^\star$$
