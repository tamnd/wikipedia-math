# Mellen Woodman Haskell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mellen Woodman Haskell"}

**Mellen Woodman Haskell** (March 17, 1863 – January 15, 1948) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), and applications of group theory to geometry.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

After secondary education at [Roxbury Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxbury_Latin_School), he received in 1883 his bachelor's degree and in 1885 his [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) and a Parker Traveling Fellowship from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). From 1885 to 1889 he studied mathematics at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where in 1889 he received, under [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887), his [Dr. phil.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._phil.)[^o4] In 1889 Haskell became an instructor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).

In 1890 he was hired by the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) as an assistant professor. He was promoted to associate professor in 1894, and in 1906 to [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor). In 1909 he became the chair of U. C. Berkeley's mathematics department in succession to [Irving Stringham](wikipedia://en/24044076), and remained the chair until retiring as professor emeritus in 1933.[^o5]

Haskell provided a foundation for [hyperbolic angle](wikipedia://en/1139981) and [hyperbolic functions](wikipedia://en/56567) with his article in *Bulletin of American Mathematical Society* in 1895. In particular, he identified the angle with [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [hyperbolic sector](wikipedia://en/1139926), and showed its invariance under [squeeze mapping](wikipedia://en/1140043).

Haskell was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1924 in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto) and in 1928 in [Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Haskell married Isabel Woodford Brown in 1928.[^o7]

He died in Berkeley on January 15, 1948.[^o7]
