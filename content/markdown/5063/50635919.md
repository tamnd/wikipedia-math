# Benjamin Abram Bernstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Abram Bernstein"}

**Benjamin Abram Bernstein** (20 May 1881, [Pasvalys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasvalys), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania) – 25 September 1964, [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California)) was an American mathematician, specializing in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

With his Jewish[^o4] family, Bernstein immigrated as a child to the United States. After completing public primary education in 1897 in Baltimore, he completed in 1902 his secondary education at [Baltimore City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_City_College), and then received in 1905 his A.B. degree from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). After completing two years of graduate study at Johns Hopkins University, he became in 1907 an instructor and continuing graduate student in mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). There he received in 1913, with supervisor [Mellen W. Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellen_W._Haskell), his Ph.D. At Berkeley, Bernstein became in 1918 an assistant professor, in 1923 an associate professor, and in 1928 a full professor of mathematics, retiring in 1951 as professor emeritus.[^o2]

When Professor Bernstein began his studies in mathematics, the subject of algebra of logic, or mathematical logic, was just beginning to receive intensive scholarly study, mainly from philosophers. Along with [E. V. Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Vermilye_Huntington) of Harvard, Professor Bernstein was a pioneer in this field from the mathematical point of view. ... During this early period in the history of mathematical logic, a notable event for the subject was the publication of *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)*, by [Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) and [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163). Professor Bernstein was an intensive and critical student of this *Principia* and discussed it in many papers.[^o2]

He was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1924 in Toronto.[^o6] He was elected in 1931 a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o7] His doctoral students include Robert Levit[^o5][^o8] and [J.C.C. McKinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.C.C._McKinsey).

In June 1920 in New York City, Professor Bernstein married Rose Davidson; her brother was the sculptor [Jo Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Davidson). Bernstein was predeceased by his wife and upon his death was survived by a daughter and a granddaughter.[^o2]
