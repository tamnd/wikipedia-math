# Hasse invariant of an algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hasse invariant of an algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hasse invariant of an algebra** is an invariant attached to a [Brauer class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brauer_class) of [algebras over a field](wikipedia://en/191788). The concept is named after [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118). The invariant plays a role in [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532).

:::

## Local fields {#Local_fields}

Let *K* be a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) with valuation *v* and *D* a *K*-algebra. We may assume *D* is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) with centre *K* of degree *n*. The valuation *v* can be extended to *D*, for example by extending it compatibly to each commutative subfield of *D*: the value group of this valuation is (1/*n*)**Z**.[^o3]

There is a commutative subfield *L* of *D* which is unramified over *K*, and *D* splits over *L*.[^o4] The field *L* is not unique but all such extensions are conjugate by the [Skolem–Noether theorem](wikipedia://en/693453), which further shows that any automorphism of *L* is induced by a conjugation in *D*. Take γ in *D* such that conjugation by γ induces the Frobenius automorphism of *L*/*K* and let *v*(γ) = *k*/*n*. Then *k*/*n* modulo 1 is the Hasse invariant of *D*. It depends only on the Brauer class of *D*.[^o5]

The Hasse invariant is thus a map defined on the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) *K* to the [divisible group](wikipedia://en/472147) **Q**/**Z**.[^o5][^o6] Every class in the Brauer group is represented by a class in the Brauer group of an unramified extension of *L*/*K* of degree *n*,[^o7] which by the [Grunwald–Wang theorem](wikipedia://en/20901695) and the [Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem](wikipedia://en/23502488) we may take to be a [cyclic algebra](wikipedia://en/43230669) (*L*,φ,π<sup>*k*</sup>) for some *k* mod *n*, where φ is the [Frobenius map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_map#Frobenius_for_local_fields) and π is a uniformiser.[^o8] The invariant map attaches the element *k*/*n* mod 1 to the class. This exhibits the invariant map as a homomorphism

$$\underset{L/K}{\operatorname{inv}} : \operatorname{Br}(L/K) \rightarrow \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} .$$

The invariant map extends to Br(*K*) by representing each class by some element of Br(*L*/*K*) as above.[^o5][^o6]

For a non-Archimedean local field, the invariant map is a [group isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397).[^o5][^o9]

In the case of the field **R** of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438), there are two Brauer classes, represented by the algebra **R** itself and the [quaternion](wikipedia://en/51440) algebra **H**.[^o10] It is convenient to assign invariant zero to the class of **R** and invariant 1/2 modulo 1 to the quaternion class.

In the case of the field **C** of complex numbers, the only Brauer class is the trivial one, with invariant zero.[^o12]

## Global fields {#Global_fields}

For a global field *K*, given a central simple algebra *D* over *K* then for each valuation *v* of *K* we can consider the extension of scalars *D*<sub>*v*</sub> = *D* ⊗ *K*<sub>*v*</sub> The extension *D*<sub>*v*</sub> splits for all but finitely many *v*, so that the *local invariant* of *D*<sub>*v*</sub> is almost always zero. The Brauer group Br(*K*) fits into an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)[^o10][^o12]

$$0\rightarrow \textrm{Br}(K)\rightarrow \bigoplus_{v\in S} \textrm{Br}(K_v)\rightarrow \mathbf{Q}/\mathbf{Z} \rightarrow 0,$$

where *S* is the set of all valuations of *K* and the right arrow is the sum of the local invariants. The injectivity of the left arrow is the content of the [Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem](wikipedia://en/23502488). Exactness in the middle term is a deep fact from [global class field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_class_field_theory).
