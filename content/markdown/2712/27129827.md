# Bálint Virág

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bálint Virág"}

**Bálint Virág** (born 1973) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada), known for his work in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), particularly determinantal processes, [random matrix](wikipedia://en/1648765) theory, and [random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) and other probabilistic questions on [groups](wikipedia://en/41890). He received his Ph.D. from [U.C. Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.C._Berkeley) in 2000, under the direction of [Yuval Peres](wikipedia://en/36477942), and was a post-doc at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). Since 2003 he has been a [Canada research chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_research_chair) at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).

:::

Virág was awarded a [Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellowship) (2004), the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize)[^o3] (2008), the [Coxeter–James Prize](wikipedia://en/18739444)[^o4] (2010), and the [John L. Synge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Synge_Award) (2014).[^o5] He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2014.[^o6] In 2024, he was named a [Fellow of the Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society_of_Canada).[^o7]
