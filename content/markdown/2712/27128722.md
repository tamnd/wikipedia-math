# Thomas Murray MacRobert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Murray MacRobert"}

**Thomas Murray MacRobert** (4 April 1884, in [Dreghorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreghorn), [Ayrshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrshire) – 1 November 1962, in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow)) was a Scottish mathematician. He became professor of mathematics at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) and introduced the [MacRobert E function](wikipedia://en/23618631), a generalisation of the [generalised hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalised_hypergeometric_series).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born on 4 April 1884 in the manse at [Dreghorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreghorn), [Ayrshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayrshire) in south-west Scotland, the son of Rev Thomas MacRobert and his wife, Isabella Edgely Fisher. He was educated at [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_North_Ayrshire) Royal Academy with his identical twin brother, Alexander,[^o3] then studied divinity at [Glasgow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_University) but transferred to study mathematics and natural philosophy (physics), graduating in 1905. He then took a second degree at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge).[^o4]

In 1910 he joined the staff of Glasgow University as an assistant to Professor Gibson, lecturing in mathematics.

In the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) he served in the [Royal Garrison Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Garrison_Artillery) and saw active service in France.[^o3]

In 1921 he was elected a fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [Andrew Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Gray_%28physicist%29), [George Alexander Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Alexander_Gibson), [James Gordon Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gordon_Gray) and [Robert Alexander Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Alexander_Houston).[^o5] He was President of the Edinburgh Mathematical Society 1921/22.[^o6] He resigned from the RSE in 1940.

Glasgow University granted him an honorary doctorate (LLD) in 1955.[^o3]

He retired in 1954 and died in Glasgow on 1 November 1962.

## Family {#Family}

In 1914, before going to war, he married Violet McIlwraith; they initially lived in a flat in North [Kelvinside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvinside) in Glasgow. They had three children: Violet, Tom and Alexander. He was a member of the Glasgow [temperance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperance_movement) and enjoyed hill-walking.[^o3]

## Artistic recognition {#Artistic_recognition}

His portrait by [Norman Hepple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Hepple) is held by the [Hunterian Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunterian_Art_Gallery) in Glasgow.[^o9]
