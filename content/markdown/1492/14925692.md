# Cleaver (geometry)

*Image: CleavanceCenter.svg.* Construction of the Spieker center via cleavers. solid black dashed black solid blue solid #cc6600 solid red

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cleaver"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **cleaver** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) that [bisects](wikipedia://en/152547) the [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of the triangle and has one endpoint at the [midpoint](wikipedia://en/437979) of one of the three sides. They are not to be confused with *[splitters](wikipedia://en/14961391)*, which also bisect the perimeter, but with an endpoint on one of the triangle's vertices instead of its sides.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Each cleaver through the midpoint of one of the sides of a triangle is parallel to the [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) at the opposite vertex of the triangle.[^o4][^o5]

The **broken chord theorem** of [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) provides another construction of the cleaver. Suppose the triangle to be bisected is $\triangle ABC$, and that one endpoint of the cleaver is the midpoint of side $\overline{AB}$. Form the [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) of $\triangle ABC$ and let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc of the circumcircle from $A$ through $C$ to $B$. Then the other endpoint of the cleaver is the closest point of the triangle to $M$, and can be found by dropping a perpendicular from $M$ to the longer of the two sides $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$.[^o4][^o5]

:::

## Related figures {#Related_figures}
