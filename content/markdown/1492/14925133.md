# Hugo Christiaan Hamaker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hugo Christiaan Hamaker"}

**Hugo Christiaan Hamaker** (23 March 1905 – 7 September 1993), or **Huug Hamaker**, was a Dutch physical chemist and applied statistician.[^o3][^o4][^o5] He was responsible for the [Hamaker theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamaker_theory) which explains the [van der Waals forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_der_Waals_forces) between objects larger than molecules.[^o6]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hamaker was born in [Broek op Langedijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broek_op_Langedijk) and his father was a family doctor. His grandfather was Dutch jurist [Hendrik Jacob Hamaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Jacob_Hamaker), who was a professor at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University).[^o8] Hamaker studied experimental physics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) and obtained his *[doctorandus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorandus)* (equivalent to a MSc degree) in 1929. In the same year, he participated in the [Snellius Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snellius_Expedition) along with geologist [Philip Kuenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Kuenen). He also got acquainted with Kuenen's sister Emily and they married years later.[^o3]

Hamaker went on to complete his doctorate *[cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cum_laude)* in physics at [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University) in 1934 under the supervision of [Leonard Ornstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Ornstein). His dissertation was titled *Reflectivity and Emissivity of [Tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten)*. From 1934 to 1967, Hamaker was employed in the [Philips Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philips_Physics_Laboratory) in Eindhoven. From 1960 to 1972, he was Professor at the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 1959 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o13] In 1979, he received the [Shewhart Medal](wikipedia://en/831964) from the [American Society for Quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Quality). In 1982, he received the W. Edwards Demming Medal from the [Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Japanese_Scientists_and_Engineers).[^o3]
