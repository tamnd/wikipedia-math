# Jacob Korevaar

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jacob Korevaar"}

**Jacob "Jaap" Korevaar** (25 January 1923 – 18 March 2025) was a Dutch mathematician. He was part of the faculty of the [University of California San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_San_Diego) and [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), as well as the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) ([Korteweg-de Vries Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/66341573)).

:::

Korevaar became a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1975.[^o3] He won the 1987 [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Randolph_Ford_Award), and the 1989 [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize), for an essay on [Louis de Branges de Bourcia](wikipedia://en/79126)'s proof of the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture).[^o4] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5]

Korevaar was the older brother of the Olympic [water polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_polo) player [Nijs Korevaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijs_Korevaar).[^o6] He died in [Bussum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bussum) on 18 March 2025, at the age of 102.[^o7][^o8]
