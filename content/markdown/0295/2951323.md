# Cramér–Wold theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cramér–Wold theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cramér–Wold theorem**[^o2][^o3] or the **Cramér–Wold device**[^o4][^o5] is a theorem in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) and which states that a [Borel](wikipedia://en/4322) [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on $\mathbb{R}^k$ is uniquely determined by the totality of its one-dimensional projections.[^o6][^o7][^o8] It is used as a method for proving joint convergence results. The theorem is named after [Harald Cramér](wikipedia://en/223129) and [Herman Ole Andreas Wold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Ole_Andreas_Wold), who published the result in 1936.[^o9]

:::

Let

$${X}_n = (X_{n1},\dots,X_{nk})$$

and

$$\; {X} = (X_1,\dots,X_k)$$

be [random vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_vector) of dimension *k*. Then ${X}_n$ [converges in distribution](wikipedia://en/50723#Convergence_in_distribution) to ${X}$ if and only if:

$$\sum_{i=1}^k t_iX_{ni} \overset{D}{\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}} \sum_{i=1}^k t_iX_i.$$

for each $(t_1,\dots,t_k)\in \mathbb{R}^k$, that is, if every fixed [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of the coordinates of ${X}_n$ converges in distribution to the correspondent linear combination of coordinates of ${X}$.[^o10]

If ${X}_n$ takes values in $\mathbb{R}_+^k$, then the statement is also true with $(t_1,\dots,t_k)\in \mathbb{R}_+^k$.[^o11]
