# Filtered algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Filtered algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **filtered algebra** is a generalization of the notion of a [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra). Examples appear in many branches of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

A filtered algebra over the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$ is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) $(A,\cdot)$ over $k$ that has an increasing sequence $\{0\} \subseteq F_0 \subseteq F_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq F_i \subseteq \cdots \subseteq A$ of subspaces of $A$ such that

$$A=\bigcup_{i\in \mathbb{N}} F_{i}$$

and that is compatible with the multiplication in the following sense:

$$\forall m,n \in \mathbb{N},\quad F_m\cdot F_n\subseteq F_{n+m}.$$

## Associated graded algebra {#Associated_graded_algebra}

In general, there is the following construction that produces a graded algebra out of a filtered algebra.

If $A$ is a filtered algebra, then the *[associated graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_graded_algebra)* $\mathcal{G}(A)$ is defined as follows: The multiplication is well-defined and endows $\mathcal{G}(A)$ with the structure of a graded algebra, with gradation $\{G_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}.$ Furthermore if $A$ is [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) then so is $\mathcal{G}(A)$. Also, if $A$ is [unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra), such that the unit lies in $F_0$, then $\mathcal{G}(A)$ will be unital as well.

As algebras $A$ and $\mathcal{G}(A)$ are distinct (with the exception of the trivial case that $A$ is graded) but as vector spaces they are [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828). (One can [prove](wikipedia://en/82285) by [induction](wikipedia://en/18881) that $\bigoplus_{i=0}^nG_i$ is isomorphic to $F_n$ as vector spaces).

## Examples {#Examples}

Any [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) graded by $\mathbb{N}$, for example $A = \bigoplus_{n\in \mathbb{N}} A_n$, has a filtration given by $F_n = \bigoplus_{i=0}^n A_i$.

An example of a filtered algebra is the [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305) $\operatorname{Cliff}(V,q)$ of a vector space $V$ endowed with a [quadratic form](wikipedia://en/2047965) $q.$ The associated graded algebra is $\bigwedge V$, the [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) of $V.$

The [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) on the dual of an [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) is a filtered algebra of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000); on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), one instead obtains a graded algebra.

The [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{g}$ is also naturally filtered. The [PBW theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBW_theorem) states that the associated graded algebra is simply $\mathrm{Sym} (\mathfrak{g})$.

Scalar [differential operators](wikipedia://en/263886) on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $M$ form a filtered algebra where the filtration is given by the degree of differential operators. The associated graded algebra is the [commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_algebra_%28structure%29) of smooth functions on the [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600) $T^*M$ which are polynomial along the fibers of the projection $\pi\colon T^*M\rightarrow M$.

The [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with a [length function](wikipedia://en/3021393) is a filtered algebra.
