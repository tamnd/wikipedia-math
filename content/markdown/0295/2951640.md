# Wedge (geometry)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wedge"}

In [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960), a **wedge** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) defined by two [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and three [trapezoid](wikipedia://en/201826) faces. A wedge has five faces, nine edges, and six vertices.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A wedge is a polyhedron of a rectangular base, with the faces are two [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428) and two [trapezoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapezoids) that meet at the top of an edge.. A [prismatoid](wikipedia://en/23917) is defined as a polyhedron where its vertices lie on two parallel planes, with its lateral faces are triangles, [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826), and [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496); the wedge is an example of prismatoid because of its top edge is parallel to the rectangular base. The volume of a wedge is

$$V = bh \left(\frac{a}{3}+\frac{c}{6}\right),$$

where the base rectangle is $a$ by $b$, $c$ is the [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079) edge length parallel to $a$, and $h$ is the height from the base rectangle to the apex edge.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Triangular prism wedge.png.* A wedge that is parallel

In some special cases, the wedge is the [right prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_prism) if all edges connecting triangles are equal in length, and the triangular faces are perpendicular to the rectangular base.

Wedges can be created from decomposition of other polyhedra. For instance, the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407) can be divided into a central [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) with 6 wedges covering the cube faces. The orientations of the wedges are such that the triangle and trapezoid faces can connect and form a regular [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014).

Two obtuse wedges can be formed by bisecting a regular tetrahedron on a plane parallel to two opposite edges.
