# Matthew Stephens (statistician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Matthew Stephens"}

*Image: Matthew Stephens.jpg.* Stephens in 2016

**Matthew Stephens** (born 1970) is a [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/404412) statistician and professor in the departments of human genetics and statistics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). He is known for the Li and Stephens model as an efficient coalescent.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Stephens has a PhD from [Magdalen College, Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford) where his advisor was [Brian D. Ripley](wikipedia://en/6608281).[^o3] He then went on to work with [Peter Donnelly](wikipedia://en/2103294) as a postdoctoral researcher.

## Career {#Career}

Stephens conducted [postdoctoral research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_research) with [Peter Donnelly](wikipedia://en/2103294) at the University of Oxford. It was there that he developed the Structure computer program, along with [Jonathan Pritchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_K._Pritchard), which is used for determining [population structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_stratification) and estimating individual [admixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_admixture).[^o5] He then went on to develop the influential Li and Stephens model as an efficient model for [linkage disequilibrium](wikipedia://en/681230).[^o6]

## Awards {#Awards}

Stephens was awarded the [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) (bronze) in 2006.[^o8] He was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 2023.[^o9]
