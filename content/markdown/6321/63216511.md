# Pride in STEM

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pride in STEM"}

**Pride in STEM** is a [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK)-based charity supporting [LGBT+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT+) scientists internationally, co-founded in 2016[^o3][^o4] by Alfredo Carpineti, a senior staff writer and space correspondent at IFLScience,[^o5][^o6] his husband Chris, and Matt Young, a researcher at the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham).[^o3] Its origins, prior to registration as a charitable trust, were as a marching group for the parade at [Pride in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_in_London).[^o7] [Ben Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Britton), one of the organisation's trustees, wrote in 2019 that the organisation's "informal mantra is to queer up science spaces and science up queer spaces".[^o8] "[STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663)" is an acronym for "science, technology, engineering and maths/medicine".[^o3]

:::

## Events {#Events}

### Out Thinkers {#Out_Thinkers}

The organisation organises a series of events called "Out Thinkers", first held in 2016,[^o11] at which LGBT+ scientists discuss their life and work.[^o12] Events in the series have been held at the [British Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Science_Festival),[^o13] at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London),[^o14] at the [Science Museum, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum,_London), and at the [Cambridge Science Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Science_Festival).[^o12] The first international Out Thinkers event was held in 2018.[^o11]

::: {#o16 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="LGBTSTEM Day"}

### LGBTSTEM Day {#LGBTSTEM_Day}

They were responsible, along with two other [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles)-based organisations, for organising the first LGBTSTEM Day, which was held on 5 July 2018,[^o17][^o18] which promotes visibility and awareness of LGBT+ people in the sciences.[^o19] The event was supported by the [Royal Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Astronomical_Society), [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics),[^o17] and the [Royal Society of Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Chemistry).[^o20]

The following year, nine groups (including Pride in STEM) were involved in organising the day, again on 5 July, with fifty organisations reported to be supporting it.[^o21] In 2020, the day will instead be held on 18 November, to mark the 60th anniversary of astronomer [Frank Kameny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kameny)'s petition to the [United States Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Supreme_Court), in a case prompted by his dismissal from the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) as a result of his sexual orientation.[^o22]

:::

### Other events {#Other_events}

Pride in STEM has marched as part of [Pride in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_in_London)'s march in 2016, 2017, 2018[^o24] and 2019.[^o25]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2017, the organisation was nominated for the [Barbara Burford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Burford) *[Gay Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_Times)* Honour for excellence in STEM.[^o27]

The journal *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* announced its support for the organisation in 2019.[^o28]
