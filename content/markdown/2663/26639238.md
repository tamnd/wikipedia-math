# Venvaroha

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Venvaroha"}

**Veṇvāroha** is a work in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) composed by [Mādhava of Sangamagrāma](wikipedia://en/2793007) (c. 1350 – c. 1425), the founder of the [Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics](wikipedia://en/3099132). It is a work in 74 verses describing methods for the computation of the true positions of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) at intervals of about half an hour for various days in an [anomalistic cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_cycle). This work is an elaboration of an earlier and shorter work of Mādhava himself titled *[Sphutacandrāpti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphuta-chandrapti)*.[^o3] *Veṇvāroha* is the most popular astronomical work of Mādhava.[^o4]

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The title *Veṇvāroha* literally means '[Bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo) Climbing' (*Veṇu* 'bamboo' + *āroha* 'climbing') and it is indicative of the computational procedure expounded in the text. The computational scheme is like climbing a bamboo tree, going up and up step by step at measured equal heights.

## Overview {#Overview}

It is dated 1403 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era). [Acyuta Piṣārati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achyuta_Pisharati) (1550–1621), another prominent [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902)/[astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) of the [Kerala school](wikipedia://en/3099132), has composed a [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam) [commentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close_reading) on *Veṇvāroha*.[^o3] This astronomical treatise is of a type generally described as Karaṇa texts in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India). Such works are characterized by the fact that they are compilations of [computational methods](wikipedia://en/775) of [practical astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy).

The novelty and ingenuity of the method attracted the attention of several of the followers of Mādhava and they composed similar texts thereby creating a genre of works in Indian mathematical tradition collectively referred to as ‘veṇvāroha texts’. These include *Drik-veṇvārohakriya* of unknown authorship of epoch 1695 and *Veṇvārohastaka* of [Putuman Somāyaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puthumana_Somayaji).[^o7]

In the technical terminology of astronomy, the ingenuity introduced by Mādhava in *Veṇvāroha* can be explained thus: Mādhava has endeavored to compute the true [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) of the Moon by making use of the true motions rather than the epicyclic astronomy of the [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204) tradition. He made use of the anomalistic revolutions for computing the true positions of the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) using the successive true daily velocity specified in *[Candravākyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candravakyas)* (Table of Moon-mnemonics) for easy memorization and use.[^o8]

Veṇvāroha has been studied from a modern perspective and the process is explained using the properties of periodic functions.[^o9]
