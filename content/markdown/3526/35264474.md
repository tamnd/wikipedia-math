# Ralph Lent Jeffery

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ralph Lent Jeffery"}

**Ralph Lent Jeffery** (3 October 1889 Overton, [Yarmouth County, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarmouth_County,_Nova_Scotia), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) – 1975 [Wolfville, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfville,_Nova_Scotia)) was a Canadian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working on analysis.

:::

He taught at several institutions including [Acadia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acadia_University), the [University of Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Saskatchewan) and [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston). Jeffery Hall at Queen's was named for him. In 1937 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada).

In 1925 Jeffery exhibited a [bounded function](wikipedia://en/311509) of two real variables, [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) in each, yet fails to have an [integral](wikipedia://en/15532).

In 1951 Jeffery published *Theory of Functions of a Real Variable* which was noted for its coverage of integration theory.[^o2][^o3]

## Selected papers {#Selected_papers}

- 1925: "Definite integrals containing a parameter", [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785) 26(3): 173 to 80
- 1926: "Functions of two variables for which the double integral does not exist", [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly) 33(3): 142,3
- 1931: "The uniform approximation of a sequence of integrals", [American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics) 53(1): 61 to 71
- 1933: "Sets of k-extent in n-dimensional space", [Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937) 35(3): 629 to 47
