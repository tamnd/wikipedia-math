# Michael G. Crandall

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Michael G. Crandall"}

**Michael Grain Crandall** (November 29, 1940 – March 21, 2026) was an American mathematician who specialized in differential equations.

:::

## Mathematical career {#Mathematical_career}

In 1962 Crandall earned a baccalaureate in engineering physics from [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), changed to mathematics, earning a master's in 1964 and a PhD in 1965 under [Heinz Cordes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Cordes) at Berkeley, with a thesis that solved a problem in celestial mechanics posed by [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602); the thesis title is *Two families of plane solutions of the four body problem*. In 1965 he was an instructor at Berkeley, in 1966 an assistant professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) and from 1969 at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA), where he was a professor from 1973 to 1976. From 1974 to 1984 he was a professor at the Mathematics Research Center at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), from 1984 to 1990 as Hille-Professor of Mathematics. From 1988 until his retirement he was a professor at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara). Crandall was several times a visiting professor at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), where he received an honorary doctorate in 1999.

Crandall worked primarily on partial differential equations, e.g., with [bifurcation theory](wikipedia://en/2326042), evolution equations, generation of semigroups of transformations on [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989)[^o6] and the theory of [Hamilton–Jacobi equations](wikipedia://en/897539). With [Pierre-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/1270068) he did research on the viscosity solutions of partial differential equations.[^o7]

In 2000 he was elected a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences). In 1999 he received the [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550). In 1974 he was an Invited Lecturer (on "Semigroups of nonlinear equations and evolution equations") at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Vancouver. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8] In 2023, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o9]

Among his doctoral students are [Lawrence C. Evans](wikipedia://en/2790063) and [Panagiotis E. Souganidis](wikipedia://en/62325203).
