# Elizabeth Stephansen

*Image: Elizabeth Stephansen.png.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elizabeth Stephansen"}

(**Mary Ann**) **Elizabeth Stephansen** (10 March 1872 – 23 February 1961) was a Norwegian mathematician and educator. She was one of the first Norwegian women to be awarded a doctorate degree.[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Stephansen was born in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), Norway. She was the eldest daughter of Anton Stephan Stephansen (1845–1929) and Gerche Reimers Jahn (1848–1935). Her father was a merchant and owner of a textile shop. He later established the textile factory, Espelandfos Spinderi & Tricotagefabrik, in [Arna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arna,_Bergen). She was educated at the [Bergen Cathedral School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_Cathedral_School) graduating in 1891. She was fluent in the German language and traveled to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) to continue her education. She attended [Eidgenössische Polytechnikum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich) and graduated in 1896. Her thesis *Ueber partielle Differentialgleichungen vierter Ordnung die ein intermediäres Integral besitzen* was published in 1902. She obtained her doctorate ([Dr. Philos.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Philos.)) in absentia from the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) in the fall of 1902.[^o5][^o6]

In 1902–1903, she traveled to the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) under a government grant to attend lectures by noted German mathematicians, [Ernst Zermelo](wikipedia://en/178655), [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). She first served as a teacher of mathematics at Bergen Cathedral School and Bergen Technical School. Between 1905–1906, she completed mathematical research and wrote further papers on difference equations. From 1906 until her retirement in 1937 she worked at the [Agricultural College of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_College_of_Norway) at [Ås](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ås,_Akershus) in [Akershus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akershus). She first taught physics and mathematics. In 1921, she was appointed [Docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) in mathematics.[^o7][^o8]

In retirement, she lived at the family farm at Espeland in Arna (*Espeland, gnr. 289, gårdsbruk i Arna*) which her father had first acquired during 1918. After the liberation of Norway in 1945, she was awarded the [King's Medal of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Medal_of_Merit) (*Kongens fortjenstmedalje*) for the assistance she rendered to Norwegian prisoners held at the Nazi operated [Espeland concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeland_concentration_camp) (*Espeland fangeleir*). She died in 1961 at [Espeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeland) in the modern-day borough of Arna, and was buried at Solheim Cemetery in the [Årstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Årstad,_Bergen) district of Bergen.
