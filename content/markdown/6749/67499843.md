# Joyce Friedman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joyce Friedman"}

**Joyce Barbara Friedman** (1928 – November 28, 2018) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [operations researcher](wikipedia://en/43476), [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), and [computational linguist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) who worked as a professor at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) and [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) and served as president of the [Association for Computational Linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computational_Linguistics).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Friedman was born in 1928. She was a Durant Scholar at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College), from which she graduated in 1949, and earned a master's degree at [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College) in 1952. In the same year she moved from the Logistics Research Project at [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) to the US Department of Defense, later working at a succession of defense contractors: ACF Industries (where she worked with Sheldon Akers on [production scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduling_%28production_processes%29)), Tech. Operations, Inc., and the [Mitre Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitre_Corporation).

Returning to graduate study at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), her interests shifted from [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) to [automated reasoning](wikipedia://en/2884728). She completed her Ph.D. in 1965, supervised by [Hao Wang](wikipedia://en/1056047), with the dissertation *A New Decision Procedure in Logic with a Computer Realization*, concerning computational methods in [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983).

## Academic career and later life {#Academic_career_and_later_life}

Friedman worked as an assistant professor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) from 1965 until 1968, when she moved to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) as an associate professor of computer and communications sciences. At Michigan, she was promoted to full professor in 1971. She moved to [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) as chair of computer science in 1983.

Even before completing her doctorate, Friedman had worked at Mitre as a programmer for Donald E. Walker on a project for the US Air Force involving the development of programs that could answer English-language questions. As an academic, her research largely concentrated on [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics) and [formal grammars](wikipedia://en/18020716); she served as president of the [Association for Computational Linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computational_Linguistics) in 1971. Some of her work on [transformational grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformational_grammar) was described in her 1971 book, *A Computational Model of Transformational Grammar*. She has over 180 [academic descendants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_genealogy) through three of her doctoral students: [C. Raymond Perrault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Raymond_Perrault), [Remko Scha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remko_Scha), and David S. Warren.

She died on November 28, 2018, in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Friedman was named a Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1986.
