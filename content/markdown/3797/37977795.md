# Lorden's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lorden's inequality"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Lorden's inequality** is a bound for the [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of overshoot for a stopped sum of [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685), first published by [Gary Lorden](wikipedia://en/80536367) in 1970.[^o2] Overshoots play a central role in [renewal theory](wikipedia://en/1945387).[^o5]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of inequality"}

## Statement of inequality {#Statement_of_inequality}

Let *X*<sub>1</sub>, *X*<sub>2</sub>, ... be [independent and identically distributed positive random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_and_identically_distributed_positive_random_variables) and define the sum *S*<sub>*n*</sub> = *X*<sub>1</sub> + *X*<sub>2</sub> + ... + *X*<sub>*n*</sub>. Consider the first time *S*<sub>*n*</sub> exceeds a given value *b* and at that time compute *R*<sub>*b*</sub> = *S*<sub>*n*</sub> − *b*. *R*<sub>*b*</sub> is called the overshoot or excess at *b*. Lorden's inequality states that the expectation of this overshoot is bounded as[^o5]

$$\operatorname E (R_b) \leq \frac{\operatorname E (X^2)}{\operatorname E(X)}.$$

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Three proofs are known due to Lorden,[^o2] Carlsson and Nerman[^o8] and Chang.[^o9]
