# Ferrero–Washington theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ferrero–Washington theorem"}

::: {#o2 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Ferrero–Washington theorem"}

**Ferrero–Washington theorem.**

[Iwasawa's μ-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwasawa's_μ-invariant) is zero for cyclotomic *p*-adic extensions of abelian number fields.

:::

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **Ferrero–Washington theorem** states that [Iwasawa's μ-invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwasawa's_μ-invariant) vanishes for cyclotomic **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extensions of abelian [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822). It was first proved by (Ferrero Washington 1979). A different proof was given by (Sinnott 1984).

:::

## History {#History}

(Iwasawa 1959) introduced the μ-invariant of a **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extension and observed that it was zero in all cases he calculated. (Iwasawa Sims 1966) used a computer to check that it vanishes for the cyclotomic **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extension of the rationals for all [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) less than 4000. (Iwasawa 1971) later conjectured that the μ-invariant vanishes for any **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extension, but shortly after (Iwasawa 1973) discovered examples of non-cyclotomic extensions of number fields with non-vanishing μ-invariant showing that his original conjecture was wrong. He suggested, however, that the conjecture might still hold for cyclotomic **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extensions.

(Iwasawa 1958) showed that the vanishing of the μ-invariant for cyclotomic **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-extensions of the rationals is equivalent to certain congruences between [Bernoulli numbers](wikipedia://en/4964), and (Ferrero Washington 1979) showed that the μ-invariant vanishes in these cases by proving that these congruences hold.

::: {#o13 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

For a number field *K*, denote the extension of *K* by *p*<sup>*m*</sup>-power roots of unity by *K*<sub>*m*</sub>, the union of the *K*<sub>*m*</sub> as *m* ranges over all positive integers by $\hat K$, and the maximal unramified abelian *p*-extension of $\hat K$ by *A*<sup>(*p*)</sup>. Let the [Tate module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_module_of_a_number_field)

$$T_p(K) = \mathrm{Gal}(A^{(p)}/\hat K) \ .$$

Then *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) is a pro-*p*-group and so a **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>-module. Using [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) one can describe *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) as isomorphic to the inverse limit of the class groups *C*<sub>*m*</sub> of the *K*<sub>*m*</sub> under norm.[^o14]

Iwasawa exhibited *T*<sub>*p*</sub>(*K*) as a module over the completion **Z**<sub>*p*</sub>*T* and this implies a formula for the exponent of *p* in the order of the class groups *C*<sub>*m*</sub> of the form

$$\lambda m + \mu p^m + \kappa \ .$$

The Ferrero–Washington theorem states that μ is zero.[^o15]

:::
