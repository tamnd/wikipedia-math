# Andrásfai graph

*Image: Andrásfai-gráf (n=4).jpg.* Two drawings of the $\operatorname{And}(4)$ graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Andrásfai graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **Andrásfai graph** is a [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996), [circulant graph](wikipedia://en/3498787) named after [Béla Andrásfai](wikipedia://en/63501457).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The Andrásfai graph $\operatorname{And}(n)$ for any [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $n \ge 1$ is a circulant graph on $3n - 1$ vertices, in which vertex $k$ is connected by an edge to vertices $k \pm j$, for every $j$ that is congruent to 1 mod 3. For instance, the [Wagner graph](wikipedia://en/17598450) is an Andrásfai graph, the graph $\operatorname{And}(3)$.

The graph family is triangle-free, and $\operatorname{And}(n)$ has an [independence number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_number) of $n$. From this the formula $R(3,n) \ge 3(n - 1)$ results, where $R(n,k)$ is the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number). The equality holds for $n = 3$ and $n = 4$ only.

The Andrásfai graphs were later generalized.[^o5][^o6]
