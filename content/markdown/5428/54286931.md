# Truncated normal hurdle model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Truncated normal hurdle model"}

In [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390), the **truncated normal hurdle model** is a variant of the [Tobit model](wikipedia://en/3722064) and was first proposed by Cragg in 1971.[^o2]

:::

In a standard Tobit model, represented as $y=(x\beta+u) 1[x\beta+u>0]$, where $u|x\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$This model construction implicitly imposes two first order assumptions:[^o3]

1. Since: $\partial P[y>0]/\partial x_j=\varphi (x\beta/\sigma) \beta_j/\sigma$ and $\partial \operatorname E[y\mid x,y >0]/\partial x_j=\beta_j\{1-\theta(x\beta/\sigma\}$, the partial effect of $x_j$ on the probability $P[y>0]$ and the [conditional expectation](wikipedia://en/435099): $\operatorname E [y\mid x,y >0]$ has the same sign:[^o4]
1. The relative effects of $x_h$ and $x_j$ on $P[y>0]$ and $\operatorname E [y\mid x,y>0]$ are identical, i.e.:

$$\frac{\partial P[y>0]/\partial x_h}{\partial P[y>0]/ \partial x_j}=\frac{\partial \operatorname E[y\mid x,y>0]/ \partial x_h}{ \partial \operatorname E[y\mid x,y>0]/ \partial x_j} = \frac{\beta_h}{\beta_j}|$$

However, these two implicit assumptions are too strong and inconsistent with many contexts in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics). For instance, when we need to decide whether to [invest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment) and build a factory, the construction [cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost) might be more influential than the product [price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price); but once we have already built the factory, the product price is definitely more influential to the [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue). Hence, the implicit assumption (2) doesn't match this context.[^o5] The essence of this issue is that the standard Tobit implicitly models a very strong link between the participation decision $( y=0$ or $y>0)$ and the amount decision (the magnitude of $y$ when $y>0$). If a [corner solution](wikipedia://en/2189949) model is represented in a general form: $y=s \centerdot w,$ , where $s$ is the participate decision and $w$ is the amount decision, standard Tobit model assumes:

$$s=1[x\beta +u>0];$$

$$w=x\beta+u.$$

To make the model compatible with more contexts, a natural improvement is to assume:

$$s=1[x \gamma +u>0], \text{ where } u \sim N (0,1);$$

$w=x\beta + e,$ where the error term ($e$) is distributed as a [truncated normal distribution](wikipedia://en/11753597) with a density as $\varphi (\cdot) / \Phi \left(\frac{x\beta}\sigma \right)/\sigma;$

$s$ and $w$ are independent conditional on $x$.

This is called Truncated Normal Hurdle Model, which is proposed in Cragg (1971).[^o2] By adding one more parameter and detach the amount decision with the participation decision, the model can fit more contexts. Under this model setup, the [density](wikipedia://en/43487) of the $y$ given $x$ can be written as:

$$f (y\mid x)= [1-\Phi (\chi\gamma)]^{1[y = 0]} \cdot \left[\frac{\Phi\ (\chi\gamma)}{\Phi ( \chi\beta/\sigma)} \left. \varphi \left(\frac{y-\chi\beta}{\sigma}\right) \right/ \sigma\right] ^{1[y>0]}$$

From this density representation, it is obvious that it will degenerate to the standard Tobit model when $\gamma= \beta/\sigma.$ This also shows that Truncated Normal Hurdle Model is more general than the standard Tobit model.

The Truncated Normal Hurdle Model is usually estimated through MLE. The log-likelihood function can be written as:

$$\begin{aligned} \ell(\beta,\gamma,\sigma) = {} & \sum_{i=1}^N 1[y_i = 0] \log [1-\Phi (x_i \gamma) ] +1 [y_i>0] \log [\Phi (x_i \gamma)] \\[5pt] & {} + 1[y_i>0] \left[ -\log \left[\Phi \left( \frac{x_i\beta} \sigma \right)\right] + \log \left(\varphi \left(\frac{y_i-x_i\beta} \sigma \right) \right) -\log (\sigma) \right] \end{aligned}$$

From the log-likelihood function, $\gamma$ can be estimated by a [probit model](wikipedia://en/1611247) and $(\beta,\sigma)$ can be estimated by a truncated normal regression model.[^o6] Based on the estimates, consistent estimates for the Average Partial Effect can be estimated correspondingly.
