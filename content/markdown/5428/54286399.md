# Curie's principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Curie's principle"}

**Curie's principle**, or **Curie's symmetry principle**, is a maxim about cause and effect formulated by [Pierre Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Curie) in 1894:[^o2] the symmetries of the causes are to be found in the effects.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

The idea was based on the ideas of [Franz Ernst Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ernst_Neumann) and [Bernhard Minnigerode](wikipedia://en/80609654). Thus, it is sometimes known as the **Neumann–Minnigerode–Curie principle**.[^o6]

Later physicists have interpreted Curie's principle in the context of thermodynamics. Dynamics close to equilibrium are described by a set of transport coefficients whose symmetries must match the symmetries of the system, according to Curie's principle. [^o7]
