# Joseph H. Sampson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joseph H. Sampson"}

**Joseph Harold Sampson Jr.** (1926–2003) was an American mathematician known for his work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), especially his work about [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484) in collaboration with [James Eells](wikipedia://en/37329912). He obtained his Ph.D. in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1951 under the supervision of [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847).[^o3]

:::

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

In 1964, Sampson and [James Eells](wikipedia://en/37329912) introduced [harmonic maps](wikipedia://en/4577484), which are mappings between [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) which solve a geometrically defined system of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). They can also be defined via the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). Generalizing Bochner's work on [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762), Eells and Sampson derived the [Bochner identity](wikipedia://en/13229499), and used it to prove the triviality of harmonic maps under certain curvature conditions.

Eells and Sampson established the existence of harmonic maps whenever the domain manifold is [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) and the target has non-positive [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618). Their proof analyzed the [harmonic map heat flow](wikipedia://en/4577484), which is a geometrically defined [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415). By establishing *[a priori estimates](wikipedia://en/30669047)* for the flow, they were able to prove its convergence under the indicated curvature assumption. The use of the Bochner identity in deriving estimates is where the assumption on sectional curvature plays a crucial role. As a result of Eells and Sampson's (subsequential) convergence theorem, they were able to prove the existence of harmonic maps in any [homotopy class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class). As such, harmonic maps may be regarded as canonically defined representatives of topological spaces of mappings. This perspective has enabled the application of harmonic maps to many problems in geometry and topology.

Eells and Sampson's work is one of the most prominent papers in the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and was a direct inspiration for [Richard Hamilton](wikipedia://en/758911)'s epochal work on the [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291). In addition to Eells and Sampson's heat flow, their main results on existence of harmonic maps can also be derived via the calculus of variations, using the regularity theory developed in the 1980s by [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) and [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796).

Later in 1978, Sampson developed unique continuation, [maximum principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_Principle), further rigidity theorems, and deformability results for harmonic maps. He also proved that a harmonic map of [degree one](wikipedia://en/3094450) between compact hyperbolic [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) must be a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564). The same result was obtained at the same time by Schoen and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137).[^o6]
