# Tits alternative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tits alternative"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Tits alternative**, named after [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570), is an important theorem about the structure of [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [linear groups](wikipedia://en/3983656).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The theorem, proven by Tits,[^o4] is stated as follows.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Let $G$ be a [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) [linear group](wikipedia://en/3983656) over a field. Then two following possibilities occur:

either $G$ is [virtually](wikipedia://en/10377971) [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) (i.e., has a solvable [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of [finite index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_index))

or it contains a nonabelian [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) (i.e., it has a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the free group on two generators).

:::

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

A linear group is not [amenable](wikipedia://en/657430) if and only if it contains a non-abelian free group (thus the [von Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/662256), while not true in general, holds for linear groups).

The Tits alternative is an important ingredient[^o7] in the proof of [Gromov's theorem on groups of polynomial growth](wikipedia://en/635483). In fact the alternative essentially establishes the result for linear groups (it reduces it to the case of solvable groups, which can be dealt with by elementary means).

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

In [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), a group *G* is said to **satisfy the Tits alternative** if for every [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) *H* of *G* either *H* is virtually solvable or *H* contains a [nonabelian](wikipedia://en/2974) free [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) (in some versions of the definition this condition is only required to be satisfied for all [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042) subgroups of *G*).

Examples of groups satisfying the Tits alternative which are either not linear, or at least not known to be linear, are:

- [Hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817)
- [Mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295);[^o10][^o11]
- [Out(Fn)](wikipedia://en/12747184);[^o12]
- Certain groups of [birational transformations](wikipedia://en/2783119) of [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311).[^o13]

Examples of groups not satisfying the Tits alternative are:

- the [Grigorchuk group](wikipedia://en/18707371);
- [Thompson's group *F*](wikipedia://en/612245).

:::

::: {#o15 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The proof of the original Tits alternative[^o4] is by looking at the [Zariski closure](wikipedia://en/241744) of $G$ in $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$. If it is solvable then the group is solvable. Otherwise one looks at the image of $G$ in the Levi component. If it is noncompact then a [ping-pong](wikipedia://en/18786361) argument finishes the proof. If it is compact then either all eigenvalues of elements in the image of $G$ are roots of unity and then the image is finite, or one can find an embedding of $k$ in which one can apply the ping-pong strategy.

Note that the proof of all generalisations above also rests on a ping-pong argument.

:::
