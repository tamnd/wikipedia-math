# Fuglede's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fuglede's conjecture"}

**Fuglede's conjecture** is a problem in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) proposed by [Bent Fuglede](wikipedia://en/3966927) in 1974, and resolved in the negative for most dimensions by [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) in 2004. It states that every domain of $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ (i.e. subset of $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with positive finite [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198)) is a [spectral set](wikipedia://en/8984861) if and only if it tiles $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ by [translation](wikipedia://en/204682).[^o2]

:::

## Spectral sets and translational tiles {#Spectral_sets_and_translational_tiles}

**Spectral sets in** $\mathbb{R}^d$

A set $\Omega$ $\subset$ $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with positive finite Lebesgue measure is said to be a spectral set if there exists a $\Lambda$ $\subset$ $\mathbb{R}^d$ such that $\left \{ e^{2\pi i\left \langle \lambda, \cdot \right \rangle} \right \}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ is an [orthogonal basis](wikipedia://en/755400) of $L^2(\Omega)$. The set $\Lambda$ is then said to be a spectrum of $\Omega$ and $(\Omega, \Lambda)$ is called a spectral pair.

**Translational tiles of** $\mathbb{R}^d$

A set $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ is said to tile $\mathbb{R}^d$ by translation (i.e. $\Omega$ is a translational tile) if there exist a discrete set $\mathrm{T}$ such that $\bigcup_{t\in\mathrm{T}}(\Omega + t)=\mathbb{R}^d$ and $(\Omega + t) \cap (\Omega + t')$ has Lebesgue [measure zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_zero) for all $t\neq t'$ in $\mathrm{T}$.[^o4]

## Partial results {#Partial_results}

- Fuglede [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) in 1974 that the conjecture holds if $\Omega$ is a [fundamental domain](wikipedia://en/469365) of a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246).
- In 2003, Alex Iosevich, [Nets Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nets_Katz) and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) proved that the conjecture holds if $\Omega$ is a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) planar domain.[^o6]
- In 2004, Terence Tao showed that the conjecture is false on $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ for $d\geq5$.[^o7] It was later shown by Bálint Farkas, Mihail N. Kolounzakis, Máté Matolcsi and Péter Móra that the conjecture is also false for $d=3$ and $4$.[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] However, the conjecture remains unknown for $d=1,2$.
- In 2015, Alex Iosevich, Azita Mayeli and Jonathan Pakianathan showed that an extension of the conjecture holds in $\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}$, where $\mathbb{Z}_{p}$ is the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) *p*.[^o12]
- In 2017, Rachel Greenfeld and Nir Lev proved the conjecture for [convex polytopes](wikipedia://en/1817885) in $\mathbb{R}^3$.[^o13]
- In 2019, Nir Lev and Máté Matolcsi settled the conjecture for convex domains affirmatively in all dimensions.[^o14]
