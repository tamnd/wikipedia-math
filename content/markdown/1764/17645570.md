# Philosophical theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philosophical theory"}

A **philosophical theory** is a [view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_view_%28philosophy%29) that attempts to explain or account for a particular problem in [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy). The use of the term "theory" is a statement of [colloquial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloquialism) English and not a technical term.[^o2] While any sort of [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) or [opinion](wikipedia://en/1348330) may be termed a position, in [analytic philosophy](wikipedia://en/159211) it is thought best to reserve the word "theory" for systematic, comprehensive attempts to solve problems.[^o3]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

The elements that comprise a philosophical position consist of [statements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_%28logic%29) which are [believed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief) to be [true](wikipedia://en/29920) by the thinkers who accept them, and which may or may not be [empirical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism). The sciences have a very clear idea of what a theory is; however in the arts such as philosophy, the definition is more hazy. Philosophical positions are not necessarily [scientific theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_theory), although they may consist of both empirical and non-empirical statements.

The collective statements of all [philosophical movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_movement), [schools of thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_thought), and [belief systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief_system) consist of philosophical positions. Also included among philosophical positions are many [principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle), [dogmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma), [doctrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine), [hypotheses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis), [rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_%28philosophy%29), [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390), [laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law), as well as 'ologies, 'isms, 'sis's, and [effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_effects).[^o5]

Some examples of philosophical positions include:

- [Metatheory](wikipedia://en/4823199); positions about the formation and content of theorems, such as [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)'s [incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incompleteness_theorem).
- [Political theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_theory); positions that underlie a [political philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy), such as [John Rawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rawls)' [theory of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Theory_of_Justice).
- [Ethical theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical_theory) and [meta-ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-ethics); positions about the nature and purpose of [ethical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethical) statements,[^o6] such as the [ethical theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantianism#Ethics) of [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant).
- [Critical theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_theory); in its narrow sense, a [Western European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Europe) body of [Frankfurt School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School) [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) thought that aims at criticizing and transforming, rather than merely explaining, social structures.[^o7] In a broader sense, "critical theory" relates to a wide variety of political, literary, and philosophical positions that take at least some of their inspiration from the Frankfurt School and its dialectic, and that typically contest the possibility of objectivity or aloofness from political positions and privileges.[^o8]

Philosophical positions may also take the form of a [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), [philosophy of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_life), [ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology), [world view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_view), or [life stance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_stance).
