# Nikodym set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nikodym set"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Nikodym set** is a subset of the unit square in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with complement of [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) zero (i.e. with an area of 1), such that, given any point in the set, there is a straight line that only intersects the set at that point.[^o2] The existence of a Nikodym set was first proved by [Otto Nikodym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nikodym) in 1927. Subsequently, constructions were found of Nikodym sets having continuum many exceptional lines for each point, and [Kenneth Falconer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Falconer_%28mathematician%29) found analogues in higher dimensions.[^o3]

:::

Nikodym sets are closely related to [Kakeya sets](wikipedia://en/588260) (also known as Besicovitch sets).

The existence of Nikodym sets is sometimes compared with the [Banach–Tarski paradox](wikipedia://en/19759220). There is, however, an important difference between the two: the Banach–Tarski paradox relies on non-measurable sets.

Mathematicians have also researched Nikodym sets over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) (as opposed to $\mathbb{R}$).[^o4]
