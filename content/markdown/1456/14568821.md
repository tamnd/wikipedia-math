# Naum Z. Shor

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Naum Z. Shor"}

**Naum Zuselevich Shor** (Наум Зуселевич Шор) (1 January 1937 – 26 February 2006) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29).

:::

He made significant contributions to [nonlinear](wikipedia://en/679709) and [stochastic programming](wikipedia://en/501898), numerical techniques for [non-smooth optimization](wikipedia://en/9891368), [discrete optimization](wikipedia://en/420567) problems, [matrix optimization](wikipedia://en/4993539), [dual quadratic bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_duality) in [multi-extremal programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiobjective_optimization) problems.

Shor became a full member of the [National Academy of Science of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Science_of_Ukraine) in 1998.

## Subgradient methods {#Subgradient_methods}

N. Z. Shor is well known for his [method](wikipedia://en/15237) of [generalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgradient) [gradient descent](wikipedia://en/201489) with [space dilation](wikipedia://en/7883127) in the direction of the difference of two successive [subgradients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgradient) (the so-called r-algorithm), that was created in collaboration with Nikolay G. Zhurbenko.[^o4][^o5] The [ellipsoid method](wikipedia://en/4331203) was re-invigorated by A.S. Nemirovsky and D.B. Yudin, who developed a careful [complexity analysis](wikipedia://en/2230) of its [approximation](wikipedia://en/563105) properties for problems of [convex minimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_minimization) with real data. However, it was [Leonid Khachiyan](wikipedia://en/565462) who provided the rational-arithmetic complexity analysis, using an [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/4331203) **[algorithm](wikipedia://en/775)**, that established that [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) problems can be solved in polynomial time.

It has long been known that the ellipsoidal methods are special cases of these subgradient-type methods.

## R-algorithm {#R-algorithm}

Shor's **r-algorithm** is for unconstrained minimization of (possibly) non-smooth functions,[^o7] which has been somewhat popular despite an unknown [convergence rate](wikipedia://en/999701).[^o8] It can be viewed as a [quasi-Newton method](wikipedia://en/7883127), although it does not satisfy the secant equation. Although the method involves [subgradients](wikipedia://en/3462862), it is distinct from his so-called *subgradient method* described above.
