# Double exponential function

*Image: Double Exponential Function.svg.* A double exponential function (red curve) compared to a single exponential function (blue curve).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double exponential function"}

A **double exponential** function is a [constant](wikipedia://en/24758132) raised to the power of an [exponential function](wikipedia://en/99491). The general formula is $f(x) = a^{b^x}=a^{(b^x)}$ (where $a > 1$ and $b > 1$), which grows much more quickly than an exponential function. For example, if $a = b = 10$:

:::

- $$f(x) = 10^{10^{x}}$$
- $$f(0) = 10$$
- $$f(1) = 10^{10}$$
- $$f(2) = 10^{100} = \operatorname{googol}$$
- $$f(3) = 10^{1000}$$
- $$f(100) = 10^{10^{100}} = \operatorname{googolplex}.$$

[Factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) grow faster than exponential functions, but much more slowly than double exponential functions. However, [tetration](wikipedia://en/482379) and the [Ackermann function](wikipedia://en/2925) grow faster. See [Big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578) for a comparison of the rate of growth of various functions.

The inverse of the double exponential function is the [double logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860#double_logarithm) $\log(\log(x))$. The complex double exponential function is [entire](wikipedia://en/10372), because it is the composition of two entire functions $f(x)=a^x=e^{x\ln a}$ and $g(x)=b^x=e^{x\ln b}$.

## Double exponential sequences {#Double_exponential_sequences}

A sequence of positive integers (or real numbers) is said to have *double exponential rate of growth* if the function giving the $n$th term of the sequence is bounded above and below by double exponential functions of $n$. Examples include

- The [Fermat numbers](wikipedia://en/91127) $$F(m) = 2^{2^m}+1$$
- The harmonic primes: The primes $p$, in which the sequence $1/2 + 1/3 + 1/5 + 1/7 + \cdots + 1/p$ exceeds 0, 1, 2, 3, …The first few numbers, starting with 0, are 2, 5, 277, 5195977, ...
- The [Double Mersenne numbers](wikipedia://en/627828) $$MM(p) = 2^{2^p-1}-1$$
- The elements of [Sylvester's sequence](wikipedia://en/4535485) $$s_n = \left\lfloor E^{2^{n+1}}+\frac12 \right\rfloor$$ where $E \approx 1.264084735305302$ is [Vardi's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vardi's_constant) .
- The number of [$k$-ary](wikipedia://en/42301) [Boolean functions](wikipedia://en/753349): $$2^{2^k}$$
- The prime numbers 2, 11, 1361, ... $$a(n) = \left\lfloor A^{3^n}\right\rfloor$$ where $A \approx 1.306377883863$ is [Mills' constant](wikipedia://en/1785049).

[Aho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Aho) and [Sloane](wikipedia://en/400062) observed that in several important [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421), each term is a constant plus the square of the previous term. They show that such sequences can be formed by rounding to the nearest integer the values of a double exponential function with middle exponent 2.[^o3] Ionaşcu and Stănică describe some more general sufficient conditions for a sequence to be the floor of a double exponential sequence plus a constant.[^o4]

## Applications {#Applications}

### Algorithmic complexity {#Algorithmic_complexity}

In [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [2-EXPTIME](wikipedia://en/11387202) is the class of decision problems solvable in double exponential time. It is equivalent to AEXPSPACE, the set of decision problems solvable by an [alternating Turing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_Turing_machine) in exponential space, and is a superset of [EXPSPACE](wikipedia://en/54703).[^o8] An example of a problem in 2-EXPTIME that is not in EXPTIME is the problem of proving or disproving statements in [Presburger arithmetic](wikipedia://en/23756).[^o9]

In some other problems in the design and analysis of algorithms, double exponential sequences are used within the design of an algorithm rather than in its analysis. An example is [Chan's algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan's_algorithm) for computing [convex hulls](wikipedia://en/40634), which performs a sequence of computations using test values $h_{i} = 2^{2^{i}}$ (estimates for the eventual output size), taking time $O(n \log h_{i})$ for each test value in the sequence. Because of the double exponential growth of these test values, the time for each computation in the sequence grows singly exponentially as a function of $i$, and the total time is dominated by the time for the final step of the sequence. Thus, the overall time for the algorithm is $O(n \log h)$ where $h$ is the actual output size.[^o10]

In a different direction, Witteveen & Jeffery showed that any [QMA](wikipedia://en/23545978) protocol can be amplified (see [BPP](wikipedia://en/4079)) to have doubly-exponential error (with mild constraints on choice of gate sets).[^o11]

### Number theory {#Number_theory}

Some [number theoretical](wikipedia://en/21527) bounds are double exponential. [Odd perfect numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_perfect_number) with $n$ distinct prime factors are known to be at most $2^{4^n}$, a result of Nielsen (2003).[^o13]

The maximal volume of a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) in a $d$-dimensional [integer lattice](wikipedia://en/3333402) with $k \ge 1$ [interior lattice points](wikipedia://en/25292663) is at most

$$k\cdot(8d)^d\cdot15^{d\cdot2^{2d+1}},$$

a result of Pikhurko (2001).[^o14]

The [largest known prime number](wikipedia://en/1669332) in the electronic era has grown roughly as a double exponential function of the year since [Miller](wikipedia://en/20706909) and [Wheeler](wikipedia://en/532495) found a 79-digit prime on [EDSAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSAC)1 in 1951.[^o15]

### Theoretical biology {#Theoretical_biology}

In [population dynamics](wikipedia://en/501466) the growth of human population is sometimes supposed to be double exponential. Varfolomeyev and Gurevich[^o17] experimentally fit

$$N(y)=375.6\cdot 1.00185^{1.00737^{y-1000}} \,$$

where $N(y)$ is the population in millions in year $y$.

### Physics {#Physics}

In the [Toda oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toda_oscillator) model of [self-pulsation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-pulsation), the logarithm of amplitude varies exponentially with time (for large amplitudes), thus the amplitude varies as double exponential function of time.[^o19]

Dendritic [macromolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecule) have been observed to grow in a doubly-exponential fashion.[^o20]
