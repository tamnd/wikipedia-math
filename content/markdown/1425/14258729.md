# Kleene–Rosser paradox

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kleene–Rosser paradox"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kleene–Rosser paradox** shows that certain systems of [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic) are [inconsistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inconsistent), in particular the version of [Haskell Curry](wikipedia://en/42182)'s [combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848) introduced in 1930, and [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731)'s original [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203), introduced in 1932–1933, both originally intended as systems of formal logic. The paradox was exhibited by [Stephen Kleene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kleene) and [J. B. Rosser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Rosser) in 1935.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=named-heading name="The paradox"}

## The paradox {#The_paradox}

Kleene and Rosser were able to show that both systems are able to characterize and enumerate their provably total, definable number-theoretic functions, which enabled them to construct a term that essentially replicates [Richard's paradox](wikipedia://en/97812) in formal language.

Curry later managed to identify the crucial ingredients of the calculi that allowed the construction of this paradox, and used this to construct a much simpler paradox, now known as [Curry's paradox](wikipedia://en/154163#Lambda_calculus).
