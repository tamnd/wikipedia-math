# Sherri Rose

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sherri Rose"}

**Sherri Rose** is an American biostatistician. She is an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of health care policy at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and once worked at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). A fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), she has served as co-editor of *[Biostatistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistics_%28journal%29)* since 2019 and Chair of the American Statistical Association’s Biometrics Section. Her research focuses on statistical machine learning for health care policy.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rose was born and raised in poverty and home violence in [Southern New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jersey).[^o4] She was evicted twice as a child and would often go to bed hungry.[^o5] Following high school, she attended [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) for her [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in statistics and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) for her PhD in Biostatistics.[^o6] Rose was originally enrolled in George Washington's pre-med mechanical and aerospace engineering program but chose to change her major to statistics.[^o4]

While completing her PhD under the guidance of [Mark van der Laan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_van_der_Laan), they co-authored a book on machine learning for [causal inference](wikipedia://en/37103476) titled *Targeted Learning: Causal Inference for Observational and Experimental Data.*[^o7][^o5] Her work was recognized with the Evelyn Fix Memorial Prize and the Chin-Long Chiang Biostatistics Student of the Year Award.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

*Image: Harvard Medical School, Boston.JPG.* A photo of Harvard Medical School, where Rose worked

Rose completed her postdoctoral research fellowship at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) (JHU) before joining [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School) as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) of Biostatistics. While at JHU, she received their Delta Omega Scholarship and a Young Investigator Award from the International Conference on Advances in Interdisciplinary Statistics and Combinatorics.[^o8] Following her first year at Harvard, she was elected to join the editorial board of the [Journal of the American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/16179313) Theory and Methods as an associate editor.[^o10] Rose also co-founded the Health Policy [Data Science](wikipedia://en/35458904) Lab with [Laura Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Hatfield) to study spending levels in markets, spending goals for accountable care organizations, and mental health outcomes.[^o11] She was later elected Secretary/Treasurer of the Biometrics Section of the American Statistical Association[^o12] and promoted to the role of [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor).[^o13]

In her role as an associate professor, Rose continued to study statistical machine learning for health care policy. She published a paper in 2017 which used statistical machine learning to determine [health economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_economics) and outcomes. The paper demonstrated that "new statistical machine learning methods may be better able to search the claims data used for risk adjustment in order to predict health spending".[^o14] As a result of her academic research, Rose was the recipient of the inaugural Harvard Data Science Initiative Grant to fund her proposed project "Improving Health Care System Performance: Computational Health Economics with Normative Data for Payment Calibration."[^o15] Another one of her studies, titled *Targeted Maximum Likelihood Estimation for Causal Inference in Observational Studies,* was selected as one of the [American Journal of Epidemiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Epidemiology) and Society for Epidemiologic Research’s 2017 Articles of the Year.[^o16] In the same year, she was also one of eight Harvard researchers awarded federal funding from the [National Institutes of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health)’s "High-Risk, High-Reward" research program.[^o17]

As she approached the end of her tenure at Harvard, Rose became the first women elected co-editor of the peer-reviewed scientific journal *[Biostatistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biostatistics_%28journal%29).*[^o18] She also published a sequel to her first book with Mark van der Laan[^o19] and was the recipient of the Bernie J. O’Brien New Investigator Award.[^o20] In her final year, she was elected a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988)[^o21] and awarded the Health Policy Statistics Section Mid-Career Award from the American Statistical Association.[^o22] Rose eventually left Harvard to join the faculty of health policy at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o23]

### Stanford {#Stanford}

As an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of medicine at Stanford, Rose was the recipient of the 2021 Gertrude M. Cox Award for her work applying statistics to improve health care.[^o25] Later that year, she was honored with the Mortimer Spiegelman Award, as a young biostatistician who has made the most significant contributions to public health statistics.[^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Rose is married to Burke, a systems administrator at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o23]
