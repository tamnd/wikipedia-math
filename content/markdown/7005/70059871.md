# Luis Fernando Alday

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Luis Fernando Alday"}

**Luis Fernando Alday** is an Argentine-British mathematician, and the [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and Head of the Mathematical Physics Group.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Alday was born on 17 October 1977 in [San Rafael, Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Rafael,_Mendoza), but he spent most of his youth in [Arrecifes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrecifes). He was the eldest child among three siblings.[^n1] He graduated from the [Balseiro Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balseiro_Institute) at the [National University of Cuyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Cuyo) in Argentina in 2001,[^o5] after studying for the first two years at the [National University of La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_La_Plata).[^o6] He completed his PhD at the [International School for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_for_Advanced_Studies) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) in 2004. He was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) for Spring 2007, and from September 2008 to September 2010.[^o7] At the IAS, he collaborated with [Juan Maldacena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Maldacena), a fellow Argentine physicist and Balseiro graduate. Alday joined the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 2010 after post-doctorate studies at the IAS and [Utrecht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_University). Since then, he has been associated with [Hertford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertford_College,_Oxford).[^n1] His research interests include a bootstrap approach to conformal [field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28physics%29) and [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), several aspects of the [AdS/CFT duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AdS/CFT_duality), four-dimensional N=2 [super-symmetric theories](wikipedia://en/53961341) and their relation to conformal field theories, and the exact computation of [observables](wikipedia://en/294248) in super-symmetric [gauge theories](wikipedia://en/23912155). He received a [Royal Society Wolfson Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Fellowship) in 2012, and the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2013. Alday has also received two grants from the [European Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council).[^o9]

Alday's research focuses on providing mathematical tools for applications in areas of physics such as [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312). He was appointed the [Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/649081) and head of the Mathematical Physics Group in 2020.[^o10] Alday was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in May 2022.[^o11] He was recognised as such for two groundbreaking papers. The first provides a method for computing "[scattering amplitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_amplitude) in planar super-symmetric [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202) at strong [coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_%28physics%29)". The second paper introduced the concept of [AGT correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGT_correspondence), partly named after Alday and developed jointly with [Davide Gaiotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Gaiotto) and Yuji Tachikawa, "relates four dimensional supersymmetric gauge theories to two dimensional conformal field theories".[^o12] At the time of his election to the Royal Society, he was a Fellow of [Wadham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadham_College,_Oxford) and associated with the Oxford [Mathematical Institute](wikipedia://en/7905850).[^o13][^o14]
