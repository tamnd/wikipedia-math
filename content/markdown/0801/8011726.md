# Costa's minimal surface

*Image: Costa's minimal surface.ogv.* Costa's minimal surface, cropped by a sphere

*Image: Flower Costa minimal surface.stl.* [STL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STL_%28file_format%29) model of the surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Costa's minimal surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Costa's surface** is an embedded [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) discovered in 1982 by the Brazilian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Celso José da Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celso_Costa). It is also a surface of finite topology, which means that it can be formed by puncturing a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) surface. Topologically, it is a thrice-punctured [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).

:::

Until its discovery, the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), [helicoid](wikipedia://en/684207) and the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329) were believed to be the only embedded minimal surfaces that could be formed by puncturing a compact surface. The Costa surface evolves from a torus, which is deformed until the planar [end](wikipedia://en/6862105) becomes catenoidal. Defining these surfaces on rectangular tori of arbitrary dimensions yields the Costa surface. Its discovery triggered research and discovery into several new surfaces and open [conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjectures) in topology.

The Costa surface can be described using the [Weierstrass zeta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_zeta_function) and the [Weierstrass elliptic function](wikipedia://en/447181).
