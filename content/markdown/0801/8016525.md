# Moore space (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moore space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically [point-set topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-set_topology), a **Moore space** is a [developable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developable_space) [regular Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Hausdorff_space). That is, a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* is a Moore space if the following conditions hold:

:::

- Any two distinct points can be [separated by neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separated_by_neighbourhoods), and any [closed set](wikipedia://en/47279) and any point in its [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) can be separated by neighbourhoods. (*X* is a [regular Hausdorff space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Hausdorff_space).)
- There is a [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) collection of [open covers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of *X*, such that for any closed set *C* and any point *p* in its complement there exists a cover in the collection such that every neighbourhood of *p* in the cover is [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) from *C*. (*X* is a [developable space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developable_space).)

Moore spaces are generally interesting in mathematics because they may be applied to prove interesting [metrization theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem). The concept of a Moore space was formulated by [R. L. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._L._Moore) in the earlier part of the 20th century.

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

1. Every [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738), *X*, is a Moore space. If {*A*<sup>(*n*)</sup><sub>*x*</sub>} is the open cover of *X* (indexed by *x* in *X*) by all balls of radius 1/*n*, then the collection of all such open covers as *n* varies over the positive integers is a development of *X*. Since all metrizable spaces are normal, all metric spaces are Moore spaces.
1. Moore spaces are different from [normal spaces](wikipedia://en/48629) in the sense that every [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926) of a Moore space is also a Moore space.
1. The image of a Moore space under an injective, continuous open map is always a Moore space. (The image of a regular space under an injective, continuous open map is always regular.)
1. Both examples 2 and 3 suggest that Moore spaces are similar to regular spaces.
1. Neither the [Sorgenfrey line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorgenfrey_line) nor the [Sorgenfrey plane](wikipedia://en/682853) are Moore spaces because they are normal and not [second countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_countable).
1. The [Moore plane](wikipedia://en/5516188) (also known as the Niemytski plane) is an example of a non-metrizable Moore space.
1. Every [metacompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacompact), [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), normal Moore space is metrizable. This theorem is known as Traylor's theorem.
1. Every [locally compact](wikipedia://en/48632), [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected) normal Moore space is metrizable. This theorem was proved by Reed and Zenor.
1. If $2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\aleph_1}$, then every [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [normal](wikipedia://en/48629) Moore space is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable). This theorem is known as Jones’ theorem.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Normal Moore space conjecture"}

## Normal Moore space conjecture {#Normal_Moore_space_conjecture}

For a long time, topologists were trying to prove the so-called normal Moore space conjecture: every normal Moore space is [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable). This was inspired by the fact that all known Moore spaces that were not metrizable were also not normal. This would have been a nice [metrization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorems). There were some nice partial results at first; namely properties 7, 8 and 9 as given in the previous section.

With property 9, we see that we can drop metacompactness from Traylor's theorem, but at the cost of a set-theoretic assumption. Another example of this is [Fleissner's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleissner's_theorem) that the [axiom of constructibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V=L) implies that locally compact, normal Moore spaces are metrizable.

On the other hand, under the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) (CH) and also under [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783) and not CH, there are several examples of non-metrizable normal Moore spaces. Nyikos proved that, under the so-called PMEA (Product Measure Extension Axiom), which needs a [large cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinal_axiom), all normal Moore spaces are metrizable. Finally, it was shown later that any model of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) in which the conjecture holds, implies the existence of a model with a large cardinal. So large cardinals are needed essentially.

(Jones 1937) gave an example of a [pseudonormal](wikipedia://en/5075270) Moore space that is not metrizable, so the conjecture cannot be strengthened in this way. [Moore](wikipedia://en/1077133) himself proved the theorem that a [collectionwise normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectionwise_normal) Moore space is metrizable, so strengthening normality is another way to settle the matter.
