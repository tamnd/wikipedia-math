# Hedgehog space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hedgehog space"}

*Image: Hedgehog space.svg.* A hedgehog space with a large but finite number of spines

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **hedgehog space** is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) consisting of a set of spines joined at a point.

:::

For any [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173) $\kappa$, the $\kappa$-hedgehog space is formed by taking the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of $\kappa$ [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [unit intervals](wikipedia://en/49324) identified at the origin (though its [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) is not the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology), but that defined by the [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) below). Each unit interval is referred to as one of the hedgehog's *spines.* A $\kappa$-hedgehog space is sometimes called a **hedgehog space of spininess $\kappa$**.

The hedgehog space is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), when endowed with the **hedgehog metric** $d(x,y)=\left| x - y \right|$ if $x$ and $y$ lie in the same spine, and by $d(x,y)=\left|x\right| + \left|y\right|$ if $x$ and $y$ lie in different spines. Although their disjoint union makes the origins of the intervals distinct, the metric makes them equivalent by assigning them 0 distance.

Hedgehog spaces are examples of [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="French railroad metric"}

## Paris metric {#Paris_metric}

The metric on the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) in which the distance between any two points is their [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) when the two points belong to a [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray) through the origin, and is otherwise the sum of the distances of the two points from the origin, is sometimes called the **Paris metric**[^o2] because navigation in this metric resembles that in the radial street plan of [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989): for almost all pairs of points, the shortest path passes through the center. The Paris metric, restricted to the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738), is a hedgehog space where *K* is the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Kowalsky's theorem"}

## Kowalsky's theorem {#Kowalsky's_theorem}

Kowalsky's theorem, named after Hans-Joachim Kowalsky,[^o7][^o8] states that any [metrizable space](wikipedia://en/19738) of [weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_of_a_topological_space) $\kappa$ can be represented as a [topological subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_subspace) of the [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of [countably many](wikipedia://en/6026) $\kappa$-hedgehog spaces.

:::
