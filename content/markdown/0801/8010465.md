The **[compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound) of ten [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606)**, also known as the icosicosahedron, is one of the five regular polyhedral compounds. This polyhedron can be seen as either a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) or a [compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound). This compound was first described by [Edmund Hess](wikipedia://en/9191575) in 1876.

:::

It can be seen as a faceting of a regular dodecahedron.

## As a compound {#As_a_compound}

*Image: Spherical compound of ten tetrahedra.png.* As a [spherical tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_tiling)

It can also be seen as the [compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound) of ten [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) with [full icosahedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_icosahedral_symmetry) (**I**<sub>h</sub>). It is one of five regular compounds constructed from identical [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905).

It shares the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644) as a [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407).

The [compound of five tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/3645795) represents two chiral halves of this compound (it can therefore be seen as a "compound of two compounds of five tetrahedra").

It can be made from the [compound of five cubes](wikipedia://en/8010502) by replacing each cube with a [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula) on the cube's vertices (which results in a "compound of five compounds of two tetrahedra").

## As a stellation {#As_a_stellation}

This [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) is a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084), and given as [Wenninger model index 25](wikipedia://en/2915428#Stellations_of_icosahedron).

## As a facetting {#As_a_facetting}

*Image: Icosiicosahedron-in-dodecahedron.png.* Ten tetrahedra in a dodecahedron.

It is also a [facetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facetting) of the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), as shown at left. Concave [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544) can be seen on the compound where the pentagonal faces of the dodecahedron are positioned.

## As a simple polyhedron {#As_a_simple_polyhedron}

If it is treated as a simple non-convex polyhedron without self-intersecting surfaces, it has 180 faces (120 triangles and 60 concave quadrilaterals), 122 vertices (60 with degree 3, 30 with degree 4, 12 with degree 5, and 20 with degree 12), and 300 edges, giving an [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of 122-300+180 = +2.
