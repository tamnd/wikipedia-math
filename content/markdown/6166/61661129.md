# Elena Moldovan Popoviciu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elena Moldovan Popoviciu"}

**Elena Moldovan Popoviciu** (26 August 1924–24 June 2009) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) mathematician known for her work in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and specializing in generalizations of the concept of a [convex function](wikipedia://en/245568). She was a winner of the [Simion Stoilow Prize](wikipedia://en/20268944) in mathematics.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Elena Moldovan was born in [Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca) to Ioan Moldovan and his wife, Rozalia. She studied mathematics at the [Victor Babeș University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University) in Cluj, earning a bachelor's degree there in 1947; afterwards, she became a schoolteacher. She returned to the university for doctoral study in the early 1950s, initially working with Grigore Calugăreanu, but she soon came under the influence of [Tiberiu Popoviciu](wikipedia://en/39854514) and began working with him in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). She completed her Ph.D. in 1960. Her dissertation, *Sets of Interpolating Functions And The Notion of Convex Function*, was supervised by Popoviciu. She married Popoviciu in 1964, remained at the university, and became a full professor there in 1969.

During her career, she supervised the Ph.D. thesis of 23 students. She served as the second editor-in-chief of the journal *Revue d’Analyse Numérique et de Théorie de l’Approximation*, founded in 1972 by her husband.

She died in Cluj-Napoca on June 24, 2009.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Popoviciu won the [Simion Stoilow Prize](wikipedia://en/20268944) of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy), for her achievements in mathematics, in 1972. A conference in honor of Popoviciu's 75th birthday was held in 1999 at the [Babeș-Bolyai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University) in Cluj, and a second conference in her honor was held five years later.
