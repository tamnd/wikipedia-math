# Leonid Vaserstein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leonid Vaserstein"}

**Leonid Nisonovich Vaserstein** (Леонид Нисонович Васерштейн) is a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians)-[American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), currently [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Penn State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_University).[^o3] His research is focused on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). He is well known for providing a simple proof of the [Quillen–Suslin theorem](wikipedia://en/539562), a result in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), first conjectured by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) in 1955, and then proved by [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) and [Andrei Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820) in 1976.[^o4]

:::

Leonid Vaserstein got his [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) and [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), where he was until 1978. He then moved to [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).

Alternate forms of the last name: Vaseršteĭn, Vasershtein, [Wasserstein](wikipedia://en/6704603).

The [Wasserstein metric](wikipedia://en/6704603) was named after him by [R.L. Dobrushin](wikipedia://en/6368767) in 1970.

## Biography {#Biography}

Leonid Vaserstein grew up in the Soviet Union. In secondary school he won the second prize in the All-Russian High School Mathematical Olympiad.[^o6] Vaserstein got his undergraduate, masters (1966), and doctoral degrees (1969) in mathematics from Moscow State University, where he worked as a lecturer concurrently with his doctoral research. After his doctoral graduation he worked for the Moscow State University-associated "Informelectro" Institute, a [Federal State Unitary Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_Unitary_Enterprise) focused on ways to develop industries in Russia with emphases on electrical engineering, energy efficiency, and environmental technologies like greenhouse gas mitigation.[^o7] He started as a senior researcher for Informelectro and continued working there until 1978, eventually becoming head of his department. In 1978 and 1979 he made his way to the United States of America by way of Europe, taking a series of visiting professor positions at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), [Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_des_Hautes_Études_Scientifiques), [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o8] In 1979, Vaserstein took a full-time position as a professor in the Department of Mathematics at [Penn State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_University).

Vaserstein's research interests extend across the areas of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), algebra, and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and the applications of these areas, including classical groups over rings, [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500), systems with local interactions, and optimization and planning. Additionally, Vaserstein maintains the Penn State University Math Department's website on Algebra and Number Theory.[^o9]
