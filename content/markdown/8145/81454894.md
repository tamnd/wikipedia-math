# Rosemarie Lierke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rosemarie Lierke"}

**Rosemarie Lierke**, born **Rosemarie Lindner** (December 1934 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin)), is a German glass artist, trained mathematician and a researcher into the history and technology of ancient glass production. Some of her artwork is held in the [Museum Kunstpalast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Kunstpalast) in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Düsseldorf), Germany.[^o2][^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

After graduating from high school in 1953 in Schnepfenthal, [Thuringia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuringia), Germany, Lierke studied mathematics in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) from 1953 to 1960. From 1962 to 1967, she was a research assistant at the [Technical University of Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University). She also began to work with enameling, for which she built her own kiln for high-temperature tasks.[^o5]

In 1967, she moved to [Toledo, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toledo,_Ohio) in the United States with her husband, the physicist Ernst-Günter Lierke, and their two sons. At that time, Toledo called itself the "Glass Capital of the World." There she learned about the American [studio glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_glass) movement and began making new artwork in glass, which she continued to do after she returned to Germany in 1970.[^o5]

*Image: Portland Vase BM Gem4036 n5.jpg.* [Portland Vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Vase): example of cameo glass

From 1975 to 1979, Lierke worked as a mathematics teacher at the high school in Sulzbach (Taunus). From 1977 to 1987, she retreated to her glassblowing workshop in [Schwalbach am Taunus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwalbach_am_Taunus), investigating lamp-working techniques and inventing a new [craquelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craquelure) technique, which she [patented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) in 1979.[^o5]

In 1987, she returned to the United States, this time going to [Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena,_California) so her husband could conduct [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA)-based research. She closed her workshop and intensified her scientific research into non-blown ancient glass vessels.[^o5]

*Image: Hedwig-glas (cropped).jpeg.* Sample Hedwig glass with eagle ([Rijksmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmuseum))

Based on her practical work with glass and her study of ancient and medieval glasses, Lierke presented new ideas for the manufacture of glasses, especially [cameo glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_glass), the [cage cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cage_cup) and the medieval [Hedwig glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedwig_glass).[^o6] Her work proved to be hotly debated and controversial in the scientific community.[^o7]

To mark Lierke's 90th birthday, an exhibition of her works took place at the [Bad Driburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Driburg) Glass Museum from January to August 2025.[^o8]
