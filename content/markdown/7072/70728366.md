# Morphism of finite type

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Morphism of finite type"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), given a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $A\to B$ of [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), $B$ is called an $A$-[algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) of **finite type** if $B$ can be [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/11985661) as an $A$-[algebra](wikipedia://en/191788#generalization:_algebra_over_a_ring). It is much stronger for $B$ to be a **finite** $A$-algebra, which means that $B$ is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/326430) as an $A$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). For example, for any commutative ring $A$ and natural number $n$, the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $A[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type, but it is not a finite $A$-algebra unless $A$ = 0 or $n$ = 0. Another example of a finite-type homomorphism that is not finite is $\mathbb{C}[t] \to \mathbb{C}[t][x,y]/(y^2 - x^3 - t)$.

:::

The analogous notion in terms of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) is that a [morphism](wikipedia://en/7482797) $f:X\to Y$ of schemes is of **finite type** if $Y$ has a covering by [affine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) [open](wikipedia://en/39358) subschemes $V_i=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ such that $f^{-1}(V_i)$ has a finite covering by affine open subschemes $U_{ij}=\operatorname{Spec}(B_{ij})$ of $X$ with $B_{ij}$ an $A_i$-algebra of finite type. One also says that $X$ is of **finite type** over $Y$.

For example, for any natural number $n$ and [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$, [affine $n$-space](wikipedia://en/298834) and [projective $n$-space](wikipedia://en/242135) over $k$ are of finite type over $k$ (that is, over $\operatorname{Spec}(k)$), while they are not finite over $k$ unless $n$ = 0. More generally, any [quasi-projective scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-projective_scheme) over $k$ is of finite type over $k$.

The [Noether normalization lemma](wikipedia://en/3003553) says, in geometric terms, that every affine scheme $X$ of finite type over a field $k$ has a [finite](wikipedia://en/2139357) surjective morphism to affine space $\mathbf{A}^n$ over $k$, where $n$ is the [dimension](wikipedia://en/58058216) of $X$. Likewise, every [projective scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_scheme) $X$ over a field has a finite surjective morphism to [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbf{P}^n$, where $n$ is the dimension of $X$.
