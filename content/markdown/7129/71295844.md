# Capacitated arc routing problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Capacitated arc routing problem"}

In mathematics, the **capacitated arc routing problem (CARP)** is that of finding the shortest tour with a minimum graph/travel distance of a [mixed graph](wikipedia://en/38713429) with undirected edges and directed arcs given capacity constraints for objects that move along the graph that represent snow-plowers, street sweeping machines, or winter gritters, or other real-world objects with capacity constraints. The constraint can be imposed for the length of time the vehicle is away from the central depot, or a total distance traveled, or a combination of the two with different weighting factors.

:::

There are many different variations of the CARP described in the book *Arc Routing:Problems, Methods, and Applications* by Ángel Corberán and Gilbert Laporte.[^o2]

Solving the CARP involves the study of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), arc routing, operations research, and geographical routing algorithms to find the [shortest path](wikipedia://en/41985) efficiently.

The CARP is [NP-hard](wikipedia://en/54681) [arc routing problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_routing_problem).

## Large-scale capacitated arc routing problem {#Large-scale_capacitated_arc_routing_problem}

A large-scale capacitated arc routing problem (LSCARP) is a variant of the CARP that covers 300 or more edges to model complex arc routing problems at large scales.

Yi Mei et al. published an algorithm for solving the large-scale capacitated arc routing problem using a cooperative co-evolution algorithm.[^o4]

LSCARP can be solved with a divide and conquer algorithm applied to route cutting off decomposition.[^o5]

The LSCARP can also be solved with an iterative local search that improves on upper and lower bounds of other methods.[^o6]

An LSCARP algorithm has been applied to waste collection in Denmark with a fast heuristic named FAST-CARP.[^o7]

The algorithm is also often referred to as the Time Capacitated Arc Routing Problem often referred to as TCARP. The TCARP can be solved with a metaheuristic in a reasonable amount of time. The TCARP often arises when volume constraints do not apply for example meter reading[^o8]

Zhang et al. created a metaheuristic to solve a generalization named the large-scale multi-depot CARP (LSMDCARP) named route clustering and search heuristic.[^o9]

An algorithm for the LSCARP named Extension step and statistical filtering for large-scale CARP (ESMAENS) was developed in 2017.[^o10]

The LSCARP can be extended to a large-scale capacitated vehicle routing problem with a hierarchical decomposition algorithm.[^o11] The LSCVRP can be solved with an evolutionary method based on local search.[^o12] Solving the LSCVRP can be done by integrated support vector machines and [random forest](wikipedia://en/1363880) methods.[^o13]

An algorithm to solve LSCARP based on [simulated annealing](wikipedia://en/172244) named FILO was developed by Accorsi et al.[^o14]
