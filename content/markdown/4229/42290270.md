# Mary Beth Ruskai

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Beth Ruskai"}

**Mary Beth Ruskai** (February 26, 1944 – September 27, 2023) was an American mathematical physicist and professor of mathematics with interest in mathematical problems in [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics). She was a Fellow of the [AAAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science),[^o2] [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o3] [APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Physical_Society),[^o4] and [AWM](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o5]

:::

## Personal life and education {#Personal_life_and_education}

Ruskai was the daughter of Michael J. Ruskai and Evelyn M. Ruskai (née Gortz). She had three sisters.[^o7] She graduated from [Notre Dame College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_College) in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio) in 1965 with a BS in chemistry. She simultaneously received her M.A. in mathematics and her Ph.D. in [physical chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_chemistry) from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1969.[^o8] Her PhD thesis was about the *[N-Representability Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-Representability_Problem)*.[^o9]

## Career {#Career}

She was the Battelle postdoctoral fellow in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) at the Institut de Physique Theorique in Geneva Switzerland from 1969 to 1971. She spent most of her career at the [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell), where she was on the faculty from 1977 until she took early retirement in 2002. From 2003 to 2013 she was based at [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), from 2011 to 2016 she was an associate member of the [Institute for Quantum Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Quantum_Computing),[^o8] and from 2016 to 2023 Adjunct Professor at the [University of Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vermont).[^o11]

Her visiting positions included appointments at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University), the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna), the Bunting Institute (later renamed the [Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies)), the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) at [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYU), [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech), [Technische Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Berlin), the [Dublin Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_of_Technology) and the [Institute for Quantum Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Quantum_Computing) in Waterloo, Canada. In 1995 she was the Flora Stone Mather Visiting Professor at [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) in her hometown of Cleveland, Ohio.[^o8] Its predecessor, the Case Institute of Technology did not admit women until after she received her B.S. in 1965.

## Research {#Research}

Ruskai's research focused on mathematics applicable to quantum mechanics. In 1972 she and [Elliot Lieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_H._Lieb) proved the [Strong Subadditivity of Quantum Entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Subadditivity_of_Quantum_Entropy), which was described in 2005 as "the key result on which virtually every nontrivial quantum coding theorem (in [quantum information](wikipedia://en/25274) theory) relies".[^o13][^o14] In 1981 she gave the first proof that an atom can have only a maximum number of electrons bound to it regardless of the charge of its nucleus.[^o15]

## Other activities {#Other_activities}

Ruskai was an organizer of international conferences, especially those with an interdisciplinary focus. Of particular note was her organization of the first US conference on [wavelet](wikipedia://en/50903) theory, at which [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989) gave *Ten Lectures on Wavelets*.[^o17] Ruskai considered this one of her most important achievements.[^o18] She was also an organizer of conferences in [Quantum Information Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_Information), including the Fall 2010 program at the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452),[^o19] as well as a series of workshops at the [Banff International Research Station](wikipedia://en/39151817) and the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768).[^o20]

Throughout her career, Ruskai was an advocate for women in mathematics and published 15 articles on gender and science.[^o21][^o22]

Based on a bequest from the estate of Ruskai the [EDGE Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_Foundation) established in 2024 *The Mary Beth Ruskai Research Fund for Women* to support awardees in their scientific career.[^o23]
