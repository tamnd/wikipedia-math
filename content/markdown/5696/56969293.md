# Jean Bertoin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Bertoin"}

**Jean Bertoin** (born 25 May 1961) is a French mathematician. He is professor at the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich) since 2011.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Bertoin was born in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon) and completed his undergraduate studies at the [École Normale Supérieure of St Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure_of_St_Cloud).[^o5] He received his doctorate from [University of Paris VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VI) in 1987, having been advised by [Marc Yor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Yor).

## Career {#Career}

Bertoin is a specialist in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). His research deals with [Lévy processes](wikipedia://en/1025748), [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), [branching processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branching_processes), random fragmentation, and coalescence processes.[^o5]

In 1988, Bertoin became a researcher at [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS), and was appointed Professor at [Pierre-and-Marie-Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-and-Marie-Curie_University) in 1992.[^o5] In the early 1990s he began a collaboration with [Ron Doney](wikipedia://en/80599670) on the topic of conditioned [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks), with the pair co-writing six papers between 1994 and 1997.[^o8]

Bertoin has received numerous awards, including the CNRS Bronze Medal (1992), the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize) (1996), and the Thérèse Gautier Prize (2015). He was an invited speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) (2002) and [ECM](wikipedia://en/13505846) (2012), and elected a corresponding member of the [Mexican Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2011.[^o5]

His doctoral students include [Grégory Miermont](wikipedia://en/46963901).
