# Microsoft Math Solver

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Microsoft Math Solver"}

**Microsoft Math Solver** (formerly **Microsoft Mathematics** and **Microsoft Math**) was an entry-level educational app that solved math and science problems. Developed and maintained by [Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft), it was primarily targeted at students as a learning tool. Until 2015, it ran on Microsoft Windows. Since then, it has been developed for the [web platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_platform) and [mobile devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_device).

:::

Microsoft Math was originally released as a bundled part of [Microsoft Student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Student). It was then available as a standalone paid version starting with version 3.0. For version 4.0, it was released as a free downloadable product[^o5] and was called Microsoft Mathematics 4.0. It is no longer in active development and has been removed from the Microsoft website.[^o6] A related [freeware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeware) add-in, called "Microsoft Mathematics Add-In for [Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Word) and [OneNote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_OneNote)," was also available from Microsoft and offered comparable functionality (Word 2007 or higher is required).[^o7][^o8]

Microsoft Math received the 2008 Award of Excellence from Tech & Learning Magazine.[^o9]

Microsoft Math was retired on July 7, 2025.[^o10]

## Features {#Features}

Microsoft Math contained features that are designed to assist in solving mathematics, science, and tech-related problems, as well as to educate the user. The application featured such tools as a [graphing calculator](wikipedia://en/335004) and a [unit converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_converter). It also included a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) solver and an [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) solver that provides step-by-step solutions to each problem.

## Versions {#Versions}

- **Microsoft Math 1.0**: Part of [Microsoft Student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Student) 2006
- **Microsoft Math 2.0**: Part of Microsoft Student 2007
- **Microsoft Math 3.0**: Standalone commercial product that requires [product activation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_activation); includes calculus support, digital ink recognition features and a special display mode for [video projectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_projector)
- **Encarta Calculator**: Lite version of Microsoft Math 3.0; part of Microsoft Student 2008
- **Microsoft Mathematics 4.0** (removed): The first [freeware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeware) version, released in 32-bit and 64-bit editions in January 2011;[^o13] features a [ribbon GUI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_%28computing%29)
- **Microsoft Math for Windows Phone** (removed): A branded mobile application for [Windows Phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Phone) released in 2015 specifically for South African and Tanzanian students; also known as *Nokia Mobile-Mathematics* or *Nokia Momaths*[^o14][^o15][^o16]
- **Microsoft Math in Bing app** – Math helper as a feature within the Bing mobile app on iOS and Android platforms, released in August 2018[^o17]
- **Microsoft Math Solver** – [Mobile app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app) for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) (first released in November 2019-No longer available in August 2024.)[^o18] and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29) (first released in December 2019),[^o19] as well as a [Microsoft Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Edge) extension. Recognizes handwritten math. Provides a detailed step-by-step explanation, interactive graphs, relevant online video lectures, and practice problems.
