# Ingo Althöfer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ingo Althöfer"}

*Image: Althöfer,Ingo 2008.jpeg.* Althöfer in 2008

**Ingo Althöfer** (born 1961) is a German mathematician and former professor of [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena).

:::

Althöfer earned his PhD in 1986 at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University). His dissertation, *Asymptotic Properties of Certain Competition Systems in Artificial Intelligence and Ecology*, was supervised by [Rudolf Ahlswede](wikipedia://en/18251788).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Topics in Althöfer's professional research include the realization of [finite metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_metric_space) by [shortest path metrics](wikipedia://en/41985) in graphs and their approximation by [greedy spanners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greedy_geometric_spanner), [algorithmic game theory](wikipedia://en/16334749) and [combinatorial game theory](wikipedia://en/292231), and heuristic [search algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_algorithm) for optimization problems.

Althöfer is also known for his inventions of games and puzzles, including dice game [EinStein würfelt nicht!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EinStein_würfelt_nicht!), for his experiments with [self-assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-assembly) of [Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego) building blocks by running them through a [washing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washing_machine), and for his innovations in computer-human chess playing. In the 1990s he tested his "drei hirn" \["3-brains"\] system, in which a human decides between the choices of two computer chess players, against strong human players including grandmaster [David Bronstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bronstein) and woman grandmaster [Sofia Polgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Polgar). In 2004 he and Timo Klaustermeyer introduced freestyle chess, a style of human chess playing allowing arbitrary consultation with computers or other people.
