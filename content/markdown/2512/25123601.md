# Balanced polygamma function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Balanced polygamma function"}

In mathematics, the **generalized polygamma function** or **balanced negapolygamma function** is a function introduced by Olivier Espinosa Aldunate and [Victor Hugo Moll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo_Moll).[^o2]

:::

It generalizes the [polygamma function](wikipedia://en/406932) to negative and fractional order, but remains equal to it for integer positive orders.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The generalized polygamma function is defined as follows:

$$\psi(z,q)=\frac{\zeta'(z+1,q)+\bigl(\psi(-z)+\gamma \bigr) \zeta (z+1,q)}{\Gamma (-z)}$$

or alternatively,

$$\psi(z,q)=e^{- \gamma z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(e^{\gamma z}\frac{\zeta(z+1,q)}{\Gamma(-z)}\right),$$

where $\psi(z)$ is the [polygamma function](wikipedia://en/406932) and $\zeta(z,q)$, is the [Hurwitz zeta function](wikipedia://en/406952).

The function is balanced, in that it satisfies the conditions

$$f(0)=f(1) \quad \text{and} \quad \int_0^1 f(x)\, dx = 0.$$

:::

## Relations {#Relations}

Several [special functions](wikipedia://en/23709110) can be expressed in terms of generalized polygamma function.

$$\begin{aligned} \psi(x) &= \psi(0,x)\\ \psi^{(n)}(x)&=\psi(n,x) \qquad n\in\mathbb{N} \\ \Gamma(x)&=\exp\left( \psi(-1,x)+\tfrac12 \ln 2\pi \right)\\ \zeta(z, q)&=\frac{(-1)^z}{\Gamma(z)} \psi(z - 1, q)\\ \zeta'(-1,x)&=\psi(-2, x) + \frac{x^2}2 - \frac{x}2 + \frac1{12} \\ \end{aligned}$$

$$K(z)=A \exp\left(\psi(-2,z)+\frac{z^2-z}{2}\right)$$

where $K(z)$ is the [$K$-function](wikipedia://en/2060130) and $A$ is the [Glaisher constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaisher_constant).

## Special values {#Special_values}

The balanced polygamma function can be expressed in a closed form at certain points (where $A$ is the [Glaisher constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaisher_constant) and $G$ is the [Catalan constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_constant)):

$$\begin{aligned} \psi\left(-2,\tfrac14\right)&=\tfrac18\ln A+\frac{G}{4\pi} && \\ \psi\left(-2,\tfrac12\right)&=\tfrac12\ln A-\tfrac{1}{24}\ln 2 & \\ \psi\left(-3,\tfrac12\right)&=\frac{3\zeta(3)}{32\pi^2}\\ \psi(-2,1)&=-\ln A &\\ \psi(-3,1)&=\frac{-\zeta(3)}{8\pi^2}\\ \psi(-2,2)&=-\ln A-1 &\\ \psi(-3,2)&=\frac{-\zeta(3)}{8\pi^2}-\tfrac34 \\\end{aligned}$$
