# Ricci soliton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ricci soliton"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a complete [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M,g)$ is called a **Ricci soliton** if, and only if, there exists a smooth vector field $V$ such that

:::

$$\operatorname{Ric}(g) = \lambda \, g - \frac{1}{2} \mathcal{L}_V g,$$

for some constant $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$. Here $\operatorname{Ric}$ is the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582) tensor and $\mathcal{L}$ represents the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437). If there exists a function $f: M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ such that $V = \nabla f$ we call $(M,g)$ a **gradient Ricci soliton** and the soliton equation becomes

$$\operatorname{Ric}(g) + \nabla^2 f= \lambda \, g.$$

Note that when $V = 0$ or $f = 0$ the above equations reduce to the Einstein equation. For this reason Ricci solitons are a generalization of [Einstein manifolds](wikipedia://en/644814).

## Self-similar solutions to Ricci flow {#Self-similar_solutions_to_Ricci_flow}

A Ricci soliton $(M,g_0)$ yields a [self-similar solution](wikipedia://en/58495269) to the [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291) equation

$$\partial_t g_t = -2 \operatorname{Ric}(g_t).$$

In particular, letting

$$\sigma(t) := 1 - 2 \lambda t$$

and integrating the time-dependent vector field $X(t) := \frac{1}{\sigma(t)} V$ to give a family of diffeomorphisms $\Psi_t$, with $\Psi_0$ the identity, yields a Ricci flow solution $(M, g_t)$ by taking

$$g_t = \sigma(t) \Psi^\ast_t(g_0).$$

In this expression $\Psi^\ast_t(g_0)$ refers to the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of the metric $g_0$ by the diffeomorphism $\Psi_t$. Therefore, up to [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) and depending on the sign of $\lambda$, a Ricci soliton homothetically shrinks, remains steady or expands under Ricci flow.

## Examples of Ricci solitons {#Examples_of_Ricci_solitons}

### Shrinking ($\lambda > 0$) {#Shrinking_(_\lambda_>_0_)}

- Gaussian shrinking soliton $(\mathbb{R}^n, g_{eucl}, f(x) = \frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^2)$
- Shrinking round sphere $S^n, n \geq 2$
- Shrinking round cylinder $S^{n-1} \times \mathbb{R}, n \geq 3$
- The four dimensional FIK shrinker (discovered by [M. Feldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Feldman), [T. Ilmanen](wikipedia://en/76365326), [D. Knopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._Knopf)) [^o5]
- The four dimensional BCCD shrinker (discovered by [Richard Bamler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bamler), [Charles Cifarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cifarelli), [Ronan Conlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_Conlon), and [Alix Deruelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alix_Deruelle))[^o6]
- Compact gradient Kähler-Ricci shrinkers[^o7][^o8][^o9]
- Einstein manifolds of positive scalar curvature

### Steady ($\lambda = 0$) {#Steady_(_\lambda_=_0_)}

- The 2d cigar soliton (a.k.a. Witten's black hole) $\left(\mathbb{R}^2, g = \frac{dx^2 + dy^2}{1 + x^2 + y^2}, V = -2 ( x \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial}{\partial y}) \right)$
- The 3d rotationally symmetric Bryant soliton and its generalization to higher dimensions [^o11]
- Ricci flat manifolds

### Expanding ($\lambda < 0$) {#Expanding_(_\lambda_<_0_)}

- Expanding Kähler-Ricci solitons on the [complex line](wikipedia://en/7975294) bundles $O(-k), k>n$ over $\mathbb{C}P^n, n \geq 1$.[^o5]
- Einstein manifolds of negative scalar curvature

## Singularity models in Ricci flow {#Singularity_models_in_Ricci_flow}

Shrinking and steady Ricci solitons are fundamental objects in the study of [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291) as they appear as blow-up limits of [singularities](wikipedia://en/288291#Singularities). In particular, it is known that all Type I singularities are modeled on non-collapsed gradient shrinking Ricci solitons.[^o14] Type II singularities are expected to be modeled on steady Ricci solitons in general, however to date this has not been proven, even though all known examples are.

## Soliton Identities {#Soliton_Identities}

Taking the trace of the Ricci soliton equation $Ric + \frac12 \mathcal{L}_V g = \lambda g$ gives

::: {#o16 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$S + \operatorname{div} V = \lambda n, \tag{1}$$

:::

where $S$ is the [scalar curvature](wikipedia://en/285622) and $n = \operatorname{dim} M$. By taking the divergence of the Ricci soliton equation and invoking the [contracted Bianchi identities](wikipedia://en/57574496) and 1, it follows that

$$\Delta V + \operatorname{Ric} \circ V = 0 \quad \text{or equivalently, in components,} \quad \Delta V_i + \operatorname{Ric}_{ij} V^j = 0.$$

For gradient Ricci solitons $V = \nabla f$, similar arguments show

$$S + \Delta f = \lambda n \quad \text{and} \quad \nabla ( S + | \nabla f|^2 - 2 \lambda f ) = 0.$$

In particular, if $M$ is connected, then there exists a constant $C$ such that

$$S + |\nabla f|^2 = 2 \lambda f + C.$$

Often, in the shrinking or expanding cases ($\lambda \ne 0$), $f$ is replaced by $f - \frac C{2 \lambda}$ to obtain a gradient Ricci soliton normalized such that $S + |\nabla f|^2 = 2 \lambda f$.
