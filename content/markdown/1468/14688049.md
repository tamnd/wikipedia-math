# Character variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Character variety"}

In the [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) of [moduli theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moduli_theory), given an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/247924), [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386), [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ and a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) $\pi$, the $G$-*character variety of* $\pi$ is a space of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of [group homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/12396) from $\pi$ to $G$:

:::

$$\mathfrak{R}(\pi,G)=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,G)/\!\sim \, .$$

More precisely, $G$ acts on $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,G)$ by [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29), and two homomorphisms are defined to be equivalent (denoted $\sim$) if and only if their [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29#Orbits_and_stabilizers) closures intersect. This is the weakest equivalence relation on the set of conjugation orbits, $\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,G)/G$, that yields a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

Formally, and when the [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386) is defined over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}$, the $G$-character variety is the [spectrum of prime ideals](wikipedia://en/37291) of the [ring of invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_invariants) (i.e., the affine [GIT quotient](wikipedia://en/41139312)).

$$\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,G)]^G .$$

Here more generally one can consider algebraically closed [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) of prime characteristic. In this generality, character varieties are only algebraic sets and are not actual varieties. To avoid technical issues, one often considers the associated reduced space by dividing by the [radical](wikipedia://en/289636) of 0 (eliminating [nilpotents](wikipedia://en/252235)). However, this does not necessarily yield an irreducible space either. Moreover, if we replace the complex group by a real group we may not even get an algebraic set. In particular, a [maximal compact subgroup](wikipedia://en/1496726) generally gives a [semi-algebraic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-algebraic_set). On the other hand, whenever $\pi$ is free we always get an honest variety; it is singular however.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An interesting class of examples arise from [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181): if $X$ is a Riemann surface then the $G$-*character variety of* $X$, or *Betti moduli space*, is the character variety of the surface group $\pi=\pi_1(X)$

$$\mathcal{M}_B(X,G) = \mathfrak{R}(\pi_1(X),G).$$

For example, if $G=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and $X$ is the Riemann sphere punctured three times, so $\pi=\pi_1(X)$ is free of rank two, then Henri G. Vogt, [Robert Fricke](wikipedia://en/10291191), and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) proved[^o6][^o7] that the character variety is $\mathbb{C}^3$; its coordinate ring is isomorphic to the complex polynomial ring in 3 variables, $\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]$. Restricting to $G=\mathrm{SU}(2)$ gives a closed real three-dimensional ball (semi-algebraic, but not algebraic).

Another example, also studied by Vogt and Fricke–Klein is the case with $G=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ and $X$ is the Riemann sphere punctured four times, so $\pi=\pi_1(X)$ is free of rank three. Then the character variety is isomorphic to the hypersurface in $\mathbb{C}^7$ given by the equation

$$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2 + x^2+y^2+z^2 -(ab+cd)x-(ad+bc)y-(ac+bd)z + abcd + xyz - 4 = 0.$$

This character variety appears in the theory of the sixth Painlevé equation,[^o8] and has a natural Poisson structure such that $a,b,c,d$ are Casimir functions, so the symplectic leaves are affine cubic surfaces of the form $xyz+x^2+y^2+z^2 +c_1x+ c_2 y + c_3z = c_4$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

## Variants {#Variants}

This construction of the character variety is not necessarily the same as that of [Marc Culler](wikipedia://en/29258614) and [Peter Shalen](wikipedia://en/3520910) (generated by evaluations of traces), although when $G=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})$ they do agree, since [Claudio Procesi](wikipedia://en/35219117) has shown that in this case the ring of invariants is in fact generated by only traces. Since trace functions are invariant by all inner automorphisms, the Culler–Shalen construction essentially assumes that we are acting by $G=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})$ on $\mathfrak{R}=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,H)$ even if $G \neq H$.

For instance, when $\pi$ is a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of rank 2 and $G=\mathrm{SO}(2)$, the conjugation action is trivial and the $G$-character variety is the torus

$$S^1\times S^1.$$

But the trace algebra is a strictly small subalgebra (there are fewer invariants). This provides an involutive action on the torus that needs to be accounted for to yield the Culler–Shalen character variety. The involution on this torus yields a 2-sphere. The point is that up to $\mathrm{SO}(2)$-conjugation all points are distinct, but the trace identifies elements with differing anti-diagonal elements (the involution).

:::

## Connection to geometry {#Connection_to_geometry}

There is an interplay between these moduli spaces and the moduli spaces of [principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950), [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524), [Higgs bundles](wikipedia://en/34143255), and geometric structures on topological spaces, given generally by the observation that, at least locally, equivalent objects in these categories are parameterized by conjugacy classes of [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) homomorphisms of flat connections. In other words, with respect to a base space $M$ for the bundles or a fixed topological space for the geometric structures, the holonomy homomorphism is a group homomorphism from $\pi_1(M)$ to the structure group $G$ of the bundle.

## Connection to skein modules {#Connection_to_skein_modules}

The coordinate ring of the character variety has been related to [skein modules](wikipedia://en/3774360) in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008).[^o13][^o14] The skein module is roughly a [deformation](wikipedia://en/340678) (or quantization) of the character variety. It is closely related to topological quantum field theory in dimension 2+1.
