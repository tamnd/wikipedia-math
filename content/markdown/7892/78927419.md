# Corina Tarnita

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Corina Tarnita"}

**Corina Tarnita** is a Romanian-American mathematician and theoretical biologist known for her work in [mathematical biology](wikipedia://en/662088) and [complex adaptive systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_adaptive_system). She is currently the Frederick D. Petrie Professor of Ecology and Evolutionary Biology at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University).[^o3] Her research examines how living organisms organize themselves into patterns at different scales. [^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova), Romania, Tarnita showed an early aptitude for mathematics, encouraged by her mother, a professor of [materials science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science) and engineering.[^o4] She won the Romanian National Mathematical Olympiad three times from 1999 to 2001.[^o6] Tarnita moved to the United States to pursue higher education, obtaining her B.A. (2006), M.A. (2008), and Ph.D. (2009) in Mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o7] She was a Junior Fellow in the [Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows) before joining the [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) faculty in 2013.[^o7]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

Initially focused on high-dimensional mathematics, Tarnita was inspired by [Martin Nowak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Nowak)'s work on [evolutionary dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_dynamics) and shifted to studying [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology). This change led her to complete her Ph.D. under Nowak's supervision, finishing in just three years.[^o6]

Tarnita's 2010 paper, "The evolution of eusociality," co-authored with Martin A. Nowak and [Edward O. Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._O._Wilson), challenged the long-standing [kin selection](wikipedia://en/66996) theory and proposed a new model for the evolution of [eusociality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusociality), sparking significant debate and reevaluation in the field of [evolutionary biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_biology).[^o9][^o10][^o11] The response included a rebuttal published in *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28magazine%29)* from over a hundred researchers.[^o12]

Working with Princeton colleagues [Robert Pringle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_M._Pringle) and Juan Bonachela, Tarnita developed new theories about the formation of large, [regular vegetation patterns](wikipedia://en/35659147), such as [fairy circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_circle_%28arid_grass_formation%29) in the [Namib Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namib_Desert).[^o13][^o14]

Tarnita's research on "loner" behavior in [slime molds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slime_mold) revealed that this seemingly non-cooperative trait is actually an [evolutionarily stable strategy](wikipedia://en/9616), challenging previous assumptions about social behavior in [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism).[^o15][^o16]

In 2024, Tarnita was named a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o17]

### Relationship with Jeffrey Epstein {#Relationship_with_Jeffrey_Epstein}

Tarnita met convicted sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein) while she was a graduate student working under Nowak at Harvard in 2008. She remained in contact with Epstein through 2012, including emails that refer to his 2008 conviction.[^o19] In 2009, she helped Epstein anonymously wire transfer \$10,000 and \$5,000 respectively to two Romanian women for scholarships.[^o20][^o21][^o22]
