# Schwarz alternating method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwarz alternating method"}

*Image: Hermann Amand Schwarz (1843-1921) by Louis Zipfel.jpg.* [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834), inventor of the method

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Schwarz alternating method** or **alternating process** is an [iterative method](wikipedia://en/15237) introduced in 1869–1870 by [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834) in the theory of [conformal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping). Given two overlapping regions in the complex plane in each of which the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109) could be solved, Schwarz described an [iterative method](wikipedia://en/15237) for solving the Dirichlet problem in their union, provided their intersection was suitably well behaved. This was one of several constructive techniques of conformal mapping developed by Schwarz as a contribution to the problem of [uniformization](wikipedia://en/288270), posed by [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) in the 1850s and first resolved rigorously by [Koebe](wikipedia://en/4313643) and [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) in 1907. It furnished a scheme for uniformizing the union of two regions knowing how to uniformize each of them separately, provided their intersection was topologically a disk or an annulus. From 1870 onwards [Carl Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551) also contributed to this theory.

:::

In the 1950s Schwarz's method was generalized in the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) to an iterative method for finding the solution of an [elliptic boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/10468961) on a [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) which is the union of two overlapping subdomains. It involves solving the boundary value problem on each of the two subdomains in turn, taking always the last values of the approximate solution as the next [boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_conditions). It is used in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), under the name **multiplicative Schwarz method** (in opposition to [additive Schwarz method](wikipedia://en/400296)) as a [domain decomposition method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_decomposition_method).

It was first formulated by [H. A. Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834)[^o3] and served as a theoretical tool: its convergence for general second order [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787) was first proved much later, in 1951, by [Solomon Mikhlin](wikipedia://en/22994055).[^o4]

## The algorithm {#The_algorithm}

The original problem considered by Schwarz was a [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109) (with the [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941)) on a domain consisting of a circle and a partially overlapping square. To solve the Dirichlet problem on one of the two subdomains (the square or the circle), the [value of the solution must be known on the border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_conditions): since a part of the border is contained in the other subdomain, the Dirichlet problem must be solved jointly on the two subdomains. An iterative algorithm is introduced:

1. Make a first guess of the solution on the circle's boundary part that is contained in the square
1. Solve the Dirichlet problem on the circle
1. Use the solution in (2) to approximate the solution on the square's boundary
1. Solve the Dirichlet problem on the square
1. Use the solution in (4) to approximate the solution on the circle's boundary, then go to step (2).

At convergence, the solution on the overlap is the same when computed on the square or on the circle.

:::

## Optimized Schwarz methods {#Optimized_Schwarz_methods}

The convergence speed depends on the size of the overlap between the subdomains, and on the transmission conditions (boundary conditions used in the interface between the subdomains). It is possible to increase the convergence speed of the Schwarz methods by choosing adapted transmission conditions: theses methods are then called Optimized Schwarz methods.[^o8]
