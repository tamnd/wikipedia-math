# Mortar methods

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mortar methods"}

In [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **mortar methods** are [discretization methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretization_method) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), which use separate [finite element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_element) discretization on nonoverlapping subdomains. The [meshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_mesh) on the subdomains do not match on the interface, and the equality of the solution is enforced by [Lagrange multipliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_multipliers), judiciously chosen to preserve the accuracy of the solution.[^o2][^o3] Mortar discretizations lend themselves naturally to the solution by iterative [domain decomposition methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_decomposition_method) such as [FETI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FETI) and [balancing domain decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balancing_domain_decomposition)[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] In the engineering practice in the finite element method, continuity of solutions between non-matching subdomains is implemented by [multiple-point constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple-point_constraint).

:::

Similar to [penalty methods](wikipedia://en/7468671), mortar methods are explicit in their nature, i.e. they require the contacting surfaces to be defined. This is in contrast to fully implicit methods, such as the [third medium contact method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_medium_contact_method), where contacting surfaces do not need to be defined.
