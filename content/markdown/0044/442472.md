# 1000 (number)

3

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="1000"}

**1000** or **one thousand** is the [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) following [999](wikipedia://en/83706615) and preceding [1001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1001_%28number%29). In most [English-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_countries), it can be written with or without a [comma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comma) or sometimes a period [separating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_mark#Digit_grouping) the thousands [digit](wikipedia://en/280582): **1,000**.

:::

A group of one thousand units is sometimes known, from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek), as a **chiliad**.[^o3] A period of one thousand years may be known as a chiliad or, more often from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), as a [millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium). The number 1000 is also sometimes described as a **short thousand** in medieval contexts where it is necessary to distinguish the [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) concept of 1200 as a [long thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_thousand). It is the first 4-digit [integer](wikipedia://en/14563).

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

- The decimal representation for one thousand is
  - **1000**—a [one](wikipedia://en/77591639) followed by three [zeros](wikipedia://en/34513), in the general notation;
  - **1 × 10<sup>3</sup>**—in [engineering notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_notation), which for this number coincides with:
  - **1 × 10<sup>3</sup>** exactly—in [scientific normalized exponential notation](wikipedia://en/59715);
  - **1 E+3** exactly—in [scientific E notation](wikipedia://en/59715#E_notation).
- The [SI prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_prefix) for a thousand units is "[kilo-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilo-)", abbreviated to "k"—for instance, a [kilogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilogram) or "kg" is a thousand [grams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram). This is sometimes extended to non-SI contexts, such as "ka" ([kiloannum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiloannum)) being used as a shorthand for periods of 1000 years. In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), however, "kilo-" is used more loosely to mean 2 to the 10th power (1024 or 2<sup>10</sup>).
- In the [SI writing style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI#SI_writing_style), a [non-breaking space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-breaking_space) can be used as a [thousands separator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousands_separator), i.e., to separate the digits of a number at every power of 1000.
- Multiples of thousands are occasionally represented by replacing their last three zeros with the letter "K" or "k": for instance, writing "\$30k" for \$30,000 or using "Y2K" to denote the [Year 2000 computer problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2000_problem).
- A thousand units of [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency), especially [dollars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar) or [pounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_%28currency%29), are colloquially called a *grand*. In the United States, this is sometimes abbreviated with a "G" suffix.

:::
