# Weierstrass M-test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Weierstrass M-test"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Weierstrass M-test** is a test for determining whether an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) converges [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) and [absolutely](wikipedia://en/61532). It applies to series whose terms are [bounded functions](wikipedia://en/311509) with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) values, and is analogous to the [comparison test](wikipedia://en/1601407) for determining the convergence of series of real or complex numbers. It is named after the German mathematician [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459) (1815–1897).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

**Weierstrass M-test.** Suppose that (*f*<sub>*n*</sub>) is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of real- or complex-valued functions defined on a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *A*, and that there is a sequence of non-negative numbers (*M*<sub>*n*</sub>) satisfying the conditions

- $|f_n(x)|\leq M_n$ for all $n \geq 1$ and all $x \in A$, and
- $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} M_n$ converges.

Then the series

$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f_n (x)$$

converges [absolutely](wikipedia://en/61532) and [uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on *A*.

A series satisfying the hypothesis is called *[normally convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normally_convergent)*. The result is often used in combination with the [uniform limit theorem](wikipedia://en/29230765). Together they say that if, in addition to the above conditions, the set *A* is a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) and the functions *f<sub>n</sub>* are [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) on *A*, then the series converges to a continuous function.

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Consider the sequence of functions

$$S_{n}(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}(x).$$

Since the series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}$ converges and $M_{n} \ge 0$ for every $n$, then by the [Cauchy criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_criterion),

$$\forall \varepsilon>0 : \exists N : \forall m>n>N : \sum_{k=n+1}^{m}M_{k}<\varepsilon.$$

For the chosen $N$,

$$\forall x \in A : \forall m> n> N$$

$$\left|S_{m}(x)-S_{n}(x)\right|=\left|\sum_{k=n+1}^{m}f_{k}(x)\right|\overset{(1)}{\leq} \sum_{k=n+1}^{m}|f_{k}(x)|\leq \sum_{k=n+1}^{m}M_{k}<\varepsilon .$$

(Inequality (1) follows from the [triangle inequality](wikipedia://en/53941).)

For each *x*, the sequence $S_{n}(x)$ is thus a [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085) in **R** or **C**, and by [completeness](wikipedia://en/29322627), it converges to some number $S(x)$ that depends on *x*. For *n* > *N* we can write

$$\left|S(x) - S_{n}(x)\right|=\left|\lim_{m\to\infty} S_{m}(x) - S_{n}(x)\right|=\lim_{m\to\infty} \left|S_{m}(x) - S_{n}(x)\right|\leq\varepsilon .$$

Since *N* does not depend on *x*, this means that the sequence $S_{n}$ of partial sums converges uniformly to the function *S*. Hence, by definition, the series $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)$ converges uniformly.

Analogously, one can prove that $\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|f_{k}(x)|$ converges uniformly.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

A more general version of the Weierstrass M-test holds if the common [codomain](wikipedia://en/50264) of the functions (*f<sub>n</sub>*) is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), in which case the premise

$$|f_n(x)|\leq M_n$$

is to be replaced by

$$\|f_n(x)\|\leq M_n,$$

where $\|\cdot\|$ is the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) on the Banach space. For an example of the use of this test on a Banach space, see the article [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727).

:::
