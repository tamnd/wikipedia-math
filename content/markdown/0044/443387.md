# Simple ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simple ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **simple ring** is a non-zero [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) that has no [two-sided ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-sided_ideal) besides the zero ideal and itself. In particular, a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) is a simple ring if and only if it is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603).

:::

The [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of a simple ring is necessarily a field. It follows that a simple ring is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over this field. It is then called a **simple algebra** over this field.

Several references (e.g., (Lang 2002) or (Bourbaki 2012)) require in addition that a simple ring be left or right [Artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) (or equivalently [semi-simple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-simple_ring)). Under such terminology a non-zero ring with no non-trivial two-sided ideals is called **quasi-simple**.

Rings which are simple as rings but are not a [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430) over themselves do exist: a full [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over a field does not have any nontrivial two-sided ideals (since any ideal of $\mathrm{M}_n(R)$ is of the form $\mathrm{M}_n(I)$ with $I$ an ideal of $R$), but it has nontrivial left ideals (for example, the sets of matrices which have some fixed zero columns).

An immediate example of a simple ring is a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067), where every nonzero element has a [multiplicative inverse](wikipedia://en/229940), for instance, the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440). Also, for any $n \ge 1$, the algebra of $n \times n$ matrices with entries in a [division ring](wikipedia://en/9067) is simple.

[Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494) proved that if a ring $R$ is a finite-dimensional simple algebra over a field $k$, it is isomorphic to a [matrix algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_algebra) over some [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625) over $k$. In particular, the only simple rings that are finite-dimensional [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788) over the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) are rings of matrices over either the real numbers, the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), or the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440).

Wedderburn proved these results in 1907 in his doctoral thesis, *On hypercomplex numbers*, which appeared in the [Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_London_Mathematical_Society). His thesis classified finite-dimensional simple and also [semisimple algebras](wikipedia://en/3876151) over fields. Simple algebras are building blocks of semisimple algebras: any finite-dimensional semisimple algebra is a Cartesian product, in the sense of algebras, of finite-dimensional simple algebras.

One must be careful of the terminology: not every simple ring is a [semisimple ring](wikipedia://en/1001361#Semisimple_rings), and not every simple algebra is a semisimple algebra. However, every finite-dimensional simple algebra is a semisimple algebra, and every simple ring that is left- or right-[artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) is a semisimple ring.

Wedderburn's result was later generalized to [semisimple rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_ring) in the [Wedderburn–Artin theorem](wikipedia://en/397264): this says that every semisimple ring is a finite product of matrix rings over division rings. As a consequence of this generalization, every simple ring that is left- or right-[artinian](wikipedia://en/489273) is a matrix ring over a division ring.

## Examples {#Examples}

Let $\mathbb{R}$ be the field of real numbers, $\mathbb{C}$ be the field of complex numbers, and $\mathbb{H}$ the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440).

- A [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) (sometimes called a [Brauer algebra](wikipedia://en/3875858)) is a simple finite-dimensional algebra over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $F$ whose [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_an_algebra) is $F$.
- Every finite-dimensional simple algebra over $\mathbb{R}$ is isomorphic to an algebra of $n \times n$ matrices with entries in $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, or $\mathbb{H}$. Every [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) over $\mathbb{R}$ is isomorphic to an algebra of $n \times n$ matrices with entries $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{H}$. These results follow from the [Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/3966889).
- Every finite-dimensional simple algebra over $\mathbb{C}$ is a central simple algebra, and is isomorphic to a matrix ring over $\mathbb{C}$.
- Every finite-dimensional central simple algebra over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) is isomorphic to a matrix ring over that field.
- Over a field of characteristic zero, the [Weyl algebra](wikipedia://en/491095) is simple but not semisimple, and in particular not a matrix algebra over a division algebra over its center; the Weyl algebra is infinite-dimensional, so Wedderburn's theorem does not apply to it.
