# Polylogarithmic function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polylogarithmic function"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **polylogarithmic function** in $n$ is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) in the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) of $n$,[^o2]

:::

$$a_k (\log n)^k + a_{k-1} (\log n)^{k-1} + \cdots + a_1(\log n) + a_0.$$

The notation $\log^{k}n$ is often used as a [shorthand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorthand) for $(\log n)^{k}$, analogous to $\sin^{2}\theta$ for $(\sin \theta)^{2}$.

In [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), polylogarithmic functions occur as the [order](wikipedia://en/44578) of [time](wikipedia://en/405944) for some [data structure](wikipedia://en/8519) operations. Additionally, the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) of a polylogarithmic function produces a function with [quasi-polynomial growth](wikipedia://en/75468672), and algorithms with this as their [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) are said to take [quasi-polynomial time](wikipedia://en/25269049).[^o3]

All polylogarithmic functions of $n$ are $o(n^{\varepsilon})$ for every exponent $\varepsilon > 0$ (for the meaning of this symbol, see [small o notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_o_notation)), that is, a polylogarithmic function grows more slowly than any positive exponent. This observation is the basis for the [soft O notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_O_notation#Extensions_to_the_Bachmann–Landau_notations) $\text{Õ}(n)$.[^o4]
