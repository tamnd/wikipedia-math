# Icosagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Icosagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **icosagon** or 20-gon is a twenty-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621). The sum of any icosagon's interior angles is 3240 degrees.

:::

## Regular icosagon {#Regular_icosagon}

The [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) icosagon has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\{20\}$, and can also be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [decagon](wikipedia://en/327393), $t\{10\}$, or a twice-truncated [pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), $\operatorname{tt}\{5\}$.

One interior angle in a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) icosagon is 162°, meaning that one exterior angle would be 18°.

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a regular icosagon with edge length $t$ is

$$A={5}t^2(1+\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}) \simeq 31.5687 t^2.$$

In terms of the radius $R$ of its [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432), the area is

$$A=\frac{5R^2}{2}(\sqrt{5}-1);$$

since the area of the circle is $\pi R^2,$ the regular icosagon fills approximately 98.36% of its circumcircle.

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The Big Wheel on the popular US game show *[The Price is Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Price_is_Right_%28U.S._game_show%29)* has an icosagonal cross-section.

The Globe, the outdoor theater used by William Shakespeare's acting company, was discovered to have been built on an icosagonal foundation when a partial excavation was done in 1989.[^o5]

As a [golygonal](wikipedia://en/4303848) path, the [swastika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swastika) is considered to be an irregular icosagon.

*Image: 4.5.20 vertex.png.*

A regular square, pentagon, and icosagon can completely [fill a plane vertex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_tilings_of_convex_regular_polygons).

## The golden ratio in an icosagon {#The_golden_ratio_in_an_icosagon}

- In the construction with given side length the circular arc around $C$ with radius $\overline{\operatorname{CD}}$, shares the segment $\overline{E_{20}F}$ in ratio of the golden ratio.

$$\frac{\overline{ E_{20}E_1}}{\overline{E_1 F}} = \frac{\overline{E_{20} F}}{\overline{ E_{20}E_1}} = \frac{1+ \sqrt{5}}{2} =\varphi \approx 1.618$$

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Symmetries\_of\_icosagon.png.* Symmetries of a regular icosagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices, and purple mirrors are drawn through edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular icosagon* has [$\operatorname{Dih}_{20}$ symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 40. There are 5 subgroup dihedral symmetries: $(\operatorname{Dih}_{10}, \operatorname{Dih}_{5})$, and $(\operatorname{Dih}_{4}, \operatorname{Dih}_{2},\text{ and } \operatorname{Dih}_{1})$, and 6 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: $(Z_{20}, Z_{10}, Z_{5})$, and ($Z_{4}, Z_{2}, Z_{1})$.

These 10 symmetries can be seen in 16 distinct symmetries on the icosagon, a larger number because the lines of reflections can either pass through vertices or edges. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o13] Full symmetry of the regular form is $\mathbf{r40}$ and no symmetry is labeled $\mathbf{a1}$. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices ($\mathbf{d}$ for diagonal) or edges ($\mathbf{p}$ for perpendiculars), and $\mathbf{i}$ when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as $\mathbf{g}$ for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the $\mathbf{g20}$ subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

The highest symmetry irregular icosagons are $\mathbf{d20}$, an [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) icosagon constructed by ten mirrors which can alternate long and short edges, and $\mathbf{p20}$, an [isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189) icosagon, constructed with equal edge lengths, but vertices alternating two different internal angles. These two forms are [duals](wikipedia://en/19865549) of each other and have half the symmetry order of the regular icosagon.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a $2m$-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into $m(m-1)/2$ parallelograms.[^o16] In particular this is true for regular polygons with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all rhombi. For the icosagon, $m=10$, and it can be divided into 45: 5 squares and 4 sets of 10 rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of a [10-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10-cube), with 45 of 11520 faces. The list enumerates the number of solutions as 18,410,581,880, including up to 20-fold rotations and chiral forms in reflection.

## Related polygons {#Related_polygons}

An **icosagram** is a 20-sided [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303), represented by symbol $\{20/n\}$. There are three regular forms given by [Schläfli symbols](wikipedia://en/489394): $\{20/3\}$, $\{20/7\}$, and $\{20/9\}$. There are also five regular star figures (compounds) using the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644): $2\{10\}$, $4\{5\}$, $5\{4\}$, $2\{10/3\}$, $4\{5/2\}$, and $10\{2\}$.

Deeper truncations of the regular decagon and decagram can produce isogonal ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)) intermediate icosagram forms with equally spaced vertices and two edge lengths.[^o22]

A regular icosagram, $\{20/9\}$, can be seen as a quasitruncated decagon, $t\{10/9\}=\{20/9\}$. Similarly a [decagram](wikipedia://en/19546742), $\{10/3\}$ has a quasitruncation $t\{10/7\}=\{20/7\}$, and finally a simple truncation of a decagram gives $t\{10/3\}=\{20/3\}$.
