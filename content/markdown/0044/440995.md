# Ambigram

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ambigram"}

*Image: Ambigram of the word ambigram - rotation animation.gif.* Animation of a half-turn ambigram of the word *ambigram*, with 180-degree rotational symmetry[^o2]

An **ambigram** is a [calligraphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphic) composition of [glyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) (letters, numbers, symbols or other shapes) that can yield different meanings depending on the orientation of observation.[^o22][^o25] Most ambigrams are visual [palindromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome) that rely on some kind of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741), and they can often be interpreted as [visual puns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_pun).[^o6] Although the concept is older, the term "ambigram" was coined by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) in 1983–1984.[^o22][^o7]

:::

Most often, ambigrams appear as visually symmetrical words. When flipped, they remain unchanged, or they mutate to reveal another [meaning](wikipedia://en/29107). "Half-turn" ambigrams undergo a [point reflection](wikipedia://en/12487536) (180-degree [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096)) and can be read upside down (for example, the word "swims"), while mirror ambigrams have [axial symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673) and can be read through a [reflective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective) surface like a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror). Many other types of ambigrams exist.[^o3]

Ambigrams can be constructed in various [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_language) and [alphabets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet), and the notion often extends to [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) and other [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673). It is a recent [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) concept, combining [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature), [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition), and [optical illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusions). Drawing symmetrical words constitutes also a [recreational activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_activity) for [amateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur). Numerous ambigram [logos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo) are famous, and ambigram [tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattoo) have become increasingly popular. There are methods to design an ambigram, a field in which some [artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist) have become specialists.

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *ambigram* was coined in 1983 by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758), an American scholar of [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science) best known as the [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize)-winning author of the 1979 book *[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103)*.[^o5][^o6][^o7] It is a [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) composed of the Latin prefix ambi- ("both") and the Greek suffix \-gram ("drawing, writing").[^o22]

Hofstadter describes ambigrams as "calligraphic designs that manage to squeeze in two different readings."[^o8] "The essence is imbuing a single [written form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy) with [ambiguity](wikipedia://en/677)".[^o204][^o9]

Hofstadter attributes the origin of the word *ambigram* to conversations among a small group of friends in 1983–1984.[^o11][^o12]

Prior to Hofstadter's [terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminology), other names were used to refer to ambigrams. Among them, the expressions "vertical [palindromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome)"[^o13] by [Dmitri Borgmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Borgmann)[^o14] (1965) and [Georges Perec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Perec),[^o15][^o16] "designatures" (1979),[^o17] "inversions" (1980) by [Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim),[^o18][^o19] or simply "upside-down words" by [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) and Robert Petrick.[^o20]

*Ambigram* was added to the [Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary) in March 2011,[^o3][^o21] and to the [Merriam-Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merriam-Webster) dictionary in September 2020.[^o22][^o23] *[Scrabble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabble)* included the word in its database in November 2022.[^o24][^o25][^o26]

## History {#History}

*Image: Sator\_Square\_at\_Oppède.jpg.* [Sator square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sator_square) ([word square](wikipedia://en/2224640) and [palindrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome)) with the letters S and N in [mirror writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_writing).

*Image: Ambigram\_-\_puzzle\_-\_the\_end\_-\_by\_Peter\_Newell\_1893\_-\_book\_Topsys\_and\_turvys\_(crop).jpg.* Rotational ambigram *Puzzle / The end* by [Peter Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Newell) designed in 1893.

*Image: Ambigram\_palindrome\_ΝΙΨΟΝΑΝΟΜΗΜΑΤΑΜΗΜΟΝΑΝΟΨΙΝ\_(Wash\_your\_sins,\_not\_only\_your\_face,\_in\_Greek).jpg.* [Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) ambigram ΝΙΨΟΝ ΑΝΟΜΗΜΑΤΑ ΜΗ ΜΟΝΑΝ ΟΨΙΝ ([*Wash your sins, not only your face*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipson_anomemata_me_monan_opsin), in [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek)) in the monastery Panagia Malevi.[^o28]

*Image: Ambigrams\_Chump,\_honey,\_M.\_H.\_Hill,\_Bet,\_and\_five\_more\_words\_-\_Strand\_Magazine\_1908.jpg.* Ambigrams published in *[The Strand Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strand_Magazine)*, June 1908.

Many ambigrams can be described as [graphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic) [palindromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromes).

The first [Sator square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sator_square) palindrome was found in the ruins of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii), meaning it was created before the [Eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD). A sator square using the [mirror writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_writing) for the representation of the letters S and N was carved in a stone wall in [Oppède](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppède) (France) between the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages),[^o29] thus producing a work made up of 25 letters and 8 different [characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_%28symbol%29), 3 naturally symmetrical (A, T, O), 3 others decipherable from left to right (R, P, E), and 2 others from right to left (S, N). This engraving is therefore readable in four directions.[^o30]

Although the term is recent, the existence of [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) ambigrams has been attested since at least the [first millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_millennium). They are generally [palindromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromes) [stylized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stylized) to be visually [symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrical).

In [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek), the phrase ["ΝΙΨΟΝ ΑΝΟΜΗΜΑΤΑ ΜΗ ΜΟΝΑΝ ΟΨΙΝ"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipson_anomemata_me_monan_opsin) (*wash the sins, not only the face*), is a [palindrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome) found in several locations, including the site of the church [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey).[^o31][^o32] It is sometimes turned into a mirror ambigram when written in capital letters with the removal of [spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_%28punctuation%29), and the stylization of the letter Ν (Ν).

A [boustrophedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boustrophedon) is a type of [bi-directional text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bi-directional_text), mostly seen in ancient manuscripts and other inscriptions. Every other line of writing is flipped or reversed, with reversed letters. Rather than going left-to-right as in modern European languages, or right-to-left as in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew), alternate lines in boustrophedon must be read in opposite directions. Also, the individual characters are reversed, or mirrored. This two-way writing system reveals that modern ambigrams can have quite ancient origins, with an intuitive component in some minds.

Mirror writing in [Islamic calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calligraphy) flourished during the early modern period, but its origins may stretch as far back as pre-Islamic mirror-image rock inscriptions in the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz).[^o33]

The earliest known non-natural [rotational](wikipedia://en/12487536) ambigram dates to 1893 by artist [Peter Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Newell).[^o34] Although better known for his children's books and illustrations for [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain) and [Lewis Carroll](wikipedia://en/26586083), he published two books of [reversible illustrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_figure), in which the picture turns into a different image entirely when flipped [upside down](wikipedia://en/12487536). The last page in his book *Topsys & Turvys* contains the phrase *The end*, which, when inverted, reads *Puzzle*. In *Topsys & Turvys Number 2* (1902), Newell ended with a variation on the ambigram in which *The end* changes into *Puzzle 2*.[^o35]

In March 1904 the Dutch-American [comic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic) artist [Gustave Verbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Verbeek) used ambigrams in three consecutive strips of *The UpsideDowns of old man Muffaroo and little lady Lovekins*.[^o36] His comics were [ambiguous images](wikipedia://en/1316947), made in such a way that one could read the six-panel comic, flip the book and keep reading.

From June to September 1908, the British monthly *[The Strand Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strand_Magazine)* published a series of ambigrams by different people in its "[Curiosities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_curiosities)" column.[^o37] Of particular interest is the fact that all four of the people submitting ambigrams believed them to be a rare property of particular words. Mitchell T. Lavin, whose "chump" was published in June, wrote, "I think it is in the only word in the English language which has this peculiarity," while Clarence Williams wrote, about his "Bet" ambigram, "Possibly B is the only letter of the alphabet that will produce such an interesting anomaly."[^o37][^o38]

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

### Natural ambigrams {#Natural_ambigrams}

*Image: SOS\_Italian\_traffic\_signs\_in\_2020.05.jpg.* The distress signal [SOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOS) is a natural rotating ambigram.

*Image: Ambigram\_OHIO\_(90\_degrees\_rotational\_symmetry\_-\_animated).gif.* "Quarter-turn" natural ambigram

*Image: 619sign.JPG.* The [number 619](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/619_%28number%29) constitutes a natural ambigram (but not the word "western").

In the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), many letters are symmetrical [glyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph). The [capital letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_letters) B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, and X have a horizontal symmetry axis. This means that all words that can be written using only these letters are natural [lake reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29) ambigrams; examples include BOOK, CHOICE, or DECIDE.

The [lowercase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowercase) letters o, s, x, and z are [rotationally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotationally_symmetric), while pairs such as b/q, d/p, n/u, and in some [typefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface) a/e, h/y and m/w, are rotations of each other. Among the lowercase letters "l" is unique since its symmetry is broken if it is close to a reference character which establishes a clear [x-height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-height). When rotated around the middle of the x-height l/ȷ or lo/oȷ it doesn't appear the same, but it does when rotated around its center like the uppercase-I. Thus, the words "sos", "pod", "suns", "yeah", "swims", "passed", or "dollop", form natural rotational ambigrams.

More generally, a "natural ambigram" is a word that possesses one or more [symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetries) when written in its natural state, requiring no [typographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typographic) [styling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_%28visual_arts%29). The words "bud", "bid", or "mom", form natural mirror ambigrams when reflected over a [vertical axis](wikipedia://en/6025673), as does "ليبيا", the name of the country [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya) in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic). The words "HIM", "TOY, "TOOTH" or "MAXIMUM", in all capitals, form natural mirror ambigrams when their letters are stacked vertically and reflected over a vertical axis. The [uppercase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppercase) word "[OHIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio)" can flip a quarter to produce a 90° [rotational](wikipedia://en/701096) ambigram when written in [serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serif) style (with large "feet" above and below the "I").

Like all [strobogrammatic numbers](wikipedia://en/2906596), [69](wikipedia://en/378157) is a natural rotational ambigram.

[Patterns in nature](wikipedia://en/35659147) are regularities found in the natural world.[^o41] Similarly, [patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patterns) in ambigrams are regularities found in [graphemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapheme). As a consequence to this "natural" property, some [shapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapes) appear more or less appropriate to handle for the [designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligrapher). Ambigram candidates can become "*almost* natural", when all the letters except maybe one or two are symmetrically cooperative, for example the word "awesome" possesses 5 compatible letters (the central s that flips around itself, and the couples a/e and w/m).

### Single word or several words {#Single_word_or_several_words}

A symmetrical ambigram can be called "homogram" (contraction of "homo-ambigram") when it remains unchanged after reflection, and "heterogram" when it transforms.[^o10][^o43] In the most common type of ambigram, the two [interpretations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_interpretation) arise when the image is rotated 180 degrees with respect to each other (in other words, a second reading is obtained from the first by simply rotating the sheet).

#### Single {#Single}

[Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) coined the word "homogram" to define an ambigram with identical letters.[^o10][^o43] In this case, the first half of the word turns into the last half.[^o45]

#### Several {#Several}

*Image: Ambigram\_India\_and\_Nepal\_-\_gray\_and\_beige.svg.* Three word ambigram: a changing combination "[India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) / [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal)" associated with the invariant conjunction "and". By Basile Morin.

A symmetrical ambigram may be called a "heterogram"[^o10][^o43] (contraction of "hetero-ambigram") when it becomes a different word after rotation. Visually, a hetero-ambigram is symmetrical only when both versions of the pairing are shown together. The [aesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetic) appearance is more difficult to design when a changing ambigram is intended to be shown in one way only, because [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741#In_the_arts) generally enhances the visual appearance of artwork. Technically, there are two times more combinations of letters involved in a *hetero-ambigram* than in a *homo-ambigram*. For example, the 180° rotational ambigram "yeah" contains only two pairs of letters: y/h and e/a, whereas the heterogram "yeah / good" contains four: y/d, e/o, a/o, and h/g.

There is no limitation to the number of words that can potentially be paired up as hetero-ambigrams, and full ambigram [sentences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_%28linguistics%29) have even been published.[^o15][^o45]

## Types {#Types}

Ambigrams are exercises in [graphic design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design) that play with [optical illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusion), [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) and [visual perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception). Some ambigrams feature a relationship between their [form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_appearance) and their [content](wikipedia://en/29107). Ambigrams usually fall into one of several categories.

### 180° rotational {#180°_rotational}

*Image: Ambigram\_Upside\_Down\_-\_animation.gif.* 180° rotational ambigram saying "Upside Down". By Basile Morin.[^o82]

"Half-turn" ambigrams or *[point reflection](wikipedia://en/12487536)* ambigrams, commonly called "upside-down words", are 180° [rotational symmetrical](wikipedia://en/701096) [calligraphies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphies).[^o5] They can be read right side up or upside down, or both.

### Reflection {#Reflection}

A [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) or [reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29) ambigram is a design that can be read when reflected in a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror) vertically, horizontally, or at 45 degrees,[^o188] giving either the same word or another word or phrase.

#### Vertical axis {#Vertical_axis}

*Image: Ambigram I love you.png.* Vertical axis reflection ambigram "I Love You" by Basile Morin, with the letter I added over like in a totem ambigram.

*Image: Ambigram Perfect.png.* "[Perfect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfection)", vertical axis mirror ambigram by Basile Morin.

*Image: Ambigram Nothing written.jpg.* "Nothing written", [self-referential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential) visual wordplay by Basile Morin, vertical axis reflection.

*Image: Historical ambigram logo MAOAM 1900s.svg.* Historical [Maoam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoam) logo (candy), vertical axis mirror ambigram, between 1900 and 1931.

When the [reflecting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29) surface is vertical (like a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror) for example), the calligraphic design is a *vertical axis mirror ambigram*.

The "museum" ambigram is almost natural with mirror symmetry, because the first two letters are easily exchanged with the last two, and the lowercase letter e can be transformed into s by a fairly obvious typographical acrobatics.[^o54]

Vertical axis mirror ambigrams find clever applications in [mirror writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_writing) (or [specular writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specular_writing)), that is formed by writing in the direction that is the reverse of the natural way for a given language, such that the result is the [mirror image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) of normal writing: it appears normal when it is reflected in a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror). For example, the word "ambulance" could be read frontward and backward in a vertical axis reflective ambigram. Following this idea, the French artist Patrice Hamel created a mirror ambigram saying "entrée" (*entrance*, in French) one way, and "sortie" (*exit*) the other way, displayed in the giant glass façade of the [Gare du Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_du_Nord) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), so that the travelers coming in read *entrance*, and those leaving read *way out*.[^o55]

#### Horizontal axis {#Horizontal_axis}

*Image: Ambigram Motel Water.png.* "[Motel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motel)" on the facade of a building is mirroring in the water of a [pond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pond) to give "[Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water)", [self-referential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential) concept by Basile Morin using a [lake reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29).

*Image: Ambigram\_Body\_Yoga,\_mirror\_symmetry.png.* Horizontal axis mirror ambigram y Basile Morin "[Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body) / [Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga)".

When the reflecting surface is horizontal (like a [mirroring lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_%28physics%29) for example), the calligraphic design is a *horizontal axis mirror ambigram*.

The book *Ambigrams Revealed* features several creations of this type, like the word "Failure" mirroring in the water of a pond to give "Success", or "Love" changing into "Lust".[^o57]

### Figure-ground {#Figure-ground}

*Image: Tessellation\_ambigramme\_Michel\_Onfray\_-\_figure-fond.png.* Figure-ground ambigram [Michel Onfray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Onfray), revealing the surname in the [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) formed by the letters of the given name. By Basile Morin.

*Image: Ambigram tessellation Liar Jail - figure-ground.png.* Figure-ground ambigram "Liar / Jail" (and incidentally also a [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671)). By Basile Morin.

In a [figure / ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure–ground_%28perception%29) ambigram, letters fit together so the [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) around and between one word spells another word.[^o188]

In [Gestalt psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestalt_psychology), [figure–ground perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure–ground_%28perception%29) is known as identifying a *figure* from the back*ground*. For example, black words on a printed paper are seen as the "figure", and the white sheet as the "background". In ambigrams, the [typographic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_%28punctuation%29) of the background is used as [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) to form new letters and new words. For example, inside a [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case) [H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H), one can easily insert a lowercase [i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I).

The oil painting *You & Me* (US) by [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) (1996) belongs to this category. The word "me" fills the space between the letters of "you".[^o59]

### Tessellations {#Tessellations}

*Image: Ambigram Future tessellation.png.* Ambigram [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) "[Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future)", 180° [rotational symmetry](wikipedia://en/701096). Upside-down, the light-color letters "future" occupy the [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) between the dark color letters. Two tints, blue and black, separate each block. By Basile Morin.

With [Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)-like [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671) associated to word [patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern), ambigrams can be oriented in three, four, and up to six directions via rotational symmetries of 120°, 90° and 60° respectively,[^o61] such as those created by French artist Alain Nicolas.[^o62] Some words can also transform in the [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space), but the multiplication of constraints often has the effect of reducing either the readability or the [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity) of the [designed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy) words.

Ambigram tessellations are word [puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle), in which [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) sets the rules.[^o62]

.

### Chain {#Chain}

*Image: Chain\_ambigram\_Michel\_Onfray.png.* Chain ambigram [Michel Onfray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Onfray), by Basile Morin.

*Image: Ambigramme Nouvel an - chaine.png.* This chain ambigram by Basile Morin, "nouvel an" (*new year*, in French) reads the same upside down.

A chain ambigram is a design where a word (or sometimes words) are interlinked, forming a repeating chain.[^o188] Letters are usually overlapped: a word will start partway through another word. Sometimes chain ambigrams are presented in the form of a circle. For example, the chain "...sunsunsunsun..." can flip upside down, but not the word "sun" alone, written horizontally. A chain ambigram can be constituted of one to several elements. A single element ambigram chain is like a [snake eating its own tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_eating_its_own_tail). A two-elements ambigram chain is like a snake eating the neighbor's tail with the neighbor eating the first snake's, and so on.

[Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim)'s "*Infinity*" works and [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29)'s "*Chain reaction*" are also [self-referential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential), since the first is infinite in the literal sense of the word, and the second, both reversible at 180° and interfering around the letter O, evokes a chain reaction.[^o64]

### Spinonyms {#Spinonyms}

A spinonym is a type of ambigram in which a word is written using the same [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) repeated in different [orientations](wikipedia://en/2544098).[^o45] The term "spinonym" was coined by Greg Huber in 1984 for his creation (or discovery) of a spinonym of the surname of physicist [Alan Guth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Guth).[^o66] WEB is an example of a word that can easily be made into a spinonym thanks to the graphic similarities among its letters.

### Perceptual shift {#Perceptual_shift}

*Image: Wave-particle.jpg.* Perceptual shift ambigram, [*Wave* and *Particle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-particle_duality), by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758).

*Image: TheCat.svg.* Ambiguous letter that can be interpreted as a H or as an A.

[Perceptual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perceptual) [shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_shift) ambigrams, also called "oscillation" ambigrams, are designs with no [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) but can be read as [two different words](wikipedia://en/1316947) depending on how the curves of the letters are interpreted.[^o188] These ambigrams work on the principle of [rabbit-duck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit–duck_illusion)-style ambiguous images.

For example [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) expresses the dual nature of light as revealed by physics with his perceptual shift ambigram [*Wave / Particle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-particle_duality).

### 90° rotational {#90°_rotational}

*Image: Ambigram Ache Heal - I Mind Dr - 90 degrees rotation - vector.svg.* "Ache Heal / I Mind Dr" (or "Mind, I'm Dr"), crossed words, with 90° rotational symmetry, by Basile Morin.

*Image: Ambigramm Jude Muslim (black and red - animated).gif.* 90° rotational ambigram "[Jude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude,_brother_of_Jesus) / [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim)" (*Jew / Muslim* in German) (and incidentally also a chain ambigram). By Basile Morin.

"Quarter-turn" ambigrams or 90° [rotational](wikipedia://en/701096) ambigrams turn [clockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockwise) or [counterclockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclockwise) to express different meanings.[^o6] For example, the letter A can turn into a D and reciprocally, or the letters M (or W) into an E (or a B).[^o45] Hofstadter illustrates this concept with the ambigram "Fuga," written vertically, transformed into "Bach," horizontally, after a 90-degree clockwise rotation.[^o69]

### Totem {#Totem}

*Image: Maria\_monogram\_in\_Franciscan\_church\_Budapest.jpg.* This historical *Maria* [monogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogram) is close to a totem ambigram, almost symmetrical over the vertical axis.

A totem ambigram is an ambigram whose letters are stacked like a [totem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totem_pole), most often offering a vertical axis [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673). This type helps when several letters fit together, but hardly the whole word. For example, in the Maria monogram, the letters M, A and I are individually symmetrical, and the pairing R/A is almost naturally mirroring. When adequately stacked, the 5 letters produce a nice totem ambigram, whereas the whole name "Maria" would not offer the same cooperativeness.

The ambigram artist [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) designed several totemic assemblages, such as the word "METRO" composed of the symmetrical letter M, then section ETR, and below O; or the sentence "THANK YOU", vertical assembly of T, H, A, then of the symmetric NK couple, then finally Y, O, U.[^o71]

### Fractal {#Fractal}

In mathematics, a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) is a geometrical [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) that exhibits [invariance](wikipedia://en/1126638) under scaling. A piece of the whole, if enlarged, has the same geometrical features as the entire object itself. A fractal ambigram is a sort of space-filling ambigrams where the [tiled](wikipedia://en/321671) word branches from itself and then shrinks in a [self-similar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similar) manner, forming a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913).[^o73] In general, only a few letters are constrained in a fractal ambigram. The other letters don't need to look like any other, and thus can be shaped freely.

### 3-dimensional {#3-dimensional}

*Image: 3D-ambigram GEB (Gödel Escher Bach).png.* The 3D ambigram "GEB" (for *[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103)*) on the cover of [Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758)'s book.

*Image: 3d-ambigram.svg.* Three-dimensional ambigram, *ABC*.

A [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_modeling) ambigram is a design where an object is presented that will appear to read several letters or words when viewed from different angles. Such designs can be generated using [constructive solid geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_solid_geometry), a technique used in [solid modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_modeling), and then physically constructed with the [rapid prototyping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_prototyping) method.

3-dimensional ambigram [sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculptures) can also be achieved in [plastic arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_arts). They are [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_rendering) ambigrams.

The original 1979 edition of [Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758)'s *[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103)* featured two 3-D ambigrams on the cover.[^o75]

### Complex {#Complex}

Complex ambigrams are ambigrams involving more than one symmetry, or satisfying the criteria for several types. For example, a complex ambigram can be both rotational and mirror with a 4-fold [dihedral](wikipedia://en/100267) symmetry. Or a spinonym that reads upside down is also a complex ambigram.

## Other glyphs {#Other_glyphs}

### Symbols {#Symbols}

Some [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673) like [summation (Σ)](wikipedia://en/246160), [dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar) [(\$)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar_sign) and the [equals sign (=)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_sign) are symmetrical.

### Languages {#Languages}

*Image: Mirror writing2.jpg.* Mirror ambigram depicting the phrase علي ولي الله (*[Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali) is the vicegerent of God*, in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet)), Ottoman panel, between 1720 and 1730.

Ambigrams exist in many languages. With the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), they generally mix [lowercase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowercase) and [uppercase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppercase) letters. But words can also be symmetrical in other alphabets, like [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_alphabet), [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_alphabets), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet), and even in [Chinese characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_characters) and Japanese [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji).

In [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), 곰 (bear) and 문 (door), 공 (ball) and 운 (luck), or 물 (water) and 롬 ([ROM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROM)) form a natural rotational ambigram. Some syllables like 응 (yes), 표 (ticket/signage) or 를 (*object particle*), and words like "허리피라우" (straighten your back) also make full ambigrams.

The [han character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_character) meaning "hundred" is written 百, that makes a natural 90° rotational ambigram when the [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) makes a quarter turn counterclockwise, one sees "100".[^o80]

### Numbers {#Numbers}

*Image: Ambigram-8-eight-math-2-1-5-rotation-mirror-basile-morin.gif.* Mirror and rotational ambigram of an arithmetic operation illustrating the [commutative property](wikipedia://en/294390) in [addition](wikipedia://en/61338).[^o82]

An ambigram of numbers, or *numeral ambigram*, contains [numerical digits](wikipedia://en/280582), like [1](wikipedia://en/77591639), [2](wikipedia://en/64516), [3](wikipedia://en/208157)...[^o45]

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a [palindromic number](wikipedia://en/105956) (also known as a *numeral palindrome*) is a number that remains the same when its digits are [reversed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflectional_symmetry) through a vertical axis (but not necessarily visually). The palindromic numbers containing only 1, 8, and 0, constitute natural numeric ambigrams (visually [symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflectional_symmetry) through a [mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror)). Also, because the [glyph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) [2](wikipedia://en/64516) is graphically the [mirror image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) of [5](wikipedia://en/6412297), it means numbers like 205 or 85128 are natural numeral mirror ambigrams. Though not palindromic in the mathematical sense, they read frontward and backward like real ambigrams.

A [strobogrammatic number](wikipedia://en/2906596) is a number whose numeral is [rotationally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotationally_symmetric), so that it appears the same when rotated 180 degrees. The numeral looks the same right-side up and upside down (e.g., 69, 96, 1001).[^o83][^o84][^o85]

Some [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_date) are natural numeral ambigrams.[^o86] In March 1961, artist [Norman Mingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Mingo) created an upside-down cover for *[Mad magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_magazine)* featuring an ambigram of the current year. The title says "No matter how you look at it... it's gonna be a *Mad* year. 1961, the first upside-down year since 1881."[^o87] Tuesday, 22 February 2022, was a palindrome and ambigram date called "[Twosday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twosday)" because it contained reversible 2 (two).[^o88][^o89][^o90]

Ambigrams of numbers receive most attention in the realm of [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446).[^o6][^o91]

Ambigrams with numbers sometimes combine letters and numerical digits. Because the number 5 is approximately shaped like the letter S, the number 6 like a lowercase b, the number 9 like the letter g, it is possible to play on these similarities to design ambigrams. A good example is the [Sochi 2014 (Olympic games)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics) logo where the four [glyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) contained in 2014 are exact symmetries of the four letters S, o, i and h, individually.[^o92]

## Other uses {#Other_uses}

*Image: Berg lulu palindrome mirror point.png.* Palindrome with [musical notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_note), here the centre part of [Alban Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alban_Berg)'s opera *[Lulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_%28opera%29)*

As [alphabet letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_letters) are [glyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyph) used in the [writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) to express the [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language) visually, other [symbols](wikipedia://en/37673) are also used in the world to code other fields, like the [prosigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosigns_for_Morse_code) in the [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code) or the [musical notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_notes) in [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music).

Similarly to the ambigrams of letters, the ambigrams with other symbols are generally visually symmetrical, either [point reflective](wikipedia://en/12487536) or [reflective through an axis](wikipedia://en/6025673).

The international [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code) [distress signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distress_signal) [SOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOS) dot is a natural ambigram constituted of dots and dashes. It flips upside down or through a mirror.

In morse code, the letter P coded dot and the letter R coded dot are individually symmetrical, like many other letters and numbers. Also, the letter G coded dash is the exact reverse of the letter W coded dot. Thus, the combination dash / dot coding the pairing G/W constitutes a natural ambigram. Consequently, meaningful natural ambigrams written in morse code certainly exist, like for example the words "gnaw" dashdash dotdot, "[Dou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dou_%28surname%29)" dash dash dot or "[mom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mom)" dash dash dash.[^o5][^o94][^o95]

In [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), the interlude from [Alban Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alban_Berg)'s opera *[Lulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulu_%28opera%29)* is a [palindrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome), thus the [score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheet_music) made up of [musical notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_note) is almost symmetrical through a vertical axis.[^o96] In August 1976, [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) premiered in [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California) a [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_%28music%29) in the form of a fully symmetrical score, reversible 180 degrees, ornamented with three ambigrams.[^o97][^o98]

In [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), researchers study the ambigrammatic property of [narnaviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narnavirus) by using visual representations of the symmetrical sequences.[^o41][^o2][^o99]

#### Calligraphy and typography {#Calligraphy_and_typography}

*Image: Typism book one ambigram Love Song by Basile Morin.jpg.* Ambigram *[Love Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_song)* published in a [typography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) book (*Typism*).

Instead of simply writing them, ambigram [lettering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettering) covers the [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art) of [drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawing) [letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet_letters). Lettering ambigrams do not translate into combinations of alphabet letters that can be used like a [typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface), since they are created with a specific candidate in mind.

The [calligrapher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligrapher), [graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti) writer and [graphic designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_designer) [Niels Shoe Meulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Shoe_Meulman) created several rotational ambigrams like the number "fifty",[^o104] the names "Shoe / Patta",[^o105] and the opposition "Love / Fear".[^o106]

The American [type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface) [designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) [Mark Simonson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Simonson) designed poetic and [humorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humorous) ambigrams, such as the words "Revelation", "Typophile", and the symbiosis "Drink / Drunk".[^o108] The last one makes a [visual pun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_pun) when printed on a [shot glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_glass), sold commercially.[^o109]

#### Visual communication {#Visual_communication}

*Image: Biden-USA-Harris ambigram animated.gif.* Ambigram "[Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Biden) USA [Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris)" by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) conveying a political message during the [2020 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election).

*Image: Anna\_(2019)\_Logo.svg.* The movie *[Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_%282019_feature_film%29)* by [Luc Besson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Besson) (2019) reveals on its [poster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_poster) a mirror ambigram with a vertical axis.

Because they are [visual puns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_pun),[^o6] ambigrams generally attract attention, and thus can be used in [visual communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_communication) to broadcast a [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing) or [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political) message.

In France, a mirror ambigram "[Penelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope_Gate) / benevole" legible through a horizontal axis became a [meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meme) on the web after its diffusion on [Wikimedia Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Commons).[^o139] [Penelope Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penelope_Fillon), wife of French politician and former Prime Minister of France [François Fillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Fillon#Conviction_for_fraud), is suspected of having received wages for a fictitious job. Ironically, her name through the mirror becomes benevole (*[voluntary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_action)* in French), suggesting dedication for a free service. Shared tens of thousands of times on the [social networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_networks), this [humorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humorous) ambigram made the [buzz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_buzz#Positive_vs._negative_buzz) via several French,[^o140] Belgian[^o141][^o142] and Swiss[^o139] [medias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media).

Ambigrams are regularly used by [communication agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_agency) such as [Publicis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicis) to engage the reader or the consumer through two-way messages.[^o143] Thus, in 2021, male first names transformed into female first names are included in a Swiss [advertising campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_campaign) aimed at raising awareness about [gender equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_equality). An intriguing [catchphrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchphrase) typography upside down invites the reader to rotate the magazine, in which the first names "Michael" or "Peter" are transformed into "Nathalie" or "Alice".[^o144][^o145]

In 2015 iSmart's logo on one of its travel [chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_charger) went [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_phenomenon) because the brand's name turned out to be a natural ambigram that read "+[Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew)!" upside down. The company noted that "...we learned a powerful lesson of what not to do when creating a [logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo)."[^o146]

[Cinema posters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_poster) sometimes seduce observers with ambigram titles, such as that of [Tenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenet) by [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan), by central symmetry.[^o30] or [Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_%282019_feature_film%29) by [Luc Besson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Besson) around a vertical axis,[^o147][^o148]

#### Comics {#Comics}

*Image: Ambigrams by Gustave Verbeek (1904) - comics The Upside Downs of Little Lady Lovekins and Old Man Muffaroo - At the house of the writing pig.jpg.* Ambigrams in [comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comics) by [Gustave Verbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Verbeek) in 1904.

The American artist and writer [Peter Newell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Newell) published a [rotational](wikipedia://en/12487536) ambigram in 1893 saying "Puzzle / The end" in the book containing [reversible illustrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_figure) *Topsys & Turvys*.[^o34]

In March 1904 the Dutch-American [comic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic) artist [Gustave Verbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Verbeek) used ambigrams in three consecutive strips of *The UpsideDowns of old man Muffaroo and little lady Lovekins*.[^o36] His comics were [ambiguous images](wikipedia://en/1316947), made in such a way that one could read the six-panel comic, flip the book and keep reading. In *The Wonderful Cure of the Waterfall* (13 March 1904) an Indian medicine man says 'Big waters would make her very sound', while when flipped the medicine man turns into an Indian woman who says 'punos dery, ery apew poom, serlem big'. Which is explained as, 'poor deary' several foreign words that meant that she would call the 'Serlem Big'. The next comic called *At the House of the Writing Pig* (20 March 1904), where two ambigram word [balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_balloon) are featured. The first features an angry pig trying to make the main protagonist leave by showing a sign that says; 'big boy go away, dis am home of mr h hog', up side down it reads 'Boy yew go away. We sip. Home of hog pig.' The protagonist asks the pig if it wants a big bun, upon which it replies 'Why big buns? Am mad u!', which flips into 'In pew we sang big hym'. Finally in *The Bad Snake and the Good Wizard* (1904 Mar 27) there are two more ambigrams. The first turns 'How do you do' into the name of a wizard called 'Opnohop Moy', the second features a squirrel telling the protagonist 'Yes further on' only to inform it that there are 'No serpents here' on his way back. In a 2012 Swedish remake of the book,[^o150] the artist Marcus Ivarsson redraws *The Bad Snake and the Good Wizard* in his own style. He removes the squirrel, but keeps the other ambigram. 'How do you do' is replaced by 'Nejnej' (Swedish for no) and the wizard is now called 'Laulau'.

.

[Oubapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oubapo), *workshop of potential [comic book art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book_art)*, is a [comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book) movement which believes in the use of [formal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formalism_%28art%29) [constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_comics) to push the boundaries of the medium. [Étienne Lécroart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne_Lécroart), [cartoonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoonist), is a founder and key member of Oubapo association, and has composed cartoons that could be read either horizontally, vertically, or in diagonal, and vice versa, sometimes including appropriate ambigrams.[^o151]

#### Drawings and paintings {#Drawings_and_paintings}

*Image: Me\_we\_co.jpg.* Ambigram painting Me / We, horizontal axis mirror type (2007).

The British [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painter), designer and illustrator [Rex Whistler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_Whistler), published in 1946 a rotational ambigram "¡OHO!" for the cover of a book gathering [reversible drawings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_figure).[^o153]

The artist [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29), specialist of ambigrams,[^o111] designed many color [paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paintings) featuring ambigrams of all kinds, figure-ground, rotational, mirror or totem. Among other influences, he particularly admires [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)'s [drawings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawings).[^o154]

The Canadian artist Kelly Klages painted several [acrylics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylic_paint) on [canvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canvas) with ambigram words and sentences referring to famous writers' novels written by [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare) or [Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie), such as *[Third Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Girl)*, *[The Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tempest)*, *[After the Funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/After_the_Funeral)*, *[The Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollow)*, Reformation, [Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes), and *[Elephants Can Remember](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephants_Can_Remember)*.[^o155]

#### Sculptures {#Sculptures}

*Image: Ambigramme\_OUI\_NON\_sculpture\_anamorphosique\_de\_Markus\_Raetz\_2002.jpg.* Ambigram *OUI / NON* (*Yes / No*, in French), by sculptor [Markus Raetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Raetz), installed at the top of a pole on the Place du Rhöne in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), observed from two angles.

The German [conceptual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_art) artist [Mia Florentine Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Florentine_Weiss) built a sculptural ambigram Love Hate (sculpture),[^o157] that has traveled Europe as a symbol of peace and change of perspective.[^o158] Depending on which side the viewer looks at it, the sculpture says "Love" or "Hate". A similar concept was installed in front of the [Reichstag building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_building) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) with the words "Now / Won". Both sculptures are mirror type ambigrams, symmetrical around a vertical axis.[^o159]

The Swiss sculptor [Markus Raetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Raetz) made several three-dimensional ambigram works, featuring words generally with related meanings, such as YES-NO (2003),[^o160] ME-WE (2004, 2010),[^o161] OUI-NON (2000–2002) in French,[^o162][^o163] SI–NO (1996)[^o164] and TODO-NADA (1998) in Spanish.[^o165][^o166] These are [anamorphic](wikipedia://en/2489512) works, which change in appearance depending on the angle of view of the observer. The OUI–NON ambigram is installed on the Place du Rhône, in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), at the top of a metal pole. Physically, the letters have the appearance of iron twists. With the perspective, this work demonstrates that reality can be [ambiguous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambiguous).[^o163]

Some ambigram sculptures by the French conjurer Francis Tabary are reversible by a half-turn rotation, and can therefore be exhibited on a support in two different ways.[^o167][^o168]

#### Tattoos {#Tattoos}

One of the most dynamic sectors that harbors ambigrams is [tattooing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattooing). Because they possess two ways of reading, ambigram tattoos inked on the skin benefit from a "mind-blowing" effect. On the arm, [sleeve tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeve_tattoo) [flip upside-down](wikipedia://en/12487536), on the back or jointly on two wrists they are more striking with a [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/6025673). A large range of [scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy) and [fonts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Font) is available. Experienced ambigram artists can create an [optical illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusion) with a complex [visual design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_design).[^o170]

In 2015, an ambigram [tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattoo) went [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_phenomenon) following an [advertising campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_campaign) developed by the [Publicis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicis) group two years earlier. The *[Samaritans of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans_of_Singapore)* organization, active in suicide prevention, has a 180° reversible "SOS" ambigram logo, [acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) of its name and [homonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homonym) of the famous [SOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOS) distress signal. In 2013, this center orders advertisements that could be inserted in magazines to make readers aware of the problem of [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_%28mood%29) among young people, and the communication agency notices the symmetrical aspect of the logo. As a result, it begins to produce several ambigrammatic visuals, staged in photographic contexts, where sentences such as "I'm fine", "I feel fantastic" or "Life is great" turn into "Save me", "I'm falling apart", and "I hate myself". Readers noticing this logo placed at the upper left corner of the page with an upside-down typographical [catchphrase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchphrase) rotate the newspaper and visualize the double calligraphed messages, which call out with the *SOS*.[^o143][^o171] These ads are so influential that Bekah Miles, an American student herself coming out of a severe depression, chooses to use the "I'm fine / Save me" ambigram to get a tattoo on her thigh. Posted on Facebook, the two-sided photography immediately appeals to many young people, impressed or sensitive to this difficulty.[^o172][^o173] To educate its students, [George Fox University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Fox_University) in the United States then relays the optical illusion in its official journal, through a video totaling more than three million views[^o174] and the information is also reproduced in several local media and international organizations, thus helping to popularize this famous two-way tattoo.[^o175][^o176] Less fortunate, another teenage girl, aged 16, committed suicide, with her also this ambigram found on a note in her room, "I'm fine / Save me", reversible calligraphy today printed on badges and bracelets, for educational purposes.[^o177]

### Literature {#Literature}

#### Palindromes {#Palindromes}

*Image: Ambigram Dogma I am god.png.* Famous [palindrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome) sentence "[Dogma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma) I am God" turned into a mirror ambigram. The [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_case) D at the left was changed into a lowercase d, and the [typographic spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_%28punctuation%29) adjusted.

*Image: Ambigramme\_de\_Georges\_Perec\_-\_andin\_basnoda\_a\_une\_epouse\_qui\_pue\_-\_animation.gif.* [Georges Perec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Perec)'s "vertical [palindrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindrome)" (rotational ambigram), in French.[^o182]

Ambigrams are sorts of [visual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_arts) [palindromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromes).[^o180] Some words turn upside down, others are symmetrical through a mirror. Natural ambigram palindromes exist, like the words "wow", "[malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam)"[^o181] (Dravidian language), or the biotechnology company [Noxxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noxxon) that possesses a [palindromic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromic) name associated to a rotational ambigram logo. But some words are natural ambigrams, though not palindromes in the literary acception, like "bud" for example, because b and d are different letters. As a result, some words and sentences are good candidates for ambigrammists, but not for palindromists, and reciprocally, since the [constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_writing) differ slightly. Authors of ambigrams also benefit from a certain flexibility by playing on the [typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface) and [graphical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_arts) adjustments to influence the reading of their visual palindromes.

[Oulipo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulipo), *workshop of potential literature*, seeks to create works using [constrained writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_writing) techniques.[^o13] [Georges Perec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Perec), French novelist and member of the Oulipo group, designed a rotational ambigram, that he called "vertical palindrome".[^o182][^o15] Sibylline, the sentence "Andin Basnoda a une épouse qui pue" in French means "Andin Basnoda has a smelly wife". Perec did not care about punctuation spaces, but his [creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_writing) flips easily with a classical font like [Arial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arial).

Visual palindromes sometimes perfectly illustrate literary contents. The American author [Dan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Brown) incorporated [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29)'s designs into the plot of his bestseller *[Angels & Demons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_&_Demons)*, and his fictional character [Robert Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Langdon)'s surname was a homage to the [ambigram artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29).[^o183]

The fantasy novel [Abarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abarat), written and illustrated by [Clive Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Barker), features an ambigram of the title on its cover.[^o184]

#### Calligrams {#Calligrams}

*Image: Ambigram\_Good\_ambigrams\_human\_face\_calligram\_design.png.* [Calligram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligram) "Good ambigrams" representing a [face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face) and mirror [self-referential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential) ambigram.

A [calligram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligram) is text arranged in such a way that it forms a thematically related image. It can be a poem, a phrase, a portion of [scripture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripture), or a single word. The visual arrangement can rely on certain use of the [typeface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typeface), [calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy) or [handwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handwriting). The image created by the words illustrates the text by expressing visually what it says, or something closely associated.

In [Islamic calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calligraphy), symmetrical calligrams appear in ancient and modern periods, forming mirror ambigrams in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet) language.[^o33]

The word "[OK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK)" turned 90° [counterclockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterclockwise) evokes a human icon, with the letter O forming the head and the letter K the arms and the legs. The Norwegian Climbing Club Oslo Klatreklubb ([acronym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acronym) "OK") borrowed the concept of this natural [calligram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligram) for their official logo.[^o186]

#### Semantics {#Semantics}

*Image: Ambigramme\_Lapin\_Rabbit.png.* [Bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingual) mirror ambigram playing on the [translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translation) of the word "Lapin" (*[rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit)*, in French).

*Image: Ambigram here away.png.* [Self-referential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential) [composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_%28visual_arts%29) "here / away" displayed in a spiral to express distance, thus enhancing the [meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_interpretation).

As described by [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758), ambigrams are [visual puns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_pun) having two or more (clear) [interpretations](wikipedia://en/26279594) as [written words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Written_word).[^o6]

[Multilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingual) ambigrams can be read one way in a [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language), and another way in a different language or [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphabet).[^o188] Multi-lingual ambigrams can occur in all of the various types of ambigrams, with multi-lingual perceptual shift ambigrams being particularly striking.

Like certain [anagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) with providential meanings such as "Listen / Silent" or "The eyes / They see", ambigrams also sometimes take on a timely sense, for example "up" becomes the abbreviation "dn", very naturally by rotation of 180°.[^o189] But on the other hand, it happens that the luck of the letters makes things bad. This is the case with the weird anagram "[Santa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa) / Satan", as it is with a rotational ambigram that has gone [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_phenomenon) because of the [paradoxical](wikipedia://en/24390) and unintentional message it expresses. Spotted in 2015 on a metal medal marketed without bad intention, the text "[hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope)" displays upside down with a fairly obvious reading "[Adolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)". This coincidence photographed by an Internet user was relayed by several media and constitutes an [ambiguous image](wikipedia://en/1316947).[^o190][^o191]

### Mathematics {#Mathematics}

*Image: 91=90+01wiki.jpg.* Ambigram of a reversible [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) operation.

[Recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) is carried out for [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment) rather than as a strictly research and application-based professional activity.[^o91] An ambigram [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) exists, with the sums of the numbers in each row, each column, and both main diagonals the same right side up and upside down (180° rotational design). Numeral ambigrams also associate with alphabet letters. A "dissection" ambigram of "[squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359)" was achieved in a puzzle where each piece of the word "circle" fits inside a perfect square.[^o6]

[Burkard Polster](wikipedia://en/56381434), professor of mathematics in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne)[^o193] conducted researches on ambigrams and published several books dealing with the topic, including *Eye Twisters, Ambigrams & Other Visual Puzzles to Amaze and Entertain*.[^o194] In the abstract *Mathemagical Ambigrams*, Polster performs several ambigrams closely related to his realm, like the words "[algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)", "[geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446)", "[math](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math)", "[maths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maths)", or "mathematics".[^o6]

[Calculator spelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculator_spelling) is an [unintended characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unintended_consequences) of the [seven-segment display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven-segment_display) traditionally used by [calculators](wikipedia://en/7593), in which, when read upside-down, the digits resemble letters of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet). Also, [palindromic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromic_numbers) and [strobogrammatic numbers](wikipedia://en/2906596) sometimes attract attention of mathematician ambigrammists.[^o84][^o83]

Ambigram [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671) and [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_modeling) ambigrams are two types particularly fun for the mathematician in [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). Word [patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern) in tessellations can start from 35 different fundamental [polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygons), such as the [rhombus](wikipedia://en/183884), the [isosceles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isosceles) [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420), or the [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496).[^o61]

[Word puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_puzzle) are used as a source of [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment), but can additionally serve an [educational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) purpose. The American [puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle) designer [Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim) published several ambigrams in *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* in [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435)'s "[Mathematical Games](wikipedia://en/44051448)" column, among them long sentences like *"Martin Gardner's celebration of [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind)"* turning into "Physics, patterns and [prestidigitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestidigitation)".[^o197]

### Psychology {#Psychology}

*Image: Ambigram Ambiguity.png.* "Ambiguity", 180° rotational ambigram.

[Legibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legibility) is an important aspect in successful ambigrams. It concerns the ease with which a reader decodes symbols. If the message is lost or difficult to perceive, an ambigram does not work.[^o8] Readability is related to [perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perception), or how our brain [interprets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interprets) the forms we see through our eyes.[^o199]

[Symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) in ambigrams generally improves the visual appearance of the [calligraphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphic) words.[^o45] [Hermann Rorschach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Rorschach), inventor of the [Rorschach Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rorschach_Test) notices that asymmetric figures are rejected by many subjects. Symmetry supplies part of the necessary artistic composition.[^o200]

For many [amateurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur), designing ambigrams represents a [recreational activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_activity), where [serendipity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serendipity) can play a fertile role, when the author makes an unplanned fortunate discovery.[^o6][^o37]

#### Duality and analogy {#Duality_and_analogy}

*Image: U%2B262F.svg.* [Yin and yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang) symbol, concept of [dualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic_cosmology).

*Image: Ambigram Two in One (animated).gif.* "Two in one", half-turn ambigram.

In the word "*ambigram*", the root *ambi-* means "both" and is a popular prefix in a [world of dualities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualistic_cosmology), such as day/night, left/right, birth/death, good/evil.[^o202] In *Wordplay: The Philosophy, Art, and Science of Ambigrams*,[^o203] [John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) mentions the [yin and yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang) symbol as one of his major influences to create upside down words.

Ambigrams are mentioned in *[Metamagical Themas](wikipedia://en/169419)*, an eclectic collection of articles that [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) wrote for the [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) magazine *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* during the early 1980s.[^o204]

### Magic {#Magic}

*Image: Ambigram\_Real\_world\_Prank\_Fake\_rainbow\_animated\_(2).gif.* "[Real world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_World_%28TV_series%29) / [Prank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prank) [Fake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion)", ambigram expressing [illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illusion).

In [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_%28illusion%29), ambigrams work like [visual illusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_illusions), revealing an unexpected new message from a particular written word.[^o207]

In the first series of the British show *[Trick or Treat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trick_or_Treat_%28TV_series%29)*, the show's host and creator [Derren Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derren_Brown) uses cards with rotational ambigrams.[^o208][^o209] These cards can read either 'Trick' or 'Treat'.

[Ambiguous images](wikipedia://en/1316947), of which ambigrams are a part, cause ambiguity in different ways. For example, by rotational symmetry, as in the Illusion of *[The Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cook_%28Arcimboldo%29)* by [Giuseppe Arcimboldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Arcimboldo) (1570);[^o210] sometimes by a [figure-ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure–ground_%28perception%29) ambivalence as in [Rubin vase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubin_vase); by perceptual shift as in the [rabbit–duck illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit–duck_illusion), or through [pareidolias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareidolia); or again, by the representation of [impossible objects](wikipedia://en/56110), such as [Necker cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necker_cube) or [Penrose triangle](wikipedia://en/56108). For all these types of images, certain ambigrams exist, and can be combined with [visuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visuals) of the same type.

[John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) designed a [figure-ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-ground_%28perception%29) ambigram "[optical illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_illusion)" with the two words "optical" and "illusion", one forming the figure and the other the background. "Optical" is easier to see initially but "illusion" emerges with longer observation.[^o211]

#### Clothing and fashion {#Clothing_and_fashion}

*Image: Ambigram Bounce, Adidas pink shoe - animated.gif.* Ambigram "Bounce", printed inside a pink [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) shoe.

[Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) marketed a line of [sneakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sneakers) called "Bounce", with an ambigram [typography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) printed inside the shoe.

#### Accessories {#Accessories}

*Image: Ambigram Cognac Danger on a set of two shot glasses (empty and full).jpg.* "[Cognac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognac) / [Danger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_alcohol)", front and back, on a set of two [shot glasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_glass). Humorous warning related to [alcohol consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_alcohol).

The [special edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_edition#Music) paper sleeve (CD with DVD) of the solo album [Chaos and Creation in the Backyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_and_Creation_in_the_Backyard) by [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney) features an ambigram of the singer's name.[^o218]

The [Grateful Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grateful_Dead) have used ambigrams several times, including on their albums *[Aoxomoxoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoxomoxoa)*[^o219] and *[American Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Beauty_%28album%29)*.[^o220]

Although the words spelled by most ambigrams are relatively short in length, one [DVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD) cover for *[The Princess Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Bride)* movie creates a rotational ambigram out of two words "Princess Bride", whether viewed right side up or upside down.[^o221]

The concept of reversible sign that some merchants use through their windows to indicate that the store is sometimes "open", sometimes "closed", was inaugurated at the beginning of the 2000s, by a rotational ambigram "Open / Closed" developed by David Holst.[^o54]

## Creation {#Creation}

*Image: Ambigram\_Escher\_and\_tessellation\_background\_-\_photomontage\_with\_reversible\_hands.jpg.* Ambigram and [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671) "Escher", handmade design.

Different ambigram [artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist), sometimes called *ambigrammists*,[^o204][^o224] may create distinctive ambigrams from the same words, differing in both [style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_%28visual_arts%29) and [form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elements_of_art#Form).

### Handmade designs {#Handmade_designs}

There are no universal guidelines for creating ambigrams, and different [ways](wikipedia://en/1467948) of approaching problems coexist. A number of books suggest [methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) for [creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity), including *WordPlay*,[^o111] *Eye Twisters*,[^o194] and *Ambigrams Revealed*,[^o45] in English.

### Generators {#Generators}

[Computerized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program) methods to [automatically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) create ambigrams have been developed such as ambigram generators.[^o227][^o228]

### Artists {#Artists}

[John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) and [Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim) each believed that they had invented ambigrams in the 1970s.[^o230]

#### Douglas Hofstadter {#Douglas_Hofstadter}

*Image: Rainbow reflection ambigram.jpg.* [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758)'s mirror ambigrams featuring the names of the seven [rainbow](wikipedia://en/3871014) colors, followed by a complex perceptual shift ambigram "2006" / "Doug", fusion of the date and his signature.[^o232]

[Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758) coined the term.[^o11][^o6]

To explain visually the numerous types of possible ambigrams, Hofstadter created many pieces with different constraints and symmetries.[^o233] Hofstadter has had several exhibitions of his artwork in various university galleries.[^o234][^o235]

According to [Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim), Hofstadter once created a series of 50 ambigrams on the name of all the states in the US.[^o236]

In 1987 a book of 200 of his ambigrams, together with a long dialogue with his alter ego Egbert G. Gebstadter on ambigrams and [creativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity), was published in Italy.[^o7][^o12]

In 2025, his book *Ambigrammia Between Creation and Discovery* (acronym "ABCD"), published by [Yale University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University_Press), presents in English hundreds of hand-crafted ambigrams, as well as a few drawn by his friends, on 320 pages.[^o237] The book is a reflection on this art form called "Ambigrammia". His works of all types are presented in their historical context, with explanations regarding the particularities of each.[^o238][^o239]

#### John Langdon {#John_Langdon}

[John Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Langdon_%28typographer%29) is a [self-taught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-taught) [artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist), [graphic designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_design) and [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painter), who started designing ambigrams in the late 1960s and early 70s. [Lettering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettering) specialist, Langdon is a professor of [typography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typography) and [corporate identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_identity) at [Drexel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drexel_University) in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia).[^o241]

John Langdon produced a mirror image logo "Starship" in 1972–1973,[^o242][^o243] that was sold to the rock band [Jefferson Starship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Starship).

Langdon's ambigram book *Wordplay* was published in 1992. It contains about 60 ambigrams. Each design is accompanied by a brief essay that explores the word's definition, its etymology, its relationship to philosophy and science, and its use in everyday life.[^o111]

Ambigrams became more popular as a result of [Dan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Brown) incorporating John Langdon's designs into the plot of his bestseller, *[Angels & Demons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_&_Demons)*, and the DVD release of the *[Angels & Demons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angels_&_Demons_%28film%29)* movie contains a bonus chapter called "This is an Ambigram". Langdon also produced the ambigram that was used for some versions of the book's cover.[^o230] Brown used the name [Robert Langdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Langdon) for the hero in his novels as a homage to John Langdon.[^o183][^o247]

[Blacksmith Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacksmith_Records), the music management company and [record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label), possesses a rotational ambigram logo[^o244] designed by John Langdon.[^o245]

#### Scott Kim {#Scott_Kim}

[Scott Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Kim) is one of the best-known masters of the art of ambigrams.[^o114] He is an American [puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle) designer and [artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artist) who published in 1981 a book called *Inversions* with ambigrams of many types.[^o18][^o247]

He wrote the foreword to his friend Douglas Hofstadter's book *ABCD*, published in 2025.[^o248]

#### Other {#Other}

[Nikita Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Prokhorov_%28graphic_artist%29) is a [graphic designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphic_designer), [lettering artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lettering_artist) and ambigram designer. His book *Ambigrams Revealed* showcases ambigram designs of all types, from all around the world.[^o45][^o250]

Born in 1946, [Alain Nicolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Nicolas) is a specialist of figurative and ambigram [tessellations](wikipedia://en/321671). In his book, he performed many tilings with various words like "infinity", "[Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein)" or "inversion" legible in many orientations.[^o61] According to *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, Nicolas has been called "the world's finest artist of [Escher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.C._Escher)-style [tilings](wikipedia://en/321671)".[^o251]
