# List of prime numbers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of prime numbers"}

This is a list of articles about [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666). A prime number (or *prime*) is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) greater than 1 that has no [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) other than 1 and itself. By [Euclid's theorem](wikipedia://en/3143591), there is an infinite number of prime numbers. Subsets of the prime numbers may be generated with various [formulas for primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formulas_for_primes).

:::

The first 1,000 primes are listed below, followed by lists of notable types of prime numbers in alphabetical order, giving their respective first terms. The number 1 is [neither prime nor composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primality_of_1).

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="List of the first 1000 prime numbers"}

The [Goldbach conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758) verification project reports that it has computed all primes smaller than 4×10${}^{18}$.[^o6] That means 95,676,260,903,887,607 primes (nearly 10${}^{17}$), but they were not stored. There are known formulae to evaluate the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) (the number of primes smaller than a given value) faster than computing the primes. This has been used to compute that there are 1,925,320,391,606,803,968,923 primes (roughly 2) smaller than 10${}^{23}$. A different computation found that there are 18,435,599,767,349,200,867,866 primes (roughly 2) smaller than 10${}^{24}$, if the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) is true.[^o8]

### Harmonic primes {#Harmonic_primes}

### Residue classes of primes {#Residue_classes_of_primes}

Of the form *an* + *d* for fixed integers *a* and *d*. Also called primes congruent to *d* [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) *a*.

The primes of the form 2*n*+1 are the odd primes, including all primes other than 2. Some sequences have alternate names: 4*n*+1 are the Pythagorean primes, 4*n*+3 are the integer Gaussian primes, and 6*n*+5 are the Eisenstein primes (with 2 omitted). The classes 10*n*+*d* (*d* = 1, 3, 7, 9) are primes ending in the decimal digit *d*.

If *a* and *d* are relatively prime, the arithmetic progression contains infinitely many primes.

### Wall–Sun–Sun primes {#Wall–Sun–Sun_primes}

A prime *p* > 5, if *p*${}^{2}$ divides the [Fibonacci number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number) $F_{p - \left(\frac{{p}}{{5}}\right)}$, where the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567) $\left(\frac{{p}}{{5}}\right)$ is defined as

$$\left(\frac{p}{5}\right) = \begin{cases} 1 &\textrm{if}\;p \equiv \pm1 \pmod 5\\ -1 &\textrm{if}\;p \equiv \pm2 \pmod 5. \end{cases}$$

, no Wall-Sun-Sun primes have been found below $2^{64}$ (about $18\cdot 10^{18}$).[^o116]
