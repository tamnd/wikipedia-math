# Algebraic function field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic function field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **algebraic function field** (often abbreviated as **function field**) of $n$ variables over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$ is a finitely generated [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) $K/k$ which has [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) $n$ over $k$.[^o2] Equivalently, an algebraic function field of $n$ variables over $k$ may be defined as a [finite field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_field_extension) of the field $K=k(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ of [rational functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_functions) in $n$ variables over $k$.

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

As an example, in the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $k[x,y]$ consider the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) $y^2-x^3$ and form the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) $k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3)$. This is a function field of one variable over $k$; it can also be written as $k(x)(\sqrt{x^3})$ (with degree 2 over $k(x)$) or as $k(y)(\sqrt[3]{y^2})$ (with degree 3 over $k(y)$). We see that the degree of an algebraic function field is not a well-defined notion.

:::

## Category structure {#Category_structure}

The algebraic function fields over $k$ form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759); the [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_%28category_theory%29) from function field $K$ to $L$ are the [ring homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/26411) $f:K\to L$ with $f(a)=a$ for all $a$ in $k$. All these morphisms are [injective](wikipedia://en/45196). If $K$ is a function field over $k$ of $n$ variables, and $L$ is a function field in $m$ variables, and $n>m$, then there are no morphisms from $K$ to $L$.

## Function fields arising from varieties, curves and Riemann surfaces {#Function_fields_arising_from_varieties,_curves_and_Riemann_surfaces}

The [function field of an algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/2783843) of dimension $n$ over $k$ is an algebraic function field of $n$ variables over $k$. Two varieties are [birationally equivalent](wikipedia://en/382733) if and only if their function fields are isomorphic (but note that non-[isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_of_varieties) varieties may have the same function field). Assigning to each variety its function field yields a [duality](wikipedia://en/379051) (contravariant equivalence) between the category of varieties over $k$ (with [dominant rational maps](wikipedia://en/2783119) as morphisms) and the category of algebraic function fields over $k$. The varieties considered here are to be taken in the [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) sense; they need not have any $k$-rational points, like the curve $x^2+y^2+1=0$ defined over the real numbers.

The case $n=1$ (irreducible algebraic curves in the [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) sense) is especially important, since every function field of one variable over $k$ arises as the function field of a uniquely defined [regular](wikipedia://en/43016199) (i.e. non-singular) projective irreducible algebraic curve over $k$. In fact, the function field yields a duality between the category of regular projective irreducible algebraic curves (with [dominant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_scheme_theory#dominant) [regular maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_map_%28algebraic_geometry%29) as morphisms) and the category of function fields of one variable over $k$.

The field $M(X)$ of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857) defined on a connected [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) $X$ is a function field of one variable over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}$. In fact, $M(X)$ yields a duality between the category of compact connected Riemann surfaces (with non-constant [holomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic) maps as morphisms) and function fields of one variable over $\mathbb{C}$. A similar correspondence exists between compact connected [Klein surfaces](wikipedia://en/20478442) and function fields in one variable over $\mathbb{R}$.

## Number fields and finite fields {#Number_fields_and_finite_fields}

The [function field analogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_field_analogy) states that almost all theorems on [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) have a counterpart on function fields of one variable over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615), and these counterparts are frequently easier to prove (see [analogue for irreducible polynomials over a finite field](wikipedia://en/23692#Analogue_for_irreducible_polynomials_over_a_finite_field)). In the context of this analogy, both number fields and function fields over finite fields are usually called "[global fields](wikipedia://en/288358)".

The study of function fields over a finite field has applications in [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) and [error correcting codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_correcting_code). For example, the function field of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) over a finite field (an important mathematical tool for [public key cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_key_cryptography)) is an algebraic function field.

Function fields over the field of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) play also an important role in solving [inverse Galois problems](wikipedia://en/739166).

## Field of constants {#Field_of_constants}

Given any algebraic function field $K$ over $k$, we can consider the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of elements of $K$ which are [algebraic](wikipedia://en/186291) over $k$. These elements form a field, known as the *field of constants* of the algebraic function field.

For instance, $\mathbb{C}(x)$ is a function field of one variable over $\mathbb{R}$; its field of constants is $\mathbb{C}$.

## Valuations and places {#Valuations_and_places}

Key tools to study algebraic function fields are [absolute values, valuations, places](wikipedia://en/5042667) and their completions.

Given an algebraic function field $K/k$ of one variable, we define the notion of a *valuation ring* of $K/k$: this is a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) $\mathcal{O}$ of $K$ that contains $k$ and is different from $k$ and $K$, and such that for any $x$ in $K$ we have $x\in\mathcal{O}$ or $x^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}$. Each such valuation ring is a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) and its maximal ideal is called a *place* of $K/k$.

A *discrete valuation* of $K/k$ is a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) function $v:K\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}$ such that for all $x,y\in K$,

- $$v(xy)=v(x)+v(y),$$
- $$v(x+y)\geq \min\{v(x),v(y)\},$$
- $$v(x)=\infty \iff x=0$$

and $v(a)=0$ for all $a\in k\setminus \{0\}$.

There are natural bijective correspondences between the set of valuation rings of $K/k$, the set of places of $K/k$, and the set of discrete valuations of $K/k$. These sets can be given a natural [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) structure: the [Zariski–Riemann space](wikipedia://en/27902093) of $K/k$.
