# Mathematics education in New York

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics education in New York"}

**Mathematics education in New York** in regard to both content and teaching method can vary depending on the type of school a person attends. Private school math education varies between schools whereas New York has statewide public school requirements where standardized tests are used to determine if the teaching method and educator are effective in transmitting content to the students. While an individual private school can choose the content and educational method to use, New York State mandates content and methods statewide. Some public schools have and continue to use established methods, such as Montessori for teaching such required content. New York State has used various foci of content and methods of teaching math including New Math (1960s), 'back to the basics' (1970s), Whole Math (1990s), Integrated Math, and Everyday Mathematics.

:::

How to teach math, what to teach, and its effectiveness has been a topic of debate in New York State and nationally since the "Math Wars" started in the 1960s. Often, current political events influence how and what is taught. The politics in turn influence state legislation. California, New York, and several other states have influenced textbook content produced by publishers.[^o2]

The state of [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29) has implemented a novel curriculum for [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

The courses *Algebra I*, *Geometry*, and *Algebra II/Trigonometry* are required [courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_%28education%29) mandated by the [New York State Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Department_of_Education) for [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school) [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation).

## 2007-present {#2007-present}

### Algebra {#Algebra}

This is the first course in the new three-year curriculum. It was originally "Math A," but was replaced with "Integrated Algebra." In 2009 when Common Core was adopted, "Algebra I" replaced "Integrated Algebra" and is still in use today.

Students learn to how write, solve, and graph [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) and [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489). They will also learn how to solve [systems of equations](wikipedia://en/249617), as well as how to simplify [exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent), [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175), [exponential functions](wikipedia://en/9678), [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000), [radicals](wikipedia://en/29208), and [rational expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_fraction). Other topics included are [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685).

Some schools divided Algebra 1 into a two-year sequence. The students who receive it begin with Algebra 1A, and will cover the rest of the Algebra 1 topics in Algebra 1B in the next school year. Typically, most students are placed in Algebra 1 which covers all lessons rather than splitting them.

At the conclusion of the one-year course, students take the New York State Regents Exam. The first administration of this exam was in June 2008.

### Geometry {#Geometry}

This is the second course in the new three year curriculum. It replaced part of "Math A" and part of "Math B"

[Geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) concepts such as [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) are introduced. The course also covers topics including [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) and [parallel lines](wikipedia://en/664497), [triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangles), [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278), and [transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_%28mathematics%29).

At the conclusion of this one-year course, students take a New York State Regents exam in Geometry. The first administration of this exam was in June 2009.

### Algebra II {#Algebra_II}

This is the third and last course of the new three-year curriculum. It replaced the elements of "Math B" not covered in geometry.

This course covers concepts of advanced [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and as well prepares students for [pre-calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-calculus) and [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176). In 2016, the Board of Regents removed some of the [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) concepts and lessons from the course, and the Regents exam has been renamed from "Algebra 2/Trigonometry" to "Algebra II".

At the conclusion of the one-year course, students take the New York State Regents exam for Algebra II. This is the last Regents exam in mathematics students could take. Like the former "Math B" Regents, it is considered one of the hardest High School Regents examinations, along with the Physical Setting/Chemistry regents and the Physical Setting/Physics regents. The Algebra 2/Trigonometry exam was given from June 2010 through January 2017 and the new Algebra II Exam has been given since June 2016.

## 2001-2009 {#2001-2009}

### Math A (former course) {#Math_A_(former_course)}

Math A replaced the former "Course 1" curriculum which focused solely on the topic of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), while Math A covered a whole range of topics. After algebra, students would take Geometry in the 10th grade and Algebra II in the 11th grade.

In Math A, students learned to how write, solve, and graph [equations](wikipedia://en/9284) and [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489). They learned how to solve [systems of equations](wikipedia://en/249617), [quadratics](wikipedia://en/25175), as well as [exponents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent), [exponential functions](wikipedia://en/9678), [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000), [radicals](wikipedia://en/29208), and [rational expressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_fraction). Other topics included are [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). Geometric concepts such as [right triangles](wikipedia://en/39420) are also introduced. The course works in conjunction with [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State)'s Standards for Mathematics.<http://www.emsc.nysed.gov/ciai/mst/pub/mststa1_2.pdf> One course lasted three semesters, after which students took the Regents Math A Examination.

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Math A/B"}

### Math A/B (former course) {#Math_A/B_(former_course)}

Math A/B took the place of the former "Course 2" curriculum, which focused almost solely on [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), while Math A/B focused on a whole range of topics. Math A/B served as a bridge between the Math A and Math B courses.

Math A/B stayed true to its geometric roots, as the first half of the course covered topics such as [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) and [parallel lines](wikipedia://en/664497), [triangles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangles), [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278), and [transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_%28mathematics%29). After their first [semester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semester), students took the [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State) Math A [Regents exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regents). June 2008 was the last administration of this exam. For the second half of the year, students would begin Math B. They covered [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [geometric figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric), and an introduction to [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261).

:::

### Math B (former course) {#Math_B_(former_course)}

Math B was required to receive a High School Regents Diploma with Advanced Designation. The course replaces the former "Course 3" curriculum, which focused almost solely on [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261). Math B focused on a whole range of topics. It was taken after the student has completed and passed Math A.

A Regents exam was taken at the end of the $1\tfrac{1}{2}$-year course. The Math B Regents was often considered one of the most difficult New York State Regents.

Math B covered concepts that can be found in [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261) and advanced [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and prepared students for [pre-calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-calculus) and [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and reviewed past topics. During their year of study, students learned different [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977), graphed complex [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690) and [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29), as well as reviewed topics such as [exponential functions](wikipedia://en/9678), systems of inequalities, and [radicals](wikipedia://en/235029). As the year progressed, students were expected to relate these [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) to the real world, create [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) through their own [research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research), and begin a [classroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classroom) discussion about these topics. At the end of their studies, they would take the [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State) Math B [Regents Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regents_%28Examinations%29). The last administration of this exam was in August 2010.

### Changes proposed in 2004 {#Changes_proposed_in_2004}

In November 2004, the Mathematics Standards Committee made a report to the [Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Regents)<https://web.archive.org/web/20051227034410/http://www.emsc.nysed.gov/osa/mathre/new_mathregents.htm> about the State's requirements for high school graduation as related to mathematics. The committee recommended that:

- The curriculum should return to its old format as a one-year course with a Regents exam taken at the end of the year, with the new exam to be administered no earlier than June 2007. Math A would have its name changed to Integrated Algebra.
- In addition to the current Regents exam at the end of the Math B course, there should be another Regents exam, at the end of the first half of Math B study (currently Math A/B), to be administered no later than June 2006, bringing the total number of Regents exams to three.
- The course's name should be changed to *Geometry* for the first half of the course, and *Algebra II and Trigonometry* for the second half of the course (currently Math B). Integrated is only used in the new Algebra course.
  - The State of New York has announced that these changes will be implemented in the 2009/10 scholastic year.
- In 2009, Integrated Algebra was switched to Algebra 1, due to the common core adoption.

The move was praised by many who thought the changes to the original draft curriculum were unnecessary.

## Pre-2002 {#Pre-2002}

### Sequential Mathematics {#Sequential_Mathematics}

Prior to the 2001-2002 school year, the New York State Board of Regents offered a three-year Sequential Mathematics series divided into three Courses I, II, and III. [^o15][^o16]
