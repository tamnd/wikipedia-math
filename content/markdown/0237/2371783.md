# Different ideal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Different ideal"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **different ideal** (sometimes simply the **different**) is defined to measure the (possible) lack of duality in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) *K*, with respect to the [field trace](wikipedia://en/457105). It then encodes the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644#In_algebraic_number_theory) data for [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of the ring of integers. It was introduced by [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106) in 1882.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If *O*<sub>*K*</sub> is the ring of integers of *K*, and *tr* denotes the field trace from *K* to the [rational number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number_field) **Q**, then

$$x \mapsto \mathrm{tr}~x^2$$

is an [integral quadratic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_quadratic_form) on *O*<sub>*K*</sub>. Its [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_of_a_quadratic_form) as quadratic form need not be +1 (in fact this happens only for the case *K* = **Q**). Define the *inverse different* or *codifferent*[^o6][^o7] or *Dedekind's complementary module*[^o8] as the set *I* of *x* ∈ *K* such that tr(*xy*) is an integer for all *y* in *O*<sub>*K*</sub>, then *I* is a [fractional ideal](wikipedia://en/460700) of *K* containing *O*<sub>*K*</sub>. By definition, the **different ideal** δ<sub>*K*</sub> is the inverse fractional ideal *I*<sup>−1</sup>: it is an ideal of *O*<sub>*K*</sub>.

The [ideal norm](wikipedia://en/460672) of *δ*<sub>*K*</sub> is equal to the ideal of *Z* generated by the [field discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) *D*<sub>*K*</sub> of *K*.

The *different of an element* α of *K* with minimal polynomial *f* is defined to be δ(α) = *f*′(α) if α generates the field *K* (and zero otherwise):[^o10] we may write

$$\delta(\alpha) = \prod \left({\alpha - \alpha^{(i)}}\right) \$$

where the α<sup>(*i*)</sup> run over all the roots of the characteristic polynomial of α other than α itself.[^o9] The different ideal is generated by the differents of all integers α in *O*<sub>*K*</sub>.[^o10][^o11] This is Dedekind's original definition.[^o12]

The different is also defined for a [finite degree extension](wikipedia://en/32769983) of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375). It plays a basic role in [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) for [p-adic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_field).

:::

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Relative different"}

## Relative different {#Relative_different}

The **relative different** δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub> is defined in a similar manner for an extension of number fields *L* / *K*. The [relative norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_norm) of the relative different is then equal to the relative discriminant Δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub>.[^o15] In a [tower of fields](wikipedia://en/28194169) *L* / *K* / *F* the relative differents are related by δ<sub>*L* / *F*</sub> = δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub>*δ*<sub>*K* / *F*</sub>.[^o8][^o16]

The relative different equals the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of the relative [Kähler differential](wikipedia://en/603916) module $\Omega^1_{O_L/O_K}$:[^o15][^o17]

$$\delta_{L/K} = \{ x \in O_L : x \mathrm{d} y = 0 \text{ for all } y \in O_L \} .$$

The [ideal class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_class) of the relative different δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub> is always a square in the [class group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_group) of *O*<sub>*L*</sub>, the ring of integers of *L*.[^o18] Since the relative discriminant is the norm of the relative different it is the square of a class in the class group of *O*<sub>*K*</sub>:[^o19] indeed, it is the square of the [Steinitz class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinitz_class) for *O*<sub>*L*</sub> as a *O*<sub>*K*</sub>-module.[^o20]

:::

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Differential exponent"}

## Ramification {#Ramification}

The relative different encodes the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644#In_algebraic_number_theory) data of the field extension *L* / *K*. A prime ideal *p* of *K* ramifies in *L* if the factorisation of *p* in *L* contains a prime of *L* to a power higher than 1: this occurs if and only if *p* divides the relative discriminant Δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub>. More precisely, if

*p* = *P*<sub>1</sub><sup>*e*(1)</sup> ... *P*<sub>*k*</sub><sup>*e*(*k*)</sup>

is the factorisation of *p* into prime ideals of *L* then *P*<sub>*i*</sub> divides the relative different δ<sub>*L* / *K*</sub> if and only if *P*<sub>*i*</sub> is ramified, that is, if and only if the ramification index *e*(*i*) is greater than 1.[^o16][^o23] The precise exponent to which a ramified prime *P* divides δ is termed the **differential exponent** of **P** and is equal to *e* − 1 if *P* is [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified): that is, when *P* does not divide *e*.[^o24] In the case when *P* is [wildly ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildly_ramified) the differential exponent lies in the range *e* to *e* + *e*ν<sub>*P*</sub>(e) − 1.[^o23][^o25][^o26] The differential exponent can be computed from the orders of the [higher ramification groups](wikipedia://en/22590101#Higher_ramification_groups) for [Galois extensions](wikipedia://en/457064):[^o27]

$$\sum_{i=0}^\infty (|G_i|-1).$$

:::

## Local computation {#Local_computation}

The different may be defined for an extension of local fields *L* / *K*. In this case we may take the extension to be [simple](wikipedia://en/4771726), generated by a primitive element α which also generates a [power integral basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_integral_basis). If *f* is the minimal polynomial for α then the different is generated by *f**(α).***
