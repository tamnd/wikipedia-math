# List of topics named after Leonhard Euler

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of topics named after Leonhard Euler"}

*Image: Leonhard Euler - Jakob Emanuel Handmann (Kunstmuseum Basel).jpg.* Leonhard Euler (1707–1783)

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), many topics are [named in honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of Swiss mathematician [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1707–1783), who made many important discoveries and innovations. These include functions, equations, formulas, identities, numbers (single or sequence), or other mathematical entities. Many of these entities have been given simple yet ambiguous names such as *Euler's function*, *Euler's equation*, and *Euler's formula*.

:::

Euler's work touched upon so many fields that he is often the earliest written reference on a given matter. In view of the high number of properties and theorems discovered by Euler, many of them are conventionally named after the first person to have proved them after him.[^o2][^o3]

## Conjectures {#Conjectures}

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's sum of powers conjecture"}

- [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](wikipedia://en/9660) – disproved for exponents 4 and 5 during the 20th century; unsolved for higher exponents

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's Graeco-Latin square conjecture"}

- [Euler's Graeco-Latin square conjecture](wikipedia://en/473979#Thirty-six_officers_problem) – proved to be true for $n=6$ and disproved otherwise, during the 20th century

:::

:::

## Equations {#Equations}

Usually, *Euler's equation* refers to one of (or a set of) [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) (DEs). It is customary to classify them into [ODEs](wikipedia://en/32742753) and [PDEs](wikipedia://en/52564).

Otherwise, *Euler's equation* may refer to a non-differential equation, as in these three cases:

::: {#o9 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Lotka equation"}

- [Euler–Lotka equation](wikipedia://en/22631822), a [characteristic equation](wikipedia://en/218268) employed in mathematical demography

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's pump and turbine equation"}

- [Euler's pump and turbine equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_pump_and_turbine_equation)

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler transform"}

- [Euler transform](wikipedia://en/3877474#Euler_transform) used to accelerate the convergence of an alternating series and is also frequently applied to the [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series)

:::

### Ordinary differential equations {#Ordinary_differential_equations}

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler rotation equations"}

- [Euler rotation equations](wikipedia://en/898792), a set of [first-order ODEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_ordinary_differential_equation) concerning the rotations of a [rigid body](wikipedia://en/455769).

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Cauchy equation"}

- [Euler–Cauchy equation](wikipedia://en/1038257), a linear equidimensional [second-order ODE](wikipedia://en/1424309) with [variable coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_coefficient). Its second-order version can emerge from [Laplace's equation](wikipedia://en/36941) in [polar coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinates).

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Bernoulli beam equation"}

- [Euler–Bernoulli beam equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Bernoulli_beam_equation), a fourth-order ODE concerning the elasticity of structural beams.

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's differential equation"}

- [Euler's differential equation](wikipedia://en/24655281), a first order nonlinear ordinary differential equation

:::

### Partial differential equations {#Partial_differential_equations}

::: {#o18 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler conservation equations"}

- [Euler conservation equations](wikipedia://en/396022), a set of quasilinear first-order [hyperbolic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_equation) used in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034) for [inviscid flows](wikipedia://en/5877457). In the (Froude) limit of no external field, they are [conservation equations](wikipedia://en/6956).

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Tricomi equation"}

- [Euler–Tricomi equation](wikipedia://en/1089161) – a second-order PDE emerging from Euler conservation equations.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Poisson–Darboux equation"}

- [Euler–Poisson–Darboux equation](wikipedia://en/16703428), a second-order PDE playing important role in solving the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691).

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Lagrange equation"}

- [Euler–Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/294995), a second-order PDE emerging from minimization problems in [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882).

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler–Arnold equation"}

- [Euler–Arnold equation](wikipedia://en/80202352), describes the evolution of a [velocity field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity) when the [Lagrangian flow](wikipedia://en/5289693) is a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096) in a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of smooth [transformations](wikipedia://en/1904373).

:::

## Laws {#Laws}

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's first law"}

- [Euler's first law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_laws#Euler's_first_law), the sum of the external forces acting on a rigid body is equal to the rate of change of [linear momentum](wikipedia://en/20431#Linear_momentum_of_a_system_of_particles) of the body.

:::

::: {#o55 .entry cue=list-entry name="Euler's second law"}

- [Euler's second law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler's_laws#Euler's_second_law), the sum of the external [moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_%28physics%29) about a point is equal to the rate of change of [angular momentum](wikipedia://en/2839) about that point.

:::
