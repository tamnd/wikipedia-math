# E-plane and H-plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E-plane and H-plane"}

The **E-plane and H-plane** are reference [planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) for [linearly polarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_polarized) [waveguides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveguide_%28electromagnetism%29), [antennas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antenna_%28radio%29) and other [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) devices.

:::

In waveguide systems, as in the electric circuits, it is often desirable to be able to split the circuit power into two or more fractions. In a waveguide system, an element called a junction is used for power division.

In a low frequency electrical network, it is possible to combine circuit elements in [series or in parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_and_parallel_circuits), thereby dividing the source power among several circuit components. In microwave circuits, a waveguide with three independent ports is called a TEE junction. The output of E-Plane Tee is 180° out of phase where the output of H-plane Tee is in phase.[^o2]

## E-Plane {#E-Plane}

For a [linearly-polarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_polarization) antenna, this is the plane containing the electric field vector (sometimes called the E aperture) and the direction of maximum radiation. The [electric field](wikipedia://en/41092) or "E" plane determines the [polarization](wikipedia://en/41564) or orientation of the radio wave. For a vertically polarized antenna, the E-plane usually coincides with the vertical/elevation plane. For a horizontally polarized antenna, the E-Plane usually coincides with the horizontal/azimuth plane. E- plane and H-plane should be 90 degrees apart.

## H-plane {#H-plane}

In the case of the same linearly polarized antenna, this is the plane containing the magnetic field vector (sometimes called the H aperture) and the direction of maximum radiation. The [magnetizing field](wikipedia://en/36563#The_H-field) or "H" plane lies at a right angle to the "E" plane. For a vertically polarized antenna, the H-plane usually coincides with the horizontal/[azimuth](wikipedia://en/47487) plane. For a horizontally polarized antenna, the H-plane usually coincides with the vertical/elevation plane.

## Co- and cross-polarizations {#Co-_and_cross-polarizations}

Co-polarization (co-pol) and cross-polarization (cross-pol) are defined for the radiating E and H planes. These directions are defined in spherical coordinates corresponding to the spherical wavefronts of the propagating wave. By convention, the co-pol direction is the direction of the E field while the cross-pol direction is the direction of the H field. Receive power for a co-pol oriented antenna is maximum while receive power is minimum for cross-pol orientation.
