# William Karush

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Karush"}

**William Karush** (1 March 1917 – 22 February 1997) was an American [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of mathematics at [California State University at Northridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University_at_Northridge) and was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) best known for his contribution to [Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions](wikipedia://en/2397362). In his master's thesis he was the first to publish these necessary conditions for the inequality-constrained problem,[^o3] although he became renowned after a seminal conference paper by [Harold W. Kuhn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_W._Kuhn) and [Albert W. Tucker](wikipedia://en/935841).[^o4] He also worked as a physicist for the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project), although he signed the [Szilárd petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szilárd_petition) and became a [peace activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_activist) afterwards.[^o5]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Sam and Tillie (formerly Shmuel and Tybel) Karush immigrated to the United States from [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Białystok), then under Russian control, now [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland).[^o7] Karush was born in Chicago, Illinois on March 1, 1917. He graduated from [Murray F. Tuley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuley_High_School) in 1934. He attended the [Central YMCA College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_YMCA_College) in Chicago for two years before transferring to [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), where he studied mathematics and received a Bachelor of Science in 1938, a Masters of Science in 1939, and a Ph.D. in 1942.[^o8]

## Career {#Career}

From 1942 to 1943, he worked as a mathematician for the [Carnegie Institution for Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Institution_for_Science) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) and from 1943–1945, he was employed by University of Chicago, while working on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project). He stayed at the University of Chicago as an associate professor until 1956 when he took up a position in industry. In 1967 he returned to academia as a professor at California State University, Northridge.[^o8]
