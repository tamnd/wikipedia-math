# Mathukumalli V. Subbarao

Mathukumalli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mathukumalli V. Subbarao"}

**Mathukumalli Venkata Subbarao** (May 4, 1921 – February 15, 2006) was an [Indo-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Canadian) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specialising in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was a long-time resident of [Edmonton, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton,_Alberta), Canada.

:::

Subbarao was born in the small village of [Yazali, Guntur, Andhra Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazali,_Guntur,_Andhra_Pradesh), India into a [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people) family. He received his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) from [Presidency College, Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_College,_Chennai) in 1941. He went on to complete a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), advised by [Ramaswamy S. Vaidyanathaswamy](wikipedia://en/11498709). He worked at Presidency College, Madras, [Sri Venkateswara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Venkateswara_University), and the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri), before moving in 1963 to the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), where he spent the rest of his professional career.[^o6]

In the 1960s Subbarao began to study the [congruence](wikipedia://en/20087) properties of the [partition function](wikipedia://en/312250), p(n), which became one of his favourite problems. For example, he conjectured[^o7] that if A and B are integers with 0 ≤ B \< A, there are infinitely many n for which p(An+B) is even and infinitely many n for which p(An+B) is odd. [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono)[^o8] showed that the even case is always true and that if there is one number n such that p(An+B) is odd, then there are infinitely many such numbers n. The odd case was finally settled by Silviu Radu.[^o9] A more general variant of the conjecture was formulated by [Morris Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Newman)[^o10] predicting that for any given r and m, there are infinitely many n such that p(n)= r(mod m). At the end of his life, Subbarao co-authored a book on partition theory[^o11] with A.K. Agarwal and Padmavathamma. [Partition theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_theory) is ubiquitous in mathematics with connections to the representation theory of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) and the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564), [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms), and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939). Thus, Subbarao's conjectures, though seemingly simple, will generate fundamental research activity for years to come. He also researched special classes of [divisors](wikipedia://en/49492) and the corresponding analogues of [divisor functions](wikipedia://en/407249) and [perfect numbers](wikipedia://en/23670), such as those arising from the [exponential divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_divisor) ("e-divisors") which he defined. Many other mathematicians have published papers building on his work in these subjects.

A prolific collaborator, Subbarao had more than 40 joint authors (including [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), giving him [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) 1). He continued producing mathematics papers into the final years of his life. He died in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton) at the age of 84.

Subbarao was the father of Prof. [Mathukumalli Vidyasagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathukumalli_Vidyasagar).[^o12]
