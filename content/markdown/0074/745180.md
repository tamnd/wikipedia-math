# Leo Moser

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leo Moser"}

**Leo Moser** (11 April 1921 – 9 February 1970) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria)-[Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) mathematician, best known for his [polygon notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser_polygon_notation).

:::

A native of [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), Leo Moser immigrated with his parents to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) at the age of three. He received his [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree from the [University of Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manitoba) in 1943, and a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1945. After two years of teaching he went to the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) to complete a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD), supervised by [Alfred Brauer](wikipedia://en/22923580). There, in 1950, he began suffering recurrent [heart problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease). He took a position at Texas Technical College for one year, and joined the faculty of the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta) in 1951, where he remained until his death at the age of 48.

In 1966, Moser posed the question "What is the region of smallest area which will accommodate every planar arc of length one?"[^o4] Rephrased to consider the planar arc a "worm", this became known as [Moser's worm problem](wikipedia://en/30774748)[^o5] and as of 2026 remains an [open problem](wikipedia://en/183091).
