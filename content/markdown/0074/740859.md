# HP 38G

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="HP 38G"}

*Image: Calculator\_hp38g.jpg.* HP 38G

The **HP 38G** (F1200A, F1892A) is a programmable [graphing calculator](wikipedia://en/335004) by [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) (HP). It was introduced in 1995 with a [suggested retail price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suggested_retail_price) of 80. HP credits a committee of eight high school, [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college), and university teachers with assisting in the design of the calculator.

:::

The calculator is derived from and the [hardware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Saturn) is based on the more powerful science/engineering oriented [HP&nbsp;48 series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;48_series) of machines.

Unlike the HP 48 series, which offered both [reverse Polish notation](wikipedia://en/26513) and algebraic entry logic, the HP 38G has only algebraic entry. The calculator is programmable, supporting small, interactive applications called "aplets".

## Successors {#Successors}

After a **HP 38G+**[^o3] prototype was cancelled in 1998, the 38G was replaced in 2000 by the [HP&nbsp;39G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;39G) and (in Europe and some other markets) [HP&nbsp;40G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;40G). The two models were differentiated by the inclusion of a [computer algebra system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Algebra_System) (CAS) in the HP 40G. An unofficial method of operating the 40G's CAS on the 39G was addressed in the next follow-up device, the [HP&nbsp;39g+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;39g+) introduced in 2003, for which there is no known CAS implementation.

Further successors have included the [HP&nbsp;39gs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;39gs) and [HP&nbsp;40gs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;40gs) (with and without CAS) introduced in 2006, and the [HP&nbsp;39gII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP&nbsp;39gII) in 2011.

Beginning with the 39g+, all of these successors have been built using an [ARM architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARM_architecture) CPU with varying degrees of emulation to support software written for previous members of the series, and have gradually added more modern methods of connectivity such as [USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB) to either replace or supplement the original [RS-232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RS-232) and [IR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared#Communications).
