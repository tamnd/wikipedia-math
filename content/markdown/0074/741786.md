# James Cullen (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Cullen"}

Father **James Cullen**, [S.J.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) (19 April 1867 – 7 December 1933) was an Irish mathematician.

:::

He was born at 89 West Street, [Drogheda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drogheda), County Louth, to Michael Cullen, a baker, and Catherine McDonough.[^o2] Initially, he was educated privately, then by the Christian Brothers.[^o3] He studied pure and applied mathematics at [Trinity College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Dublin), then at Mungret College, Limerick, before deciding to become a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit). He studied in England in Mansera House, and St. Mary's, and was ordained as a priest on 31 July 1901.[^o5]

In 1905, he taught mathematics at [Mount St Mary's College, Spinkhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St_Mary's_College,_Spinkhill) in [Derbyshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire) and published his finding of what is now known as [Cullen numbers](wikipedia://en/321895) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).[^o4]

He ended up looking after accounts for the English province of the Jesuits, while contributing to mathematics journals.[^o5]
