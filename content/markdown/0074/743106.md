# Scott continuity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scott continuity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), given two [partially ordered sets](wikipedia://en/23572) *P* and *Q*, a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *f*: *P* → *Q* between them is **Scott-continuous** (named after the mathematician [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875)) if it preserves all [directed suprema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_supremum). That is, for every [directed subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_subset) *D* of *P* with [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) in *P*, its [image](wikipedia://en/579311) has a supremum in *Q*, and that supremum is the image of the supremum of *D*, i.e. ∐*f*\[*D*\] = *f*(∐*D*), where ∐ is the directed join.[^o2] When *Q* is the poset of truth values, i.e. [Sierpiński space](wikipedia://en/387651), then Scott-continuous functions are [characteristic functions](wikipedia://en/240790) of open sets, and thus Sierpiński space is the classifying space for open sets.

:::

A subset *U* of a partially ordered set *P* is called **Scott-open** if it is an [upper set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_set) and if it is **inaccessible by directed joins**, i.e. if all directed sets *D* with supremum in *U* have non-empty [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) with *U.* The Scott-open subsets of a partially ordered set *P* form a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) on *P,* the **Scott topology**. A function between partially ordered sets is Scott-continuous if and only if it is [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) with respect to the Scott topology.[^o2]

The Scott topology was first defined by Dana Scott for [complete lattices](wikipedia://en/44777) and later defined for arbitrary partially ordered sets.[^o4]

Scott-continuous functions are used in the study of models for [lambda calculi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambda_calculi)[^o4] and the [denotational semantics](wikipedia://en/55275) of computer programs.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A Scott-continuous function is always [monotonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function), meaning that if *A* ≤<sub>*P*</sub> *B* for *A, B* ∈ *P,* then *f*(*A*) ≤<sub>*Q*</sub> *f*(*B*).

A subset of a directed complete partial order is [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) with respect to the Scott topology induced by the partial order if and only if it is a [lower set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_set) and closed under suprema of directed subsets.[^o7]

A [directed complete partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_complete_partial_order) (dcpo) with the Scott topology is always a [Kolmogorov space](wikipedia://en/68121) (i.e., it satisfies the [T<sub>0</sub> separation axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T0_separation_axiom)).[^o7] However, a dcpo with the Scott topology is a [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) if and only if the order is trivial.[^o7] The Scott-open sets form a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777) when ordered by [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29).[^o10]

For any Kolmogorov space, the topology induces an order relation on that space, the [specialization order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialization_order): *x* ≤ *y* if and only if every [open neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighbourhood) of *x* is also an open neighbourhood of *y*. The order relation of a dcpo *D* can be reconstructed from the Scott-open sets as the specialization order induced by the Scott topology. However, a dcpo equipped with the Scott topology need not be [sober](wikipedia://en/602490): the specialization order induced by the topology of a sober space makes that space into a dcpo, but the Scott topology derived from this order is finer than the original topology.[^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The open sets in a given topological space when ordered by [inclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusion_%28set_theory%29) form a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) on which the Scott topology can be defined. A subset *X* of a topological space *T* is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) with respect to the topology on *T* (in the sense that every [open cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_cover) of *X* contains a [finite subcover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_subcover) of *X*) if and only if the set of [open neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighbourhood) of *X* is open with respect to the Scott topology.[^o10]

For **CPO**, the [cartesian closed category](wikipedia://en/237037) of dcpo's, two particularly notable examples of Scott-continuous functions are [curry](wikipedia://en/6600) and [apply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apply).[^o11]

[Nuel Belnap](wikipedia://en/1251311) used Scott continuity to extend [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) to a [four-valued logic](wikipedia://en/4213424).[^o12]
