# Meyniel graph

*Image: Chordal-graph.svg.* In a Meyniel graph, every long odd cycle (such as the black 5-cycle shown here) must have at least two chords (green)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Meyniel graph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **Meyniel graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in which every odd [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) of length five or more has at least two [chords](wikipedia://en/744165) (edges connecting non-consecutive [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of the cycle).[^o8] The chords may be uncrossed (as shown in the figure) or they may cross each other, as long as there are at least two of them.

:::

The Meyniel graphs are named after Henri Meyniel (also known for [Meyniel's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop_number)), who proved that they are [perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/670531) in 1976,[^o2] long before the proof of the [strong perfect graph theorem](wikipedia://en/744171) completely characterized the perfect graphs. The same result was independently discovered by (Markosjan Karapetjan 1976).[^o4]

## Perfection {#Perfection}

The Meyniel graphs are a subclass of the perfect graphs. Every [induced subgraph](wikipedia://en/1655188) of a Meyniel graph is another Meyniel graph, and in every Meyniel graph the size of a [maximum clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique) equals the minimum number of colors needed in a [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743). Thus, the Meyniel graphs meet the definition of being a perfect graph, that the clique number equals the chromatic number in every induced subgraph.[^o8][^o2][^o4]

Meyniel graphs are also called the **very strongly perfect graphs**, because (as Meyniel conjectured and Hoàng proved) they can be characterized by a property generalizing the defining property of the [strongly perfect graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_perfect_graph): in every induced subgraph of a Meyniel graph, every vertex belongs to an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) that intersects every [maximal clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_clique).[^o8][^o6]

## Related graph classes {#Related_graph_classes}

The Meyniel graphs contain the [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165), the [parity graphs](wikipedia://en/51745979), and their subclasses the [interval graphs](wikipedia://en/363423), [distance-hereditary graphs](wikipedia://en/17315337), [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431), and [line perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/54306580).[^o8]

*Image: House graph.svg.* The house graph is perfect but not Meyniel

Although Meyniel graphs form a very general subclass of the perfect graphs, they do not include all perfect graphs. For instance the house graph (a pentagon with only one chord) is perfect but is not a Meyniel graph.

## Algorithms and complexity {#Algorithms_and_complexity}

Meyniel graphs can be recognized in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time),[^o10] and several graph optimization problems including [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) that are [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) for arbitrary graphs can be solved in polynomial time for Meyniel graphs.[^o11][^o12]
