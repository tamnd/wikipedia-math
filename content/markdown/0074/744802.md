# Stein's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stein's lemma"}

**Stein's lemma**, named in honor of [Charles Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stein_%28statistician%29), is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) that is of interest primarily because of its applications to [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) — in particular, to [James–Stein estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James–Stein_estimation) and [empirical Bayes methods](wikipedia://en/744988) — and its applications to [portfolio choice theory](wikipedia://en/734434).[^o2] The theorem gives a formula for the [covariance](wikipedia://en/157059) of one [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with the value of a function of another, when the two random variables are [jointly normally distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_normal_distribution).

:::

Note that the name "Stein's lemma" is also commonly used[^o3] to refer to a different result in the area of [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), which connects the [error exponents in hypothesis testing](wikipedia://en/60531312) with the [Kullback–Leibler divergence](wikipedia://en/1115052). This result is also known as the Chernoff–Stein lemma[^o4] and is not related to the lemma discussed in this article.

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Suppose *X* is a [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462) [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653) μ and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) σ<sup>2</sup>. Further suppose *g* is a [differentiable function](wikipedia://en/330206) for which the two expectations $\operatorname{E}(g(X) (X - \mu))$ and $\operatorname{E}(g'(X))$ both exist. (The existence of the expectation of any random variable is equivalent to the finiteness of the expectation of its [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991).) Then

$$\operatorname{E}\bigl(g(X)(X-\mu)\bigr)=\sigma^2 \operatorname{E}\bigl(g'(X)\bigr).$$

### Multidimensional {#Multidimensional}

In general, suppose *X* and *Y* are jointly normally distributed. Then

$$\operatorname{Cov}(g(X),Y)= \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)\operatorname{E}(g'(X)).$$

For a general multivariate Gaussian random vector $(X_1, ..., X_n) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ it follows that

$$\operatorname{E}\bigl(g(X)(X-\mu)\bigr)=\Sigma\cdot E\bigl(\nabla g(X)\bigr).$$

Similarly, when $\mu = 0$,

$$\operatorname{E}[\partial_i g(X) ] = \operatorname{E}[g(X) (\Sigma^{-1}X)_i], \quad \operatorname{E}[\partial_i\partial_j g(X) ] = \operatorname{E}[g(X) ((\Sigma^{-1}X)_i(\Sigma^{-1}X)_j - \Sigma^{-1}_{ij})]$$

### Gradient descent {#Gradient_descent}

Stein's lemma can be used to stochastically estimate gradient:

$$\nabla \operatorname{E}_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)}\bigl(g(x + \Sigma^{1/2}\epsilon)\bigr) = \Sigma^{-1/2} \operatorname{E}_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)}\bigl(g(x + \Sigma^{1/2}\epsilon)\epsilon\bigr) \approx \Sigma^{-1/2} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i = 1}^N g(x + \Sigma^{1/2}\epsilon_i )\epsilon_i$$

where $\epsilon_1, \dots, \epsilon_N$ are IID samples from the standard normal distribution $\mathcal N(0, I)$. This form has applications in Stein variational [gradient descent](wikipedia://en/201489)[^o9] and Stein variational [policy gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policy_gradient).[^o10]

:::

::: {#o12 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The univariate [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) for the univariate normal distribution with expectation 0 and variance 1 is

$$\varphi(x)={1 \over \sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^2/2}$$

Since $\int x \exp(-x^2/2)\,dx = -\exp(-x^2/2)$ we get from [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252):

$$\operatorname{E}[g(X)X] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int g(x) x \exp(-x^2/2)\,dx = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int g'(x) \exp(-x^2/2)\,dx = \operatorname{E}[g'(X)].$$

The case of general variance $\sigma^2$ follows by [substitution](wikipedia://en/193748).

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

[Isserlis' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem#Gaussian_integration_by_parts) is equivalently stated as

$$\operatorname{E}(X_1 f(X_1,\ldots,X_n))=\sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{Cov}(X_1,X_i)\operatorname{E}(\partial_{X_i}f(X_1,\ldots,X_n)).$$

where $(X_1,\dots X_{n})$ is a zero-mean [multivariate normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_normal) random vector.

Suppose *X* is in an [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174), that is, *X* has the density

$$f_\eta(x)=\exp(\eta'T(x) - \Psi(\eta))h(x).$$

Suppose this density has support $(a,b)$ where $a,b$ could be $-\infty ,\infty$ and as $x\rightarrow a\text{ or }b$, $\exp (\eta'T(x))h(x) g(x) \rightarrow 0$ where $g$ is any differentiable function such that $E|g'(X)|<\infty$ or $\exp (\eta'T(x))h(x) \rightarrow 0$ if $a,b$ finite. Then

$$E\left[\left(\frac{h'(X)}{h(X)} + \sum \eta_i T_i'(X)\right)\cdot g(X)\right] = -E[g'(X)].$$

The derivation is same as the special case, namely, integration by parts.

If we only know $X$ has support $\mathbb{R}$, then it could be the case that $E|g(X)| <\infty \text{ and } E|g'(X)| <\infty$ but $\lim_{x\rightarrow \infty} f_\eta(x) g(x) \not= 0$. To see this, simply put $g(x)=1$ and $f_\eta(x)$ with infinitely spikes towards infinity but still integrable. One such example could be adapted from $f(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & x \in [n, n + 2^{-n}) \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$ so that $f$ is smooth.

Extensions to elliptically-contoured distributions also exist.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
