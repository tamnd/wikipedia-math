# Carl Runge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Runge"}

**Carl David Tolmé Runge** (; 30 August 1856 – 3 January 1927) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Reich) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), and [spectroscopist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopist).

:::

He was co-developer and co-[eponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym) of the [Runge–Kutta method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runge–Kutta_method) (), in the field of what is today known as [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Runge spent the first few years of his life in [Havana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havana), where his father Julius Runge was the Danish consul. His mother was Fanny Schwartz Tolmé.[^o4] The family later moved to [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_%28city%29), where his father died early (in 1864).

In 1880, he received his Ph.D. in mathematics at [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), where he studied under [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459). In 1886, he became a professor at the [Technische Hochschule Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover) in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany).

His interests included mathematics, [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy), [geodesy](wikipedia://en/12608), and [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics). In addition to pure mathematics, he did experimental work studying [spectral lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_line) of various elements (together with [Heinrich Kayser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Kayser)), and was very interested in the application of this work to [astronomical spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_spectroscopy).

In 1904, on the initiative of [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) he was offered a position at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), which he accepted. There he remained until his retirement in 1925.

### Family {#Family}

His daughter [Iris](wikipedia://en/41305556) also became a mathematician and his son [Wilhelm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Runge) was an early developer of radar. Another of his daughters, Nerina (Nina), married the mathematician [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238).

## Honors {#Honors}

The [crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater) [Runge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runge_%28crater%29) on the [Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon) is named after him. The [Schumann–Runge bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann–Runge_bands) of [molecular oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allotropes_of_oxygen) are named after him and [Victor Schumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Schumann).
