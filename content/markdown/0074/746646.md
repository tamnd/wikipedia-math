# Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie"}

**Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie** (; German for *Lectures on Number Theory*) is the name of several different textbooks of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). The best known was written by [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) and [Richard Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106), and published in 1863. Others were written by [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), and [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118). They all cover elementary number theory, Dirichlet's theorem, quadratic fields and forms, and sometimes more advanced topics.

:::

## Dirichlet and Dedekind's book {#Dirichlet_and_Dedekind's_book}

Based on Dirichlet's number theory course at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), the Vorlesungen were edited by Dedekind and published after Lejeune Dirichlet's death. Dedekind added several appendices to the Vorlesungen, in which he collected further results of Lejeune Dirichlet's and also developed his own original mathematical ideas.

### Scope {#Scope}

The Vorlesungen cover topics in elementary number theory, [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), including [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087), quadratic congruences, [quadratic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/25272) and binary [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478).

### Contents {#Contents}

The contents of Professor [John Stillwell](wikipedia://en/15066763)'s 1999 translation of the Vorlesungen are as follows

Chapter 1. On the divisibility of numbers

Chapter 2. On the congruence of numbers

Chapter 3. On quadratic residues

Chapter 4. On quadratic forms

Chapter 5. Determination of the class number of binary quadratic forms

Supplement I. Some theorems from Gauss's theory of circle division

Supplement II. On the limiting value of an infinite series

Supplement III. A geometric theorem

Supplement IV. Genera of quadratic forms

Supplement V. Power residues for composite moduli

Supplement VI. Primes in arithmetic progressions

Supplement VII. Some theorems from the theory of circle division

Supplement VIII. On the Pell equation

Supplement IX. Convergence and continuity of some infinite series

This translation does not include Dedekind's Supplements X and XI in which he begins to develop the theory of [ideals](wikipedia://en/25977).

The German titles of supplements X and XI are:

Supplement X: Über die Composition der binären quadratische Formen (On the composition of binary quadratic forms)

Supplement XI: Über die Theorie der ganzen algebraischen Zahlen (On the theory of algebraic integers)

Chapters 1 to 4 cover similar ground to [Gauss'](wikipedia://en/6125) [Disquisitiones Arithmeticae](wikipedia://en/736713), and Dedekind added footnotes which specifically cross-reference the relevant sections of the Disquisitiones. These chapters can be thought of as a summary of existing knowledge, although Dirichlet simplifies Gauss's presentation, and introduces his own proofs in some places.

Chapter 5 contains Dirichlet's derivation of the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) formula for real and imaginary [quadratic fields](wikipedia://en/454781). Although other mathematicians had conjectured similar formulae, Dirichlet gave the first rigorous proof.

Supplement VI contains Dirichlet's proof that an arithmetic progression of the form *a*+*nd* where *a* and *d* are coprime contains an infinite number of primes.

### Importance {#Importance}

The Vorlesungen can be seen as a watershed between the classical number theory of [Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966), [Jacobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustav_Jakob_Jacobi) and [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), and the modern number theory of Dedekind, [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) and [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). Dirichlet does not explicitly recognise the concept of the [group](wikipedia://en/41890) that is central to [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), but many of his proofs show an implicit understanding of group theory.

The Vorlesungen contains two key results in number theory which were first proved by Dirichlet. The first of these is the [class number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) formulas for binary quadratic forms. The second is a proof that arithmetic progressions contain an infinite number of primes (known as [Dirichlet's theorem](wikipedia://en/101453)); this proof introduces [Dirichlet L-series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_L-series). These results are important milestones in the development of analytic number theory.

## Kronecker's book {#Kronecker's_book}

[Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450)'s book was first published in 1901 in 2 parts and reprinted by Springer in 1978. It covers elementary and algebraic number theory, including Dirichlet's theorem.

## Landau's book {#Landau's_book}

[Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230)'s book *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie* was first published as a 3-volume set in 1927. The first half of volume 1 was published as *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie. Aus der elementare Zahlentheorie* in 1950, with an English translation in 1958 under the title *Elementary number theory*. In 1969 Chelsea republished the second half of volume 1 together with volumes 2 and 3 as a single volume.

Volume 1 on elementary and [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333) includes the topics such as Dirichlet's theorem, Brun's sieve, binary quadratic forms, Goldbach's conjecture, Waring's problem, and the Hardy–Littlewood work on the singular series. Volume 2 covers topics in analytic number theory, such as estimates for the error in the prime number theorem, and topics in geometric number theory such as estimating numbers of lattice points. Volume 3 covers algebraic number theory, including ideal theory, quadratic number fields, and applications to Fermat's last theorem. Many of the results described by Landau were state of the art at the time but have since been superseded by stronger results.

## Hasse's book {#Hasse's_book}

[Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118)'s book *Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie* was published in 1950, and is different from and more elementary than his book *Zahlentheorie*. It covers elementary number theory, Dirichlet's theorem, and quadratic fields.
