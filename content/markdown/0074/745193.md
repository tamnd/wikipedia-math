# Herbert Robbins

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herbert Robbins"}

**Herbert Ellis Robbins** (January 12, 1915 – February 12, 2001) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He did research in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory), [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), and a variety of other fields.

:::

He was the co-author, with [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), of *[What is Mathematics?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_is_Mathematics?)*. The [Robbins lemma](wikipedia://en/13342698), used in [empirical Bayes methods](wikipedia://en/744988), is named after him. [Robbins algebras](wikipedia://en/5825422) are named after him because of a conjecture (since proved) that he posed concerning [Boolean algebras](wikipedia://en/3959). The [Robbins' theorem](wikipedia://en/36633800), in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), is also named after him, as is the [Whitney–Robbins synthesis](wikipedia://en/32183016), a tool he introduced to prove this theorem. The well-known unsolved problem of minimizing in sequential selection the expected rank of the selected item under full information, sometimes referred to as the fourth [secretary problem](wikipedia://en/1971750), also bears his name: [Robbins' problem (of optimal stopping)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbins'_problem_%28of_optimal_stopping%29).

## Biography {#Biography}

Robbins was born in [New Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castle,_Pennsylvania), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania).

As an undergraduate, Robbins attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523) influenced him to become interested in mathematics. Robbins received a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from Harvard in 1938 under the supervision of [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) and was an instructor at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) from 1939 to 1941. After [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Robbins taught at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) from 1946 to 1952, where he was one of the original members of the department of mathematical statistics, then spent a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). In 1953, he became a professor of mathematical statistics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University). He retired from full-time activity at Columbia in 1985 and was then a professor at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) until his retirement in 1997. He has 567 descendants listed at the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](http://genealogy.math.ndsu.nodak.edu/id.php?id=7781).

In 1955, Robbins introduced [empirical Bayes methods](wikipedia://en/744988) at the Third Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability. Robbins was also one of the inventors of the first [stochastic approximation](wikipedia://en/8979437) algorithm, the Robbins–Monro method, and worked on the theory of [power-one tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-one_test) and [optimal stopping](wikipedia://en/7189886). In 1985, in the paper "Asymptotically efficient adaptive allocation rules", with [TL Lai](wikipedia://en/63735520), he constructed uniformly convergent population selection policies for the [multi-armed bandit](wikipedia://en/2854828) problem that possess the fastest rate of convergence to the population with highest mean, for the case that the population reward distributions are the one-parameter exponential family. These policies were simplified in the 1995 paper "Sequential choice from several populations", with [Michael Katehakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MN_Katehakis).

He was a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and was past president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).

## Selected writings {#Selected_writings}

**Books by Herbert Robbins**

- *What is Mathematics? An Elementary Approach to Ideas and Methods*, with [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), London: Oxford University Press, 1941.
- "Great Expectations: The Theory of Optimal Stopping", with Y. S. Chow and [David Siegmund](wikipedia://en/28862740) Boston: Houghton Mifflin, 1971.
- "Introduction to Statistics", with John Van Ryzin, Science Research Associates, 1975.

**Articles (selection)**

- A theorem on graphs with an application to a problem on traffic control, *American Mathematical Monthly*, vol. **46** (1939), pp. 281–283.
- The [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) for dependent [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685), with [Wassily Hoeffding](wikipedia://en/2547060), *Duke Mathematical Journal*, vol. **15** (1948), pp. 773–780.
- A [stochastic approximation](wikipedia://en/8979437) method, with Sutton Monro, *Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, vol. **22**, no. 3 (September 1951), pp. 400–407.
- Some aspects of the sequential design of experiments, in "Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society", vol. 58, 1952.
- Two-stage procedures for estimating the difference between means, with Ghurye, SG, "Biometrika", 41(1), 146–152, 1954.
- The strong law of large numbers when the first moment does not exist, with [C. Derman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_Derman), in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, vol. 41, 1955.
- An empirical Bayes approach to statistics, in *Proceedings of the Third Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability*, Jerzy Neyman, ed., vol. 1, Berkeley, California: University of California Press, 1956, pp. 157–163.
- On the asymptotic theory of fixed-width sequential confidence intervals for the mean, with Chow, Y.S., "The Annals of Mathematical Statistics", 36(2), 457–462, 1965.
- Statistical methods related to the law of the iterated logarithm, "The Annals of Mathematical Statistics", 41(5), 1397–1409, 1970.
- Optimal stopping, "The American Mathematical Monthly", 77(4), 333–343, 1970.
- A convergence theorem for nonnegative almost supermartingales and some applications, with [David Siegmund](wikipedia://en/28862740), "Optimizing methods in statistics", 233–257, 1971.
- Sequential tests involving two populations, with [David Siegmund](wikipedia://en/28862740), "Journal of the American Statistical Association, 132–139, 1974.
- A class of dependent random variables and their maxima, with Lai, T.L. "Probability Theory and Related Fields", 42(2), 89–111, 1978
- Asymptotically efficient adaptive allocation rules with TL Lai, in "Advances in applied mathematics", vol. 6, 1985.
- Sequential choice from several populations with [M. N. Katehakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_N._Katehakis), in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, vol. 92, 1995.
