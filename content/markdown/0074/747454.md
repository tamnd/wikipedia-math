# Paradigm (experimental)

*Image: Skinner box scheme 01.svg.* Skinner box

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paradigm"}

In the [behavioural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behaviour) sciences (e.g. [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [neurosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurosciences)), an experimental **paradigm** is an [experimental](wikipedia://en/59861) setup or way of conducting a certain type of experiment (a [protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_%28science%29)) that is defined by certain fine-tuned standards, and often has a theoretical background. A paradigm in this technical sense, however, is not a way of thinking as it is in the [epistemological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) meaning ([paradigm](wikipedia://en/175308)).

:::

In the social sciences empiricist experimentation has [independent \[and dependent\] variables](wikipedia://en/437701) and control conditions...What is the origin of the hypotheses which are studied? Given the basic [design](wikipedia://en/9541), the [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) and the particular conditions for the experiment, an experimental paradigm must be made up. The paradigm typically includes factors such as experimental instructions for the subjects, the physical design of the experiment room, and the rules for process of the trial or trials to be carried out.[^o2]

The more paradigms which are attempted, and the more variables within a single paradigm are attempted, with the same results, the more sure one is of the results, that, "the effect is a true one and not merely a product of artifacts engendered by the use of a particular paradigm."[^o3] The three core factors of paradigm design may be considered: "(a) ...the 'nuts and bolts' of the paradigm itself...; (b) ...implementation concerns...; and (c) resources available."[^o4]

An experimental paradigm is a model of research that is copied by many researchers who all tend to use the same variables, start from the same assumptions, and use similar procedures. Those using the same paradigm tend to frame their questions similarly.[^o5]

For example, the [stop-signal paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop-signal_paradigm), "is a popular experimental paradigm to study [response inhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Response_inhibition)."[^o6] The [cooperative pulling paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_pulling_paradigm) is used to study [cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperation). The [weather prediction test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_prediction_test) is a paradigm used to study [procedural learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procedural_learning).[^o6] Other examples include [Skinner boxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinner_box), [rat mazes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_maze), and [trajectory mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory_mapping).
