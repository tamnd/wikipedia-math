# William Clarke (cryptographer)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William Clarke"}

**William Francis "Nobby" Clarke** (1883–1961) was a British intelligence officer and cryptographer of naval codes in both World Wars.

:::

## Background and early life {#Background_and_early_life}

Clarke was educated at [Harrow School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrow_School) and [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford), and trained as a lawyer, being admitted to the bar in 1906 by his father, [Sir Edward Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Clarke_%28barrister%29), a prominent lawyer and later Solicitor-General.[^o3]

## Naval career {#Naval_career}

In 1915, he was commissioned as an assistant paymaster, having failed the eye examination for executive officer. He knew German, and in March 1916 joined [Room 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_40).[^o3] His talent was for information analysis rather than code-breaking. He was on duty during the [Battle of Jutland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jutland), and was unimpressed by the inefficient handling and distribution of intelligence. When Clarke and [Francis Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Birch_%28cryptographer%29) were chosen in 1919 to write a history of Room 40, their outspoken criticism of the Navy's mishandling of intelligence led to the history being allegedly "suppressed" in Clarke's opinion.[^o3]

## Later career {#Later_career}

In 1919, Clarke joined the [Government Code and Cypher School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Code_and_Cypher_School), working for four years on American diplomatic traffic. In 1924 he was promoted to head of the new naval section in GC&CS, holding the position to 1941. He was succeeded as head by his colleague [Francis Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Birch_%28cryptographer%29), and then concentrated on Italian naval codes, retiring in October 1945.[^o3]

## Death and burial {#Death_and_burial}

Clarke died in 1961, and he was buried at [Church of All Saints, Selworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_All_Saints,_Selworthy).[^o3]
