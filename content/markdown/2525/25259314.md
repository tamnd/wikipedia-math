# Levi Hedge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Levi Hedge"}

**Levi Hedge** (April 19, 1766 – January 3, 1844) was an American educator and professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Hedge was born in [Hardwick, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwick,_Massachusetts). He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1792. His independent stand against [hazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazing_in_Greek_letter_organizations) while still a student was instrumental in ridding Harvard of the injustice associated with its "hat law".[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

He was a teacher at [Westford Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westford_Academy) in [Westford, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westford,_Massachusetts) from 1792 to 1794.

In 1795, he was appointed a tutor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). In 1805, he was elected a fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o5]

In 1810, Hedge became professor of logic and metaphysics. In 1816, he was elected a member of the [American Antiquarian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Antiquarian_Society).[^o6] He published *Elements of Logick* in 1816, and subsequent editions to it, and was translated into [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language).

In 1827, he was appointed the Alford professorship of natural religion, moral philosophy, and civil polity. That year, he also prepared an abridgment of [Thomas Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Brown_%28philosopher%29)'s *Mental Philosophy*.

An attack of paralysis compelled him to resign from Harvard in 1830.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1801, he married Mary Kneeland, with whom he had eight children, including [Frederic Henry Hedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Henry_Hedge), who became a clergyman, transcendentalist, scholar of German literature, and also a Harvard professor.

## Death {#Death}

He died in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) in 1844.
