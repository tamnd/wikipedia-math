# Littlewood subordination theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Littlewood subordination theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Littlewood subordination theorem**, proved by [J. E. Littlewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._Littlewood) in 1925, is a theorem in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). It states that any [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) [univalent](wikipedia://en/1534382) self-mapping of the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) in the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) that fixes 0 induces a [contractive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraction_operator) [composition operator](wikipedia://en/2702039) on various [function spaces](wikipedia://en/340630) of holomorphic functions on the disk. These spaces include the [Hardy spaces](wikipedia://en/314780), the [Bergman spaces](wikipedia://en/3205927) and [Dirichlet space](wikipedia://en/34043560).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Subordination theorem"}

## Subordination theorem {#Subordination_theorem}

Let *h* be a holomorphic univalent mapping of the unit disk *D* into itself such that *h*(0) = 0. Then the composition operator *C*<sub>*h*</sub> defined on holomorphic functions *f* on *D* by

$$C_h(f) = f\circ h$$

defines a linear operator with [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722) less than 1 on the Hardy spaces $H^p(D)$, the Bergman spaces $A^p(D)$. (1 ≤ *p* \< ∞) and the Dirichlet space $\mathcal{D}(D)$.

The norms on these spaces are defined by:

$$\|f\|_{H^p}^p = \sup_r {1\over 2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} |f(re^{i\theta})|^p \, d\theta$$

$$\|f\|_{A^p}^p = {1\over \pi} \iint_D |f(z)|^p\, dx\,dy$$

$$\|f\|_{\mathcal D}^2 = {1\over \pi} \iint_D |f^\prime(z)|^2\, dx\,dy= {1\over 4 \pi} \iint_D |\partial_x f|^2 + |\partial_y f|^2\, dx\,dy$$

:::

## Littlewood's inequalities {#Littlewood's_inequalities}

Let *f* be a holomorphic function on the unit disk *D* and let *h* be a holomorphic univalent mapping of *D* into itself with *h*(0) = 0. Then if 0 \< *r* \< 1 and 1 ≤ *p* \< ∞

$$\int_0^{2\pi} |f(h(re^{i\theta}))|^p \, d\theta \le \int_0^{2\pi} |f(re^{i\theta})|^p \, d\theta.$$

This inequality also holds for 0 \< *p* \< 1, although in this case there is no operator interpretation.

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proofs"}

## Proofs {#Proofs}

### Case *p* = 2 {#Case_p_=_2}

To prove the result for *H*<sup>2</sup> it suffices to show that for *f* a polynomial[^o8]

$$\displaystyle{\|C_h f\|^2 \le \|f\|^2,}$$

Let *U* be the unilateral shift defined by

$$\displaystyle{Uf(z)= zf(z)}.$$

This has adjoint *U*\* given by

$$U^*f(z) ={f(z)-f(0)\over z}.$$

Since *f*(0) = *a*<sub>0</sub>, this gives

$$f= a_0 + zU^*f$$

and hence

$$C_h f = a_0 + h C_hU^*f.$$

Thus

$$\|C_h f\|^2 = |a_0|^2 + \|hC_hU^*f\|^2 \le |a_0^2|+ \|C_h U^*f\|^2.$$

Since *U*\**f* has degree less than *f*, it follows by induction that

$$\|C_h U^*f\|^2 \le \|U^*f\|^2 = \|f\|^2 - |a_0|^2,$$

and hence

$$\|C_h f\|^2 \le \|f\|^2.$$

The same method of proof works for *A*<sup>2</sup> and $\mathcal D.$

### General Hardy spaces {#General_Hardy_spaces}

If *f* is in Hardy space *H*<sup>*p*</sup>, then it has a [factorization](wikipedia://en/314780#Factorization_into_inner_and_outer_functions_%28Beurling%29)[^o10]

$$f(z) = f_i(z)f_o(z)$$

with *f*<sub>*i*</sub> an [inner function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_function) and *f*<sub>*o*</sub> an [outer function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_function).

Then

$$\|C_h f\|_{H^p} \le \|(C_hf_i) (C_h f_o)\|_{H^p} \le \|C_h f_o\|_{H^p} \le \|C_h f_o^{p/2}\|_{H^2}^{2/p} \le \|f\|_{H^p}.$$

### Inequalities {#Inequalities}

Taking 0 \< *r* \< 1, Littlewood's inequalities follow by applying the Hardy space inequalities to the function

$$f_r(z)=f(rz).$$

The inequalities can also be deduced, following (Riesz 1925), using [subharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/3237359).[^o13][^o14] The inequaties in turn immediately imply the subordination theorem for general Bergman spaces.
