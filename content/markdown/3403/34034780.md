# Hans Rådström

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hans Rådström"}

**Hans Vilhem Rådström** (1919–1970) was a Swedish mathematician who worked on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), [continuous groups](wikipedia://en/152760), [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292), [set-valued analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-valued_analysis), and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924). From 1952, he was [*lektor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_rank_in_Sweden#Lektor) ([assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor)) at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University),[^o3] and from 1969, he was Professor of Applied Mathematics at [Linköping University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linköping_University).[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Hans Rådström was the son of the writer and editor Karl Johan Rådström, and the elder brother of the writer and journalist [Pär Rådström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pär_Rådström).

Rådström studied mathematics and obtained his Ph.D. under the joint supervision of [Torsten Carleman](wikipedia://en/6063370) and [Fritz Carlson](wikipedia://en/5094208). His early work pertained to the [theory of functions of a complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_functions_of_a_complex_variable), particularly, [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518). He was appointed *lektor* ([assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor)) at Stockholm University in 1952.[^o3] Later, he was associated with the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) in Stockholm.

In 1952 he became co-editor of the Scandinavian popular-mathematics journal *Nordisk Matematisk Tidskrift*.[^o6] He also edited the Swedish edition of *The Scientific American Book of Mathematical Puzzles and Diversions*, a [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) book by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435).[^o7]

## Set-valued analysis {#Set-valued_analysis}

*Image: Lars Hörmander.jpg.* [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958) *(pictured)* proved a variant of Rådström's embedding theorem using [support functions](wikipedia://en/9377661).

*Image: Per Enflo.jpg.* [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) *(pictured)* wrote his doctoral thesis under the supervision of Hans Rådström.

Rådström was interested in [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760) on the analyticity of the continuous operation of [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315). The solution of this problem by [Andrew Gleason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Gleason) used constructions of [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752),[^o9] (rather than simply [points](wikipedia://en/381798)), and inspired Rådström's research on [set-valued analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-valued_analysis).

He visited the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (IAS) in Princeton from 1948 to 1950,[^o10] where he co-organized a seminar on convexity.[^o11] Together with [Olof Hanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olof_Hanner), who, like Rådström, would earn his Ph.D. from Stockholm University in 1952, he improved [Werner Fenchel](wikipedia://en/4784468)'s version of [Carathéodory's lemma](wikipedia://en/892014).[^o12]

In the 1950s, he obtained important results on [convex sets](wikipedia://en/6292). He proved the [Rådström embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/81880778), which implies that the collection of all [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) compact convex subsets of a [normed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space) real vector-space (endowed with the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296)) can be [isometrically](wikipedia://en/254777) embedded as a [convex cone](wikipedia://en/3558266) in a normed real vector-space. Under the embedding, the nonempty compact convex sets are mapped to [points](wikipedia://en/381798) in the [range](wikipedia://en/275991) space. In Rådström's construction, this embedding is additive and positively homogeneous.[^o14] Rådström's approach used ideas from the theory of topological semi-groups.[^o13] Later, [Lars Hörmander](wikipedia://en/500958) proved [a variant of this theorem](wikipedia://en/81880778#Extensions) for [locally convex topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/518844) using the [support function](wikipedia://en/9377661) (of [convex analysis](wikipedia://en/3237201)); in Hörmander's approach, the range of the embedding was the [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989) [lattice](wikipedia://en/8099018) *L*<sub>1</sub>, and the embedding was [isotone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-preserving).[^o14][^o13][^o15]

Rådström characterized the generators of [continuous semigroups](wikipedia://en/17359213) of sets as [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) convex sets.[^o16]

## Students {#Students}

Rådström's Ph.D. students included [Per Enflo](wikipedia://en/7180897) and [Martin Ribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ribe), both of whom wrote Ph.D. theses in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). In the [uniform](wikipedia://en/32339) and [Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/51731) [categories](wikipedia://en/5869) of [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752), Enflo's results[^o18] concerned [spaces with local convexity](wikipedia://en/518844), especially [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989).[^o19][^o20]

In 1970,[^o21] Hans Rådström died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack).[^o22] Enflo supervised one of Rådström's Linköping students, Lars-Erik Andersson, from 1970–1971, helping him with his 1972 thesis,[^o22] *On connected subgroups of Banach spaces*, on [Hilbert's fifth problem](wikipedia://en/152760) for [complete, normed spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). The Swedish [functional analyst](wikipedia://en/11180) [Edgar Asplund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Asplund), then Professor of Mathematics at [Aarhus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus_University) in Denmark, assisted Ribe as supervisor of his 1972 thesis,[^o23] before dying of cancer in 1974.[^o24] Ribe's results concerned topological vector spaces without assuming local convexity;[^o19] Ribe constructed a counter-example to naive extensions of the [Hahn–Banach theorem](wikipedia://en/13860) to topological vector spaces that lack local convexity.[^o25]
