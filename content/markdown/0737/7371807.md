# Superpattern

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superpattern"}

In the mathematical study of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) and [permutation patterns](wikipedia://en/24360911), a **superpattern** or **universal permutation** is a permutation that contains all of the patterns of a given length. More specifically, a *k*-superpattern contains all possible patterns of length *k*.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions and example"}

## Definitions and example {#Definitions_and_example}

If π is a permutation of length *n*, represented as a sequence of the numbers from 1 to *n* in some order, and *s* = *s*<sub>1</sub>, *s*<sub>2</sub>, ..., *s*<sub>*k*</sub> is a subsequence of π of length *k*, then *s* corresponds to a unique *pattern*, a permutation of length *k* whose elements are in the same order as *s*. That is, for each pair *i* and *j* of indexes, the *i*-th element of the pattern for *s* should be less than the *j*-th element if and only if the *i*-th element of *s* is less than the *j*-th element. Equivalently, the pattern is [order-isomorphic](wikipedia://en/23916899) to the subsequence. For instance, if π is the permutation 25314, then it has ten subsequences of length three, forming the following patterns:

::: {#o5 .table cue=table}

| Subsequence | Pattern |
|---|---|
| 253 | 132 |
| 251 | 231 |
| 254 | 132 |
| 231 | 231 |
| 234 | 123 |
| 214 | 213 |
| 531 | 321 |
| 534 | 312 |
| 514 | 312 |
| 314 | 213 |

:::

A permutation π is called a *k*-superpattern if its patterns of length *k* include all of the length-*k* permutations. For instance, the length-3 patterns of 25314 include all six of the length-3 permutations, so 25314 is a 3-superpattern. No 3-superpattern can be shorter, because any two subsequences that form the two patterns 123 and 321 can only intersect in a single position, so five symbols are required just to cover these two patterns.

:::

## Length bounds {#Length_bounds}

(txt) introduced the problem of determining the length of the shortest possible *k*-superpattern.[^o8] He observed that there exists a superpattern of length *k*<sup>2</sup> (given by the [lexicographic ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographic_ordering) on the coordinate vectors of points in a square grid) and also observed that, for a superpattern of length *n*, it must be the case that it has at least as many subsequences as there are patterns. That is, it must be true that $\tbinom{n}{k}\ge k!$, from which it follows by [Stirling's approximation](wikipedia://en/151783) that *n* ≥ *k*<sup>2</sup>/*e*<sup>2</sup>, where *e* ≈ 2.71828 is [Euler's number](wikipedia://en/9633). This lower bound was later improved very slightly by (txt), who increased it to 1.000076*k*<sup>2</sup>/*e*<sup>2</sup>,[^o10] disproving [Arratia](wikipedia://en/39539859)'s conjecture that the *k*<sup>2</sup>/*e*<sup>2</sup> lower bound was tight.[^o8]

The upper bound of *k*<sup>2</sup> on superpattern length proven by Arratia is not tight. After intermediate improvements,[^o11] (txt) proved that there is a *k*-superpattern of length at most *k*(*k* + 1)/2 for every *k*.[^o13] This bound was later improved by (txt), who lowered it to ⌈(*k*<sup>2</sup> + 1)/2⌉.[^o15]

Eriksson et al. conjectured that the true length of the shortest *k*-superpattern is asymptotic to *k*<sup>2</sup>/2.[^o11] However, this is in contradiction with a conjecture of [Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651) on random superpatterns described below.

## Random superpatterns {#Random_superpatterns}

Researchers have also studied the length needed for a sequence generated by a random process to become a superpattern.[^o17] (Arratia 1999) observes that, because the [longest increasing subsequence](wikipedia://en/4587078) of a random permutation has length (with high probability) approximately 2√*n*, it follows that a random permutation must have length at least *k*<sup>2</sup>/4 to have high probability of being a *k*-superpattern: permutations shorter than this will likely not contain the identity pattern.[^o8] He attributes to [Alon](wikipedia://en/4939651) the conjecture that, for any ε > 0, with high probability, random permutations of length *k*<sup>2</sup>/(4 − ε) will be *k*-superpatterns.
