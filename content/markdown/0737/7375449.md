# Rough number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rough number"}

A *k*-**rough number**, as defined by Finch in 2001 and 2003, is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) whose [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) are all greater than or equal to *k*. *k*-roughness has alternately been defined as requiring all prime factors to strictly exceed *k*.[^o2]

:::

## Examples (after Finch) {#Examples_(after_Finch)}

1. Every odd positive integer is 3-rough.
1. Every positive integer that is [congruent](wikipedia://en/48167) to 1 or 5 mod 6 is 5-rough.
1. Every positive integer is 2-rough, since all its prime factors, being prime numbers, exceed 1.

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Powerrough"}

## Powerrough numbers {#Powerrough_numbers}

Like [powersmooth numbers](wikipedia://en/1035039#Powersmooth_numbers), we define "*n*-powerrough numbers" as the numbers whose [prime factorization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorization) $p_1^{r_1} \cdot p_2^{r_2} \cdot p_3^{r_3} \cdot \dots p_k^{r_k}$ has $p_i^{r_i} \ge n$ for every $1 \le i \le k$ (while the condition is $p_i^{r_i} \le n$ for *n*-powersmooth numbers), e.g. every positive integer is 2-powerrough, 3-powerrough numbers are exactly the numbers not == 2 mod 4, 4-powerrough numbers are exactly the numbers neither == 2 mod 4 nor == 3, 6 mod 9, 5-powerrough numbers are exactly the numbers neither == 2, 4, 6 mod 8 nor == 3, 6 mod 9, etc.

:::
