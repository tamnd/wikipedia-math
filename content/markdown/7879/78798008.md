# Mackey functor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mackey functor"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a **Mackey functor** is a type of [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that generalizes various constructions in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [equivariant homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/42486328). Named after [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [George Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey), these functors were first introduced by [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) mathematician [Andreas Dress](wikipedia://en/57220260) in 1971.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Classical definition {#Classical_definition}

Let $G$ be a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707). A Mackey functor $M$ for $G$ consists of:

- For each [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H \leq G$, an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) $M(H)$,
- For each pair of subgroups $H, K \leq G$ with $H \subseteq K$:
  - A [restriction homomorphism](wikipedia://en/1589032) $R^K_H: M(K) \to M(H)$,
  - A [transfer homomorphism](wikipedia://en/648166) $I^K_H: M(H) \to M(K)$.

These maps must satisfy the following axioms:

**Functoriality**: For nested subgroups $H \subseteq K \subseteq L$, $R^L_H = R^K_H \circ R^L_K$ and $I^L_H = I^L_K \circ I^K_H$.

**Conjugation**: For any $g \in G$ and $H \leq G$, there are [isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphisms) $c_g: M(H) \to M(gHg^{-1})$ compatible with restriction and transfer.

**[Double coset](wikipedia://en/982771) formula**: For subgroups $H, K \leq G$, the following identity holds:

$R^G_H I^G_K = \sum_{x \in [H\backslash G/K]} I^H_{H \cap xKx^{-1}} \circ c_x \circ R^K_{x^{-1}Hx \cap K}$.[^o2]

### Modern definition {#Modern_definition}

In modern [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a Mackey functor can be defined more elegantly using the language of [spans](wikipedia://en/10318351). Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a disjunctive [quasi-category](wikipedia://en/28662219) and $\mathcal{A}$ be an additive quasi-category. A Mackey functor is a [product-preserving functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product-preserving_functor) $M: \text{Span}(\mathcal{C}) \to \mathcal{A}$ where $\text{Span}(\mathcal{C})$ is the quasi-category of correspondences in $\mathcal{C}$.[^o8]

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

### In equivariant homotopy theory {#In_equivariant_homotopy_theory}

Mackey functors play an important role in [equivariant stable homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/42486328). For a genuine $G$-spectrum $E$, its equivariant homotopy groups form a Mackey functor given by:

$$\pi_n(E): G/H \mapsto [G/H_+ \wedge S^n, X]^G$$

where $[-,-]^G$ denotes [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms) in the equivariant stable homotopy category.[^o12]

### Cohomology with Mackey functor coefficients {#Cohomology_with_Mackey_functor_coefficients}

For a pointed G-CW complex $X$ and a Mackey functor $A$, one can define [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) with coefficients in $A$ as:

$$H^n_G(X,A) := H^n(\text{Hom}(C_\bullet(X), A))$$

where $C_\bullet(X)$ is the [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) of Mackey functors given by stable equivariant [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) of [quotient spaces](wikipedia://en/237213).[^o14]
