# Height (abelian group)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Height"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **height** of an element *g* of an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) *A* is an invariant that captures its divisibility properties: it is the largest [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *N* such that the equation *Nx* = *g* has a solution *x* ∈ *A*, or the symbol ∞ if there is no such *N*. The ***p*-height** considers only divisibility properties by the powers of a fixed [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*. The notion of height admits a refinement so that the *p*-height becomes an [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932). Height plays an important role in [Prüfer theorems](wikipedia://en/27970235) and also in **Ulm's theorem**, which describes the classification of certain infinite abelian groups in terms of their **Ulm factors** or **Ulm invariants**.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition of height"}

## Definition of height {#Definition_of_height}

Let *A* be an abelian group and *g* an element of *A*. The ***p*-height** of *g* in *A*, denoted *h*<sub>*p*</sub>(*g*), is the largest natural number *n* such that the equation *p*<sup>*n*</sup>*x* = *g* has a solution in *x* ∈ *A*, or the symbol ∞ if a solution exists for all *n*. Thus *h*<sub>*p*</sub>(*g*) = *n* if and only if *g* ∈ *p*<sup>*n*</sup>*A* and *g* ∉ *p*<sup>*n*+1</sup>*A*. This allows one to refine the notion of height.

For any ordinal *α*, there is a subgroup *p*<sup>*α*</sup>*A* of *A* which is the image of the multiplication map by *p* iterated *α* times, defined using [transfinite induction](wikipedia://en/30461):

- $$p^0A = A;$$
- $$p^{\alpha+1} A = p(p^\alpha A);$$
- $p^\beta A = \bigcap_{\alpha<\beta} p^\alpha(A)$ if *β* is a [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137).

The subgroups *p*<sup>*α*</sup>*A* form a decreasing filtration of the group *A*, and their intersection is the subgroup of the *p*-divisible elements of *A*, whose elements are assigned height ∞. The modified *p*-height *h*<sub>*p*</sub><sup>∗</sup>(*g*) = *α* if *g* ∈ *p*<sup>*α*</sup>*A*, but *g* ∉ *p*<sup>*α*+1</sup>*A*. The construction of *p*<sup>*α*</sup>*A* is [functorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functorial) in *A*; in particular, sub-quotients of the filtration are isomorphism invariants of *A*.

:::

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Ulm factor"}

## Ulm subgroups {#Ulm_subgroups}

Let *p* be a fixed prime number. The (first) **Ulm subgroup** of an abelian group *A*, denoted *U*(*A*) or *A*<sup>1</sup>, is *p*<sup>*ω*</sup>*A* = ∩<sub>*n*</sub> *p*<sup>*n*</sup>*A*, where *ω* is the [smallest infinite ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137). It consists of all elements of *A* of infinite height. The family {*U*<sup>*σ*</sup>(*A*)} of Ulm subgroups indexed by ordinals *σ* is defined by transfinite induction:

- $$U^0(A) = A;$$
- $$U^{\sigma+1}(A) = U(U^\sigma(A));$$
- $U^\tau(A) = \bigcap_{\sigma<\tau} U^\sigma(A)$ if *τ* is a [limit ordinal](wikipedia://en/373137).

Equivalently, *U*<sup>*σ*</sup>(*A*) = *p*<sup>*ωσ*</sup>*A*, where *ωσ* is the product of ordinals *ω* and *σ*.

Ulm subgroups form a decreasing filtration of *A* whose quotients *U*<sub>*σ*</sub>(*A*) = *U*<sup>*σ*</sup>(*A*)/*U*<sup>*σ*+1</sup>(*A*) are called the **Ulm factors** of *A*. This filtration stabilizes and the smallest ordinal *τ* such that *U*<sup>*τ*</sup>(*A*) = *U*<sup>*τ*+1</sup>(*A*) is the **Ulm length** of *A*. The smallest Ulm subgroup *U*<sup>*τ*</sup>(*A*), also denoted *U*<sup>∞</sup>(*A*) and *p*<sup>∞</sup>A, is the largest *p*-divisible subgroup of *A*; if *A* is a *p*-group, then *U*<sup>∞</sup>(*A*) is [divisible](wikipedia://en/472147), and as such it is a direct summand of *A*.

For every Ulm factor *U*<sub>*σ*</sub>(*A*) the *p*-heights of its elements are finite and they are unbounded for every Ulm factor except possibly the last one, namely *U*<sub>*τ*−1</sub>(*A*) when the Ulm length *τ* is a [successor ordinal](wikipedia://en/375256).

:::

## Ulm's theorem {#Ulm's_theorem}

The [second Prüfer theorem](wikipedia://en/27970235) provides a straightforward extension of the [fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_finitely_generated_abelian_groups) to countable abelian *p*-groups without elements of infinite height: each such group is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups whose orders are powers of *p*. Moreover, the [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of the set of summands of order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> is uniquely determined by the group and each sequence of at most countable cardinalities is realized. [Helmut Ulm](wikipedia://en/27973380) (1933) found an extension of this classification theory to general countable *p*-groups: their isomorphism class is determined by the isomorphism classes of the Ulm factors and the *p*-divisible part.

**Ulm's theorem**. *Let* *A* *and* *B* *be countable abelian* *p*-*groups such that for every ordinal* *σ* *their Ulm factors are isomorphic*, *U*<sub>*σ*</sub>(*A*) ≅ *U*<sub>*σ*</sub>(*B*) *and the* *p*-*divisible parts of* *A* *and* *B* *are isomorphic*, *U*<sup>∞</sup>(*A*) ≅ *U*<sup>∞</sup>(*B*). *Then* *A* *and* *B* *are isomorphic.*

There is a complement to this theorem, first stated by [Leo Zippin](wikipedia://en/31837971) (1935) and proved in Kurosh (1960), which addresses the existence of an abelian *p*-group with given Ulm factors.

*Let* *τ* *be an ordinal and* {*A*<sub>*σ*</sub>} *be a family of countable abelian* *p*-*groups indexed by the ordinals* *σ* \< *τ* *such that the* *p*-*heights of elements of each* *A*<sub>*σ*</sub> *are finite and, except possibly for the last one, are unbounded. Then there exists a reduced abelian* *p*-*group* *A* *of Ulm length* *τ* *whose Ulm factors are isomorphic to these* *p*-*groups*, *U*<sub>*σ*</sub>(*A*) ≅ *A*<sub>*σ*</sub>.

Ulm's original proof was based on an extension of the theory of [elementary divisors](wikipedia://en/4577402) to infinite [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859).

### Alternative formulation {#Alternative_formulation}

[George Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey) and [Irving Kaplansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Kaplansky) generalized Ulm's theorem to certain [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) over a [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_ring) [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633). They introduced invariants of abelian groups that lead to a direct statement of the classification of countable periodic abelian groups: given an abelian group *A*, a prime *p*, and an ordinal *α*, the corresponding ***α*th Ulm invariant** is the dimension of the quotient

*p*<sup>*α*</sup>*A*\[*p*\]/*p*<sup>*α*+1</sup>*A*\[*p*\],

where *B*\[*p*\] denotes the *p*-torsion of an abelian group *B*, i.e. the subgroup of elements of order *p*, viewed as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) with *p* elements.

*A countable periodic reduced abelian group is determined uniquely up to isomorphism by its Ulm invariants for all prime numbers*p*and countable ordinals*α*.*

Their simplified proof of Ulm's theorem served as a model for many further generalizations to other classes of abelian groups and modules.
