# Konstantinos Tzechanis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Konstantinos Tzechanis"}

**Konstantinos Tzechanis** (Κωνσταντίνος Τζεχάνης, Constantinus Tzechani, Kostë Xhehani, 1740–1800) was a [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poet) from the 18th century [Aromanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) center of [Moscopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscopole).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Tzechanis was born in [Moscopole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscopole) (Voskopojë, southeast [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania)), an 18th-century cultural and commercial metropolis of the Balkans and center of Greek culture.[^o4] His ethnicity is disputed, with various sources claiming that he is of [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians),[^o5] [Aromanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians) or [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks) origin.[^o13]

Tzechanis initially studied in his home town with [Theodore Kavalliotis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Kavalliotis) being his teacher in the [New Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Academy_%28Moscopole%29). He later attended lessons in [Modra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modra), today in [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia). At 1760, he moved together with his parents to [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), where his father became a merchant.[^o6][^o7][^o8] Tzechanis became a teacher at the Greek schools of [Temesvár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temesvár), [Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest,_Hungary) and [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun).[^o9] Later in 1768–74 he went to [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_%28Saale%29), then a city of the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), to study literature and mathematics, and in 1776 he studied in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He also studied in [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig) for three years and moved to several countries of Western Europe.[^o10] He also lived in [Wallachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia), where he composed a satyrical poem.[^o11]

In 1769 as a student he had written in Latin and Greek a treatise in geometry, the *Introduction to Geometry: new theory of squaring the circle*, 1774 (Προγύμνασμα Γεωμετρικόν, ήτοι νέα θεωρία τετραγωνισμού του κύκλου), in which he proposed a new theoretical solution to the problem of [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359).[^o16] Tzechanis also gave to the Swedish linguist [Johann Thunmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Thunmann) a copy of *Protopireia*, one of the most significant works of Kavalliotis,[^o12] he also assisted Thunmann on his works regarding the [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language) and the [Aromanian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanian_language).[^o7] During his stay in [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyden), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), Tzechanis composed two large poems in Greek, praising the local university on the one and the ancient Greek authors on the other.[^o16]

In the 1770s, he wrote in Greek a biography of [Skanderbeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanderbeg), the national hero of Albania, based on [Marin Barleti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_Barleti)'s biography.[^o16] He taught [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and mathematics in [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University). His best known poetry work is *Έπος ηρωελεγείον προς Αικατερίνην* (Heroic Epic to [Catherine II of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_II_of_Russia)), a patriotic work that aimed at the national awakening of the Greek people that lived under [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Empire) rule (1776), written in Latin and Ancient Greek and published in 1776.[^o13][^o14] He died at 1800 in Leyden.[^o16]

## Work {#Work}

Tzechani composed the following works:[^o16]

- Εγχειρίδιον αριθμητικής, (Manual of Mathematics, Halle, 1769)
- Έπος ηρωελεγείον εις Βικέντιον Ιωάννοβιτζ τον Τεμεσβαρίου επίσκοπον, (Honorary poem to bishop Vincent Jovanovic, Vienna, 1772)
- Έπος εις τον κόμητα Φραγκίσκον Κολλάριον, (Epic to count Francis Collaro, 1772)
- Έπος ηρωελεγείον εις τον Κωνσταντίνον Αλέξανδρον φιλιππίδην τον Γάιον, (Honorary poem to Konstantine Alexander, 1773)
- Έπος ηρωελεγείον εις τον κραταιότατον και αήττητον των Ρωμαίων αυτοκράτορα, (Honorary poem to the mighty and invincible Emperor of the Romans, Halle, 1773)
- Προγύμνασμα γεωμετρικόν, ήτοι νέα θεωρία τετραγωνισμού του κύκλου, (Introduction to Geometry: new theory of squaring the circle, 1774)
- Έπος ηρωελεγείον προς Αικατερίνη Β΄, (Honorary poem to Empress Catherine II, Leyden, 1776)
- Έπη δύο (Lugduni Batanorum), (Two Epics, Leyden, 1776)
- Ποίημα σαπφικόν εις τον ελλογιμώτατον και ευγενέστατον Σκαρλάτον τον Στούρλαν, (Sapphic poem to scholar and gentleman Scarlato Sturla, Vienna, 1777)
- Επίγραμμα ηρωελεγείον εις τον ιερομονάχοις... εφημέριον Άνθιμον..., (Honorary epigram to monk and priest Antimos, Vienna)
- Έπος σαπφικόν εις την γενέθλιον ημέραν του βασιλέως των Πρώσσων Φρεδερίκου Β΄ (Sapphic poem for the birthday of King of Prussia, [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II_of_Prussia))
- Δύο επιγράμματα προς τον ηγεμόνα Υψηλάντην, (Two Epigrams to lord Ipsilantis, Leipzig)

Translation from German to Greek:

- Γραμματική του Ρενίου

Translations from Ancient Greek to German:

- Κανόνας του ορθού και τιμίου βίου
- Κατήχησις του Πλάτωνος
