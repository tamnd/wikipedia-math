# Helmut Ulm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Helmut Ulm"}

*Image: Toeplitz Hagemann Vieth Ulm Köthe 1930 MFO4233.jpg.* According to the annotation of this photo by Konrad Jacobs, it depicts [Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682), E. Hagemann, D. Vieth, H. Ulm, [Gottfried Köthe](wikipedia://en/27641787) in 1930.

**Helmut Ulm** (born 21 June 1908 in [Gelsenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelsenkirchen); died 13 June 1975) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who established the classification of [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group) [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) by means of their [Ulm invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulm_invariant).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Helmut Ulm's father was an elementary school teacher in [Elberfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elberfeld). After finishing high school in [Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuppertal) in 1926, he attended the universities of [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) (1926–1927), [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) (1927) and [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) (1927–1930), where he studied mathematics and physics, attending the lectures of [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), Erich Bessel-Hagen, [Felix Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/10989), and the joint Hausdorff–[Otto Toeplitz](wikipedia://en/547682) seminar. He graduated [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) in 1930 with a thesis about countable periodic abelian groups (1933). In 1933–1935 he was an assistant in Göttingen and worked with [Wilhelm Magnus](wikipedia://en/8337666) and [Olga Taussky-Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Taussky-Todd) editing [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s *Collected Works*. His [Habilitationsschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitationsschrift) developed a generalization of the [elementary divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_divisor) theory to infinite matrices, continuing ideas of Ulm's teacher Toeplitz. It was submitted in Münster in 1936 and refereed by [Heinrich Behnke](wikipedia://en/22922678), [Gottfried Köthe](wikipedia://en/27641787), F. K. Schmidt, and [B. L. van der Waerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._L._van_der_Waerden). Ulm's promotion was delayed, apparently, due to his anti-[Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) views.

From 1935 until his retirement in 1974 Ulm worked at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster), as an assistant, docent, and eventually professor (1968). Besides his three important papers on the classification of infinite abelian groups, Ulm published only a few notes in the proceedings of the Münster mathematical seminar, one of which dealt with solution of systems of linear equations by a computer. He suffered from poor health. During the [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Ulm worked as a [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) at [Pers Z S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pers_Z_S) starting in August 1941 on a part-time basis from Thursday through to Saturday and on Monday through Wednesday at Münster.[^o3] After the war he mostly taught courses in applied mathematics and supervised several Ph.D. theses; those of G. Tillmann (1952) and G. Roth (1961) were close to his old work on infinite-dimensional linear algebra and infinite groups.
