# Prüfer theorems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prüfer theorems"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), two **Prüfer theorems**, named after [Heinz Prüfer](wikipedia://en/1655401), describe the structure of certain [infinite](wikipedia://en/4587030) [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974). They have been generalized by L. Ya. Kulikov.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *A* be an abelian group. If *A* is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/58527) then by the [fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_finitely_generated_abelian_groups), *A* is decomposable into a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011), which leads to the classification of finitely generated abelian groups [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397). The structure of general infinite abelian groups can be considerably more complicated and the conclusion needs not to hold, but Prüfer [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that it remains true for [periodic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group) in two special cases.

The **first Prüfer theorem** states that an abelian group of bounded [exponent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponent_%28group_theory%29) is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups. The **second Prüfer theorem** states that a [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) abelian *p*-group whose non-trivial elements have finite [*p*-height](wikipedia://en/27972322) is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups. Examples show that the assumption that the group be countable cannot be removed.

The two Prüfer theorems follow from a general criterion of decomposability of an abelian group into a direct sum of cyclic subgroups due to L. Ya. Kulikov:

> An abelian [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) *A* is isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic groups if and only if it is a [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of a sequence {*A*<sub>*i*</sub>} of subgroups with the property that the heights of all elements of *A*<sub>*i*</sub> are bounded by a constant (possibly depending on *i*).
