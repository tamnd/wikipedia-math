# Basic subgroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Basic subgroup"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **basic subgroup** is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) which is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of [cyclic subgroups](wikipedia://en/52327) and satisfies further technical conditions. This notion was introduced by L. Ya. Kulikov (for [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664)) and by László Fuchs (in general) in an attempt to formulate classification theory of infinite abelian groups that goes beyond the [Prüfer theorems](wikipedia://en/27970235). It helps to reduce the classification problem to classification of possible [extensions](wikipedia://en/345807) between two well understood classes of abelian groups: direct sums of cyclic groups and [divisible groups](wikipedia://en/472147).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition and properties"}

## Definition and properties {#Definition_and_properties}

A [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011), $B$, of an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974), $A$, is called ***p*-basic**, for a fixed [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), $p$, if the following conditions hold:

1. $B$ is a direct sum of [cyclic groups](wikipedia://en/52327) of order $p^{n}$ and infinite cyclic groups;
1. $B$ is a *p*-[pure subgroup](wikipedia://en/5739585) of $A$;
1. The quotient group, $A/B$, is a *p*-[divisible group](wikipedia://en/472147).

Conditions 1–3 imply that the subgroup, $B$, is [Hausdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_topological_space) in the *p*-adic topology of $B$, which moreover coincides with the topology [induced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_topology) from $A$, and that $B$ is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_subset) in $A$. Picking a generator in each cyclic direct summand of $B$ creates a p*\-basis* of *B*, which is analogous to a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) or a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296).

Every abelian group, $A$, contains *p*-basic subgroups for each $p$, and any 2 *p*-basic subgroups of $A$ are isomorphic. Abelian groups that contain a unique *p*-basic subgroup have been completely characterized. For the case of [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) they are either [divisible](wikipedia://en/472147) or *bounded*; i.e., have bounded exponent. In general, the isomorphism class of the quotient, $A/B$ by a basic subgroup, $B$, may depend on $B$.
