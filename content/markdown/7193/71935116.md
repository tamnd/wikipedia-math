# Louis Paul Émile Richard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Louis Paul Émile Richard"}

**Louis Paul Émile Richard** (31 March 1795 – 11 March 1849) was a French mathematician and teacher at the [Collège Louis-le-Grand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Louis-le-Grand). Although he published nothing himself, he was known for his inspiring teaching and was one of the teachers of [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815).

:::

Richard was born in [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) where his father was an artillery colonel. A physical injury in childhood prevented him from following a military career. This made him become a mathematics teacher for military aspirants, first at the [lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée) at [Douai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douai), then at the Collège de Pontivy (from 1815), Collège Saint-Louis (from 1820) and finally the Collège Louis-le-Grand (from 1822). Students from the college typically went into the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) and [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_%28Paris%29). His students included Evariste Galois, [Urbain Le Verrier](wikipedia://en/176356), [Joseph Serret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Serret), and [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422).[^o2][^o3]
