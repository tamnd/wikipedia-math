# Stably finite ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stably finite ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) *R* is said to be **stably finite** (or **weakly finite**) if, for all [square matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_matrices) *A* and *B* of the same size with entries in *R*, *AB* = 1 implies *BA* = 1.[^o2] This is a stronger property for a ring than having the [invariant basis number](wikipedia://en/3327998) (IBN) property. Namely, any [nontrivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_ring)[^o3] stably finite ring has IBN. [Commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346), [Noetherian rings](wikipedia://en/56129) and [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) are stably finite. [Subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145) of stably finite rings and [matrix rings](wikipedia://en/487541) over stably finite rings are stably finite. A ring satisfying [Klein's nilpotence condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_nilpotence_condition) is stably finite.[^o4]

:::
