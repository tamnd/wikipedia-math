In [six-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-dimensional_space) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **six-dimensional polytope** or **6-polytope** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471), bounded by [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A 6-polytope is a closed six-dimensional figure with [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) (3-faces), 4-faces, and 5-faces. A vertex is a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) where six or more edges meet. An edge is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) where four or more faces meet, and a face is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) where three or more cells meet. A cell is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). A 4-face is a [polychoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychoron), and a 5-face is a [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670). Furthermore, the following requirements must be met:

- Each 4-face must join exactly two 5-faces (facets).
- Adjacent facets are not in the same five-dimensional [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862).
- The figure is not a compound of other figures which meet the requirements.

:::

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The topology of any given 6-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o8]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, and is zero for all 6-polytopes, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o8]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o8]

## Classification {#Classification}

6-polytopes may be classified by properties like "[convexity](wikipedia://en/6292)" and "[symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741)".

- A 6-polytope is *[convex](wikipedia://en/1817885)* if its boundary (including its 5-faces, 4-faces, cells, faces and edges) does not intersect itself and the line segment joining any two points of the 6-polytope is contained in the 6-polytope or its interior; otherwise, it is *non-convex*. Self-intersecting 6-polytope are also known as [*star 6-polytopes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_polytope), from analogy with the star-like shapes of the non-convex [Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot_polyhedra).
- A **regular 6-polytope** has all identical regular 5-polytope facets. All regular 6-polytope are convex.
- A **[semi-regular](wikipedia://en/11684875) 6-polytope** contains two or more types of [regular 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/18801624) facets. There is only one such figure, called [2<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19232473).
- A **uniform 6-polytope** has a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) under which all vertices are equivalent, and its facets are [uniform 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338). The faces of a uniform polytope must be [regular](wikipedia://en/333306).
- A **prismatic 6-polytope** is constructed by the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two lower-dimensional polytopes. A prismatic 6-polytope is uniform if its factors are uniform. The [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) is prismatic (product of a [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) and a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)), but is considered separately because it has symmetries other than those inherited from its factors.
- A *5-space [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671)* is the division of five-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) into a regular grid of 5-polytope facets. Strictly speaking, tessellations are not 6-polytopes as they do not bound a "6D" volume, but we include them here for the sake of completeness because they are similar in many ways to 6-polytope. A *uniform 5-space tessellation* is one whose vertices are related by a [space group](wikipedia://en/463721) and whose facets are [uniform 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338).

## Regular 6-polytopes {#Regular_6-polytopes}

Regular 6-polytopes can be generated from [Coxeter groups](wikipedia://en/297004) represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s,t} with **t** {p,q,r,s} 5-polytope [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29).

There are only three such [convex regular 6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_5):

- {3,3,3,3,3} - [6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex)
- {4,3,3,3,3} - [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462)
- {3,3,3,3,4} - [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518)

There are no nonconvex regular polytopes of 5 or more dimensions.
