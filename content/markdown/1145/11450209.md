# Antiparallel lines

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Antiparallel lines"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), two [lines](wikipedia://en/946975) $l_1$ and $l_2$ are **antiparallel** with respect to a given line $m$ if they each make [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) with $m$ in opposite [senses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_of_rotation). More generally, lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ are *antiparallel* with respect to another pair of lines $m_1$ and $m_2$ if they are antiparallel with respect to the [angle bisector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector) of $m_1$ and $m_2.$

:::

In any [cyclic quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/194131), any two opposite sides are antiparallel with respect to the other two sides.

|---|---|
| *Image: anti1.svg.* Given two lines $m_1$ and $m_2$, lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ are antiparallel with respect to $m_1$ and $m_2$ if $\angle 1 = \angle 2$. | *Image: anti5.svg.* In any quadrilateral inscribed in a circle, any two opposite sides are antiparallel with respect to the other two sides. |

## Relations {#Relations}

1. The line joining the feet to two [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is antiparallel to the third side. (any [cevians](wikipedia://en/6135790) which 'see' the third side with the same angle create antiparallel lines)
1. The tangent to a triangle's [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432) at a vertex is antiparallel to the opposite side.
1. The radius of the circumcircle at a vertex is [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to all lines antiparallel to the opposite sides.

## Conic sections {#Conic_sections}

In an [oblique cone](wikipedia://en/782427#Elliptic_cone), there are exactly two families of parallel planes whose sections with the cone are circles. One of these families is parallel to the fixed generating circle and the other is called by Apollonius the **subcontrary sections**.[^o4]

| *Image: Apollonius-5 2.png.* A cone with two directions of circular sections | *Image: Apollonius-5 1.png.* Side view of a cone with the two antiparallel directions of circular sections. | *Image: Apollonius 6-6 13.png.* Triangles ABC and ADB are similar |
|---|---|---|

If one looks at the triangles formed by the diameters of the circular sections (both families) and the vertex of the cone (triangles $ABC$ and $ADB$), they are all similar. That is, if $CB$ and $BD$ are **antiparallel** with respect to lines $AB$ and $AC$, then all sections of the cone parallel to either one of these circles will be circles. This is Book 1, Proposition 5 in [Apollonius](wikipedia://en/257242#Book_I).
