# Hughes plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hughes plane"}

In mathematics, a **Hughes plane** is one of the [non-Desarguesian projective planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Desarguesian_projective_plane) found by (Hughes 1957). There are examples of order *p*<sup>2*n*</sup> for every odd prime *p* and every positive integer *n*.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The construction of a Hughes plane is based on a [nearfield](wikipedia://en/4033255) **N** of order *p*<sup>*2n*</sup> for *p* an odd prime whose kernel **K** has order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> and coincides with the center of **N**.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

A Hughes plane **H**:[^o7]

1. is a non-Desarguesian projective plane of odd square prime power order of Lenz-Barlotti type I.1,
1. has a Desarguesian Baer subplane **H**<sub>0</sub>,
1. is a self-dual plane in which every orthogonal polarity of **H**<sub>0</sub> can be extended to a polarity of **H**,
1. every central collineation of **H**<sub>0</sub> extends to a central collineation of **H**, and
1. the full collineation group of **H** has two point orbits (one of which is **H**<sub>0</sub>), two line orbits, and four flag orbits.

:::

## The smallest Hughes Plane (order 9) {#The_smallest_Hughes_Plane_(order_9)}

The Hughes plane of order 9 was actually found earlier by Veblen and Wedderburn in 1907.[^o9] A construction of this plane can be found in (Room Kirkpatrick 1971) where it is called the plane Ψ.
