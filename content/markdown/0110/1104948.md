# Grigory Margulis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grigory Margulis"}

**Grigory Aleksandrovich Margulis** (Григо́рий Алекса́ндрович Маргу́лис, first name often given as **Gregory**, **Grigori** or **Gregori**; born February 24, 1946) is a [Russian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-American)[^o3] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work on [lattices](wikipedia://en/15782871) in [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945), and the introduction of methods from [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) into [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558). He was awarded a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978, a [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 2005, and an [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2020 (with [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152)), becoming the fifth mathematician to receive the three prizes.[^o4] In 1991, he joined the faculty of [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he is currently the [Erastus L. De Forest](wikipedia://en/54267896) Professor of Mathematics.[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Margulis was born to a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians) family of [Lithuanian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Jews) descent in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). At age 16 in 1962 he won the silver medal at the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208). He received his PhD in 1970 from the [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University), starting research in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) under the supervision of [Yakov Sinai](wikipedia://en/1751626). Early work with [David Kazhdan](wikipedia://en/3052833) produced the [Kazhdan–Margulis theorem](wikipedia://en/51147390), a basic result on [discrete groups](wikipedia://en/468536). His [superrigidity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superrigidity_theorem) from 1975 clarified an area of classical conjectures about the characterisation of [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741) amongst lattices in [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups).

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978, but was not permitted to travel to [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) to accept it in person, allegedly due to [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) against Jewish mathematicians in the Soviet Union.[^o7] His position improved, and in 1979 he visited [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn), and was later able to travel freely, though he still worked in the Institute of Problems of Information Transmission, a research institute rather than a university. In 1991, Margulis accepted a professorial position at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).

Margulis was elected a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2001.[^o8] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]

In 2005, Margulis received the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) for his contributions to theory of lattices and applications to ergodic theory, [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory).

In 2020, Margulis received the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) jointly with [Hillel Furstenberg](wikipedia://en/6450152) "For pioneering the use of methods from probability and dynamics in group theory, number theory and combinatorics."[^o10]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Margulis's early work dealt with [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724) and the questions of rigidity and arithmeticity of [lattices](wikipedia://en/15782871) in [semisimple algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_algebraic_group) of higher rank over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375). It had been known since the 1950s ([Borel](wikipedia://en/365672), [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190)) that a certain simple-minded way of constructing subgroups of semisimple Lie groups produces examples of lattices, called *arithmetic lattices*. It is analogous to considering the subgroup *SL*(*n*,**Z**) of the [real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) *SL*(*n*,**R**) that consists of matrices with *integer* entries. Margulis proved that under suitable assumptions on *G* (no compact factors and [split rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_rank) greater or equal than two), *any* (irreducible) lattice *Γ* in it is arithmetic, i.e. can be obtained in this way. Thus *Γ* is [commensurable](wikipedia://en/29343178) with the subgroup *G*(**Z**) of *G*, i.e. they agree on subgroups of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in both. Unlike general lattices, which are defined by their properties, arithmetic lattices are defined by a construction. Therefore, these results of Margulis pave a way for classification of lattices. Arithmeticity turned out to be closely related to another remarkable property of lattices discovered by Margulis. *Superrigidity* for a lattice *Γ* in *G* roughly means that any [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12695) of *Γ* into the group of real invertible *n* × *n* matrices extends to the whole *G*. The name derives from the following variant:

If *G* and *G'* are semisimple algebraic groups over a local field without compact factors and whose split rank is at least two and *Γ* and *Γ*<sup>$'$</sup> are irreducible lattices in them, then any homomorphism *f*: *Γ* → *Γ*<sup>$'$</sup> between the lattices agrees on a finite index subgroup of *Γ* with a homomorphism between the algebraic groups themselves.

(The case when *f* is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) is known as the [strong rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_rigidity).) While certain rigidity phenomena had already been known, the approach of Margulis was at the same time novel, powerful, and very elegant.

Margulis solved the [Banach](wikipedia://en/29646)–[Ruziewicz problem](wikipedia://en/3100245) that asks whether the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) is the only normalized rotationally invariant [finitely additive measure](wikipedia://en/1339598) on the [*n*-dimensional sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). The affirmative solution for *n* ≥ 4, which was also independently and almost simultaneously obtained by [Dennis Sullivan](wikipedia://en/7349608), follows from a construction of a certain dense subgroup of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) that has property (T).

Margulis gave the first construction of [expander graphs](wikipedia://en/9313), which was later generalized in the theory of [Ramanujan graphs](wikipedia://en/849412).

In 1986, Margulis gave a complete resolution of the [Oppenheim conjecture](wikipedia://en/5007342) on [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) and diophantine approximation. This was a question that had been open for half a century, on which considerable progress had been made by the [Hardy–Littlewood circle method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood_circle_method); but to reduce the number of variables to the point of getting the best-possible results, the more structural methods from [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) proved decisive. He has formulated a further program of research in the same direction, that includes the [Littlewood conjecture](wikipedia://en/627501).
