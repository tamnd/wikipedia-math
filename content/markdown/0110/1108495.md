# Malcolm J. Williamson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Malcolm J. Williamson"}

**Malcolm John Williamson** (2 November 1950 – 15 September 2015) was a British mathematician and cryptographer. In 1974 he developed what is now known as [Diffie–Hellman key exchange](wikipedia://en/7903).[^o6] He was then working at [GCHQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Communications_Headquarters) and was therefore unable to publicise his research as his work was classified. [Martin Hellman](wikipedia://en/422387), who independently developed the key exchange at the same time, received credit for the discovery until Williamson's research was declassified by the British government in 1997.[^o6]

:::

Williamson studied at [Manchester Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Grammar_School), winning first prize in the 1968 [British Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/3264277).[^o7] He also won a Silver prize[^o8] at the 1967 [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in Cetinje, Yugoslavia and a Gold prize[^o9] at the 1968 International Mathematical Olympiad in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow). He read mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), graduating in 1971. After a year at [Liverpool University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_University), he joined [GCHQ](wikipedia://en/12884), and worked there until 1982.

From 1985 to 1989 Williamson worked at Nicolet Instruments in Madison, Wisconsin where he was the primary author on two digital hearing aid patents.[^o11][^o12] After that, he moved to the [IDA Center for Communications Research, La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Defense_Analyses#Center_for_Communications_and_Computing),[^o13] where he worked for the rest of his career.

His contributions to the invention of [public-key cryptography](wikipedia://en/24222), together with [Clifford Cocks](wikipedia://en/578040) and [James Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_H._Ellis), have been recognized by the IEEE Milestone Award #104[^o14] in 2010 and by induction into the [Cryptologic Hall of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Hall_of_Honor) in 2021.
