# Jones polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jones polynomial"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **Jones polynomial** is a [knot polynomial](wikipedia://en/313735) discovered by [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628) in 1984.[^o2][^o3] Specifically, it is an [invariant](wikipedia://en/166898) of an oriented [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) or [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) which assigns to each oriented knot or link a [Laurent polynomial](wikipedia://en/61351) in the variable $t^{1/2}$ with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203).[^o4]

:::

## Definition by the bracket {#Definition_by_the_bracket}

*Image: Reidemeister move 1.png.* Type I Reidemeister move

Suppose we have an [oriented link](wikipedia://en/1161784) $L$, given as a [knot diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_diagram). We will define the Jones polynomial $V(L)$ by using [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman)'s [bracket polynomial](wikipedia://en/3774360), which we denote by $\langle~\rangle$. Here the bracket polynomial is a [Laurent polynomial](wikipedia://en/61351) in the variable $A$ with integer coefficients.

First, we define the auxiliary polynomial (also known as the normalized bracket polynomial)

$$X(L) = (-A^3)^{-w(L)}\langle L \rangle,$$

where $w(L)$ denotes the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of $L$ in its given diagram. The writhe of a diagram is the number of positive crossings ($L_{+}$ in the figure below) minus the number of negative crossings ($L_{-}$). The writhe is not a knot invariant.

$X(L)$ is a knot invariant since it is invariant under changes of the diagram of $L$ by the three [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374). Invariance under type II and III Reidemeister moves follows from invariance of the bracket under those moves. The bracket polynomial is known to change by a factor of $-A^{\pm 3}$ under a type I Reidemeister move. The definition of the $X$ polynomial given above is designed to nullify this change, since the writhe changes appropriately by $+1$ or $-1$ under type I moves.

Now make the substitution $A = t^{-1/4}$ in $X(L)$ to get the Jones polynomial $V(L)$. This results in a Laurent polynomial with integer coefficients in the variable $t^{1/2}$.

### Jones polynomial for tangles {#Jones_polynomial_for_tangles}

This construction of the Jones polynomial for [tangles](wikipedia://en/30858815) is a simple generalization of the [Kauffman bracket](wikipedia://en/3774360) of a link. The construction was developed by [Vladimir Turaev](wikipedia://en/56815253) and published in 1990.[^o7]

Let $k$ be a non-negative integer and $S_k$ denote the set of all isotopic types of tangle diagrams, with $2k$ ends, having no crossing points and no closed components (smoothings). Turaev's construction makes use of the previous construction for the Kauffman bracket and associates to each $2k$-end oriented tangle an element of the free $\mathrm{R}$-module $\mathrm{R}[S_k]$, where $\mathrm{R}$ is the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [Laurent polynomials](wikipedia://en/61351) with integer coefficients in the variable $t^{1/2}$.

## Definition by braid representation {#Definition_by_braid_representation}

Jones' original formulation of his polynomial came from his study of operator algebras. In Jones' approach, it resulted from a kind of "trace" of a particular braid representation into an algebra which originally arose while studying certain models, e.g. the [Potts model](wikipedia://en/1162226), in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).

Let a link *L* be given. A [theorem of Alexander](wikipedia://en/45513201) states that it is the trace closure of a braid, say with *n* strands. Now define a representation $\rho$ of the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) on *n* strands, *B<sub>n</sub>*, into the [Temperley–Lieb algebra](wikipedia://en/2802848) $\operatorname{TL}_n$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z} [A, A^{-1}]$ and $\delta = -A^2 - A^{-2}$. The standard braid generator $\sigma_i$ is sent to $A\cdot e_i + A^{-1}\cdot 1$, where $1, e_1, \dots, e_{n-1}$ are the standard generators of the Temperley–Lieb algebra. It can be checked easily that this defines a representation.

Take the braid word $\sigma$ obtained previously from $L$ and compute $\delta^{n-1} \operatorname{tr} \rho(\sigma)$ where $\operatorname{tr}$ is the [Markov trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_trace). This gives $\langle L \rangle$, where $\langle$ $\rangle$ is the bracket polynomial. This can be seen by considering, as [Louis Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Kauffman) did, the Temperley–Lieb algebra as a particular diagram algebra.

An advantage of this approach is that one can pick similar representations into other algebras, such as the *R*-matrix representations, leading to "generalized Jones invariants".

## Properties {#Properties}

The Jones polynomial is characterized by taking the value 1 on any diagram of the unknot and satisfies the following [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902):

$$(t^{1/2} - t^{-1/2})V(L_0) = t^{-1}V(L_{+}) - tV(L_{-}) \,$$

where $L_{+}$, $L_{-}$, and $L_{0}$ are three oriented link diagrams that are identical except in one small region where they differ by the crossing changes or smoothing shown in the figure below:

*Image: Skein (HOMFLY).svg.*

The definition of the Jones polynomial by the bracket makes it simple to show that for a knot $K$, the Jones polynomial of its mirror image is given by substitution of $t^{-1}$ for $t$ in $V(K)$. Thus, an [amphicheiral knot](wikipedia://en/4333700), a knot equivalent to its mirror image, has [palindromic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromic) entries in its Jones polynomial. See the article on [skein relation](wikipedia://en/314902) for an example of a computation using these relations.

Another remarkable property of this invariant states that the Jones polynomial of an alternating link is an [alternating polynomial](wikipedia://en/20717382). This property was proved by [Morwen Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411)[^o11] in 1987. Another proof of this last property is due to [Hernando Burgos-Soto](wikipedia://en/48583348), who also gave an extension of the property to tangles.[^o12]

The Jones polynomial is not a complete invariant. There exist an infinite number of non-equivalent knots that have the same Jones polynomial. An example of two distinct knots having the same Jones polynomial can be found in the book by Murasugi.[^o13]

:::

## Colored Jones polynomial {#Colored_Jones_polynomial}

For a positive integer $N$, the $N$-colored Jones polynomial $V_N(L,t)$ is a generalisation of the Jones polynomial. It is the [Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant](wikipedia://en/60958267) associated with the $(N+1)$-irreducible representation of the [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678) $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. In this scheme, the Jones polynomial is the 1-colored Jones polynomial, the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant associated to the standard representation (irreducible and two-dimensional) of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$. One thinks of the strands of a link as being "colored" by a representation, hence the name.

More generally, given a link $L$ of $k$ components and representations $V_1,\ldots,V_k$ of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, the $(V_1,\ldots,V_k)$-colored Jones polynomial $V_{V_1,\ldots,V_k}(L,t)$ is the [Reshetikhin–Turaev invariant](wikipedia://en/60958267) associated to $V_1,\ldots,V_k$ (here we assume the components are ordered). Given two representations $V$ and $W$, colored Jones polynomials satisfy the following two properties:[^o15]

- $$V_{V\oplus W}(L,t)=V_V(L,t)+V_W(L,t),$$
- $V_{V\otimes W}(L,t) = V_{V,W}(L^2,t)$, where $L^2$ denotes the [2-cabling](wikipedia://en/9100897) of $L$.

These properties are deduced from the fact that colored Jones polynomials are Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants.

Let $K$ be a knot. Recall that by viewing a diagram of $K$ as an element of the Temperley-Lieb algebra thanks to the Kauffman bracket, one recovers the Jones polynomial of $K$. Similarly, the $N$-colored Jones polynomial of $K$ can be given a combinatorial description using the [Jones-Wenzl idempotents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jones-Wenzl_idempotents), as follows:

- consider the $N$-cabling $K^N$ of $K$;
- view it as an element of the Temperley-Lieb algebra;
- insert the Jones-Wenzl idempotents on some $N$ parallel strands.

The resulting element of $\mathbb{Q}(t)$ is the $N$-colored Jones polynomial. See appendix H of [^o16] for further details.

## Relationship to other theories {#Relationship_to_other_theories}

### Link with Chern–Simons theory {#Link_with_Chern–Simons_theory}

As first shown by [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894),[^o19] the Jones polynomial of a given knot $\gamma$ can be obtained by considering [Chern–Simons theory](wikipedia://en/391908) on the three-sphere with [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and computing the [vacuum expectation value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_expectation_value) of a [Wilson loop](wikipedia://en/616448) $W_F(\gamma)$, associated to $\gamma$ and the [fundamental representation](wikipedia://en/649743) $F$ of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$.

### Link with quantum knot invariants {#Link_with_quantum_knot_invariants}

By substituting $e^h$ for the variable $t$ of the Jones polynomial and expanding it as the series of h each of the coefficients turn to be the [Vassiliev invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassiliev_invariant) of the knot $K$. In order to unify the Vassiliev invariants (or, finite type invariants), [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509) constructed the [Kontsevich integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontsevich_integral). The value of the Kontsevich integral, which is the infinite sum of 1, 3-valued [chord diagrams](wikipedia://en/69127586), named the Jacobi chord diagrams, reproduces the Jones polynomial along with the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ weight system studied by [Dror Bar-Natan](wikipedia://en/10363723).

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Link with the volume conjecture"}

### Link with the volume conjecture {#Link_with_the_volume_conjecture}

By numerical examinations on some hyperbolic knots, [Rinat Kashaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinat_Kashaev) discovered that substituting the [*n*-th root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_n-th_root_of_unity) into the parameter of the colored Jones polynomial corresponding to the *n*-dimensional representation, and limiting it as *n* grows to infinity, the limit value would give the [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628) of the [knot complement](wikipedia://en/1350120). (See [Volume conjecture](wikipedia://en/22173048).)

:::

### Link with Khovanov homology {#Link_with_Khovanov_homology}

In 2000 [Mikhail Khovanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khovanov) constructed a certain chain complex for knots and links and showed that the homology induced from it is a knot invariant (see [Khovanov homology](wikipedia://en/2639906)). The Jones polynomial is described as the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) for this homology.

## Detection of the unknot {#Detection_of_the_unknot}

*Image: JonesLink.png.* The simplest link with the same Jones polynomial as the unlink. The black and red components are a trefoil and figure-eight knot respectively.

It is an [open question](wikipedia://en/1333335) whether there is a nontrivial knot with Jones polynomial equal to that of the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075). It is known that there are nontrivial *links* with Jones polynomial equal to that of the corresponding [unlinks](wikipedia://en/6718898) by the work of [Morwen Thistlethwaite](wikipedia://en/16705411).[^o25] The simplest such example has 15 essential crossings and consists of a [trefoil knot](wikipedia://en/404773) linked to a [figure-eight knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-eight_knot). Every prime knot with up to 24 crossings, of which there are over three hundred million,[^o26] is known to have a non-trivial Jones polynomial. This was determined by computing the Jones polynomial of several trillion knot diagrams, and verifying those diagrams with trivial Jones polynomials corresponded to trivial knots.[^o27] It was shown by Kronheimer and Mrowka that there is no nontrivial knot with Khovanov homology equal to that of the unknot.[^o28] While no nontrivial knot is known to have the same Jones polynomial as the unknot, two or more knots may have the same Jones polynomial and require other invariants to distinguish them. Examples include the [Conway knot](wikipedia://en/3727038) and the [Kinoshita-Terasaka knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinoshita-Terasaka_knot), with 11 crossings.[^o29]
