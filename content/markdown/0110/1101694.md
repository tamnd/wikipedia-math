# Isometry group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Isometry group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **isometry group** of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) (that is, bijective, [distance-preserving maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance-preserving_map)) from the metric space onto itself, with the [function composition](wikipedia://en/195947) as [group](wikipedia://en/19447) operation.[^o2] Its [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) is the [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069).[^o3] The elements of the isometry group are sometimes called [motions](wikipedia://en/36132944) of the space.

:::

Every isometry group of a metric space is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of isometries. It represents in most cases a possible set of [symmetries](wikipedia://en/53741) of objects/figures in the space, or functions defined on the space. See [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186).

A discrete isometry group is an isometry group such that for every point of the space the set of images of the point under the isometries is a [discrete set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_set).

In [pseudo-Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/10962546) the metric is replaced with an [isotropic quadratic form](wikipedia://en/10917170); transformations preserving this form are sometimes called "isometries", and the collection of them is then said to form an isometry group of the pseudo-Euclidean space.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The isometry group of the [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) consisting of the points of a [scalene triangle](wikipedia://en/30654#Types_of_triangle) is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059). A similar space for an [isosceles triangle](wikipedia://en/315428) is the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) two, C<sub>2</sub>. A similar space for an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) is D<sub>3</sub>, the [dihedral group of order 6](wikipedia://en/2375824).
- The isometry group of a two-dimensional [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) O(3).[^o6]
- The isometry group of the *n*-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is the [Euclidean group](wikipedia://en/652164) E(*n*).[^o7]
- The isometry group of the [Poincaré disc model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_disc_model) of the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane) is the projective special unitary group [PSU(1,1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_unitary_group).
- The isometry group of the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942) of the hyperbolic plane is [PSL(2,R)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSL%282,R%29).
- The isometry group of [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) is the [Poincaré group](wikipedia://en/230487).[^o8]
- [Riemannian symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_symmetric_space) are important cases where the isometry group is a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945).
