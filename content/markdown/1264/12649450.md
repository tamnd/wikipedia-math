# Peripheral cycle

*Image: 6n-graf-clique.svg.* In this graph, the red triangle formed by vertices 1, 2, and 5 is a peripheral cycle: the four remaining edges form a single bridge. However, pentagon 1–2–3–4–5 is not peripheral, as the two remaining edges form two separate bridges.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peripheral cycle"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **peripheral cycle** (or **peripheral circuit**) in an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is, intuitively, a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) that does not separate any part of the graph from any other part. Peripheral cycles (or, as they were initially called, **peripheral polygons**, because [Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte) called cycles "polygons") were first studied by (Tutte 1963), and play important roles in the characterization of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) and in generating the [cycle spaces](wikipedia://en/262243) of nonplanar graphs.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A peripheral cycle $C$ in a graph $G$ can be defined formally in one of several equivalent ways:

- $C$ is peripheral if it is a [simple cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_cycle) in a [connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_graph) with the property that, for every two edges $e_1$ and $e_2$ in $G\setminus C$, there exists a path in $G$ that starts with $e_1$, ends with $e_2$, and has no interior vertices belonging to $C$.[^o6]
- If $C$ is any subgraph of $G$, a *bridge*[^o7] of $C$ is a minimal subgraph $B$ of $G$ that is edge-disjoint from $C$ and that has the property that all of its points of attachment (vertices adjacent to edges in both $B$ and $G\setminus B$) belong to $C$.[^o8] A simple cycle $C$ is peripheral if it has exactly one bridge.[^o9]
- In a connected graph that is not a [theta graph](wikipedia://en/325802#theta), peripheral cycles cannot have chords, because any chord would be a bridge, separated from the rest of the graph. In this case, $C$ is peripheral if it is an [induced cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_cycle) with the property that the subgraph $G\setminus C$ formed by deleting the edges and vertices of $C$ is connected.[^o10]

The equivalence of these definitions is not hard to see: a connected subgraph of $G\setminus C$ (together with the edges linking it to $C$), or a chord of a cycle that causes it to fail to be induced, must in either case be a bridge, and must also be an [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of the [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) on edges in which two edges are related if they are the ends of a path with no interior vertices in $C$.[^o11]

## Properties {#Properties}

Peripheral cycles appear in the theory of [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270), that is, [3-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314). For every planar graph $G$, and every planar embedding of $G$, the faces of the embedding that are induced cycles must be peripheral cycles. In a polyhedral graph, all faces are peripheral cycles, and every peripheral cycle is a face.[^o14] It follows from this fact that (up to combinatorial equivalence, the choice of the outer face, and the orientation of the plane) every polyhedral graph has a unique planar embedding.[^o15]

In planar graphs, the [cycle space](wikipedia://en/262243) is generated by the faces, but in non-planar graphs peripheral cycles play a similar role: for every 3-vertex-connected finite graph, the cycle space is generated by the peripheral cycles.[^o16] The result can also be extended to locally finite but infinite graphs.[^o17] In particular, it follows that 3-connected graphs are guaranteed to contain peripheral cycles. There exist 2-connected graphs that do not contain peripheral cycles (an example is the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{2,4}$, for which every cycle has two bridges) but if a 2-connected graph has minimum degree three then it contains at least one peripheral cycle.[^o18]

Peripheral cycles in 3-connected graphs can be computed in linear time and have been used for designing planarity tests.[^o19] They were also extended to the more general notion of non-separating ear decompositions. In some algorithms for testing planarity of graphs, it is useful to find a cycle that is not peripheral, in order to partition the problem into smaller subproblems. In a biconnected graph of [circuit rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_rank) less than three (such as a [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) or [theta graph](wikipedia://en/325802#Walks)) every cycle is peripheral, but every biconnected graph with circuit rank three or more has a non-peripheral cycle, which may be found in linear time.[^o20]

Generalizing [chordal graphs](wikipedia://en/744165), (Seymour Weaver 1984) define a [strangulated graph](wikipedia://en/36971277) to be a graph in which every peripheral cycle is a triangle. They characterize these graphs as being the [clique-sums](wikipedia://en/17598460) of chordal graphs and [maximal planar graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_planar_graph).[^o22]

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

Peripheral cycles have also been called non-separating cycles,[^o6] but this term is ambiguous, as it has also been used for two related but distinct concepts: simple cycles the removal of which would disconnect the remaining graph,[^o24] and cycles of a [topologically embedded graph](wikipedia://en/8149170) such that cutting along the cycle would not disconnect the surface on which the graph is embedded.[^o25]

In [matroids](wikipedia://en/244321), a non-separating circuit is a circuit of the matroid (that is, a minimal dependent set) such that [deleting](wikipedia://en/36749660) the circuit leaves a smaller matroid that is connected (that is, that cannot be written as a direct sum of matroids).[^o26] These are analogous to peripheral cycles, but not the same even in [graphic matroids](wikipedia://en/745714) (the matroids whose circuits are the simple cycles of a graph). For example, in the [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{2,3}$, every cycle is peripheral (it has only one bridge, a two-edge path) but the graphic matroid formed by this bridge is not connected, so no circuit of the graphic matroid of $K_{2,3}$ is non-separating.
