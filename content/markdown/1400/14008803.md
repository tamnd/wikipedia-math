# George Chrystal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Chrystal"}

*Image: George Chrystal. Etching by W. Hole, 1884. Wellcome V0001125.jpg.* George Chrystal at work. Etching by W. Hole, 1884

*Image: Foveran Cemetery.jpg.* [Foveran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foveran) Churchyard

**George Chrystal** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) (8 March 1851 – 3 November 1911) was a [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3] He is primarily known for his books on algebra and his studies of [seiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiche) (wave patterns in large inland bodies of water) which earned him a Gold Medal from the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) that was confirmed shortly after his death.[^o4]

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Old Meldrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Meldrum) on 8 March 1851, the son of Margaret (née Burr) and William Chrystal, a wealthy farmer and [grain merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_trade).[^o6]

He was educated at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School) and the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen). In 1872, he moved to study under [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696) at [Peterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse), [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). He graduated [Second Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Wrangler) in 1875, joint with [William Burnside](wikipedia://en/251883), and was elected a fellow of [Corpus Christi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge). He was appointed to the [Regius Chair of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regius_Chair_of_Mathematics) at the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) in 1877, and then in 1879 to the Chair in Mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh). In 1911, he was awarded the [Royal Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Medal) of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) for his researches into the surface oscillations of Scottish [lochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch).

He was a contributor to the drafting of the [Universities (Scotland) Act 1889](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universities_%28Scotland%29_Act_1889), and was one of the founders of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940).

He was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1880, his proposers including [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696). He was awarded the Society's [Keith Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Medal) for 1879-81 and their [Gunning Victoria Jubilee Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunning_Victoria_Jubilee_Prize) for the period 1904–8. He served as Vice President of the Society from 1895-1901 and General Secretary from 1901–1911.[^o9] He is credited with instigating the move of the Society from the Mound to George Street.[^o10][^o11]

He was awarded honorary doctorates (LLD) from the [University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen) in 1887 and the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) in 1911.

In later life he is listed as living at 5 Belgrave Crescent in western Edinburgh.[^o12]

The mathematician [Alexander G. Burgess](wikipedia://en/46994122) trained under him.[^o13]

He grew ill in 1909 and this worsened early in 1911, leading the University to grant him leave of absence from April of that year. A work-free summer did not improve him.[^o14] He died on 3 November 1911[^o15] at 5 Belgrave Crescent in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). He is buried in [Foveran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foveran) Churchyard in [Aberdeenshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeenshire).

## Family {#Family}

*Image: 5 Belgrave Crescent, Edinburgh.png.* 5 Belgrave Crescent, Edinburgh

He married Margaret Anne Balfour (1851-1903) in 1879. She died before him and is buried in the northern Victorian extension to [Dean Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Cemetery) with their son Walter MacDonald Chrystal who died in infancy. They had four sons and two daughters.
