# Toda's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toda's theorem"}

**Toda's theorem** is a result in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) that was proven by [Seinosuke Toda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seinosuke_Toda) in his paper "PP is as Hard as the Polynomial-Time Hierarchy"[^o2] and was given the 1998 [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The theorem states that the entire [polynomial hierarchy PH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH_%28complexity%29) is contained in P<sup>PP</sup>; this implies a closely related statement, that PH is contained in P<sup>\#P</sup>.

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

[\#P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp-P) is the class of problems of the form of exactly counting the number of solutions to a polynomially-verifiable question (that is, to a question in [NP](wikipedia://en/21562)), while loosely speaking, [PP](wikipedia://en/659322) is the class of problems for which there is a [polynomial-time algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time_algorithm) that gives a correct answer more than half the time. The class P<sup>\#P</sup> consists of all problems that can be solved in polynomial time if you have access to instantaneous answers to any counting problem in #P (polynomial time relative to a #P [oracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker-Gill-Solovay_theorem)). Thus Toda's theorem implies that for any problem in the polynomial hierarchy there is a deterministic [polynomial-time Turing reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time_Turing_reduction) to a [counting problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_problem_%28complexity%29).[^o7]

An analogous result in the complexity theory over the reals (in the sense of [Blum–Shub–Smale real Turing machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blum–Shub–Smale_machine)) was proved by [Saugata Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugata_Basu) and [Thierry Zell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Zell) in 2010[^o8] and a complex analogue of Toda's theorem was proved by [Saugata Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugata_Basu) in 2011.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The proof is broken into two parts.

- First, it is established that

$$\Sigma^P \cdot \mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P}$$

The proof uses a variation of [Valiant–Vazirani theorem](wikipedia://en/3003434). Because $\mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P}$ contains $\mathsf{P}$ and is closed under complement, it follows by induction that $\mathsf{PH} \subseteq \mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P}$.

- Second, it is established that

$$\mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{P}^{\# P}$$

Together, the two parts imply

$$\mathsf{PH} \subseteq \mathsf{BP} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{P} \cdot \oplus \mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{P}^{\# P}$$

See (Fortnow 2009) for details.[^o13] A more leisurely proof is in the textbook (Arora Barak 2009).[^o15]

:::
