# Contraction morphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Contraction morphism"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **contraction morphism** is a surjective [projective morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_morphism) $f: X \to Y$ between [normal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_variety) projective varieties (or projective schemes) such that $f_* \mathcal{O}_X = \mathcal{O}_Y$ or, equivalently, the geometric fibers are all connected ([Zariski's connectedness theorem](wikipedia://en/13982192)). It is also commonly called an **algebraic fiber space**, as it is an analog of a [fiber space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_space) in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

By the [Stein factorization](wikipedia://en/1205940), any surjective projective morphism is a contraction morphism followed by a [finite morphism](wikipedia://en/2139357).

Examples include [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158) and [Mori fiber spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mori_fiber_space).

## Birational perspective {#Birational_perspective}

The following perspective is crucial in [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733) (in particular in [Mori's minimal model program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mori's_minimal_model_program)).

Let $X$ be a projective variety and $\overline{NS}(X)$ the closure of the span of irreducible curves on $X$ in $N_1(X)$ = the real vector space of [numerical equivalence classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_equivalence) of real 1-cycles on $X$. Given a face $F$ of $\overline{NS}(X)$, the **contraction morphism associated to $F$**, if it exists, is a contraction morphism $f: X \to Y$ to some projective variety $Y$ such that for each irreducible curve $C \subset X$, $f(C)$ is a point if and only if $[C] \in F$.[^o3] The basic question is which face $F$ gives rise to such a contraction morphism (cf. [cone theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_theorem)).
