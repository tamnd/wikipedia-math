# Gwyneth Stallard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gwyneth Stallard"}

*Image: Gwyneth Stallard.jpg.* Stallard in 2009

**Gwyneth Mary Stallard** is a British mathematician whose research concerns [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518) and the [iteration](wikipedia://en/1448702) of [meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857). She is a professor of pure mathematics at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Stallard read mathematics at [King's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge), finishing in 1985, and earned her Ph.D. from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) in 1991. Her dissertation, *Some problems in the iteration of meromorphic functions*, was supervised by Irvine Noel Baker. She has spoken about the difficulty of finding postdoctoral research positions at a time when there were few such positions in England and the ties of her husband's job prevented her from moving abroad; she maintained her mathematical career at this stage by taking a temporary lectureship teaching engineering students at the [University of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southampton). When she became a professor of mathematics at the Open University, she became the first woman to be a professor in the department.

## Activism and recognition {#Activism_and_recognition}

Stallard won the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) in 2000, and describes this point as the moment when she became confident in her mathematical research abilities.

Stallard was the chair of the Women in Mathematics Committee of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) from 2006 to 2015, and in 2015 she was named an [Officer of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) for her work in support of women in mathematics. In 2016 she was given a special award by the Suffrage Science Scheme on [Ada Lovelace Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Lovelace_Day) in recognition of her work in this area.

In 2016, the London Mathematical Society selected her as their Mary Cartwright Lecturer.
