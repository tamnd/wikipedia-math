# Derived tensor product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Derived tensor product"}

In algebra, given a [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863) *A* over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*, the **derived tensor product** functor is

:::

$$- \otimes_A^{\textbf{L}} - : D(\mathsf{M}_A) \times D({}_A \mathsf{M}) \to D({}_R \mathsf{M})$$

where $\mathsf{M}_A$ and ${}_A \mathsf{M}$ are the [categories of right *A*-modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) and left *A*-modules and *D* refers to the homotopy category (i.e., [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897)).[^o2] By definition, it is the left derived functor of the [tensor product functor](wikipedia://en/2993836) $- \otimes_A - : \mathsf{M}_A \times {}_A \mathsf{M} \to {}_R \mathsf{M}$.

## Derived tensor product in derived ring theory {#Derived_tensor_product_in_derived_ring_theory}

If *R* is an ordinary ring and *M*, *N* right and left modules over it, then, regarding them as discrete spectra, one can form the smash product of them:

$$M \otimes_R^L N$$

whose *i*-th homotopy is the *i*-th [Tor](wikipedia://en/856347):

$$\pi_i (M \otimes_R^L N) = \operatorname{Tor}^R_i(M, N).$$

It is called the **derived tensor product** of *M* and *N*. In particular, $\pi_0 (M \otimes_R^L N)$ is the usual [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) *M* and *N* over *R*.

Geometrically, the derived tensor product corresponds to the [intersection product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_product) (of [derived schemes](wikipedia://en/40776948)).

::: {#o4 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example**: Let *R* be a [simplicial commutative ring](wikipedia://en/40782000), *Q*(*R*) → *R* be a cofibrant replacement, and $\Omega_{Q(R)}^1$ be the module of Kähler differentials. Then

$$\mathbb{L}_R = \Omega_{Q(R)}^1 \otimes^L_{Q(R)} R$$

is an *R*-module called the [cotangent complex](wikipedia://en/17346860) of *R*. It is functorial in *R*: each *R* → *S* gives rise to $\mathbb{L}_R \to \mathbb{L}_S$. Then, for each *R* → *S*, there is the cofiber sequence of *S*-modules

$$\mathbb{L}_{S/R} \to \mathbb{L}_R \otimes_R^L S \to \mathbb{L}_S.$$

The cofiber $\mathbb{L}_{S/R}$ is called the relative cotangent complex.

:::
