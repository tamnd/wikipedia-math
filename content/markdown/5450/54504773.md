# Euler characteristic of an orbifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler characteristic of an orbifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **Euler characteristic of an orbifold**, or **orbifold Euler characteristic**, is a generalization of the [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) that includes contributions coming from nontrivial [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160). In particular, unlike a topological Euler characteristic, it is not restricted to [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) values and is in general a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024). It is of interest in mathematical physics, specifically in [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305). Given a compact manifold $M$ quotiented by a finite group $G$, the Euler characteristic of $M/G$ is

:::

$$\chi(M,G) = \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{g_1 g_2 = g_2 g_1} \chi(M^{g_1, g_2}),$$

where $|G|$ is the order of the group $G$, the sum runs over all pairs of commuting elements of $G$, and $M^{g_1, g_2}$ is the space of simultaneous fixed points of $g_1$ and $g_2$. (The appearance of $\chi$ in the summation is the usual Euler characteristic.) If the action is free, the sum has only a single term, and so this expression reduces to the topological Euler characteristic of $M$ divided by $|G|$.
