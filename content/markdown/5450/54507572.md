# Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques"}

*Image: Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques atrium.jpg.* Atrium in the main building at the Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques in Luminy, Marseille, France.

The **Centre International de Rencontres Mathématiques** (**CIRM**) is a mathematics research institute associated with the [Centre National de la Recherche Scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_National_de_la_Recherche_Scientifique) (CNRS) and the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) (SMF). It is located in Luminy, [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille), France, and is affiliated with [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University). CIRM hosts weekly workshops on diverse topics where mathematicians and scientists from all over the world come to do collaborative research. Modeled as a "[villa Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_villas) of mathematics", it receives around 3,500 visitors per year.[^o2]

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Luminy (4).jpg.* CIRM Luminy residential hall

In 1954, a report from the CNRS discussed potential sites for a meeting place to hold international seminars and workshops in mathematics similar to the [Mathematisches Forschunginstitut Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach). The Luminy estate, formerly owned by the prominent [Fabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabre_Line) shipping family, was chosen in 1976.[^o4] The estate was handed over to the SMF in 1979. The center opened in 1981 and the first workshop was held in 1982.[^o5]

## Scientific programs {#Scientific_programs}

CIRM supports a variety of residential programs and workshops. Each year, CIRM runs around 35 week-long workshops with an average of 75 weekly participants.[^o7] CIRM also supports joint programs with [Société de Mathématiques Appliquées et Industrielles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_de_Mathématiques_Appliquées_et_Industrielles), [Institut Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/10513595), and [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France).[^o8]

### Jean-Morlet Chair {#Jean-Morlet_Chair}

CIRM hosts the Jean-Morlet Chair, which is a six-month residential program for international researchers to collaborate with a local project leader from [Aix-Marseille University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_University) to plan events and projects. The chair was named after [Jean Morlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Morlet), a French geophysicist who worked with Marseille-based researcher [Alex Grossmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Grossmann), among others, to develop the [wavelet transform](wikipedia://en/3352292).[^o10] Past chairs have included Nicola Kistler, Boris Hasselblatt, Igor Shparlinski, [Hans Georg Feichtinger](wikipedia://en/31979049), Herwig Hauser, [François Lalonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Lalonde), [Dipendra Prasad](wikipedia://en/38106303), [Mariusz Lemańczyk](wikipedia://en/54594784), [Konstantin Khanin](wikipedia://en/54593563), Shigeki Akiyama, and Genevieve Walsh.[^o11]
