# Mary Rickett

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mary Rickett"}

**Mary Ellen Rickett** (4 March 1861 – 20 March 1925) was a British mathematician who lectured in mathematics at [Newnham College, University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newnham_College,_Cambridge) for more than twenty years. She was also the Vice-Principal of Old Hall from 1895 until her death in 1925. She was the first woman to win [Bedford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College,_London)'s Gold Medal when she graduated with her bachelor's degree, and the first [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) of Newnham College.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Rickett was born on 4 March 1861.[^o4] She was educated by private tutors, and then at a private school in [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton), and at Priory School in [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover).[^o5][^o4] Rickett earned a bachelor's degree in French and German from Bedford College, London, part of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), in 1881, and was the first woman to win the gold medal of the University of London.[^o6]

She studied at Newnham College beginning in 1882, and took both the [Classical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Tripos) in 1884 and the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) in 1885 and 1886.[^o5] In the Mathematical Tripos, her score was next after the 24th Wrangler (and before the 25th Wrangler), making her the first Wrangler of Newnham.[^o5][^o7] In 1893, she was elected one of the first thirty associates of the college.[^o5]

She became a lecturer in mathematics at Newnham College in 1886,[^o5][^o6] associate of the college in 1893,[^o5] acting vice-principal of Old Hall in 1889,[^o6] and permanent vice-principal in 1895.[^o5] Rickett was regarded as an "especially successful teacher",[^o5] whose approach was "precise and clear".[^o4] In Newnham, she coached [Philippa Fawcett](wikipedia://en/639426) to become the first woman to get the highest score on the Mathematical Tripos, 1890.[^o8] Rickett retired in 1908, and died on 20 March 1925, aged 64.[^o6][^o4]
