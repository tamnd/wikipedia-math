# Thomas Jones (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Jones"}

**Thomas Jones** (23 June 1756 – 18 July 1807) was a Welsh mathematician who was Head Tutor at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), for twenty years and an outstanding teacher of mathematics. He is notable as a mentor of [Adam Sedgwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sedgwick).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jones was born at [Berriew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berriew), [Montgomeryshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomeryshire), in [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales).

On completing his studies at [Shrewsbury School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury_School), Jones was admitted to [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), on 28 May 1774, as a 'pensioner' (i.e. a fee-paying student, as opposed to a scholar or [sizar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizar)).[^o4] He was believed to be an illegitimate son of Mr Owen Owen, of [Tyncoed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyncoed), and his housekeeper, who afterwards married a Mr Jones, of [Traffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffin), [County Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Kerry), Thomas then being brought up as his son.[^o4]

On 27 June 1776, Jones migrated from St John's College to Trinity College. He became a scholar in 1777 and obtained his BA in 1779, winning the First [Smith's Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith's_Prize) and becoming [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609). In 1782, he obtained his MA and became a Fellow of Trinity College in 1781. He became a Junior Dean, 1787–1789 and a Tutor, 1787–1807. He was ordained a deacon at the [Peterborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterborough) parish on 18 June 1780. Then he was ordained priest, at the [Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Cambridgeshire) parish on 6 June 1784, [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_%28priest%29) of [Fen Ditton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen_Ditton), Cambridgeshire, in 1784, and then canon of [Swaffham Prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaffham_Prior), also 1784. On 11 December 1791, he preached before the university, at [Great St Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_St_Mary's), a sermon against duelling (from Exodus XX. 13), which was prompted by a duel that had lately taken place near [Newmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmarket,_Suffolk) between [Henry Applewhaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Applewhaite) and [Richard Ryecroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Ryecroft), undergraduates of Pembroke, in which the latter was fatally wounded. Jones died on 18 July 1807, in lodgings in [Edgware Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgware_Road), [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London). He is buried in the cemetery of [Dulwich College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulwich_College). A bust and a memorial tablet are in the ante-chapel of Trinity College.

His academic mentor was [John Cranke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cranke) (1746–1816). His Cambridge tutor was [Thomas Postlethwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Postlethwaite).
