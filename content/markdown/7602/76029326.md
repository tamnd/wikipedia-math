# San Francisco Proposition G (March 2024)

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="San Francisco Proposition G"}

On November 20, 2023, the [San Francisco Board of Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Board_of_Supervisors) passed a motion ordering the inclusion of **Proposition G: Offering Algebra 1 to Eighth Graders** on the **March 2024 Local Ballot**.[^o4] Written by Supervisor [Joel Engardio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Engardio), the measure acts as a [non-binding policy statement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binding_resolution) urging the [San Francisco Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Unified_School_District) to reverse their decision on delaying enrollment in [Algebra I](wikipedia://en/18716923) until ninth grade.[^o7] The measure passed with 81.75% of the vote.[^o5] The passage of the measure did not immediately change course offerings for students. The San Francisco Unified School District is a [public agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_agency) that is separate from the City and retains [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) in regards to when Algebra I is able to be offered to students.[^o6] Passage of the proposal would inform the San Francisco Unified School District on the stance of the constituents in regards to their preferred math offerings for students.[^o7] Additionally, the proposal would: support the School District's development of its [math curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum) for students at all grade levels, explore [differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiated_instruction) options for students both below and above grade-level in math across all grades, and promote pathways for students interested in studying [STEM](wikipedia://en/3437663) at the [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) level.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

The election was held on March 5, 2024. Proposition G passed with 81.75% of the vote, receiving 182,066 "Yes" votes and 40,638 "No" votes.[^o5]
