# Arthur Stafford Hathaway

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arthur Stafford Hathaway"}

**Arthur Stafford Hathaway** (1855 — 1934) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

Arthur was born September 15, 1855, in [Keeler, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeler,_Michigan).[^o2]

A student at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), Hathaway earned a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in 1879. For two years he was instructor in mathematics at Friends High School in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore). Hathaway studied with [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). From Sylvester's lectures he learned some [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and published notes on congruences.

He was an instructor at Cornell University from 1885 to 1890 and an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) in 1891.

In October 1884 [William Thomson, Baron Kelvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thomson,_Baron_Kelvin) led a [master class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_class) on "Molecular Dynamics and the Wave Theory of Light" at Johns Hopkins. Kelvin did not provide a text for his course and Hathaway made notes in short-hand. He wrote up the notes and duplicated them with a [Papyrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimeograph#Papyrograph), a recent [stencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stencil)-based device. As the demand outstripped the supply, Hathaway corresponded with Kelvin back in Glasgow to prepare for proper publication. After nineteen years the lectures were published.[^o3] In 1987 Hathaway's original transcription from 1884 was published when Johns Hopkins Center for the History and Philosophy of Science decided to commemorate the centennial of Kelvin's lectures.[^o4]

In [Terre Haute, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terre_Haute,_Indiana) Hathaway taught at [Rose Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Polytechnic_Institute) until 1920 and published *A Primer on Quaternions* in 1896. He became the U.S. national secretary for the international [Quaternion Association](wikipedia://en/16416512) in 1899.
