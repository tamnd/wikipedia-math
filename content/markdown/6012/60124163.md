# Mathieu Weill

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mathieu Weill"}

**Isaac Mathieu Weill** (24 May 1851 – 5 November 1939) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and principal of the [Collège Chaptal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Chaptal).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mathieu Weill was born to a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) family in [Haguenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haguenau), the son of Valentine and Isidore Weill, a mathematics teacher. He was educated in the [lyceums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyceums) of [Burg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourg-en-Bresse) and [Strasburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), and was admitted to the [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique) in 1870. He received a degree in mathematics in November 1872, and a degree in physical sciences in November 1876. Meanwhile, Weill began studies at the [military school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Applied_Artillery) in [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau) in 1872. He attained the rank of [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) of [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery), but resigned in April 1877.

He married Delphine Lévi in 1878, with whom he had four children. That same year, he became a lecturer at the [Collège Chaptal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée_Chaptal) at Paris. He was appointed professor of mathematics in 1881, and in October 1898 the school's principal. He was appointed Chevalier of the [Legion of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Honour) on 28 February 1904.[^o4]

## Publications {#Publications}

Weill published several essays in French mathematical journals, including the *[Nouvelles Annales de Mathématiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouvelles_Annales_de_Mathématiques)* and the *[Bulletin de la Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulletin_de_la_Société_Mathématique_de_France)*. He was the author of "Cours de Géométrie Analytique" (1888) and of "Précis d'Arithmétique, de Géométrie, d'Algèbre, de Trigonométrie," in four volumes (1895–1897).[^o6]
