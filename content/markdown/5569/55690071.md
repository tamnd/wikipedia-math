# Frobenius reciprocity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frobenius reciprocity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and in particular [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), **Frobenius reciprocity** is a theorem expressing a [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) between the process of [restricting](wikipedia://en/880754) and [inducing](wikipedia://en/357452). It can be used to leverage knowledge about representations of a subgroup to find and classify representations of "large" groups that contain them. It is named for [Ferdinand Georg Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048), the inventor of the [representation theory of finite groups](wikipedia://en/874400).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

### Character theory {#Character_theory}

The theorem was originally stated in terms of [character theory](wikipedia://en/691741). Let $G$ be a finite [group](wikipedia://en/19447) with a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H$, let $\operatorname{Res}_H^G$ denote the restriction of a character, or more generally, [class function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_function_%28algebra%29) of $G$ to $H$, and let $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G$ denote the [induced class function](wikipedia://en/357452) of a given class function on $H$. For any finite group $A$, there is an [inner product](wikipedia://en/14856) $\langle -,-\rangle_A$ on the [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) of class functions $A\to\mathbb{C}$ (described in detail in the article [Schur orthogonality relations](wikipedia://en/7947411)). Now, for any class functions $\psi:H\to\mathbb{C}$ and $\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}$, the following equality holds:[^n1][^n2]

$$\langle\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\psi, \varphi\rangle_G=\langle\psi,\operatorname{Res}_H^G\varphi\rangle_H.$$

In other words, $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G$ and $\operatorname{Res}_H^G$ are [Hermitian adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175).

::: {#o7 .proof cue=collapse name="Proof of Frobenius reciprocity for class functions"}

Let $\psi:H\to\mathbb{C}$ and $\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}$ be class functions.

::: {#o8 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof.** Every class function can be written as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of irreducible characters. As $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is a [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477), we can, without loss of generality, assume $\psi$ and $\varphi$ to be characters of irreducible representations of $H$ in $W$ and of $G$ in $V,$ respectively. We define $\psi(s)=0$ for all $s\in G\setminus H.$ Then we have

$$\begin{aligned} \langle \text{Ind}(\psi), \varphi\rangle_G &= \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{t\in G} \text{Ind}(\psi)(t) \varphi(t^{-1}) \\ &= \frac{1}{|G|} \sum_{t\in G} \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{s\in G \atop s^{-1}ts \in H} \psi(s^{-1}ts) \varphi(t^{-1}) \\ &= \frac{1}{|G|} \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{t\in G} \sum_{s\in G} \psi(s^{-1}ts) \varphi((s^{-1}ts)^{-1}) \\ &= \frac{1}{|G|} \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{t\in G} \sum_{s\in G} \psi(t) \varphi(t^{-1})\\ &= \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{t\in G} \psi(t) \varphi(t^{-1})\\ &= \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{t\in H} \psi(t) \varphi(t^{-1})\\ &= \frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{t\in H} \psi(t) \text{Res}(\varphi)(t^{-1})\\ &= \langle \psi, \text{Res}(\varphi)\rangle_H \end{aligned}$$

In the course of this sequence of equations we used only the definition of induction on class functions and the properties of characters. $\Box$

**Alternative proof.** In terms of the group algebra, i.e. by the alternative description of the induced representation, the Frobenius reciprocity is a special case of a general equation for a change of rings:

$$\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C} [H]}(W,U)=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C} [G]}(\mathbb{C} [G]\otimes_{\mathbb{C} [H]}W, U).$$

Taking the dimension of both sides, we obtain

$$\langle W,\text{Res}(U)\rangle_H=\langle W,U\rangle_H=\langle \text{Ind}(W),U\rangle_G.$$

As this bilinear form tallies the bilinear form on the corresponding characters, the theorem follows without calculation. $\Box$

:::

:::

### Module theory {#Module_theory}

As explained in the section [Representation theory of finite groups#Representations, modules and the convolution algebra](wikipedia://en/874400#Representations,_modules_and_the_convolution_algebra), the theory of the representations of a group $G$ over a field $K$ is, in a certain sense, equivalent to the theory of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) $K$\[$G$\].[^o10] Therefore, there is a corresponding Frobenius reciprocity theorem for $K$\[$G$\]-modules.

Let $G$ be a group with subgroup $H$, let $M$ be an $H$-module, and let $N$ be a $G$-module. In the language of module theory, the [induced module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_module) $K[G]\otimes_{K[H]} M$ corresponds to the induced representation $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G$, whereas the [restriction of scalars](wikipedia://en/41410139#Restriction_of_scalars) ${_{K[H]}}N$ corresponds to the restriction $\operatorname{Res}_H^G$. Accordingly, the statement is as follows: The following sets of module homomorphisms are in bijective correspondence:

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{K[G]}(K[G]\otimes_{K[H]} M,N)\cong \operatorname{Hom}_{K[H]}(M,{_{K[H]}}N)$$

.[^o11][^n3]

As noted below in the section on category theory, this result applies to modules over all rings, not just modules over group algebras.

### Category theory {#Category_theory}

Let $G$ be a group with a subgroup $H$, and let $\operatorname{Res}_H^G,\operatorname{Ind}_H^G$ be defined as above. For any group $A$ and [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ let $\textbf{Rep}_A^K$ denote the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of linear representations of $A$ over $K$. There is a [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783)

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Res}_H^G:\textbf{Rep}_G&\longrightarrow\textbf{Rep}_H \\ (V,\rho) &\longmapsto \operatorname{Res}_H^G(V,\rho) \end{aligned}$$

This functor acts as the [identity](wikipedia://en/15069) on [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891). There is a functor going in the opposite direction:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Ind}_H^G:\textbf{Rep}_H &\longrightarrow\textbf{Rep}_G \\ (W,\tau) &\longmapsto \operatorname{Ind}_H^G(W,\tau) \end{aligned}$$

These functors form an [adjoint pair](wikipedia://en/53991) $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\dashv\operatorname{Res}_H^G$.[^o14] In the case of finite groups, they are actually both left- and right-adjoint to one another. This adjunction gives rise to a [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) for the induced representation (for details, see [Induced representation#Properties](wikipedia://en/357452#Properties)).

In the language of module theory, the corresponding adjunction is an instance of the more general [relationship between restriction and extension of scalars](wikipedia://en/41410139#Relation_between_the_extension_of_scalars_and_the_restriction_of_scalars).
