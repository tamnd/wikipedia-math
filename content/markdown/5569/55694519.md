# Lenore E. Bixby

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lenore E. Bixby"}

**Lenore Alice Epstein Bixby** (August 12, 1914 – April 5, 1994)[^o3] was an American statistician who worked with the [Social Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Administration) and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).

:::

## Education {#Education}

She was born in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), and some of her earlier publications are listed under that name. As a student of the class of 1935 at [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College), she became chairman of the school's branch of the [League of Women Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Women_Voters), and graduated [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa) with honors as a Senior Durant Scholar. She did graduate study in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), earning a master's degree there in 1937.

## Career {#Career}

After completing her studies, she came to work for the [Bureau of Labor Statistics](wikipedia://en/72528) and then, after [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), as an operations officer for the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan). This work took her away from Washington to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989). She joined the Social Security Administration in 1954, and worked there as deputy assistant commissioner of the Office of Research and Statistics and later as director of the Division of Retirement and Survivors' Studies. While there, she married F. Lovell Bixby, a prominent [penologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penologist), who died in 1975.

She retired from the administration in 1977, but continued to work as a statistician for the National Academy of Sciences, in their Committee on National Statistics.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 1969, she was honored by the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), who elected her as one of their [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow). She was also a fellow of the Gerontological Society. She was a founding member of the National Academy of Social Insurance.
