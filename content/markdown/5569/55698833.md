# Envelope (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Envelope"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and related fields of mathematics, an **envelope** is a construction that generalizes the operations of "exterior completion", like completion of a locally convex space, or [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) of a topological space. A dual construction is called [refinement](wikipedia://en/56509649).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose $K$ is a category, $X$ an object in $K$, and $\Omega$ and $\Phi$ two classes of morphisms in $K$. The definition[^n1] of an envelope of $X$ in the class $\Omega$ with respect to the class $\Phi$ consists of two steps.

*Image: Extension-3.jpg.* Extension.

- A morphism $\sigma:X\to X'$ in $K$ is called an *extension of the object $X$ in the class of morphisms $\Omega$ with respect to the class of morphisms $\Phi$*, if $\sigma\in\Omega$, and for any morphism $\varphi:X\to B$ from the class $\Phi$ there exists a unique morphism $\varphi':X'\to B$ in $K$ such that $\varphi=\varphi'\circ\sigma$.

*Image: Envelope-3.jpg.* Envelope.

- An extension $\rho:X\to E$ of the object $X$ in the class of morphisms $\Omega$ with respect to the class of morphisms $\Phi$ is called an *envelope of $X$ in $\Omega$ with respect to $\Phi$*, if for any other extension $\sigma:X\to X'$ (of $X$ in $\Omega$ with respect to $\Phi$) there is a unique morphism $\upsilon:X'\to E$ in $K$ such that $\rho=\upsilon\circ\sigma$. The object $E$ is also called an *envelope of $X$ in $\Omega$ with respect to $\Phi$*.

Notations:

$$\rho=\text{env}_{\Phi}^{\Omega}X, \qquad E=\text{Env}_{\Phi}^{\Omega}X.$$

In a special case when $\Omega$ is a class of all morphisms whose ranges belong to a given class of objects $L$ in $K$ it is convenient to replace $\Omega$ with $L$ in the notations (and in the terms):

$$\rho=\text{env}_{\Phi}^L X, \qquad E=\text{Env}_{\Phi}^L X.$$

Similarly, if $\Phi$ is a class of all morphisms whose ranges belong to a given class of objects $M$ in $K$ it is convenient to replace $\Phi$ with $M$ in the notations (and in the terms):

$$\rho=\text{env}_M^{\Omega} X, \qquad E=\text{Env}_M^{\Omega} X.$$

For example, one can speak about an *envelope of $X$ in the class of objects $L$ with respect to the class of objects $M$*:

$$\rho=\text{env}_M^L X, \qquad E=\text{Env}_M^L X.$$

:::

## Nets of epimorphisms and functoriality {#Nets_of_epimorphisms_and_functoriality}

Suppose that to each object $X\in\operatorname{Ob}({K})$ in a category ${K}$ it is assigned a subset ${\mathcal N}^X$ in the class $\operatorname{Epi}^X$ of all epimorphisms of the category ${K}$, going from $X$, and the following three requirements are fulfilled:

- for each object $X$ the set ${\mathcal N}^X$ is non-empty and is directed to the left with respect to the pre-order inherited from $\operatorname{Epi}^X$

$$\forall \sigma,\sigma'\in {\mathcal N}^X\quad \exists\rho\in{\mathcal N}^X\quad \rho\to\sigma\ \& \ \rho\to\sigma',$$

- for each object $X$ the covariant system of morphisms generated by ${\mathcal N}^X$

$$\{\iota_\rho^\sigma;\ \rho,\sigma\in{\mathcal N}^X,\ \rho\to\sigma\}$$

has a colimit $\varprojlim {\mathcal N}^X$ in $K$, called the *local limit* in $X$;

- for each morphism $\alpha:X\to Y$ and for each element $\tau\in{\mathcal N}^Y$ there are an element $\sigma\in{\mathcal N}^X$ and a morphism $\alpha_\sigma^\tau:\operatorname{Cod}\sigma\to\operatorname{Cod}\tau$[^o6] such that

$$\tau\circ\alpha=\alpha_\sigma^\tau\circ\sigma.$$

Then the family of sets ${\mathcal N}=\{{\mathcal N}^X;\ X\in\operatorname{Ob}({K})\}$ is called a *net of epimorphisms* in the category ${K}$.

**Examples.**

1. For each [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) $X$ and for each closed convex balanced neighbourhood of zero $U\subseteq X$ let us consider its kernel $\operatorname{Ker}U=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\varepsilon\cdot U$ and the quotient space $X/\operatorname{Ker}U$ endowed with the normed topology with the unit ball $U+\operatorname{Ker}U$, and let $X/U=(X/\operatorname{Ker}U)^\blacktriangledown$ be the completion of $X/\operatorname{Ker}U$ (obviously, $X/U$ is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), and it is called the *quotient Banach space* of $X$ by $U$). The system of natural mappings $X\to X/U$ is a net of epimorphisms in the category $\text{LCS}$ of locally convex topological vector spaces.
1. For each locally convex topological algebra $A$ and for each *submultiplicative* closed convex balanced neighbourhood of zero $U\subseteq X$,

$$U\cdot U\subseteq U,$$

let us again consider its kernel $\operatorname{Ker}U=\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\varepsilon\cdot U$ and the quotient algebra $A/\operatorname{Ker}U$ endowed with the normed topology with the unit ball $U+\operatorname{Ker}U$, and let $A/U=(A/\operatorname{Ker}U)^\blacktriangledown$ be the completion of $A/\operatorname{Ker}U$ (obviously, $A/U$ is a [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665), and it is called the *quotient Banach algebra* of $X$ by $U$). The system of natural mappings $A\to A/U$ is a net of epimorphisms in the category $\text{LCS}$ of locally convex topological algebras.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**[^n2] *Let ${\mathcal N}$ be a net of epimorphisms in a category ${K}$ that generates a class of morphisms $\varPhi$ on the inside:*

$${\mathcal N} \subseteq \varPhi \subseteq \operatorname{Mor}({K}) \circ {\mathcal N}.$$

*Then for any class of epimorphisms $\varOmega$ in $K$, which contains all local limits $\varprojlim {\mathcal N}^X$,*

$$\{\varprojlim {\mathcal N}^X; \ X\in\operatorname{Ob}(K)\}\subseteq\varOmega\subseteq\operatorname{Epi}(K),$$

*the following holds:*

(i) *for each object $X$ in ${K}$ the local limit $\varprojlim {\mathcal N}^X$ is an envelope* $\operatorname{env}_\varPhi^\varOmega X$ in $\varOmega$ with respect to $\varPhi$:

$$\varprojlim {\mathcal N}^X=\operatorname{env}_\varPhi^\varOmega X,$$

(ii) *the envelope $\operatorname{Env}_\varPhi^\varOmega$ can be defined as a functor.*

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**[^n3] *Let ${\mathcal N}$ be a net of epimorphisms in a category ${K}$ that generates a class of morphisms $\varPhi$ on the inside:*

$${\mathcal N} \subseteq \varPhi \subseteq \operatorname{Mor}({K}) \circ {\mathcal N}.$$

*Then for any monomorphically complementable class of epimorphisms $\varOmega$ in $K$ such that $K$ is co-well-powered[^o11] in $\varOmega$ the envelope $\operatorname{Env}_\varPhi^\varOmega$ can be defined as a functor.*

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**[^n4] *Suppose a category $K$ and a class of objects $L$ have the following properties:*

(i) $K$ *is [cocomplete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocomplete),*

(ii) $K$ *has [nodal decomposition](wikipedia://en/56302591),*

(iii) $K$ *is co-well-powered in the class $\operatorname{Epi}$,*[^o14]

(iv) $\operatorname{Mor}(K,L)$ *goes from $K$:*

$$\forall X\in\operatorname{Ob}(K)\quad \exists \varphi\in\operatorname{Mor}(K)\quad \operatorname{Dom}\varphi=X\quad\&\quad\operatorname{Cod}\varphi\in L,$$

(v) $L$ *differs morphisms on the outside: for any two different parallel morphisms $\alpha\ne\beta:X\to Y$ there is a morphism $\varphi:Y\to Z\in L$ such that $\varphi\circ\alpha\ne\varphi\circ\beta$,*

(vi) $L$ *is closed with respect to passage to colimits,*

(vii) $L$ *is closed with respect to passage from the codomain of a morphism to its [nodal image](wikipedia://en/56302591): if $\operatorname{Cod}\alpha\in L$, then $\operatorname{Im}_\infty\alpha\in L$.*

*Then the envelope $\operatorname{Env}_L^L$ can be defined as a functor.*

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

In the following list all envelopes can be defined as functors.

\1. The *completion* $X^\blacktriangledown$ of a [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) $X$ is an envelope of $X$ in the category $\text{LCS}$ of all locally convex spaces with respect to the class $\text{Ban}$ of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989):[^n5] $X^\blacktriangledown=\text{Env}_{\text{Ban}}^{\text{LCS}}X$. Obviously, $X^\blacktriangledown$ is the inverse limit of the quotient Banach spaces $X/U$ (defined above):

$$X^\blacktriangledown=\lim_{0\gets U}X/U.$$

\2. The [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897) $\beta:X\to\beta X$ of a Tikhonov [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X$ is an envelope of $X$ in the category $\text{Tikh}$ of all Tikhonov spaces in the class $\text{Com}$ of [compact spaces](wikipedia://en/6042) with respect to the same class $\text{Com}$:[^n5] $\beta X=\text{Env}_{\text{Com}}^{\text{Com}}X.$

\3. The *Arens-Michael envelope*[^n6][^n7][^n8][^n9] $A^{\text{AM}}$ of a locally convex topological algebra $A$ with a separately continuous multiplication is an envelope of $A$ in the category $\text{TopAlg}$ of all (locally convex) topological algebras (with separately continuous multiplications) in the class $\text{TopAlg}$ with respect to the class $\text{Ban}$ of Banach algebras: $A^{\text{AM}}= \text{Env}_{\text{Ban}}^{\text{TopAlg}}A$. The algebra $A^{\text{AM}}$ is the inverse limit of the quotient Banach algebras $A/U$ (defined above):

$$A^{\text{AM}}=\lim_{0\gets U}A/U.$$

\4. The *holomorphic envelope*[^n10] $\text{Env}_{\cal O} A$ of a [stereotype algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) $A$ is an envelope of $A$ in the category $\text{SteAlg}$ of all stereotype algebras in the class $\text{DEpi}$ of all *dense* epimorphisms[^o24] in $\text{SteAlg}$ with respect to the class $\text{Ban}$ of all Banach algebras: $\text{Env}_{\cal O} A= \text{Env}_{\text{Ban}}^{\text{DEpi}}A.$

\5. The *smooth envelope*[^n11] $\text{Env}_{\cal E} A$ of a [stereotype algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) $A$ is an envelope of $A$ in the category $\text{InvSteAlg}$ of all involutive stereotype algebras in the class $\text{DEpi}$ of all *dense* epimorphisms[^o24] in $\text{InvSteAlg}$ with respect to the class $\text{DiffMor}$ of all differential homomorphisms into various C\*-algebras with joined self-adjoined nilpotent elements: $\text{Env}_{\cal E} A= \text{Env}_{\text{DiffMor}}^{\text{DEpi}}A.$

\6. The *continuous envelope*[^n12][^n13] $\text{Env}_{\cal C} A$ of a [stereotype algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) $A$ is an envelope of $A$ in the category $\text{InvSteAlg}$ of all involutive stereotype algebras in the class $\text{DEpi}$ of all *dense* epimorphisms[^o24] in $\text{InvSteAlg}$ with respect to the class $\text{C}^*$ of all C\*-algebras: $\text{Env}_{\cal C} A= \text{Env}_{\text{C}^*}^{\text{DEpi}}A.$

:::

::: {#o29 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Envelopes appear as standard functors in various fields of mathematics. Apart from the examples given above,

- the [Gelfand transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfand_transform) $G_A:A\to C(\text{Spec} A)$ of a commutative involutive [stereotype algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) $A$ is a continuous envelope of $A$;[^n14][^n15]
- for each [locally compact abelian group](wikipedia://en/454325) $G$ the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) $F_A:C^\star(G)\to C(\widehat{G})$ is a continuous envelope of the [stereotype group algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_algebra_of_a_topological_group#Stereotype_group_algebras) $C^\star(G)$ of measures with compact support on $G$.[^n14]

In [abstract harmonic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_harmonic_analysis) the notion of envelope plays a key role in the generalizations of the [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) theory[^n16] to the classes of non-commutative groups: the holomorphic, the smooth and the continuous envelopes of [stereotype algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) (in the examples given above) lead respectively to the constructions of the holomorphic, the smooth and the continuous dualities in *big geometric disciplines* – [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) – for certain classes of (not necessarily commutative) topological groups considered in these disciplines ([affine algebraic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_algebraic_group), and some classes of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and Moore groups).[^n17][^n14][^n16][^n18]
