# Betty Johnson (physicist)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Betty Johnson"}

**Elizabeth "Betty" Johnson** **PhD** (18 October 1936 – 11 September 2003) was an American [theoretical physicist](wikipedia://en/19594028) with a PhD in elementary particle theory, who was awarded an MBE in 2002 for "services to people returning to careers in science, engineering and technology". Johnson was influential in establishing the [Daphne Jackson Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphne_Jackson_Trust) and was one of the first women to receive a fellowship after a career break for family commitments. Johnson died after a long fight with breast cancer on September 11th, 2003.

:::

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Johnson was born in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia). She received a BA in physics and mathematics at [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College), Pennsylvania in 1958.[^o4] She completed a PhD in elementary particle physics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) with support of the State Department [Fulbright Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) scheme between 1958 – 1960, and an honorary [Woodrow Wilson fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_National_Fellowship_Foundation).[^o4][^o5] She continued her research in the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison), [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) and the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland).[^o6] Johnson held several part-time positions at the [University of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Surrey) whilst raising two children. In 1975 Johnson was elected a fellow of the [Institute of Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Physics).[^o7]

Her research interests evolved from [elementary particle theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle_theory) to [gas dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_dynamics) and spin-dependent effects in [semiconductors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor). Her work focused on understanding [fundamental physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_physics) and using it to predict the properties of real materials.[^o4]

During her time at the [University of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Surrey), Johnson became a colleague and friend of [Daphne Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphne_Jackson), the first woman in the United Kingdom to become a professor of physics.[^o8] When Daphne Jackson began to award fellowships for women who wanted to resume research after taking a break from their careers; Johnson was one of the first recipients. In 1986 Johnson joined the Condensed Matter Theory Group at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^o9]

Johnson was crucial in establishing the [Daphne Jackson Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daphne_Jackson_Trust) after Jackson's death in 1992. She became a trustee in 2002, as well as sitting on the council of the [Women's Engineering Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Engineering_Society).[^o5] She was awarded in an [MBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) for her commitment to women in science in 2002.[^o10][^o5]
