# Artemas Martin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Artemas Martin"}

**Artemas Martin** (August 3, 1835 – November 7, 1918) was a self-educated American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Martin was born on August 3, 1835, in [Steuben County, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steuben_County,_New_York), grew up in [Venango County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venango_County,_Pennsylvania), and spent most of his life in [Erie County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_County,_Pennsylvania). He was home-schooled until the age of 14, when he began studying mathematics at the local school, later moving to the Franklin Select School a few miles away and then to the Franklin Academy, finishing his formal education at age approximately 20. He worked as a farmer, oil driller, and schoolteacher. In 1881, he declined an invitation to become a professor of mathematics at the Normal School in Missouri. In 1885, he became the librarian for the Survey Office of the [United States Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard),[^o5][^o6][^o8][^o7] and in 1898 he became a [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_computer) in the Division of Tides.[^o8] He died on November 7, 1918.[^o8]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

Martin was a prolific contributor of problems and solutions to mathematical puzzle columns in popular magazines beginning at the age of 18 in the *Pittsburgh Almanac* and the *Philadelphia Saturday Evening Post*. From 1870 to 1875, he was editor of the "Stairway Department" of *Clark's School Visitor*, one of the magazines to which he had previously contributed. From 1875 to 1876 Martin moved to the *Normal Monthly*, where he published 16 articles on [diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis). He subsequently became editor of the *Mathematical Visitor* in 1877 and of the *Mathematical Magazine* in 1882.[^o5][^o8] In 1893 in Chicago, his paper *On fifth-power numbers whose sum is a fifth power* was read (but not by him) at the International Mathematical Congress held in connection with the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition).[^o10] He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1912 in Cambridge UK.[^o11]

Martin maintained an extensive mathematical library,[^o5][^o7] now in the collections of [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University).[^o8][^o12]

## Awards and societies {#Awards_and_societies}

In 1877 Martin was given an honorary M.A. from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o5][^o8][^o7][^o14] In 1882 he was awarded another honorary degree, a Ph.D. from [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), and his third honorary degree, an LL.D., was given to him in 1885 by [Hillsdale College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsdale_College).[^o5][^o8][^o7] He was elected to the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1878, the [Société Mathématique de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Société_Mathématique_de_France) in 1884, the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940) in 1885, the [Philosophical Society of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Society_of_Washington) in 1886, the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) in 1890, and the [New York Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mathematical_Society) in 1891.[^o5] He was also a member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the [Circolo Matematico di Palermo](wikipedia://en/32146789), the [Mathematical Association of England](wikipedia://en/2742403), and the [Deutsche Mathematiker-Vereinigung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Mathematiker-Vereinigung).[^o7]
