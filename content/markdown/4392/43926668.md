# David Acheson (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Acheson"}

**David John Acheson** (born 1946) is a British [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

He was educated at [Highgate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highgate_School), [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) (BSc Mathematics and Physics, 1967) and the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia) (PhD, 1971).[^o5] He was appointed a Fellow in Mathematics at [Jesus College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_College,_Oxford) in 1977 and became an emeritus Fellow in 2008. He served as president of the [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403) from 2010 to 2011. He was awarded an Honorary Doctorate of Science from the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia) in 2013.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

His early research was on geophysical and astrophysical fluid dynamics, beginning with the discovery in 1972 of a magnetic 'field gradient' instability in rotating fluids.[^o8] In 1976, he discovered the first examples of wave over-reflection (i.e. reflection coefficient greater than unity) in a stable system. In 1978 his research focused on magnetic fields and differential rotation in stars, with new results on [magnetic buoyancy](wikipedia://en/73370539), the Taylor instability, Goldreich-Schubert instability, and [magnetorotational instability](wikipedia://en/12010787). In 1992 he discovered the 'upside-down pendulums theorem' (which is very loosely connected with the [Indian Rope Trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rope_Trick)).
