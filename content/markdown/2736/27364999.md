# Robert V. Hogg

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Robert V. Hogg"}

**Robert Vincent Hogg** (8 November 1924 – 23 December 2014)[^o4] was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and professor of statistics of the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa). Hogg is known for his widely used textbooks on [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) (with his 1963 Ph.D. student Elliot Alan Tanis) and on [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) (with his 1950 Ph.D. advisor Allen Thornton Craig). Hogg has received recognition for his research on [robust](wikipedia://en/2885691) and [adaptive](wikipedia://en/3509530) [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369) and for his [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) on [total quality management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_quality_management) and [statistics education](wikipedia://en/24985094).[^o5]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

### Early life {#Early_life}

Hogg was born on 8 November 1924 in [Hannibal, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal,_Missouri) and graduated from [West Rockford High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rockford_High_School) in 1942. He served three years in the [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy) from 1943 through 1946. He was in the [V-12 Navy College Training Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-12_Navy_College_Training_Program) at [Illinois Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Institute_of_Technology) in Chicago and the [Naval Reserve Officers Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Reserve_Officers_Training_Corps) at the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico).[^o8][^o9] In 1947, he graduated from the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) with a bachelor's degree in mathematics. With the goal of becoming an [actuary](wikipedia://en/43405), Hogg matriculated at the mathematics department of the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa) (then the "[State University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Iowa)").[^o10] However, Hogg studied statistics under Allen Craig, who became his mentor and helped him obtain a job teaching statistics at the Mathematics Department. Hogg earned his Ph.D. 1950 under Allen Craig. After graduating, Hogg remained at the Mathematics Department, where he remained to become a long-serving professor.[^o10]

### Basu's theorem: Special cases {#Basu's_theorem:_Special_cases}

Hogg independently discovered a special case of "[Basu's theorem](wikipedia://en/7807871)", a few years [before](wikipedia://en/682348) the publication by [Deb Basu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb_Basu).[^o13][^o14][^o15] Hogg's second paper on the topic of Basu's theorem was never published, because of a negative report by an anonymous referee in 1953.[^o16] Later, Basu refers "to Hogg and Craig (1956) for several interesting uses \[of [Basu's theorem](wikipedia://en/7807871)\] in proving results in distribution theory".[^o17]

:::

### Collaboration and friendship with Allen Craig {#Collaboration_and_friendship_with_Allen_Craig}

The textbook "Hogg and Craig" was innovative, particularly in emphasizing [sufficient statistics](wikipedia://en/140841): Sufficient statistics were treated not only for [parametric families](wikipedia://en/339174) but also for nonparametric probability distributions: In particular, the sufficiency and [completeness](wikipedia://en/194618) of the [order statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_statistics) from a [continuous](wikipedia://en/252670) [distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) were treated. Another innovation was the systematic derivation of the distributions of functions of several random variables by using the [change-of-variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_of_variable#Application_in_probability) method.[^o19]

As noted before, Craig was Hogg's mentor, helping him to obtain a teaching position while a graduate student and also supervising his thesis. Later, after Hogg had graduated, Craig became a close friend, and served as the [best man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_man) at Hogg's wedding and later as the "[godparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godparent)" to each of Hogg's four children. Indeed, Hogg's son Allen was [named](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namesake) after Craig.[^o20]

### Chairing a new Department of Statistics {#Chairing_a_new_Department_of_Statistics}

In 1965 Hogg became the founding chair of the new Department of Statistics and Actuarial Science, and he remained as the chair for nineteen years. At Iowa, Hogg held other positions, including Chair of the Quality Management and Productivity Program and the Hanson Chair of Manufacturing Productivity. After serving 51 years as an instructor at the University of Iowa, Hogg became Professor Emeritus in 2001.[^o5]

## Statistics education {#Statistics_education}

Hogg has been a leader in [statistics education](wikipedia://en/24985094) in the United States.[^o23]

Hogg has received a number of educational awards by state and national organizations: these awards include the Iowa Governor’s Science Medal for Teaching in 1990, the [Iowa Board of Regents'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Regents,_State_of_Iowa) Award for Faculty Excellence in 1992, and the Iowa Section of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) (MAA)'s Distinguished Teaching Award in 1992 with the national MAA's Distinguished Teaching Award in 1993.[^o24] At the University of Iowa, Hogg won his first teaching award after a student submitted a nomination with the title "There is a hog in my statistics book!".[^o25]

## Honors {#Honors}

Hogg has been recognized internationally as a leading researcher in statistics and as an exemplary professor of statistics who has served as a public spokesperson for the profession. Hogg has had a particularly visible role in the United States, where he was elected as President of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988) (ASA), serving in 1988. One of the ASA President's tasks is to arrange an annual meeting, and Hogg's diligence was rewarded by the ASA staff, who presented him with the name tag, "[Boss Hogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boss_Hogg)" (after the name of a character in the television series *[The Dukes of Hazzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dukes_of_Hazzard)*).[^o27] Hogg was awarded the [American Statistical Association Founders Award](wikipedia://en/81174224) in 1991.[^o28]

In 1993 Hogg received the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o29]

Hogg's 70th birthday was marked by a conference organized by the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), whose proceedings were published as a [special issue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) of *Communications in Statistics – Theory and Methods* in 1996; in the conference proceedings, a list of Hogg's publications appears on pages 2467–2481.[^o30][^o31][^o32]

For his research in [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), Hogg received the [Gottfried Noether](wikipedia://en/11071873) Senior Scholar Award in 2001.

Hogg has also been internationally active on behalf of the statistics profession. Hogg is a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), which awarded him its [Carver](wikipedia://en/8135657) Medal.[^o33] Hogg is also an Elected Member of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute).[^o5]
