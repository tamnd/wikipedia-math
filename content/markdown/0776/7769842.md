# Conference graph

*Image: Paley graph 9.svg.* The [Paley graph](wikipedia://en/5719307) of order 9, for which *v* = 9, *k* = (*v* - 1)/2 = 4, λ = (*v* - 5)/4 = 1, and μ = (*v* − 1)/4 = 2

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does there exist a conference graph for every number of vertices $v>1$ where $v \equiv 1 \bmod 4$ and $v$ is an odd sum of two squares?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Conference graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **conference graph** is a [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with parameters *v*, *k* = (*v* − 1)/2, λ = (*v* − 5)/4, and μ = (*v* − 1)/4. It is the graph associated with a symmetric [conference matrix](wikipedia://en/7770047), and consequently its order *v* must be 1 ([modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 4) and a [sum of two squares](wikipedia://en/46248976).[^o3]

:::

Conference graphs are known to exist for all small values of *v* allowed by the restrictions, e.g., *v* = 5, 9, 13, 17, 25, 29, and (the [Paley graphs](wikipedia://en/5719307)) for all prime powers congruent to 1 (modulo 4). However, there are many values of *v* that are allowed, for which the existence of a conference graph is unknown. The smallest value of *v* which has no Paley graph but *does* have a conference graph is *v* = 45, found in 1978.[^o4] The next smallest, *v* = 65, was found over 4 decades later in 2021.[^o5][^o6] As of now, the smallest open case is *v* = 85.[^o6]

The eigenvalues of a conference graph need not be integers, unlike those of other strongly regular graphs. If the graph is connected, the eigenvalues are *k* with multiplicity 1, and two other eigenvalues,

$$\frac{-1 \pm \sqrt v}{2} ,$$

each with multiplicity (*v* − 1)/2.

The [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649) of a conference graph is always a conference graph with the same parameters, and in many cases is self-complementary, such as for all the Paley graphs.
