# Distance-regular graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Distance-regular graph"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **distance-regular graph** is a [regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) such that for any two [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $v$ and $w$, the number of vertices at [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) $j$ from $v$ and at distance $k$ from $w$ depends only upon $j$, $k$, and the distance between $v$ and $w$.

:::

Some authors exclude the complete graphs and disconnected graphs from this definition.

Every [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222) is distance regular. Indeed, distance-regular graphs were introduced as a combinatorial generalization of distance-transitive graphs, having the numerical regularity properties of the latter without necessarily having a large [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/15094186).

## Intersection arrays {#Intersection_arrays}

The **intersection array** of a distance-regular graph is the array $( b_0, b_1, \ldots, b_{d-1}; c_1, \ldots, c_d )$ in which $d$ is the diameter of the graph and for each $1 \leq j \leq d$, $b_j$ gives the number of neighbours of $u$ at distance $j+1$ from $v$ and $c_j$ gives the number of neighbours of $u$ at distance $j - 1$ from $v$ for any pair of vertices $u$ and $v$ at distance $j$. There is also the number $a_j$ that gives the number of neighbours of $u$ at distance $j$ from $v$. The numbers $a_j, b_j, c_j$ are called the **intersection numbers** of the graph. They satisfy the equation $a_j + b_j + c_j = k,$ where $k = b_0$ is the [valency](wikipedia://en/1374448), i.e., the number of neighbours, of any vertex.

It turns out that a graph $G$ of diameter $d$ is distance regular if and only if it has an intersection array in the preceding sense.

## Cospectral and disconnected distance-regular graphs {#Cospectral_and_disconnected_distance-regular_graphs}

A pair of connected distance-regular graphs are [cospectral](wikipedia://en/534914#Cospectral_graphs) if their [adjacency matrices](wikipedia://en/244463) have the same [spectrum](wikipedia://en/206136). This is equivalent to their having the same intersection array.

A distance-regular graph is disconnected if and only if it is a [disjoint union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_Union) of cospectral distance-regular graphs.

## Properties {#Properties}

Suppose $G$ is a connected distance-regular graph of valency $k$ with intersection array $( b_0, b_1, \ldots, b_{d-1}; c_1, \ldots, c_d )$. For each $0 \leq j \leq d,$ let $k_j$ denote the number of vertices at distance $j$ from any given vertex and let $G_{j}$ denote the $k_{j}$-regular graph with [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463) $A_j$ formed by relating pairs of vertices on $G$ at distance $j$.

### Graph-theoretic properties {#Graph-theoretic_properties}

- $\frac{k_{j+1}}{k_{j}} = \frac{b_{j}}{c_{j+1}}$ for all $0 \leq j < d$.
- $b_0 > b_1 \geq \cdots \geq b_{d-1} > 0$ and $1 = c_1 \leq \cdots \leq c_d \leq b_0$.

### Spectral properties {#Spectral_properties}

- $G$ has $d + 1$ distinct eigenvalues.
- The only simple eigenvalue of $G$ is $k,$ or both $k$ and $-k$ if $G$ is bipartite.
- $k \leq \frac{1}{2} (m - 1)(m + 2)$ for any eigenvalue multiplicity $m > 1$ of $G,$ unless $G$ is a complete multipartite graph.
- $d \leq 3m - 4$ for any eigenvalue multiplicity $m > 1$ of $G,$ unless $G$ is a cycle graph or a complete multipartite graph.

If $G$ is [strongly regular](wikipedia://en/1399856), then $n \leq 4m - 1$ and $k \leq 2m - 1$.

### Association scheme {#Association_scheme}

The $i$-distance adjacency matrices $A_i$ for $i = 0, 1, ..., d$ of a distance-regular graph form an [association scheme](wikipedia://en/2238152).

## Classification of distance-regular graphs {#Classification_of_distance-regular_graphs}

There are only finitely many distinct connected distance-regular graphs of any given valency $k > 2$.[^o12]

Similarly, there are only finitely many distinct connected distance-regular graphs with any given eigenvalue multiplicity $m > 2$[^o13] (with the exception of the complete multipartite graphs).

### Cubic distance-regular graphs {#Cubic_distance-regular_graphs}

The [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) distance-regular graphs have been completely classified.

The 13 distinct cubic distance-regular graphs are [K<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/85816) (or [Tetrahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_graph)), [K<sub>3,3</sub>](wikipedia://en/525320), the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), the [Cubical graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubical_graph), the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870), the [Pappus graph](wikipedia://en/7250518), the [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570), the [Tutte–Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/1396880), the [Dodecahedral graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecahedral_graph), the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059), [Tutte 12-cage](wikipedia://en/24384368), the [Biggs–Smith graph](wikipedia://en/24030130), and the [Foster graph](wikipedia://en/11197065).
