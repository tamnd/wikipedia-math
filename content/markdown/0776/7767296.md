# T-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="T-theory"}

**T-theory** is a branch of [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) dealing with analysis of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) and discrete [metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_spaces).

:::

## General history {#General_history}

T-theory originated from a question raised by [Manfred Eigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Eigen) in the late 1970s. He was trying to fit twenty distinct [t-RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_RNA) [molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecule) of the *[Escherichia coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_coli)* bacterium into a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560).

An important concept of T-theory is the [tight span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_span) of a metric space. If *X* is a metric space, the tight span *T*(*X*) of *X* is, up to isomorphism, the unique minimal [injective metric space](wikipedia://en/7840768) that contains *X*. [John Isbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Isbell) was the first to discover the tight span in 1964, which he called the **injective envelope**. [Andreas Dress](wikipedia://en/57220260) independently constructed the same construct, which he called the tight span.

## Application areas {#Application_areas}

- Phylogenetic analysis, which is used to create [phylogenetic trees](wikipedia://en/149326).
- [Online algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_algorithm) - [*k*-server problem](wikipedia://en/7767038)

## Recent developments {#Recent_developments}

- [Bernd Sturmfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Sturmfels), Professor of Mathematics and Computer Science at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), and Josephine Yu classified six-point metrics using T-theory.
