# Distance-transitive graph

*Image: BiggsSmith.svg.* The [Biggs-Smith graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biggs-Smith_graph), the largest [3-regular](wikipedia://en/980508) distance-transitive graph.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Distance-transitive graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **distance-transitive graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) such that, given any two [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) $v$ and $w$ at any [distance](wikipedia://en/1020021) $i$, and any other two vertices $x$ and $y$ at the same distance, there is an [automorphism](wikipedia://en/15094186) of the graph that carries $v$ to $x$ and $w$ to $y$. Distance-transitive graphs were first defined in 1971 by [Norman L. Biggs](wikipedia://en/26991095) and D. H. Smith.

:::

A distance-transitive graph is interesting partly because it has a large [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772). Some interesting [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) are the automorphism groups of distance-transitive graphs, especially of those whose [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) is 2.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Some first examples of families of distance-transitive graphs include:

- The [Johnson graphs](wikipedia://en/2948666).
- The [Grassmann graphs](wikipedia://en/54639900).
- The [Hamming Graphs](wikipedia://en/8046549) (including [Hypercube graphs](wikipedia://en/6706815)).
- The [folded cube graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folded_cube_graph).
- The square [rook's graphs](wikipedia://en/7324959).
- The [Livingstone graph](wikipedia://en/45209715).

:::

## Relation to distance-regular graphs {#Relation_to_distance-regular_graphs}

Every distance-transitive graph is [distance-regular](wikipedia://en/7768943), but the converse is not necessarily true.

In 1969, before publication of the Biggs–Smith definition, a Russian group led by [Georgy Adelson-Velsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Adelson-Velsky) showed that there exist graphs that are distance-regular but not distance-transitive. The smallest distance-regular graph that is not distance-transitive is the [Shrikhande graph](wikipedia://en/13672415), with 16 vertices and degree 6. The only graph of this type with degree three is the 126-vertex [Tutte 12-cage](wikipedia://en/24384368). Complete lists of distance-transitive graphs are known for some degrees larger than three, but the classification of distance-transitive graphs with arbitrarily large vertex degree remains open.
