# Grand Cru (cipher)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Grand Cru"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **Grand Cru** is a [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) invented in 2000 by [Johan Borst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Borst). It was submitted to the [NESSIE](wikipedia://en/497430) project, but was not selected.

:::

Grand Cru is a 10-round [substitution–permutation network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution–permutation_network) based largely on [Rijndael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijndael) (or AES).[^o3] It replaces a number of Rijndael's unkeyed operations with key-dependent ones, in a way consistent with the security purposes of each operation. The intent is to produce a cipher at least as secure as Rijndael, and perhaps much more secure. The [block size](wikipedia://en/294065) and [key size](wikipedia://en/5749) are both 128 bits, and the [key schedule](wikipedia://en/609603) is the same as [Rijndael's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijndael_key_schedule).

Grand Cru is designed on the principle of *multiple layered security*.[^o4][^o5] It is equivalent to a chain of 4 subciphers with independent keys, such that if 3 of the keys are known, the remaining cipher should still be secure.
