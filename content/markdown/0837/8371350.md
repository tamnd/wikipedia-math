# Pierre van Moerbeke

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pierre van Moerbeke"}

**Pierre van Moerbeke** (born 1 October 1944 in [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium)) is a retired Belgian mathematician. He studies non-linear [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), with [soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) behavior. The [Volterra lattice](wikipedia://en/39362527), also called the Kac-van Moerbeke lattice, is named for him.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Van Moerbeke studied mathematics at the [Catholic University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Leuven_%281834–1968%29), where he received his degree in 1966. He then obtained a PhD in mathematics at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University), [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) (1972). Formerly a professor of mathematics at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) ([United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States)) and the Catholic University of Leuven,[^o4] he has retired to become a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at Brandeis.[^o5]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1988, he was awarded the [Francqui Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francqui_Prize) on Exact Sciences.[^o4]
