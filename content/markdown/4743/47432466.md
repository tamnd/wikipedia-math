# Michel Lazard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michel Lazard"}

*Image: MichelLazard 1987.jpg.*

**Michel Paul Lazard** (5 December 1924 – 15 September 1987) was a French mathematician who worked on the theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) in the context of [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327).

:::

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Born in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), Lazard studied at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris)–[Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), where he obtained his Ph.D. in 1954 under the direction of [Albert Châtelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Châtelet), with thesis titled *Sur les groupes nilpotents et les anneaux de Lie*. Subsequently he was a professor at the [University of Poitiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poitiers) and the [University of Paris 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Diderot_University). He died of suicide at the age of 63.

His work took on a life of its own in the hands of [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519) in the late 20th century. Quillen's discovery, that a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) Lazard used to classify [formal group laws](wikipedia://en/480335) was [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to an important ring in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), led to the subject of [chromatic homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/42245935). Lazard's self-contained treatise on one-dimensional formal groups also gave rise to the field of [*p*-divisible groups](wikipedia://en/29750969). His major contributions were:

- The classification of [*p*-adic Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_Lie_group): every *p*-adic Lie group is a closed [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of ${\rm GL}_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.
- The classification of (1-dimensional commutative) [formal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_group).
- The universal formal group law coefficient ring ([Lazard's universal ring](wikipedia://en/13949634)) is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065).
- The concept of "analyseurs", reinvented by [J. Peter May](wikipedia://en/14644098) under the name [operads](wikipedia://en/3126358).

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1958, Lazard was the first recipient of the *Prix Audin*, named after the young French mathematician [Maurice Audin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Audin), who had been assassinated in Algeria.[^o5][^o6] In 1972, he was awarded the [Prix Poncelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Poncelet) by the [Académie des Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Académie_des_Sciences) for his work on [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384).[^o7]
