# Selberg's identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Selberg's identity"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Selberg's identity** is an approximate identity involving [logarithms](wikipedia://en/17860) of [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) found by [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021). The identity forms the crucial starting point in the first [elementary proof](wikipedia://en/9318685) for the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692), arrived at jointly[^o2] by Selberg and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

There are several different but equivalent forms of Selberg's identity. One form is

$$\sum_{p<x}(\log p)^2 +\sum_{pq<x}\log p \log q = 2x\log x +O(x)$$

where the sums are over primes *p* and *q*.

:::

## Explanation {#Explanation}

The strange-looking expression on the left side of Selberg's identity is (up to smaller terms) the sum

$$\sum_{n<x} c_n$$

where the numbers

$$c_n = \Lambda(n)\log n +\sum_{d\,|\,n}\Lambda(d)\Lambda(n/d)$$

are the coefficients of the [Dirichlet series](wikipedia://en/393258)

$$\frac{\zeta^{\prime \prime}(s)}{\zeta(s)}=\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime }(s)}{\zeta(s)}\right)^\prime +\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime }(s)}{\zeta(s)}\right)^2=\sum \frac{c_n}{n^s}.$$

This [function](wikipedia://en/185427) has a [pole](wikipedia://en/81560) of order 2 at *s* = 1 with coefficient 2, which gives the dominant term 2*x* log(*x*) in the [asymptotic expansion](wikipedia://en/642090) of $\sum_{n<x} c_n.$

::: {#o7 .statement cue=named-heading name="Another variation of the identity"}

## Another variation of the identity {#Another_variation_of_the_identity}

**Selberg's identity** sometimes also refers to the following divisor sum identity involving the [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862) and the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961) when $n \geq 1$:[^o8]

$$\Lambda(n) \log(n) + \sum_{d\,|\,n} \Lambda(d) \Lambda\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right) = \sum_{d\,|\,n} \mu(d) \log^2\left(\frac{n}{d}\right).$$

This variant of Selberg's identity is proved using the concept of taking derivatives of [arithmetic functions](wikipedia://en/3170) defined by $f^{\prime}(n) = f(n) \cdot \log(n)$ in Section 2.18 of Apostol's book (see also [this link](https://artofproblemsolving.com/community/c2934t37385f2934_selberg_identity)).

:::
