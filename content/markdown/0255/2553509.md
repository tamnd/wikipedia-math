# Erwin Kreyszig

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Erwin Kreyszig"}

**Erwin Otto Kreyszig** (6 January 1922 in [Pirna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirna), Germany – 12 December 2008) was a [German Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Canadian) [applied mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematician) and the Professor of Mathematics at [Carleton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_University) in [Ottawa, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa), Canada. He was a pioneer in the field of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969): non-wave replicating [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503). He was also a distinguished author, having written the [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) *Advanced Engineering Mathematics*, the leading textbook for civil, mechanical, electrical, and chemical engineering undergraduate [engineering mathematics](wikipedia://en/5381408).

:::

Kreyszig received his PhD degree in 1949 at the [University of Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt) under the supervision of [Alwin Walther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alwin_Walther). He then continued his research activities at the universities of [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen) and [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). Prior to joining Carleton University in 1984, he held positions at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1954/1955), the [University of Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ottawa) (1955/1956), [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) (1956–1960, professor 1957) and he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz). In 1960 he became professor at the [Technical University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graz_University_of_Technology) and organized the Graz 1964 Mathematical Congress. He worked at the [University of Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Düsseldorf) (1967–1971) and at the [University of Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Karlsruhe) (1971–1973). From 1973 through 1984 he worked at the [University of Windsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Windsor) and since 1984 he had been at Carleton University. He was awarded the title of Distinguished Research Professor in 1991 in recognition of a research career during which he published 176 papers in refereed journals, and 37 in refereed conference proceedings.

Kreyszig was also an administrator, developing a Computer Centre at the [University of Graz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Graz), and at the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Düsseldorf). In 1964, he took a leave of absence from Graz to initiate a doctoral program in mathematics at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University).

Kreyszig authored 14 books, including *Advanced Engineering Mathematics*, which was published in its 10th edition in 2011. He supervised 104 master's and 22 doctoral students as well as 12 postdoctoral researchers. Together with his son he founded the *Erwin and Herbert Kreyszig Scholarship* which has funded graduate students since 2001.
