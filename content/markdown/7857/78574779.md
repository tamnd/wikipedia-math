# Iron Finance

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Iron Finance"}

**Iron Finance** was a [decentralized finance](wikipedia://en/65438324) (DeFi) protocol on the [Polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_%28blockchain%29) blockchain.[^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

Iron Finance was founded by anonymous developers as a fork of the Frax protocol.[^o5][^o6] Its early investors included [Mark Cuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Cuban).[^o5]

In June 2021, the value of the protocol's tokens collapsed after large holders began liquidating their TITAN positions at peak prices, causing both the IRON [stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stablecoin) and TITAN token to fall sharply.[^o7][^o6] The situation worsened when a [smart contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_contract) malfunction temporarily prevented holders from redeeming IRON.[^o7]

Iron Finance denied that the collapse was a [rug pull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rug_pull), stating in a blog post that it resulted from a [bank run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_run) triggered by the protocol's algorithmic design.[^o8] *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* described the event as a "wave of panic selling" after major investors sold large volumes of TITAN. The price decline exposed a design flaw that created [arbitrage](wikipedia://en/3162) opportunities, accelerating further sell-offs.[^o9]

## Tokens {#Tokens}

IRON was an [algorithmic stablecoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_stablecoin) backed by 75 percent USDC and 25 percent TITAN. The Target Collateral Ratio (TCR) determined the proportion of USDC required to mint \$1 worth of IRON, with the remainder supplied by TITAN at market value.[^o3] Users could redeem IRON for USDC and TITAN based on the Effective Collateral Ratio (ECR), which reflected the proportion of USDC per \$1 of IRON redeemed. Both ratios adjusted based on IRON's price relative to its \$1 peg and the protocol's USDC reserves.[^o3]

TITAN was the protocol's native token and had no governance or ownership rights. It was required for minting IRON and could be staked in liquidity pools to facilitate trading.[^o3]

The protocol's partial-collateral model contributed to its failure.[^o11] A rapid increase in TITAN's price prompted large holders to sell, which triggered a [negative feedback loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_feedback_loop) known as a "death spiral."[^o11] TITAN's value fell to near zero, and IRON lost its dollar peg. The collapse resulted in an estimated \$2 billion in losses and is widely cited as the first major DeFi "bank run."[^o11][^o13]

## Liquidity pools {#Liquidity_pools}

Iron Finance operated four liquidity pools across three pairs: USDC–IRON (two pools), TITAN–IRON, and TITAN–MATIC.[^o3][^o13] Liquidity providers earned TITAN as incentives.[^o3]

## Tokenomics {#Tokenomics}

Iron Finance planned a total supply of 1 billion TITAN tokens. Of these, 700 million were allocated as rewards over 36 months, and 300 million were reserved for protocol sponsors, vesting linearly over 12 months.[^o3]
