# Discrete spline interpolation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Discrete spline interpolation"}

In the mathematical field of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), **discrete spline interpolation** is a form of [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) where the [interpolant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpolant) is a special type of [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise) [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) called a discrete spline. A discrete spline is a piecewise polynomial such that its [central differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_difference) are [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) at the knots whereas a [spline](wikipedia://en/457680) is a piecewise polynomial such that its [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) are continuous at the knots. Discrete cubic splines are discrete splines where the central differences of orders 0, 1, and 2 are required to be continuous.[^o2]

:::

Discrete splines were introduced by Mangasarin and Schumaker in 1971 as solutions of certain minimization problems involving differences.[^o3]

## Discrete cubic splines {#Discrete_cubic_splines}

Let *x*<sub>1</sub>, *x*<sub>2</sub>, . . ., *x*<sub>*n*-1</sub> be an increasing sequence of real numbers. Let *g*(*x*) be a piecewise polynomial defined by

$$g(x)= \begin{cases} g_1(x) & x<x_1 \\ g_i(x) & x_{i-1}\le x < x_i \text{ for } i = 2,3, \ldots, n-1\\ g_n(x) & x\ge x_{n-1} \end{cases}$$

where *g*<sub>1</sub>(*x*), . . ., *g*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) are polynomials of degree 3. Let *h* > 0. If

$$(g_{i+1}-g_i)(x_i +jh)=0 \text{ for } j=-1,0,1 \text{ and } i=1,2,\ldots, n-1$$

then *g*(*x*) is called a discrete cubic spline.[^o2]

### Alternative formulation 1 {#Alternative_formulation_1}

The conditions defining a discrete cubic spline are equivalent to the following:

$$g_{i+1}(x_i-h) = g_i(x_i-h)$$

$$g_{i+1}(x_i) = g_i(x_i)$$

$$g_{i+1}(x_i+h) = g_i(x_i+h)$$

### Alternative formulation 2 {#Alternative_formulation_2}

The central differences of orders 0, 1, and 2 of a function *f*(*x*) are defined as follows:

$$D^{(0)}f(x) = f(x)$$

$$D^{(1)}f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x-h)}{2h}$$

$$D^{(2)}f(x)=\frac{f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)}{h^2}$$

The conditions defining a discrete cubic spline are also equivalent to[^o2]

$$D^{(j)}g_{i+1}(x_i)=D^{(j)}g_i(x_i) \text{ for } j=0,1,2 \text{ and } i=1,2, \ldots, n-1.$$

This states that the central differences $D^{(j)}g(x)$ are continuous at *x*<sub>*i*</sub>.

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

### Example {#Example}

Let *x*<sub>1</sub> = 1 and *x*<sub>2</sub> = 2 so that *n* = 3. The following function defines a discrete cubic spline:[^o2]

$$g(x) = \begin{cases} x^3 & x<1 \\ x^3 - 2(x-1)((x-1)^2-h^2) & 1\le x < 2\\ x^3 - 2(x-1)((x-1)^2-h^2)+(x-2)((x-2)^2-h^2) & x \ge 2 \end{cases}$$

:::

## Discrete cubic spline interpolant {#Discrete_cubic_spline_interpolant}

Let *x*<sub>0</sub> \< *x*<sub>1</sub> and *x*<sub>*n*</sub> > *x*<sub>*n*-1</sub> and *f*(*x*) be a function defined in the closed interval \[*x*<sub>0</sub> - h, *x*<sub>*n*</sub> + h\]. Then there is a unique cubic discrete spline *g*(*x*) satisfying the following conditions:

$$g(x_i) = f(x_i) \text{ for } i=0,1,\ldots, n.$$

$$D^{(1)}g_1(x_0) = D^{(1)}f(x_0).$$

$$D^{(1)}g_n(x_n) = D^{(1)}f(x_n).$$

This unique discrete cubic spline is the discrete spline interpolant to *f*(*x*) in the interval \[*x*<sub>0</sub> - h, *x*<sub>*n*</sub> + h\]. This interpolant agrees with the values of *f*(*x*) at *x*<sub>0</sub>, *x*<sub>1</sub>, . . ., *x*<sub>n</sub>.

::: {#o11 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- Discrete cubic splines were originally introduced as solutions of certain minimization problems.[^o2][^o3]
- They have applications in computing nonlinear splines.[^o2][^o12]
- They are used to obtain approximate solution of a second order boundary value problem.[^o13]
- Discrete interpolatory splines have been used to construct biorthogonal wavelets.[^o14]

:::
