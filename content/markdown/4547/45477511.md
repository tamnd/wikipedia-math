# Nicolae Culianu

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicolae Culianu"}

*Image: Neculai Culianu.jpg.* Nicolae Culianu

**Nicolae Culianu** (28 August 1832 – 28 November 1915) was a [Moldavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia), later [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) mathematician and astronomer.

:::

A native of [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași), he enrolled in the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) after graduating from *[Academia Mihăileană](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Mihăileană)* in 1855, and earned his undergraduate degree in mathematics in 1860. He remained there until 1863, performing research at the [Paris Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Observatory).[^o2] Initially a high school teacher,[^o3] he later joined the astronomy and geodesy faculty of the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University),[^o2][^o6] where he served as dean of the sciences faculty, and from 1880 to 1898 as [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29).[^o6][^o4] He was a close associate of [Titu Maiorescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titu_Maiorescu), a member of the *[Junimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junimea)* movement that the latter led, and involved in the educational reform movement it promoted. As such, he was among the founders of a private high school in Iași, to which he donated a group of buildings.[^o6] While active in *Junimea*, his renowned affability and venerable bearing earned him the nickname "Papa Culiano".[^o3] He was elected a corresponding [member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) in 1889.[^o5]

He helped found the Iași astronomical observatory for the use of students and teachers, and published textbooks on mathematics and geodesy.[^o6] He was among the founders *Recreații Științifice*, the country's first scientific periodical addressed to young people and to a generalist audience.[^o2][^o4] Briefly involved in politics, he was vice president of the [Romanian Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Senate) during the fourth [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_%28Romania,_1880–1918%29) government of [Lascăr Catargiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lascăr_Catargiu) (1892–1896).[^o2][^o6]

Culianu's textbooks include an 1870 one on differential and integral calculus, the first published Romanian-language course on mathematical analysis; and ones on elementary algebra (1872), applied geometry (1874), plane and spherical trigonometry (1875), cosmography (1893), plane trigonometry (1894), and high-school cosmography (1895).[^o2] He was buried in [Eternitatea Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternitatea_cemetery).[^o7] His great-grandson was [Ioan Petru Culianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioan_Petru_Culianu).[^o6]
