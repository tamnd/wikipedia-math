# Steinmetz solid

*Image: Steinmetz-solid.svg.* Bicylinder

*Image: Tricylinder.png.* Tricylinder

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Steinmetz solid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **Steinmetz solid** is the [solid body](wikipedia://en/507960) obtained as the [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) of two or three [cylinders](wikipedia://en/859283) of equal radius [at right angles](wikipedia://en/76944). Each of the curves of the intersection of two cylinders is an ellipse.

:::

The intersection of two cylinders is called a **bicylinder**. [Topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically), it is equivalent to a square [hosohedron](wikipedia://en/2316192). The intersection of three cylinders is called a **tricylinder**. A [bisected](wikipedia://en/152547) bicylinder is called a **vault**,[^o7] and a [cloister vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloister_vault) in architecture has this shape.

Steinmetz solids are named after mathematician [Charles Proteus Steinmetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Proteus_Steinmetz),[^o2] who solved the problem of determining the volume of the intersection. However, the same problem had been solved earlier, by [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) in the ancient Greek world,[^o5][^o3] [Zu Chongzhi](wikipedia://en/151762) in ancient China,[^o4] and [Piero della Francesca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_della_Francesca) in the early Italian Renaissance.[^o5] They appear prominently in the sculptures of [Frank Smullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Smullin).

## Bicylinder {#Bicylinder}

*Image: Steinmetz-cc.svg.* The generation of a bicylinder

*Image: Steinmetz-cc2-ag.svg.* Calculating the volume of a bicylinder

A bicylinder generated by two cylinders with radius $r$ has the volume

$$V = \frac{16}{3} r^3,$$

and the surface area[^o8]

$$A = 16 r^2.$$

The upper half of a bicylinder is the square case of a domical vault, a dome-shaped solid based on any convex polygon whose cross-sections are similar copies of the polygon, and analogous formulas calculating the volume and surface area of a domical vault as a rational multiple of the volume and surface area of its enclosing [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) hold more generally.[^o9] In China, the bicylinder is known as *móu hé fāng gài* (牟合方蓋), literally "two square umbrella"; it was described by the third-century mathematician [Liu Hui](wikipedia://en/202719).[^o10]

::: {#o12 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of the volume formula"}

### Proof of the volume formula {#Proof_of_the_volume_formula}

For deriving the volume formula it is convenient to use the common idea for calculating the [volume of a sphere](wikipedia://en/32498#Formula_derivations): collecting thin cylindric slices. In this case the thin slices are [square cuboids](wikipedia://en/38848329) (see diagram). This leads to

$$\begin{aligned} V &= \int_{-r}^{r} (2x)^2 \ \mathrm{d}z \\[2pt] &= 4\cdot \int_{-r}^{r} x^2 \ \mathrm{d}z \\[2pt] &= 4\cdot \int_{-r}^{r} (r^2-z^2) \ \mathrm{d}z \\[2pt] &= \frac{16}{3} r^3. \end{aligned}$$

It is [well known](wikipedia://en/27859#Enclosed_volume) that the relations of the volumes of a right circular cone, one half of a sphere and a right circular cylinder with same radii and heights are $1 : 2 : 3$. For one half of a bicylinder a similar statement is true:

- The relations of the volumes of the inscribed square pyramid $(a=2r,\ h=r,\ V=\tfrac{4}{3}r^3),$ the half bicylinder $(V=\tfrac{8}{3} r^3)$ and the surrounding squared cuboid $(a= 2r,\ h=r,\ V=4r^3)$ are $1 : 2 : 3$: $$\begin{array}{ccccc} \frac{4}{3}r^3 &:& \frac{8}{3}r^3 &:& 4r^3 \\[2pt] 1 &:& 2 &:& 3 \end{array}$$

#### Using multivariable calculus {#Using_multivariable_calculus}

Consider the equations of the cylinders:

$$\begin{aligned} x^2+z^2 &= r^2 \\ x^2+y^2 &= r^2 \end{aligned}$$

The volume will be given by:

$$V = \iiint_V \mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x$$

With the limits of integration:

$$\begin{array}{rcccl} -\sqrt{r^2-x^2} &\leqslant& z &\leqslant& \sqrt{r^2-x^2} \\[4pt] -\sqrt{r^2-x^2} &\leqslant& y &\leqslant& \sqrt{r^2-x^2} \\[4pt] -r &\leqslant& x &\leqslant& r \end{array}$$

Substituting, we have:

$$\begin{aligned} V &= \int_{-r}^{r}\int_{-\sqrt{r^2-x^2}}^{\sqrt{r^2-x^2}}\int_{-\sqrt{r^2-x^2}}^{\sqrt{r^2-x^2}} \mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x \\[2pt] &= 8r^3-\frac{8r^3}{3} \\[2pt] &= \frac{16r^3}{3} \end{aligned}$$

:::

::: {#o15 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of the area formula"}

### Proof of the area formula {#Proof_of_the_area_formula}

The surface area consists of two red and two blue cylindrical biangles. One red biangle is cut into halves by the $yz$-plane and developed into the plane such that half circle (intersection with the $yz$-plane) is developed onto the positive $\xi$-axis and the development of the biangle is bounded upwards by the sine arc $\eta=r\sin\tfrac{\xi}{r}, \ 0\le\xi\le\pi r.$ Hence the area of this development is

*Image: Klostergew.svg.* cloister vault

$$B = \int_{0}^{\pi r} r\sin\frac{\xi}{r} \ \mathrm{d}\xi = r^2\cos{0}-r^2\cos{\pi} = 2r^2$$

and the total surface area is:

$$A = 8B=16r^2.$$

:::

::: {#o17 .proof cue=named-heading name="Alternate proof of the volume formula"}

### Alternate proof of the volume formula {#Alternate_proof_of_the_volume_formula}

To derive the volume of a bicylinder (white), one can enclose it within a cube (red). When a plane, parallel to the axes of the cylinders, intersects the bicylinder, it forms a square. This plane's intersection with the cube results in a larger square. The area difference between these two squares corresponds to four smaller squares (blue). As the plane traverses through the solids, these blue squares form square pyramids with isosceles faces at the cube's corners. The apexes of these pyramids are located at the midpoints of the cube's four edges. Moving the plane through the entire bicylinder results in a total of eight pyramids.

The volume of the cube (red) minus the volume of the eight pyramids (blue) is the volume of the bicylinder (white). The [volume of the 8 pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045#Mensuration) is:

$$8 \times \frac{1}{3} r^2 \times r = \frac{8}{3} r^3,$$

and then we can calculate that the bicylinder volume is

$$(2 r)^3 - \frac{8}{3} r^3 = \frac{16}{3} r^3.$$

:::

## Tricylinder {#Tricylinder}

*Image: Steinmetz-ccc.svg.* Generating the surface of a tricylinder: At first two cylinders (red, blue) are cut. The so generated bicylinder is cut by the third (green) cylinder.

The intersection of three cylinders with perpendicularly intersecting axes generates a surface of a solid with vertices where 3 edges meet and vertices where 4 edges meet. The set of vertices can be considered as the edges of a [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631). The key for the determination of volume and surface area is the observation that the tricylinder can be resampled by the cube with the vertices where 3 edges meet (s. diagram) and 6 curved pyramids (the triangles are parts of cylinder surfaces). Similar considerations can determine the volume and the surface area of the curved triangles as it is done for the bicylinder above.[^o7][^o8]

The volume of a tricylinder is

$$V = 8(2 - \sqrt{2}) r^3$$

and the surface area is

$$A = 24(2 - \sqrt{2}) r^2.$$

## More cylinders {#More_cylinders}

With four cylinders, with axes connecting the vertices of a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) to the corresponding points on the other side of the solid, the volume is[^o7][^o8]

$$V_4 = 12 \left( 2\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6} \right) r^3 \,$$

With six cylinders, with axes parallel to the diagonals of the faces of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), the volume is:[^o7][^o8]

$$V_6 = \frac{16}{3} \left( 3 + 2\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{2} \right) r^3 \,$$
