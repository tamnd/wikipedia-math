# Cantelli's inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cantelli's inequality"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Cantelli's inequality** (also called the **Chebyshev-Cantelli inequality** and the **one-sided Chebyshev inequality**) is an improved version of [Chebyshev's inequality](wikipedia://en/156533) for one-sided tail bounds.[^o2][^o3][^o4] The inequality states that, for $\lambda > 0,$

:::

$$\Pr(X-\mathbb{E}[X]\ge\lambda) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2 + \lambda^2},$$

where

$X$ is a real-valued [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685),

$\Pr$ is the [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437),

$\mathbb{E}[X]$ is the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of $X$,

$\sigma^2$ is the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of $X$.

Applying the Cantelli inequality to $-X$ gives a bound on the lower tail,

$$\Pr(X-\mathbb{E}[X]\le -\lambda) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma^2 + \lambda^2}.$$

While the inequality is often attributed to [Francesco Paolo Cantelli](wikipedia://en/3166906) who published it in 1928,[^o5] it originates in Chebyshev's work of 1874.[^o6] When bounding the event random variable deviates from its [mean](wikipedia://en/9653) in only one direction (positive or negative), Cantelli's inequality gives an improvement over Chebyshev's inequality. The Chebyshev inequality has ["higher moments versions"](wikipedia://en/156533#Higher_moments) and ["vector versions"](wikipedia://en/156533#Vector_version), and so does the Cantelli inequality.

::: {#o8 .statement cue=named-heading name="Comparison to Chebyshev's inequality"}

## Comparison to Chebyshev's inequality {#Comparison_to_Chebyshev's_inequality}

For one-sided tail bounds, Cantelli's inequality is better, since Chebyshev's inequality can only get

$$\Pr(X - \mathbb{E}[X] \geq \lambda) \leq \Pr(|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\ge\lambda) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\lambda^2}.$$

On the other hand, for two-sided tail bounds, Cantelli's inequality gives

$$\Pr(|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\ge\lambda) = \Pr(X-\mathbb{E}[X]\ge\lambda) + \Pr(X-\mathbb{E}[X]\le-\lambda) \le \frac{2\sigma^2}{\sigma^2 + \lambda^2},$$

which is always worse than Chebyshev's inequality (when $\lambda \geq \sigma$; otherwise, both inequalities bound a probability by a value greater than one, and so are trivial).

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Various stronger inequalities can be shown. He, Zhang, and Zhang showed[^o11] (Corollary 2.3) when $\mathbb{E}[X]=0,\,\mathbb{E}[X^2]=1$ and $\lambda\ge0$:

$$\Pr(X\ge\lambda) \le 1- (2\sqrt{3}-3)\frac{(1+\lambda^2)^2}{\mathbb{E}[X^4]+6\lambda^2+\lambda^4}.$$

In the case $\lambda=0$ this matches a bound in Berger's "The Fourth Moment Method",[^o12]

$$\Pr(X\ge 0) \ge \frac{2\sqrt{3}-3}{\mathbb{E}[X^4]}.$$

This improves over Cantelli's inequality in that we can get a non-zero lower bound, even when $\mathbb{E}[X]=0$.

:::
