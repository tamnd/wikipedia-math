# Stanisław Leśniewski

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stanisław Leśniewski"}

**Stanisław Leśniewski** (; 30 March 1886 – 13 May 1939) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) and [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician). A professor of mathematics at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), he was a leading representative of the [Lwów–Warsaw School of Logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_school_of_logic) and is known for coining and introducing the concept of [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380) as part of a comprehensive framework for logic and mathematics.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Leśniewski was born on 28 March 1886 at [Serpukhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhov), near [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), to father Izydor, an engineer working on the construction of the [Trans-Siberian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Siberian_Railway), and mother Helena (*née* Palczewska).[^o4] Leśniewski went to a high school in [Irkutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irkutsk). Later he attended lectures by [Hans Cornelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Cornelius) at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich)[^o5] and lectures by [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) at [Lviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lviv_University).[^o6]

Leśniewski belonged to the first generation of the [Lwów–Warsaw School of logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lwów–Warsaw_school_of_logic) founded by [Kazimierz Twardowski](wikipedia://en/1022980). Together with [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) and [Jan Łukasiewicz](wikipedia://en/305064), he formed a trio which made the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), during the [interbellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbellum), perhaps the most important research center in the world for [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic).[^o7]

His main contribution was the construction of three nested [formal systems](wikipedia://en/396102), to which he gave the Greek-derived names of protothetic, [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) ("Calculus of names" is sometimes used instead of ontology, a term widely employed in [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) in a very different sense), and [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380),[^o8] which, in its first, 1916, version introduced "the notion of *collective class*, a concrete notion of class elaborated by Leśniewski directly against [Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216)’s sets, [Frege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frege)’s extensions of concepts and [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)’s and [Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395)’s classes as incomplete symbols."[^o9]

A good textbook presentation of these systems is that by [Simons](wikipedia://en/1569373) (1987), who compares and contrasts them with the variants of [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380), more popular nowadays, descending from the *calculus of individuals* of Leonard and Goodman. Simons clarifies something that is very difficult to determine by reading Leśniewski and his students, namely that Polish mereology is a [first-order theory](wikipedia://en/10983) equivalent to what is now called classical extensional [mereology](wikipedia://en/375380) ([modulo](wikipedia://en/1352428) choice of language).

While he did publish a fair body of work (Leśniewski, 1992, is his collected works in English translation), some of it in German, the leading language for mathematics of his day, his writings had limited impact because of their enigmatic style and highly idiosyncratic notation. Leśniewski was also a radical [nominalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominalist): he rejected [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) at a time when that theory was in full flower. He pointed to [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095) and the like in support of his rejection, and devised his three formal systems as a concrete alternative to set theory. Even though [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) was his sole doctoral pupil, Leśniewski nevertheless strongly influenced an entire generation of Polish logicians and mathematicians via his teaching at the University of Warsaw. It is mainly thanks to the writings of his students (e.g., Srzednicki and Rickey 1984) that Leśniewski's thought is known.[^o10]

During the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Soviet_War) of 1919-21, Leśniewski served the cause of Poland's independence by breaking [Soviet Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR) [ciphers](wikipedia://en/5244) for the [Polish General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_General_Staff)'s [Cipher Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biuro_Szyfrów).[^o11]

Leśniewski died suddenly of cancer, shortly before the German [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland), which resulted in the destruction of his *[Nachlass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlass)*. He was buried at Warsaw's [Powązki Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powązki_Cemetery).[^o12]
