# Primorial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Primorial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more particularly in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **primorial**, denoted by "$p_{n}\#$", is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) from [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) to natural numbers similar to the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) function, but rather than successively multiplying positive integers, the function only multiplies [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).

:::

The name "primorial", coined by [Harvey Dubner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Dubner), draws an analogy to *primes* similar to the way the name "factorial" relates to *factors*.

## Definition for prime numbers {#Definition_for_prime_numbers}

*Image: Primorial pn plot.png.* $p_{n}\#$ as a function of $n$, plotted logarithmically.

The primorial $p_n\#$ is defined as the product of the first $n$ primes:

$$p_n\# = \prod_{k=1}^n p_k,$$

where $p_k$ is the $k$th prime number. For instance, $p_5\#$ signifies the product of the first 5 primes:

$$p_5\# = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11= 2310.$$

The first few primorials $p_n\#$ are:

[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_%28number%29), [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_%28number%29), [30](wikipedia://en/341668), [210](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/210_%28number%29), [2310](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2310_%28number%29), 30030, 510510, 9699690... .

Asymptotically, primorials grow according to[^o4]

$$p_n\# = e^{(1 + o(1)) n \log n}.$$

## Definition for natural numbers {#Definition_for_natural_numbers}

*Image: Primorial n plot.png.* $n!$ (yellow) as a function of $n$, compared to $n\#$ (red), plotted logarithmically.

In general, for a positive integer $n$, its primorial $n\#$ is the product of all primes less than or equal to $n$; that is,[^o3]

$$n\# = \prod_{p\,\leq\, n\atop p\,\text{prime}} p = \prod_{i=1}^{\pi(n)} p_i = p_{\pi(n)}\#,$$

where $\pi(n)$ is the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826) . This is equivalent to

$$n\# = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }n = 0,\ 1 \\ (n-1)\# \times n & \text{if } n \text{ is prime} \\ (n-1)\# & \text{if } n \text{ is composite}. \end{cases}$$

For example, $12\#$ represents the product of all primes no greater than $12$:

$$12\# = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11= 2310.$$

Since $\pi(12)=5$, this can be calculated as:

$$12\# = p_{\pi(12)}\# = p_5\# = 2310.$$

Consider the first 12 values of the sequence $n\#$:

$$1, 2, 6, 6, 30, 30, 210, 210, 210, 210, 2310, 2310.$$

We see that for [composite](wikipedia://en/82289) $n$, every term $n\#$ is equal to the preceding term $(n-1)\#$. In the above example we have $12\# = p_5\# = 11\#$ since $12$ is composite.

Primorials are related to the first [Chebyshev function](wikipedia://en/5793598) $\vartheta(n)$ by

$$\ln (n\#) = \vartheta(n).$$

Since $\vartheta(n)$ asymptotically approaches $n$ for large values of $n$, primorials therefore grow according to:

$$n\# = e^{(1+o(1))n}.$$

## Properties {#Properties}

- For any $n, p \in \mathbb{N}$, $n\#=p\#$ [iff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iff) $p$ is the largest prime such that $p\leq n$.
- Let $p_k$ be the $k$th prime. Then $p_k\#$ has exactly $2^k$ divisors.
- The sum of the reciprocal values of the primorial [converges](wikipedia://en/1743431) towards a constant

$$\sum_{p\,\text{prime}} {1 \over p\#} = {1 \over 2} + {1 \over 6} + {1 \over 30} + \ldots = 0{.}7052301717918\ldots$$

The [Engel expansion](wikipedia://en/2967256) of this number results in the sequence of the prime numbers. Griffiths (2015) proved that it is irrational.[^o10]

- Euclid's proof of his [theorem on the infinitude of primes](wikipedia://en/3143591) can be paraphrased by saying that, for any prime $p$, the number $p\# +1$ has a prime divisor not contained in the set of primes less than or equal to $p$.
- $\lim_{n \to \infty}\sqrt[n]{n\#} = e$. For $n<10^{11}$, the values are smaller than $e$,[^o11] but for larger $n$, the values of the function exceed $e$ and oscillate infinitely around $e$ later on.
- Since the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) $\tbinom{2n}{n}$ is divisible by every prime between $n+1$ and $2n$, and since $\tbinom{2n}{n} \leq 4^{n}$, we have the following upper bound:[^o12] $n\#\leq 4^n$.
  - Using elementary methods, Denis Hanson showed that $n\#\leq 3^n$.[^o13]
  - Using more advanced methods, Rosser and Schoenfeld showed that $n\#\leq (2.763)^n$.[^o14] Furthermore, they showed that for $n \ge 563$, $n\#\geq (2.22)^n$.[^o14]

## Applications {#Applications}

Primorials play a role in the search for [prime numbers in additive arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/11740178). For instance, $2 236 133 941+23\#$ results in a prime, beginning a sequence of thirteen primes found by repeatedly adding $23\#$, and ending with $5136341251$. $23\#$ is also the common difference in arithmetic progressions of fifteen and sixteen primes.

Every [highly composite number](wikipedia://en/208732) is a product of primorials.

Primorials are all [square-free integers](wikipedia://en/29525), and each one has more distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) than any number smaller than it. For each primorial $n$, the fraction $\varphi(n)/n$ is smaller than for any positive integer less than $n$, where $\varphi$ is the [Euler totient function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_totient_function).

Any [completely multiplicative function](wikipedia://en/1055940) is defined by its values at primorials, since it is defined by its values at primes, which can be recovered by division of adjacent values.

Base systems corresponding to primorials (such as base 30, not to be confused with the [primorial number system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_Radix#Primorial_number_system)) have a lower proportion of [repeating fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repeating_fraction) than any smaller base.

Every primorial is a [sparsely totient number](wikipedia://en/2011159).[^o18]

## Compositorial {#Compositorial}

The $n$-compositorial of a [composite number](wikipedia://en/82289) $n$ is the product of all composite numbers up to and including $n$.[^o21] The $n$-compositorial is equal to the $n$-[factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) divided by the primorial $n\#$. The compositorials are

[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_%28number%29), [24](wikipedia://en/362201), [192](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/192_%28number%29), [1728](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1728_%28number%29), 17280, 207360, 2903040, 43545600, 696729600, ...

:::

## Riemann zeta function {#Riemann_zeta_function}

The [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) at positive integers greater than one can be expressed[^o30] by using the primorial function and [Jordan's totient function](wikipedia://en/19486387) $J_k$:

$$\zeta(k)=\frac{2^k}{2^k-1}+\sum_{r=2}^\infty\frac{(p_{r-1}\#)^k}{J_k(p_r\#)},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}.$$
