# Edge of chaos

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Edge of chaos"}

The **edge of chaos** is a transition space between order and [disorder](wikipedia://en/19196523) that is hypothesized to exist within a wide variety of systems. This transition zone is a region of bounded instability that engenders a constant dynamic interplay between order and disorder.[^o3]

:::

Even though the idea of the edge of chaos is an abstract one, it has many applications in such fields as ecology,[^o4] business management,[^o5] psychology,[^o6] [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), and other domains of the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science). Physicists have shown that adaptation to the edge of chaos occurs in almost all systems with feedback.[^o7]

## History {#History}

The phrase *edge of chaos* was coined in the late 1980s by [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295) physicist [Norman Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Packard).[^o9][^o10] In the next decade, Packard and mathematician [Doyne Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doyne_Farmer) co-authored many papers on understanding how self-organization and order emerges at the edge of chaos.[^o9] One of the original catalysts that led to the idea of the edge of chaos were the experiments with [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata) done by [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) [Christopher Langton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Langton) where a transition phenomenon was discovered.[^o11][^o12][^o13] The phrase refers to an area in the range of a [variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_%28programming%29), λ (lambda), which was varied while examining the behaviour of a [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) (CA). As λ varied, the behaviour of the CA went through a [phase transition](wikipedia://en/54423) of behaviours. Langton found a small area conducive to produce CAs capable of [universal computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_computation).[^o12][^o11][^o14] At around the same time physicist [James P. Crutchfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Crutchfield) and others used the phrase *onset of chaos* to describe more or less the same concept.[^o15]

In the sciences in general, the phrase has come to refer to a metaphor that some physical, biological, economic and social systems operate in a region between order and either complete [randomness](wikipedia://en/19196523) or [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295), where the [complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complexity) is maximal.[^o16][^o17] The generality and significance of the idea, however, has since been called into question by [Melanie Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanie_Mitchell) and others.[^o18]

[Stuart Kauffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Kauffman) has studied [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590) of evolving systems in which the rate of evolution is maximized near the edge of chaos.[^o19]

## Adaptation {#Adaptation}

[Adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation) plays a vital role for all living organisms and systems. All of them are constantly changing their inner properties to better fit in the current environment.[^o21] The most important instruments for the [adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation) are the [self-adjusting parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_systems) inherent for many natural systems. The prominent feature of systems with self-adjusting parameters is an ability to avoid [chaos](wikipedia://en/6295). The name for this phenomenon is *"Adaptation to the edge of chaos"*.

Adaptation to the edge of chaos refers to the idea that many [complex adaptive systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_adaptive_systems) (CASs) seem to intuitively evolve toward a regime near the boundary between chaos and order.[^o22] Physics has shown that edge of chaos is the optimal settings for control of a system.[^o23] It is also an optional setting that can influence the ability of a physical system to perform primitive functions for computation.[^o24] In CAS, [coevolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevolution) generally occurs near the edge of chaos, and a balance should be maintained between flexibility and stability to avoid structural failure.[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28] As a response to coping with turbulent environments, CAS bring out flexibility, creativity,[^o29] [agility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agility), [anti-fragility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fragility), and innovation near the edge of chaos, provided these systems are sufficiently decentralized and non-hierarchical.[^o27][^o26][^o25]

Because of the importance of adaptation in many natural systems, adaptation to the edge of the chaos takes a prominent position in many scientific researches. Physicists demonstrated that adaptation to state at the boundary of chaos and order occurs in population of [cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automata) rules which optimize the performance evolving with a [genetic algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_algorithm).[^o30][^o31] Another example of this phenomenon is the [self-organized criticality](wikipedia://en/718855) in [avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalanche) and earthquake models.[^o32]

The simplest model for chaotic dynamics is the [logistic map](wikipedia://en/18137). Self-adjusting logistic map dynamics exhibit adaptation to the edge of chaos.[^o33] Theoretical analysis allowed prediction of the location of the narrow parameter regime near the boundary to which the system evolves.[^o34]
