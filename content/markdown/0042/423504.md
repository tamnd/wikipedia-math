# Dirichlet boundary condition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dirichlet boundary condition"}

In mathematics, the **Dirichlet** **boundary condition** is imposed on an [ordinary](wikipedia://en/32742753) or [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564), such that the values that the solution takes along the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the domain are fixed. The question of finding solutions to such equations is known as the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109). In the sciences and engineering, a Dirichlet boundary condition may also be referred to as a **fixed boundary condition** or **boundary condition of the first type**. It is named after [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) (1805–1859).[^o2]

:::

In [finite-element analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite-element_analysis), the *essential* or Dirichlet boundary condition is defined by weighted-integral form of a differential equation.[^o3] The dependent unknown *u in the same form as the weight function w* appearing in the boundary expression is termed a *primary variable*, and its specification constitutes the *essential* or Dirichlet boundary condition.

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### ODE {#ODE}

For an [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753), for instance,

$$y'' + y = 0,$$

the Dirichlet boundary conditions on the interval $[a,b]$ take the form

$$y(a) = \alpha, \quad y(b) = \beta,$$

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are given numbers.

### PDE {#PDE}

For a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564), for example,

$$\nabla^2 y + y = 0,$$

where $\nabla^2$ denotes the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706), the Dirichlet boundary conditions on a domain $\Omega \subset \mathbf{R}^{n}$ take the form

$$y(x) = f(x) \quad \forall x \in \partial\Omega,$$

where $f$ is a known [function](wikipedia://en/185427) defined on the boundary $\partial\Omega$.

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

### Applications {#Applications}

For example, the following would be considered Dirichlet boundary conditions:

- In [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) and [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) ([beam theory](wikipedia://en/2064935#Boundary_considerations)), where one end of a beam is held at a fixed position in space.
- In [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726), where a surface is held at a fixed temperature.
- In [electrostatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatics), where a node of a circuit is held at a fixed voltage.
- In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), the [no-slip condition](wikipedia://en/3855724) for viscous fluids states that at a solid boundary, the fluid will have zero velocity relative to the boundary.

## Other boundary conditions {#Other_boundary_conditions}

Many other boundary conditions are possible, including the [Cauchy boundary condition](wikipedia://en/1725025) and the [mixed boundary condition](wikipedia://en/2784886). The latter is a combination of the Dirichlet and [Neumann](wikipedia://en/735443) conditions.
