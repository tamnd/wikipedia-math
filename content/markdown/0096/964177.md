# Maurer–Cartan form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maurer–Cartan form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Maurer–Cartan form** for a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ is a distinguished [differential one-form](wikipedia://en/221519) on $G$ that carries the basic [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) information about the structure of $G$. It was much used by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) as a basic ingredient of his [method of moving frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_moving_frames), and bears his name together with that of [Ludwig Maurer](wikipedia://en/17155880).

:::

As a one-form, the Maurer–Cartan form is peculiar in that it takes its values in the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) associated to the Lie group $G$. The Lie algebra is identified with the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) of $G$ at the identity, denoted $T_{e}G$. The Maurer–Cartan form $\omega$ is thus a one-form defined globally on $G$, that is, a linear mapping of the tangent space $T_{g}G$ at each $g \in G$ into $T_{e}G$. It is given as the [pushforward](wikipedia://en/1558510) of a vector in $T_{g}G$ along the left-translation in the group:

$$\omega(v) = (L_{g^{-1}})_* v,\quad v\in T_gG.$$

## Motivation and interpretation {#Motivation_and_interpretation}

A Lie group acts on itself by multiplication under the mapping

$$G\times G \ni (g,h) \mapsto gh \in G.$$

A question of importance to Cartan and his contemporaries was how to identify a [principal homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363441) of $G$. That is, a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) $P$ identical to the group $G$, but without a fixed choice of unit element. This motivation came, in part, from [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887)'s [Erlangen programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen_programme) where one was interested in a notion of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) on a space, where the symmetries of the space were [transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group) forming a Lie group. The geometries of interest were [homogeneous spaces](wikipedia://en/363325) $G/H$, but usually without a fixed choice of origin corresponding to the [coset](wikipedia://en/98759) $eH$.

A principal homogeneous space of $G$ is a manifold $P$ abstractly characterized by having a [free and transitive action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of $G$ on $P$. The **Maurer–Cartan form**[^o3] gives an appropriate *infinitesimal* characterization of the principal homogeneous space. It is a one-form defined on $P$ satisfying an [integrability condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrability_condition) known as the Maurer–Cartan equation. Using this integrability condition, it is possible to define the [exponential map](wikipedia://en/44612290) of the Lie algebra and in this way obtain, locally, a group action on $P$.

## Construction {#Construction}

### Intrinsic construction {#Intrinsic_construction}

Let $\mathbf{g} \cong T_{e}G$ be the tangent space of a Lie group $G$ at the identity (its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944)). $G$ acts on itself by left translation

$$L : G \times G \to G$$

such that for a given $g \in G$ we have

$$L_g : G \to G \quad \mbox{where} \quad L_g(h) = gh,$$

and this induces a map of the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) to itself: $(L_g)_*:T_hG\to T_{gh}G.$ A left-invariant [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) is a section $X$ of $TG$ such that [^o7]

$$(L_g)_{*}X = X \quad \forall g \in G.$$

The **Maurer–Cartan form** $\omega$ is a $\mathbf{g}$-valued one-form on $G$ defined on vectors $v \in T_{g}G$ by the formula

$$\omega_g(v)=(L_{g^{-1}})_*v.$$

### Extrinsic construction {#Extrinsic_construction}

If $G$ is embedded in $\operatorname{GL}(n)$ by a matrix valued mapping $g =(g_{ij})$, then one can write $\omega$ explicitly as

$$\omega_g = g^{-1} \,dg.$$

In this sense, the Maurer–Cartan form is always the left [logarithmic derivative](wikipedia://en/470327) of the [identity map](wikipedia://en/15069) of $G$.

### Characterization as a connection {#Characterization_as_a_connection}

If we regard the Lie group $G$ as a [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) over a manifold consisting of a single point then the Maurer–Cartan form can also be characterized abstractly as the unique [principal connection](wikipedia://en/470632) on the principal bundle $G$. Indeed, it is the unique $\mathbf{g} = T_{e}G$ valued $1$-form on $G$ satisfying

1. $$\omega_e = \mathrm{id} : T_eG\rightarrow {\mathfrak g},\text{ and}$$
1. $$\forall g \in G \quad \omega_g = \mathrm{Ad}(h)(R_h^*\omega_e),\text{ where }h=g^{-1},$$

where $R_{h}*$ is the [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of forms along the right-translation in the group and $\operatorname{Ad}(h)$ is the [adjoint action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group) on the Lie algebra.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

If $X$ is a left-invariant vector field on $G$, then $\omega(X)$ is constant on $G$. Furthermore, if $X$ and $Y$ are both left-invariant, then

$$\omega([X,Y])=[\omega(X),\omega(Y)]$$

where the bracket on the left-hand side is the [Lie bracket of vector fields](wikipedia://en/10282799), and the bracket on the right-hand side is the bracket on the Lie algebra $\mathbf{g}$. (This may be used as the definition of the bracket on $\mathbf{g}$.) These facts may be used to establish an isomorphism of Lie algebras

$$\mathfrak{g}=T_eG\cong \{\hbox{left-invariant vector fields on G}\}.$$

By the definition of the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782), if $X$ and $Y$ are arbitrary vector fields then

$$d\omega(X,Y)=X(\omega(Y))-Y(\omega(X))-\omega([X,Y]).$$

Here $\omega(Y)$ is the $\mathbf{g}$-valued function obtained by duality from pairing the one-form $\omega$ with the vector field $Y$, and $X(\omega(Y))$ is the [Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437) of this function along $X$. Similarly $Y(\omega(X))$ is the Lie derivative along $Y$ of the $\mathbf{g}$-valued function $\omega(X)$.

In particular, if $X$ and $Y$ are left-invariant, then

$$X(\omega(Y))=Y(\omega(X))=0,$$

so

$$d\omega(X,Y)+[\omega(X),\omega(Y)]=0$$

but the left-invariant fields span the tangent space at any point (the push-forward of a basis in $T_{e}G$ under a diffeomorphism is still a basis), so the equation is true for any pair of vector fields $X$ and $Y$. This is known as the **Maurer–Cartan equation**. It is often written as

$$d\omega + \frac{1}{2}[\omega,\omega]=0.$$

Here $[\omega, \omega]$ denotes the [bracket of Lie algebra-valued forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracket_of_Lie_algebra-valued_forms).

:::

## Maurer–Cartan frame {#Maurer–Cartan_frame}

One can also view the Maurer–Cartan form as being constructed from a **Maurer–Cartan frame**. Let $E_{i}$ be a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_space_basis) of sections of $TG$ consisting of left-invariant vector fields, and $\theta^{j}$ be the [dual basis](wikipedia://en/1182982) of sections of $T^{*}G$ such that $\theta^{j}(E_{i}) = \delta_{i}^{j}$, the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). Then $E_{i}$ is a Maurer–Cartan frame, and $\theta^{i}$ is a **Maurer–Cartan coframe**.

Since $E_{i}$ is left-invariant, applying the Maurer–Cartan form to it simply returns the value of $E_{i}$ at the identity. Thus $\omega(E_{i}) = E_{i}(e) \in \mathbf{g}$. Thus, the Maurer–Cartan form can be written

::: {#o13 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\omega=\sum_iE_i(e)\otimes\theta^i. \tag{1}$$

:::

Suppose that the Lie brackets of the vector fields $E_{i}$ are given by

$$[E_i,E_j]=\sum_k{c_{ij}}^kE_k.$$

The quantities $c_{ij}^{k}$ are the [structure constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_constant) of the Lie algebra (relative to the basis $E_{i}$). A simple calculation, using the definition of the exterior derivative $d$, yields

$$d\theta^i(E_j,E_k) = -\theta^i([E_j,E_k]) = -\sum_r {c_{jk}}^r\theta^i(E_r) = -{c_{jk}}^i = -\frac{1}{2}({c_{jk}}^i - {c_{kj}}^i),$$

so that by duality

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$d\theta^i=-\frac12 \sum_{jk} {c_{jk}}^i\theta^j\wedge\theta^k. \tag{2}$$

:::

This equation is also often called the **Maurer–Cartan equation**. To relate it to the previous definition, which only involved the Maurer–Cartan form $\omega$, take the exterior derivative of ((1)):

$$d\omega = \sum_i E_i(e)\otimes d\theta^i\,=\,-\frac12 \sum_{ijk}{c_{jk}}^iE_i(e)\otimes\theta^j\wedge\theta^k.$$

The frame components are given by

$$d\omega(E_j,E_k) = -\sum_i {c_{jk}}^iE_i(e) = -[E_j(e),E_k(e)]=-[\omega(E_j),\omega(E_k)],$$

which establishes the equivalence of the two forms of the Maurer–Cartan equation.

## On a homogeneous space {#On_a_homogeneous_space}

Maurer–Cartan forms play an important role in Cartan's [method of moving frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_moving_frames). In this context, one may view the Maurer–Cartan form as a $1$-form defined on the tautological [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) associated with a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325). If $H$ is a [closed subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subgroup) of $G$, then $G/H$ is a smooth manifold of dimension $\dim G - \dim H$. The quotient map $G \to G/H$ induces the structure of an $H$-principal bundle over $G/H$. The Maurer–Cartan form on the Lie group $G$ yields a flat [Cartan connection](wikipedia://en/412272) for this principal bundle. In particular, if $H = \{e$}, then this Cartan connection is an ordinary [connection form](wikipedia://en/706412), and we have

$$d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega=0$$

which is the condition for the vanishing of the curvature.

In the method of moving frames, one sometimes considers a local section of the [tautological bundle](wikipedia://en/2913502), say $s : G/H \to G$. (If working on a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095) of the homogeneous space, then $s$ need only be a local section over the submanifold.) The [pullback](wikipedia://en/494995) of the Maurer–Cartan form along $s$ defines a non-degenerate $\mathbf{g}$-valued $1$-form $\theta = s^{*}\omega$ over the base. The Maurer–Cartan equation implies that

$$d\theta + \frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]=0.$$

Moreover, if $s_{U}$ and $s_{V}$ are a pair of local sections defined, respectively, over open sets $U$ and $V$, then they are related by an element of $H$ in each fibre of the bundle:

$$h_{UV}(x) = s_V\circ s_U^{-1}(x),\quad x \in U \cap V.$$

The differential of $h$ gives a compatibility condition relating the two sections on the overlap region:

$$\theta_V = \operatorname{Ad}(h^{-1}_{UV})\theta_U + (h_{UV})^* \omega_H$$

where $\omega_{H}$ is the Maurer–Cartan form on the group $H$.

A system of non-degenerate $\mathbf{g}$-valued $1$-forms $\theta_{U}$ defined on open sets in a manifold $M$, satisfying the Maurer–Cartan structural equations and the compatibility conditions endows the manifold $M$ locally with the structure of the homogeneous space $G/H$. In other words, there is locally a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of $M$ into the homogeneous space, such that $\theta_{U}$ is the pullback of the Maurer–Cartan form along some section of the tautological bundle. This is a consequence of the existence of primitives of the [Darboux derivative](wikipedia://en/5194894).
