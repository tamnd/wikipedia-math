# Finitely generated group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finitely generated group"}

*Image: Dih4 cycle graph.svg.* The [dihedral group of order 8](wikipedia://en/78749515) requires two generators, as represented by this [cycle diagram](wikipedia://en/1681010).

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), a **finitely generated group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* that has some [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) [generating set](wikipedia://en/99945) *S* so that every element of *G* can be written as the combination (under the group operation) of finitely many elements of *S* and of [inverses](wikipedia://en/45199) of such elements.[^o2]

:::

By definition, every [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) is finitely generated, since *S* can be taken to be *G* itself. Every infinite finitely generated group must be [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) but countable groups need not be finitely generated. The additive group of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) **Q** is an example of a countable group that is not finitely generated.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a finitely generated group *G* is finitely generated; the quotient group is generated by the images of the generators of *G* under the [canonical projection](wikipedia://en/11526#Properties).
- A group that is generated by a single element is called [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327). Every infinite cyclic group is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the additive group of the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563#Algebraic_properties) **Z**.
  - A [locally cyclic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_cyclic_group) is a group in which every finitely generated [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) is cyclic.
- The [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on a finite set is finitely generated by the elements of that set ([§Examples](wikipedia://en/99945#Examples)).
- [A fortiori](wikipedia://en/13474947), every [finitely presented group](wikipedia://en/99494#Definition) ([§Examples](wikipedia://en/99494#Examples)) is finitely generated.

:::

## Finitely generated abelian groups {#Finitely_generated_abelian_groups}

*Image: Cyclic group.svg.* The six 6th [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) form a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) under multiplication.

Every [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) can be seen as a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) over the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of integers **Z**, and in a [finitely generated abelian group](wikipedia://en/58527) with generators *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*n*</sub>, every group element *x* can be written as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of these generators,

*x* = *α*<sub>1</sub>⋅*x*<sub>1</sub> + *α*<sub>2</sub>⋅*x*<sub>2</sub> + ... + *α*<sub>*n*</sub>⋅*x*<sub>*n*</sub>

with integers *α*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *α*<sub>*n*</sub>.

Subgroups of a finitely generated abelian group are themselves finitely generated.

The [fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_theorem_of_finitely_generated_abelian_groups) states that a finitely generated abelian group is the [direct sum](wikipedia://en/106341) of a [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) of finite [rank](wikipedia://en/244541) and a finite abelian group, each of which are unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) isomorphism.

## Subgroups {#Subgroups}

A subgroup of a finitely generated group need not be finitely generated. The [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847) of the free group $F_2$ on two generators is an example of a subgroup of a finitely generated group that is not finitely generated.

On the other hand, all subgroups of a finitely generated abelian group are finitely generated.

A subgroup of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in a finitely generated group is always finitely generated, and the [Schreier index formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schreier_index_formula) gives a bound on the number of generators required.[^n1]

In 1954, [Albert G. Howson](wikipedia://en/72427614) showed that the [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) of two finitely generated subgroups of a free group is again finitely generated. Furthermore, if $m$ and $n$ are the numbers of generators of the two finitely generated subgroups then their intersection is generated by at most $2mn - m - n + 1$ generators.[^o8] This upper bound was then significantly improved by [Hanna Neumann](wikipedia://en/7043308) to $2(m-1)(n-1) + 1$; see [Hanna Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/19296207).

The [lattice of subgroups](wikipedia://en/3983172) of a group satisfies the [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) all subgroups of the group are finitely generated. A group such that all its subgroups are finitely generated is called [Noetherian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noetherian_group).

A group such that every finitely generated subgroup is finite is called [locally finite](wikipedia://en/4939270). Every locally finite group is [periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_group), i.e., every element has finite [order](wikipedia://en/493527). [Conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), every periodic abelian group is locally finite.[^n2]

## Applications {#Applications}

Finitely generated groups arise in diverse mathematical and scientific contexts. A frequent way they do so is by the [Švarc-Milnor lemma](wikipedia://en/51457085), or more generally thanks to an [action](wikipedia://en/12781) through which a group inherits some finiteness property of a space. [Geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) studies the connections between algebraic properties of finitely generated groups and [topological](wikipedia://en/29954) and [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) properties of [spaces](wikipedia://en/5308894) on which these groups act.

### Differential geometry and topology {#Differential_geometry_and_topology}

- [Fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of compact [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) are finitely generated. Their geometry coarsely reflects the possible geometries of the manifold: for instance, non-positively curved compact manifolds have [CAT(0)](wikipedia://en/12942017) fundamental groups, whereas uniformly positively-curved manifolds have finite fundamental group (see [Myers' theorem](wikipedia://en/932460#Corollaries)).
- [Mostow's rigidity theorem](wikipedia://en/2782164): for compact [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) of dimension at least 3, an isomorphism between their fundamental groups extends to a [Riemannian isometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometry_%28Riemannian_geometry%29).
- [Mapping class groups of surfaces](wikipedia://en/31060586) are also important finitely generated groups in low-dimensional topology.

### Algebraic geometry and number theory {#Algebraic_geometry_and_number_theory}

- [Lattices in Lie groups](wikipedia://en/15782871#Lattices_in_semisimple_Lie_groups), [in p-adic groups](wikipedia://en/15782871#Lattices_in_p-adic_Lie_groups)...
- [Superrigidity](wikipedia://en/15783129), [Margulis' arithmeticity theorem](wikipedia://en/630741#Margulis_arithmeticity_theorem)

### Combinatorics, algorithmics and cryptography {#Combinatorics,_algorithmics_and_cryptography}

- Infinite families of [expander graphs](wikipedia://en/9313) can be constructed thanks to finitely generated groups with [property T](wikipedia://en/1362724)
- Algorithmic problems in [combinatorial group theory](wikipedia://en/979834)
- [Group-based cryptography](wikipedia://en/24701674) attempts to make use of hard algorithmic problems related to group presentations in order to construct quantum-resilient cryptographic protocols

### Analysis {#Analysis}

### Probability theory {#Probability_theory}

- [Random walks](wikipedia://en/235451) on [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277) of finitely generated groups provide approachable examples of [random walks on graphs](wikipedia://en/235451#On_graphs)
- [Percolation](wikipedia://en/187805) on Cayley graphs

### Physics and chemistry {#Physics_and_chemistry}

- [Crystallographic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_group)
- [Molecular symmetry groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_symmetry)
- Mapping class groups appear in [topological quantum field theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_quantum_field_theories)

### Biology {#Biology}

- [Knot groups](wikipedia://en/1164126) are used to study [molecular knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_knot)

## Related notions {#Related_notions}

The [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) for a finitely generated group is the [decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336) of whether two [words](wikipedia://en/12792326) in the generators of the group represent the same element. The word problem for a given finitely generated group is solvable if and only if the group can be embedded in every [algebraically closed group](wikipedia://en/7156954).

The [rank of a group](wikipedia://en/18720088) is often defined to be the smallest [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a generating set for the group. By definition, the rank of a finitely generated group is finite.
