# Argument from poor design

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Argument from poor design"}

The **argument from poor design**, also known as the **dysteleological argument**, is an argument against the assumption of the [existence of](wikipedia://en/621169) a [creator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity) [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God), based on the reasoning that any [omnipotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnipotence) and [omnibenevolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnibenevolence) [deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deity) or deities would not create [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism) with the perceived suboptimal designs that occur in nature.

:::

The argument is structured as a basic *[modus ponens](wikipedia://en/18900)*: if "creation" contains many defects, then design appears an implausible theory for the origin of earthly existence. Proponents most commonly use the argument in a weaker way, however: not with the aim of disproving the existence of God, but rather as a *[reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146)* of the well-known [argument from design](wikipedia://en/30731) (which suggests that [living things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology) appear too well-designed to have originated by chance, and so an intelligent God or gods must have deliberately created them).

Although the phrase "argument from poor design" has seen little use, this type of argument has been advanced many times using words and phrases such as "poor design", "suboptimal design", "unintelligent design" or ["dysteleology/dysteleological"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysteleology). The nineteenth-century biologist [Ernst Haeckel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Haeckel) applied the term "dysteleology" to the implications of organs so rudimentary as to be useless to the life of an organism.[^o2] In his 1868 book *Natürliche Schöpfungsgeschichte* (*The History of Creation*), Haeckel devoted most of a chapter to the argument, ending with the proposition (perhaps with tongue slightly in cheek) of "a theory of the *unsuitability of parts* in organisms, as a counter-hypothesis to the old popular doctrine of the *suitability of parts*".[^o2] In 2005, Donald Wise of the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) popularised the term "incompetent design" (a play on "[intelligent design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_design)"), to describe aspects of nature seen as flawed in design.[^o3]

Traditional Christian theological responses generally posit that God constructed a perfect universe but that humanity's misuse of its [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will) to [rebel against God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_man) has resulted in the corruption of divine good design.[^o4][^o5][^o6]

## Overview {#Overview}

*Image: Fitness-landscape-cartoon.png.* Natural selection is expected to push fitness to a peak, but that peak often is not the highest.

The argument runs that:

1. An [omnipotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnipotence), [omniscient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omniscience), [omnibenevolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnibenevolence) creator God would create organisms that have optimal design.
1. Organisms have features that are suboptimal.
1. Therefore, God either did not create these organisms or is not omnipotent, omniscient and omnibenevolent.

It is sometimes used as a [reductio ad absurdum](wikipedia://en/22456146) of the well-known [argument from design](wikipedia://en/30731), which runs as follows:

1. Living things are too well-designed to have originated by chance.
1. Therefore, life must have been created by an intelligent creator.
1. This creator is God.

"Poor design" is consistent with the predictions of the [scientific theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_theory) of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) by means of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection). This predicts that features that were evolved for certain uses are then reused or co-opted for different uses, or abandoned altogether; and that suboptimal state is due to the inability of the [hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity) mechanism to eliminate the particular vestiges of the evolutionary process.

In [fitness landscape](wikipedia://en/310599) terms, natural selection will always push "up the hill", but a species cannot normally get from a lower peak to a higher peak without first going through a valley.

The argument from poor design is one of the arguments that was used by [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin);[^o8] modern proponents have included [Stephen Jay Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Jay_Gould), [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins), and [Nathan H. Lents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_H._Lents). They argue that such features can be explained as a consequence of the gradual, cumulative nature of the evolutionary process. [Theistic evolutionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theistic_evolutionists) generally reject the argument from design, but do still maintain belief in the existence of God.

## Examples {#Examples}

::: {#o12 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Flaws in the human body"}

### In humans {#In_humans}

#### Fatal flaws {#Fatal_flaws}

*Image: Ectopic.png.* [Artist's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regnier_de_Graaf) representation of an [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy). Critics cite such common biological occurrences as contradictory to the '[watchmaker analogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchmaker_analogy)'.

American scientist [Nathan H. Lents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_H._Lents) published his book on poor design in the human body and genome in 2018 titled *Human Errors*. The book ignited a firestorm of criticism from the creationist community[^o14][^o15] but was well received by the scientific community and received unanimously favorable reviews[^o16] in the dozens of non-creationist media outlets that covered it.

Several defects in human anatomy can result in death, especially without modern medical care:

- In the human female, a [fertilized egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote) can implant into the [fallopian tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallopian_tube), [cervix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervix) or [ovary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovary) rather than the [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus) causing an [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy). The existence of a cavity between the ovary and the fallopian tube could indicate a flawed design in the female reproductive system. Prior to modern surgery, ectopic pregnancy invariably caused the deaths of both mother and baby. Even in modern times, in almost all cases the pregnancy must be aborted to save the life of the mother.
- In the human female, the [birth canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagina) passes through the [pelvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_pelvis). The prenatal skull will deform to a surprising extent. However, if the baby's head is significantly larger than the pelvic opening, the baby cannot be born naturally. Prior to the development of modern surgery ([caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section)), such a complication would lead to the death of the mother, the baby, or both. Other birthing complications such as [breech birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breech_birth) are worsened by this position of the birth canal.
- In the human male, [testes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testes) develop initially within the [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen). Later during gestation, they migrate through the abdominal wall into the [scrotum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrotum). This causes two weak points in the abdominal wall where [hernias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernia) can later form. Prior to modern surgical techniques, complications from hernias, such as intestinal blockage and [gangrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangrene), usually resulted in death.[^o17]
- The existence of the [pharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharynx), a passage used for both [ingestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingestion) and [respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiration_%28physiology%29), with the consequent drastic increase in the risk of [choking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choking).
- The breathing reflex is stimulated not directly by the absence of [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) but indirectly by the presence of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide). This means that high concentrations of inert gases, such as [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen) and [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium), can cause suffocation without any biological warning. Furthermore, at high altitudes, oxygen deprivation can occur in unadapted individuals who do not consciously increase their breathing rate.
- The human [appendix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendix_%28anatomy%29) is a [vestigial organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestigial_organ) thought to serve no purpose. [Appendicitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendicitis), an infection of this organ, is a certain death without medical intervention. "During the past few years, however, several studies have suggested its immunological importance for the development and preservation of the intestinal immune system."[^o18]
- [Tinnitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinnitus), a phantom auditory sensation, is a maladaptation resulting from hearing loss most often caused by exposure to loud noise.[^o19] Tinnitus serves no practical purpose, reduces quality of life, may cause depression, and when severe can lead to suicide.[^o20]

#### Other flaws {#Other_flaws}

- Barely used nerves and muscles, such as the [plantaris muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantaris_muscle) of the foot,[^o22] that are missing in part of the human population and are routinely harvested as spare parts if needed during operations. Another example is the muscles that move the ears, which some people can learn to control to a degree, but serve no purpose in any case.[^o23]
- The common malformation of the human spinal column, leading to [scoliosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoliosis), [sciatica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciatica) and congenital misalignment of the vertebrae. The [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord) cannot ever properly heal if it is damaged, because neurons have become so specialized that they are no longer able to regrow once they reach their mature state. The spinal cord, if broken, will never repair itself and will result in permanent [paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralysis).[^o24]
- The route of the [recurrent laryngeal nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrent_laryngeal_nerve) is such that it travels from the brain to the larynx by looping around the [aortic arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aortic_arch). This same configuration holds true for many animals; in the case of the [giraffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraffe), this results in about twenty feet of extra nerve.
- Almost all animals and plants synthesize their own [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C), but humans cannot because the gene for this enzyme is defective ([Pseudogene ΨGULO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-gulonolactone_oxidase)).[^o25] Lack of vitamin C results in [scurvy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurvy) and eventually death. The gene is also non-functional in other [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) and in [guinea pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_pig), but is functional in most other animals.[^o26]
- The prevalence of [congenital diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congenital_disease) and genetic disorders such as [Huntington's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington's_disease).
- The male [urethra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urethra) passes directly through the [prostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate), which can produce urinary difficulties if the prostate becomes swollen.[^o27]
- Crowded [teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teeth) and poor [sinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranasal_sinus) drainage, as human faces are significantly flatter than those of other [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primates) although humans share the same tooth set. This results in a number of problems, most notably with [wisdom teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisdom_teeth), which can damage neighboring teeth or cause serious infections of the mouth.[^o28]
- The structure of [human eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_eye) (as well as those of all vertebrates). The [retina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retina) is 'inside out'. The nerves and blood vessels lie on the *surface* of the retina instead of behind it as is the case in many [invertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate) species. This arrangement forces a number of complex adaptations and gives mammals a [blind spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_spot_%28vision%29).[^o29] Having the optic nerve connected to the side of the retina that does not receive the light, as is the case in [cephalopods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopods), would avoid these problems.[^o30] [Lents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_H._Lents) and colleagues have proposed that the [tapetum lucidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapetum_lucidum), the reflective surface behind vertebrate retinas, has evolved to overcome the limitations of the inverted retina,[^o31] as cephalopods have never evolved this structure.[^o32] However, an 'inverted' retina actually improves image quality through [müller cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Müller_glia) by reducing distortion.[^o33] The effects of the blind spots resulting from the inverted retina are cancelled by [binocular vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binocular_vision), as the blind spots in both eyes are oppositely angled. Additionally, as [cephalopod eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalopod_eye) lack cone cells and might be able to judge color by bringing specific wavelengths to a focus on the retina, an inverted retina might interfere with this mechanism.[^o34]
- Humans are attracted to [junk food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_food)'s non-nutritious ingredients, and even wholly non-nutritious [psychoactive drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive_drugs), and can experience [physiological adaptations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_dependence) to prefer them to nutrients.

:::

### Other life {#Other_life}

- In the [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa) [locust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust), [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve) cells start in the abdomen but connect to the wing. This leads to unnecessary use of materials.[^o17]
- Intricate reproductive devices in [orchids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid), apparently constructed from components commonly having different functions in other flowers.
- The use by [pandas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panda) of their enlarged [radial sesamoid bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesamoid_bone#Other_animals) in a manner similar to how other creatures use [thumbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thumb).[^o17]
- The existence of unnecessary wings in flightless birds, e.g. ostriches.[^o36]
- The enzyme [RuBisCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RuBisCO) has been described as a "notoriously inefficient" enzyme,[^o37] as it is [inhibited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzyme_inhibitor) by oxygen, has a very slow turnover and is not saturated at current levels of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. The enzyme is inhibited as it is unable to distinguish between carbon dioxide and molecular oxygen, with oxygen acting as a [competitive enzyme inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_inhibition). However, RuBisCO remains the key enzyme in [carbon fixation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_fixation), and plants overcome its poor activity by having massive amounts of it inside their cells, making it the most abundant protein on Earth.[^o38]
- Sturdy but heavy bones, suited for non-flight, occurring in animals like bats. Or, on the converse: unstable, light, hollow bones, suited for flight, occurring in birds like penguins and ostriches, which cannot fly.
- Various [vestigial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestigial) body parts, like the femur and pelvis in whales (evolution indicates the ancestors of whales lived on land).
- *[Turritopsis dohrnii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turritopsis_dohrnii)* and species of the genus *[Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydra_%28genus%29)* have [biological immortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_immortality), but most animals do not.
- Many species have strong instincts to behave in response to a certain stimulus. Natural selection can leave animals behaving in detrimental ways when they encounter a [supernormal stimulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernormal_stimulus) - like a [moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moth) flying into a flame.
- Plants are green and not black, as [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll) absorbs green light poorly, even though black plants would absorb more light energy.
- [Whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale) and [dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin) breathe air, but live in the water, meaning they must swim to the surface frequently to breathe.
- [Albatrosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatross) cannot take off or land properly.

## Counterarguments {#Counterarguments}

### Specific examples {#Specific_examples}

[Intelligent design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_design) proponent [William Dembski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dembski) questions the first premise of the argument, claiming that "intelligent design" does not need to be optimal.[^o41]

While the [appendix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermiform_appendix) has been previously credited with very little function, research has shown that it serves an important role in the fetus and young adults. Endocrine cells appear in the appendix of the human fetus at around the 11th week of development, which produce various biogenic amines and peptide hormones, compounds that assist with various biological control (homeostatic) mechanisms. In young adults, the appendix has some immune functions.[^o42]

### Responses to counterarguments {#Responses_to_counterarguments}

In response to the claim that uses have been found for "junk" DNA, proponents note that the fact that some [non-coding DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-coding_DNA) has a purpose does not establish that all non-coding DNA has a purpose, and that the human genome does include [pseudogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudogenes) that are nonfunctional "junk", with others noting that some sections of DNA can be randomized, cut, or added to with no apparent effect on the organism in question.[^o44] The original study that suggested that the *Makorin1-p1* served some purpose[^o45] has been disputed.[^o46] However, the original study is still frequently cited in newer studies and articles on pseudogenes previously thought to be nonfunctional.[^o47]

## As an argument regarding God {#As_an_argument_regarding_God}

The argument from poor design is sometimes interpreted, by the argumenter or the listener, as an [argument against the existence of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_against_the_existence_of_God), or against characteristics commonly attributed to a [creator deity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creator_deity), such as [omnipotence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnipotence), [omniscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omniscience), or personality. In a weaker form, it is used as an argument for the incompetence of God. The existence of "poor design" (as well as the perceived prodigious "wastefulness" of the evolutionary process) would seem to imply a "poor" designer, or a "blind" designer, or no designer at all. In Gould's words, "If God had designed a beautiful machine to reflect his wisdom and power, surely he would not have used a collection of parts generally fashioned for other purposes. Orchids are not made by an ideal engineer; they are jury-rigged...."[^o49]

The apparently suboptimal design of organisms has also been used by proponents of [theistic evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theistic_evolution) to argue in favour of a creator deity who uses [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection) as a mechanism of his creation.[^o50] Arguers from poor design regard counter-arguments as a [false dilemma](wikipedia://en/17687), imposing that either a creator deity designed life on earth well or flaws in design indicate the life is not designed. This allows proponents of [intelligent design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_design) to [cherry pick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_picking_%28fallacy%29) which aspects of life constitute design, leading to the [unfalsifiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfalsifiability) of the theory. Christian proponents of both intelligent design and creationism may claim that good design indicates the creative intelligence of their God, while poor design indicates corruption of the world as a result of [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvin_Plantinga's_free_will_defense) that caused the [fall of man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_man) (for example, in [Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) 3:16 [Yahweh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahweh) says to [Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eve) "I will increase your trouble in pregnancy").[^o51]
