# Yuri Petunin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Yuri Petunin"}

**Yuri Ivanovich Petunin** (Юрий Иванович Петунин) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) and [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) mathematician. Petunin was born in the city of [Michurinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michurinsk) ([USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR)) on September 30, 1937. After graduating from the [Tambov State Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambov_State_University) he began his studies at [Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh) State University under the supervision of S. G. Krein. He completed his postgraduate studies in 1962, and in 1968 he received his [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) Degree, the highest scientific degree awarded in the Soviet Union. In 1970 he joined the faculty of the computational mathematics department at [Kyiv State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv).

:::

Yuri Petunin is highly regarded for his results in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). He developed the theory of Scales in [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989) spaces,[^o3] the theory of characteristics of linear manifolds in conjugate Banach spaces,[^o4] and with S. G. Krein and E. M. Semenov contributed to the theory of [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) of linear operators.[^o5] He solved Banach's problem of norming subspaces in conjugate Banach spaces[^o4] as well as a problem posted by [Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Calderón) and [Lions](wikipedia://en/3735500) concerning interpolation in factor spaces.[^o5]

In addition to his work in functional analysis, Professor Petunin made significant contributions to [pattern recognition](wikipedia://en/126706) and [mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711). He also worked on developing differential diagnostics for oncological disease.[^o6] The [Vysochanskij–Petunin inequality](wikipedia://en/435813) that bears his name formally justifies the so-called 3-sigma rule for [unimodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimodal) distributions, a rule that has been broadly used in statistics since the time of [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss). In the area of pattern recognition he developed a theory of linear discriminant rules where he investigated the problems of linear separability of any number of sets in *n*-dimensional space.[^o7]

In his later years Yuri Petunin returned to the area of functional analysis where he had begun his scientific research. Together with his colleagues at the department of computational mathematics, he successfully worked toward a solution of [Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert)'s 20th problem.[^o8]
