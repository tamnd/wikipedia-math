# Yang–Mills–Higgs equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yang–Mills–Higgs equations"}

In mathematics, the **Yang–Mills–Higgs equations** are a set of [non-linear partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_partial_differential_equation) for a [Yang–Mills field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang–Mills_field), given by a connection, and a [Higgs field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higgs_field), given by a section of a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) (specifically, the [adjoint bundle](wikipedia://en/9943932)). These equations are[^o2]

:::

$$\begin{aligned} D_A*F_A + *[\Phi, D_A\Phi] &= 0, \\ D_A*D_A\Phi &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

with a [boundary condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_condition)

$$\lim_{|x|\rightarrow \infty}|\Phi|(x) = 1$$

where

*A* is a connection on a vector bundle,

*D*${}_{A}$ is the exterior covariant derivative,

*F*${}_{A}$ is the curvature of that connection,

Φ is a section of that vector bundle,

∗ is the Hodge star, and

\[·,·\] is the natural, graded bracket.

These equations are named after [Chen Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang), [Robert Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mills_%28physicist%29), and [Peter Higgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Higgs). They are very closely related to the [Ginzburg–Landau equations](wikipedia://en/396286), when these are expressed in a general geometric setting.

M.V. Goganov and L.V. Kapitanskii have shown that the Cauchy problem for hyperbolic Yang–Mills–Higgs equations in Hamiltonian gauge on 4-dimensional Minkowski space have a unique global solution with no restrictions at the spatial infinity. Furthermore, the solution has the finite propagation speed property.

where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is an invariant symmetric bilinear form on the adjoint bundle. This is sometimes written as $\text{tr}$ due to the fact that such a form can arise from the trace on $\mathfrak{g}$ under some representation; in particular here we are concerned with the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188), and the trace on this representation is the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386).

For the particular form of the Yang–Mills–Higgs equations given above, the potential $V(\phi)$ is vanishing. Another common choice is $V(\phi) = \frac{1}{2}m^2\langle \phi, \phi\rangle$, corresponding to a massive Higgs field.

This theory is a particular case of [scalar chromodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_chromodynamics) where the Higgs field $\phi$ is valued in the adjoint representation as opposed to a general representation.
