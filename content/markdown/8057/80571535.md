# Claudia Spiro

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Claudia Spiro"}

**Claudia Alison Spiro** (March 4, 1956 – January 4, 2023) was an American mathematician who worked primarily in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904).[^o3] She is known for her work in number theory and for having an Erdős number of 1.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Spiro was born on March 4, 1956, to Robert Spiro and Lorraine Shatz. Sprio had a younger brother and younger sister. Her father remarried to in addition to Constance Mobley who gave her a half sister and two half brothers. Spiro grew up in [Altadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altadena,_California) where she enjoyed attending the [rose bowl parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Parade_floats). Spiro was taught the musical instaments: the clarinet and the piano. In her childhood she loved mathematics, as early as 5 years old she would do difficult [mental arithimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_arithmetic) such as triple digit multiplication problems. She was also a [chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess) player.[^o3]

## Education {#Education}

Sprio obtained bachelor's degree and master's degree both in mathematics at [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech). In 1977, she started her work on her PhD at [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign) where she worked with her advisor [Paul Trevier Bateman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Trevier_Bateman)."[^o3] On October 15, 1981, she obtained her PhD for which she wrote her dissertation "The Frequency with Which an Integral-Valued, Prime-Independent, Multiplicative or Additive Function of n Divides a Polynomial Function of n.[^o6][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

After finishing up the work for her PhD in the summer of 1981, Spiro was accepted the position of [George William Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_William_Hill) and [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) Research Instructor in the mathematics department at [SUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SUNY) in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), which started September of that year.[^o7][^o9]

In 2006, Spiro started working at [University of Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Phoenix).[^o10] She was hired in 2012 as an assistant professor at [Southern Polytechnic State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Polytechnic_State_University), which was consolidated into [Kennesaw State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennesaw_State_University), to assist with their newly created Teacher Education program.[^o11]

## Research and contributions {#Research_and_contributions}

Determining whether the number of divisors of some number $n$ and $n+k$ had infinitely many solutions for some k was solved by Spiro. Spiro proved that for the case $d(n)=d(n + 5040)$, there are infinitely many solutions. [Roger Heath-Brown](wikipedia://en/2984550) and Chris Pinner built upon her work to show this is true for all integer values of $k$.[^o13][^o14][^o15]

She is one of the few women to have an [Erdős Number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1, through a research collaboration with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), [Andrew Granville](wikipedia://en/2910434), and [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159).[^o16][^o17]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Spiro married Robert Silverman and had three children: Joel, Sarah, and Isaac. She was also an active member in her church, going on missions and leading Bible studies.[^o3]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

Spiro died of cancer on January 4, 2023.[^o3]
