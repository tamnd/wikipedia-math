# Arithmetica Universalis

*Image: arithmetica.jpg.* *Arithmetica* Title page (1707)

*Image: UniversalArithmetick.gif.* Raphson 's Eng. Tr. (1720)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetica Universalis"}

***Arithmetica Universalis*** ("Universal Arithmetic") is a [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) text by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627). Written in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language), it was edited and published by [William Whiston](wikipedia://en/188885), Newton's successor as [Lucasian Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/147921) at the University of Cambridge. The *Arithmetica* was based on Newton's lecture notes.

:::

## Publication history {#Publication_history}

Whiston's original edition was published in 1707. It was translated into English by [Joseph Raphson](wikipedia://en/4849065), who published it in 1720 as the *Universal Arithmetick*. [John Machin](wikipedia://en/274756) published a second Latin edition in 1722.

## Lack of credit for the writer {#Lack_of_credit_for_the_writer}

None of these editions credit Newton as author; Newton was unhappy with the publication of the *Arithmetica*, and so refused to have his name appear. In fact, when Whiston's edition was published, Newton was so upset he considered purchasing all of the copies so he could destroy them.[^o4]

## Content {#Content}

The *Arithmetica* touches on algebraic notation, arithmetic, the relationship between [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), and the solution of equations. Newton also applied [Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525)' [rule of signs](wikipedia://en/2641493) to [imaginary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_numbers) roots. He also offered, without proof, a rule to determine the number of imaginary roots of polynomial equations. A rigorous proof of Newton's counting formula for equations up to and including the fifth degree was published by [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) in 1864.[^o6]
