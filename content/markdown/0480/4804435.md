# External ray

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="External ray"}

An **external ray** is a [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) that runs from [infinity](wikipedia://en/403139) toward a [Julia](wikipedia://en/56434) or [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562).[^o2] Although this curve is only rarely a [half-line (ray)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray) it is called a [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray) because it is an image of a ray.

:::

External rays are used in [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), particularly in [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518) and [geometric function theory](wikipedia://en/5458932).

## History {#History}

External rays were introduced in [Douady](wikipedia://en/1604604) and [Hubbard](wikipedia://en/6674772)'s study of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562).

## Types {#Types}

Criteria for classification:

- Plane: parameter or dynamic
- Map
- Bifurcation of dynamic rays
- Stretching
- Landing[^o5]

### Plane {#Plane}

External rays of (connected) [Julia sets](wikipedia://en/56434) on [dynamical plane](wikipedia://en/11735693#Dynamical_plane) are often called **dynamic rays**.

External rays of the Mandelbrot set (and similar one-dimensional [connectedness loci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connectedness_locus)) on [parameter plane](wikipedia://en/11735693#Parameter_plane) are called **parameter rays**.

### Bifurcation {#Bifurcation}

Dynamic rays can be:

- Bifurcated, branched,[^o8] broken [^o9]
- Smooth, unbranched, unbroken

When the [filled Julia set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filled_Julia_set) is connected, there are no branching external rays. When the Julia set is not connected then some external rays branch.[^o10]

### Stretching {#Stretching}

Stretching rays were introduced by Branner and Hubbard:[^o12][^o13] "The notion of stretching rays is a generalization of that of external rays for the Mandelbrot set to higher degree polynomials."[^o14]

### Landing {#Landing}

Every rational parameter ray of the Mandelbrot set lands at a single parameter.[^o16][^o17]

## Maps {#Maps}

### Polynomials {#Polynomials}

#### Dynamical plane = z-plane {#Dynamical_plane_=_z-plane}

**External rays** are associated to a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [full](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_set_%28topology%29), [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) subset $K\,$ of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) as :

- the images of radial rays under the [Riemann map](wikipedia://en/26215) of the complement of $K\,$
- the [gradient lines](wikipedia://en/12461) of the [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) of $K\,$
- [field lines](wikipedia://en/5000404) of Douady-Hubbard potential[^o21]
- an [integral curve](wikipedia://en/1322061) of the gradient [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) of the [Green's function](wikipedia://en/311001) on neighborhood of [infinity](wikipedia://en/403139)[^o22]

External rays together with equipotential lines of Douady-Hubbard potential ( level sets) form a new [polar coordinate system](wikipedia://en/25120) for [exterior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_%28topology%29) ( [complement](wikipedia://en/54347) ) of $K\,$.

In other words the external rays define vertical [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) which is orthogonal to horizontal foliation defined by the level sets of potential.[^o23]

##### Uniformization {#Uniformization}

Let $\Psi_c\,$ be the [conformal](wikipedia://en/50627) [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) from the [complement (exterior)](wikipedia://en/54347) of the [closed unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ to the complement of the [filled Julia set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filled_Julia_set) $\ K_c$.

$$\Psi_c: \hat{\mathbb{C}} \setminus \overline{\mathbb{D}} \to \hat{\mathbb{C}} \setminus K_c$$

where $\hat{\mathbb{C}}$ denotes the [extended complex plane](wikipedia://en/30876799). Let $\Phi_c = \Psi_c^{-1}\,$ denote the **[Boettcher map](wikipedia://en/34458555)**.[^o25] $\Phi_c\,$ is a [uniformizing](wikipedia://en/288270) map of the basin of attraction of infinity, because it conjugates $f_c$ on the complement of the filled Julia set $K_c$ to $f_0(z)=z^2$ on the complement of the unit disk:

$$\begin{aligned} \Phi_c: \hat{\mathbb{C}} \setminus K_c &\to \hat{\mathbb{C}} \setminus \overline{\mathbb{D}}\\ z & \mapsto \lim_{n\to \infty} (f_c^n(z))^{2^{-n}} \end{aligned}$$

and

$$\Phi_c \circ f_c \circ \Phi_c^{-1} = f_0$$

A value $w = \Phi_c(z)$ is called the **[Boettcher coordinate](wikipedia://en/34458555)** for a point $z \in \hat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K_c$.

##### Formal definition of dynamic ray {#Formal_definition_of_dynamic_ray}

*Image: Erays.svg.* Polar coordinate system and $\psi_c$ for $c=-2$

The **external ray** of angle $\theta\,$ noted as $\mathcal{R}^K _{\theta}$ is:

- the image under $\Psi_c\,$ of straight lines $\mathcal{R}_{\theta} = \{\left(r\cdot e^{2\pi i \theta}\right) : \ r > 1 \}$

$$\mathcal{R}^K _{\theta} = \Psi_c(\mathcal{R}_{\theta})$$

- set of points of exterior of filled-in Julia set with the same external angle $\theta$

$$\mathcal{R}^K _{\theta} = \{ z\in \hat{\mathbb{C}} \setminus K_c : \arg(\Phi_c(z)) = \theta \}$$

::: {#o28 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

###### Properties {#Properties}

The external ray for a periodic angle $\theta\,$ satisfies:

$$f(\mathcal{R}^K _{\theta}) = \mathcal{R}^K _{2 \theta}$$

and its landing point[^o29] $\gamma_f(\theta)$ satisfies:

$$f(\gamma_f(\theta)) = \gamma_f(2\theta)$$

:::

#### Parameter plane = c-plane {#Parameter_plane_=_c-plane}

"Parameter rays are simply the curves that run perpendicular to the equipotential curves of the M-set."[^o31]

##### Uniformization {#Uniformization}

*Image: Jung200.png.* Boundary of [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) as [an image](wikipedia://en/579311) of [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) under $\Psi_M\,$

*Image: Jung50e.png.* [Uniformization](wikipedia://en/288270) of [complement (exterior)](wikipedia://en/54347) of [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562)

Let $\Psi_M\,$ be the mapping from the [complement (exterior)](wikipedia://en/54347) of the [closed unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ to the complement of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) $\ M$.[^o33]

$$\Psi_M:\mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus \overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus M$$

and Boettcher map (function) $\Phi_M\,$, which is [uniformizing](wikipedia://en/288270) map[^o34] of complement of Mandelbrot set, because it conjugates complement of the [Mandelbrot set](wikipedia://en/19562) $\ M$ and the [complement (exterior)](wikipedia://en/54347) of the [closed unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738)

$$\Phi_M: \mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus M \to \mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus \overline{\mathbb{D}}$$

it can be normalized so that :

$\frac{\Phi_M(c)}{c} \to 1 \ as\ c \to \infty \,$[^o35]

where :

$\mathbb{\hat{C}}$ denotes the [extended complex plane](wikipedia://en/30876799)

Jungreis function $\Psi_M\,$ is the inverse of [uniformizing](wikipedia://en/288270) map :

$$\Psi_M = \Phi_{M}^{-1} \,$$

In the case of [complex quadratic polynomial](wikipedia://en/11735693) one can compute this map using [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213) about [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824)[^o36][^o37]

$$c = \Psi_M (w) = w + \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} b_m w^{-m} = w -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{8w} - \frac{1}{4w^2} + \frac{15}{128w^3} + ...\,$$

where

$$c \in \mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus M$$

$$w \in \mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus \overline{\mathbb{D}}$$

##### Formal definition of parameter ray {#Formal_definition_of_parameter_ray}

The **external ray** of angle $\theta\,$ is:

- the image under $\Psi_c\,$ of straight lines $\mathcal{R}_{\theta} = \{\left(r*e^{2\pi i \theta}\right) : \ r > 1 \}$

$$\mathcal{R}^M _{\theta} = \Psi_M(\mathcal{R}_{\theta})$$

- set of points of exterior of Mandelbrot set with the same external angle $\theta$[^o39]

$$\mathcal{R}^M _{\theta} = \{ c\in \mathbb{\hat{C}}\setminus M : \arg(\Phi_M(c)) = \theta \}$$

##### Definition of the Boettcher map {#Definition_of_the_Boettcher_map}

Douady and Hubbard define:

$$\Phi_M(c) \ \overset{\underset{\mathrm{def}}{}}{=} \ \Phi_c(z=c)\,$$

so external angle of point $c\,$ of parameter plane is equal to external angle of point $z=c\,$ of dynamical plane

#### External angle {#External_angle}

Angle $\theta$ is named **external angle** ( [argument](wikipedia://en/5826#Complex_plane) ).[^o42]

[Principal value](wikipedia://en/408225) of external angles are [measured](wikipedia://en/1196#Units) in [turns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29) [modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_operation) 1

1 [turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28geometry%29) = 360 [degrees](wikipedia://en/1195294) = 2 × $\pi$ [radians](wikipedia://en/26003)

Compare different types of angles :

- external ( point of set's exterior )
- internal ( point of component's interior )
- plain ( [argument of complex number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arg_%28mathematics%29) )

::: {#o43 .table cue=table}

|  | external angle | internal angle | plain angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| parameter plane | $\arg(\Phi_M(c)) \,$ | $\arg(\rho_n(c)) \,$ | $\arg(c) \,$ |
| dynamic plane | $\arg(\Phi_c(z)) \,$ |  | $\arg(z) \,$ |

:::

##### Computation of external argument {#Computation_of_external_argument}

- argument of Böttcher coordinate as an external argument[^o45]
  - $$\arg_M(c) = \arg(\Phi_M(c))$$
  - $$\arg_c(z) = \arg(\Phi_c(z))$$
- kneading sequence as a binary expansion of external argument[^o46][^o47][^o48]

### Transcendental maps {#Transcendental_maps}

For [transcendental](wikipedia://en/297021) maps ( for example [exponential](wikipedia://en/23467710) ) [infinity](wikipedia://en/403139) is not a fixed point but an [essential singularity](wikipedia://en/80862) and there is no [Boettcher isomorphism](wikipedia://en/34458555).[^o50][^o51]

Here dynamic ray is defined as a curve :

- connecting a point in an [escaping set](wikipedia://en/29115268) and [infinity](wikipedia://en/403139)
- lying in an [escaping set](wikipedia://en/29115268)
