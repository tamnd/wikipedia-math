# Dubreil-Jacotin–Long equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dubreil-Jacotin–Long equation"}

*Image: Exploring High-Resolution Sea Surface Height Data from NASA’s SWOT Satellite (SVS5538 - 8k 05820).jpg.* Satellite view of internal solitary waves in the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea) off the coast of Thailand. Dubreil-Jacotin–Long equation describes their motion.

In [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988), the **Dubreil-Jacotin–Long** (**DJL**) **equation** is one of the main [nonlinear partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/16678376) used to describe [gravity waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_waves) (waves in water). This equation is equivalent to [Euler equations of fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/396022) for stratified media.[^o2][^o3] It describes the motion of [internal waves](wikipedia://en/900160) as [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654).[^o4]

:::

The equation was first proposed by [Marie-Louise Dubreil-Jacotin](wikipedia://en/46727148) in 1932[^o5][^o6] and was popularized by Robert R. Long in 1953.[^o2][^o7]

## Description {#Description}

The DJL equation assumes a two-dimensional [inviscid](wikipedia://en/5877457), [incompressible](wikipedia://en/461477), [irrotational](wikipedia://en/58320) flow under the action of [surface gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_gravity) described under [Boussinesq approximation](wikipedia://en/14993993).[^o3] The simplest DJL equation for a fixed frame without background flow is given by[^o3]

$$\nabla^2 \eta +\frac{N(z-\eta)}{c^2}\eta = 0,$$

where $c$ is the unknown wave propagation speed, $z$ is the vertical coordinate, and $\eta(x,z)$ denotes the vertical ([isopycnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isopycnal)) displacement, where $x$ is the horizontal coordinate and $N^2$ is the [Brunt–Väisälä frequency](wikipedia://en/2630316) (buoyancy frequency), given by

$$N^2(z)=-g\frac{\mathrm d \bar{\rho}(z)}{\mathrm d z},$$

where $g$ is the [gravitational acceleration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_acceleration) and $\bar\rho(z)$ is the average density. Boundary conditions are such that $\eta(x,0)=\eta(x,-H)=0$ and $\eta(|x|\to \infty)\to 0$, where $H$ is the total water depth.

Solving the DJL equation allows to obtain $\eta(x,z)$ and $c$. The DJL equation is a nonlinear [elliptic partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/662787). It is an eigenvalue problem where $c$ is the eigenvalue for the solution $\eta(x,z)$.[^o4]

DJL equation is useful for the description of internal solitary waves. The DJL equation describes waves of permanent form and cannot describe their formation. Rigid-lid approximation is necessary to avoid coexisting small-amplitude waves with the same $c$ which carry energy into or away. For their formation, the [Korteweg–De Vries equation](wikipedia://en/344116) is often used.[^o4]

By using the relation $\varphi=\eta c$ one can derive the [flow velocity field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity), where $\varphi$ is the [stream function](wikipedia://en/218091).[^o3]

## Equation with background flow {#Equation_with_background_flow}

If a steady background current of speed $U(x,z)$ is present, the full DJL equation becomes[^o4]

$$\nabla^2 \eta - \frac{U'(z-\eta)}{U(z-\eta)-c}[\eta_x^2+(\eta_z-2)\eta_z] +\frac{N(z-\eta)}{(U(z-\eta)-c)^2}\eta = 0,$$

where $\eta_x,\eta_z$ are the horizontal and vertical derivatives of $\eta$.
