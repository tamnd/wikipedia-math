# Little Astronomy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Little Astronomy"}

***Little Astronomy*** (Μικρὸς Ἀστρονομούμενος grc) is a collection of minor works in [Ancient Greek mathematics](wikipedia://en/1503369) and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_astronomy) dating from the 4th to 2nd century BCE that were probably used as an astronomical curriculum starting around the 2nd century CE. In the [astronomy of the medieval Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy_in_the_medieval_Islamic_world), with a few additions, the collection became known as the ***Middle Books*** (كتاب المتوسطات ar), mathematical preparation for [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy)'s *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)*, intended for students who had already studied [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107).[^n1]

:::

## Works in the collection {#Works_in_the_collection}

The works contained in the collection are:[^n2][^n3]

- *[Spherics](wikipedia://en/37098504)* by [Theodosius of Bithynia](wikipedia://en/2828050): On [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026), in the style of the *Elements*.
- *[On the Moving Sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Moving_Sphere)* by [Autolycus of Pitane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autolycus_of_Pitane): On the movements of points and arcs on a sphere as it rotates on its axis.
- *[Optics](wikipedia://en/24877937)* by [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331): On various effects involving propagation of light, including shadows, parallax, and perspective.
- *[Phaenomena](wikipedia://en/76351898)* by Euclid: A treatise in 18 propositions, each dealing with important arcs on the [celestial sphere](wikipedia://en/48239).
- *[On Habitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Habitations)* by Theodosius: Description of the appearance of the sky as seen from different places on earth.
- *[On Days and Nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Days_and_Nights)* by Theodosius: A treatise in 31 propositions on the lengths of days and nights at different times of the year.
- *[On the Sizes and Distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Sizes_and_Distances_%28Aristarchus%29)* by [Aristarchus of Samos](wikipedia://en/74857): On the size of the Sun and Moon in the sky.
- *[On Risings and Settings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Risings_and_Settings)* by Autolycus: On the relationship between the rising and setting of stars throughout the year.
- *[On Ascensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Ascensions)* by [Hypsicles](wikipedia://en/12815034): A treatise on arithmetic progressions used to calculate approximate times for the signs of the Zodiac to rise above the horizon.

In Arabic translation as the *Middle Books*, additional works, also originally written in Ancient Greek, were often included:[^n1]

- *[Spherics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherics_%28Menelaus%29)* by [Menelaus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/46301): A treatise on the geometry of spherical triangles, which only survives in Arabic translation
- *[Data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_Data)* by Euclid
- Various works by or attributed to Archimedes: *[On the Sphere and Cylinder](wikipedia://en/18084436)*, *[On the Measurement of the Circle](wikipedia://en/18203202)*, *[Book of Lemmas](wikipedia://en/17941791)*

Although these works are all generally found together in numerous medieval Byzantine and Arabic manuscripts, it is unclear whether this specific set of works was originally intentionally compiled together as a collection.[^n1] All of the works are elementary treatises that would have been useful in a classroom setting, which increased their chance of survival through continuous use by students, and may have resulted in several of them being gathered together multiple different times independently.[^n1] The earliest known author to mention the existence of a discrete "Little Astronomy" collection by name is [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300), in the 4th century CE, who devotes book VI of his *Collection* to a commentary on selected works by Theodosius, Menelaus, Aristarchus, Euclid, and Autolycus.[^n3][^n1] The oldest manuscript in which all of the extant Greek works are preserved together is Codex Vaticanus Graecus 204, which dates from the 9th or 10th century CE.[^n1]
