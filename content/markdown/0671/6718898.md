# Unlink

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Unlink"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), an **unlink** is a [link](wikipedia://en/1161784) that is equivalent (under [ambient isotopy](wikipedia://en/7404467)) to finitely many disjoint circles in the plane.[^o7]

:::

The **two-component unlink**, consisting of two non-interlinked [unknots](wikipedia://en/60075), is the simplest possible unlink.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- An *n*-component link *L* ⊂ **S**<sup>3</sup> is an unlink if and only if there exists *n* disjointly embedded discs *D*<sub>*i*</sub> ⊂ **S**<sup>3</sup> such that *L* = ∪<sub>*i*</sub>∂*D*<sub>*i*</sub>.
- A link with one component is an unlink [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075).
- The [link group](wikipedia://en/22722334) of an *n*-component unlink is the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) on *n* generators, and is used in classifying [Brunnian links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunnian_links).

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [Hopf link](wikipedia://en/1515511) is a simple example of a link with two components that is not an unlink.
- The [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118) form a link with three components that is not an unlink; however, any two of the rings considered on their own do form a two-component unlink.
- Taizo Kanenobu has shown that for all *n* > 1 there exists a [hyperbolic link](wikipedia://en/1248299) of *n* components such that any proper sublink is an unlink (a [Brunnian link](wikipedia://en/2492788)). The [Whitehead link](wikipedia://en/1966668) and [Borromean rings](wikipedia://en/873118) are such examples for *n* = 2, 3.[^o7]

:::
