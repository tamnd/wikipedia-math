# Terry Millar

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Terry Millar"}

**Terrence Staples** (**Terry**) **Millar** (September 18, 1948 – March 9, 2019) was professor emeritus of mathematics and former associate dean for physical sciences in the Graduate School and assistant to the provost at the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). He joined the math faculty in 1976 after serving two years in the Marines and obtaining a Ph.D. from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o2] Millar retired in 2015 and was considered to be one of the world's foremost researchers in computable model theory.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He earned a bachelor’s degree in mathematics from Cornell University in 1970 and his Ph.D. in Mathematics from Cornell University in 1975.[^o5]

Millar died March 9, 2019, at the age of 70 due to pancreatic cancer.[^o2]

## Career {#Career}

Along with physics professors [Sau Lan Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sau_Lan_Wu) and [Wesley Smith (academic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Smith_%28academic%29), he “was central to Wisconsin’s contribution to development of the Large Hadron Collider.”[^o7][^o8] Working alongside [Francis Halzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Halzen), he was “integral in launching the [IceCube South Pole Neutrino Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IceCube_Neutrino_Observatory).”[^o2]
