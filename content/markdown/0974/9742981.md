# Charles Leedham-Green

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Leedham-Green"}

**Charles R. Leedham-Green** is a retired professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Queen Mary, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary,_University_of_London), known for his work in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890). He completed his [DPhil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPhil) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o3]

:::

His parents were John Charles Leedham-Green (1902–1984), a surgeon and general practitioner in [Southwold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwold), and Gertrude Mary Somerville Caldwell.[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

With [Leonard Soicher](wikipedia://en/81005363), Leedham-Green designed the [product replacement algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_replacement_algorithm);[^o6] an algorithm within [computational group theory](wikipedia://en/618584) that generates random elements of groups by taking a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) through the group. This algorithm has been implemented in both [GAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAP_computer_algebra_system) and [MAGMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_computer_algebra_system).

He is responsible for a great body of work in group theory. In recent times, this has involved research in computational group theory and [pro-p groups](wikipedia://en/1842075).

The 300th edition of the *[Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra)* was dedicated to him for his 65th birthday.[^o7] On the occasion of his retirement in 2006, the Mathematics Research Centre at Queen Mary held a conference in celebration of his mathematical achievements.[^o8]

## Translator of Newton's *Principia* {#Translator_of_Newton's_Principia}

In 2021, [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) published Leedham-Green's full annotated English translation of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s *magnum opus*, *[Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/48781)* ("The Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy"), first published in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) in 1687. Leedham-Green brought to that task a command of the Latin language and a professional's grasp of the relevant mathematics. He was motivated to produce that new translation in part by his dissatisfaction with the earlier translation of the *Principia* prepared by historian of science [I. Bernard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._Bernard_Cohen) and classicist Anne Whitman, published in 1999 by the [University of California Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Press). Leedham-Green found that text too obscure because of the translators' excessive concern with Newton's linguistic choices and their limited grasp of his mathematical and physical arguments.[^o10] Leedham-Green's is only the fourth English translation of the complete *Principia*, after those of Andrew Motte (1729), [Florian Cajori](wikipedia://en/542738) (1934, based on a modernization of Motte's text), and Cohen and Whitman (1999). In preparing his translation, Leedham-Green also consulted the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) translation by [Émilie du Châtelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Émilie_du_Châtelet) (1756) and the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) translation by Volkmar Schüller (1999), as well as the larger corpus of modern scholarship on Newton's scientific work.[^o10]
