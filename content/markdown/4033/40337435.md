# Kirstine Smith

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kirstine Smith"}

*Image: Kirstine Smith in app. 1896-1897.jpg.* Kirstine Smith in app. 1896-1897

*Image: Kirstine Smith 1919 copy.jpg.*

**Kirstine Smith** (April 12, 1878 – November 11, 1939) was a Danish [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). She is credited with the creation of the field of [optimal design of experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design).

:::

## Background {#Background}

Smith grew up in the town of [Nykøbing Mors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nykøbing_Mors), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark). In 1903, she graduated from the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) with a degree in mathematics and physics. After, she worked as secretary to astronomer and statistician [Thorvald Thiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorvald_Nicolai_Thiele)[^o3] and later with the [International Council for the Exploration of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_for_the_Exploration_of_the_Sea) for which she authored several volumes on fish populations.[^o4]

In 1916, Smith was admitted for doctoral training at the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) where [Karl Pearson](wikipedia://en/164695) had founded the first university statistics department. She was a student of Pearson who described her as "brilliant" in a letter to [Ronald Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Aylmer_Fisher).[^o5] At London, she produced an influential paper in the journal *[Biometrika](wikipedia://en/1734178)* on [minimum chi-square estimation](wikipedia://en/39452093) of the correlation coefficient.[^o5] Disagreements about aspects of her work led to increased friction between Pearson and Fisher.[^o5]

In her dissertation, which was published in 1918 (see below), she invented [optimal design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_design) where she computed G-optimal designs for [polynomial regression](wikipedia://en/21893202) of order up to 6.[^o5] After finishing her doctorate she moved to [Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen), where she worked as a researcher for the Commission for Ocean Research 1918 to 1924 and with [Johannes Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Schmidt_%28biologist%29) at the [Carlsberg Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsberg_Laboratory) from 1920 to 1921.[^o3] She eventually left research after obtaining her teaching credentials to become a high school teacher.[^o4]

## Selected statistical papers {#Selected_statistical_papers}

- Smith, K. (1916). On the 'best' values of the constants in frequency distributions. *Biometrika*, *11*(3), 262–276.
- Smith, K. (1918). On the standard deviations of adjusted and interpolated values of an observed polynomial function and its constants and the guidance they give towards a proper choice of the distribution of observations. *Biometrika*, *12*(1/2), 1–85.
- Smith, K. (1922).The standard deviations of fraternal and parental correlation coefficients. *Biometrika*, *14*(1/2), 1–22.
