# Jan Camiel Willems

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jan Camiel Willems"}

**Jan Camiel Willems** (18 September 1939 – 31 August 2013) was a Belgian mathematical system theorist who has done most of his scientific work while residing in the Netherlands and the United States. He is most noted for the introduction of the notion of a dissipative system and for the development of the behavioral approach to [systems theory](wikipedia://en/29238).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jan Willems was born in [Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruges) in 1939. He studied engineering at the [University of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghent), obtained the M.Sc. degree from the [University of Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rhode_Island), and the Ph.D. degree from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) in 1968. He was an assistant professor in the department of [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) at MIT from 1968 to 1973. On 1 February 1973 he was appointed professor of systems and control in the mathematics department of the [University of Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Groningen). In 2003 he became [emeritus professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus_professor). Afterwards, he became guest professor at the [KU Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KU_Leuven). He served terms as chairperson of the European Union Control Association and of the Dutch Mathematical Society (Wiskundig Genootschap). He was managing editor of the *SIAM Journal of Control and Optimization* and as founding and managing editor of *Systems & Control Letters*.

## Research contributions {#Research_contributions}

In his Ph.D. dissertation, Willems worked on input/output stability. In an often-cited 1972 paper[^o6] he introduced the notion of a *dissipative system*. This notion is a generalization of [Lyapunov function](wikipedia://en/352267) to input/state/output systems. The construction of the storage function, as the analogue of a Lyapunov function is called, led to the study of the [linear matrix inequality](wikipedia://en/5410577) (LMI) in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039). Applied to [linear-quadratic-Gaussian control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear-quadratic-Gaussian_control), the construction of the storage function leads to the [Kalman–Yakubovich–Popov lemma](wikipedia://en/6267642). In the 1980s Willems worked on the geometric theory of linear systems, where he introduced the notion of almost invariant subspace. Since the 1990s, he has devoted his interest to the development of the behavioral approach to systems theory and control.[^o7][^o8] In the behavioral approach a dynamical system is simply viewed as a family of trajectories. This approach avoids having to separate the system variables into inputs and outputs.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Willems was a fellow of the [IEEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE), the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376), the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o10] and [International Federation of Automatic Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Automatic_Control). In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o11] In 1998, he received the [IEEE Control Systems Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Control_Systems_Award) "for seminal contributions to control theory and leadership in systems research". In 2010, he became doctor [honoris causa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoris_causa) of the [University of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liège).
