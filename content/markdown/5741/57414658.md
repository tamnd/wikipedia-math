# Tropical cryptography

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tropical cryptography"}

In [tropical analysis](wikipedia://en/59158120), **tropical cryptography** refers to the study of a class of [cryptographic](wikipedia://en/18934432) protocols built upon [tropical algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_algebra).[^o2] In many cases, tropical cryptographic schemes have arisen from adapting classical (non-tropical) schemes to instead rely on tropical algebras. The case for the use of tropical algebras in cryptography rests on at least two key features of tropical mathematics: in the tropical world, there is no classical multiplication (a computationally expensive operation), and the problem of solving systems of tropical polynomial equations has been shown to be [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard).

:::

## Basic Definitions {#Basic_Definitions}

The key [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961) at the heart of tropical cryptography is the [tropical semiring](wikipedia://en/3892336) $(\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\},\oplus,\otimes)$ (also known as the [min-plus algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min-plus_algebra)), or a generalization thereof. The operations are defined as follows for $x,y \in \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$:

$x \oplus y = \min\{x,y\}$ $x \otimes y = x + y$

It is easily verified that with $\infty$ as the [additive identity](wikipedia://en/4178225), these binary operations on $\mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$ form a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468).
