# Laura Taalman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laura Taalman"}

*Image: 3D Printing Guru, Laura Taalman.jpg.* Laura Taalman in South Africa

**Laura Anne Taalman**, also known as **mathgrrl**, is an American mathematician known for her work on the [mathematics of Sudoku](wikipedia://en/2912292) and for her mathematical [3D printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing) models. Her mathematical research concerns [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008) and [singular algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/981915); she is a professor of mathematics at [James Madison University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Taalman earned a bachelor's degree in mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1994. She completed her Ph.D. at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) in 2000; her dissertation, *Monomial Generators for the Nash Sheaf of a Complete Resolution*, was supervised by William L. Pardon. On finishing her doctorate, she joined the James Madison faculty; she has also worked as the mathematician-in-residence at the [National Museum of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mathematics) in 2014–2015.

## 3d printing {#3d_printing}

*Image: 3D Printing and Mathematics models.jpg.* 3D Printing and Mathematics models by Taalman

In 2013–2014, after becoming head of the 3d printing lab at James Madison University, Taalman set out on a project of printing one 3d model per day. Her models have included subjects from mathematics including knots, fractals, and snap-together polyhedra.

## Books {#Books}

With Peter Kohn, Taalman is the author of a textbook *Calculus*. She is also the author of a different text, *Calculus I with Integrated Precalculus*.

With [Jason Rosenhouse](wikipedia://en/40906169) (also a mathematics professor at James Madison University) she is the author of *[Taking Sudoku Seriously: The Mathematics Behind the World’s Most Popular Pencil Puzzle](wikipedia://en/63295951)*. She has also written a series of Sudoku puzzle books with Philip Riley.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Taalman won the Trevor Evans Award of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2003 for her work with [Eugénie Hunsicker](wikipedia://en/61820104) on the mathematics of [modular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_design).

In 2005, Taalman won the Henry L. Alder Award for Distinguished Teaching by a Beginning College or University Mathematics Faculty Member, given by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772).

Her book *Taking Sudoku Seriously* was the 2012 winner of the [PROSE Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROSE_Awards) in the popular science and popular mathematics category.
