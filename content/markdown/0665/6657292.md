# Gromov's compactness theorem (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gromov's compactness theorem"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), [Mikhael Gromov](wikipedia://en/771562) proved a fundamental **compactness theorem** for sequences of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018). In the special case of [Riemannian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_manifolds), the key assumption of his compactness theorem is automatically satisfied under an assumption on [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582). These theorems have been widely used in the fields of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) and [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Metric compactness theorem"}

## Metric compactness theorem {#Metric_compactness_theorem}

The [Gromov–Hausdorff distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov–Hausdorff_metric) defines a notion of distance between any two [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018), thereby setting up the concept of a sequence of metric spaces which converges to another metric space. This is known as [Gromov–Hausdorff convergence](wikipedia://en/482366). Gromov found a condition on a sequence of [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness_%28topology%29) metric spaces which ensures that a [subsequence](wikipedia://en/236098) converges to some metric space relative to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance:

> Let $(X_{i}, d_{i})$ be a sequence of compact metric spaces with uniformly bounded diameter. Suppose that for every positive number $\varepsilon$ there is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $N$ and, for every $i$, the set $X_{i}$ can be covered by $N$ [metric balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_ball) of radius $\varepsilon$. Then the sequence $(X_{i}, d_{i})$ has a subsequence which converges relative to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

The role of this theorem in the theory of Gromov–Hausdorff convergence may be considered as analogous to the role of the [Arzelà–Ascoli theorem](wikipedia://en/596622) in the theory of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652). Gromov first formally introduced it in his 1981 resolution of the [Milnor–Wolf conjecture](wikipedia://en/635483) in the field of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), where he applied it to define the [asymptotic cone](wikipedia://en/18609360) of certain metric spaces. These techniques were later extended by Gromov and others, using the theory of [ultrafilters](wikipedia://en/31911).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Riemannian compactness theorem"}

## Riemannian compactness theorem {#Riemannian_compactness_theorem}

Specializing to the setting of [geodesically complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesically_complete) [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) with a fixed lower bound on the [Ricci curvature](wikipedia://en/276582), the crucial covering condition in Gromov's metric compactness theorem is automatically satisfied as a corollary of the [Bishop–Gromov volume comparison theorem](wikipedia://en/929870). As such, it follows that:

> Consider a sequence of [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) Riemannian manifolds with a uniform lower bound on the Ricci curvature and a uniform upper bound on the diameter. Then there is a subsequence which converges relative to the Gromov–Hausdorff distance.

The limit of a convergent subsequence may be a metric space without any smooth or Riemannian structure. This special case of the metric compactness theorem is significant in the field of [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), as it isolates the purely metric consequences of lower Ricci curvature bounds.

:::
