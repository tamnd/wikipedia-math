# Complex algebraic variety

*Image: RiemannKugel.svg.* The [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799) is one of the simplest complex algebraic varieties.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex algebraic variety"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **complex algebraic variety** is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) (in the [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) sense or otherwise) over the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Chow's theorem"}

## Chow's theorem {#Chow's_theorem}

[Chow's theorem](wikipedia://en/630505#Chow.27s_theorem) states that a projective [complex analytic variety](wikipedia://en/18748850), i.e., a closed analytic subvariety of the [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) $\mathbb{C}\mathbf{P}^n$, is an algebraic variety. These are usually simply referred to as [projective varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_varieties).

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Hironaka's theorem"}

## Hironaka's theorem {#Hironaka's_theorem}

Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety. Then there is a [projective resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_resolution) of [singularities](wikipedia://en/981915) $X' \to X$.[^o7]

:::

## Relation with similar concepts {#Relation_with_similar_concepts}

Despite Chow's theorem, not every complex analytic variety is a complex algebraic variety.
