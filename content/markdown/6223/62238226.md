# Tara E. Brendle

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tara E. Brendle"}

**Tara Elise Brendle** FRSE is an American mathematician who works in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), which involves the intersection of [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) and [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774). In particular, she studies mapping class group of surfaces, including [braid groups](wikipedia://en/550138), and their relationship to [automorphism groups](wikipedia://en/56661772) of [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735) and [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741). She is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Brendle received her B.S. in mathematics, magna cum laude, from [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) in 1995. At Haverford, she won All Middle-Atlantic Conference honors in 1992 for her [volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball) playing, and won honorable mention in the 1995 [Alice T. Schafer Prize for Excellence in Mathematics by an Undergraduate Woman](wikipedia://en/57181252) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) for her undergraduate research in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). She received her M.A. in mathematics from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1996 and went on to complete her Ph.D. at Columbia under the supervision of [Joan Birman](wikipedia://en/8625257) in 2002.. After receiving her Ph.D. from Columbia, Brendle was a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) VIGRE Assistant Professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) and an assistant professor at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University). She moved to her present position at the [University of Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Glasgow) in 2008.

Recent:

- Elected Member of the Council of the London Mathematical Society (2014-20)
- Member of the Scottish Government advisory group Making Maths Count to oversee and drive forward policy and practice to transform Scotland into a maths-positive nation (2015-21)
- President of the Edinburgh Mathematical Society (2023-25); Vice President (2025-26)

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Brendle became a member of the Young Academy of Scotland in 2014. She was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in the 2020 class, "for contributions to topology and geometry, for expository lectures, and for service to the profession aimed at the full participation of women in mathematics." She became a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 2021, and in the same year won the [Senior Whitehead Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Whitehead_Prize) "for her fundamental work in geometric group theory, concentrating on the study of groups arising in low-dimensional topology, and for her exemplary record of work in support of mathematics and mathematicians". She was invited to give a Mary Cartwright Lecture in 2023.

## Work {#Work}

In 2026, Brendle, with Vasudha Bharathram and [Joan Birman](wikipedia://en/8625257) posted a proof of the faithfulness of the [Burau representation](wikipedia://en/19140638) when n = 4 to [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv), whose status had not previously been known.[^o6]
