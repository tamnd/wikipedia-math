# Thomas H. Brylawski

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas H. Brylawski"}

**Thomas Henry Brylawski** (June 17, 1944 – July 18, 2007) was an American mathematician and professor at the [University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina,_Chapel_Hill). He worked primarily in [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Brylawski was born in 1944, and grew up in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o4][^o5] He attended the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) for his undergraduate degree, finishing with a Bachelor of Science in 1966. He then went on to [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) for his graduate work. He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) under the direction of [Gian-Carlo Rota](wikipedia://en/101891) and Robert Norman in 1970. After his PhD, he moved to the [University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina,_Chapel_Hill), where he spent the rest of his career.[^o7][^o4]

Brylawski was an editor for the *[Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society)* from 1977 until 1989.[^o8] Brylawski wrote 40 mathematical publications,[^o9] and advised 6 PhD students.[^o6]

He died in 2007 of [esophageal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer) at the Duke Hospice inpatient facility in [Hillsborough, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough,_North_Carolina).[^o5]

## Work {#Work}

Brylawski's early work used ideas and tools from [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) to understand the [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303) of a matroid. Indeed, this idea already appeared in his thesis, which made constructions in matroid theory similar to the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790).[^o11][^o12] He developed similar ideas in two papers in the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*.[^o4][^o13][^o14] Another influential early paper of Brylawski's, published in the same journal, described the influence of a [modular](wikipedia://en/1089311#Modular_pairs_and_related_notions) element in the lattice of [flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_%28matroids%29) on the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/244321#Characteristic_polynomial) of a matroid.[^o4][^o15]

Brylawski also contributed expository chapters to several matroid theory books that appeared in the *Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications* series published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press).[^o4] The Tutte polynomial chapter (written jointly with [James Oxley](wikipedia://en/38875933)) has around 500 citations.[^o16]

In addition to his work in matroid theory, Brylawski also had an interest in mathematics in art, particularly in the role of symmetry in art.[^o4] He gave lectures on mathematics in art on two occasions at the [National Gallery of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o17][^o18]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

A memorial conference was held in honor of Brylawski in October 2008 at the [University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina,_Chapel_Hill),[^o4] and a special issue of the *[European Journal of Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journal_of_Combinatorics)* in 2011 was dedicated as a tribute to the work of Brylawski.[^o20]
