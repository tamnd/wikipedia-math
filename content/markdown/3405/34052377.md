# Cyclotruncated simplicial honeycomb

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclotruncated simplicial honeycomb"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **cyclotruncated simplicial honeycomb** (or **cyclotruncated n-simplex honeycomb**) is a dimensional infinite series of [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654), based on the symmetry of the ${\tilde{A}}_n$ affine [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004). It is given a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) t<sub>0,1</sub>{3<sup>\[n+1\]</sup>}, and is represented by a [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) as a cyclic graph of *n+1* nodes with two adjacent nodes ringed. It is composed of n-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) facets, along with all [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) n-simplices.

:::

It is also called a **[Kagome lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagome_lattice)** in two and three dimensions, although it is not a lattice.

In n-dimensions, each can be seen as a set of *n+1* sets of parallel [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) that divide space. Each hyperplane contains the same honeycomb of one dimension lower.

In 1-dimension, the honeycomb represents an [apeirogon](wikipedia://en/5658261), with alternately colored [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860). In 2-dimensions, the honeycomb represents the [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397), with Coxeter graph . In 3-dimensions it represents the [quarter cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3757507), with Coxeter graph filling space with alternately tetrahedral and truncated tetrahedral cells. In 4-dimensions it is called a [cyclotruncated 5-cell honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotruncated_5-cell_honeycomb), with Coxeter graph , with [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821), [truncated 5-cell](wikipedia://en/4046891), and [bitruncated 5-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated_5-cell) facets. In 5-dimensions it is called a [cyclotruncated 5-simplex honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31737243), with Coxeter graph , filling space by [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex), [truncated 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_5-simplex), and [bitruncated 5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated_5-simplex) facets. In 6-dimensions it is called a [cyclotruncated 6-simplex honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotruncated_6-simplex_honeycomb), with Coxeter graph , filling space by [6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex), [truncated 6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_6-simplex), [bitruncated 6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated_6-simplex), and [tritruncated 6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tritruncated_6-simplex) facets.

## Projection by folding {#Projection_by_folding}

The cyclotruncated (2*n*+1)- and 2*n*-simplex honeycombs and (2*n*−1)-simplex honeycombs can be projected into the n-dimensional [hypercubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/12237982) by a [geometric folding](wikipedia://en/5358554#Geometric_folding) operation that maps two pairs of mirrors into each other, sharing the same [vertex arrangement](wikipedia://en/14308644):
