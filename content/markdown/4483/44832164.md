# Ruth J. Williams

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth J. Williams"}

**Ruth Jeannette Williams** is an Australian-born American mathematician at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) where she holds the Charles Lee Powell Chair as a Distinguished Professor of Mathematics. Her research concerns [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Williams was born in Australia and moved to the United States in 1978.[^o17]

Williams graduated from the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne) with a Bachelor of Sciences, with honors, in 1976 and a Master of Science in mathematics in 1978.[^o5] Williams went on to earn her Ph.D. from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1983, under the supervision of [Chung Kai-lai](wikipedia://en/23576590).[^o7]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Williams was president of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics) from 2011 to 2012.

Williams is a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) and a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics), the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences),[^o9] and the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376).[^o10] In 1998 she was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o11] Williams was an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS) Council member at large.[^o12]

Her other awards and honors include:

- Alfred P. Sloan Fellow (1988) [^o13]
- Guggenheim Fellow (2001)[^o14]
- Best Publication Award of the INFORMS Applied Probability Society (2007), jointly with [Amber L. Puha](wikipedia://en/70551889) and [H. Christian Gromoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Christian_Gromoll)[^o15]
- [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize) (2016), jointly with [Martin I. Reiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_I._Reiman) for "seminal research contributions over the past several decades, to the theory and applications of stochastic networks/systems and their heavy traffic approximations".[^o15][^o16]
- Honorary Doctorate from the University of Melbourne (2018) [^o17]
- [Award for the Advancement of Women in Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WORMS_Award) (2017), annual INFORMS meeting[^o15]
- Honorary Doctor of Science degree from La Trobe University in Australia[^o15]
- National Science Foundation Presidential Young Investigator[^o15]
