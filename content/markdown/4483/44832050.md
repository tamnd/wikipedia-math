# Brooke Shipley

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Brooke Shipley"}

**Brooke Elizabeth Shipley** is an American mathematician. She works as a professor at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago), where she was head of the Department of Mathematics, Statistics and Computer Science from 2014 to 2022.[^o3] Her research concerns [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) and [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).[^o9]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Shipley graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1990.[^o3] She earned her Ph.D. in 1995 from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), under the supervision of [Haynes Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haynes_Miller), for her work on the convergence of the homology [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) of a cosimplicial space.[^o5]

Shipley then was awarded a NSF Postdoctoral Research Fellowship. After postdoctoral studies at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), she joined the faculty of [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in 1998 and earned tenure there in 2002.[^o6] She then moved to [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago) in 2003.[^o3]

In 2009, Shipley became Co-Principal Investigator on UIC's National Science Foundation's ADVANCE grant to support the Women in Science and Engineering System Transformation (WISEST) program. She served as the director of WISEST from 2012 to 2013.[^o6] She served as an [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Council member at large from 2018 to 2020.[^o7]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 2014, she was elected as a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to homotopy theory and homological algebra as well as for service to the mathematical community."[^o9] Then in 2016, she became a representative of the Committee of Academic Sponsors at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute)[^o10]

She and John Greenlees were the joint winners of the 2022 [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Berwick_Prize) for their paper "An algebraic model for rational torus-equivariant spectra".[^o11]
