# Éva Tardos

Tardos Éva

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Éva Tardos"}

**Éva Tardos** (; born 1 October 1957) is a Hungarian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and the [Jacob Gould Schurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Gould_Schurman) Professor of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), known for her work in theoretical computer science.[^o7] For her work, she has received the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) (1988), the [Dantzig Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dantzig_Prize) (2006), and the [IEEE John von Neumann Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_John_von_Neumann_Medal) (2019).[^o7]

:::

Tardos's research interest is algorithms and algorithmic game theory. Her work focuses on the design and analysis of efficient methods for [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555) problems on graphs or networks. She has done some work on network flow algorithms like [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105) for [network flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_flow_%28function%29), cut, and clustering problems. Her recent work focuses on [algorithmic game theory](wikipedia://en/16334749) and simple auctions.[^o3]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Tardos received her Dipl. Math in 1981 and her Ph.D. 1984 from the [Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELTE_Faculty_of_Sciences) of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) under her advisor [András Frank](wikipedia://en/22912777).

She was the Chair of the Department of Computer Science at Cornell from 2006 to 2010, and she is currently serving as the Associate Dean of the [College of Computing and Information Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Ann_S._Bowers_College_of_Computing_and_Information_Science).[^o6]

She was editor-in-chief of *[SIAM Journal on Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Journal_on_Computing)* from 2004 to 2009, and from 2015 until 2021, she was editor-in-chief of the *[Journal of the ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_ACM)* (*JACM*).[^o7] and is currently the [Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [Computation](wikipedia://en/5926) area editor of the *Journal of the ACM* as well as on the board of editors of *[Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_Computing)*.[^o8]

She has co-authored, with [Jon Kleinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Kleinberg), the 2005 textbook *Algorithm Design* (). Along with being a co-editor of 2007 textbook *Algorithmic Game Theory,* working alongside [Noam Nisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Nisan), [Tim Roughgarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Roughgarden), and [Vijay V. Vazirani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Vazirani).[^o9]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

Tardos has been elected to the National Academy of Engineering (2007), the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) (2013)[^o11] and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) (2020)[^o12] She is also an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) (since 1998), a Fellow of [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS),[^o13] and a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (2013)[^o14] She is the recipient of [Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packard_Fellow), [Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Research_Fellowship), and [Guggenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) fellowships.[^o15]

She is the winner of the [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916) (1988), the [George B. Dantzig Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dantzig_Prize) (2006),[^o16] the [Van Wijngaarden Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Wijngaarden_Award) (2011), the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) (2012)[^o17] and the [EATCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EATCS) Award (2017),[^o18] In 2018, the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) and [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) selected her as their annual [Sonia Kovalevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Kovalevsky) Lecturer.[^o19] In 2019 she was awarded the [IEEE John von Neumann Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_John_von_Neumann_Medal).[^o3]

Tardos was named the ACM Athena Lecturer for 2022-2023, for her "fundamental research contributions to combinatorial optimization, approximation algorithms, and algorithmic game theory, and for dedicated mentoring and service to these communities."[^o20][^o21]

## Personal {#Personal}

Tardos is married to [David Shmoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shmoys), a fellow professor at Cornell. Her younger brother, [Gábor Tardos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gábor_Tardos), is a mathematics professor in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866).[^o23] Her father was Márton Tardos, a Hungarian economist and politician.[^o24] Her mother was child psychologist Anna Tardos, and her grandmother was child psychologist [Emmi Pikler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmi_Pikler).[^o24][^o25]
