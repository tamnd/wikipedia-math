# Equivariant cohomology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equivariant cohomology"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **equivariant cohomology** (or *Borel cohomology*) is a [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory) from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) which applies to [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) with a [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29). It can be viewed as a common generalization of [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) and an ordinary [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory).

:::

If $G$ acts freely, then the equivariant cohomology ring is just the singular cohomology ring of the quotient space $X / G$. In particular, if $G$ is the [trivial group](wikipedia://en/193059), then the equivariant cohomology ring is the ordinary [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of $X$. If $X$ is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible), it reduces to the cohomology ring of the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) $BG$ (that is, the group cohomology of $G$ when $G$ is finite.)

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $X$ be a topological space, with a (left) action by a group $G$. Let $EG$ be a contractible space with a free (right) action by the same group. Define $EG \times_G X$ to be the quotient space $(EG \times X) / {\sim}$, where the equivalence relation $\sim$ identifies $(e \cdot g, x)$ with $(e, g \cdot x)$ for all $e \in EG, x \in X, g \in G$. The equivariant cohomology ring of $X$ (with respect to the group $G$) is then defined as the singular cohomology ring of this space:[^o4]

$$H^*_G(X) := H^*(EG \times_G X).$$

The space $EG \times_G X$ is known as the **Borel construction**, or **[homotopy quotient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_quotient)**. Projection onto the first factor gives it the structure of a [fibre bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) over the classifying space $BG$. This is the [associated](wikipedia://en/398874) $X$-bundle obtained from the action of $G$ on $X$ and the [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) $EG \to BG$. The bundle $X \to EG \times_G X \to BG$ is called the Borel fibration.

The group cohomology ring of $G$ can be recovered by taking $X$ to be a point, as in this case the homotopy quotient is just the classifying space of $G$. If $G$ acts freely on $X$, then the canonical map $EG \times_G X \to X/G$ is a homotopy equivalence and so one gets $H_G^*(X) = H^*(X/G).$

It is also possible to define the equivariant cohomology $H_G^*(X;A)$ of $X$ with coefficients in a $G$-module *A*; these are [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974). This construction is the analogue of cohomology with local coefficients.

If *X* is a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470), *G* a [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) and coefficients are taken in the field of real numbers or the field of complex numbers (the most typical situation), then the above cohomology may be computed using the so-called [Cartan model](wikipedia://en/4110341) (see [equivariant differential forms](wikipedia://en/41418803).)

The construction should not be confused with other cohomology theories, such as [Bredon cohomology](wikipedia://en/43619732) or the cohomology of [invariant differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_differential_form): if *G* is a compact Lie group, then, by the averaging argument, any form may be made invariant; thus, cohomology of invariant differential forms does not yield new information.

[Koszul duality](wikipedia://en/42478011) is known to hold between equivariant cohomology and ordinary cohomology.

### Relation with groupoid cohomology {#Relation_with_groupoid_cohomology}

For a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923) $\mathfrak{X} = [X_1 \rightrightarrows X_0]$ equivariant cohomology of a smooth manifold[^o6] is a special example of the groupoid cohomology of a Lie groupoid. This is because given a $G$-space $X$ for a compact Lie group $G$, there is an associated groupoid

> $$\mathfrak{X}_G = [G\times X \rightrightarrows X]$$

whose equivariant cohomology groups can be computed using the Cartan complex $\Omega_G^\bullet(X)$ which is the totalization of the de-Rham double complex of the groupoid. The terms in the Cartan complex are

> $$\Omega^n_G(X) = \bigoplus_{2k+i = n}(\text{Sym}^k(\mathfrak{g}^\vee)\otimes \Omega^i(X))^G$$

where $\text{Sym}^\bullet(\mathfrak{g}^\vee)$ is the [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098) of the dual Lie algebra from the Lie group $G$, and $(-)^G$ corresponds to the $G$-invariant forms. This is a particularly useful tool for computing the cohomology of $BG$ for a compact Lie group $G$ since this can be computed as the cohomology of

> $$[G \rightrightarrows *]$$

where the action is trivial on a point. Then,

> $$H^*_{dR}(BG) = \bigoplus_{k\geq 0 }\text{Sym}^{2k}(\mathfrak{g}^\vee)^G$$

For example,

> $$\begin{aligned} H^*_{dR}(BU(1)) &= \bigoplus_{k=0}\text{Sym}^{2k}(\mathbb{R}^\vee) \\ &\cong \mathbb{R}[t] \\ &\text{ where } \deg(t) = 2 \end{aligned}$$

since the $U(1)$-action on the dual Lie algebra is trivial.

## An example of a homotopy quotient {#An_example_of_a_homotopy_quotient}

The following example is Proposition 1 of <http://www.math.harvard.edu/~lurie/282ynotes/LectureIV-Approaches.pdf>.

Let *X* be a complex projective [algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260). We identify *X* as a topological space with the set of the complex points $X(\mathbb{C})$, which is a compact [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181). Let *G* be a complex simply connected semisimple Lie group. Then any principal *G*-bundle on *X* is isomorphic to a trivial bundle, since the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) $BG$ is [2-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-connected) and *X* has real dimension 2. Fix some smooth *G*-bundle $P_\text{sm}$ on *X*. Then any principal *G*-bundle on $X$ is isomorphic to $P_\text{sm}$. In other words, the set $\Omega$ of all isomorphism classes of pairs consisting of a principal *G*-bundle on *X* and a complex-analytic structure on it can be identified with the set of complex-analytic structures on $P_\text{sm}$ or equivalently the set of holomorphic connections on *X* (since connections are integrable for dimension reason). $\Omega$ is an infinite-dimensional complex affine space and is therefore contractible.

Let $\mathcal{G}$ be the group of all automorphisms of $P_\text{sm}$ (i.e., [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group).) Then the homotopy quotient of $\Omega$ by $\mathcal{G}$ classifies complex-analytic (or equivalently algebraic) principal *G*-bundles on *X*; i.e., it is precisely the classifying space $B\mathcal{G}$ of the discrete group $\mathcal{G}$.

One can define the [moduli stack of principal bundles](wikipedia://en/41790574) $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$ as the [quotient stack](wikipedia://en/40579087) $[\Omega/\mathcal{G}]$ and then the homotopy quotient $B\mathcal{G}$ is, by definition, the [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type) of $\operatorname{Bun}_G(X)$.

## Equivariant characteristic classes {#Equivariant_characteristic_classes}

Let *E* be an [equivariant vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant_vector_bundle) on a *G*-manifold *M*. It gives rise to a vector bundle $\widetilde{E}$ on the homotopy quotient $EG \times_G M$ so that it pulls-back to the bundle $\widetilde{E}=EG \times E$ over $EG \times M$. An equivariant [characteristic class](wikipedia://en/363530) of *E* is then an ordinary characteristic class of $\widetilde{E}$, which is an element of the completion of the cohomology ring $H^*(EG \times_G M) = H^*_G(M)$. (In order to apply [Chern–Weil theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern–Weil_theory), one uses a finite-dimensional approximation of *EG*.)

Alternatively, one can first define an equivariant [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) and then define other characteristic classes as invariant polynomials of Chern classes as in the ordinary case; for example, the equivariant [Todd class](wikipedia://en/1568489) of an equivariant line bundle is the [Todd function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_function) evaluated at the equivariant first Chern class of the bundle. (An equivariant Todd class of a line bundle is a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) (not a polynomial as in the non-equivariant case) in the equivariant first Chern class; hence, it belongs to the completion of the equivariant cohomology ring.)

In the non-equivariant case, the first Chern class can be viewed as a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between the set of all isomorphism classes of [complex line](wikipedia://en/7975294) bundles on a manifold *M* and $H^2(M; \mathbb{Z}).$[^o9] In the equivariant case, this translates to: the equivariant first Chern gives a bijection between the set of all isomorphism classes of equivariant complex line bundles and $H^2_G(M; \mathbb{Z})$.
