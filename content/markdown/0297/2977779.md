# Friedrich Hartogs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrich Hartogs"}

**Friedrich Moritz "Fritz" Hartogs** (20 May 1874 – 18 August 1943) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)-[Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) mathematician, known for his work on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and foundational results on [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Hartogs was the son of the merchant Gustav Hartogs and his wife Elise Feist and grew up in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main). He studied at the [Königliche Technische Hochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Königliche_Technische_Hochschule) in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), at the Technische Hochschule Charlottenburg, at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin), and at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), graduating with a doctorate in 1903 that was supervised by [Alfred Pringsheim](wikipedia://en/1068126). He obtained his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1905 and was [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) and from 1910 professor at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), until 1927 as extraordinary professor and afterward as ordinary professor. As a Jew, he suffered greatly under the Nazi regime: he was fired in 1935, was mistreated and briefly interned in [Dachau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp) in 1938, and eventually committed suicide in 1943.

## Work {#Work}

Hartogs' main work was in [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) where he is known for [Hartogs's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartogs's_theorem), [Hartogs's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartogs's_lemma) (also known as Hartogs's principle or Hartogs's extension theorem) and the concepts of holomorphic hull and [domain of holomorphy](wikipedia://en/3236377).

In set theory, he contributed to the theory of [well-orders](wikipedia://en/33456) and proved what is also known as [Hartogs's theorem](wikipedia://en/2977752): for every set *x* there is a well-ordered set that cannot be injectively embedded in *x*. The smallest such set is known as the [Hartogs number](wikipedia://en/2977752) or Hartogs Aleph of *x*.
