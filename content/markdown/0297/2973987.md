# Penrose graphical notation

*Image: Matrix product state obc tikz.svg.* Penrose graphical notation (tensor diagram notation) of a [matrix product state](wikipedia://en/42099889) of five particles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Penrose graphical notation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), **Penrose graphical notation** or **tensor diagram notation** is a (usually handwritten) visual depiction of [multilinear functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilinear_function) or [tensors](wikipedia://en/29965) proposed by [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193) in 1971.[^o2] A diagram in the notation consists of several shapes linked together by lines.

:::

The notation widely appears in modern [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25202), particularly in [matrix product states](wikipedia://en/42099889) and [quantum circuits](wikipedia://en/707404). In particular, [categorical quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/28267626) (which includes [ZX-calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZX-calculus)) is a fully comprehensive reformulation of quantum theory in terms of Penrose diagrams.

The notation has been studied extensively by [Predrag Cvitanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predrag_Cvitanović), who used it, along with [Feynman's diagrams](wikipedia://en/11617) and other related notations in developing "birdtracks", a group-theoretical diagram to classify the [classical Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Lie_groups).[^o3] Penrose's notation has also been generalized using [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) to [spin networks](wikipedia://en/173196) in physics, and with the presence of [matrix groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_group) to [trace diagrams](wikipedia://en/14674709) in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).

## Interpretations {#Interpretations}

### Multilinear algebra {#Multilinear_algebra}

In the language of [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946), each shape represents a [multilinear function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilinear_function). The lines attached to shapes represent the inputs or outputs of a function, and attaching shapes together in some way is essentially the [composition of functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_of_functions).

### Tensors {#Tensors}

In the language of [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/29965), a particular tensor is associated with a particular shape with many lines projecting upwards and downwards, corresponding to [abstract](wikipedia://en/1674987) [upper and lower](wikipedia://en/202886) indices of tensors respectively. Connecting lines between two shapes corresponds to [contraction of indices](wikipedia://en/202722). One advantage of this [notation](wikipedia://en/277184) is that one does not have to invent new letters for new indices. This notation is also explicitly [basis](wikipedia://en/18420)-independent.[^o7]

### Matrices {#Matrices}

Each shape represents a matrix, and [tensor multiplication](wikipedia://en/30844) is done horizontally, and [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280) is done vertically.

## Representation of special tensors {#Representation_of_special_tensors}

### Metric tensor {#Metric_tensor}

The [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) is represented by a U-shaped loop or an upside-down U-shaped loop, depending on the type of tensor that is used.

| *Image: Penrose g.svg.* metric tensor $g^{ab}$ | *Image: Penrose g ab.svg.* metric tensor $g_{ab}$ |
|---|---|

### Levi-Civita tensor {#Levi-Civita_tensor}

The [Levi-Civita antisymmetric tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi-Civita_tensor) is represented by a thick horizontal bar with sticks pointing downwards or upwards, depending on the type of tensor that is used.

| *Image: Penrose varepsilon a-n.svg.* $\varepsilon_{ab\ldots n}$ | *Image: Penrose epsilon^a-n.svg.* $\varepsilon^{ab\ldots n}$ | *Image: Penrose varepsilon a-n epsilon^a-n.svg.* $\varepsilon_{ab\ldots n}\,\varepsilon^{ab\ldots n}$$= n!$ |
|---|---|---|

### Structure constant {#Structure_constant}

*Image: Penrose gamma ab^c.svg.* Structure constant ${\gamma_{\alpha\beta}}^\chi = -{\gamma_{\beta\alpha}}^\chi$

The structure constants (${\gamma_{ab}}^c$) of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) are represented by a small triangle with one line pointing upwards and two lines pointing downwards.

## Tensor operations {#Tensor_operations}

### Contraction of indices {#Contraction_of_indices}

[Contraction](wikipedia://en/202722) of indices is represented by joining the index lines together.

| *Image: Penrose delta^a b.svg.* [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890) $\delta^a_b$ | *Image: Penrose beta a xi^a.svg.* [Dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) $\beta_a\,\xi^a$ | *Image: Penrose g ab g^bc-d^c a-g^cb g ba.svg.* $g_{ab}\,g^{bc} = \delta_a^c = g^{cb}\,g_{ba}$ |
|---|---|---|

### Symmetrization {#Symmetrization}

[Symmetrization](wikipedia://en/23087738) of indices is represented by a thick zigzag or wavy bar crossing the index lines horizontally.

| *Image: Penrose asymmetric Q^a-n.svg.* Symmetrization$Q^{(ab\ldots n)}$(with ${}_{Q^{ab}=Q^{[ab]}+Q^{(ab)}}$) |
|---|

### Antisymmetrization {#Antisymmetrization}

[Antisymmetrization](wikipedia://en/696955) of indices is represented by a thick straight line crossing the index lines horizontally.

| *Image: Penrose symmetric E a-n.svg.* Antisymmetrization$E_{[ab\ldots n]}$(with ${}_{E_{ab}=E_{[ab]}+E_{(ab)}}$) |
|---|

## Determinant {#Determinant}

The determinant is formed by applying antisymmetrization to the indices.

| *Image: Penrose det T.svg.* [Determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) $\det\mathbf{T} = \det\left(T^a_{\ b}\right)$ | *Image: Penrose T^-1.svg.* Inverse of matrix $\mathbf{T}^{-1} = \left(T^a_{\ b}\right)^{-1}$ |
|---|---|

### Covariant derivative {#Covariant_derivative}

The [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) ($\nabla$) is represented by a circle around the tensor(s) to be differentiated and a line joined from the circle pointing downwards to represent the lower index of the derivative.

| *Image: Penrose covariant derivate.svg.* covariant derivative $12\nabla_a\left( \xi^f\,\lambda^{(d}_{fb[c}\,D^{e)b}_{gh]} \right)$ $= 12\left( \xi^f (\nabla_a \lambda^{(d}_{fb[c}) \, D^{e)b}_{gh]} + (\nabla_a \xi^f) \lambda^{(d}_{fb[c}\,D^{e)b}_{gh]} + \xi^f \lambda^{(d}_{fb[c} \, (\nabla_a D^{e)b}_{gh]} ) \right)$ |
|---|

## Tensor manipulation {#Tensor_manipulation}

The diagrammatic notation is useful in manipulating tensor algebra. It usually involves a few simple "[identities](wikipedia://en/298428)" of tensor manipulations.

For example, $\varepsilon_{a...c} \varepsilon^{a...c} = n!$, where *n* is the number of dimensions, is a common "identity".

### Riemann curvature tensor {#Riemann_curvature_tensor}

The Ricci and Bianchi identities given in terms of the Riemann curvature tensor illustrate the power of the notation

| *Image: Penrose riemann curvature tensor.svg.* Notation for the [Riemann curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) | *Image: Penrose ricci tensor.svg.* [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) $R_{ab} = R_{acb}^{\ \ \ c}$ | *Image: Penrose ricci identity.svg.* Ricci identity $(\nabla_a\,\nabla_b -\nabla_b\,\nabla_a)\,\mathbf{\xi}^d$$= R_{abc}^{\ \ \ d}\,\mathbf{\xi}^c$ | *Image: Penrose bianchi identity.svg.* [Bianchi identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianchi_identity) $\nabla_{[a} R_{bc]d}^{\ \ \ e} = 0$ |
|---|---|---|---|
