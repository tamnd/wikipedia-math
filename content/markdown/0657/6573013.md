# Cameron–Martin theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cameron–Martin theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Cameron–Martin theorem** or **Cameron–Martin formula** (named after [Robert Horton Cameron](wikipedia://en/37645495) and [W. T. Martin](wikipedia://en/37661503)) is a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) that describes how [abstract Wiener measure](wikipedia://en/6592746) changes under [translation](wikipedia://en/204682) by certain elements of the Cameron–Martin [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

The standard [Gaussian measure](wikipedia://en/6572909) $\gamma^n$ on $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbf{R}^n$ is not translation-[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638). (In fact, there is a unique translation invariant [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933) up to scale by [Haar's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haar's_theorem): the $n$-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198), denoted here $dx$.) Instead, a measurable subset $A$ has [Gaussian measure](wikipedia://en/6572909)

$$\gamma_n(A) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_A \exp\left(-\tfrac12\langle x, x\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}\right)\,dx.$$

Here $\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}$ refers to the standard Euclidean [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) in $\mathbf{R}^n$. The Gaussian measure of the translation of $A$ by a vector $h \in \mathbf{R}^n$ is

$$\begin{aligned} \gamma_n(A-h) &= \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_A \exp\left(-\tfrac12\langle x-h, x-h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}\right)\,dx\\[4pt] &=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\int_A \exp\left(\frac{2\langle x, h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n} - \langle h, h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}}{2}\right)\exp\left(-\tfrac12\langle x, x\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}\right)\,dx. \end{aligned}$$

So under translation through $h$, the Gaussian measure scales by the distribution function appearing in the last display:

$$\exp\left(\frac{2\langle x, h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n} - \langle h, h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n}}{2} \right)=\exp\left(\langle x, h\rangle_{\mathbf R^n} - \tfrac12\|h\|_{\mathbf R^n}^ 2\right).$$

The measure that associates to the set $A$ the number $\gamma_n(A - h)$ is the [pushforward measure](wikipedia://en/9412979), denoted $(T_h)_* (\gamma^n).$ Here $T_h : \mathbf{R}^n \to \mathbf{R}^n$ refers to the translation map: $T_h(x) = x + h$. The above calculation shows that the [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative) of the pushforward measure with respect to the original Gaussian measure is given by

$$\frac{\mathrm{d} (T_h)_{*} (\gamma^n)}{\mathrm{d} \gamma^n} (x) = \exp \left( \left \langle h, x \right \rangle_{\mathbf{R}^n} - \tfrac{1}{2} \| h \|_{\mathbf{R}^n}^2 \right).$$

The abstract Wiener measure $\gamma$ on a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $E$, where $i : H \to E$ is an [abstract Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6592746), is also a "Gaussian measure" in a suitable sense. How does it change under translation? It turns out that a similar formula to the one above holds if we consider only translations by elements of the [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) $i(H) \subseteq E$.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

### For abstract Wiener spaces (Banach space setting) {#For_abstract_Wiener_spaces_(Banach_space_setting)}

Let $i : H \to E$ be an abstract Wiener space with abstract Wiener measure $\gamma : \operatorname{Borel}(E) \to [0, 1]$. For $h \in H$, define $T_h : E \to E$ by $T_h(x) = x + i(h)$. Then $(T_h)_*(\gamma)$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/6612667) to $\gamma$ with Radon–Nikodym derivative

$$\frac{\mathrm{d} (T_{h})_{*} (\gamma)}{\mathrm{d} \gamma} (x) = \exp \left( \langle h, x \rangle^{\sim} - \tfrac{1}{2} \| h \|_{H}^{2} \right),$$

where

$$\langle h, x \rangle^{\sim} = i(h) (x)$$

denotes the [Paley–Wiener integral](wikipedia://en/6583010).

The Cameron–Martin formula is valid only for translations by elements of the dense subspace $i(H) \subseteq E$, called **Cameron–Martin space**, and not by arbitrary elements of $E$. If the Cameron–Martin formula did hold for arbitrary translations, it would contradict the following result:

If $E$ is a separable Banach space and $\mu$ is a locally finite [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on $E$ that is equivalent to its own push forward under any translation, then either $E$ has finite dimension or $\mu$ is the [trivial (zero) measure](wikipedia://en/10122069). (See [quasi-invariant measure](wikipedia://en/2749048).)

In fact, $\gamma$ is quasi-invariant under translation by an element $v$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $v \in i(H)$. Vectors in $i(H)$ are sometimes known as **Cameron–Martin directions**.

### General version for locally convex vector spaces {#General_version_for_locally_convex_vector_spaces}

Consider a [locally convex vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_vector_space) $E$, with a Gaussian measure $\gamma$ on the [cylindrical σ-algebra](wikipedia://en/21236657) $\sigma(\operatorname{Cyl}(E,E'))$ and let $\gamma_{m}:=\gamma(\cdot - m)$ denote the translation by $m\in E$. For an element in the topological dual $f\in E'$ define the distance to the mean $t_{\gamma}(f):=f-\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}[f],$ and denote the closure in $L^2(E,\gamma)$ as $E_a^{\gamma}:=\operatorname{clos}\left\{(t_{\gamma}(f_n))_{n}\colon\ f\in E'\right\}$. Define the covariance operator $\overline{R_{\gamma}}:E_a^{\gamma}\to (E')^*$ extended to the closure as

$$\overline{R_{\gamma}}(f)(g)=\langle f,g-\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}[g]\rangle_{L^2(\gamma)}.$$

Define the norm

$$\|h\|_{H_\gamma}:=\sup\{f(h)\colon f\in E',\; \overline{R_{\gamma}}(f)(f) \leq 1\},$$

then the **Cameron–Martin space** $H_\gamma$ of $\gamma$ in $E$ is

$$H_\gamma=\{h\in E\colon \|h\|_{H_\gamma}<\infty\}.$$

If for $h\in E$ there exists an $g\in E_a^{\gamma}$ such that $h=\overline{R_{\gamma}}(g)$ then $h\in H_\gamma$ and $\|h\|_{H_\gamma} = \|g\|_{L^2(\gamma)}$. Further there is [equivalence](wikipedia://en/6612667) $\gamma_h \sim \gamma$ with Radon-Nikodým density

$$\frac{d\gamma_h}{d\gamma}=\exp\left(g(x)-\frac{1}{2}\|h\|_{H_\gamma}^2\right).$$

If $h\not\in H_\gamma$ the two measures are [singular](wikipedia://en/4304500).[^o7]

## Integration by parts {#Integration_by_parts}

The Cameron–Martin formula gives rise to an [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) formula on $E$: if $F : E \to \mathbf{R}$ has [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727) $\mathrm{D}F : E \to \operatorname{Lin}(E; \mathbf{R}) = E^*$, integrating the Cameron–Martin formula with respect to Wiener measure on both sides gives

$$\int_{E} F(x + t i(h)) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (x) = \int_{E} F(x) \exp \left( t \langle h, x \rangle^{\sim} - \tfrac{1}{2} t^2 \| h \|_{H}^{2} \right) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (x)$$

for any $t \in \mathbf{R}$. Formally differentiating with respect to $t$ and evaluating at $t = 0$ gives the integration by parts formula

$$\int_E \mathrm{D} F(x) (i(h)) \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (x) = \int_E F(x) \langle h, x \rangle^\sim \, \mathrm{d} \gamma (x).$$

Comparison with the [divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864) of [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) suggests

$$\mathop{\mathrm{div}} [V_h] (x) = - \langle h, x \rangle^\sim,$$

where $V_h : E \to E$ is the constant "[vector field](wikipedia://en/62641)" $V_h(x) = i(h)$ for all $x \in E$. The wish to consider more general vector fields and to think of stochastic integrals as "divergences" leads to the study of [stochastic processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_processes) and the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), and, in particular, the [Clark–Ocone theorem](wikipedia://en/6599348) and its associated integration by parts formula.

## An application {#An_application}

Using Cameron–Martin theorem one may establish (See Liptser and Shiryayev 1977, p. 280) that for a $q \times q$ symmetric non-negative [definite matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definiteness_of_a_matrix) $H(t)$ whose elements $H_{j, k}(t)$ are continuous and satisfy the condition

$$\int_0^T \sum_{j,k=1} ^q |H_{j,k}(t)|\,dt < \infty,$$

it holds for a $q$−dimensional [Wiener process](wikipedia://en/149984) $w(t)$ that

$$E \left[ \exp \left( -\int_0^T w(t)^*H(t)w(t) \, dt \right) \right] = \exp \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} \int_0^T \operatorname{tr} (G(t)) \, dt \right],$$

where $G(t)$ is a $q \times q$ nonpositive definite matrix which is a unique solution of the matrix-valued [Riccati differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccati_differential_equation)

$$\frac{dG(t)}{dt} = 2H(t)-G^2(t)$$

with the boundary condition $G(T) = 0$.

In the special case of a one-dimensional Brownian motion where $H(t)=1/2$, the unique solution is $G(t)=\tanh(t-T)$, and we have the original formula as established by Cameron and Martin:

$$E\left[\exp\left(-\tfrac12\int_0^T w(t)^2\,dt\right)\right] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh T}}.$$
