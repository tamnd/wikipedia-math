# Dennis Gaitsgory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dennis Gaitsgory"}

**Dennis Gaitsgory** (born 17 November 1973) is an Israeli-American mathematician. He is a mathematician at [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) (MPIM) at Bonn and is known for his research on the [geometric Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Born in [Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chișinău) (now in [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova)) he grew up in [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan), before studying at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) under [Joseph Bernstein](wikipedia://en/5815383) (1990–1996). He received his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1997 for a thesis entitled "Automorphic Sheaves and Eisenstein Series". He has been awarded a [Harvard Junior Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Society_of_Fellows), a [Clay Research Fellowship](wikipedia://en/7655), the prize of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470), and a [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933) for his work.

His work in geometric Langlands resulted in a joint 2002 paper with [Edward Frenkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frenkel) and [Kari Vilonen](wikipedia://en/56918693),[^o4] establishing the conjecture for finite fields, and a separate 2004 paper,[^o5] generalizing the proof to include the field of complex numbers as well. This was followed by a series of papers in 2024 that claim to prove the (categorical, unramified) [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178), by a team of led by Gaitsgory and Sam Raskin; for this, Gaitsgory was awarded the 2025 Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics.

Prior to his current appointment at [MPIM](wikipedia://en/1386960) Bonn, he was a professor of mathematics at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) and an associate professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) from 2001–2005.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

In 2025, he received the [Breakthrough Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/43125933).[^o7]
