# Gaussian measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Gaussian measure** is a [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) on finite-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$, closely related to the [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). There is also a generalization to infinite-dimensional spaces. Gaussian measures are named after the [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125). One reason why Gaussian measures are so ubiquitous in probability theory is the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406). Loosely speaking, it states that if a random variable $X$ is obtained by summing a large number $N$ of independent random variables with variance 1, then $X$ has variance $N$ and its law is approximately Gaussian.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $n \in N$ and let $B_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$ denote the [completion](wikipedia://en/44775) of the [Borel $\sigma$-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra) on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Let $\lambda^n : B_0(\mathbb{R}^n) \to [0, +\infty]$ denote the usual $n$-dimensional [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198). Then the **standard Gaussian measure** $\gamma^n : B_0(\mathbb{R}^n) \to [0, 1]$ is defined by

$$\gamma^{n} (A) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi}^{n}} \int_{A} \exp \left( - \frac{1}{2} \left\| x \right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2} \right) \, \mathrm{d} \lambda^{n} (x)$$

for any measurable set $A \in B_0(\mathbb{R}^n)$. In terms of the [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative),

$$\frac{\mathrm{d} \gamma^{n}}{\mathrm{d} \lambda^{n}} (x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi}^{n}} \exp \left( - \frac{1}{2} \left\| x \right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2} \right).$$

More generally, the Gaussian measure with [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean) $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) $\sigma^2 > 0$ is given by

$$\gamma_{\mu, \sigma^{2}}^{n} (A) := \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi \sigma^{2}}^{n}} \int_{A} \exp \left( - \frac{1}{2 \sigma^{2}} \left\| x - \mu \right\|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2} \right) \, \mathrm{d} \lambda^{n} (x).$$

Gaussian measures with mean $\mu = 0$ are known as **centered Gaussian measures**.

The [Dirac measure](wikipedia://en/1019002) $\delta_\mu$ is the [weak limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures) of $\gamma_{\mu, \sigma^{2}}^{n}$ as $\sigma \to 0$, and is considered to be a **degenerate Gaussian measure**; in contrast, Gaussian measures with finite, non-zero variance are called **non-degenerate Gaussian measures**.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The standard Gaussian measure $\gamma^n$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$

- is a [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) (in fact, as remarked above, it is defined on the completion of the Borel sigma algebra, which is a finer structure);
- is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/6612667) to Lebesgue measure: $\lambda^{n} \ll \gamma^n \ll \lambda^n$, where $\ll$ stands for [absolute continuity](wikipedia://en/252670#Absolute_continuity_of_measures) of measures;
- is [supported](wikipedia://en/6591796) on all of Euclidean space: $\operatorname{supp}(\gamma^n) = \mathbb{R}^n$;
- is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) $(\gamma^n(\mathbb{R}^n) = 1)$, and so it is [locally finite](wikipedia://en/6621919);
- is [strictly positive](wikipedia://en/6607051): every non-empty [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) has positive measure;
- is [inner regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_regular_measure): for all Borel sets $A$, $$\gamma^n (A) = \sup \{ \gamma^n (K) \mid K \subseteq A, K \text{ is compact} \},$$ so Gaussian measure is a [Radon measure](wikipedia://en/521933);
- is not [translation](wikipedia://en/204682)-[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638), but does satisfy the relation $$\frac{\mathrm{d} (T_h)_{*} (\gamma^n)}{\mathrm{d} \gamma^n} (x) = \exp \left( \langle h, x \rangle_{\mathbb{R}^n} - \frac{1}{2} \| h \|_{\mathbb{R}^n}^2 \right),$$ where the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) on the left-hand side is the [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative), and $(T_h)_*(\gamma^n)$ is the [push forward](wikipedia://en/9412979) of standard Gaussian measure by the translation map $T_h : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$, $T_h(x) = x + h$;
- is the probability measure associated to a [normal](wikipedia://en/21462) [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543): $$Z \sim \operatorname{Normal} (\mu, \sigma^2) \implies \mathbb{P} (Z \in A) = \gamma_{\mu, \sigma^2}^n (A).$$

:::

## Infinite-dimensional spaces {#Infinite-dimensional_spaces}

It can be shown that [there is no analogue of Lebesgue measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_is_no_infinite-dimensional_Lebesgue_measure) on an infinite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). Even so, it is possible to define Gaussian measures on infinite-dimensional spaces, the main example being the [abstract Wiener space](wikipedia://en/6592746) construction. A Borel measure $\gamma$ on a [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $E$ is said to be a **non-degenerate (centered) Gaussian measure** if, for every [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $L \in E^*$ except $L = 0$, the [push-forward measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push-forward_measure) $L_*(\gamma)$ is a non-degenerate (centered) Gaussian measure on $\mathbb{R}$ in the sense defined above.

For example, [classical Wiener measure](wikipedia://en/6810363) on the space of [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [paths](wikipedia://en/1189553) is a Gaussian measure.
