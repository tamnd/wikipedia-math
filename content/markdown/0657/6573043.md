# Exceptional Lie algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exceptional Lie algebra"}

In mathematics, an **exceptional Lie algebra** is a complex [simple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/59500649) whose [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109) is of exceptional (nonclassical) type.[^o2] There are exactly five of them: $\mathfrak{g}_2, \mathfrak{f}_4, \mathfrak{e}_6, \mathfrak{e}_7, \mathfrak{e}_8$; their respective dimensions are 14, 52, 78, 133, 248.[^o3] The corresponding diagrams are:[^o4]

:::

- [G<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/292864): node
- [F<sub>4</sub>](wikipedia://en/292877): node
- [E<sub>6</sub>](wikipedia://en/293725): node
- [E<sub>7</sub>](wikipedia://en/649115): node
- [E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994): node

In contrast, simple Lie algebras that are not exceptional are called [classical Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Lie_algebra) (there are infinitely many of them).

::: {#o6 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

There is no simple universally accepted way to construct exceptional Lie algebras; in fact, they were discovered only in the process of the classification program. Here are some constructions:

- § 22.1-2 from Fulton and Harris' book[^o7] give a detailed construction of $\mathfrak{g}_2$.
- Exceptional Lie algebras may be realized as the derivation algebras of appropriate nonassociative algebras.
- Construct $\mathfrak{e}_8$ first and then find $\mathfrak{e}_6, \mathfrak{e}_7$ as subalgebras.
- [Tits](wikipedia://en/219570) has given a uniformed construction of the five exceptional Lie algebras.[^o8]

:::
