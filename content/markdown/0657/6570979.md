# Avi Wigderson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Avi Wigderson"}

**Avi Wigderson** (אבי ויגדרזון; born 9 September 1956[^o3]) is an Israeli [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is the Herbert H. Maass Professor in the school of mathematics at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), New Jersey, United States of America.[^o4] His research interests include [complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [parallel algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_algorithm), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), and [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing).[^o14] Wigderson received the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2021 for his work in [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392).[^o5] He also received the 2023 [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) for his contributions to the understanding of randomness in the theory of computation.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Early life and studies {#Early_life_and_studies}

Avi Wigderson was born in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa), Israel, to [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) survivors.[^o9] Wigderson is a graduate of the [Hebrew Reali School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Reali_School) in Haifa. He began his undergraduate studies at the [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion) in 1977 in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa), graduating in 1980.[^o10] In the Technion he met his wife Edna.[^o10] He went on to graduate study at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he received his Ph.D. in computer science in 1983 after completing a doctoral dissertation, titled "Studies in [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511)", under the supervision of [Richard Lipton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lipton).[^o11][^o12] He is credited with significantly expanding the field of computational complexity.[^o10]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

After short-term positions at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), the [IBM Almaden Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Almaden_Research_Center) in [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in Berkeley, he returned to Israel and joined the faculty of the [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) in 1986. He received tenure in 1987 and became a full professor in 1991.[^o10] In 1999 he also took a position at the Institute for Advanced Study, and in 2003 he gave up his Hebrew University position to take up full-time residence at the [IAS](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o14]

Wigderson investigated computational questions and specifically the role of randomness in the field. Together with [Noam Nisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Nisan) and [Russell Impagliazzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Impagliazzo), Wigderson discovered that for algorithms that solve problems through coin flipping, there exists an algorithm that is almost as fast that does not use coin flipping as long as presets are met.[^o10]

Wigderson developed the [Zig Zag product](wikipedia://en/24387478) together with [Omer Reingold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omer_Reingold) and [Salil Vadhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salil_Vadhan). The Zig Zag product links complexity theory, graph theory and group theory; for example, it can help one understand how to get out of a maze.[^o10] Today complexity theory is used in cryptography.[^o10]

Working with [Silvio Micali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Micali) and [Oded Goldreich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oded_Goldreich), Wigderson demonstrated that [zero-knowledge proofs](wikipedia://en/450541) can be utilized in proving public results on secret data in secret.[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Wigderson's son, Yuval Wigderson, is a professor of mathematics at the [Institute of Science and Technology Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Science_and_Technology_Austria).[^o16]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

- 1994: [Nevanlinna Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevanlinna_Prize) for his work on [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543).[^o18]
- 2009: The [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) for work on the [zig-zag product of graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zig-zag_product_of_graphs), a method of combining smaller graphs to produce larger ones used in the construction of [expander graphs](wikipedia://en/9313).[^o19]
- 2011: Elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o20]
- 2018: Elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) for "contributions to theoretical computer science and mathematics".[^o21]
- 2019: The [Knuth Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knuth_Prize) for his contributions to "the foundations of computer science in areas including randomized computation, cryptography, [circuit complexity](wikipedia://en/7641969), [proof complexity](wikipedia://en/2801284), parallel computation, and our understanding of fundamental graph properties".[^o22]
- 2021: Shared the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) with [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) "for their foundational contributions to theoretical computer science and discrete mathematics, and their leading role in shaping them into central fields of modern mathematics."[^o23][^o24][^o25]
- April 2024: The [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award), by the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery), for "reshaping our understanding of the role of randomness in computation, and for decades of intellectual leadership in theoretical computer science."[^o6][^o7]
- 2025: [Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Corporation_of_New_York) [Great Immigrant Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Immigrants_Award)[^o26]
