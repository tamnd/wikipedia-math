# Robert Blanché

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Blanché"}

**Robert Blanché** (1898–1975) was an associate professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at the [University of Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toulouse). He wrote many books addressing the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439).

:::

## About *Structures intellectuelles* {#About_Structures_intellectuelles}

*Image: Logical-hexagon.png.* The logical hexagon, co-discovered independently by Blanché and Augustin Sesmat extends the [square of opposition](wikipedia://en/246976) to six statements.

Robert Blanché died in 1975. Nine years before, in 1966, he published with Vrin: *Structures intellectuelles*. Therein, he deals with the [logical hexagon](wikipedia://en/29594530). Whereas the [logical square](wikipedia://en/246976) or square of Apuleius represents four values: A, E, I, O, the logical hexagon represents six, that is to say, not only A, E, I, O but also two new values: Y and U. It is advisable to read the article: [logical hexagon](wikipedia://en/29594530) as well what concerns [Indian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_logic).

In *La Logique et son histoire d' Aristote à Russell*, published with [Armand Colin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Colin) in 1970, Robert Blanché, the author of Structures intellectuelles (Vrin, 1966) mentions that [Józef Maria Bocheński](wikipedia://en/1029376) speaks of a sort of Indian logical triangle to be compared with the square of Aristotle (or square of Apuleius), in other words with the square of opposition.
