# Multipliers and centralizers (Banach spaces)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multipliers and centralizers"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **multipliers and centralizers** are algebraic objects in the study of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). They are used, for example, in generalizations of the [Banach–Stone theorem](wikipedia://en/12772382).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let (*X*, ‖·‖) be a Banach space over a field **K** (either the [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826)), and let Ext(*X*) be the set of [extreme points](wikipedia://en/454968) of the [closed unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_unit_ball) of the [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) *X*<sup>∗</sup>.

A [continuous linear operator](wikipedia://en/1802169) *T* : *X* → *X* is said to be a **multiplier** if every point *p* in Ext(*X*) is an [eigenvector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) for the [adjoint operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_operator) *T*<sup>∗</sup> : *X*<sup>∗</sup> → *X*<sup>∗</sup>. That is, there exists a function *a*<sub>*T*</sub> : Ext(*X*) → **K** such that

$$p \circ T = a_{T} (p) p \; \mbox{ for all } p \in \mathrm{Ext} (X),$$

making $a_{T} (p)$ the eigenvalue corresponding to *p*. Given two multipliers *S* and *T* on *X*, *S* is said to be an **adjoint** for *T* if

$$a_{S} = \overline{a_{T}},$$

i.e. *a*<sub>*S*</sub> agrees with *a*<sub>*T*</sub> in the real case, and with the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) of *a*<sub>*T*</sub> in the complex case.

The **centralizer** (or **commutant**) of *X*, denoted *Z*(*X*), is the set of all multipliers on *X* for which an adjoint exists.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The multiplier adjoint of a multiplier *T*, if it exists, is unique; the unique adjoint of *T* is denoted *T*<sup>∗</sup>.
- If the field **K** is the real numbers, then every multiplier on *X* lies in the centralizer of *X*.

:::
