# Oskar Perron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oskar Perron"}

**Oskar Perron** (7 May 1880 – 22 February 1975) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

He was a professor at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) from 1914 to 1922 and at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) from 1922 to 1951. He made numerous contributions to [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) and [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), including the [Perron method](wikipedia://en/26444076) to solve the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109) for [elliptic partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/662787). He wrote an encyclopedic book on [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199) *Die Lehre von den Kettenbrüchen*. He introduced *Perron's paradox* to illustrate the danger of assuming that the solution of an optimization problem exists:

*Let N be the largest positive integer. If N > 1, then N<sup>2</sup> > N, contradicting the definition of N. Hence N = 1*.
