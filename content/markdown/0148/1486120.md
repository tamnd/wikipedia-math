# Nicholas Mercator

*Image: Nicolaus Mercator Logarithmotechnia.png.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Mercator"}

**Nicholas** (Nikolaus) **Mercator** (c. 1620, [Prince-Bishopric of Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Lübeck) – 1687, [Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_%28city%29)), also known by his [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) name Kauffmann, was a 17th-century [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

He was born in [Eutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutin), [Prince-Bishopric of Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Lübeck), [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) and educated at [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rostock) and [Leyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) after which he lived from 1642 to 1648 in the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic). He lectured at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) during 1648–1654 and lived in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) from 1655 to 1657. He was mathematics tutor to [Joscelyne Percy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joscelyne_Percy), son of the 10th [Earl of Northumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Northumberland), at [Petworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petworth), [Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex) (1657). He taught mathematics in London (1658–1682). On 3 May 1661 he observed a [transit of Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_of_Mercury) with [Christiaan Huygens](wikipedia://en/42127) and [Thomas Streete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Street_%28astronomer%29) from [Long Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Acre), London.[^o2] On 14 November 1666 he was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society).[^o3] He designed a marine chronometer for [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England).[^o4]

In 1682 [Jean Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Colbert) invited Mercator to assist in the design and construction of the fountains at the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles), so he relocated there, but a falling-out with Colbert followed.[^o5]

Mathematically, he is most well known for his treatise *Logarithmo-technia* on [logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithms), published in 1668. In this treatise he described the [Mercator series](wikipedia://en/3995885):

$$\ln(1 + x) = x - \frac{1}{2}x^2 + \frac{1}{3}x^3 - \frac{1}{4}x^4 + \cdots \quad \text{for } x \in \ ]-1, 1].$$

Nicholas Mercator was the first person to use the term [natural logarithm](wikipedia://en/21476).[^o6]

To the field of music, Mercator contributed the first precise account of [53 equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53_equal_temperament), which was of theoretical importance, but not widely practised.[^o7]

He died at Versailles in 1687.

## Works {#Works}

*Image: Mercator, Nicolaus – Institutionum astronomicarum libri, 1685 – BEIC 1397087.jpg.* *Institutionum astronomicarum libri*, 1685

- 1676: *Institutionum astronomicarum*, London (1685, Padua)
- (1685). "Institutionum astronomicarum libri duo". Tipografia del Seminario. <https://gutenberg.beic.it/webclient/DeliveryManager?pid=1397087>
- Kinkhuysen (1661) *Algebra ofte Stelkonst*, translated by N. Mercator, appears 1968 in *Mathematical Papers of Isaac Newton* II: 295–364 with Newton commentary 364–446.
- 1664: *Hypothesis astronomica nova*, London
- 1666: "Certain problems touching some points of navigation", [Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_Transactions_of_the_Royal_Society) 1: 215–18
- 1668: [Logarithmo-technia](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/009310290?type%5B%5D=title&lookfor%5B%5D=logarithmotechnia&ft=ft) from [HathiTrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HathiTrust) or [Logarithmtechnia](https://archive.org/details/ita-bnc-mag-00000857-001) from [Internet Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Archive)
- Wallis (1668) Review of *Logarithmotechnia*, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* 3: 753–9, followed by "Some further Illustration" by N. Mercator, pp 759–64.
- 1670: "Some considerations … method of Cassini", *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* 5: 1168–75.
