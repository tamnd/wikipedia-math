# Euclidean topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euclidean topology"}

In mathematics, and especially [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), the **Euclidean topology** is the [natural topology](wikipedia://en/11934923) induced on $n$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ by the [Euclidean metric](wikipedia://en/53932).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the non-negative function $\|\cdot\| : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ defined by

$$\left\|\left(p_1, \ldots, p_n\right)\right\| ~:=~ \sqrt{p_1^2 + \cdots + p_n^2}.$$

Like all [norms](wikipedia://en/990534), it induces a canonical [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) defined by $d(p, q) = \|p - q\|.$ The metric $d : \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ induced by the [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) is called the [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric) or the [Euclidean distance](wikipedia://en/53932) and the distance between points $p = \left(p_1, \ldots, p_n\right)$ and $q = \left(q_1, \ldots, q_n\right)$ is

$$d(p, q) ~=~ \|p - q\| ~=~ \sqrt{\left(p_1 - q_1\right)^2 + \left(p_2 - q_2\right)^2 + \cdots + \left(p_i - q_i\right)^2 + \cdots + \left(p_n - q_n\right)^2}.$$

In any [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), the [open balls](wikipedia://en/160556) form a [base](wikipedia://en/59220) for a topology on that space.[^o4] The Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the topology generated by these balls. In other words, the open sets of the Euclidean topology on $\mathbb{R}^n$ are given by (arbitrary) unions of the open balls $B_r(p)$ defined as $B_r(p) := \left\{x \in \mathbb{R}^n : d(p,x) < r\right\},$ for all real $r > 0$ and all $p \in \mathbb{R}^n,$ where $d$ is the Euclidean metric.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

When endowed with this topology, the real line $\mathbb{R}$ is a [T<sub>5</sub> space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T5_space). Given two subsets say $A$ and $B$ of $\mathbb{R}$ with $\overline{A} \cap B = A \cap \overline{B} = \varnothing,$ where $\overline{A}$ denotes the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of $A,$ there exist open sets $S_A$ and $S_B$ with $A \subseteq S_A$ and $B \subseteq S_B$ such that $S_A \cap S_B = \varnothing.$[^o7]

:::
