# George Bohrnstedt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George Bohrnstedt"}

**George William Bohrnstedt** (born September 28, 1938, Arcadia, WI) is an American sociologist, statistician, and education researcher.[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bohrnstedt received his PhD from [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) in 1966[^o4] under the supervision of Edgar F. Borgatta.[^o5] He spent most of his 20-year academic career at the [University of Minnesota-Twin Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) and [Indiana University-Bloomington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University_Bloomington) and was chair of the Department of Sociology at both universities.[^o6][^o4] In 1988 he moved to the [American Institutes for Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institutes_for_Research) where he was active as Senior Vice-president and Institute Fellow until his retirement in March of 2025.[^o7]

One of his statistics articles "On the exact covariance of products of random variables" (with Arthur S. Goldberger)[^o8] which appeared in the *Journal of the American Statistical Association* has been cited in research articles in areas as diverse as psychology, chemistry, electrical engineering, forestry, operations research, and genetics.[^o9]

His textbook *Statistics for Social Data Analysis* co-authored with David Knoke (and Alicia Potter Mee for the 4th edition) is widely cited and was used in the teaching of upper level first year graduate level statistics courses.[^o9][^o10] He also co-edited *Sociological Methodology: 1969* with Edgar F. Borgatta) and the two of them were also the founding editors of *Sociological Methods and Research*[^o6][^o2]*.* He was also served the editor of *Social Psychology Quarterly* from 1979 to 1982.[^o2][^o6]

Bohrnstedt was also the leader and clarinetist with the *Riverboat Ramblers*,[^o11] a popular traditional band that regularly played venues in the Madison, Milwaukee, and Lake Geneva areas of Wisconsin in the 1960s.[^o12]
