# (Q,r) model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="(Q,r) model"}

In [inventory theory](wikipedia://en/21749554), the **(Q,r) model** is used to determine optimal ordering policies.[^o2] It is a class of inventory control models that generalize and combine elements of both the [Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) model](wikipedia://en/849762) and the [base stock model](wikipedia://en/50212353).[^o3] The (Q,r) model addresses the question of *when* and *how much* to order, aiming to minimize total inventory costs, which typically include ordering costs, holding costs, and shortage costs. It specifies that an order of size *Q* should be placed when the inventory level reaches a reorder point *r*. The (Q,r) model is widely applied in various industries to manage inventory effectively and efficiently.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

### Assumptions {#Assumptions}

1. Products can be analyzed individually
1. Demands occur one at a time (no batch orders)
1. Unfilled demand is back-ordered (no lost sales)
1. Replenishment lead times are fixed and known
1. Replenishments are ordered one at a time
1. Demand is modeled by a continuous probability distribution
1. There is a [fixed cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_cost) associated with a replenishment order
1. There is a constraint on the number of replenishment orders per year

### Variables {#Variables}

- $D$ = Expected demand per year
- $\ell$ = Replenishment [lead time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_time)
- $X$ = Demand during replenishment lead time
- $g(x)$ = [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of demand during lead time
- $G(x)$ = [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of demand during lead time
- $\theta$ = mean demand during lead time
- $A$ = setup or [purchase order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchase_order) cost per replenishment
- $c$ = unit production cost
- $h$ = annual unit holding cost
- $k$ = cost per stockout
- $b$ = annual unit backorder cost
- $Q$ = replenishment quantity
- $r$ = [reorder point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reorder_point)
- $SS=r-\theta$, [safety stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_stock) level
- $F(Q,r)$ = order frequency
- $S(Q,r)$ = fill rate
- $B(Q,r)$ = average number of outstanding back-orders
- $I(Q,r)$ = average on-hand inventory level

### Costs {#Costs}

The number of orders per year can be computed as $F(Q,r) = \frac {D}{Q}$, the annual fixed order cost is F(Q,r)A. The fill rate is given by:

$$S(Q,r)=\frac{1}{Q} \int_{r}^{r+Q} G(x)dx$$

The annual stockout cost is proportional to D\[1 - S(Q,r)\], with the fill rate beying:

$$S(Q,r)=\frac{1}{Q} \int_{r}^{r+Q} G(x) dx = 1 - \frac{1}{Q} [B(r))-B(r+Q)]$$

Inventory holding cost is $hI(Q,r)$, average inventory being:

$$I(Q,r)=\frac{Q+1}{2}+r-\theta+B(Q,r)$$

#### Backorder cost approach {#Backorder_cost_approach}

The annual backorder cost is proportional to backorder level:

$$B(Q,r) = \frac{1}{Q} \int_{r}^{r+Q} B(x+1)dx$$

##### Total cost function and optimal reorder point {#Total_cost_function_and_optimal_reorder_point}

The total cost is given by the sum of setup costs, purchase order cost, backorders cost and inventory carrying cost:

$$Y(Q,r) = \frac{D}{Q} A + b B(Q,r) +h I(Q,r)$$

The optimal reorder quantity and optimal reorder point are given by:

### Lead-Time Variability {#Lead-Time_Variability}

X is the random demand during replenishment lead time:

$$X = \sum_{t=1}^{L} D_{t}$$

In [expectation](wikipedia://en/9653):

$$\operatorname{E}[X] = \operatorname{E}[L] \operatorname{E}[D_{t}] =\ell d = \theta$$

Variance of demand is given by:

$$\operatorname{Var}(x) = \operatorname{E}[L] \operatorname{Var}(D_{t}) + \operatorname{E}[D_{t}]^{2}\operatorname{Var}(L) = \ell \sigma^{2}_{D} + d^{2} \sigma^{2}_{L}$$

Hence [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590) is:

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)} =\sqrt{ \ell \sigma^{2}_{D} + d^{2} \sigma^{2}_{L} }$$

#### Poisson distribution {#Poisson_distribution}

if demand is [Poisson distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_distributed):

$$\sigma = \sqrt{ \ell \sigma^{2}_{D} + d^{2} \sigma^{2}_{L} }= \sqrt{\theta + d^{2} \sigma^{2}_{L}}$$
