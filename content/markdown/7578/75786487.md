# Jeffrey B. Remmel

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jeffrey B. Remmel"}

**Jeffrey Brian Remmel** (October 12, 1948 – September 29, 2017) was an American mathematician employed by the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego).[^o3] At the time of his death he held a [distinguished professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor)—his title was Distinguished Professor of Mathematics;[^o4] he also held a position as a professor of [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science).[^o5]

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Remmel was born on October 12, 1948, in [Clintonville, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clintonville,_Wisconsin).[^o7] He died aged 68 at [Scripps Memorial Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Memorial_Hospital) in [La Jolla, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla,_California) on September 29, 2017,[^o3] with a reported cause of death being a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack).[^o8]

## Education {#Education}

Remmel received a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in mathematics from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) in 1970. Later, he received two degrees from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University)—a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in mathematics and a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy), also in math (1972 and 1974, respectively).[^o5] At Cornell, he was advised by [Anil Nerode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anil_Nerode), and his dissertation was entitled *Co-recursively Enumerable Structures*.[^o10]

## Career {#Career}

After obtaining his Ph.D., though before he had published a single paper,[^o7] Remmel joined the faculty of the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) as an [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor), where he worked for his entire career.[^o4] Remmel was noted for his successful publication record in two separate fields—[logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), in which he published in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636); and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), where he published papers on [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582).[^o10] He published over 20 papers in logic with [Victor W. Marek](wikipedia://en/51001800),[^o12] and Remmel's more prominent career in combinatorics included over 20 co-authored papers with [Sergey Kitaev](wikipedia://en/55632689).[^o7] A double issue of the *Journal of Combinatorics* was published in his memory.[^o13]

Remmel's work is highly cited in the fields of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370), including [computably enumerable sets](wikipedia://en/332090) and vector spaces.
