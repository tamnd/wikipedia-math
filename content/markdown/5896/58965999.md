# Mark Sapir

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mark Sapir"}

**Mark Sapir** (February 12, 1957 - October 8, 2022)[^o3][^o4] was a U.S. and Russian mathematician working in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory) and combinatorial algebra. He was a Centennial Professor of Mathematics in the Department of Mathematics at [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Sapir received his undergraduate degree in mathematics ([diploma of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploma_of_higher_education)) from the [Ural State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_State_University) in [Yekaterinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinburg) (then called Sverdlovsk), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia), in 1978.[^o3] He received his PhD in mathematics ([Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences)) degree, joint from the [Ural State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_State_University) and [Moscow State Pedagogical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Pedagogical_Institute) in 1983, with [Lev Shevrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Shevrin) as the advisor.[^o3]

Afterwards Sapir held faculty appointments at the [Ural State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_State_University), Sverdlovsk Pedagogical Institute, [University of Nebraska–Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nebraska–Lincoln), before coming as a professor of mathematics to [Vanderbilt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderbilt_University) in 1997. He was appointed a Centennial Professor of Mathematics at Vanderbilt in 2001.

Sapir gave an invited talk at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Madrid in 2006.[^o6] He gave an AMS Invited Address at the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) Sectional Meeting in Huntsville, Alabama in October 2008.[^o7] He gave a plenary talk at the December 2008 Winter Meeting of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055).[^o8] Sapir gave the 33d William J. Spencer Lecture at the [Kansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_State_University) in November 2008.[^o9] He gave the 75th KAM Mathematical Colloquium lecture at the [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in Prague in June 2010.[^o10]

Sapir became a member of the inaugural class of Fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2012.[^o11]

Sapir founded the *[Journal of Combinatorial Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Combinatorial_Algebra)*, published by the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470), and served as its founding editor-in-chief starting in 2016.[^o12] He also was an editorial board member for the journals [Groups, Complexity, Cryptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups,_Complexity,_Cryptology) and [Algebra and Discrete Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_and_Discrete_Mathematics). His past editorial board positions include [Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Pure_and_Applied_Algebra), [Groups, Geometry, and Dynamics](wikipedia://en/30654850), [Algebra Universalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_Universalis), and [International Journal of Algebra and Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Algebra_and_Computation) (as Managing Editor).

A special mathematical conference in honor of Sapir's 60th birthday took place at the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) in May 2017.[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mark Sapir's elder daughter, [Jenya Sapir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenya_Sapir), is also a mathematician; she was [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655)'s first (out of two) students.[^o15] Currently, she is an assistant professor in the Department of Mathematics of [Binghamton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton_University).[^o16]

Mark Sapir and his wife Olga Sapir became [naturalized U.S. citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law) in July 2003,[^o17] after suing the [BCIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCIS) in federal court over a multi-year delay of their citizenship application originally filed in 1999.[^o18]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Sapir's early mathematical work concerned mostly [semigroup theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semigroup_theory).

In [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) his most well-known and significant results were obtained in two papers published in the [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785) in 2002,[^o20][^o21] the first joint with Jean-Camille Birget and [Eliyahu Rips](wikipedia://en/673693), and the second joint with Birget, Rips and [Aleksandr Olshansky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Olshansky). The first paper provided an essentially complete description of all the possible growth types of [Dehn functions](wikipedia://en/20017026) of [finitely presented groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group). The second paper proved that a finitely presented group has the [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) solvable in [non-deterministic polynomial time (**NP**)](wikipedia://en/21562) if and only if this group embeds as a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of a finitely presented group with polynomial Dehn function. A combined featured review of these two papers in [Mathematical Reviews](wikipedia://en/1485646) characterized them as \`\`remarkable foundational results regarding isoperimetric functions of finitely presented groups and their connections with the complexity of the word problem".[^o22]

Sapir was also known for his work, mostly joint with [Cornelia Druţu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia_Druţu), on developing the [asymptotic cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_cone) approach to the study of [relatively hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/21993809).[^o23][^o24]

A 2002 paper of Sapir and Olshansky constructed the first known [finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group) counter-examples to the [Von Neumann conjecture](wikipedia://en/662256).[^o25]

Sapir also introduced, in a 1993 paper with Meakin,[^o26] the notion of a [diagram group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagram_group), based on finite semigroup presentations. He further developed this notion in subsequent joint papers with Guba.[^o27] Diagram groups provided a new approach to the study of [Thompson groups](wikipedia://en/612245), which appear as important examples of diagram groups.
