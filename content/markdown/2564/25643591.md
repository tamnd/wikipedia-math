# Quasiperiodicity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasiperiodicity"}

**Quasiperiodicity** is the property of a [system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System) that displays irregular [periodicity](wikipedia://en/76848). Periodic behavior is defined as recurring at regular intervals, such as "every 24 hours".[^o2] Quasiperiodic behavior is almost but not quite periodic.[^o3] The term used to denote oscillations that appear to follow a regular pattern but which do not have a fixed period. The term thus used does not have a precise definition and should not be confused with more strictly defined mathematical concepts such as an [almost periodic function](wikipedia://en/405512) or a [quasiperiodic function](wikipedia://en/2657905).

:::

## Climatology {#Climatology}

[Climate oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_oscillation) that appear to follow a regular pattern but which do not have a fixed period are called *quasiperiodic*.[^o5][^o6]

Within a [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) such as the ocean-atmosphere system, oscillations may occur regularly when they are forced by a regular external forcing: for example, the familiar winter-summer cycle is forced by variations in sunlight from the (very close to perfectly) periodic motion of the Earth around the Sun. Or, like the recent [ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age) cycles, they may be less regular but still locked by external forcing. However, when the system contains the potential for an oscillation, but there is no strong external forcing to be [phase-locked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase-locked) to, the "period" is likely to be irregular.

The canonical example of quasiperiodicity in climatology is [El Niño–Southern Oscillation](wikipedia://en/317311) (ENSO).[^o7] ENSO is highly consequential for [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) cultivation in Australia.[^o7] Models to predict and thereby assist adaptation to ENSO have a large potential benefit to Australian wheat farmers.[^o7] In the modern era, it has a "period" somewhere between four and twelve years and a peak [spectral density](wikipedia://en/202672) around five years.
