# Jensen's covering theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jensen's covering theorem"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **Jensen's covering theorem** states that if [0<sup>\#</sup>](wikipedia://en/248085) does not exist then every [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) set of [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) is contained in a [constructible set](wikipedia://en/343335) of the same [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174). Informally this conclusion says that the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) is close to the universe of all sets. The first proof appeared in (Devlin Jensen 1975). [Silver](wikipedia://en/21626512) later gave a fine-structure-free proof using his [machines](wikipedia://en/5363856)[^o3] and finally (txt) gave an even simpler proof.

:::

The converse of Jensen's covering theorem is also true: if 0<sup>\#</sup> exists then the countable set of all cardinals less than $\aleph_\omega$ cannot be covered by a constructible set of cardinality less than $\aleph_\omega$.

In his book *Proper Forcing*, [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) proved a strong form of Jensen's covering lemma.

[Hugh Woodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Woodin) states it as:[^o5]

Theorem 3.33 (Jensen). One of the following holds.

(1) Suppose λ is a singular cardinal. Then λ is singular in L and its successor cardinal is its successor cardinal in L.

(2) Every uncountable cardinal is inaccessible in L.
