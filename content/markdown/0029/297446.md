# Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a discipline within [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), given a [$C^*$-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) $A$, the **Gelfand–Naimark–Segal construction** establishes a correspondence between cyclic $*$-representations of $A$ and certain [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on $A$ (called *states*). The correspondence is shown by an explicit construction of the $*$-representation from the state. It is named for [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643), [Mark Naimark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Naimark), and [Irving Segal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Segal).

:::

## States and representations {#States_and_representations}

A **$*$-representation** of a [$C^*$-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) $A$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $H$ is a [mapping](wikipedia://en/516931) $\pi$ from $A$ into the algebra of [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on $H$ such that

- $\pi$ is a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) which carries [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) on $A$ into involution on operators
- $\pi$ is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate), that is the space of vectors $\pi (x)$ $\xi$ is dense as $x$ ranges through $A$ and $\xi$ ranges through $H$. Note that if $A$ has an identity, nondegeneracy means exactly $\pi$ is unit-preserving, i.e. $\pi$ maps the identity of $A$ to the identity operator on $H$.

A [state](wikipedia://en/454959) on a $C^*$-algebra $A$ is a [positive linear functional](wikipedia://en/454930) $f$ of norm $1$. If $A$ has a multiplicative unit element this condition is equivalent to $f(1) = 1$.

For a representation $\pi$ of a $C^*$-algebra $A$ on a Hilbert space $H$, an element $\xi$ is called a **cyclic vector** if the set of vectors

$$\{\pi(x)\xi:x\in A\}$$

is norm dense in $H$, in which case π is called a **cyclic representation**. Any non-zero vector of an [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) is cyclic. However, non-zero vectors in a general cyclic representation may fail to be cyclic.

### The GNS construction {#The_GNS_construction}

Let $\pi$ be a $*$-representation of a $C^*$-algebra $A$ on the Hilbert space $H$ and $\xi$ be a unit norm cyclic vector for $\pi$. Then

$$a \mapsto \langle \pi(a) \xi, \xi\rangle$$

is a state of $A$.

Conversely, every state of $A$ may be viewed as a [vector state](wikipedia://en/454959#Vector_states) as above, under a suitable canonical representation.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem."}

**Theorem.[^o4].**

Given a state $\rho$ of $A$, there is a $*$-representation $\pi$ of $A$ acting on a Hilbert space $H$ with distinguished unit cyclic vector $\xi$ such that $\rho(a)=\langle \pi(a) \xi, \xi \rangle$ for every $a$ in $A$.

:::

The method used to produce a $*$-representation from a state of $A$ in the proof of the above theorem is called the **GNS construction**. For a state of a $C^*$-algebra $A$, the corresponding GNS representation is essentially uniquely determined by the condition, $\rho(a) = \langle \pi(a) \xi, \xi \rangle$ as seen in the theorem below.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem."}

**Theorem.[^o7].**

Given a state $\rho$ of $A$, let $\pi$, $\pi'$ be $*$-representations of $A$ on Hilbert spaces $H$, $H^\prime$ respectively each with unit norm cyclic vectors $\xi \in H$, $\xi' \in H'$ such that $\rho(a) = \langle \pi(a) \xi, \xi \rangle = \langle \pi'(a) \xi ', \xi ' \rangle$ for all $a \in A$. Then $\pi$, $\pi'$ are unitarily equivalent $*$-representations i.e. there is a unitary operator $U$ from $H$ to $H^\prime$ such that $\pi'(a) = U \pi(a) U^*$ for all $a$ in $A$. The operator $U$ that implements the unitary equivalence maps $\pi(a)\xi$ to $\pi'(a)\xi'$ for all $a$ in $A$.

:::

### Significance of the GNS construction {#Significance_of_the_GNS_construction}

The GNS construction is at the heart of the proof of the [Gelfand–Naimark theorem](wikipedia://en/315414) characterizing $C^*$-algebras as algebras of operators. A $C^*$-algebra has sufficiently many pure states (see below) so that the direct sum of corresponding irreducible GNS representations is [faithful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_functor).

The direct sum of the corresponding GNS representations of all states is called the **[universal representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_representation_%28C*-algebra%29)** of $A$. The universal representation of $A$ contains every cyclic representation. As every $*$-representation is a direct sum of cyclic representations, it follows that every $*$-representation of $A$ is a direct summand of some sum of copies of the universal representation.

If $\Phi$ is the universal representation of a $C^*$-algebra $A$, the closure of $\Phi(A)$ in the [weak operator topology](wikipedia://en/458698) is called the **[enveloping von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/15374396)** of $A$. It can be identified with the double dual $A^{**}$.

## Irreducibility {#Irreducibility}

Also of significance is the relation between [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) $*$-representations and extreme points of the convex set of states. A representation π on $H$ is irreducible if and only if there are no closed subspaces of $H$ which are invariant under all the operators $\pi(x)$ other than $H$ itself and the trivial subspace $\{0\}$.

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

The set of states of a $C^*$-algebra $A$ with a unit element is a compact [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) under the weak-$*$ topology. In general, (regardless of whether or not $A$ has a unit element) the set of positive functionals of norm $\leq 1$ is a compact convex set.

:::

Both of these results follow immediately from the [Banach–Alaoglu theorem](wikipedia://en/577366).

In the unital commutative case, for the $C^*$-algebra $C(X)$ of continuous functions on some compact $X$, [Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem](wikipedia://en/38948504) says that the positive functionals of norm $\leq 1$ are precisely the Borel positive measures on $X$ with total mass $\leq 1$. It follows from [Krein–Milman theorem](wikipedia://en/3206654) that the extremal states are the Dirac point-mass measures.

On the other hand, a representation of $C(X)$ is irreducible if and only if it is one-dimensional. Therefore, the GNS representation of $C(X)$ corresponding to a measure $\mu$ is irreducible if and only if $\mu$ is an extremal state. This is in fact true for $C^*$-algebras in general.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Let $A$ be a $C^*$-algebra. If $\pi$ is a $*$-representation of $A$ on the Hilbert space $H$ with unit norm cyclic vector $\xi$, then $\pi$ is irreducible if and only if the corresponding state $f$ is an [extreme point](wikipedia://en/454968) of the convex set of positive linear functionals on $A$ of norm $\leq 1$.

:::

To prove this result one notes first that a representation is irreducible if and only if the [commutant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutant) of $\pi(A)$, denoted by $\pi(A)'$, consists of scalar multiples of the identity.

Any positive linear functionals $g$ on $A$ dominated by $f$ is of the form

$$g(x^*x) = \langle \pi(x) \xi, \pi(x) T_g \, \xi \rangle$$

for some positive operator $T_g$ in $\pi(A)'$ with $0 \leq T \leq 1$ in the operator order. This is a version of the [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746).

For such $g$, one can write $f$ as a sum of positive linear functionals: $f = g + g'$. So $\pi$ is unitarily equivalent to a [subrepresentation](wikipedia://en/55943952) of $\pi_g \oplus \pi_{g'}$. This shows that π is irreducible if and only if any such $\pi_g$ is unitarily equivalent to $\pi$, i.e. $g$ is a scalar multiple of $f$, which proves the theorem.

Extremal states are usually called [pure states](wikipedia://en/454959#Pure_states). Note that a state is a pure state if and only if it is extremal in the convex set of states.

The theorems above for $C^*$-algebras are valid more generally in the context of [$B^*$-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-star_algebra) with approximate identity.

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The [Stinespring factorization theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stinespring_factorization_theorem) characterizing [completely positive maps](wikipedia://en/6083511) is an important generalization of the GNS construction.

:::

## History {#History}

Gelfand and Naimark's paper on the Gelfand–Naimark theorem was published in 1943.[^o16] Segal recognized the construction that was implicit in this work and presented it in sharpened form.[^o17]

In his paper of 1947 Segal showed that it is sufficient, for any physical system that can be described by an algebra of operators on a Hilbert space, to consider the *irreducible* representations of a $C^*$-algebra. In quantum theory this means that the $C^*$-algebra is generated by the observables. This, as Segal pointed out, had been shown earlier by [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) only for the specific case of the non-relativistic Schrödinger-Heisenberg theory.[^o18]
