# Lie superalgebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lie superalgebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Lie superalgebra** is a generalisation of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) to include a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$[grading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra). Lie superalgebras are important in [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) where they are used to describe the mathematics of [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636).

:::

The notion of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ grading used here is distinct from a second $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ grading having cohomological origins. A [graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) (say, graded by $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{N}$) that is anticommutative and has a graded [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370) also has a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ grading; this is the "rolling up" of the algebra into odd and even parts. This rolling-up is not normally referred to as "super". Thus, supergraded Lie superalgebras carry a *pair* of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$gradations: one of which is supersymmetric, and the other is classical. [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) calls the supersymmetric one the *super gradation*, and the classical one the *cohomological gradation*. These two gradations must be compatible, and there is often disagreement as to how they should be regarded.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Formally, a Lie superalgebra is a nonassociative **Z**<sub>2</sub>-[graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra), or *[superalgebra](wikipedia://en/469950)*, over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) (typically **R** or **C**) whose product \[·, ·\], called the **Lie superbracket** or **supercommutator**, satisfies the two conditions (analogs of the usual [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) axioms, with grading):

Super skew-symmetry:

$$[x,y]=-(-1)^{|x| |y|}[y,x].\$$

The super Jacobi identity:[^o5]

$$(-1)^{|x||z|}[x, [y, z]] + (-1)^{|y||x|}[y, [z, x]] + (-1)^{|z||y|}[z, [x, y]] = 0,$$

where *x*, *y*, and *z* are pure in the **Z**<sub>2</sub>-grading. Here, |*x*| denotes the degree of *x* (either 0 or 1). The degree of \[x,y\] is the sum of degree of x and y modulo 2.

One also sometimes adds the axioms $[x,x]=0$ for |*x*| = 0 (if 2 is invertible this follows automatically) and $[[x,x],x]=0$ for |*x*| = 1 (if 3 is invertible this follows automatically). When the ground ring is the integers or the Lie superalgebra is a free module, these conditions are equivalent to the condition that the [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) holds (and, in general, they are necessary conditions for the theorem to hold).

Just as for Lie algebras, the [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) of the Lie superalgebra can be given a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714) structure.

:::

## Comments {#Comments}

Lie superalgebras show up in physics in several different ways. In conventional [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636), the *even* elements of the superalgebra correspond to [bosons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) and *odd* elements to [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion). This corresponds to a bracket that has a grading of zero:

$$|[a,b]| = |a|+|b|$$

This is not always the case; for example, in [BRST supersymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRST_supersymmetry) and in the [Batalin–Vilkovisky formalism](wikipedia://en/672259), it is the other way around, which corresponds to the bracket of having a grading of -1:

$$|[a,b]| = |a|+|b|-1$$

This distinction becomes particularly relevant when an algebra has not one, but two [graded associative products](wikipedia://en/103094). In addition to the Lie bracket, there may also be an "ordinary" product, thus giving rise to the [Poisson superalgebra](wikipedia://en/882793) and the [Gerstenhaber algebra](wikipedia://en/883034). Such gradings are also observed in [deformation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak g_0 \oplus \mathfrak g_1$ be a Lie superalgebra. By inspecting the Jacobi identity, one sees that there are eight cases depending on whether arguments are even or odd. These fall into four classes, indexed by the number of odd elements:[^o9]

1. No odd elements. The statement is just that $\mathfrak g_0$ is an ordinary Lie algebra.
1. One odd element. Then $\mathfrak g_1$ is a $\mathfrak g_0$-module for the action $\mathrm{ad}_a: b \rightarrow [a, b], \quad a \in \mathfrak g_0, \quad b, [a, b] \in \mathfrak g_1$.
1. Two odd elements. The Jacobi identity says that the bracket $\mathfrak g_1 \otimes \mathfrak g_1 \rightarrow \mathfrak g_0$ is a *symmetric* $\mathfrak g_0$-map.
1. Three odd elements. For all $b \in \mathfrak g_1$, $[b,[b,b]] = 0$.

Thus the even subalgebra $\mathfrak g_0$ of a Lie superalgebra forms a (normal) Lie algebra as all the signs disappear, and the superbracket becomes a normal Lie bracket, while $\mathfrak g_1$ is a [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_a_Lie_algebra) of $\mathfrak g_0$, and there exists a [symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric) $\mathfrak g_0$-[equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) $\{\cdot,\cdot\}:\mathfrak g_1\otimes \mathfrak g_1\rightarrow \mathfrak g_0$ such that,

$$[\left\{x, y\right\},z]+[\left\{y, z\right\},x]+[\left\{z, x\right\},y]=0, \quad x,y, z \in \mathfrak g_1.$$

Conditions (1)–(3) are linear and can all be understood in terms of ordinary Lie algebras. Condition (4) is nonlinear, and is the most difficult one to verify when constructing a Lie superalgebra starting from an ordinary Lie algebra ($\mathfrak g_0$) and a representation ($\mathfrak g_1$).

:::

## Involution {#Involution}

A $*$ **Lie superalgebra** is a complex Lie superalgebra equipped with an [involutive](wikipedia://en/293450) [antilinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilinear) map from itself to itself which respects the **Z**<sub>2</sub> grading and satisfies \[*x*,*y*\]<sup>\*</sup> = \[*y*<sup>\*</sup>,*x*<sup>\*</sup>\] for all *x* and *y* in the Lie superalgebra. (Some authors prefer the convention \[*x*,*y*\]<sup>\*</sup> = (−1)<sup>\|*x*||*y*|</sup>\[*y*<sup>\*</sup>,*x*<sup>\*</sup>\]; changing \* to −\* switches between the two conventions.) Its [universal enveloping algebra](wikipedia://en/363551) would be an ordinary [<sup>\*</sup>-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star-algebra).

## Examples {#Examples}

Given any [associative superalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_superalgebra) $A$ one can define the supercommutator on homogeneous elements by

$$[x,y] = xy - (-1)^{|x||y|}yx\$$

and then extending by linearity to all elements. The algebra $A$ together with the supercommutator then becomes a Lie superalgebra. The simplest example of this procedure is perhaps when $A$ is the space of all linear functions $\mathbf {End}(V)$ of a super vector space $V$ to itself. When $V = \mathbb K^{p|q}$, this space is denoted by $M^{p|q}$ or $M(p|q)$.[^o13] With the Lie bracket per above, the space is denoted $\mathfrak {gl}(p|q)$.[^o14]

A [Poisson algebra](wikipedia://en/345704) is an associative algebra together with a Lie bracket. If the algebra is given a **Z**<sub>2</sub>-grading, such that the Lie bracket becomes a Lie superbracket, then one obtains the [Poisson superalgebra](wikipedia://en/882793). If, in addition, the associative product is made [supercommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercommutative), one obtains a supercommutative Poisson superalgebra.

The [Whitehead product](wikipedia://en/3129615) on homotopy groups gives many examples of Lie superalgebras over the integers.

The [super-Poincaré algebra](wikipedia://en/1057955) generates the isometries of flat [superspace](wikipedia://en/432630).

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

The simple complex finite-dimensional Lie superalgebras were classified by [Victor Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697).

They are (excluding the Lie algebras):[^o16]

The **special linear lie superalgebra** $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$**.**

The lie superalgebra $\mathfrak{sl}(m|n)$ is the subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)$ consisting of matrices with super trace zero. It is simple when $m\not=n$. If $m=n$, then the identity matrix $I_{2m}$generates an ideal. Quotienting out this ideal leads to $\mathfrak{sl}(m|m) / \langle I_{2m} \rangle$ which is simple for $m \geq 2$.

The **orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra** $\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n)$.

Consider an even, non-degenerate, supersymmetric bilinear form $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ on $\mathbb{C}^{m|2n}$. Then the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra is the subalgebra of $\mathfrak{gl}(m|2n)$ consisting of matrices that leave this form invariant:

$$\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n) = \{ X \in \mathfrak{gl}(m|2n) \mid \langle X u,v \rangle + (-1)^{|X||u|} \langle u, X v\rangle =0 \text{ for all } u,v \in \mathbb{C}^{m|2n} \}.$$

Its even part is given by $\mathfrak{so}(m) \oplus \mathfrak{sp}(2n)$.

The **exceptional Lie superalgebra** $D(2,1;\alpha)$.

There is a family of (9∣8)-dimensional Lie superalgebras depending on a parameter $\alpha$. These are deformations of $D(2,1)=\mathfrak{osp}(4|2)$. If $\alpha\not=0$ and $\alpha\not=-1$, then D(2,1,α) is simple. Moreover $D(2,1;\alpha) \cong D(2,1;\beta)$ if $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are under the same orbit under the maps $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^{-1}$ and $\alpha \mapsto -1-\alpha$.

The **exceptional Lie superalgebra** $F(4)$.

It has dimension (24|16). Its even part is given by $\mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus \mathfrak{so}(7)$.

The **exceptional Lie superalgebra** $G(3)$.

It has dimension (17|14). Its even part is given by $\mathfrak{sl}(2) \oplus G_2$.

There are also two so-called **strange** series called $\mathfrak{pe}(n)$ and $\mathfrak{q}(n)$.

The **Cartan types**. They can be divided in four families: $W(n)$, $S(n)$, $\widetilde{S}(2n)$ and $H(n)$. For the Cartan type of simple Lie superalgebras, the odd part is no longer completely reducible under the action of the even part.

## Classification of infinite-dimensional simple linearly compact Lie superalgebras {#Classification_of_infinite-dimensional_simple_linearly_compact_Lie_superalgebras}

The classification consists of the 10 series **W**(*m*, *n*), **S**(*m*, *n*) ((m, n) ≠ (1, 1)), **H(2m, n)**, **K**(2*m* + 1, *n*), **HO(m, m)** (*m* ≥ 2), **SHO**(*m*, *m*) (*m* ≥ 3), **KO**(*m*, *m* + 1), **SKO(m, m + 1; β)** (*m* ≥ 2), **SHO** ~ (2*m*, 2*m*), **SKO** ~ (2*m* + 1, 2*m* + 3) and the five exceptional algebras:

**E(1, 6)**, **E(5, 10)**, **E(4, 4)**, **E(3, 6)**, **E(3, 8)**

The last two are particularly interesting (according to Kac) because they have the standard model gauge group **SU**(3)×**S**U(2)×**U**(1) as their zero level algebra. Infinite-dimensional (affine) Lie superalgebras are important symmetries in [superstring theory](wikipedia://en/19595676). Specifically, the Virasoro algebras with $\mathcal{N}$ supersymmetries are $K(1, \mathcal{N})$ which only have central extensions up to $\mathcal{N} = 4$.[^o18]

## Category-theoretic definition {#Category-theoretic_definition}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **Lie superalgebra** can be defined as a nonassociative [superalgebra](wikipedia://en/469950) whose product satisfies

- $$[\cdot,\cdot]\circ ({\operatorname{id}}+\tau_{A,A})=0$$
- $$[\cdot,\cdot]\circ ([\cdot,\cdot]\otimes {\operatorname{id}} \circ({\operatorname{id}}+\sigma+\sigma^2)=0$$

where σ is the cyclic permutation braiding $({\operatorname{id}} \otimes\tau_{A,A}) \circ (\tau_{A,A}\otimes {\operatorname{id}})$. In diagrammatic form:
