# Arithmetization of analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetization of analysis"}

The **arithmetization of analysis** was a research program in the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) carried out in the second half of the 19th century which aimed to abolish all geometric intuition from the proofs in [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396). For the followers of this program, the fundamental concepts of calculus should also not make references to the ideas of motion and velocity. This ideal was pursued by [Augustin-Louis Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842), [Bernard Bolzano](wikipedia://en/302185), [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), among others, who developed solid foundations for [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and analysis.

:::

## History {#History}

[Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450) originally introduced the term *arithmetization of analysis*, by which he meant its constructivization in the context of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) (see quotation at bottom of page). The meaning of the term later shifted to signify the [set-theoretic](wikipedia://en/27553) construction of the [real line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_line). Its main proponent was [Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), who argued the geometric foundations of [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) were not solid enough for [rigorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigour) work.

## Research program {#Research_program}

The highlights of this research program are:

- the various (but equivalent) [constructions of the real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructions_of_the_real_numbers) by [Dedekind](wikipedia://en/23475106) and [Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) resulting in the modern [axiomatic](wikipedia://en/928) definition of the [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) [field](wikipedia://en/10603);
- the epsilon-delta definition of [limit](wikipedia://en/26551602); and
- the [naïve set-theoretic](wikipedia://en/4944) definition of [function](wikipedia://en/185427).

## Legacy {#Legacy}

An important spinoff of the arithmetization of analysis is set theory. Naive set theory was created by Cantor and others after arithmetization was completed as a way to study the singularities of functions appearing in calculus.

The arithmetization of analysis had several important consequences:

- the widely held belief in the banishment of [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) from mathematics until the creation of [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis) by [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024) in the 1960s, whereas in reality the work on non-Archimedean systems continued unabated, as documented by P. Ehrlich;
- the shift of the emphasis from [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) to [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) reasoning: this has had important consequences in the way mathematics is taught today;
- it made possible the development of modern [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) by [Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) and the rudiments of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) by [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302);
- it motivated the currently prevalent philosophical position that all of mathematics should be derivable from [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and set theory, ultimately leading to [Hilbert's program](wikipedia://en/607286), [Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)'s theorems and [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).
