# Henry Flynt

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Henry Flynt"}

**Henry Flynt** (born 1940 in [Greensboro, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina)) is an American philosopher, musician, writer, activist, and artist connected to the 1960s New York [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde). He coined the term "[concept art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_art#Origins)" in the early 1960s, during which time he was associated with figures in the [Fluxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxus) scene.[^o4][^o5] He later received attention for his [anti-art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-art) demonstrations against New York [cultural institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_institution) in 1963 and 1964.

:::

Since 1983, he has focused on philosophical writing related to [nihilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism), science, [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), [post-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-capitalist) economics, and [personhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personhood). A number of his archival musical recordings, which fuse [hillbilly music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillbilly_music) with [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) techniques, were released in the 2000s. He has collaborated with artists such as [C.C. Hennix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.C._Hennix), [La Monte Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Monte_Young), [George Maciunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maciunas), and [John Berndt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Berndt).

## Background {#Background}

Henry Flynt was born and raised in [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina), where he first studied classical [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin). He became interested in [logical positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism) as a teenager, and later attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) on a scholarship, where he studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) alongside companions [Tony Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Conrad) and John Alten. At Harvard, Flynt was introduced to [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) and the "New Music" of [John Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cage) by graduate students [Christian Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wolff_%28composer%29) and [Frederic Rzewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Rzewski),[^o7] and he also discovered [country blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_blues) music through [Samuel Charters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Charters)'s [1959 book on the subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Country_Blues_%28book%29) at this time.[^o8] He soon dropped out and visited New York in 1960, where through Conrad he was introduced to [La Monte Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Monte_Young) and other figures in the city's [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) scene.[^o9] Young would dedicate his 1960 composition "X for Henry Flynt" to him.[^o10][^o11] In 1960 and 1961, Flynt took part in the monthly concert series held at [Yoko Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoko_Ono)'s [Chambers Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers_Street_%28Manhattan%29) loft.[^o7] He moved permanently to New York in 1963.[^o9]

Flynt's work developed from what he called "cognitive [nihilism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism)", a concept he first announced in the 1960 and 1961 drafts of a paper called *Philosophy Proper.* The concept derives from insights about the vulnerabilities of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and mathematics, and aims to turn the principles of scientific [positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivism) and [analytical philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytical_philosophy) against themselves. Embracing [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987)'s [empiricism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empiricism) and his positivist critique of [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), Flynt concluded that science itself did not satisfy the syntactical criteria for empirical claims; he therefore set about developing a "radical empiricism" (or "radical unbelief") which undermined scientific systematization and much "avant-garde" art.[^o12] Flynt refined these dispensations in the essay *Is there language?* that was published as *Primary Study* in 1964.

## Concept art and activism {#Concept_art_and_activism}

In 1961, Flynt coined the term "[concept art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_art#Origins)"[^o14] in the proto-[Fluxus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluxus) book *[An Anthology of Chance Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Anthology_of_Chance_Operations)*[^o15] (co-published by [Jackson Mac Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Mac_Low) and La Monte Young), released in 1963,[^o16] alongside works by Fluxus artists such as [George Brecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Brecht) and [Dick Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Higgins). Flynt's *concept art*, he maintained, devolved from cognitive nihilism and described art in which the medium was [concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept).[^o17] Drawing exclusively on the [syntax](wikipedia://en/26860) of logic and mathematics, concept art was meant to surpass both mathematics and "serious" compositional practices by evacuating concepts of substance via [logical paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_paradox). Flynt maintained that, to merit the label *concept art*, a work had to be a critique of logic or mathematics in which the material is a linguistic concept, a quality which he claims is absent from subsequent "conceptual art".[^o18]

In 1962, Flynt began to campaign for an [anti-art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-art) approach to the arts.[^o19] He advocated that [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) art and its institutions be superseded by the terms of *veramusement* and *brend*—[neologisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) meaning (approximately) pure [recreation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreation).[^o9] As part of his campaign, he demonstrated against cultural institutions in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) (such as [MoMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoMA) and [Lincoln Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts)) with [Tony Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Conrad) and [Jack Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Smith_%28film_director%29) in 1963 and against the composer [Karlheinz Stockhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlheinz_Stockhausen) twice in 1964 (accusing Stockhausen of [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy) and [cultural imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_imperialism)).[^o20] Flynt read publicly from his text, *From Culture to Veramusment*, at [Walter De Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_De_Maria)'s loft on February 28, 1963—an act which can, in hindsight, be considered [performance art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_art). In the mid-1960s, Flynt converted himself to [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), joining the [Workers World Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_World_Party),[^o9] and published the article "Communists Must Give Revolutionary Leadership in Culture" in collaboration with [George Maciunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maciunas), criticizing the [white supremacist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacist) cultural touchstones of the left-wing tradition and championing [African-American music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_music). From 1964 to 1966, Flynt wrote regularly for the WWP's *[Workers World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_World_%28newspaper%29)* under the pseudonym "Harry Stone", and edited the newspaper briefly in 1965.[^o9] He left the party in 1967 after becoming dissatisfied with the party's support of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^o9]

In 1987 he revived his "concept art" for tactical reasons; and spent seven years in the art world.

## Music {#Music}

Henry Flynt is known for musical work that attempts to fuse [hillbilly music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillbilly_music) with the [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde_music),[^o7] often with him performing on violin or guitar.[^o10] Other influences included the [free jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_jazz) of [Ornette Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornette_Coleman); [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly) and [country blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_blues) music; and the [North Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian_classical_music) he learned with singer [Pandit Pran Nath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandit_Pran_Nath).[^o10] With the exception of the 1981 German cassette *You Are My Everlovin'/Celestial Power*, Flynt's recordings remained unreleased until the 21st century, via labels such as Recorded and Locust.[^o10][^o22]

Flynt performed duets with [La Monte Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Monte_Young) in the 1960s, but recordings of these performances were rejected by [Mainstream Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainstream_Records) employee [Earle Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_Brown) as being too unconventional for a classical label.[^o8] In an early 1960s conversation with [John Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cage), Flynt announced his intention to abandon "serious" modern composition and pursue music in the style of rock artists like [Bo Diddley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo_Diddley) and [Chuck Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry), to which Cage expressed confusion.[^o8] Flynt briefly performed violin with [the Velvet Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Underground) in 1966 as a fill-in for [John Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cale), and received guitar lessons from [Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed),[^o10] regarding the performance Flynt stated:[^o23]

In 1966, Flynt recorded several rehearsal demo tapes with [Walter De Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_De_Maria), [Art Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Murphy), and Paul Breslin as part of the [experimental rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_rock) band the Insurrections,[^o9] which were later compiled and released in 2004 on [Locust Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locust_Music) as *[I Don't Wanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Don't_Wanna_%28album%29)*.[^o24][^o25]

In 1974 and 1975, Flynt led the group Nova'Billy and recorded material spanning rock, [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music), and [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk) that was later collected on *Henry Flynt & Nova'Billy* (Locust, 2007). With [Catherine Christer Hennix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Christer_Hennix), Flynt formed the [jazz-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz-rock) group Dharma Warriors in 1978, initially including [Arthur Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Russell_%28musician%29) on keyboard.[^o8] *Dharma Warriors* (Locust, 2008) showcases another meeting between Hennix & Flynt recorded in 1980 in Woodstock, New York. *Purified by the Fire*, recorded in December 1981, features Hennix on tamboura and Flynt on electric violin and was released in 2005 on Locust.

Flynt's first CD release was a reissue of *You Are My Everlovin'/Celestial Power* on Recorded (curated by [John Berndt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Berndt), and initiating the *New American Ethnic Music* series on that label), quickly followed by *Spindizzy* and *Hillbilly Tape Music* also on Recorded. Later Recorded released NAEM 4, *Ascent to The Sun.* Recently, Flynt's *Glissando No. 1* was published by Recorded (2010).

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

Flynt's philosophical writing attempts to sketch out a [post-capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-capitalist), post-scientific civilization which would be at odds with the current civilization's values.[^o27] Flynt's early philosophical writings on logic and epistemology, including the 1961 draft of *Philosophy Proper*, was published in [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) in the book *Blueprint for a Higher Civilization* (1975). Deriving broadly from his early arguments around "cognitive nihilism" and positivism, Flynt's work aims to overturn the dogmatic [scientism](wikipedia://en/167742) and apparent coherence of contemporary scientific and mathematical discourse.[^o28]

In the early 1970s, he returned to college to study [communist economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_economics).[^o28] In the late 1970s, he organized several meetings on the "crisis in physics" in an attempt to identify the areas where modern science represses incoherent or irrational logics in order to propagate its "objective" worldview. From about 1980, Flynt has written on philosophy and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) in mostly unpublished papers,[^o10] focusing on two concepts which did not achieve the notoriety of the early actions: his concepts of meta-technology[^o29] and personhood theory.[^o30] These concepts continue his work in sketching a worldview which would supersede scientific objectification and dissolve contemporary determinations of [objective reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Objective_reality).[^o31] Much of his writing is now available on his website.

## Relationship with Fluxus {#Relationship_with_Fluxus}

Because of his friendship and collaboration with [La Monte Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Monte_Young) and [George Maciunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Maciunas), Flynt sometimes is linked to Fluxus. While Flynt himself describes Fluxus as his "publisher of last resort" (Flynt did permit Fluxus to publish his work, and took part in several Fluxus exhibitions) he claims no affiliation or interest in the Fluxus sensibility. In fact, he is a strong critic of the neo-Dada sensibility.[^o33]
