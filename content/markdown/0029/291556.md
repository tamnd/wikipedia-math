# Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics"}

*Image: Arthur\_Cayley.jpg.* Arthur Cayley, mathematician

The **Sadleirian Professorship of Pure Mathematics**, originally spelled in the statutes and for the first two professors as **Sadlerian**,[^o2] is a [professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_professorships_at_the_University_of_Cambridge) in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) within the [DPMMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPMMS) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). It was founded on a bequest from [Lady Mary Sadleir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Croone#Legacy) for [lectureships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecture) "for the full and clear explication and teaching that part of mathematical knowledge commonly called [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923)". She died in 1706 and lectures began in 1710 but eventually these failed to attract undergraduates. In 1860 the foundation was used to establish the [professorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship).[^o3][^o4] On 10 June 1863 Arthur Cayley was elected with the statutory duty "to explain and teach the principles of pure mathematics, and to apply himself to the advancement of that science." The stipend attached to the professorship was modest although it improved in the course of subsequent legislation.
