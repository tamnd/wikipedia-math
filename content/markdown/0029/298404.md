# Michael O. Rabin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Michael O. Rabin"}

**Michael Oser Rabin** (מִיכָאֵל עוזר רַבִּין; September 1, 1931 – April 14, 2026) was a [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) who was co-recipient, with [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875), of the 1976 ACM [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) for their work on computational complexity.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

### Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rabin was born in 1931 in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), [Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Lower_Silesia), [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Prussia), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) (today [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)), the son of a [rabbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbi). In 1935, he [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah) with his family to [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine). As a young boy, he was very interested in mathematics and his father sent him to the best high school in [Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa), where he studied under mathematician [Elisha Netanyahu](wikipedia://en/23745967), who was then a high school teacher.[^o5]

He graduated from the [Hebrew Reali School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Reali_School) in Haifa in 1948, and was drafted into the army during the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab–Israeli_War). The mathematician [Abraham Fraenkel](wikipedia://en/528451), who was a professor of mathematics in [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem), intervened with the army command, and Rabin was discharged to study at the university in 1949.[^o5] Afterwards, he received an M.Sc from [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem). He began graduate studies at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) before receiving a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1956.[^o6]

### Career {#Career}

In the late 1950s, Rabin was invited for a summer to do research for [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) at the Lamb Estate in [Westchester County, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County,_New_York), with other promising mathematicians and scientists. It was there that he and [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) wrote the paper "Finite Automata and Their Decision Problems".[^o8] Soon, using nondeterministic automata, they were able to re-prove [Kleene's](wikipedia://en/28805) result that finite state machines exactly accept regular languages.[^o5]

As to the origins of what was to become [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), the next summer Rabin returned to the Lamb Estate. [John McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCarthy_%28computer_scientist%29) posed a puzzle to him about spies, guards, and passwords, which Rabin studied and soon after he wrote an article, "Degree of Difficulty of Computing a Function and Hierarchy of Recursive Sets".[^o5][^o9]

[Nondeterministic machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondeterministic_machine) have become a key concept in computational complexity theory, particularly with the description of the [complexity classes P and NP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_=_NP_problem).

He then returned to Jerusalem, researching logic, and working on the foundations of what would later be known as [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). He was an associate professor and the head of the Institute of Mathematics at the Hebrew University at 29 years old, and a full professor by 33. Rabin recalls, "There was absolutely no appreciation of the work on the issues of computing. Mathematicians did not recognize the emerging new field".[^o5]

In 1960, Rabin was invited by [Edward F. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_F._Moore) to work at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs), where Rabin introduced [probabilistic automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_automata) that employ coin tosses to decide which state transitions to take. He showed examples of regular languages that required a very large number of states, but for which you get an exponential reduction of the number of states with probabilistic automata.[^o5]

Rabin was a Visiting Associate Professor of Mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in the 1961–62 school year and at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) for the 1962-1963 school year. Before moving to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as Gordon McKay Professor of Computer Science in 1981, he was a professor at the Hebrew University.[^o10]

In 1966 (published in conference proceedings in 1967),[^o11] Rabin introduced the notion of [polynomial time](wikipedia://en/658550) (introduced independently and very shortly before by [Cobham](wikipedia://en/68162815)[^o12] and [Edmonds](wikipedia://en/3625459)[^o13]).

In 1969, Rabin introduced [infinite-tree automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-tree_automaton) and proved that the [monadic second-order theory](wikipedia://en/14650395) of *n* successors ([S2S](wikipedia://en/72252525) when *n* = 2) is [decidable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decidable_language).[^o14] A key component of the proof implicitly showed [determinacy](wikipedia://en/2596030) of [parity games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_game), which lie in the third level of the [Borel hierarchy](wikipedia://en/5539282).

In 1975, Rabin finished his tenure as Rector of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and went to the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in the USA as a visiting professor. While there, Rabin invented the [Miller–Rabin primality test](wikipedia://en/188999), a randomized algorithm that can determine very quickly (but with a tiny probability of error) whether a number is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666).[^o15][^o16] Rabin's method was based on previous work of [Gary Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Miller_%28computer_scientist%29) that solved the problem deterministically with the assumption that the [generalized Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/152567) is true, but Rabin's version of the test made no such assumption. Fast primality testing is key in the successful implementation of most public-key cryptography, and in 2003 Miller, Rabin, [Robert M. Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110), and [Volker Strassen](wikipedia://en/771960) were given the [Paris Kanellakis Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Award) for their work on primality testing.

In 1976 Rabin was invited by [Joseph Traub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_F._Traub) to meet at [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) and presented the primality test, which Traub called "revolutionary".[^o5]

In 1978, Rabin invented the [Rabin signature algorithm](wikipedia://en/5954800), the first asymmetric cryptosystem whose security was proved equivalent to the intractability of [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491).[^o17][^o18]

In 1981, Rabin reinvented a weak variant of the technique of [oblivious transfer](wikipedia://en/554716) invented by Wiesner under the name of multiplexing,[^o19] allowing a sender to transmit a message to a receiver where the receiver has some probability between 0 and 1 of learning the message, with the sender being unaware whether the receiver was able to do so.

In 1987, Rabin, together with [Richard Karp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Karp), created one of the most well-known efficient [string search algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_search_algorithm), the [Rabin–Karp string search algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabin–Karp_string_search_algorithm), known for its [rolling hash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_hash).[^o20]

Rabin's subsequent research concentrated on computer security. During the spring semester of 2007, he was a visiting professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) teaching *Introduction to [Cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432)*. He retired from full-time academic life as the [Thomas J. Watson Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_J._Watson) Professor of Computer Science, Emeritus at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and Professor of Computer Science (Emeritus) at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University).

### Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Rabin died on April 14, 2026, at the age of 94.[^o22]

His daughter, [Tal Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal_Rabin), is also a distinguished computer scientist.[^o23]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Rabin was a foreign member of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o25] a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society),[^o26] a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences),[^o27] a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences),[^o28] and a foreign member of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o29]

In 1976, the [Turing Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award) was awarded jointly to Rabin and [Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) for a paper written in 1959, the citation for which states that the award was granted:

> *For their joint paper "Finite Automata and Their Decision Problems," which introduced the idea of nondeterministic machines, which has proved to be an enormously valuable concept. Their (Scott & Rabin) classic paper has been a continuous source of inspiration for subsequent work in this field.*[^o30]

In 1995, Rabin was awarded the [Israel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prize), in computer sciences.[^o31] In 2010, Rabin was awarded the [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) [Dan David Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_David_Prize) ("Future" category), jointly with [Leonard Kleinrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Kleinrock) and [Gordon E. Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_E._Moore), for Computers and Telecommunications.[^o32] Rabin was awarded an Honorary Doctor of Science from Harvard University in 2017.[^o33]
