# Richard Borcherds

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Borcherds"}

**Richard Ewen Borcherds** (; born 29 November 1959)[^o3] is a British[^o5] mathematician currently working in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267). He is known for his work in [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), and infinite-dimensional [algebras](wikipedia://en/191788),[^o6][^o7] for which he was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1998. He is well known for his proof of [monstrous moonshine](wikipedia://en/728168) using ideas from [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Borcherds was born in [Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town), South Africa, but the family moved to [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) in the United Kingdom when he was six months old.[^o17]

Borcherds was educated at [King Edward's School, Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward's_School,_Birmingham). As a student, Borcherds won a gold medal, silver medal, and special prize in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208).[^o9] He attended university at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge),[^o10] where he studied under [John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807).[^o11]

## Career {#Career}

After receiving his doctorate in 1985, Borcherds has held various alternating positions at Cambridge and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), serving as Morrey assistant professor of mathematics at Berkeley from 1987 to 1988. He was a [Royal Society University Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_University_Research_Fellow).[^o13][^o10] From 1996 he held a [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) Research Professorship at Cambridge before returning to Berkeley in 1999 as professor of mathematics.[^o10]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

He did notable work on the [Monstrous moonshine theory](wikipedia://en/728168).[^o6]

He introduced [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra).[^o15]

## Autism {#Autism}

An interview with [Simon Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Singh) for *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, in which Borcherds suggested he might have some sort of traits possibly associated with [Asperger syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asperger_syndrome),[^o17] led to a chapter about him in a book on autism by [Simon Baron-Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Baron-Cohen).[^o18][^o19] Baron-Cohen insinuated that while Borcherds may have had autistic traits, he did not meet a formal diagnosis of Asperger's syndrome.[^o18]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

In 1992 Borcherds was one of the first recipients of the [EMS prizes](wikipedia://en/969470) awarded at the first [European Congress of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13505846) in Paris, and in 1994 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Zurich.[^o11] In 1994, he was elected to be a Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o21] In 1998 at the 23rd [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin, Germany he received the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) together with [Maxim Kontsevich](wikipedia://en/104509), [William Timothy Gowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Timothy_Gowers) and [Curtis T. McMullen](wikipedia://en/680168).[^o11] The award cited him "for his contributions to algebra, the theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), and mathematical physics, including the introduction of vertex algebras and [Borcherds' Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1646167), the proof of the [Conway-Norton moonshine conjecture](wikipedia://en/728168)[^o22] and the discovery of a new class of automorphic infinite products." In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822),[^o23] and in 2014 he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o24]
