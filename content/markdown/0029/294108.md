# SEAL (cipher)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="SEAL"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **SEAL (Software-Optimized Encryption Algorithm)** is a [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) optimised for machines with a 32-[bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit) [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_%28computer_architecture%29) size and plenty of [RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_Access_Memory) with a reported performance of around 4 [cycles per byte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycles_per_byte). SEAL is actually a [pseudorandom function family](wikipedia://en/3454616) in that it can easily generate arbitrary portions of the [keystream](wikipedia://en/10441065) without having to start from the beginning. This makes it particularly well suited for applications like encrypting hard drives.

:::

The first version was published by [Phillip Rogaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Rogaway) and [Don Coppersmith](wikipedia://en/540090) in 1994. The current version, published in 1997, is 3.0. SEAL, covered by two patents in the United States, both of which are assigned to [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM).
