# \*-algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="*-algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically in [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **\*-algebra** (or **involutive algebra**; read as "star-algebra") is a mathematical structure consisting of two **involutive rings** $R$ and $A$, where $R$ is commutative and $A$ has the structure of an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over $R$. Involutive algebras generalize the idea of a number system equipped with conjugation, for example the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) and [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation), [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over the complex numbers and [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548), and [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) over a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) and [Hermitian adjoints](wikipedia://en/667175). However, it may happen that an algebra admits no [involution](wikipedia://en/293450).

:::

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### \*-ring {#*-ring}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **\*-ring** is a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $A$ with a map $* : A \to A$ that is an [antiautomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiautomorphism) and an [involution](wikipedia://en/22679231).

More precisely, $*$ is required to satisfy the following properties:[^o5]

- $$(x + y)* = x* + y*$$
- $$(x\,y)* = y*\,x*$$
- $$1* = 1$$
- $$(x*)* = x$$

for all $x,\,y$ in $A$.

This is also called an **involutive ring**, **involutory ring**, and **ring with involution**. The third axiom is implied by the second and fourth axioms, making it redundant.

Elements such that $x* = x$ are called *[self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint)*.[^o6]

Archetypical examples of a \*-ring are fields of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) with [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation) as the involution. One can define a [sesquilinear form](wikipedia://en/860138) over any \*-ring.

Also, one can define \*-versions of algebraic objects, such as [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) and [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145), with the requirement to be \*-[invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638#Invariant_set): $x \in I \Rightarrow x* \in I$ and so on.

\*-rings are unrelated to [star semirings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_semiring) in the theory of computation.

### \*-algebra {#*-algebra}

A **\*-algebra** $A$ is a \*-ring, with involution \* that is an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over a [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346) \*-ring $R$ with involution , such that $(r\,x)* = r\,x* \forall r \in R, x \in A$.

The base \*-ring $R$ is often the complex numbers (with acting as complex conjugation).

It follows from the axioms that \* on $A$ is [conjugate-linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate-linear) in $R$, meaning

$$(\lambda\,x + \mu\,y)* = \lambda\,x* + \mu\,y*$$

for $\lambda,\,\mu \in R, x,\,y \in A$.

A **\*-homomorphism** $f : A \to B$ is an [algebra homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_homomorphism) that is compatible with the involutions of $A$ and $B$, i.e.,

- $f(a*) = f(a)*$ for all $a$ in $A$.[^o6]

### Philosophy of the \*-operation {#Philosophy_of_the_*-operation}

The \*-operation on a \*-ring is analogous to [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation) on the complex numbers. The \*-operation on a \*-algebra is analogous to taking [adjoints](wikipedia://en/217548) in complex [matrix algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_algebra).

::: {#o11 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

### Notation {#Notation}

The \* involution is a [unary operation](wikipedia://en/161006) written with a postfixed star glyph centered above or near the [mean line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_line):

$x \mapsto x*$, or

$x \mapsto x^{\ast}$ ([TeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeX): `x^*`),

but not as "$x\ast$"; see the [asterisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk) article for details.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) becomes a \*-ring with the trivial ([identical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_map)) involution.
- The most familiar example of a \*-ring and a \*-algebra over [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438) is the field of complex numbers $\mathbf{C}$ where \* is just [complex conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation).
- More generally, a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) made by adjunction of a [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) (such as the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) $\sqrt{-1}$) is a \*-algebra over the original field, considered as a trivially-\*-ring. The \* [flips the sign](wikipedia://en/228312) of that square root.
- A [quadratic integer](wikipedia://en/21834785) ring (for some $D$) is a commutative \*-ring with the \* defined in the similar way; [quadratic fields](wikipedia://en/454781) are \*-algebras over appropriate quadratic integer rings.
- [Quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), [split-complex numbers](wikipedia://en/893559), [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169), and possibly other [hypercomplex number](wikipedia://en/51438) systems form \*-rings (with their built-in conjugation operation) and \*-algebras over reals (where \* is trivial). None of the three is a complex algebra.
- [Hurwitz quaternions](wikipedia://en/772241) form a non-commutative \*-ring with the quaternion conjugation.
- The [matrix algebra](wikipedia://en/487541) of $n\,\times\,n$[matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over **R** with \* given by the [transposition](wikipedia://en/173844).
- The matrix algebra of $n\,\times\,n$matrices over **C** with \* given by the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548).
- Its generalization, the [Hermitian adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) in the algebra of [bounded linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_operator) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) also defines a \*-algebra.
- The [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $R[x]$ over a commutative trivially-\*-ring $R$ is a \*-algebra over $R$ with $P\,*(x) = P\,(-x)$.
- If $(A,\,+,\,\times,\,*)$ is simultaneously a \*-ring, an [algebra over a ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_over_a_ring) $R$ (commutative), and $(r\,x)* = r\,(x*) \forall r \in R, x \in A$, then $A$ is a \*-algebra over $R$ (where \* is trivial).
  - As a partial case, any \*-ring is a \*-algebra over [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).
- Any commutative \*-ring is a \*-algebra over itself and, more generally, over any its \*-subring.
- For a commutative \*-ring $R$, its [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) by any its \*-ideal is a \*-algebra over $R$.
  - For example, any commutative trivially-\*-ring is a \*-algebra over its [dual numbers ring](wikipedia://en/42169#Generalization), a \*-ring with *non-trivial* \*, because the quotient by $\varepsilon = 0$ makes the original ring.
  - The same about a commutative ring $K$ and its polynomial ring $K[x]$: the quotient by $x = 0$ restores $K$.
- In [Hecke algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_algebra_of_a_Coxeter_group), an involution is important to the [Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial](wikipedia://en/10161645).
- The [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623) of an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) becomes a \*-algebra over the integers, where the involution is given by taking the [dual isogeny](wikipedia://en/7045490). A similar construction works for [abelian varieties](wikipedia://en/247152) with a [polarization](wikipedia://en/247152), in which case it is called the [Rosati involution](wikipedia://en/34645248) (see Milne's lecture notes on abelian varieties).

[Involutive Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714#Examples) are important examples of \*-algebras (with the additional structure of a compatible [comultiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comultiplication)); the most familiar example being:

- The [group Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/12842125): a [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223), with involution given by $g \mapsto g^{-1}$.

:::

## Non-Example {#Non-Example}

Not every algebra admits an involution:

Regard the 2×2 [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) over the complex numbers. Consider the following subalgebra:

$$\mathcal{A} := \left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&0\end{pmatrix} : a,b\in\mathbb{C}\right\}$$

Any nontrivial antiautomorphism necessarily has the form:[^o16]

$$\varphi_z\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\right] = \begin{pmatrix}1&z\\0&0\end{pmatrix} \quad \varphi_z\left[\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\right] = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$$

for any complex number $z\in\mathbb{C}$.

It follows that any nontrivial antiautomorphism fails to be involutive:

$$\varphi_z^2\left[\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\right] = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\neq\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$$

Concluding that the subalgebra admits no involution.

:::

## Additional structures {#Additional_structures}

Many properties of the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) hold for general \*-algebras:

- The [Hermitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) elements form a [Jordan algebra](wikipedia://en/936371);
- The skew Hermitian elements form a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944);
- If 2 is invertible in the \*-ring, then the operators $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + *)$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 - *)$ are [orthogonal idempotents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idempotent),[^o6] called *symmetrizing* and *anti-symmetrizing*, so the algebra decomposes as a direct sum of [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) ([vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) if the \*-ring is a field) of symmetric and anti-symmetric (Hermitian and skew Hermitian) elements. These spaces do not, generally, form associative algebras, because the idempotents are [operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator), not elements of the algebra.

### Skew structures {#Skew_structures}

Given a \*-ring, there is also the map $-* : x \mapsto -x*$. It does not define a \*-ring structure (unless the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) is 2, in which case −\* is identical to the original \*), as $1 \mapsto -1$, neither is it antimultiplicative, but it satisfies the other axioms (linear, involution) and hence is quite similar to \*-algebra where $x \mapsto x*$.

Elements fixed by this map (i.e., such that $a = -a*$) are called *skew Hermitian*.

For the complex numbers with complex conjugation, the real numbers are the Hermitian elements, and the imaginary numbers are the skew Hermitian.
