# Schwinger model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schwinger model"}

In [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), the **Schwinger model** is a model describing 1+1D (time + 1 spatial dimension) [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268) (QED) which includes [electrons](wikipedia://en/39407), coupled to [photons](wikipedia://en/19737). It is named after [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980) who developed it in 1962.[^o2]

:::

The model defines the usual QED [Lagrangian density](wikipedia://en/40752010)

$$\mathcal{L} = - \frac{1}{4g^2}F_{\mu \nu}F^{\mu \nu} + \bar{\psi} (i \gamma^\mu D_\mu -m) \psi$$

over a [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) with one spatial dimension and one temporal dimension. Where $F_{\mu \nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is the photon [field strength](wikipedia://en/1912367) with symmetry group $\mathrm{U}(1)$ ([unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993)), $D_\mu = \partial_\mu - iA_\mu$ is the [gauge covariant derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_covariant_derivative), $\psi$ is the [fermion spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor), $m$ is the fermion mass and $\gamma^0, \gamma^1$ form the two-dimensional representation of the [Clifford algebra](wikipedia://en/45305).

This model exhibits [confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_confinement) of the fermions and as such, is a toy model for [quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264). A handwaving argument why this is so is because in two dimensions, classically, the potential between two charged particles goes linearly as $r$, instead of $1/r$ in 4 dimensions, 3 spatial, 1 time. This model also exhibits a [spontaneous symmetry breaking](wikipedia://en/297466) of the U(1) symmetry due to a [chiral condensate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral_condensate) due to a pool of [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077). The [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon) in this model becomes a massive particle at low temperatures. This model can be solved exactly and is used as a [toy model](wikipedia://en/701142) for other more complex theories.[^o3][^o4]
