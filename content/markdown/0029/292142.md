# Sarvadaman Chowla

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sarvadaman Chowla"}

**Sarvadaman D. S. Chowla** (22 October 1907 – 10 December 1995) was an English-American mathematician, specializing in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in London, since his father, Gopal Chowla, a professor of mathematics in [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore), was then studying in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge).[^o4][^o5] His family returned to India, where he received his master's degree in 1928 from the [Government College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_College_University,_Lahore) in Lahore. In 1931 he received his doctorate from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), where he studied under [J. E. Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371). [^o6] He was born into a [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Hindus) [Arora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arora) family.

## Career and awards {#Career_and_awards}

Chowla then returned to India, where he taught at several universities, becoming head of mathematics at Government College, Lahore in 1936.[^o6] During the difficulties arising from the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, he left for the United States. There he visited the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) until the fall of 1949, then taught at the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas) in Lawrence until moving to the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_at_Boulder) in 1952.[^o6] He moved to [Penn State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) in 1963 as a research professor, where he remained until his retirement in 1976.[^o6] He was a member of the [Indian National Science Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Science_Academy).[^o6]

Among his contributions are a number of results which bear his name. These include the [Bruck–Ryser–Chowla theorem](wikipedia://en/456781), the [Ankeny–Artin–Chowla congruence](wikipedia://en/534610), the [Chowla–Mordell theorem](wikipedia://en/595896), and the [Chowla–Selberg formula](wikipedia://en/596179), and the [Mian–Chowla sequence](wikipedia://en/8410911).

He also posed [Chowla's cosine problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowla's_cosine_problem), concerning how negative a sum of cosine functions with distinct positive integer frequencies must become, in terms of the number of terms.[^o9]
