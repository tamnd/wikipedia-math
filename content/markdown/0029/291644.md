# Glossary of field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Glossary of field theory"}

[Field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28mathematics%29) is the branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in which [fields](wikipedia://en/10603) are studied.

:::

Field (mathematics)

This is a glossary of some terms of the subject. (See [field theory (physics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_theory_%28physics%29) for the unrelated field theories in physics.)

## Definition of a field {#Definition_of_a_field}

A **field** is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) (*F*, +, \*) in which 0 ≠ 1 and every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. In a field we thus can perform the operations addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division.

The non-zero elements of a field *F* form an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) under multiplication; this group is typically denoted by *F*<sup>×</sup>;

The [ring of polynomials](wikipedia://en/373065) in the variable *x* with coefficients in *F* is denoted by *F*\[*x*\].

## Basic definitions {#Basic_definitions}

::: {#o4 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Characteristic"}

**[Characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621)**

The *characteristic* of the field *F* is the smallest positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n* such that *n*·1 = 0; here *n*·1 stands for *n* summands 1 + 1 + 1 + ... + 1. If no such *n* exists, we say the characteristic is zero. Every non-zero characteristic is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666). For example, the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and the [*p*-adic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_numbers) have characteristic 0, while the finite field **Z**<sub>*p*</sub> with *p* being prime has characteristic *p*.

:::

::: {#o5 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Subfield"}

**Subfield**

A *subfield* of a field *F* is a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of *F* which is closed under the field operation + and \* of *F* and which, with these operations, forms itself a field.

:::

::: {#o6 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Prime field"}

**[Prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field)**

The *prime field* of the field *F* is the unique smallest subfield of *F*.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Extension field"}

**[Extension field](wikipedia://en/11634)**

If *F* is a subfield of *E* then *E* is an *extension field* of *F*. We then also say that *E*/*F* is a *field extension*.

:::

::: {#o8 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Degree of an extension"}

**[Degree of an extension](wikipedia://en/4382697)**

Given an extension *E*/*F*, the field *E* can be considered as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the field *F*, and the [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) of this vector space is the *degree* of the extension, denoted by \[*E* : *F*\].

:::

::: {#o9 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Finite extension"}

**Finite extension**

A *finite extension* is a field extension whose degree is finite.

:::

::: {#o10 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraic extension"}

**[Algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125)**

If an element *α* of an extension field *E* over *F* is the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root) of a non-zero polynomial in *F*\[*x*\], then *α* is *algebraic* over *F*. If every element of *E* is algebraic over *F*, then *E*/*F* is an *algebraic extension*.

:::

::: {#o11 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Generating set"}

**Generating set**

Given a field extension *E*/*F* and a subset *S* of *E*, we write *F*(*S*) for the smallest subfield of *E* that contains both *F* and *S*. It consists of all the elements of *E* that can be obtained by repeatedly using the operations +, −, \*, / on the elements of *F* and *S*. If *E* = *F*(*S*), we say that *E* is generated by *S* over *F*.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Primitive element"}

**[Primitive element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_element_%28field_theory%29)**

An element *α* of an extension field *E* over a field *F* is called a *primitive element* if *E*=*F*(*α*), the smallest extension field containing *α*. Such an extension is called a **[simple extension](wikipedia://en/4771726)**.

:::

::: {#o13 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Splitting field"}

**[Splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440)**

A field extension generated by the complete factorisation of a polynomial.

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Normal extension"}

**[Normal extension](wikipedia://en/929925)**

A field extension generated by the complete factorisation of a set of polynomials.

:::

::: {#o15 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Separable extension"}

**[Separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340)**

An extension generated by roots of [separable polynomials](wikipedia://en/362400).

:::

::: {#o16 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Perfect field"}

**[Perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088)**

A field such that every finite extension is separable. All fields of characteristic zero, and all finite fields, are perfect.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Imperfect degree"}

**[Imperfect degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperfect_degree)**

Let *F* be a field of characteristic *p* > 0; then *F*<sup>*p*</sup> is a subfield. The degree \[*F* : *F*<sup>*p*</sup>\] is called the *imperfect degree* of *F*. The field *F* is perfect if and only if its imperfect degree is *1*. For example, if *F* is a function field of *n* variables over a finite field of characteristic *p* > 0, then its imperfect degree is *p*<sup>*n*</sup>.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o19 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraically closed field"}

**[Algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018)**

A field *F* is *algebraically closed* if every polynomial in *F*\[*x*\] has a root in *F*; equivalently: every polynomial in *F*\[*x*\] is a product of linear factors.

:::

::: {#o20 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraic closure"}

**[Algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129)**

An *algebraic closure* of a field *F* is an algebraic extension of *F* which is algebraically closed. Every field has an algebraic closure, and it is unique up to an isomorphism that fixes *F*.

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Transcendental"}

**[Transcendental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendental_element)**

Those elements of an extension field of *F* that are not algebraic over *F* are *transcendental* over *F*.

:::

::: {#o22 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraically independent elements"}

**Algebraically independent elements**

Elements of an extension field of *F* are *algebraically independent* over *F* if they don't satisfy any non-zero polynomial equation with coefficients in *F*.

:::

::: {#o23 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Transcendence degree"}

**[Transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree)**

The number of algebraically independent transcendental elements in a field extension. It is used to define the [dimension of an algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/253251).

:::

## Homomorphisms {#Homomorphisms}

::: {#o25 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Field homomorphism"}

**Field homomorphism**

A *field homomorphism* between two fields *E* and *F* is a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411), i.e., a [function](wikipedia://en/185427)

*f* : *E* → *F*

such that, for all *x*, *y* in *E*,

*f*(*x* + *y*) = *f*(*x*) + *f*(*y*)

*f*(*xy*) = *f*(*x*) *f*(*y*)

*f*(1) = 1.

For fields *E* and *F*, these properties imply that *f*(0) = 0, *f*(*x*<sup>−1</sup>) = *f*(*x*)<sup>−1</sup> for *x* in *E*<sup>×</sup>, and that *f* is [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective). Fields, together with these homomorphisms, form a [category](wikipedia://en/5869). Two fields *E* and *F* are called **isomorphic** if there exists a [bijective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijective) homomorphism

*f* : *E* → *F*.

The two fields are then identical for all practical purposes; however, not necessarily in a *unique* way. See, for example, *[Complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918)*.

:::

## Types of fields {#Types_of_fields}

::: {#o27 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Finite field"}

**[Finite field](wikipedia://en/11615)**

A field with finitely many elements, a.k.a. **Galois field**.

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Ordered field"}

**[Ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430)**

A field with a [total order](wikipedia://en/30330) compatible with its operations.

:::

::: {#o29 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Rational numbers"}

**[Rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024)**

:::

::: {#o30 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Real numbers"}

**[Real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438)**

:::

::: {#o31 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Complex numbers"}

**[Complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826)**

:::

::: {#o32 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Number field"}

**[Number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field)**

Finite extension of the field of rational numbers.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraic numbers"}

**[Algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158)**

The field of algebraic numbers is the smallest algebraically closed extension of the field of rational numbers. Their detailed properties are studied in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

::: {#o34 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Quadratic field"}

**[Quadratic field](wikipedia://en/454781)**

A degree-two extension of the rational numbers.

:::

::: {#o35 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Cyclotomic field"}

**[Cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462)**

An extension of the rational numbers generated by a [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950).

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Totally real field"}

**[Totally real field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_real_field)**

A number field generated by a root of a polynomial, having all its roots real numbers.

:::

::: {#o37 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Formally real field"}

**[Formally real field](wikipedia://en/1067223)**

:::

::: {#o38 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Real closed field"}

**[Real closed field](wikipedia://en/1066093)**

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Global field"}

**[Global field](wikipedia://en/288358)**

A number field or a function field of one variable over a finite field.

:::

::: {#o40 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Local field"}

**[Local field](wikipedia://en/145375)**

A completion of some global field ([w.r.t.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W.r.t.) a prime of the integer ring).

:::

::: {#o41 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Complete field"}

**[Complete field](wikipedia://en/21156575)**

A field complete w.r.t. some valuation.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Pseudo algebraically closed field"}

**[Pseudo algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/3696313)**

A field in which every variety has a [rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200).[^n2]

:::

::: {#o44 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Henselian field"}

**[Henselian field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henselian_field)**

A field satisfying [Hensel lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hensel_lemma) w.r.t. some valuation. A generalization of complete fields.

:::

::: {#o45 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Hilbertian field"}

**[Hilbertian field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbertian_field)**

A field satisfying [Hilbert's irreducibility theorem](wikipedia://en/1951153): formally, one for which the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) is not [thin in the sense of Serre](wikipedia://en/3143411).[^n3][^n4]

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Kroneckerian field"}

**Kroneckerian field**

A totally real algebraic number field or a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field.[^n5]

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=glossary-term name="CM-field or J-field"}

**[CM-field](wikipedia://en/10240442) or **J-field**

An algebraic number field which is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field.[^n6]

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Linked field"}

**[Linked field](wikipedia://en/39227440)**

A field over which no [biquaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/42600802) is a [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625).[^n7]

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Frobenius field"}

**Frobenius field**

A [pseudo algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/3696313) whose [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) has the embedding property.[^n8]

:::

## Field extensions {#Field_extensions}

Let *E*/*F* be a field extension.

::: {#o57 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Algebraic extension"}

**[Algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125)**

An extension in which every element of *E* is algebraic over *F*.

:::

::: {#o58 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Simple extension"}

**[Simple extension](wikipedia://en/4771726)**

An extension which is generated by a single element, called a **primitive element**, or **generating element**.[^n9] The [primitive element theorem](wikipedia://en/448933) classifies such extensions.[^n10]

:::

::: {#o61 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Normal extension"}

**[Normal extension](wikipedia://en/929925)**

An extension that splits a family of polynomials: every root of the minimal polynomial of an element of *E* over *F* is also in *E*.

:::

::: {#o62 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Separable extension"}

**[Separable extension](wikipedia://en/363340)**

An algebraic extension in which the minimal polynomial of every element of *E* over *F* is a [separable polynomial](wikipedia://en/362400), that is, has distinct roots.[^n11]

:::

::: {#o64 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Galois extension"}

**[Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064)**

A normal, separable field extension.

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Primary extension"}

**[Primary extension](wikipedia://en/30505550)**

An extension *E*/*F* such that the algebraic closure of *F* in *E* is [purely inseparable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purely_inseparable) over *F*; equivalently, *E* is [linearly disjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_disjoint) from the [separable closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_closure) of *F*.[^n12]

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Purely transcendental extension"}

**[Purely transcendental extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purely_transcendental_extension)**

An extension *E*/*F* in which every element of *E* not in *F* is transcendental over *F*.[^n13][^n14]

:::

::: {#o70 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Regular extension"}

**[Regular extension](wikipedia://en/26047190)**

An extension *E*/*F* such that *E* is separable over *F* and *F* is algebraically closed in *E*.[^n12]

:::

::: {#o72 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Simple radical extension"}

**[Simple radical extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_radical_extension)**

A [simple extension](wikipedia://en/4771726) *E*/*F* generated by a single element *α* satisfying *α*<sup>*n*</sup> = *b* for an element *b* of *F*. In [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*, we also take an extension by a root of an [Artin–Schreier polynomial](wikipedia://en/2672057) to be a simple radical extension.[^n15]

:::

::: {#o74 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Radical extension"}

**[Radical extension](wikipedia://en/33085116)**

A tower *F* = *F*<sub>0</sub> \< *F*<sub>1</sub> \< ⋅⋅⋅ \< *F*<sub>*k*</sub> = *E* where each extension *F*<sub>*i*</sub> / *F*<sub>*i*−1</sub> is a simple radical extension.[^n15]

:::

::: {#o76 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Self-regular extension"}

**[Self-regular extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-regular_extension)**

An extension *E*/*F* such that *E* ⊗<sub>*F*</sub> *E* is an integral domain.[^n16]

:::

::: {#o78 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Totally transcendental extension"}

**Totally transcendental extension**

An extension *E*/*F* such that *F* is algebraically closed in *F*.[^n14]

:::

::: {#o80 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Distinguished class"}

**Distinguished class**

A class *C* of field extensions with the three properties[^n17]

1. If *E* is a C-extension of *F* and *F* is a C-extension of *K* then *E* is a C-extension of *K*.
1. If *E* and *F* are C-extensions of *K* in a common overfield *M*, then the [compositum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositum) *EF* is a C-extension of *K*.
1. If *E* is a C-extension of *F* and *E* > *K* > *F* then *E* is a C-extension of *K*.

:::

## Galois theory {#Galois_theory}

::: {#o83 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Galois extension"}

**[Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064)**

A normal, separable field extension.

:::

::: {#o84 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Galois group"}

**[Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673)**

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of a Galois extension. When it is a finite extension, this is a finite group of order equal to the degree of the extension. Galois groups for infinite extensions are [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266).

:::

::: {#o85 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Kummer theory"}

**[Kummer theory](wikipedia://en/581610)**

The Galois theory of taking *n*th roots, given enough [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity). It includes the general theory of [quadratic extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension).

:::

::: {#o86 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Artin–Schreier theory"}

**[Artin–Schreier theory](wikipedia://en/2672057)**

Covers an exceptional case of Kummer theory, in characteristic *p*.

:::

::: {#o87 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Normal basis"}

**[Normal basis](wikipedia://en/1028589)**

A basis in the vector space sense of *L* over *K*, on which the Galois group of *L* over *K* acts transitively.

:::

::: {#o88 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Tensor product of fields"}

**[Tensor product of fields](wikipedia://en/372240)**

A different foundational piece of algebra, including the [compositum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compositum) operation ([join](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Join_%28mathematics%29) of fields).

:::

## Extensions of Galois theory {#Extensions_of_Galois_theory}

::: {#o90 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Inverse problem of Galois theory"}

**[Inverse problem of Galois theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_problem_of_Galois_theory)**

Given a group *G*, find an extension of the rational number or other field with *G* as Galois group.

:::

::: {#o91 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Differential Galois theory"}

**[Differential Galois theory](wikipedia://en/833506)**

The subject in which symmetry groups of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) are studied along the lines traditional in Galois theory. This is actually an old idea, and one of the motivations when [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) founded the theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). It has not, probably, reached definitive form.

:::

::: {#o92 .entry cue=glossary-term name="Grothendieck's Galois theory"}

**[Grothendieck's Galois theory](wikipedia://en/372470)**

A very abstract approach from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), introduced to study the analogue of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).

:::
