# Brownian tree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brownian tree"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Brownian tree**, or **Aldous tree**, or **Continuum Random Tree (CRT)**[^o2] is a random [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) that can be defined from a [Brownian excursion](wikipedia://en/27097375). The Brownian tree was defined and studied by [David Aldous](wikipedia://en/14785295) in three articles published in 1991 and 1993. This tree has since then been generalized.

:::

This random tree has several equivalent definitions and constructions:[^o3] using sub-trees generated by finitely many leaves, using a Brownian excursion, Poisson separating a straight line or as a limit of Galton-Watson trees.

Intuitively, the Brownian tree is a [binary tree](wikipedia://en/4321) whose nodes (or branching points) are [dense](wikipedia://en/23523594) in the tree; which is to say that for any distinct two points of the tree, there will always exist a node between them. It is a [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) object which can be approximated with computers[^o4] or by physical processes with [dendritic structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrite_%28crystal%29).

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

The following definitions are different characterisations of a Brownian tree, they are taken from Aldous's three articles.[^o7][^o8][^o9] The notions of *leaf, node, branch, root* are the intuitive notions on a tree (for details, see [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914)).

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="Finite-dimensional laws"}

### Finite-dimensional laws {#Finite-dimensional_laws}

This definition gives the finite-dimensional laws of the subtrees generated by finitely many leaves.

Let us consider the space of all binary trees with $k$ leaves numbered from $1$ to $k$. These trees have $2k-1$ edges with lengths $(\ell_1,\dots,\ell_{2k-1})\in \mathbb{R}_+^{2k-1}$. A tree is then defined by its shape $\tau$ (which is to say the order of the nodes) and the edge lengths. We define a [probability law](wikipedia://en/23542) $\mathbb{P}$ of a random variable $(T,(L_i)_{1\leq i\leq 2k-1})$ on this space by:

$$\mathbb P(T=\tau \,, \, L_i\in [\ell_i, \ell_i + d\ell_i], \forall 1 \leq i \leq 2k-1)= s \exp(-s^2/2)\, d\ell_1 \ldots d\ell_{2k-1}$$

where $\textstyle s = \sum \ell_i$.

In other words, $\mathbb P$ depends not on the shape of the tree but rather on the total sum of all the edge lengths.

::: {#o12 .definition cue=math-theorem name="Definition"}

**Definition.**

Let $X$ be a random metric space with the tree property, meaning there exists a unique path between two points of $X$. Equip $X$ with a probability measure $\mu$. Suppose the sub-tree of $X$ generated by $k$ points, chosen randomly under $\mu$, has law $\mathbb P$. Then $X$ is called a **Brownian tree**.

:::

In other words, the Brownian tree is defined from the laws of all the finite sub-trees one can generate from it.

:::

### Continuous tree {#Continuous_tree}

The Brownian tree is a [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) defined from a [Brownian excursion](wikipedia://en/27097375) (see characterisation 4 in [Real tree](wikipedia://en/371914)).

Let $e=(e(x),0\leq x\leq 1)$be a Brownian excursion. Define a [pseudometric](wikipedia://en/20018) $d$ on $[0,1]$ with

$d(x, y) := e(x)+e(y)-2 \min\big\{e(z)\, ; z\in[x,y]\big\},$ for any $x,y\in [0,1]$

We then define an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259), noted $\sim_e$ on $[0,1]$ which relates all points $x,y$ such that $d(x,y)=0$ .

$$x\sim_e y \Leftrightarrow d(x,y)=0.$$

$d$ is then a distance on the [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) $[0,1]\,/\!\sim_e$.

::: {#o14 .definition cue=math-theorem name="Definition"}

**Definition.**

The random metric space $\big([0,1]\,/\!\sim_e,\, d\big)$ is called a **Brownian tree**.

:::

It is customary to consider the excursion $e/2$ rather than $e$.

### Poisson line-breaking construction {#Poisson_line-breaking_construction}

This is also called *stick-breaking construction*.

Consider a non-homogeneous [Poisson point process](wikipedia://en/41597450) $N$ with intensity $r(t)=t$. In other words, for any $t>0$, $N_t$ is a [Poisson variable](wikipedia://en/23009144) with parameter $t^2$. Let $C_1, C_2, \ldots$ be the points of $N$. Then the lengths of the intervals $[C_i,C_{i+1}]$ are [exponential variables](wikipedia://en/45906) with decreasing means. We then make the following construction:

- (*initialisation*) The first step is to pick a random point $u$ [uniformly](wikipedia://en/1699223) on the interval $[0,C_1]$. Then we glue the segment $]C_1,C_2]$ to $u$ (mathematically speaking, we define a new distance). We obtain a tree $T_1$ with a root (the point 0), two leaves ($C_1$ and $C_2$), as well as one binary branching point (the point $u$).
- (*iteration*) At step $k$, the segment $]C_k,C_{k+1}]$ is similarly glued to the tree $T_{k-1}$, on a uniformly random point of $T_{k-1}$.

::: {#o16 .definition cue=math-theorem name="Definition"}

**Definition.**

The [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) $\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 1}T_k}$, equipped with the distance previously built, is called a **Brownian tree**.

:::

This algorithm may be used to simulate numerically Brownian trees.

### Limit of Galton-Watson trees {#Limit_of_Galton-Watson_trees}

Consider a [Galton-Watson tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton-Watson_tree) whose reproduction law has finite non-zero variance, conditioned to have $n$ nodes. Let $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}G_n$ be this tree, with the edge lengths divided by $\sqrt{n}$. In other words, each edge has length $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$. The construction can be formalized by considering the Galton-Watson tree as a metric space or by using renormalized [contour processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton-Watson_tree).

::: {#o18 .definition cue=math-theorem name="Definition and Theorem"}

**Definition and Theorem.**

$\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}G_n$ converges in distribution to a random real tree, which we call a **Brownian tree**.

:::

Here, the limit used is the [convergence in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) in the [Skorokhod space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skorokhod_space) (if we consider the contour processes) or the convergence in distribution defined from the [Hausdorff distance](wikipedia://en/429296) (if we consider the metric spaces).

:::
