# Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) the **Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations**, or **KZ equations**, are linear differential equations satisfied by the [correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29) (on the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799)) of [two-dimensional conformal field theories](wikipedia://en/52183913) associated with an [affine Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1350865) at a fixed level. They form a system of complex [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) with [regular singular points](wikipedia://en/3166061) satisfied by the *N*-point functions of [affine primary fields](wikipedia://en/1593924#Fields_and_correlation_functions) and can be derived using either the formalism of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) or that of [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra).

:::

The structure of the genus-zero part of the conformal field theory is encoded in the [monodromy](wikipedia://en/251250) properties of these equations. In particular, the braiding and fusion of the primary fields (or their associated representations) can be deduced from the properties of the four-point functions, for which the equations reduce to a single matrix-valued first-order complex [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753) of Fuchsian type.

Originally the Russian physicists [Vadim Knizhnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Knizhnik) and [Alexander Zamolodchikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Zamolodchikov) derived the equations for the [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29) [Wess–Zumino–Witten model](wikipedia://en/1593924) using the classical formulas of [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) for the [connection coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connection_coefficients) of the [hypergeometric differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_differential_equation).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_k$ denote the affine Lie algebra with level $k$ and dual [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) $h$. Let $v$ be a vector from a zero mode representation of $\hat{\mathfrak{g}}_k$ and $\Phi(v,z)$ the primary field associated with it. Let $t^a$ be a basis of the underlying [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak{g}$, $t^a_i$ their representation on the primary field $\Phi(v_i,z)$ and $\eta$ the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386). Then for $i,j=1,2,\ldots,N$ the **Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations** read

$$\left( (k+h)\partial_{z_i} + \sum_{j \neq i} \frac{\sum_{a,b} \eta_{ab} t^a_i \otimes t^b_j}{z_i-z_j} \right) \left \langle \Phi(v_N,z_N)\dots\Phi(v_1,z_1) \right \rangle = 0.$$

:::

## Informal derivation {#Informal_derivation}

The Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations result from the [Sugawara construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wess%E2%80%93Zumino%E2%80%93Witten_model#Sugawara_construction) of the Virasoro algebra from the affine Lie algebra. More specifically, they result from applying the identity

$$L_{-1} = \frac{1}{2(k+h)} \sum_{m \in \mathbf{Z}} \sum_{a, b} \eta_{ab}J^a_{-m}J^b_{m-1}$$

to the affine primary field $\Phi(v_i,z_i)$ in a correlation function of affine primary fields. In this context, only the terms $k=0,1$ are non-vanishing. The action of $J^a_{-1}$ can then be rewritten using global [Ward identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_identities),

$$\left( \left(J^a_{-1}\right)_i + \sum_{j\neq i} \frac{t^a_j}{z_i-z_j} \right) \left\langle \Phi(v_N,z_N)\dots \Phi(v_1,z_1)\right\rangle = 0,$$

and $L_{-1}$ can be identified with the [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) translation operator $\frac{\partial}{\partial z}$.

## Mathematical formulation {#Mathematical_formulation}

Since the treatment in (Tsuchiya Kanie 1988), the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equation has been formulated mathematically in the language of [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra) due to (Borcherds 1986) and (Frenkel Lepowsky Meurman 1988). This approach was popularized amongst theoretical physicists by (Goddard 1989) and amongst mathematicians by (Kac 1997).

The vacuum representation *H*<sub>0</sub> of an [affine Kac–Moody algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_Kac–Moody_algebra) at a fixed level can be encoded in a [vertex algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebra). The derivation $d$ acts as the energy operator *L*<sub>0</sub> on *H*<sub>0</sub>, which can be written as a direct sum of the non-negative integer eigenspaces of *L*<sub>0</sub>, the zero energy space being generated by the vacuum vector Ω. The eigenvalue of an eigenvector of *L*<sub>0</sub> is called its energy. For every state *a* in *L* there is a vertex operator *V*(*a*,*z*) which creates *a* from the vacuum vector Ω, in the sense that

$$V(a,0)\Omega = a.$$

The vertex operators of energy 1 correspond to the generators of the affine algebra

$$X(z)=\sum X(n) z^{-n-1}$$

where *X* ranges over the elements of the underlying finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$.

There is an energy 2 eigenvector $L_{-2}\Omega$ which give the generators *L*<sub>*n*</sub> of the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029) associated to the Kac–Moody algebra by the *Segal–[Sugawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirotaka_Sugawara) construction*

$$T(z) = \sum L_n z^{-n-2}.$$

If *a* has energy $\alpha$, then the corresponding vertex operator has the form

$$V(a,z) = \sum V(a,n)z^{-n-\alpha}.$$

The vertex operators satisfy

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dz} V(a,z) &= \left [L_{-1},V(a,z) \right ]= V \left (L_{-1}a,z \right ) \\ \left [L_0,V(a,z) \right ] &= \left (z^{-1} \frac{d}{dz} + \alpha \right )V(a,z) \end{aligned}$$

as well as the locality and associativity relations

$$V(a,z)V(b,w) = V(b,w) V(a,z) = V(V(a,z-w)b,w).$$

These last two relations are understood as analytic continuations: the inner products with finite energy vectors of the three expressions define the same polynomials in $z^{\pm1}, w^{\pm1}$ and $(z - w)^{-1}$ in the domains |*z*| \< |*w*|, |*z*| > |*w*| and |*z* – *w*| \< |*w*|. All the structural relations of the Kac–Moody and Virasoro algebra can be recovered from these relations, including the Segal–Sugawara construction.

Every other integral representation *H<sub>i</sub>* at the same level becomes a module for the vertex algebra, in the sense that for each *a* there is a vertex operator $V_{i}(a, z)$ on *H<sub>i</sub>* such that

$$V_i(a,z)V_i(b,w) = V_i(b,w) V_i(a,z)=V_i(V(a,z-w)b,w).$$

The most general vertex operators at a given level are [intertwining operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertwining_operator) $\Phi(v, z)$ between representations *H<sub>i</sub>* and *H*<sub>*j*</sub> where *v* lies in *H*<sub>*k*</sub>. These operators can also be written as

$$\Phi(v,z)=\sum \Phi(v,n) z^{-n-\delta}$$

but δ can now be [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). Again these intertwining operators are characterized by properties

$$V_j(a,z) \Phi(v,w)= \Phi(v,w) V_i(a,w) = \Phi \left (V_k(a,z-w)v,w \right)$$

and relations with *L*<sub>0</sub> and *L*<sub>−1</sub> similar to those above.

When *v* is in the lowest energy subspace for *L*<sub>0</sub> on *H<sub>k</sub>*, an irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{g}$, the operator $\Phi(v, w)$ is called a [primary field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_field) of charge *k*.

Given a chain of *n* primary fields starting and ending at *H*<sub>0</sub>, their correlation or *n*-point function is defined by

$$\left \langle \Phi(v_1,z_1) \Phi(v_2,z_2) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle = \left(\Phi \left (v_1,z_1 \right ) \Phi \left (v_2,z_2 \right ) \cdots \Phi \left (v_n,z_n \right ) \Omega, \Omega \right ).$$

In the physics literature the *v*<sub>*i*</sub> are often suppressed and the primary field written Φ<sub>*i*</sub>(*z<sub>i</sub>*), with the understanding that it is labelled by the corresponding irreducible representation of $\mathfrak{g}$.

### Vertex algebra derivation {#Vertex_algebra_derivation}

If (*X*<sub>*s*</sub>) is an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) of $\mathfrak{g}$ for the Killing form, the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations may be deduced by integrating the correlation function

$$\sum_s \left \langle X_s(w)X_s(z)\Phi(v_1,z_1) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle (w-z)^{-1}$$

first in the *w* variable around a small circle centred at *z*; by Cauchy's theorem the result can be expressed as sum of integrals around *n* small circles centred at the *z*<sub>*j*</sub>'s:

$${1\over 2}(k+h) \left \langle T(z)\Phi(v_1,z_1)\cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle = - \sum_{j,s} \left \langle X_s(z)\Phi(v_1,z_1) \cdots \Phi(X_s v_j,z_j) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle (z-z_j)^{-1}.$$

Integrating both sides in the *z* variable about a small circle centred on *z<sub>i</sub>* yields the *i*<sup>th</sup> Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equation.

### Lie algebra derivation {#Lie_algebra_derivation}

It is also possible to deduce the Knizhnik–Zamodchikov equations without explicit use of vertex algebras. The term$\Phi(v_{i}, z_{i})$ may be replaced in the correlation function by its commutator with *L<sub>r</sub>* where *r* = 0, ±1. The result can be expressed in terms of the derivative with respect to *z<sub>i</sub>*. On the other hand, *L<sub>r</sub>* is also given by the Segal–Sugawara formula:

$$\begin{aligned} L_0 &= (k+h)^{-1}\sum_s\left[ \frac{1}{2}X_s(0)^2 + \sum_{m>0} X_s(-m)X_s(m)\right] \\ L_{\pm 1 } &=(k+h)^{-1} \sum_s\sum_{ m\ge 0} X_s(-m\pm 1)X_s(m) \end{aligned}$$

After substituting these formulas for *L<sub>r</sub>*, the resulting expressions can be simplified using the commutator formulas

$$[X(m),\Phi(a,n)]= \Phi(Xa,m+n).$$

### Original derivation {#Original_derivation}

The original proof of (Knizhnik Zamolodchikov 1984), reproduced in (Tsuchiya Kanie 1988), uses a combination of both of the above methods. First note that for *X* in $\mathfrak{g}$

$$\left \langle X(z)\Phi(v_1,z_1) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle = \sum_j \left \langle \Phi(v_1,z_1)\cdots \Phi(Xv_j,z_j) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n) \right \rangle (z-z_j)^{-1}.$$

Hence

$$\sum_s \langle X_s(z)\Phi(z_1,v_1) \cdots \Phi(X_sv_i,z_i) \cdots \Phi(v_n,z_n)\rangle = \sum_j\sum_s \langle\cdots \Phi(X_s v_j, z_j) \cdots \Phi(X_s v_i,z_i) \cdots\rangle (z-z_j)^{-1}.$$

On the other hand,

$$\sum_s X_s(z)\Phi \left (X_sv_i,z_i \right ) = (z-z_i)^{-1} \Phi \left (\sum_s X_s^2v_i,z_i \right ) + (k+g){\partial\over \partial z_i} \Phi(v_i,z_i) +O(z-z_i)$$

so that

$$(k+g)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_i} \Phi(v_i,z_i) = \lim_{z\to z_i} \left[\sum_s X_s(z)\Phi \left (X_sv_i,z_i \right ) -(z-z_i)^{-1}\Phi \left (\sum_s X_s^2 v_i,z_i \right )\right].$$

The result follows by using this limit in the previous equality.

## Monodromy representation of KZ equation {#Monodromy_representation_of_KZ_equation}

In [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913) along the above definition the *n*-point correlation function of the primary field satisfies KZ equation. In particular, for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and non negative integers *k* there are $k + 1$ primary fields $\Phi_j(z_j)$ 's corresponding to [spin <sub>j</sub> representation](wikipedia://en/10160091) ($j = 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2,\ldots , k/2$). The correlation function $\Psi(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ of the primary fields $\Phi_j(z_j)$ 's for the representation $(\rho,V_i)$ takes values in the tensor product $V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_n$ and its KZ equation is

$$(k+2)\frac{\partial}{\partial z_i}\Psi=\sum_{i,j\ne i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_i-z_j}\Psi,$$

where $\Omega_{ij}=\sum_a\rho_i(J^a)\otimes\rho_j(J_a)$ as the above informal derivation.

This *n*-point correlation function can be analytically continued as multi-valued [holomorphic function](wikipedia://en/14110) to the domain $X_n \subset \mathbb{C}^n$ with $z_i\ne z_j$ for $i\ne j$. Due to this [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842), the [holonomy](wikipedia://en/644921) of the KZ equation can be described by the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) $B_n$ introduced by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351).[^o17] In general, A complex semi-simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ and its representations $(\rho,V_i)$ give the [linear representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation) of braid group

$$\theta \colon B_n \rightarrow V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_n$$

as the holonomy of KZ equation. Oppositely, a KZ equation gives the linear representation of braid groups as its holonomy.

The action on $V_1\otimes\dots\otimes V_n$ by the analytic continuation of KZ equation is called **monodromy representation of KZ equation**. In particular, if all $V_i$ 's have spin <sub>1/2</sub> representation then the linear representation obtained from KZ equation agrees with the representation constructed from [operator algebra theory](wikipedia://en/455987) by [Vaughan Jones](wikipedia://en/1227628). It is known that the monodromy representation of KZ equation with a general semi-simple Lie algebra agrees with the linear representation of braid group given by [R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996) of the corresponding [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678).

## KZ-BPZ relation {#KZ-BPZ_relation}

In the case when the underlying Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2)$, the KZ equations are mapped to [BPZ equations](wikipedia://en/52183913#BPZ_equations) by [Sklyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Sklyanin)'s separation of variables for the $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ [Gaudin model](wikipedia://en/71851317).[^o19]
