# Small Veblen ordinal

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Small Veblen ordinal"}

In mathematics, the **small Veblen ordinal** is a certain [large countable ordinal](wikipedia://en/4376814), named after [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen). It is occasionally called the **Ackermann ordinal**, though the [Ackermann ordinal](wikipedia://en/16137162) described by (Ackermann 1951) is somewhat smaller than the small Veblen ordinal.

:::

There is no standard notation for ordinals beyond the [Feferman–Schütte ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084455) $\Gamma_0$. Most systems of notation use symbols such as $\psi(\alpha)$, $\theta(\alpha)$, $\psi_\alpha(\beta)$, some of which are modifications of the [Veblen functions](wikipedia://en/16124925) to produce countable ordinals even for uncountable arguments, and some of which are "[collapsing functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapsing_function)".

The small Veblen ordinal $\theta_{\Omega^\omega}(0)$ or $\psi(\Omega^{\Omega^\omega})$ is the limit of ordinals that can be described using a version of [Veblen functions](wikipedia://en/16124925) with finitely many arguments. It is the ordinal that measures the strength of [Kruskal's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruskal's_theorem). It is also the ordinal type of a certain ordering of [rooted trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooted_tree) (Jervell 2005).

The small Veblen ordinal (SVO) is $\sup \{ \omega=\varphi (1), \, \varepsilon_0=\varphi (1,0), \, \Gamma_0=\varphi (1,0,0), \, \varphi (1,0,0,0), \, \varphi (1,0,0,0,0), \, \varphi (1,0,0,0,0,0), \ldots \}$ using the finitary Veblen function. SVO is the least ordinal which is not the sum of a finite number of values of the finitary Veblen function when applied to functions of finite support from ω to SVO. These functions can be encoded as ordinals less than $\operatorname{SVO}^{\omega}$ ([ordinal exponentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_exponentiation)) and $\Omega$ can be used as a place holder for SVO. Notice that $\operatorname{SVO} = \omega^\operatorname{SVO} = \epsilon_\operatorname{SVO} = \Gamma_\operatorname{SVO}$.
