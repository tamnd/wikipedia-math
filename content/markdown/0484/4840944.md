# Presheaf (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Presheaf"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **presheaf** on a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $C$ is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $F\colon C^\mathrm{op}\to\mathbf{Set}$. If $C$ is the [poset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poset) of [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) in a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450), interpreted as a category, then one recovers the usual notion of [presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf_%28mathematics%29) on a topological space.

:::

A [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) of presheaves is defined to be a [natural transformation](wikipedia://en/45022) of functors. This makes the collection of all presheaves on $C$ into a category, and is an example of a [functor category](wikipedia://en/407039). It is often written as $\widehat{C} = \mathbf{Set}^{C^\mathrm{op}}$ and it is called the **category of presheaves** on $C$. A functor into $\widehat{C}$ is sometimes called a [profunctor](wikipedia://en/4403842).

A presheaf that is [naturally isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_isomorphism) to the contravariant [hom-functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hom-functor) Hom(–, *A*) for some [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) *A* of **C** is called a [representable presheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representable_presheaf).

Some authors refer to a functor $F\colon C^\mathrm{op}\to\mathbf{V}$ as a **$\mathbf{V}$-valued presheaf**.

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- A [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784) is a **Set**-valued presheaf on the [simplex category](wikipedia://en/1028841) $C=\Delta$.
- A [directed multigraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_multigraph) is a presheaf on the category with two objects and two parallel morphisms between them i.e. $C = (E \overset{s}{\underset{t}{\longrightarrow}} V)$.
- An [arrow category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_category) is a presheaf on the category with two objects and one morphism between them. i.e. $C = (E \overset{f}{\longrightarrow} V)$.
- A [right group action](wikipedia://en/12781#Right_group_action) is a presheaf on the category created from a group $G$, i.e. a category with one object and invertible morphisms.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- When $C$ is a [small category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_category), the functor category $\widehat{C}=\mathbf{Set}^{C^\mathrm{op}}$ is [cartesian closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_closed).
- The poset of [subobjects](wikipedia://en/540476) of $P$ form a [Heyting algebra](wikipedia://en/309343), whenever $P$ is an object of $\widehat{C}=\mathbf{Set}^{C^\mathrm{op}}$ for small $C$.
- For any morphism $f:X\to Y$ of $\widehat{C}$, the pullback functor of subobjects $f^*:\mathrm{Sub}_{\widehat{C}}(Y)\to\mathrm{Sub}_{\widehat{C}}(X)$ has a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint), denoted $\forall_f$, and a left adjoint, $\exists_f$. These are the [universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_quantifier#As_adjoint) and existential quantifiers.
- A locally small category $C$ embeds [fully and faithfully](wikipedia://en/876534) into the category $\widehat{C}$ of set-valued presheaves via the [Yoneda embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_embedding) which to every object $A$ of $C$ associates the [hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037) $C(-,A)$.
- The category $\widehat{C}$ admits small [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and small [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit).[^o7] See [limit and colimit of presheaves](wikipedia://en/54494563) for further discussion.
- The [density theorem](wikipedia://en/54487799) states that every presheaf is a colimit of representable presheaves; in fact, $\widehat{C}$ is the [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) completion of $C$ (see #Universal property below.)

:::

## Universal property {#Universal_property}

The construction $C \mapsto \widehat{C} = \mathbf{Fct}(C^{\text{op}}, \mathbf{Set})$ is called the **colimit completion** of *C* because of the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245):

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=math-theorem name="Proposition"}

**Proposition[^o9].**

Let *C*, *D* be categories and assume *D* admits small colimits. Then each functor $\eta: C \to D$ factorizes as

$C \overset{y}\longrightarrow \widehat{C} \overset{\widetilde{\eta}}\longrightarrow D$ where *y* is the Yoneda embedding and $\widetilde{\eta}: \widehat{C} \to D$ is a, unique up to isomorphism, colimit-preserving functor called the **[Yoneda extension](wikipedia://en/34359#Yoneda_extension)** of $\eta$.

:::

*Proof*: Given a presheaf *F*, by the [density theorem](wikipedia://en/54487799), we can write $F =\varinjlim y U_i$ where $U_i$ are objects in *C*. Then let $\widetilde{\eta} F = \varinjlim \eta U_i,$ which exists by assumption. Since $\varinjlim -$ is functorial, this determines the functor $\widetilde{\eta}: \widehat{C} \to D$. Succinctly, $\widetilde{\eta}$ is the left [Kan extension](wikipedia://en/2502518) of $\eta$ along *y*; hence, the name "Yoneda extension". To see $\widetilde{\eta}$ commutes with small colimits, we show $\widetilde{\eta}$ is a left-adjoint (to some functor). Define $\mathcal{H}om(\eta, -): D \to \widehat{C}$ to be the functor given by: for each object *M* in *D* and each object *U* in *C*,

$$\mathcal{H}om(\eta, M)(U) = \operatorname{Hom}_D(\eta U, M).$$

Then, for each object *M* in *D*, since $\mathcal{H}om(\eta, M)(U_i) = \operatorname{Hom}(y U_i, \mathcal{H}om(\eta, M))$ by the Yoneda lemma, we have:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Hom}_D(\widetilde{\eta} F, M) &= \operatorname{Hom}_D(\varinjlim \eta U_i, M) = \varprojlim \operatorname{Hom}_D(\eta U_i, M) = \varprojlim \mathcal{H}om(\eta, M)(U_i) \\ &= \operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{C}}(F, \mathcal{H}om(\eta, M)), \end{aligned}$$

which is to say $\widetilde{\eta}$ is a left-adjoint to $\mathcal{H}om(\eta, -)$. $\square$

The proposition yields several corollaries. For example, the proposition implies that the construction $C \mapsto \widehat{C}$ is functorial: i.e., each functor $C \to D$ determines the functor $\widehat{C} \to \widehat{D}$.

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variants"}

## Variants {#Variants}

A **presheaf of spaces** on an ∞-category *C* is a contravariant functor from *C* to the [∞-category of spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category_of_spaces) (for example, the nerve of the category of [CW-complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex).)[^o13] It is an [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category) version of a presheaf of sets, as a "set" is replaced by a "space". The notion is used, among other things, in the ∞-category formulation of [Yoneda's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda's_lemma) that says: $C \to \widehat{C}$ is [fully faithful](wikipedia://en/876534) (here *C* can be just a [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784).)[^o14]

A **copresheaf** of a category *C* is a presheaf of *C<sup>op</sup>*. In other words, it is a covariant functor from *C* to *Set*.[^o15]
