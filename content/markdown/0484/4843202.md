# Elementary abelian group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elementary abelian group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), an **elementary abelian group** is an [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) in which all elements other than the identity have the same [order](wikipedia://en/493527). This common order must be a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666), and the elementary abelian groups in which the common order is *p* are a particular kind of [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664).[^o2][^o3] A group for which *p* = 2 (that is, an elementary abelian 2-group) is sometimes called a **Boolean group**.[^o4]

:::

Every elementary abelian *p*-group is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [prime field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_field) with *p* elements, and conversely every such vector space is an elementary abelian group. By the [classification of finitely generated abelian groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_finitely_generated_abelian_groups), or by the fact that every vector space has a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28vector_space%29), every finite elementary abelian group must be of the form (**Z**/*p***Z**)<sup>*n*</sup> for *n* a non-negative integer (sometimes called the group's *rank*). Here, **Z**/*p***Z** denotes the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order *p* (or equivalently the integers [mod](wikipedia://en/20087) *p*), and the superscript notation means the *n*-fold [direct product of groups](wikipedia://en/3706015).[^o3]

In general, a (possibly infinite) **elementary abelian *p*-group** is a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687#Direct_sum_of_abelian_groups) of cyclic groups of order *p*.[^o5] (Note that in the finite case the direct product and direct sum coincide, but this is not so in the infinite case.)

## Examples and properties {#Examples_and_properties}

- The elementary abelian group (**Z**/2**Z**)<sup>2</sup> has four elements: {(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1)} . Addition is performed componentwise, taking the result modulo 2. For instance, (1,0) + (1,1) = (0,1). This is in fact the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928).
- In the group generated by the [symmetric difference](wikipedia://en/262288) on a (not necessarily finite) set, every element has order 2. Any such group is necessarily abelian because, since every element is its own inverse, *xy* = (*xy*)<sup>−1</sup> = *y*<sup>−1</sup>*x*<sup>−1</sup> = *yx*. Such a group (also called a Boolean group), generalizes the Klein four-group example to an arbitrary number of components.
- (**Z**/*p***Z**)<sup>*n*</sup> is generated by *n* elements, and *n* is the least possible number of generators. In particular, the set {*e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub>} , where *e*<sub>*i*</sub> has a 1 in the *i*th component and 0 elsewhere, is a minimal generating set.
- Every finite elementary abelian group has a fairly simple [finite presentation](wikipedia://en/99494):

$$(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^n \cong \langle e_1,\ldots,e_n\mid e_i^p = 1,\ e_i e_j = e_j e_i \rangle$$

## Vector space structure {#Vector_space_structure}

Suppose *V* $\cong$ (**Z**/*p***Z**)<sup>*n*</sup> is a finite elementary abelian group. Since **Z**/*p***Z** $\cong$ **F**<sub>*p*</sub>, the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) of *p* elements, we have *V* $\cong$ (**Z**/*p***Z**)<sup>*n*</sup> $\cong$ **F**<sub>*p*</sub><sup>*n*</sup>, hence *V* can be considered as an *n*-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the field **F**<sub>*p*</sub>. Note that an elementary abelian group does not in general have a distinguished basis: choice of isomorphism *V* $\overset{\cong}{\to}$ (**Z**/*p***Z**)<sup>*n*</sup> corresponds to a choice of basis.

To the observant reader, it may appear that **F**<sub>*p*</sub><sup>*n*</sup> has more structure than the group *V*, in particular that it has scalar multiplication in addition to (vector/group) addition. However, *V* as an abelian group has a unique *Z*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) structure where the action of *Z* corresponds to repeated addition, and this *Z*-module structure is consistent with the **F**<sub>*p*</sub> scalar multiplication. That is, *c*⋅*g* = *g* + *g* + ... + *g* (*c* times) where *c* in **F**<sub>*p*</sub> (considered as an integer with 0 ≤ *c* \< *p*) gives *V* a natural **F**<sub>*p*</sub>-module structure.

## Automorphism group {#Automorphism_group}

As a finite-dimensional vector space *V* has a basis {*e*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *e*<sub>*n*</sub>} as described in the examples, if we take {*v*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *v*<sub>*n*</sub>} to be any *n* elements of *V*, then by [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) we have that the mapping *T*(*e*<sub>*i*</sub>) = *v*<sub>*i*</sub> extends uniquely to a linear transformation of *V*. Each such *T* can be considered as a group homomorphism from *V* to *V* (an [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569)) and likewise any endomorphism of *V* can be considered as a linear transformation of *V* as a vector space.

If we restrict our attention to [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of *V* we have Aut(*V*) = { *T* : *V* → *V* | ker *T* = 0 } = GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*p*</sub>), the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) of *n* × *n* invertible matrices on **F**<sub>*p*</sub>.

The automorphism group GL(*V*) = GL<sub>*n*</sub>(**F**<sub>*p*</sub>) acts [transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#transitive) on *V* \\ {0} (as is true for any vector space). This in fact characterizes elementary abelian groups among all finite groups: if *G* is a finite group with identity *e* such that Aut(*G*) acts transitively on *G* \\ {*e*}, then *G* is elementary abelian. (Proof: if Aut(*G*) acts transitively on *G* \\ {*e*}, then all nonidentity elements of *G* have the same (necessarily prime) order. Then *G* is a *p*-group. It follows that *G* has a nontrivial [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_center), which is necessarily invariant under all automorphisms, and thus equals all of *G*.)

## A generalisation to higher orders {#A_generalisation_to_higher_orders}

It can also be of interest to go beyond prime order components to prime-power order. Consider an elementary abelian group *G* to be of *type* (*p*,*p*,...,*p*) for some prime *p*. A *homocyclic group*[^o10] (of rank *n*) is an abelian group of type (*m*,*m*,...,*m*) i.e. the direct product of *n* isomorphic cyclic groups of order *m*, of which groups of type (*p<sup>k</sup>*,*p<sup>k</sup>*,...,*p<sup>k</sup>*) are a special case.

## Related groups {#Related_groups}

The [extra special groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_special_group) are extensions of elementary abelian groups by a cyclic group of order *p,* and are analogous to the [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518).
