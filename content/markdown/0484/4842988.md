# Central product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Central product"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the field of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **central product** is one way of producing a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) from two smaller groups. The central product is similar to the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015), but in the central product two [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) [central](wikipedia://en/7125) [subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroups) of the smaller groups are merged into a single central subgroup of the product. Central products are an important construction and can be used for instance to classify [extraspecial groups](wikipedia://en/6990619).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

There are several related but distinct notions of central product. Similarly to the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015), there are both internal and external characterizations, and additionally there are variations on how strictly the intersection of the factors is controlled.

A group *G* is an **internal central product** of two subgroups *H*, *K* if

1. *G* is generated by *H* and *K*.
1. Every element of *H* commutes with every element of *K*. (Gorenstein 1980, 29)

Sometimes the stricter requirement that $H\cap K$ is exactly equal to the center is imposed, as in (Leedham-Green McKay 2002, 32). The subgroups *H* and *K* are then called central factors of *G*.

The **external central product** is constructed from two groups *H* and *K*, two subgroups $H_1 \le Z(H)$ and $K_1 \le Z(K)$, and a group isomorphism $\theta\colon H_1 \to K_1$. The external central product is the quotient of the direct product $H\times K$ by the normal subgroup

$$N = \{ (h,k) : h\in H_1, k\in K_1, \text{ and } \theta(h)\cdot k = 1 \},$$

(Gorenstein 1980, 29). Sometimes the stricter requirement that *H*<sub>1</sub> = Z(*H*) and *K*<sub>1</sub> = Z(*K*) is imposed, as in (Leedham-Green McKay 2002, 32).

An internal central product is isomorphic to an external central product with *H*<sub>1</sub> = *K*<sub>1</sub> = *H* ∩ *K* and *θ* the identity. An external central product is an internal central product of the images of *H* × 1 and 1 × *K* in the quotient group $(H\times K) / N$. This is shown for each definition in (Gorenstein 1980, 29) and (Leedham-Green McKay 2002, 32–33).

Note that the external central product is not in general determined by its factors *H* and *K* alone. The isomorphism type of the central product will depend on the isomorphism *θ*. It is however well defined in some notable situations, for example when *H* and *K* are both finite [extra special groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_special_group) and $H_1 = Z(H)$ and $K_1 = Z(K)$.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [Pauli group](wikipedia://en/5045613) is the central product of the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_4$ and the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) $D_4$.
- Every [extra special group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_special_group) is a central product of extra special groups of order *p*<sup>3</sup>.
- The layer of a finite group, that is, the subgroup generated by all [subnormal](wikipedia://en/2774415) [quasisimple subgroups](wikipedia://en/1642729), is a central product of quasisimple groups in the sense of Gorenstein.

:::

::: {#o13 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The [representation theory](wikipedia://en/874400) of central products is very similar to the representation theory of direct products, and so is well understood, (Gorenstein 1980, Ch. 3.7).

Central products occur in many structural lemmas, such as (Gorenstein 1980, 350) which is used in [George Glauberman](wikipedia://en/9744285)'s result that finite groups admitting a [Klein four group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_four_group) of fixed-point-free automorphisms are [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012).

In certain context of a tensor product of Lie modules (and other related structures), the automorphism group contains a central product of the automorphism groups of each factor (Aranda-Orna 2022, \\S 4).
