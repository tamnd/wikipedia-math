# Jean-Marc Deshouillers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Marc Deshouillers"}

**Jean-Marc Deshouillers** (born on September 12, 1946[^o3] in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989)) is a French mathematician, specializing in [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). He is a professor at the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Deshouillers attended the Paris [École Polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique),[^o5][^o6] graduating with an engineer diploma in 1968.

He received his PhD in 1972 at the [University Paris VI *Pierre et Marie Curie*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-and-Marie-Curie_University).

In the seventies, he was assistant professor in mathematics at the École Polytechnique, which moved from Paris to [Palaiseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaiseau).

Deshouillers is a professor at the University of Bordeaux. In 2009 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In 1985 he showed with [Ramachandran Balasubramanian](wikipedia://en/18734196) and François Dress that, in the case of the fourth powers of [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569), the least number of fourth powers that is necessary to express any positive integer as a sum of fourth powers is 19.[^o8]

With [Henryk Iwaniec](wikipedia://en/9338319), he improved the Kuznetsov trace formula.[^o9] In 1997, with Effinger and Herman te Riele, he proved the [ternary Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ternary_Goldbach_conjecture) (every odd number greater than 5 is a sum of three [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666)) under the [Generalized Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Riemann_Hypothesis).[^o10] Together with [François Dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/François_Dress) and [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646) he discovered the [DDT theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DDT_theorem).

Among his students was [Gérald Tenenbaum](wikipedia://en/36258646).
