# Completions in category theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Completions in category theory"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of mathematics, there are several ways (**completions**) to enlarge a given category in a way somehow analogous to a [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28metric_space%29) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). These are (ignoring the set-theoretic matters for simplicity):

:::

- **free cocompletion**, **free completion**. These are obtained by freely adding colimits or limits. Explicitly, the free cocompletion of a category *C* is the [Yoneda embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoneda_embedding) of *C* into the category of [presheaves](wikipedia://en/4840944) on *C*.[^o2][^o3] The free completion of *C* is the free cocompletion of the [opposite](wikipedia://en/372399) of *C*.[^o4]
  - [ind-completion](wikipedia://en/61387167). This is obtained by freely adding [filtered colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filtered_colimit).
- **Cauchy completion** of a category *C* is roughly the closure of *C* in some ambient category so that all functors preserve limits.[^o5][^o6] For example, if a metric space is viewed as an enriched category (see [generalized metric space](wikipedia://en/77411704)), then the Cauchy completion of it coincides with the usual completion of the space.
- **Isbell completion** (also called **reflexive completion**), introduced by [Isbell](wikipedia://en/31277386) in 1960,[^o7] is in short the fixed-point category of the [Isbell conjugacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isbell_conjugacy) adjunction.[^o8][^o9] It should not be confused with the [Isbell envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isbell_envelope), which was also introduced by Isbell.
- **[Karoubi envelope](wikipedia://en/933541)** or **idempotent completion** of a category *C* is (roughly) the universal enlargement of *C* so that every idempotent is a split idempotent.[^o10]
- [Exact completion](wikipedia://en/51639135)
