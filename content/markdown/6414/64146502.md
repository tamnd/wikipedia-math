# Rebecca Andridge

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rebecca Andridge"}

**Rebecca Roberts Andridge** is an American statistician. Her statistical research concerns the [imputation](wikipedia://en/1309220) of [missing data](wikipedia://en/7859676) and the statistics of [group-randomized trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_randomised_controlled_trial); she has also performed highly-cited applied statistical work on [omega-3 nutritional supplements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega-3_fatty_acid) and on the health benefits of using [yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga) to lower stress. Andridge is a professor of [biostatistics](wikipedia://en/3878) at the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Andridge majored in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), graduating in 1999. She went to the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) for graduate study, earning a master's degree in 2005 and completing her Ph.D. in biostatistics in 2009. Her dissertation, *Statistical Methods for Missing Data in Complex Sample Surveys*, was supervised by [Roderick J. A. Little](wikipedia://en/64771099).

In 2009, after completing her Ph.D., she joined the Ohio State University College of Public Health as an assistant professor of biostatistics. She was tenured as an associate professor in 2016. At Ohio State, she also holds affiliations with the Institute for Population Research, Food Innovation Center, and Decision Sciences Collaborative.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Andridge was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 2020.
