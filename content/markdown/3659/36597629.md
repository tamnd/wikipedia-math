# Genus of a quadratic form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genus of a quadratic form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **genus** is a classification of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) and [lattices](wikipedia://en/469246) over the ring of integers. An [integral quadratic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_quadratic_form) is a quadratic form on **Z**<sup>*n*</sup>, or equivalently a free **Z**-module of finite rank. Two such forms are in the same *genus* if they are equivalent over the local rings **Z**<sub>*p*</sub> for each prime *p* and also equivalent over **R**.

:::

Equivalent forms are in the same genus, but the converse does not hold. For example, *x*<sup>2</sup> + 82*y*<sup>2</sup> and 2*x*<sup>2</sup> + 41*y*<sup>2</sup> are in the same genus but not equivalent over **Z**. Forms in the same genus have equal [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Determinant_of_a_quadratic_form) and hence there are only finitely many equivalence classes in a genus.

The [Smith–Minkowski–Siegel mass formula](wikipedia://en/13362584) gives the *weight* or *mass* of the quadratic forms in a genus, the count of equivalence classes weighted by the reciprocals of the orders of their automorphism groups.

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Generic character (mathematics)"}

## Binary quadratic forms {#Binary_quadratic_forms}

For [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129) there is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) structure on the set *C* of [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of forms with given [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_of_a_quadratic_form). The genera are defined by the *generic characters*. The principal genus, the genus containing the principal form, is precisely the subgroup *C*<sup>2</sup> and the genera are the cosets of *C*<sup>2</sup>: so in this case all genera contain the same number of classes of forms.

:::
