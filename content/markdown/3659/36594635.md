# Karen Parshall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karen Parshall"}

**Karen Hunger Parshall** (born 1955, [Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia); *née* Karen Virginia Hunger) is an American [historian of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian_of_mathematics). She is the Commonwealth Professor of History and Mathematics at the University of Virginia with a joint appointment in the Corcoran Department of History and Department of Mathematics.[^o2] From 2009 to 2012, Parshall was the Associate Dean for the Social Sciences in the [College of Arts in Sciences at UVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia_College_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and from 2016 to 2019 she was the chair of the Corcoran Department of History.[^o2]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Parshall double-majored in French and mathematics at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), where she earned her master's degree in mathematics in 1978. She earned her PhD in 1982 in history from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) under the direction of the historian [Allen G. Debus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_G._Debus) (1926–2009) and the mathematician [Israel Herstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Herstein). The subject of her dissertation was the history of the [theory of algebras](wikipedia://en/19616384), especially the work of [Joseph Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/2969494) (*The contributions of J. H. M. Wedderburn to the theory of algebras, 1900–1910*).[^o2]

From 1982 to 1987, Parshall was an assistant professor at [Sweet Briar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Briar_College) and in 1987/88 at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). Since 1988 she has taught mathematics, the history of mathematics, and the history of science at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), where she became in 1988 an assistant professor, in 1993 an associate professor and in 1999 a professor. She was a visiting professor at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), at the [École des Hautes Etudes en Sciences Sociales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_for_Advanced_Studies_in_the_Social_Sciences) (1985 and 2010) and at the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) in Paris (2016).

## Work {#Work}

Parshall's academic specialty is the development of mathematics in the US in the late 19th century and early 20th century (particularly the Chicago School).[^o5] As one example, she has studied the work of [Leonard Dickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Dickson),[^o6] who was greatly influenced by contact with German mathematicians such as [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) at the time of the [Columbian Exposition of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition).[^o7] She has also focused on the history of algebra. She edited the correspondence of [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) published by [Oxford University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University_Press) and wrote a biography of Sylvester.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In the academic year 1996/97 Parshall was a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship).[^o9] In 1994 she was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) (ICM) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) (*Mathematics in National Contexts (1875–1900): An International Overview*). Since 2002 she has been a corresponding member of the [Académie internationale d’histoire des sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Academy_of_the_History_of_Science) in Paris. From 1996 to 1999, she was editor of the journal *[Historia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/10841658)*. Parshall was in the governing body of the [History of Science Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Science_Society) and from 1998 to 2001 of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS).

In 2012, she became an inaugural fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] She is the 2018 winner of the [Albert Leon Whiteman Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/26015197) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for her outstanding work in the history of mathematics, and in particular, for her work on the evolution of mathematics in the US and on the history of algebra, as well as for her substantial contribution to the international life of her discipline through students, editorial work, and conferences."[^o11]

In 2020, she was elected Fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science), in the Section on Mathematics.[^o12]

Parshall is also a former AMS Council member at large.[^o13]
