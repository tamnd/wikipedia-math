# Spinor genus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spinor genus"}

In mathematics, the **spinor genus** is a classification of [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) and lattices over the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613), introduced by [Martin Eichler](wikipedia://en/4224478). It refines the [genus](wikipedia://en/36597629) but may be coarser than proper equivalence.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

We define two **Z**-lattices *L* and *M* in a [quadratic space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_space) *V* over **Q** to be spinor equivalent if there exists a transformation *g* in the proper orthogonal group *O*<sup>\+</sup>(*V*) and for every prime *p* there exists a local transformation *f*<sub>*p*</sub> of *V*<sub>*p*</sub> of [spinor norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinor_norm) 1 such that *M* = *g* *f*<sub>*p*</sub>*L*<sub>*p*</sub>.

A *spinor genus* is an equivalence class for this [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259). Properly equivalent lattices are in the same spinor genus, and lattices in the same spinor genus are in the same genus. The number of spinor genera in a genus is a power of two, and can be determined effectively.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Results"}

## Results {#Results}

An important result is that for [indefinite forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefinite_form) of dimension at least three, each spinor genus contains exactly one proper equivalence class.

:::
