# Wolfgang Lück

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Lück"}

*Image: Wolfgang Lück.jpg.* Lück at [Oberwolfach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Research_Institute_of_Oberwolfach), 2006

**Wolfgang Lück** (born 19 February 1957 in [Herford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herford)) is a German mathematician who is an internationally recognized expert in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

After receiving his *[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)* from the Ravensberger [Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in [Herford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herford) in 1975, he studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) where he obtained his [Diplom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplom) in 1981 and his [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree) under [Tammo tom Dieck](wikipedia://en/56729165) in 1984. His thesis was entitled *Eine allgemeine Beschreibung für Faserungen auf projektiven Klassengruppen und Whiteheadgruppen*.

From 1982 on he was research assistant and from 1985 on he was assistant in Göttingen. In 1989 Lück received his [Habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation). From 1990 to 1991, he was associate professor at the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky) in [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky). From 1991 until 1996, he was professor at the [University of Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Mainz), and from 1996 until 2010 he taught at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster). Since 2010 he has been a professor at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). In 2003, he was awarded the [Max Planck Research Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Society#Max_Planck_Research_Awards), in 2008 the [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Wilhelm_Leibniz_Prize), and in 2025 the [von Staudt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Karl_Georg_Christian_von_Staudt_Prize).

Lück has made significant contributions in topology; he and his coauthors resolved many cases of the [Farrell-Jones conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrell-Jones_conjecture) and the [Borel conjecture](wikipedia://en/7181855). He has also contributed to the development of the theory of L<sup>2</sup>-invariants (such as L<sup>2</sup>-Betti numbers and [L<sup>2</sup>-cohomology](wikipedia://en/10465223)) of [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), which were originally introduced by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and are defined by means of operator algebras. These invariants have applications in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

In 2009 and 2010 Lück was president of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973), whose vice president he had been since 2006. From 2011 until 2017, he was Director of the [Hausdorff Research Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/23690221) (HIM) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn). In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o3] From October 2019 to September 2022, he was spokesperson for the Hausdorff Center for Mathematics (HCM) in Bonn.

His doctoral students include [Thomas Schick](wikipedia://en/59090155).
