# Benson Mates

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benson Mates"}

**Benson Mates** (May 19, 1919 in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon) – May 14, 2009 in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California)) was an [American philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_philosopher) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), noted for his work in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), the [history of philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_philosophy), and [skepticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skepticism).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Mates studied philosophy and mathematics at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), and the [University of California at Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_at_Berkeley). Some of his teachers included [J. Barkley Rosser](wikipedia://en/679224), [Harold Cherniss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Cherniss), and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920). From 1948 until his retirement in 1989, he was a professor of philosophy at Berkeley. He remained Professor Emeritus of philosophy at University of California at Berkeley until his death.

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Mates's 1948 dissertation, "On the Logic of the Old Stoa", formed the basis for his 1953 book *Stoic Logic*, of which [Peter Geach](wikipedia://en/302884) wrote, "Stoic logic is a difficult subject \[...\] Dr. Mates's monograph is a strenuous and successful effort to overcome these difficulties."[^o4] Mates's 1965 book, *Elementary Logic*, remains a widely used introductory textbook in [symbolic logic](wikipedia://en/19636). His 1986 study of [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) is also highly regarded.[^o5]

In his own philosophical work, Mates defends a stance akin to [Pyrrhonian skepticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhonian_skepticism). He argues that the major problems of philosophy (such as the [liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967), the existence of an external world, and [free will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_will)) are intelligible and non-trivial yet utterly defy solution.[^o6] Unlike the classical Pyrrhonists, however, Mates finds that skeptical arguments lead to unsatisfactory perplexity rather than [ataraxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ataraxia).[^o7]

## Books {#Books}

- *Stoic Logic*, Berkeley: University of California Press, 1953 (second revised edition 1961).
- *Elementary Logic*, 1965, second edition New York: Oxford University Press, 1972.
- *Skeptical Essays*, Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1981.
- *The Philosophy of Leibniz. Metaphysics and Language*, New York: Oxford University Press, 1986.
- *The Skeptic Way: Sextus Empiricus's Outlines of Pyrrhonism*, Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1996.
