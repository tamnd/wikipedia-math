# Sandy Green (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sandy Green"}

**James Alexander** "**Sandy**" **Green** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (26 February 19267 April 2014) was a mathematician and professor at the Mathematics Institute at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick), who worked in the field of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Sandy Green was born in February 1926 in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_state), but moved to [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto) with his emigrant [Scottish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people) parents later that year. The family returned to Britain in May 1935 when his father, Frederick C. Green, took up the [Drapers Professorship of French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drapers_Professor_of_French) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

## Education {#Education}

Green was educated at [the Perse School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perse_School), Cambridge. He won a scholarship to the [University of St Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_St_Andrews) and matriculated aged 16 in 1942. He took an ordinary BSc in 1944, and then, after scientific service in the war, was awarded a BSc Honours in 1947. He gained his PhD at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) in 1951, under the supervision of [Philip Hall](wikipedia://en/1655033) and [David Rees](wikipedia://en/15398568).[^o5][^o6][^o7]

## Career {#Career}

### World War II {#World_War_II}

In the summer of 1944, he was conscripted for national scientific service at the age of eighteen, and was assigned to work at [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037), where he acted as a [human computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_%28occupation%29) carrying out calculations in Hut F, the "Newmanry", a department led by [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867), which used special-purpose [Colossus computers](wikipedia://en/6229) to assist in breaking German naval codes.

### Academic work {#Academic_work}

His first lecturing post (1950) was at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester), where Newman was his Head of department. In 1964 he became a Reader at the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex), and then in 1965 was appointed as a professor at the newly formed Mathematics Institute at [Warwick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_University), where he led the algebra group.[^o10] He spent several periods as a visiting academic in the United States, beginning with a year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) in 1960–61, as well as similar visits to universities in France, Germany and Portugal. After retiring from Warwick he became a member of the faculty and Professor Emeritus at the [Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7905850) of the University of Oxford, in whose meetings he participated actively.[^o20]

## Work in mathematics {#Work_in_mathematics}

According to the Fields Medallist [John G. Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189),[^o13] Green showed, in two important papers,[^o14][^o15] that information about finite groups can be derived from knowledge about indecomposable modules of [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664).

Green found all the [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) of [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) (Green 1955) and invented the Green correspondence in [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685). Both [Green functions](wikipedia://en/10574794) in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/12695) of [groups of Lie type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groups_of_Lie_type) and [Green's relations](wikipedia://en/1192012) in the area of [semigroups](wikipedia://en/27799) are named after him. His final publication (2007) was a revised and augmented edition of his 1980 work, *Polynomial Representations of GL(n)*.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Green met his wife, Margaret Lord, at Bletchley Park, where she worked as a Colossus operator, also in the Newmanry section (Hut F). The couple married in August 1950, and had two daughters and a son.[^o10]

## Honours {#Honours}

He was elected to the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1968 and the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1987[^o18] and was awarded two [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) prizes: the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prizes) in 1984[^o19] and the [de Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) in 2001.[^o18][^o20]
