# Quartic surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quartic surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **quartic surface** is a [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) defined by an equation of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) 4.

:::

More specifically there are two closely related types of quartic surface: affine and projective. An *affine* quartic surface is the solution set of an equation of the form

$$f(x,y,z)=0\$$

where $f$ is a polynomial of degree 4, such as $f(x,y,z) = x^4 + y^4 + xyz + z^2 - 1$. This is a surface in [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834) $\mathbf{A}^{3}$.

On the other hand, a projective quartic surface is a surface in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) $\mathbf{P}^{3}$ of the same form, but now $f$ is a [*homogeneous* polynomial](wikipedia://en/1698977) of 4 variables of degree 4, so for example $f(x,y,z,w) = x^4 + y^4 + xyzw + z^2 w^2 - w^4$.

If the base field is $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ the surface is said to be *[real](wikipedia://en/20646438)* or *[complex](wikipedia://en/5826)* respectively. One must be careful to distinguish between algebraic [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181), which are in fact [quartic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartic_curve) over $\mathbb{C}$, and quartic surfaces over $\mathbb{R}$. For instance, the [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976) is a *real* surface given as a quartic curve over $\mathbb{C}$. If on the other hand the base field is finite, then it is said to be an *arithmetic quartic surface*.

## Special quartic surfaces {#Special_quartic_surfaces}

- [Dupin cyclides](wikipedia://en/705128)
- The [Fermat quartic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_quartic), given by *x*<sup>4</sup> + *y*<sup>4</sup> + *z*<sup>4</sup> + *w*<sup>4</sup> =0 (an example of a K3 surface).
- More generally, certain [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116) are examples of quartic surfaces.
- [Kummer surface](wikipedia://en/647758)
- [Plücker surface](wikipedia://en/23440969)
- [Weddle surface](wikipedia://en/23439425)
