# Aleksandrov–Clark measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aleksandrov–Clark measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Aleksandrov–Clark (AC) measures** are specially constructed [measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory) named after the two [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902), [A. B. Aleksandrov](wikipedia://en/56751111) and [Douglas Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Clark_%28mathematician%29), who discovered some of their deepest properties. The measures are also called either Aleksandrov measures, Clark measures, or occasionally spectral measures.

:::

AC measures are used to extract information about self-maps of the [unit disc](wikipedia://en/252738), and have applications in a number of areas of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), most notably those related to [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675). Systems of AC measures have also been constructed for higher dimensions, and for the [half-plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-plane).

## Construction of the measures {#Construction_of_the_measures}

The original construction of Clark relates to one-dimensional perturbations of compressed shift operators on subspaces of the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780):

$$H^2(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}).$$

By virtue of [Beurling's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurling's_theorem), any shift-invariant subspace of this space is of the form

$$\theta H^2(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C}),$$

where $\theta$ is an [inner function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_function). As such, any [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) of the adjoint of the shift is of the form

$$K_\theta = \left(\theta H^2(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})\right)^\perp.$$

We now define $S_\theta$ to be the shift operator compressed to $K_\theta$, that is

$$S_\theta = P_{K_\theta} S|_{K_\theta}.$$

Clark noticed that all the one-dimensional perturbations of $S_\theta$, which were also unitary maps, were of the form

$$U_\alpha (f) = S_\theta (f) + \alpha \left\langle f , \frac{\theta}{z} \right\rangle,$$

and related each such map to a measure, $\sigma_\alpha$ on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071), via the [Spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495). This collection of measures, one for each $\alpha$ on the unit circle $^\mathbb{T}$, is then called the collection of AC measures associated with $\theta$.

## An alternative construction {#An_alternative_construction}

The collection of measures may also be constructed for any [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) (that is, not necessarily an inner function). Given an analytic self map, $\phi$, of the unit disc, $^\mathbb{D}$, we can construct a collection of functions, $u_\alpha$, given by

$$u_\alpha(z) = \Re \left(\frac{\alpha + \varphi(z)}{\alpha - \varphi(z)}\right),$$

one for each $^{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}$. Each of these functions is positive and harmonic, so by Herglotz' Theorem each is the Poisson integral of some positive measure $\mu_\alpha$ on $^\mathbb{T}$. This collection is the set of AC measures associated with $\varphi$. It can be shown that the two definitions coincide for inner functions.
