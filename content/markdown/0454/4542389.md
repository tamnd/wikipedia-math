# Alexander Gelfond

Osipovich

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Gelfond"}

**Alexander Osipovich Gelfond** (Алекса́ндр О́сипович Ге́льфонд; 24 October 1906 – 7 November 1968) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) mathematician. [Gelfond's theorem](wikipedia://en/609487), also known as the Gelfond–Schneider theorem, is named after him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Alexander Gelfond was born in Saint Petersburg, [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), the son of a professional [physician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) and amateur [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher) [Osip Gelfond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osip_Gelfond).[^o4] He entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in 1924, started his postgraduate studies there in 1927, and obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1930. His advisors were [Aleksandr Khinchin](wikipedia://en/1029748) (1894-1959) and [Vyacheslav Stepanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Stepanov) (1889-1950).[^o5][^o6]

In 1930, he stayed for five months in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) (in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen)) where he worked with [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230), [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602), and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). In 1931 he started teaching as a Professor at the Moscow State University and worked there until the last day of his life. Since 1933 he also worked at the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494).

In 1939, he was elected a [Corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corresponding_member) of the [Academy of Sciences of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) for his works in the field of [Cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). According to [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490), during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) Gelfond was the Chief Cryptographer of the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy).[^o7]

## Results {#Results}

Gelfond obtained important results in several mathematical domains including [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [analytic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_analytic_functions), [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), but his most famous result is his [eponymous theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfond's_theorem): If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) (with $\alpha \ne 0$ and $\alpha \ne 1$), and if $\beta$ is not a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024), then any value of $\alpha^{\beta}$ is a [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325).

This is the famous [7th Hilbert's problem](wikipedia://en/392579). Gelfond proved a special case of the theorem in 1929 when he was a postgraduate student and fully proved it in 1934. The same theorem was independently proven by [Theodor Schneider](wikipedia://en/10312621), and so the theorem is often known as the [Gelfond–Schneider theorem](wikipedia://en/609487). In 1929 Gelfond proposed an extension of the theorem known as [Gelfond's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelfond's_conjecture) that was proven by [Alan Baker](wikipedia://en/18950800) in 1966.

Before Gelfond's works only a few numbers such as $e$ and [$\pi$](wikipedia://en/23601) were known to be transcendental. After his works, an infinite number of transcendentals could be easily obtained. Some of them are named in Gelfond's honor:

- $2^{\sqrt{2}}$ is known as the [Gelfond–Schneider constant](wikipedia://en/886866)
- $e^{\pi}$ is known as [Gelfond's constant](wikipedia://en/1984963).
