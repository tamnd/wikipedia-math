# Raymond Redheffer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raymond Redheffer"}

**Raymond Moos Redheffer** (April 17, 1921 – May 13, 2005)[^o2] was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and inventor. He was the creator of one of the first electronic games, [Nim](wikipedia://en/21885), a knowledge game.[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

From 1939 to 1950, Redheffer was an undergraduate and graduate student at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and as a Pierce Fellow at Harvard. While in graduate school, he worked at the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Radiation_Laboratory), a [WW II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WW_II) research laboratory developing [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar), and the [Research Laboratory of Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Laboratory_of_Electronics), which were both part of [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). He taught as a [Benjamin Peirce](wikipedia://en/364270) Fellow at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) from 1948 to 1950.[^o2] He earned his PhD in 1948 from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology under [Norman Levinson](wikipedia://en/1090122).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1949, Redheffer and co-inventors Edward B. McMillan and Herbert Leaderman applied for a patent for a [dielectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric) wall for the transmission of [microwave radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_radiation).[^o7] In 1950, Redheffer began teaching mathematics at UCLA.[^o2] In 1953, he was awarded the patent for a computer for evaluating complex integrals.[^o8] Later that year, he was awarded a patent for a computing machine. Both [patents were assigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_%28patent%29) to the [U.S. Navy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy)[^o9][^o10] In November of that same year, Redheffer, McMillan, and Leaderman were awarded the 1953 patent for their 1949 dielectric patent application, with the patent assigned to McMillan[^o7] In the world of mathematics, Redheffer would later be known for the [Redheffer star product](wikipedia://en/67981364) which he had introduced in 1959.[^o11] Redheffer and McMillan were awarded a 1960 patent for another dielectric wall application, this for a more generalized application, which was a dielectric wall for transmission of [electromagnetic radiation](wikipedia://en/9426).[^o12]

In 1961, he collaborated with [Charles Eames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Eames) on a series of animated short films about mathematics for the exhibition at the [California Museum of Science and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Museum_of_Science_and_Industry) in [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles). He later worked and with Eames on his 1966 [timeline of mathematics](wikipedia://en/19537780) entitled *[Men of Modern Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men_of_Modern_Mathematics)* which was printed and distributed by [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM).[^o2][^o13][^o14] In that same year, notable and unusual is the physically motivated discussion of the functions of [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640) in his book with [Ivan Sokolnikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_S._Sokolnikoff), *Mathematics of Physics and Modern Engineering* (1966).[^o15] Eleven years later, his paper *An explicitly solvable optimization task* (1977) was the source for what is now the [Redheffer matrix](wikipedia://en/22113019) and was also a notable mathematical achievement.[^o16]

His teaching skills were acknowledged 6 decades later by one of his students.[^o17] He taught for 55 years at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), writing more than 200 research papers, ranging from microwave analysis and dielectric constants, and three textbooks.[^o2] He may have invented a version of [Nim](wikipedia://en/21885) with electronic components.
