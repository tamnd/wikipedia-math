# Kolmogorov's two-series theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kolmogorov's two-series theorem"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), **Kolmogorov's two-series theorem** is a result about the convergence of random series. It follows from [Kolmogorov's inequality](wikipedia://en/2975185) and is used in one proof of the [strong law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_law_of_large_numbers).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\left( X_n \right)_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be [independent random variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_random_variables) with [expected values](wikipedia://en/9653) $\mathbf{E} \left[ X_n \right] = \mu_n$ and [variances](wikipedia://en/32344) $\mathbf{Var} \left( X_n \right) = \sigma_n^2$, such that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mu_n$ [converges](wikipedia://en/50723) in $\mathbb{R}$ and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sigma_n^2$ converges in $\mathbb{R}$. Then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} X_n$ converges in $\mathbb{R}$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908).

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

Assume [WLOG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WLOG) $\mu_n = 0$. Set $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N X_n$, and we will see that $\limsup_N S_N - \liminf_NS_N = 0$ with probability 1.

For every $m \in \mathbb{N}$,

$$\limsup_{N \to \infty} S_N - \liminf_{N \to \infty} S_N = \limsup_{N \to \infty} \left( S_N - S_m \right) - \liminf_{N \to \infty} \left( S_N - S_m \right) \leq 2 \max_{k \in \mathbb{N} } \left| \sum_{i=1}^{k} X_{m+i} \right|$$

Thus, for every $m \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\epsilon > 0$,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbb{P} \left( \limsup_{N \to \infty} \left( S_N - S_m \right) - \liminf_{N \to \infty} \left( S_N - S_m \right) \geq \epsilon \right) &\leq \mathbb{P} \left( 2 \max_{k \in \mathbb{N} } \left| \sum_{i=1}^{k} X_{m+i} \right| \geq \epsilon \ \right) \\ &= \mathbb{P} \left( \max_{k \in \mathbb{N} } \left| \sum_{i=1}^{k} X_{m+i} \right| \geq \frac{\epsilon}{2} \ \right) \\ &\leq \limsup_{N \to \infty} 4\epsilon^{-2} \sum_{i=m+1}^{m+N} \sigma_i^2 \\ &= 4\epsilon^{-2} \lim_{N \to \infty} \sum_{i=m+1}^{m+N} \sigma_i^2 \end{aligned}$$

While the second inequality is due to [Kolmogorov's inequality](wikipedia://en/2975185).

By the assumption that $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sigma_n^2$ converges, it follows that the last term tends to 0 when $m \to \infty$, for every arbitrary $\epsilon > 0$.

:::
