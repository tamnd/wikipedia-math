# Standard monomial theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard monomial theory"}

In algebraic geometry, **standard monomial theory** describes the sections of a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over a [generalized flag variety](wikipedia://en/385339) or [Schubert variety](wikipedia://en/10469987) of a [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) by giving an explicit basis of elements called **standard monomials**. Many of the results have been extended to [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292) and their groups.

:::

There are monographs on standard monomial theory by (Lakshmibai Raghavan 2008) and (Seshadri 2007) and survey articles by (txt, 432–435) and (txt, 279–322).

One of important open problems is to give a completely geometric construction of the theory.[^o6]

## History {#History}

(txt) introduced monomials associated to standard [Young tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux). (txt) (see also (Hodge Pedoe 1994, p.378)) used Young's monomials, which he called standard power products, named after standard tableaux, to give a basis for the homogeneous coordinate rings of complex [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810). (txt) initiated a program, called **standard monomial theory**, to extend Hodge's work to varieties *G*/*P*, for *P* any [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of any [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) in any characteristic, by giving explicit bases using standard monomials for sections of line bundles over these varieties. The case of Grassmannians studied by Hodge corresponds to the case when *G* is a special linear group in characteristic 0 and *P* is a maximal parabolic subgroup. Seshadri was soon joined in this effort by V. Lakshmibai and [Chitikila Musili](wikipedia://en/37394830). They worked out standard monomial theory first for [minuscule representations](wikipedia://en/29353987) of *G* and then for groups *G* of classical type, and formulated several conjectures describing it for more general cases. (txt) proved their conjectures using the [Littelmann path model](wikipedia://en/11167326), in particular giving a uniform description of standard monomials for all reductive groups.

(Lakshmibai 2003) and (Musili 2003) and (Seshadri 2012) give detailed descriptions of the early development of standard monomial theory.

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- Since the sections of line bundles over generalized flag varieties tend to form irreducible representations of the corresponding algebraic groups, having an explicit basis of standard monomials allows one to give character formulas for these representations. Similarly one gets character formulas for [Demazure modules](wikipedia://en/32561183). The explicit bases given by standard monomial theory are closely related to [crystal bases](wikipedia://en/10330610) and [Littelmann path models](wikipedia://en/11167326) of representations.
- Standard monomial theory allows one to describe the singularities of Schubert varieties, and in particular sometimes proves that Schubert varieties are normal or [Cohen–Macaulay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohen–Macaulay).
- Standard monomial theory can be used to prove [Demazure's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demazure's_conjecture).
- Standard monomial theory proves the [Kempf vanishing theorem](wikipedia://en/37552988) and other vanishing theorems for the higher cohomology of effective line bundles over Schubert varieties.
- Standard monomial theory gives explicit bases for some rings of invariants in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762).
- Standard monomial theory gives generalizations of the [Littlewood–Richardson rule](wikipedia://en/16004359) about decompositions of tensor products of representations to all reductive algebraic groups.
- Standard monomial theory can be used to prove the existence of [good filtrations](wikipedia://en/37479627) on some representations of reductive algebraic groups in positive characteristic.

:::
