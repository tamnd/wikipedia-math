# Andronov–Pontryagin criterion

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andronov–Pontryagin criterion"}

The **Andronov–Pontryagin criterion** is a necessary and sufficient condition for the stability of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) in the plane. It was derived by [Aleksandr Andronov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Andronov) and [Lev Pontryagin](wikipedia://en/582075) in 1937.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

A dynamical system

$$\dot{x} = v(x),$$

where $v$ is a $C^{1}$-[vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on the [plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), $x \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$, is [orbitally topologically stable](wikipedia://en/5506149) if and only if the following two conditions hold:

1. All [equilibrium points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_point) and [periodic orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) are *hyperbolic*.
1. There are no *saddle connections*.

The same statement holds if the vector field $v$ is defined on the [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) and is transversal to the boundary.

:::

## Clarifications {#Clarifications}

**Orbital topological stability** of a dynamical system means that for any sufficiently small perturbation (in the *C*<sup>1</sup>-metric), there exists a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) close to the identity map which transforms the orbits of the original dynamical system to the orbits of the perturbed system (cf [structural stability](wikipedia://en/2515807)).

The first condition of the theorem is known as **global hyperbolicity**. A zero of a vector field *v*, i.e. a point *x*<sub>0</sub> where *v*(*x*<sub>0</sub>)=0, is said to be **hyperbolic** if none of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the linearization of *v* at *x*<sub>0</sub> is purely imaginary. A periodic orbit of a flow is said to be hyperbolic if none of the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the [Poincaré return map](wikipedia://en/876732) at a point on the orbit has absolute value one.

Finally, **saddle connection** refers to a situation where an orbit from one saddle point enters the same or another saddle point, i.e. the unstable and stable [separatrices](wikipedia://en/2922081) are connected (cf [homoclinic orbit](wikipedia://en/3948734) and [heteroclinic orbit](wikipedia://en/3948758)).
