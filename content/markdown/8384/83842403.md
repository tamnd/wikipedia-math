# Kapustin–Witten equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kapustin–Witten equations"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) in mathematics, the **Kapustin–Witten equations** are the gauge [field equations](wikipedia://en/1410595) of **Kapustin–Witten theory**, which is obtained as a [topologically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topologically) twisted [N = 4 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_4_supersymmetric_Yang-Mills_theory) (N = 4 SYM) using the **Kapustin–Witten twist** (or **geometric Langlands twist** due to its connection to the [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178)). The Kapustin–Witten equations are formulated on four-dimensional [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) (short [4-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1362795)), representing [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) in physics, and as [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) depend on its [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238). Considering the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of its solutions, which are all solutions up to [gauge](wikipedia://en/39240530), meaning physical equivalence, therefore encodes informations about it, similar to the much more well-known [Yang–Mills moduli space](wikipedia://en/80813159) and [Seiberg–Witten moduli space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiberg–Witten_moduli_space). The Kapustin–Witten equations are named after [Anton Kapustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Kapustin) and [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894), who developed them in 2007.

:::

## Topological twist {#Topological_twist}

A topological twist is a choice for how the four-dimensional [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231) $\operatorname{Spin}(4)=\operatorname{SU}(2)\times\operatorname{SU}(2)$ acts on the canonical inclusion [representation](wikipedia://en/19378200) $\mathbf{4}\colon\operatorname{SU}(4)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb{C})$ of the [R-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-symmetry) group, which relates the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) to the supersymmetry. The Kapustin-Witten twist is then the splitting $(\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})$.[^o3]

## Development {#Development}

In 1988, Edward Witten developed [topological quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/443757) (TQFT),[^o5] a contribution listed for his [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1990,[^o6] and used it to showed that [Donaldson theory](wikipedia://en/7684340), a contribution listed for [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305)'s Fields Medal in 1986,[^o7] is a TT [N = 2 SYM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_2_supersymmetric_Yang-Mills_theory). In 1994, [Nathan Seiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Seiberg) and Edward Witten then constructed the dual TT N = 2 SYM, known as [Seiberg–Witten theory](wikipedia://en/3526032).[^o8][^o9] Both theories were very successful in describing smooth 4-manifolds. Later development then shifted from the N = 2 case with two inequivalent twists to the N = 4 case with three inequivalent twists. Besides the [Kapustin–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapustin–Witten_twist), these are the [Donaldson–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaldson–Witten_twist) ($(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})$) and the [Vafa–Witten twist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vafa–Witten_twist) ($(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})\oplus(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})$).[^o3]

## Field equations {#Field_equations}

The [bosonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boson) fields of Kapustin–Witten theory are a [gauge field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_field) $A$ with [field strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_strength) $F$ and 1-form $\phi$. The Kapustin-Witten equations are then given by:[^o3]

$$(F-\phi\wedge\phi+tD\phi)^+=0;$$

$$(F-\phi\wedge\phi-t^{-1}D\phi)^-=0;$$

$$D^*\phi=0.$$
