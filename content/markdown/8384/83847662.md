# Tião Bocalom

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tião Bocalom"}

**Sebastião Bocalom Rodrigues** (May 18, 1953), better known as **Tião Bocalom**, is a [Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [politician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politician) affiliated with the [Brazilian Social Democracy Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Social_Democracy_Party) (PSDB).[^o3][^o4][^o5] He served as mayor of [Rio Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Branco,_Acre), the capital of the state of [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_%28state%29), from 2021 to 2026, having been elected in 2020 Rio Branco mayoral election and reelected in the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rio_Branco_mayoral_election) election.[^o6] In April 2026, he resigned from the mayor’s office to run for governor in the 2026 Acre gubernatorial election of that same year.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### First years and education {#First_years_and_education}

Sebastião was born in [Bela Vista do Paraíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bela_Vista_do_Paraíso), a city located in the interior of the state of [Paraná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraná_%28state%29).[^o10][^o11] He moved to [Mandaguari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaguari), also in the interior of Paraná, where he earned a degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from the Mandaguari School of Philosophy, Sciences, and Letters.[^o12] After graduating, he began working as a math teacher.[^o13][^o14]

### Politics {#Politics}

He began his political career in the city of [Nova Olímpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Olímpia,_Paraná), in the interior of Paraná, as a member of the [Democratic Social Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Social_Party) (PDS).[^o16][^o17] In the 1982 election, he was elected city councilor after receiving 166 votes, making him the second-highest vote-getter in the election.[^o18] In 1986, he went to the state of [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_%28state%29) on vacation and was invited to start a lumber business, where he settled with his family the following year.[^o16] A resident of Acre and a member of the [Reform Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Progressive_Party) (PPR), he became, in 1993, the first mayor of the city of [Acrelândia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrelândia), which had been founded in 1992.[^o19] He served as mayor until 1996.[^o12] He served as State Secretary of Agriculture for the State of Acre from January 1999 to May 2000, during [Jorge Viana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Viana)’s ([PT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_%28Brazil%29)) administration.[^o16][^o20]

In 2000, as a member of the [Brazilian Social Democracy Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Social_Democracy_Party) (PSDB), he ran again for mayor of Acrelândia.[^o19] He was elected, becoming mayor of Acrelândia once again until 2004.[^o19] In 2004, he ran for mayor again. He was elected after receiving more than 2,000 votes.[^o19][^o21] He resigned from his position on March 24, 2006, to run for governor of Acre.[^o22] Running as a candidate for the PSDB in the 2006 Acre gubernatorial election, he finished in third place in a race won by [Binho Marques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binho_Marques) (PT).[^o23] In the 2008 municipal elections, he ran for mayor of Rio Branco, the capital of the state of Acre, as a candidate for the PSDB.[^o24][^o25] Tião finished the election in third place, in a race won by Raimundo Angelim (PT).[^o25]

In the [2010 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Acre_gubernatorial_election), he ran again for governor of Acre as a candidate for the PSDB. This time, he formed a broad coalition with [Brazilian Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Democratic_Movement) (PMDB), [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_%28Brazil%29) (DEM), [Social Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Party_%28Brazil%29) (PSL), [Social Christian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Christian_Party_%28Brazil%29) (PSC), [Popular Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Socialist_Party_%28Brazil%29) (PPS), [National Mobilization Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Mobilization_Party) (PMN) [Labour Party of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_of_Brazil) (PTdoB) and managed to secure 49.18% of the valid votes in the first round, but was defeated by candidate [Tião Viana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tião_Viana) (PT), who secured 50.51% and won the election in the first round.[^o26][^o27] In the end, the margin between the candidates was less than 5,000 votes, making it one of the closest gubernatorial elections in modern Brazilian history.[^o28][^o29][^o30]

In the 2012 municipal elections, he ran for mayor of the capital, Rio Branco, also as a candidate for the PSDB, and advanced to the runoff against candidate Marcus Alexandre (PT).[^o31] While he received 43.85% of the vote in the first round, he secured 49.81% of the valid votes in the runoff, losing by a margin of fewer than 3,000 votes.[^o32] In the [2014 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Acre_gubernatorial_election), for the third consecutive time, Tião Bocalom ran for governor of Acre.[^o33][^o34] However, he ran as a candidate for the DEM, while the PSDB fielded another candidate: [Márcio Bittar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Márcio_Bittar).[^o35][^o36] In addition to the DEM, its coalition included the PMN and the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_%28Brazil%29) (PV).[^o35][^o37] Bocalom finished in third place in an election won by Tião Vianna.[^o38]

After deciding to support [Jair Bolsonaro’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jair_Bolsonaro) [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jair_Bolsonaro_2018_presidential_campaign) bid, he joined the PSL to run in the 2018 elections.[^o39] This time, he chose to run for federal deputy. After the polls closed, it became clear that he had failed to win a seat due to the electoral coefficient.[^o40] He was the third-highest-voted candidate for federal deputy in the city of Rio Branco and the highest-voted candidate in the city of Acrelândia, where he had served as mayor.[^o41] However, even though he was the fifth-highest-voted candidate in the state, he failed to win a seat.[^o41]

In the 2020 Rio Branco mayoral election, he ran again for mayor of Rio Branco.[^o42] This time, as a member of the [Progressistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressistas) (PP), he received 49.58% of the valid votes in the first round.[^o43] In the runoff election against then-Mayor [Socorro Neri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socorro_Neri) ([PSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Socialist_Party)), he was elected mayor with more than 62% of the vote.[^o44] According to a report by *[G1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G1_%28website%29)*, the [Grupo Globo's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Globo) online news portal, he has fulfilled only one of his 49 campaign promises in his first year in office; the politician claims that the low number is due to the economic effects of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Brazil).[^o45] In March 2024, he left the PP and joined the [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_%28Brazil,_2006%29) (PL).[^o46] As a candidate for the PL, he was reelected in the [Rio Branco municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Rio_Branco_mayoral_election), winning in the first round with 54% of the valid votes and defeating his predecessor as mayor, Marcus Alexandre (MDB).[^o47][^o48] In April 2026, he resigned as mayor to run for governor in the October elections of that same year, handing over the office to his deputy, Alysson Bestene.[^o49][^o50][^o51] As part of a political maneuver led by [Aécio Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aécio_Neves), the PSDB’s national president, Tião Bocalom returned to the party in 2026.[^o52][^o53] Bocalom had been a member of the PSDB for sixteen years.[^o54][^o55]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He was married to Elisabeth Aparecida Garcia Rodrigues, who died in 2021 from [amyotrophic lateral sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALS), with whom he had two children, Luciana and Júnior.[^o65][^o66] Júnior died at the age of 12 from [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia).[^o67] Luciana married Josivan, and they had three granddaughters: Isabela, Ana Luisa, and Maria Rita.[^o67][^o68]
