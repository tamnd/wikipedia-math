# Fuglede's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fuglede's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Fuglede's theorem** is a result in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675), named after [Bent Fuglede](wikipedia://en/3966927).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="The result"}

## The result {#The_result}

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Fuglede)"}

**Theorem (Fuglede)** Let *T* and *N* be [bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on a [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) with *N* being [normal](wikipedia://en/190167). If *TN* = *NT*, then *TN\** = *N\*T*, where *N\** denotes the [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175) of *N*.

Normality of *N* is necessary, as is seen by taking *T*=*N*. When *T* is [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408), the claim is trivial regardless of whether *N* is normal:

$$TN^* = (NT)^* = (TN)^* = N^*T.$$

**Tentative Proof**: If the underlying Hilbert space is [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267), the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) says that *N* is of the form

$$N = \sum\nolimits_i \lambda_i P_i$$

where *P<sub>i</sub>* are pairwise orthogonal projections. One expects that *TN* = *NT* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *TP<sub>i</sub>* = *P<sub>i</sub>T*. Indeed, it can be proved to be true by elementary arguments (e.g. it can be shown that all *P<sub>i</sub>* are representable as [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) of *N* and for this reason, if *T* commutes with *N*, it has to commute with *P<sub>i</sub>*...). Therefore *T* must also commute with

$$N^* = \sum\nolimits_i {\bar \lambda_i} P_i.$$

In general, when the Hilbert space is not finite-dimensional, the normal operator *N* gives rise to a [projection-valued measure](wikipedia://en/1046155) *P* on its spectrum, *σ*(*N*), which assigns a projection *P*<sub>Ω</sub> to each Borel subset of *σ*(*N*). *N* can be expressed as

$$N = \int_{\sigma(N)} \lambda d P(\lambda).$$

Differently from the finite-dimensional case, it is by no means obvious that *TN = NT* implies *TP*<sub>Ω</sub> = *P*<sub>Ω</sub>*T*. Thus, it is not so obvious that *T* also commutes with any simple function of the form

$$\rho = \sum\nolimits_i {\bar \lambda} P_{\Omega_i}.$$

Indeed, following the construction of the spectral decomposition for a bounded, normal, not self-adjoint, operator *T*, one sees that to verify that *T* commutes with $P_{\Omega_i}$, the most straightforward way is to assume that *T* commutes with both *N* and *N\**, giving rise to a vicious circle!

That is the relevance of Fuglede's theorem: The latter hypothesis is not really necessary.

:::

:::

## Putnam's generalization {#Putnam's_generalization}

The following contains Fuglede's result as a special case. The proof by Rosenblum pictured below is just that presented by Fuglede for his theorem when assuming *N*=*M*.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Calvin Richard Putnam)"}

**Theorem (Calvin Richard Putnam)**[^o7] Let *T*, *M*, *N* be [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) on a complex Hilbert space, and suppose that *M* and *N* are [normal](wikipedia://en/190167), *T* is bounded and *MT* = *TN*. Then *M*\**T* = *TN*\*.

**First proof (Marvin Rosenblum)**: By [induction](wikipedia://en/18881), the hypothesis implies that *M*<sup>*k*</sup>*T* = *TN*<sup>*k*</sup> for all *k*. Thus for any λ in $\mathbb{C}$,

$$e^{\bar\lambda M}T = T e^{\bar\lambda N}.$$

Consider the function

$$F(\lambda) = e^{\lambda M^*} T e^{-\lambda N^*}.$$

This is equal to

$$e^{\lambda M^*} \left[e^{-\bar\lambda M}T e^{\bar\lambda N}\right] e^{-\lambda N^*} = U(\lambda) T V(\lambda)^{-1},$$

where $U(\lambda) = e^{\lambda M^* - \bar\lambda M}$ because $M$ is normal, and similarly $V(\lambda) = e^{\lambda N^* - \bar\lambda N}$. However we have

$$U(\lambda)^* = e^{\bar\lambda M - \lambda M^*} = U(\lambda)^{-1}$$

so *U* is unitary, and hence has norm 1 for all λ; the same is true for *V*(λ), so

$$\|F(\lambda)\| \le \|T\|\ \forall \lambda.$$

So *F* is a bounded analytic vector-valued function, and is thus constant, and equal to *F*(0) = *T*. Considering the first-order terms in the expansion for small λ, we must have *M\*T* = *TN\**.

The original paper of Fuglede appeared in 1950; it was extended to the form given above by Putnam in 1951.[^o7] The short proof given above was first published by Rosenblum in 1958; it is very elegant, but is less general than the original proof which also considered the case of unbounded operators. Another simple proof of Putnam's theorem is as follows:

**Second proof:** Consider the matrices

$$T' = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ T & 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{and} \quad N' = \begin{bmatrix} N & 0 \\ 0 & M \end{bmatrix}.$$

The operator *N'* is normal and, by assumption, *T' N' = N' T'*. By Fuglede's theorem, one has

$$T' (N')^* = (N')^*T'.$$

Comparing entries then gives the desired result.

From Putnam's generalization, one can deduce the following:

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=inline-head name="Corollary"}

**Corollary** If two normal operators *M* and *N* are similar, then they are unitarily equivalent.

:::

::: {#o9 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof**: Suppose *MS* = *SN* where *S* is a bounded invertible operator. Putnam's result implies *M\*S* = *SN\**, i.e.

$$S^{-1} M^* S = N^*.$$

Take the adjoint of the above equation and we have

$$S^* M (S^{-1})^* = N.$$

So

$$S^* M (S^{-1})^* = S^{-1} M S \quad \Rightarrow \quad SS^* M (SS^*)^{-1} = M.$$

Let *S\*=VR*, with *V* a unitary (since *S* is invertible) and *R* the positive square root of *SS\**. As *R* is a limit of polynomials on *SS\**, the above implies that *R* commutes with *M*. It is also invertible. Then

$$N = S^*M (S^*)^{-1}=VRMR^{-1}V^*=VMV^*.$$

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/corollary cue=inline-head name="Corollary"}

**Corollary** If *M* and *N* are normal operators, and *MN* = *NM*, then *MN* is also normal.

:::

::: {#o11 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof**: The argument invokes only Fuglede's theorem. One can directly compute

$$(MN) (MN)^* = MN (NM)^* = MN M^* N^*.$$

By Fuglede, the above becomes

$$= M M^* N N^*.$$

But *M* and *N* are normal, so

$$= M^* M N^*N.$$

By Fuglede (again), the above becomes

$$= M^* N^* MN = (NM)^* MN$$

and the hypothesis *MN = NM* shows that it is

$$= (MN)^* MN.$$

:::

## *C\**-algebras {#C*-algebras}

The theorem can be rephrased as a statement about elements of [C\*-algebras](wikipedia://en/7184).

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem (Fuglede-Putnam-Rosenblum)"}

**Theorem (Fuglede-Putnam-Rosenblum)** Let *x, y* be two normal elements of a *C\**-algebra *A* and *z* such that *xz* = *zy*. Then it follows that *x\* z = z y\**.

:::
