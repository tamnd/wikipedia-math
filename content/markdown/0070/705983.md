# Illicit major

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Illicit major"}

**Illicit major** is a [formal fallacy](wikipedia://en/3637937) committed in a [categorical syllogism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_syllogism) that is [invalid](wikipedia://en/21304742) because its [major term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_term) is [undistributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_of_terms) in the [major premise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_premise).

:::

This fallacy has the following argument form:

1. *All A are B*
1. *No C are A*
1. *Therefore, no C are B*

::: {#o2 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example:**

1. *All dogs are mammals*
1. *No cats are dogs*
1. *Therefore, no cats are mammals*

In this argument, the major term is "mammals". This is distributed in the conclusion (the last statement) because we are making a claim about a property of *all* mammals: that they are not cats. However, it is not distributed in the major premise (the first statement) where we are only talking about a property of *some* mammals: Only some mammals are dogs.

The error is in assuming that the converse of the first statement (that all mammals are dogs) is also true.

However, an argument in the following form differs from the above, and is valid (Camestres):

1. *All A are B*
1. *No B are C*
1. *Therefore, no C are A*

:::
