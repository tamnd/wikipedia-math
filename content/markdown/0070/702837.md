# Cocoloring

*Image: Cocoloring.svg.* Cocoloring with 3 colors (upper left figure): a [proper](wikipedia://en/426743#Vertex_coloring) 3-coloring of this graph is impossible. The blue [subgraph](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraphs) forms a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) (bottom right figure), while the red and green subgraphs form cliques on the graph's [complement](wikipedia://en/325802#Basics).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cocoloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **cocoloring** of a graph *G* is an assignment of [colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color) to the vertices such that each color class forms an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) in *G* or in the [complement](wikipedia://en/325802#Basics) of *G*. The **cochromatic number** z(*G*) of *G* is the fewest colors needed in any cocolorings of *G*. The graphs with cochromatic number 2 are exactly the [bipartite graphs](wikipedia://en/244431), complements of bipartite graphs, and [split graphs](wikipedia://en/9928681).

:::

As the requirement that each color class be a clique or independent is weaker than the requirement for [coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) (in which each color class must be an independent set) and stronger than for [subcoloring](wikipedia://en/702867) (in which each color class must be a disjoint union of cliques), it follows that the cochromatic number of *G* is less than or equal to the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of *G*, and that it is greater than or equal to the subchromatic number of *G*.

Cocoloring was named and first studied by (Lesniak Straight 1977). (Jørgensen 1995) characterizes critical 3-cochromatic graphs, while (Fomin Kratsch Novelli 2002) describe algorithms for approximating the cochromatic number of a graph. (Zverovich 2000) defines a class of *perfect cochromatic graphs*, analogous to the definition of perfect graphs via graph coloring, and provides a forbidden subgraph characterization of these graphs.
