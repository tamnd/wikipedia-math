# Affirming a disjunct

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affirming a disjunct"}

*Image: Affirming a disjunct.png.* Affirming a disjunct is a formal logical fallacy.

The [formal fallacy](wikipedia://en/3637937) of **affirming a disjunct**, also known as the **fallacy of the alternative disjunct** or a **false exclusionary disjunct**, occurs when a [deductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive_logic) argument takes the following [logical form](wikipedia://en/3331706):[^o2]

:::

A [or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusive_or) B

A

Therefore, not B

Or in [logical operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_operators):

$$p \vee q$$

$$p$$

${} \vdash {}$ ¬ $q$

Where ${} \vdash {}$ denotes a [logical assertion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_assertion).

## Explanation {#Explanation}

*Image: Venn0111.svg.* [Venn diagram](wikipedia://en/61701) for "A or B", with [inclusive or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclusive_or) (OR)

*Image: Venn0110.svg.* [Venn diagram](wikipedia://en/61701) for "A or B", with [exclusive or](wikipedia://en/105979) (XOR)

The fallacy lies in concluding that one [disjunct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disjunction) must be false because the other disjunct is true; in fact they may both be true because "or" is defined inclusively rather than exclusively. It is a fallacy of [equivocation](wikipedia://en/17690) between the operations [OR](wikipedia://en/7962) and [XOR](wikipedia://en/105979).

Affirming the disjunct should not be confused with the valid argument known as [disjunctive syllogism](wikipedia://en/7963).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following argument indicates the unsoundness of affirming a disjunct:

Max is a mammal or Max is a cat.

Max is a mammal.

Therefore, Max is not a cat.

This [inference](wikipedia://en/317465) is unsound because **all** cats, by definition, are mammals.

A second example provides a first proposition that appears realistic and shows how an obviously flawed conclusion still arises under this fallacy.[^o7]

To be on the cover of [Vogue Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_%28magazine%29), one must be a celebrity or very beautiful.

This month's cover was a celebrity.

Therefore, this celebrity is not very beautiful.

:::
