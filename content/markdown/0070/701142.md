# Toy model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toy model"}

In [scientific modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_modeling), a **toy model** is a deliberately simplistic model with many details removed so that it can be used to explain a mechanism concisely. It is also useful in a description of the fuller model.

:::

- In **toy mathematical models** used in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), this is usually done by reducing or extending the number of dimensions or reducing the number of fields/variables or restricting them to a particular symmetric form.[^o2]
- In **toy [economic models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_model)**, some may be only loosely based on theory, others more explicitly so. They allow for a quick first pass at some question, and present the essence of the answer from a more complicated model or from a class of models. For the researcher, they may come before writing a more elaborate model, or after, once the elaborate model has been worked out. Blanchard's list of examples includes the [IS–LM model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IS–LM_model), the [Mundell–Fleming model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundell–Fleming_model), the RBC model, and the New Keynesian model.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of toy models in physics include:

- the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) as a toy model for [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism), or [lattice models](wikipedia://en/291472) more generally. It is the simplest model that allows for [Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_field_theory) [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) in [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics).[^o6][^o7][^o8]
- Newtonian [orbital mechanics](wikipedia://en/182146) as described by assuming that Earth is attached to the Sun by an elastic band;
- the [Schwarzschild metric](wikipedia://en/264606), general relativistic model describing a single symmetrical non-rotating non-charged concentration of mass (such as a perfect spherical mass): a simple relativistic "equivalent" of the classical symmetric Newtonian mass (in fact, the first [solution of the Einstein field equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solution_of_the_Einstein_field_equations) to be developed);
- [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724) around a [black hole](wikipedia://en/4650) described as conventional radiation from a fictitious membrane at radius [$r = 2m$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R=2M) (the black hole [membrane paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_paradigm));
- [frame-dragging](wikipedia://en/27086745) around a rotating star considered as the effect of space being a conventional [viscous fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscous_fluid);
- the [null dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_dust);
- the [Gödel metric](wikipedia://en/2085068) in general relativity, which allows [closed timelike curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_timelike_curves);
- the [Lambda-CDM model](wikipedia://en/985963) of cosmology, in which general relativistic effects of structure formation are not taken into account.[^o9]
- the [empty universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_universe), a simple expanding universe model;
- the [Bohr model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_model) of the atom, a "semi-classical" quantum mechanical model of the atom, which can be solved exactly for the hydrogen atom;
- the [particle in a box](wikipedia://en/24048) in quantum mechanics;
- the [Spekkens model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spekkens_toy_model), a [hidden-variable theory](wikipedia://en/211602);
- the [primon gas](wikipedia://en/7618877), which illustrates some connections between [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and physics.
