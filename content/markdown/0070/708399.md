# List of publications in mathematics

*Image: Oxyrhynchus papyrus with Euclid's Elements.jpg.* One of the oldest surviving fragments of [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107), found at [Oxyrhynchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxyrhynchus) and dated to circa AD 100. The diagram accompanies Book II, Proposition 5.[^o1]

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="List of publications in mathematics"}

This is a list of **publications** in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), organized by field.

:::

Some reasons a particular publication might be regarded as important:

- **Topic creator** – A publication that created a new topic
- **Breakthrough** – A publication that changed scientific knowledge significantly
- **Influence** – A publication which has significantly influenced the world or has had a massive impact on the teaching of mathematics.

Among published compilations of important publications in mathematics are *Landmark writings in Western mathematics 1640–1940* by [Ivor Grattan-Guinness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivor_Grattan-Guinness)[^o3] and *A Source Book in Mathematics* by [David Eugene Smith](wikipedia://en/3711980).[^o4]

## Algebra {#Algebra}

### [Theory of equations](wikipedia://en/583600) {#Theory_of_equations}

#### *[Baudhayana](wikipedia://en/47408155) [Sulba Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulba_Sutras)* {#Baudhayana_Sulba_Sutra}

::: {#o8 .entry cue=list-entry name="Baudhayana"}

- [Baudhayana](wikipedia://en/47408155) (8th century BCE)

:::

With linguistic evidence suggesting this text to have been written between the 8th and 5th centuries century BCE, this is one of the oldest mathematical texts. It laid the foundations of [Indian mathematics](wikipedia://en/1848052) and was influential in [South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asia). It was primarily a geometrical text and also contained some important developments, including the list of Pythagorean triples, geometric solutions of linear and quadratic equations and [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682).[^o9]

#### *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)* {#The_Nine_Chapters_on_the_Mathematical_Art}

::: {#o11 .entry cue=list-entry name="The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art"}

- *[The Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art](wikipedia://en/204207)* (10th–2nd century BCE)

:::

Contains the earliest description of [Gaussian elimination](wikipedia://en/13035) for solving system of linear equations, it also contains method for finding square root and cubic root.

#### *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)* {#Arithmetica}

::: {#o13 .entry cue=list-entry name="Diophantus"}

- [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) (3rd century CE)

:::

Contains the collection of 130 algebraic problems giving numerical solutions of determinate equations (those with a unique solution) and indeterminate equations.[^o14]

#### *[Haidao Suanjing](wikipedia://en/15419368)* {#Haidao_Suanjing}

::: {#o16 .entry cue=list-entry name="Liu Hui"}

- [Liu Hui](wikipedia://en/202719) (220-280 CE)

:::

Contains the application of right angle triangles for survey of depth or height of distant objects.

#### *[Sunzi Suanjing](wikipedia://en/30291690)* {#Sunzi_Suanjing}

- Sunzi (5th century CE)

Contains the earliest description of [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713).

#### *[Aryabhatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhatiya)* {#Aryabhatiya}

::: {#o19 .entry cue=list-entry name="Aryabhata"}

- [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204) (499 CE)

:::

The text contains 33 verses covering mensuration (kṣetra vyāvahāra), arithmetic and geometric progressions, gnomon / shadows (shanku-chhAyA), simple, quadratic, simultaneous, and indeterminate equations. It also gave the modern standard algorithm for solving first-order [diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109).

#### *[Jigu Suanjing](wikipedia://en/30257071)* {#Jigu_Suanjing}

[Jigu Suanjing](wikipedia://en/30257071) (626 CE)

This book by Tang dynasty mathematician Wang Xiaotong contains the world's earliest third order equation.

#### *[Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta)* {#Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta}

::: {#o22 .entry cue=list-entry name="Brahmagupta"}

- [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) (628 CE)

:::

Contained rules for manipulating both negative and positive numbers, rules for dealing with the number zero, a method for computing square roots, and general methods of solving linear and some quadratic equations, solution to Pell's equation. [^o23] [^o24] [^o25] [^o26]

#### *[Al-Kitāb al-mukhtaṣar fī hīsāb al-ğabr wa'l-muqābala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing)* {#Al-Kitāb_al-mukhtaṣar_fī_hīsāb_al-ğabr_wa'l-muqābala}

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Muhammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī"}

- [Muhammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Mūsā_al-Khwārizmī) (820 CE)

:::

The first book on the systematic [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) solutions of [linear](wikipedia://en/17570) and [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175) by the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) scholar [Muhammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Mūsā_al-Khwārizmī). The book is considered to be the foundation of modern [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [Islamic mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics).[^o29] The word "algebra" itself is derived from the *al-Jabr* in the title of the book.[^o30]

### *[Līlāvatī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Līlāvatī)*, *[Siddhānta Shiromani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhānta_Shiromani)* and *[Bijaganita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijaganita)* {#Līlāvatī,_Siddhānta_Shiromani_and_Bijaganita}

One of the major treatises on mathematics by [Bhāskara II](wikipedia://en/480509) provides the solution for indeterminate equations of 1st and 2nd order.

#### *[Yigu yanduan](wikipedia://en/25519521)* {#Yigu_yanduan}

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Li Ye"}

- [Li Ye](wikipedia://en/25515540) (13th century)

:::

Contains the earliest invention of 4th order polynomial equation.

#### *[Mathematical Treatise in Nine Sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Treatise_in_Nine_Sections)* {#Mathematical_Treatise_in_Nine_Sections}

::: {#o35 .entry cue=list-entry name="Qin Jiushao"}

- [Qin Jiushao](wikipedia://en/612837) (1247)

:::

This 13th-century book contains the earliest complete solution of 19th-century [Horner's method](wikipedia://en/14263) of solving high order polynomial equations (up to 10th order). It also contains a complete solution of [Chinese remainder theorem](wikipedia://en/7713), which predates [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler) and [Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss) by several centuries.

#### *[Ceyuan haijing](wikipedia://en/25519358)* {#Ceyuan_haijing}

::: {#o37 .entry cue=list-entry name="Li Zhi"}

- [Li Zhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zhi_%28mathematician%29) (1248)

:::

Contains the application of high order polynomial equation in solving complex geometry problems.

#### *[Jade Mirror of the Four Unknowns](wikipedia://en/40223285)* {#Jade_Mirror_of_the_Four_Unknowns}

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Zhu Shijie"}

- [Zhu Shijie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Shijie) (1303)

:::

Contains the method of establishing system of high order polynomial equations of up to four unknowns.

#### *[Ars Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Magna_%28Gerolamo_Cardano%29)* {#Ars_Magna}

::: {#o41 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gerolamo Cardano"}

- [Gerolamo Cardano](wikipedia://en/13145) (1545)

:::

Otherwise known as *The Great Art*, provided the first published methods for solving [cubic](wikipedia://en/180787) and [quartic equations](wikipedia://en/251591) (due to [Scipione del Ferro](wikipedia://en/4560966), [Niccolò Fontana Tartaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Fontana_Tartaglia), and [Lodovico Ferrari](wikipedia://en/18720)), and exhibited the first published calculations involving non-real [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers).[^o42][^o43]

#### *Vollständige Anleitung zur Algebra* {#Vollständige_Anleitung_zur_Algebra}

::: {#o45 .entry cue=list-entry name="Leonhard Euler"}

- [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1770)

:::

Also known as [Elements of Algebra](wikipedia://en/8181025), Euler's textbook on elementary algebra is one of the first to set out algebra in the modern form we would recognize today. The first volume deals with determinate equations, while the second part deals with [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109). The last section contains a proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) for the case *n* = 3, making some valid assumptions regarding $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ that Euler did not prove.[^o46]

#### *Demonstratio nova theorematis omnem functionem algebraicam rationalem integram unius variabilis in factores reales primi vel secundi gradus resolvi posse* {#Demonstratio_nova_theorematis_omnem_functionem_algebraicam_rationalem_integram_unius_variabilis_in_factores_reales_primi_vel_secundi_gradus_resolvi_posse}

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="Carl Friedrich Gauss"}

- [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (1799)

:::

Gauss's doctoral dissertation,[^o49] which contained a widely accepted (at the time) but incomplete proof[^o50] of the [fundamental theorem of algebra](wikipedia://en/51414).

### [Abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) {#Abstract_algebra}

#### [Group theory](wikipedia://en/19447) {#Group_theory}

##### *Réflexions sur la résolution algébrique des équations* {#Réflexions_sur_la_résolution_algébrique_des_équations}

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Joseph Louis Lagrange"}

- [Joseph Louis Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) (1770)

:::

The title means "Reflections on the algebraic solutions of equations". Made the prescient observation that the roots of the [Lagrange resolvent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrange_resolvent) of a polynomial equation are tied to permutations of the roots of the original equation, laying a more general foundation for what had previously been an ad hoc analysis and helping motivate the later development of the theory of [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), and [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). The Lagrange resolvent also introduced the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811) of order 3.

#### *Articles Publiés par Galois dans les Annales de Mathématiques* {#Articles_Publiés_par_Galois_dans_les_Annales_de_Mathématiques}

- Journal de Mathematiques pures et Appliquées, II (1846)

Posthumous publication of the mathematical manuscripts of [Évariste Galois](wikipedia://en/9815) by [Joseph Liouville](wikipedia://en/341810). Included are Galois' papers *Mémoire sur les conditions de résolubilité des équations par radicaux* and *Des équations primitives qui sont solubles par radicaux*.

#### *Traité des substitutions et des équations algébriques* {#Traité_des_substitutions_et_des_équations_algébriques}

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="Camille Jordan"}

- [Camille Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931) (1870)

:::

**Online version:** [Online version](https://books.google.com/books?id=TzQAAAAAQAAJ)

Traité des substitutions et des équations algébriques (Treatise on Substitutions and Algebraic Equations). The first book on group theory, giving a then-comprehensive study of permutation groups and Galois theory. In this book, Jordan introduced the notion of a [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) and [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) (which he called isomorphisme mériédrique),[^o58] proved part of the [Jordan–Hölder theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Hölder_theorem), and discussed matrix groups over finite fields as well as the [Jordan normal form](wikipedia://en/312877).[^o59]

#### *Theorie der Transformationsgruppen* {#Theorie_der_Transformationsgruppen}

::: {#o61 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sophus Lie"}

- [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010), [Friedrich Engel](wikipedia://en/5686283) (1888–1893).

:::

**Publication data:** 3 volumes, B.G. Teubner, Verlagsgesellschaft, mbH, Leipzig, 1888–1893. [Volume 1](https://archive.org/details/theotransformation01liesrich), [Volume 2](https://archive.org/details/theotransformation02liesrich), [Volume 3](https://archive.org/details/theotransformation03liesrich).

The first comprehensive work on [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_group), serving as the foundation for the modern theory of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

#### *Solvability of groups of odd order* {#Solvability_of_groups_of_odd_order}

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Walter Feit"}

- [Walter Feit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Feit) and [John Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Griggs_Thompson) (1960)

:::

**Description:** Gave a complete proof of the [solvability of finite groups of odd order](wikipedia://en/1656473), establishing the long-standing Burnside conjecture that all finite non-abelian simple groups are of even order. Many of the original techniques used in this paper were used in the eventual [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).

#### [Homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) {#Homological_algebra}

#### *Homological Algebra* {#Homological_Algebra}

::: {#o66 .entry cue=list-entry name="Henri Cartan"}

- [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501) (1956)

:::

Provided the first fully worked out treatment of abstract homological algebra, unifying previously disparate presentations of homology and cohomology for [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112), [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), and [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) into a single theory.

#### "[Sur Quelques Points d'Algèbre Homologique](wikipedia://en/44572732)" {#"Sur_Quelques_Points_d'Algèbre_Homologique"}

::: {#o68 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alexander Grothendieck"}

- [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) (1957)

:::

Often referred to as the "Tôhoku paper", it revolutionized [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) by introducing [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063) and providing a general framework for Cartan and Eilenberg's notion of [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875).

## [Algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) {#Algebraic_geometry}

### *Theorie der Abelschen Functionen* {#Theorie_der_Abelschen_Functionen}

::: {#o71 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernhard Riemann"}

- [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) (1857)

:::

**Publication data:** *Journal für die Reine und Angewandte Mathematik*

Developed the concept of [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181) and their topological properties beyond Riemann's 1851 thesis work, proved an index theorem for the genus (the original formulation of the [Riemann–Hurwitz formula](wikipedia://en/497413)), proved the Riemann inequality for the dimension of the space of meromorphic functions with prescribed poles (the original formulation of the [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261)), discussed birational transformations of a given curve and the dimension of the corresponding moduli space of inequivalent curves of a given genus, and solved more general inversion problems than those investigated by [Abel](wikipedia://en/21573) and [Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340). [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) once wrote that this paper "*is one of the greatest pieces of mathematics that has ever been written; there is not a single word in it that is not of consequence.*"[^o72]

### *Faisceaux Algébriques Cohérents* {#Faisceaux_Algébriques_Cohérents}

::: {#o74 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jean-Pierre Serre"}

- [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483)

:::

**Publication data:** *Annals of Mathematics*, 1955

*FAC*, as it is usually called, was foundational for the use of [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) in algebraic geometry, extending beyond the case of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742). Serre introduced [Čech cohomology](wikipedia://en/856614) of sheaves in this paper, and, despite some technical deficiencies, revolutionized formulations of algebraic geometry. For example, the [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) in sheaf cohomology allows one to show that some surjective maps of sheaves induce surjective maps on sections; specifically, these are the maps whose kernel (as a sheaf) has a vanishing first cohomology group. The dimension of a vector space of sections of a [coherent sheaf](wikipedia://en/603780) is finite, in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), and such dimensions include many discrete invariants of varieties, for example [Hodge numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodge_number). While Grothendieck's [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) cohomology has replaced Čech cohomology for technical reasons, actual calculations, such as of the cohomology of projective space, are usually carried out by Čech techniques, and for this reason Serre's paper remains important.

### *[Géométrie Algébrique et Géométrie Analytique](wikipedia://en/630505)* {#Géométrie_Algébrique_et_Géométrie_Analytique}

::: {#o76 .entry cue=list-entry name="Jean-Pierre Serre"}

- [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) (1956)

:::

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) are closely related subjects, where *analytic geometry* is the theory of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) and the more general [analytic spaces](wikipedia://en/2202) defined locally by the vanishing of [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). A (mathematical) theory of the relationship between the two was put in place during the early part of the 1950s, as part of the business of laying the foundations of algebraic geometry to include, for example, techniques from [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590). (*NB*: While [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) as use of Cartesian coordinates is also in a sense included in the scope of algebraic geometry, that is not the topic being discussed in this article.) The major paper consolidating the theory was *Géometrie Algébrique et Géométrie Analytique* by [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), now usually referred to as *GAGA*. A *GAGA-style result* would now mean any theorem of comparison, allowing passage between a category of objects from algebraic geometry, and their morphisms, and a well-defined subcategory of analytic geometry objects and holomorphic mappings.

### *Le théorème de Riemann–Roch, d'après A. Grothendieck* {#Le_théorème_de_Riemann–Roch,_d'après_A._Grothendieck}

::: {#o78 .entry cue=list-entry name="Armand Borel"}

- [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672), [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) (1958)

:::

Borel and Serre's exposition of [Grothendieck's version of the Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/1532606), published after Grothendieck made it clear that he was not interested in writing up his own result. Grothendieck reinterpreted both sides of the formula that [Hirzebruch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirzebruch) proved in 1953 in the framework of [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms) between varieties, resulting in a sweeping generalization.[^o79] In his proof, Grothendieck broke new ground with his concept of [Grothendieck groups](wikipedia://en/1634790), which led to the development of [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748).[^o80]

### *[Éléments de géométrie algébrique](wikipedia://en/471387)* {#Éléments_de_géométrie_algébrique}

::: {#o82 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alexander Grothendieck"}

- [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) (1960–1967)

:::

Written with the assistance of [Jean Dieudonné](wikipedia://en/1070084), this is [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck)'s exposition of his reworking of the foundations of algebraic geometry. It has become the most important foundational work in modern algebraic geometry. The approach expounded in EGA, as these books are known, transformed the field and led to monumental advances.

### *[Séminaire de géométrie algébrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique)* {#Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique}

::: {#o84 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alexander Grothendieck"}

- [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) et al.

:::

These seminar notes on Grothendieck's reworking of the foundations of algebraic geometry report on work done at [IHÉS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHÉS) starting in the 1960s. SGA 1 dates from the seminars of 1960–1961, and the last in the series, SGA 7, dates from 1967 to 1969. In contrast to EGA, which is intended to set foundations, SGA describes ongoing research as it unfolded in Grothendieck's seminar; as a result, it is quite difficult to read, since many of the more elementary and foundational results were relegated to EGA. One of the major results building on the results in SGA is [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544)'s proof of the last of the open [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705) in the early 1970s. Other authors who worked on one or several volumes of SGA include [Michel Raynaud](wikipedia://en/4760928), [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821), [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893), [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544), and [Nicholas Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Katz).

## [Number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) {#Number_theory}

### *[Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta)* {#Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta}

::: {#o87 .entry cue=list-entry name="Brahmagupta"}

- [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) (628)

:::

Brahmagupta's [Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta) is the first book that mentions zero as a number, hence Brahmagupta is considered the first to formulate the concept of zero. The current system of the four fundamental operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication and division) based on the Hindu-Arabic number system also first appeared in Brahmasphutasiddhanta. It was also one of the first texts to provide concrete ideas on positive and negative numbers.

### *De fractionibus continuis dissertatio* {#De_fractionibus_continuis_dissertatio}

::: {#o89 .entry cue=list-entry name="Leonhard Euler"}

- [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) (1744)

:::

First presented in 1737, this paper [^o90] provided the first then-comprehensive account of the properties of [continued fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continued_fractions). It also contains the first proof that the number [e](wikipedia://en/9633) is irrational.[^o91]

### *Recherches d'Arithmétique* {#Recherches_d'Arithmétique}

::: {#o93 .entry cue=list-entry name="Joseph Louis Lagrange"}

- [Joseph Louis Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) (1775)

:::

Developed a general theory of [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129) to handle the general problem of when an integer is representable by the form $ax^2 + by^2 + cxy$. This included a reduction theory for binary quadratic forms, where he proved that every form is equivalent to a certain canonically chosen reduced form.[^o94][^o95]

### *[Disquisitiones Arithmeticae](wikipedia://en/736713)* {#Disquisitiones_Arithmeticae}

::: {#o97 .entry cue=list-entry name="Carl Friedrich Gauss"}

- [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (1801)

:::

The *[Disquisitiones Arithmeticae](wikipedia://en/736713)* is a book on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) written by [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) and first published in 1801 when Gauss was 24. In this book Gauss brings together results in number theory obtained by mathematicians such as [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat), [Euler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler), [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) and [Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695) and adds many important new results of his own. Among his contributions was the first complete proof known of the [Fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556), the first two published proofs of the law of [quadratic reciprocity](wikipedia://en/25272), a deep investigation of [binary quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/1867129) going beyond Lagrange's work in *Recherches d'Arithmétique*, a first appearance of [Gauss sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss_sums), [cyclotomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomy#Cyclotomic_fields), and the theory of [constructible polygons](wikipedia://en/523430) with a particular application to the constructibility of the regular [17-gon](wikipedia://en/196890). Of note, in section V, article 303 of Disquisitiones, Gauss summarized his calculations of [class numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_number_%28number_theory%29) of imaginary quadratic number fields, and in fact found all imaginary quadratic number fields of class numbers 1, 2, and 3 (confirmed in 1986) as he had [conjectured](wikipedia://en/596282).[^o98] In section VII, article 358, Gauss proved what can be interpreted as the first non-trivial case of the Riemann Hypothesis for curves over finite fields (the [Hasse–Weil theorem](wikipedia://en/1108758#Hasse-Weil_Bound)).[^o99]

### "Beweis des Satzes, daß jede unbegrenzte arithmetische Progression, deren erstes Glied und Differenz ganze Zahlen ohne gemeinschaftlichen Factor sind, unendlich viele Primzahlen enthält" {#"Beweis_des_Satzes,_daß_jede_unbegrenzte_arithmetische_Progression,_deren_erstes_Glied_und_Differenz_ganze_Zahlen_ohne_gemeinschaftlichen_Factor_sind,_unendlich_viele_Primzahlen_enthält"}

::: {#o101 .entry cue=list-entry name="Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet"}

- [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) (1837)

:::

### "[Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse](wikipedia://en/536817)" {#"Über_die_Anzahl_der_Primzahlen_unter_einer_gegebenen_Grösse"}

::: {#o104 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernhard Riemann"}

- [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) (1859)

:::

"Über die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse" (or "On the Number of Primes Less Than a Given Magnitude") is a seminal 8-page paper by Bernhard Riemann published in the November 1859 edition of the *Monthly Reports of the Berlin Academy*. Although it is the only paper he ever published on number theory, it contains ideas which influenced dozens of researchers during the late 19th century and up to the present day. The paper consists primarily of definitions, heuristic arguments, sketches of proofs, and the application of powerful analytic methods; all of these have become essential concepts and tools of modern [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). It also contains the famous [Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_Hypothesis), one of the most important open problems in mathematics.[^o105]

### *[Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie](wikipedia://en/746646)* {#Vorlesungen_über_Zahlentheorie}

::: {#o107 .entry cue=list-entry name="Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet"}

:::

*[Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie](wikipedia://en/746646)* (*Lectures on Number Theory*) is a textbook of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) written by [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) mathematicians P. G. Lejeune Dirichlet and R. Dedekind, and published in 1863. The *Vorlesungen* can be seen as a watershed between the classical number theory of [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat), [Jacobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustav_Jakob_Jacobi) and [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), and the modern number theory of Dedekind, [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) and [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). Dirichlet does not explicitly recognise the concept of the [group](wikipedia://en/41890) that is central to [modern algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), but many of his proofs show an implicit understanding of group theory.

::: {#o134 .entry cue=list-entry name="Leonhard Euler"}

The eminent historian of mathematics [Carl Boyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Boyer) once called Euler's *[Introductio in analysin infinitorum](wikipedia://en/19777721)* the greatest modern textbook in mathematics.[^o135] Published in two volumes,[^o136][^o137] this book more than any other work succeeded in establishing [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) as a major branch of mathematics, with a focus and approach distinct from that used in geometry and algebra.[^o138] Notably, Euler identified functions rather than curves to be the central focus in his book.[^o139] Logarithmic, exponential, trigonometric, and transcendental functions were covered, as were expansions into partial fractions, evaluations of $\zeta(2k)$ for $k$ a positive integer between 1 and 13, infinite series and infinite product formulas,[^o135] [continued fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continued_fractions), and [partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_%28number_theory%29) of integers.[^o140] In this work, Euler proved that every rational number can be written as a finite continued fraction, that the continued fraction of an irrational number is infinite, and derived continued fraction expansions for $e$ and $\textstyle\sqrt{e}$.[^o136] This work also contains a statement of [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/9613) and a statement of the [pentagonal number theorem](wikipedia://en/340294), which he had discovered earlier and would publish a proof for in 1751.

The ***Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica*** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): "mathematical principles of natural philosophy", often *Principia* or *Principia Mathematica* for short) is a three-volume work by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) published on 5 July 1687. When he submitted Principia for publication, however, Newton chose to recast the majority of his proofs as geometric arguments.[^o146]

### Fourier analysis {#Fourier_analysis}

#### *Mémoire sur la propagation de la chaleur dans les corps solides* {#Mémoire_sur_la_propagation_de_la_chaleur_dans_les_corps_solides}

::: {#o170 .entry cue=list-entry name="Joseph Fourier"}

- [Joseph Fourier](wikipedia://en/25767401) (1807)[^o171]

:::

Introduced [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), specifically [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038). Key contribution was to not simply use [trigonometric series](wikipedia://en/4099125), but to model *all* functions by trigonometric series: $\varphi(y)=a\cos\frac{\pi y}{2}+a'\cos 3\frac{\pi y}{2}+a''\cos5\frac{\pi y}{2}+\cdots.$

Multiplying both sides by $\cos(2i+1)\frac{\pi y}{2}$, and then integrating from $y=-1$ to $y=+1$ yields:

$a_i=\int_{-1}^1\varphi(y)\cos(2i+1)\frac{\pi y}{2}\,dy.$ When Fourier submitted his paper in 1807, the committee (which included [Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange), [Laplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace), [Malus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étienne-Louis_Malus) and [Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695), among others) concluded: *...the manner in which the author arrives at these equations is not exempt of difficulties and \[...\] his analysis to integrate them still leaves something to be desired on the score of generality and even rigour*. Making Fourier series rigorous, which in detail took over a century, led directly to a number of developments in analysis, notably the rigorous statement of the integral via the [Dirichlet integral](wikipedia://en/1235977) and later the [Lebesgue integral](wikipedia://en/26064288).

#### *Sur la convergence des séries trigonométriques qui servent à représenter une fonction arbitraire entre des limites données* {#Sur_la_convergence_des_séries_trigonométriques_qui_servent_à_représenter_une_fonction_arbitraire_entre_des_limites_données}

This paper introduced the nowhere continuous [Dirichlet function](wikipedia://en/630005) and an early version of the [Riemann–Lebesgue lemma](wikipedia://en/1461209).[^o175]

## [Topology](wikipedia://en/29954) {#Topology}

**Description:** Poincaré's [Analysis Situs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analysis_Situs_%28paper%29) and his Compléments à l'Analysis Situs laid the general foundations for [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). In these papers, Poincaré introduced the notions of [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) and the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), provided an early formulation of [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), gave the [Euler–Poincaré characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler–Poincaré_characteristic) for [chain complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_complexes), and mentioned several important conjectures including the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798), demonstrated by [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) in 2003.

## [Logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) {#Logic}

### *[The Laws of Thought](wikipedia://en/1993328)* {#The_Laws_of_Thought}

::: {#o237 .entry cue=list-entry name="George Boole"}

- [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) (1854)

:::

Published in 1854, [The Laws of Thought](wikipedia://en/1993328) was the first book to provide a mathematical foundation for logic. Its aim was a complete re-expression and extension of Aristotle's logic in the language of mathematics. Boole's work founded the discipline of algebraic logic and would later be central for [Claude Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) in the development of digital logic.

### *[Begriffsschrift](wikipedia://en/900125)* {#Begriffsschrift}

::: {#o239 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gottlob Frege"}

- [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) (1879)

:::

Published in 1879, the title ***Begriffsschrift*** is usually translated as *concept writing* or *concept notation*; the full title of the book identifies it as "*a [formula](wikipedia://en/164040) [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language), modelled on that of [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), of pure [thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thought)*". Frege's motivation for developing his [formal logical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logical_system) was similar to [Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281)'s desire for a *[calculus ratiocinator](wikipedia://en/876255)*. Frege defines a logical calculus to support his research in the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358). ***Begriffsschrift*** is both the name of the book and the calculus defined therein. It was arguably the most significant publication in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) since [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308).

:::

First published in 1895, the **Formulario mathematico** was the first mathematical book written entirely in a [formalized language](wikipedia://en/10939). It contained a description of [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) and many important theorems in other branches of mathematics. Many of the notations introduced in the book are now in common use.

### *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)* {#Principia_Mathematica}

::: {#o243 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bertrand Russell"}

- [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) (1910–1913)

:::

The ***Principia Mathematica*** is a three-volume work on the foundations of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), written by [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) and published in 1910–1913. It is an attempt to derive all mathematical truths from a well-defined set of axioms and inference rules in [symbolic logic](wikipedia://en/19636). The questions remained whether a contradiction could be derived from the Principia's axioms, and whether there exists a mathematical statement which could neither be proven nor disproven in the system. These questions were settled, in a rather surprising way, by [Gödel's incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem) in 1931.

### "Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme, I" {#"Über_formal_unentscheidbare_Sätze_der_Principia_Mathematica_und_verwandter_Systeme,_I"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), **[Gödel's incompleteness theorems](wikipedia://en/58863)** are two celebrated theorems proved by [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) in 1931. The first incompleteness theorem states:

> For any formal system such that (1) it is $\omega$-consistent ([omega-consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega-consistent)), (2) it has a [recursively definable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursively_definable) set of [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms) and [rules of derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_derivation), and (3) every [recursive](wikipedia://en/1139338#Computable_sets_and_relations) relation of natural numbers is definable in it, there exists a formula of the system such that, according to the intended interpretation of the system, it expresses a truth about natural numbers and yet it is not a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of the system.

#### "Полиномиальный алгоритм в линейном программировании" {#"Полиномиальный_алгоритм_в_линейном_программировании"}

- Khachiyan, Leonid Genrikhovich. (1979). *Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR*. 244. p. 1093–1096.

Khachiyan's work on the ellipsoid method. This was the first polynomial time algorithm for linear programming.

## Early manuscripts {#Early_manuscripts}

These are publications that are not necessarily relevant to a mathematician nowadays, but are nonetheless important publications in the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).

### *[Moscow Mathematical Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Papyrus)* {#Moscow_Mathematical_Papyrus}

This is one of the earliest mathematical treatises that still survives today. The Papyrus contains 25 problems involving arithmetic, geometry, and algebra, each with a solution given. Written in Ancient Egypt at approximately 1850 BC.[^o296]

### *[Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](wikipedia://en/9210114)* {#Rhind_Mathematical_Papyrus}

::: {#o298 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ahmes"}

- [Ahmes](wikipedia://en/386234) ([scribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scribe))

:::

One of the oldest mathematical texts, dating to the [Second Intermediate Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intermediate_Period) of [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt). It was copied by the scribe [Ahmes](wikipedia://en/386234) (properly *Ahmose*) from an older [Middle Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Kingdom_of_Egypt) [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus). It laid the foundations of [Egyptian mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_mathematics) and in turn, later influenced [Greek and Hellenistic mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mathematics). Besides describing how to obtain an approximation of π only missing the mark by less than one per cent, Ahmes make use of a kind of an analogue of the [cotangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotangent).

### *[Archimedes Palimpsest](wikipedia://en/167417)* {#Archimedes_Palimpsest}

::: {#o300 .entry cue=list-entry name="Archimedes of Syracuse"}

- [Archimedes of Syracuse](wikipedia://en/1844)

:::

Although the only mathematical tools at its author's disposal were what we might now consider secondary-school [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), he used those methods with rare brilliance, explicitly using [infinitesimals](wikipedia://en/160990) to solve problems that would now be treated by integral calculus. Among those problems were that of the [center of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_gravity) of a solid hemisphere, that of the center of gravity of a frustum of a circular paraboloid, and that of the area of a region bounded by a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) and one of its secant lines. For explicit details of the method used, see [Archimedes' use of infinitesimals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes'_use_of_infinitesimals).

### *[The Sand Reckoner](wikipedia://en/276396)* {#The_Sand_Reckoner}

::: {#o302 .entry cue=list-entry name="Archimedes of Syracuse"}

- [Archimedes of Syracuse](wikipedia://en/1844)

:::

**Online version:** [Online version](https://web.archive.org/web/20120204045847/http://web.fccj.org/~ethall/archmede/sandreck.htm)

The first known (European) [system of number-naming](wikipedia://en/21170) that can be expanded beyond the needs of everyday life.

## Textbooks {#Textbooks}

### *[Abstract Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_Algebra)* {#Abstract_Algebra}

- [David Dummit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Dummit) and [Richard Foote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Foote_%28mathematician%29)

*[Dummit and Foote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dummit_and_Foote)* has become the modern dominant abstract algebra textbook following Jacobson's Basic Algebra.

### *Arithmetika Horvatzka* {#Arithmetika_Horvatzka}

- [Mihalj Šilobod Bolšić](wikipedia://en/64106864)

*Arithmetika Horvatzka* (1758) was the first Croatian language arithmetic textbook, written in the vernacular [Kajkavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajkavian) dialect of [Croatian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language). It established a complete system of [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) terminology in [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), and vividly used examples from everyday life in [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia) to present mathematical operations.[^o306] Although it was clear that Šilobod had made use of words that were in dictionaries, this was clearly insufficient for his purposes; and he made up some names by adapting [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) terminology to Kaikavian use.[^o307] Full text of [*Arithmetika Horvatszka*](https://archive.org/details/arithmetika_horvatszka_1758-sillobod_bolssich) is available via archive.org.

### *[Synopsis of Pure Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31968302)* {#Synopsis_of_Pure_Mathematics}

- [G. S. Carr](wikipedia://en/1596400)

Contains over 6000 theorems of mathematics, assembled by George Shoobridge Carr for the purpose of training his students for the Cambridge Mathematical Tripos exams. Studied extensively by [Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramanujan). [(first half here)](https://archive.org/details/asynopsiselemen00carrgoog/page/n8)

### *[Éléments de mathématique](wikipedia://en/38333812)* {#Éléments_de_mathématique}

- [Nicolas Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394)

One of the most influential books in French mathematical literature. It introduces some of the notations and definitions that are now usual (the symbol ∅ or the term bijective for example). Characterized by an extreme level of rigour, formalism and generality (up to the point of being highly criticized for that), its publication started in 1939 and is still unfinished today.

### *[Arithmetick: or, The Grounde of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetick:_or,_The_Grounde_of_Arts)* {#Arithmetick:_or,_The_Grounde_of_Arts}

- [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247)

Written in 1542, it was the first really popular arithmetic book written in the English Language.

### *[Cocker's Arithmetick](wikipedia://en/16706727)* {#Cocker's_Arithmetick}

- [Edward Cocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cocker) (authorship disputed)

Textbook of arithmetic published in 1678 by John Hawkins, who claimed to have edited manuscripts left by Edward Cocker, who had died in 1676. This influential mathematics textbook used to teach arithmetic in schools in the United Kingdom for over 150 years.

### *[The Schoolmaster's Assistant, Being a Compendium of Arithmetic both Practical and Theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Schoolmaster's_Assistant,_Being_a_Compendium_of_Arithmetic_both_Practical_and_Theoretical)* {#The_Schoolmaster's_Assistant,_Being_a_Compendium_of_Arithmetic_both_Practical_and_Theoretical}

- [Thomas Dilworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Dilworth)

An early and popular English arithmetic textbook published in [America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_America) in the 18th century. The book reached from the introductory topics to the advanced in five sections.

### *Geometry* {#Geometry}

- [Andrei Kiselyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Petrovich_Kiselyov)

**Publication data:** 1892

The most widely used and influential textbook in Russian mathematics. (See Kiselyov page.)

### *[A Course of Pure Mathematics](wikipedia://en/641270)* {#A_Course_of_Pure_Mathematics}

- [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599)

A classic textbook in introductory [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), written by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599). It was first published in 1908, and went through many editions. It was intended to help reform mathematics teaching in the UK, and more specifically in the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and in schools preparing pupils to study mathematics at Cambridge. As such, it was aimed directly at "scholarship level" students – the top 10% to 20% by ability. The book contains a large number of difficult problems. The content covers introductory [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) and the theory of [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series).

### *[Moderne Algebra](wikipedia://en/37252648)* {#Moderne_Algebra}

- [B. L. van der Waerden](wikipedia://en/91390)

The first introductory textbook (graduate level) expounding the abstract approach to algebra developed by Emil Artin and Emmy Noether. First published in German in 1931 by Springer Verlag. A later English translation was published in 1949 by [Frederick Ungar Publishing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Ungar_Publishing_Company).

### *[Algebra](wikipedia://en/80131269)* {#Algebra}

- [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) and [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916)

A definitive introductory text for abstract algebra using a [category theoretic](wikipedia://en/5869) approach. Both a rigorous introduction from first principles, and a reasonably comprehensive survey of the field.

### *Calculus, Vol. 1* {#Calculus,_Vol._1}

- [Tom M. Apostol](wikipedia://en/2196529)

### *[Algebraic Geometry](wikipedia://en/6921017)* {#Algebraic_Geometry}

- [Robin Hartshorne](wikipedia://en/5693379)

The first comprehensive introductory (graduate level) text in algebraic geometry that used the language of schemes and cohomology. Published in 1977, it lacks aspects of the scheme language which are nowadays considered central, like the [functor of points](wikipedia://en/2741037).

### *[Naive Set Theory](wikipedia://en/21986)* {#Naive_Set_Theory}

- [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598)

An undergraduate introduction to not-very-naive set theory which has lasted for decades. It is still considered by many to be the best introduction to set theory for beginners. While the title states that it is naive, which is usually taken to mean without axioms, the book does introduce all the axioms of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) and gives correct and rigorous definitions for basic objects. Where it differs from a "true" axiomatic set theory book is its character: There are no long-winded discussions of axiomatic minutiae, and there is next to nothing about topics like [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644). Instead it aims, and succeeds, in being intelligible to someone who has never thought about set theory before.

### *[Cardinal and Ordinal Numbers](wikipedia://en/65058773)* {#Cardinal_and_Ordinal_Numbers}

- [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122)

A canonical reference on transfinite numbers, first published in the early 1950s but based on the author's lectures on the subject over the preceding 40 years.

### *[Set Theory: An Introduction to Independence Proofs](wikipedia://en/2672905)* {#Set_Theory:_An_Introduction_to_Independence_Proofs}

- [Kenneth Kunen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Kunen)

A graduate textbook in axiomatic [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), first published in 1980, provides an introduction to proofs of [consistency](wikipedia://en/75802) and [independence](wikipedia://en/1141208) relative to [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC). The text covers the foundations of axiomatic set theory, combinatorial set theory, basic methods for proving consistency, and [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205).

### *Topologie* {#Topologie}

- [Pavel Sergeevich Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Sergeevich_Alexandrov)
- [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082)

First published round 1935, this text was a pioneering "reference" text book in topology, already incorporating many modern concepts from set-theoretic topology, homological algebra and homotopy theory.

### *General Topology* {#General_Topology}

- [John L. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._Kelley)

First published in 1955, for many years the only introductory graduate level textbook in the US, teaching the basics of point set, as opposed to algebraic, topology. Prior to this the material, essential for advanced study in many fields, was only available in bits and pieces from texts on other topics or journal articles.

### *Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint* {#Topology_from_the_Differentiable_Viewpoint}

- [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632)

This short book introduces the main concepts of differential topology in Milnor's lucid and concise style. While the book does not cover very much, its topics are explained beautifully in a way that illuminates all their details.

### *Number Theory, An approach through history from Hammurapi to Legendre* {#Number_Theory,_An_approach_through_history_from_Hammurapi_to_Legendre}

- [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019)

*[Number Theory: An Approach Through History from Hammurapi to Legendre](wikipedia://en/32122567)* *was first published in 1984*. The book covers over three millennia of research on the development of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) but the bulk of it is devoted to a detailed study and exposition of the work of Fermat, Euler, Lagrange, and Legendre.

### *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers* {#An_Introduction_to_the_Theory_of_Numbers}

- [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [E. M. Wright](wikipedia://en/529941)

*[An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers](wikipedia://en/31962097)* was first published in 1938, and is still in print, with the latest edition being the 6th (2008). It is likely that almost every serious student and researcher into number theory has consulted this book, and probably has it on their bookshelf. It was not intended to be a textbook, and is rather an introduction to a wide range of differing areas of number theory which would now almost certainly be covered in separate volumes. The writing style has long been regarded as exemplary, and the approach gives insight into a variety of areas without requiring much more than a good grounding in algebra, calculus and complex numbers.

## Handbooks {#Handbooks}

### *[Bronshtein and Semendyayev](wikipedia://en/49746051)* {#Bronshtein_and_Semendyayev}

::: {#o332 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein"}

- [Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein](wikipedia://en/50109073) and [Konstantin Adolfovic Semendyayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Adolfovic_Semendyayev)

:::

*[Bronshtein and Semendyayev](wikipedia://en/49746051)* is the informal name of a comprehensive handbook of fundamental working knowledge of mathematics and table of formulas originally compiled by the Russian mathematician [Ilya Nikolaevich Bronshtein](wikipedia://en/50109073) and engineer [Konstantin Adolfovic Semendyayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Adolfovic_Semendyayev). The work was first published in 1945 in Russia and soon became a "standard" and frequently used guide for scientists, engineers, and technical university students. It has been translated into German, English, and many other languages. The latest edition was published in 2015 by [Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Publishing).

### *[CRC Standard Mathematical Tables](wikipedia://en/77583596)* {#CRC_Standard_Mathematical_Tables}

- Editors-in-chief: Charles D. Hodgman (14th edition and earlier); Samuel M. Selby (15th–23rd editions); William H. Beyer (24th–29th editions); Daniel Zwillinger (30th–33rd editions)

*[CRC Standard Mathematical Tables](wikipedia://en/77583596)* is a comprehensive one-volume handbook of fundamental working knowledge of mathematics and table of formulas. The handbook was originally published in 1928. The latest edition was published in 2018 by [CRC Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRC_Press), with [Daniel Zwillinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Zwillinger) as the editor-in-chief.

## Popular writings {#Popular_writings}

### *Gödel, Escher, Bach* {#Gödel,_Escher,_Bach}

::: {#o336 .entry cue=list-entry name="Douglas Hofstadter"}

- [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758)

:::

*[Gödel, Escher, Bach](wikipedia://en/48103): an Eternal Golden Braid* is a Pulitzer Prize-winning book, first published in 1979 by Basic Books. It is a book about how the creative achievements of logician Kurt Gödel, artist M. C. Escher and composer Johann Sebastian Bach interweave. As the author states: "I realized that to me, Gödel and Escher and Bach were only shadows cast in different directions by some central solid essence. I tried to reconstruct the central object, and came up with this book."

### *The World of Mathematics* {#The_World_of_Mathematics}

::: {#o338 .entry cue=list-entry name="James R. Newman"}

- [James R. Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Newman)

:::

*[The World of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_of_Mathematics)* was specially designed to make mathematics more accessible to the inexperienced. It comprises nontechnical essays on every aspect of the vast subject, including articles by and about scores of eminent mathematicians, as well as literary figures, economists, biologists, and many other eminent thinkers. Includes the work of Archimedes, Galileo, Descartes, Newton, Gregor Mendel, Edmund Halley, Jonathan Swift, John Maynard Keynes, Henri Poincaré, Lewis Carroll, George Boole, Bertrand Russell, Alfred North Whitehead, John von Neumann, and many others. In addition, an informative commentary by distinguished scholar James R. Newman precedes each essay or group of essays, explaining their relevance and context in the history and development of mathematics. Originally published in 1956, it does not include many of the exciting discoveries of the later years of the 20th century but it has no equal as a general historical survey of important topics and applications.
