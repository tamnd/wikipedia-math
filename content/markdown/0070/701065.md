# Luigi Bianchi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Luigi Bianchi"}

**Luigi Bianchi** (18 January 1856 – 6 June 1928) was an [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma), [Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia-Romagna), and died in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa). He was a leading member of the vigorous [geometric school which flourished in Italy](wikipedia://en/383424) during the later years of the 19th century and the early years of the twentieth century.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Like his friend and colleague [Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro](wikipedia://en/499223), Bianchi studied at the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) under [Enrico Betti](wikipedia://en/979158), a leading [differential geometer](wikipedia://en/8625) who is today best remembered for his seminal contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), and [Ulisse Dini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulisse_Dini), a leading expert on [function theory](wikipedia://en/26478). Bianchi was also greatly influenced by the geometrical ideas of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) and by the work on [transformation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformation_groups) of [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887). Bianchi became a professor at the [Scuola Normale Superiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore) in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) in 1896, where he spent the remainder of his career. At Pisa, his colleagues included the talented [Ricci](wikipedia://en/499223). In 1890, Bianchi and Dini supervised the dissertation of the noted analyst and geometer [Guido Fubini](wikipedia://en/349051).

In 1898, Bianchi worked out the [Bianchi classification](wikipedia://en/7811800) of nine possible [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) classes of three-dimensional [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) of [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) of a (sufficiently symmetric) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652). As Bianchi knew, this is essentially the same thing as classifying, up to [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828), the three-dimensional real [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). This complements the earlier work of [Lie himself](wikipedia://en/54010), who had earlier classified the *complex* Lie algebras.

Through the influence of [Luther P. Eisenhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_P._Eisenhart) and [Abraham Haskel Taub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Haskel_Taub), Bianchi's classification later came to play an important role in the development of the theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). Bianchi's list of nine isometry classes, which can be regarded as Lie algebras, Lie groups, or as three-dimensional homogeneous (possibly nonisotropic) Riemannian manifolds, are now often called collectively the [Bianchi groups](wikipedia://en/3238478).

In 1902, Bianchi rediscovered[^o4] what are now called the [Bianchi identities](wikipedia://en/489440#Bianchi_identities) for the [Riemann tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_tensor), which play an even more important role in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024). (They are essential for understanding the [Einstein field equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_field_equation).) According to [Tullio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), these identities had first been discovered by Ricci in about 1889, but Ricci apparently forgot all about the matter, which led to Bianchi's rediscovery.[^o5] However, the [contracted Bianchi identities](wikipedia://en/57574496), which are sufficient for the proof that the divergence of the [Einstein tensor](wikipedia://en/1057638) always vanishes, had been published by [Aurel Voss](wikipedia://en/56692263) in 1880.[^o6]
