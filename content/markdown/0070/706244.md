# Conical surface

*Image: Elliptical Cone Quadric.Png.* An elliptic cone, a special case of a conical surface, shown truncated for simplicity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conical surface"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **conical surface** is an [unbounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unbounded_set) [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853) formed from the union of infinite [lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29) that pass through a fixed point and a [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A (*general*) conical surface is the unbounded surface formed by the union of all the straight lines that pass through a fixed point — the *apex* or *vertex* — and any point of some fixed [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) — the *directrix* — that does not contain the apex. Each of those lines is called a *generatrix* of the surface. The directrix is often taken as a [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691), in a plane not containing the apex, but this is not a requirement.[^o4]

In general, a conical surface consists of two congruent unbounded halves joined by the apex. Each half is called a **nappe**, and is the union of all the [rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29#Ray) that start at the apex and pass through a point of some fixed space curve.[^o5] Sometimes the term "conical surface" is used to mean just one nappe.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

If the directrix is a circle $C$, and the apex is located on the circle's *axis* (the line that contains the center of $C$ and is perpendicular to its plane), one obtains the *right circular conical surface* or [double cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_cone_%28geometry%29).[^o5] More generally, when the directrix $C$ is an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), or any [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673), and the apex is an arbitrary point not on the plane of $C$, one obtains an [elliptic cone](wikipedia://en/66221593).[^o9]

:::

## Equations {#Equations}

A conical surface $S$ can be described [parametrically](wikipedia://en/453198) as

$$S(t,u) = v + u q(t),$$

where $v$ is the apex and $q$ is the directrix.[^o11]

## Related surface {#Related_surface}

Conical surfaces are [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158), surfaces that have a straight line through each of their points.[^o13] Patches of conical surfaces that avoid the apex are special cases of [developable surfaces](wikipedia://en/2878111), surfaces that can be unfolded to a flat plane without stretching. When the directrix has the property that the angle it subtends from the apex is exactly $2\pi$, then each nappe of the conical surface, including the apex, is a developable surface.[^o14]

A [cylindrical surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylindrical_surface) can be viewed as a [limiting case](wikipedia://en/40400729) of a conical surface whose apex is moved off to infinity in a particular direction. Indeed, in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) a cylindrical surface is just a special case of a conical surface.[^o15]
