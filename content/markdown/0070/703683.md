# David Gregory (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David Gregory"}

**David Gregory** (originally spelt **Gregorie**) [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (3 June 1659[^o3] – 10 October 1708) was a Scottish mathematician and astronomer. He was professor of mathematics at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and later [Savilian Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Astronomy) at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and a proponent of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)'s *[Principia](wikipedia://en/48781)*.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: Gregory, David – Astronomiae physicae et geometricae elementa, 1726 – BEIC 1496003.jpg.* *Astronomiae physicae et geometricae elementa*, 1726

The fourth of the fifteen children of [David Gregorie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gregory_%28physician%29), a doctor from Kinnairdy, Banffshire, and Jean Walker of Orchiston, David was born in Upper Kirkgate, Aberdeen. The nephew of astronomer and mathematician [James Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gregory_%28astronomer_and_mathematician%29), David, like his influential uncle before him, studied at [Aberdeen Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Grammar_School) and [Marischal College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marischal_College) ([University of Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen)), from 1671 to 1675. The Gregorys were [Jacobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobitism) and left Scotland to escape religious discrimination. Young David visited several countries on the continent, including the Netherlands (where he began studying medicine at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University)) and France, and did not return to Scotland until 1683.

On 28 November 1683, Gregory graduated M.A. at [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh), and in October 1683 he became Chair of Mathematics at University of Edinburgh. He was "the first to openly teach the doctrines of the *Principia*, in a public seminary...in those days this was a daring innovation."[^o5]

Gregory decided to leave for England where, in 1691, he was elected [Savilian Professor of Astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savilian_Professor_of_Astronomy) at the University of Oxford, due in large part to the influence of [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627). The same year he was elected to be a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society). In 1692, he was elected a Fellow of [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford).

Gregory spent several days with [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627) in 1694, discussing revisions for a second edition of Newton's [Principia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_system_of_the_world). Gregory made notes of these discussions, but the second edition of 1713 was not due to Gregory.[^o6]

In 1695 he published *Catoptricae et dioptricae sphaericae elementa* which addressed [chromatic aberration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_aberration) and the possibility of its correction with [achromatic lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achromatic_lens).

In 1705 Gregory became an Honorary Fellow of the [Royal College of Physicians of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Physicians_of_Edinburgh). At the [Union of 1707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_1707), he was given the responsibility of re-organising the [Scottish Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Mint). He was an uncle of philosopher [Thomas Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Reid).

Gregory and his wife, Elizabeth Oliphant, had nine children, but seven died while still children.

On his death in [Maidenhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidenhead), Berkshire he was buried in Maidenhead churchyard.
