# Ribet's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ribet's theorem"}

**Ribet's theorem** (earlier called the **epsilon conjecture** or **ε-conjecture**) is part of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). It concerns properties of [Galois representations](wikipedia://en/486929) associated with [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000). It was proposed by [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) by [Ken Ribet](wikipedia://en/30876850). The proof was a significant step towards the proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) (FLT). As shown by Serre and Ribet, the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) (whose status was unresolved at the time) and the epsilon conjecture together imply that FLT is true.

:::

In mathematical terms, Ribet's theorem shows that if the Galois representation associated with an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) has certain properties, then that curve cannot be modular (in the sense that there cannot exist a modular form that gives rise to the same representation).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $f$ be a weight 2 [newform](wikipedia://en/7483985) on $\Gamma_{0}(qN)$ – i.e. of level $qN$ where $q$ does not divide $N$ – with absolutely irreducible 2-dimensional mod $p$ Galois representation $\rho_{f,p}$ unramified at $q$ if $q \ne p$ and finite flat at $q = p$. Then there exists a weight 2 newform $g$ of level $N$ such that

$$\rho_{f,p} \simeq \rho_{g,p}.$$

In particular, if $E$ is an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) over $\mathbb{Q}$ with [conductor](wikipedia://en/43604164) $qN$, then the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) guarantees that there exists a weight 2 newform $f$ of level $qN$ such that the 2-dimensional mod $p$ Galois representation $\rho_{f, p}$ of $f$ is isomorphic to the 2-dimensional mod $p$ Galois representation $\rho_{E, p}$ of $E$. To apply Ribet's Theorem to $\rho_{E, p}$, it suffices to check the irreducibility and ramification of $\rho_{E, p}$. Using the theory of the [Tate curve](wikipedia://en/35189927), one can prove that $\rho_{E, p}$ is unramified at $q \ne p$ and finite flat at $q = p$ if $p$ divides the power to which $q$ appears in the minimal discriminant $\Delta_{E}$. Then Ribet's theorem implies that there exists a weight 2 newform $g$ of level $N$ such that $\rho_{g, p} \approx \rho_{E, p}$.

:::

## Level lowering {#Level_lowering}

Ribet's theorem states that beginning with an elliptic curve $E$ of conductor $qN$ does not guarantee the existence of an elliptic curve $E$ of level $N$ such that $\rho_{E, p} \approx \rho_{E, p}$. The newform $g$ of level $N$ may not have rational [Fourier](wikipedia://en/59038) coefficients, and hence may be associated to a higher-dimensional [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152), not an elliptic curve. For example, elliptic curve 4171a1 in the Cremona database given by the equation

$$E: y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - 663204x + 206441595$$

with conductor $43 \times 97$ and discriminant $43^{7} \times 97^{3}$ does not level-lower mod 7 to an elliptic curve of conductor 97. Rather, the mod $p$ Galois representation is isomorphic to the mod $p$ Galois representation of an irrational newform $g$ of level 97.

However, for $p$ large enough compared to the level $N$ of the level-lowered newform, a rational newform (e.g. an elliptic curve) must level-lower to another rational newform (e.g. elliptic curve). In particular for $p \gg N^{N^{1+\varepsilon}}$, the mod $p$ Galois representation of a rational newform cannot be isomorphic to an irrational newform of level $N$.[^o6]

Similarly, the Frey-[Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) conjecture predicts that for large enough $p$ (independent of the conductor $N$), elliptic curves with isomorphic mod $p$ Galois representations are in fact [isogenous](wikipedia://en/1085176), and hence have the same conductor. Thus non-trivial level-lowering between rational newforms is not predicted to occur for large $p (p > 17)$.

## History {#History}

In his thesis, Yves Hellegouarch originated the idea of associating solutions (*a*,*b*,*c*) of Fermat's equation with a different mathematical object: an elliptic curve.[^o8] If *p* is an odd prime and *a*, *b*, and *c* are positive integers such that

$$a^p + b^p = c^p,$$

then a corresponding [Frey curve](wikipedia://en/5249765) is an algebraic curve given by the equation

$$y^2 = x(x - a^p)(x + b^p).$$

This is a nonsingular algebraic curve of genus one defined over $\mathbb{Q}$, and its projective completion is an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$.

In 1982 [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312) called attention to the unusual properties of the same curve, now called a [Frey curve](wikipedia://en/5249765).[^o9] This provided a bridge between [Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966) and [Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843) by showing that a counterexample to FLT would create a curve that would not be modular. The conjecture attracted considerable interest when Frey suggested that the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture implies FLT. However, his argument was not complete.[^o10] In 1985 [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) proposed that a Frey curve could not be modular and provided a partial proof.[^o11][^o12] This showed that a proof of the semistable case of the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture would imply FLT. Serre did not provide a complete proof and the missing bit became known as the epsilon conjecture or ε-conjecture. In the summer of 1986, [Kenneth Alan Ribet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Alan_Ribet) proved the epsilon conjecture, thereby proving that the [Modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) implied FLT.[^o13]

The origin of the name is from the ε part of "Taniyama-Shimura conjecture + ε ⇒ Fermat's last theorem".

## Implications {#Implications}

Suppose that the Fermat equation with exponent $p \ge 5$[^o13] had a solution in non-zero integers $a, b, c$. The corresponding Frey curve $E_{a^{p},b^{p},c^{p}}$ is an elliptic curve whose [minimal discriminant](wikipedia://en/55607) $\Delta$ is equal to $2^{-8} (abc)^{2p}$ and whose conductor $N$ is the [radical](wikipedia://en/1634886) of $abc$, i.e. the product of all distinct primes dividing $abc$. An elementary consideration of the equation $a^{p} + b^{p} = c^{p}$, makes it clear that one of $a, b, c$ is even and hence so is *N*. By the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture, $E$ is a modular elliptic curve. Since all odd primes dividing $a, b, c$ in $N$ appear to a $p\operatorname{th}$ power in the minimal discriminant $\Delta$, by Ribet's theorem repetitive [level](wikipedia://en/7483985) [descent](wikipedia://en/361449) modulo $p$ strips all odd primes from the conductor. However, no newforms of level 2 remain because the genus of the modular curve $X_{0}(2)$ is zero (and newforms of level *N* are differentials on $X_{0}(N))$.
