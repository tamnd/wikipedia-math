# Most-perfect magic square

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Most-perfect magic square"}

A **most-perfect magic square** of order *n* is a [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) containing the numbers 1 to *n*<sup>2</sup> with two additional properties:

:::

1. Each 2 × 2 subsquare sums to 2*s*, where $s = n^{2} + 1$.
1. All pairs of integers distant *n*/2 along a (major) diagonal sum to *s*.

There are 384 such combinations.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All most-perfect magic squares are [panmagic squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panmagic_square).

Apart from the trivial case of the first order square, most-perfect magic squares are all of order 4*n*. In their book, [Kathleen Ollerenshaw](wikipedia://en/872857) and [David S. Brée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_S._Brée) give a method of construction and enumeration of all most-perfect magic squares. They also show that there is a [one-to-one correspondence](wikipedia://en/3942) between [reversible squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reversible_square) and most-perfect magic squares.

For *n* = 36, there are about 2.7 × 10<sup>44</sup> [essentially different](wikipedia://en/706510) most-perfect magic squares.
