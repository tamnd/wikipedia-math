# Subcoloring

*Image: Subcoloring graph.svg.* A non-optimal subcoloring with four colors. Merging the red and blue colors, and the green and yellow colors, produces a subcoloring with only two colors.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subcoloring"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **subcoloring** is an assignment of [colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color) to a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806)'s [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) such that each color class [induces](wikipedia://en/1655188) a vertex disjoint union of [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466). That is, each color class should form a [cluster graph](wikipedia://en/50931136).

:::

The **subchromatic number** $\chi_S(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the fewest colors needed in any subcoloring of $G$.

Subcoloring and subchromatic number were introduced by Michael O. Albertson, Robert E. Jamison, [Stephen T. Hedetniemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_T._Hedetniemi), and Stephen C. Locke in 1989.

Every [proper coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) and [cocoloring](wikipedia://en/702837) of a graph are also subcolorings, so the subchromatic number of any graph is at most equal to the cochromatic number, which is at most equal to the chromatic number.

Subcoloring is as difficult to solve exactly as coloring, in the sense that (like coloring) it is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete). More specifically, the problem of determining whether a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) has subchromatic number at most 2 is NP-complete, even if it is a

- [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996) graph with maximum [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 4,
- [comparability graph](wikipedia://en/6978672) with maximum degree 4,
- [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of a [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) with maximum degree 4,
- or a graph with [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 5.

The subchromatic number of a [cograph](wikipedia://en/1175666) can be computed in polynomial time. For every fixed integer $r$, it is possible to decide in polynomial time whether the subchromatic number of [interval](wikipedia://en/363423) and [permutation](wikipedia://en/17625375) graphs is at most $r$.
