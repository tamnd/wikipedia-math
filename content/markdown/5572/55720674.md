# Rachel Justine Pries

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Rachel Justine Pries"}

**Rachel Justine Pries** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) whose research focuses on [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). She is a professor at [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University) and both a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o3] and a Fellow of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o17]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Pries was a student at the [Cambridge Rindge and Latin School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Rindge_and_Latin_School), in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts).[^o5] She received a B.S. degree from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in [Providence, Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence,_Rhode_Island) in 1994,[^o6] and received a Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania) in 2000 under the supervision of [David Harbater](wikipedia://en/3521313).

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After her doctoral studies, Pries was appointed a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) VIGRE post-doctoral fellow at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) for 2000 to 2003. After her post-doc at Columbia, Rachel joined the faculty at [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University), [Fort Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Collins), [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado), where she is currently a full professor.[^o9]

In one of her most cited works, *Families of wildly ramified covers of curves*,[^o10] Pries studied smooth Galois covers of curves, ramified over only one point. In a second highly cited paper, *Hyperelliptic curves with prescribed p-torsion*,[^o11] Pries and co-author Darren Glass, proved several results regarding the existence of Jacobian varieties having interesting *p*-torsion as measured in terms of invariants such as the *p*-rank and the *a*-number.

Pries serves on the Steering Committee of Women in Number Theory (WIN),[^o12] a research collaboration community for women mathematicians interested in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). She was an editor of Directions in Number Theory: Proceedings of the 2014 WIN3 Workshop (Association for Women in Mathematics Series), which was published by Springer Verlag in 2016.[^o13]

## Honors {#Honors}

Pries was elected to the 2018 class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). Her citation read "for contributions to arithmetic geometry, and for service to the mathematical community." Pries was selected as the inaugural lecturer in the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) Distinguished Lecture Series at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon), [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon), in 2013.[^o15] In 2004, Pries was selected as Outstanding Professor in Graduate Instruction by the mathematics graduate students of Colorado State University [^o16] Pries was elected to the 2023 class of fellows of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) "for supporting the research careers of women through mentorship and advocacy; for her vision and hard work establishing the Women in Numbers workshops and research network; and for broadening the participation of women in mathematics through service and leadership both at her institution and in high-profile national and international programs."[^o17]
