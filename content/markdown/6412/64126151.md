# Sequentially complete

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sequentially complete"}

In mathematics, specifically in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a subspace $S$ of a [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339) $X$ is said to be **sequentially complete** or **semi-complete** if every [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085) in $S$ converges to an element in $S$. $X$ is called **sequentially complete** if it is a sequentially complete subset of itself.

:::

## Sequentially complete topological vector spaces {#Sequentially_complete_topological_vector_spaces}

Every [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) is a [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339) so the notion of sequential completeness can be applied to them.

### Properties of sequentially complete topological vector spaces {#Properties_of_sequentially_complete_topological_vector_spaces}

1. A bounded sequentially complete [disk](wikipedia://en/1734292) in a Hausdorff topological vector space is a [Banach disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_disk).[^n1]
1. A Hausdorff locally convex space that is sequentially complete and [bornological](wikipedia://en/1804457) is [ultrabornological](wikipedia://en/63904912).[^n2]

## Examples and sufficient conditions {#Examples_and_sufficient_conditions}

1. Every [complete space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) is sequentially complete but not conversely.
1. For metrizable spaces, sequential completeness implies completeness. Together with the previous property, this means sequential completeness and completeness are equivalent over metrizable spaces.
1. Every [complete topological vector space](wikipedia://en/53695856) is [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) and every quasi-complete topological vector space is sequentially complete.[^n3]
