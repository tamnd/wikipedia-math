# Stochastic cellular automaton

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stochastic cellular automaton"}

A **stochastic cellular automaton** (**SCA**), also known as a **probabilistic cellular automaton** (**PCA**), is a type of [computational model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_model). It consists of a grid of cells, where each cell has a particular state (e.g., "on" or "off"). The states of all cells evolve in discrete time steps according to a set of rules.

:::

Unlike a standard [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342) where the rules are deterministic (fixed), the rules in a stochastic cellular automaton are [probabilistic](wikipedia://en/292222). This means a cell's next state is determined by chance, according to a set of probabilities that depend on the states of neighboring cells.[^o2]

Despite the simple, local, and random nature of the rules, these models can produce complex global patterns through processes like [emergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergence) and [self-organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-organization). They are used to model a wide variety of real-world phenomena where randomness is a factor, such as the spread of [forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildfire), the dynamics of disease epidemics, or the simulation of [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism) in physics (see [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744)).

As a mathematical object, a stochastic cellular automaton is a discrete-time [random dynamical system](wikipedia://en/7185428). It is often analyzed within the frameworks of [interacting particle systems](wikipedia://en/37809826) and [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876), where it may be called a system of **locally interacting Markov chains**.[^o3][^o4] See [^o5] for a more detailed introduction.

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

From the perspective of [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a stochastic cellular automaton is a discrete-time [Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process). The configuration of all cells at a given time is a state $\eta$ in a [product space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_space) $E=\prod_{k \in G} S_k$. Here, $G$ is a graph representing the grid of cells (e.g., $\mathbb{Z}^d$), and each $S_k$ is the finite set of possible states for the cell $k$ (e.g., $S_k = \{0, 1\}$).

The transition probability, which defines the dynamics, has a product form:

$$P(d\sigma | \eta) = \bigotimes_{k \in G} p_k(d\sigma_k | \eta)$$

where $\sigma$ is the next configuration and $p_k(d\sigma_k | \eta)$ is a probability distribution on $S_k$.

Locality is a key requirement, meaning the probability of a cell $k$ changing its state depends only on the states of its neighbors. This is expressed as $p_k(d\sigma_k | \eta) = p_k(d\sigma_k | \eta_{V_k})$, where $V_k$ is a finite neighborhood of cell $k$ and $\eta_{V_k}$ are the states of the cells in that neighborhood. See [^o8] for a more detailed introduction from this point of view.

:::

## Examples of stochastic cellular automaton {#Examples_of_stochastic_cellular_automaton}

### Majority cellular automaton {#Majority_cellular_automaton}

There is a version of the [majority cellular automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_problem_%28cellular_automaton%29) with probabilistic updating rules. See the [Toom's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toom's_rule).

### Relation to lattice random fields {#Relation_to_lattice_random_fields}

PCA may be used to simulate the [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744) of [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism) in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481).[^o2] Some categories of models were studied from a statistical mechanics point of view.

### Cellular Potts model {#Cellular_Potts_model}

There is a strong connection[^o14] between probabilistic cellular automata and the [cellular Potts model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_Potts_model) in particular when it is implemented in parallel.

### Non Markovian generalization {#Non_Markovian_generalization}

The [Galves–Löcherbach model](wikipedia://en/50799933) is an example of a generalized PCA with a non Markovian aspect.
