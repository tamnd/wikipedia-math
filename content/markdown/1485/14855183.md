::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Prismatic compound of antiprisms"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **prismatic compound of antiprism** is a category of [uniform polyhedron compound](wikipedia://en/8606809). Each member of this infinite family of uniform polyhedron compounds is a symmetric arrangement of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) sharing a common axis of rotational symmetry.

:::

## Infinite family {#Infinite_family}

This infinite family can be enumerated as follows:

- For each positive integer *n* ≥ 1 and for each rational number *p*/*q* > 3/2 (expressed with *p* and *q* [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime)), there occurs the compound of *n* *p*/*q*-gonal antiprisms, with symmetry group:
  - **D<sub>*np*d</sub>** if *nq* is odd
  - **D<sub>*np*h</sub>** if *nq* is even

When *p*/*q* = 2, or equivalently p = 2, q = 1, the component is the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) (or dyadic antiprism). In this case, if *n* = 2 then the compound is the [stella octangula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_octangula), with higher symmetry (**O<sub>h</sub>**).

## Compounds of two antiprisms {#Compounds_of_two_antiprisms}

Compounds of two *n*-antiprisms share their vertices with a 2*n*-[prism](wikipedia://en/283007), and exist as two [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) set of vertices.

[Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) for the vertices of an antiprism with *n*-gonal bases and isosceles triangles are

- $$\left( \cos\frac{k\pi}{n}, \sin\frac{k\pi}{n}, (-1)^k h \right)$$
- $$\left( \cos\frac{k\pi}{n}, \sin\frac{k\pi}{n}, (-1)^{k+1} h \right)$$

with *k* ranging from 0 to 2*n*−1; if the triangles are equilateral,

$$2h^2=\cos\frac{\pi}{n}-\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}.$$
