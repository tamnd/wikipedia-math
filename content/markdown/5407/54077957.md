# Kenneth M. Golden

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Kenneth M. Golden"}

**Kenneth Morgan Golden** (born September 30, 1958) is an American applied mathematician and Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah), where he is also an adjunct professor of Biomedical Engineering. He works on modeling complex materials with a focus on [sea ice.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice) Golden has been on nineteen expeditions to study the physics and biology of sea ice in the Arctic and Antarctic.

:::

## Education and training {#Education_and_training}

Golden's interest in sea ice began in high school, studying satellite images of Antarctic sea ice with Jay Zwally at NASA's [Goddard Space Flight Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddard_Space_Flight_Center).[^o4] While majoring in math and physics at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College), he worked at the US Army [Cold Regions Research and Engineering Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Regions_Research_and_Engineering_Laboratory) from 1977 to 1980 with Steve Ackley on measuring sea ice thickness with radar, treating sea ice as a composite of pure ice with brine inclusions. This work led to his 1984 Ph.D. from [NYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) in Mathematics with [George Papanicolaou](wikipedia://en/24000364) on the transport properties of composite materials.

Golden studied diffusion processes and quasiperiodic media as a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) Mathematical Sciences Postdoctoral Fellow with [Joel Lebowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Lebowitz) at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University). As an assistant professor of Mathematics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) from 1987 to 1991, Golden continued working in mathematical physics, and then moved to the University of Utah as an associate professor in 1991.[^o5]

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

As a material, sea ice exhibits complex composite structure on many length scales.[^o7] Golden's interests range from the brine microstructure to ice pack dynamics on the scale of the [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean), and from sea ice algae to polar bears.[^o8] For example, the [fluid permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeability_%28materials_science%29) of sea ice, which depends on the porous microstructure, regulates the evolution of [melt ponds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melt_pond) on the surface of Arctic sea ice as well as fluxes of nutrients that fuel [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_algae) blooms. Golden and colleagues found that sea ice has a *percolation threshold*,[^o9] a critical [porosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porosity) or temperature that must be exceeded for brine pathways to form and fluid to flow, and used [percolation theory](wikipedia://en/187805) to accurately predict the permeability.[^o10] The melt ponds determine sea ice reflectance or [albedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo), a key parameter in climate modeling. As the ponds grow and complexify, they undergo a transition in [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) geometry.[^o11] The 100 year old [Ising model](wikipedia://en/292744), originally developed to understand magnetic materials, was adapted to accurately predict melt pond geometry.[^o12] These works are improving projections of Earth's sea ice covers and the ecosystems they support,[^o13] and represent principal examples of how statistical physics is contributing to sea ice modeling and prediction.[^o14]

Golden is interested not only in how the physics of sea ice affects the life it hosts, but in how the presence of life impacts sea ice. For example, sea ice algae secrete [exopolymeric substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_polymeric_substance) to help them live in their extreme surroundings. But this alters the brine microstructure[^o15] and the fluid permeability.[^o16] On the other hand, sea ice is locally highly variable. Parameters in models of algal dynamics must be treated as random variables. Such models have been solved using methods of [uncertainty quantification](wikipedia://en/5987648).[^o17]

Sea ice microstructure shares similarities with many natural and artificial materials. Modeling sea ice often advances other fields. Examples of this *cross pollination* include developing new materials with exotic properties such as twisted bilayer composites,[^o18] a theory of electromagnetic transport in polycrystalline media,[^o19] and a novel way of monitoring osteoporosis.[^o20]

During his years at the University of Utah, Golden has led several federally funded, multi-disciplinary, multi-institution projects, as well as successful efforts to hire faculty working in the mathematics of materials and related areas.[^o8] He has mentored over 100 young researchers, from high school and undergraduate to Ph.D. and postdoctoral, with many assisting in field experiments in [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica) and the [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic).[^o21] Golden has given over 500 invited lectures on six continents, including four presentations to the U.S. Congress, and won awards for teaching, mentoring, and science communication.

## Media and movies {#Media_and_movies}

Golden's research and public lectures have been covered by media around the world, including [Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Magazine),[^o23] [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American),[^o24] [Physics Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_Today),[^o25] [Popular Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mechanics),[^o26] and the [BBC.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News)[^o27] He's been interviewed on radio, television, and online, and featured in short films produced by the [NSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation),[^o28][^o29] the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376),[^o30] [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News), and the University of Utah.[^o31]

In a [San Diego Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Tribune) article[^o32] about Golden's Porter Public Lectures at the 2013 Joint Math Meetings, Golden was given the nickname "mathematical Indiana Jones," which has also appeared in various other articles about his career.[^o33]
