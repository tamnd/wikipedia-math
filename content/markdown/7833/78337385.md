# Garry Tee

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Garry Tee"}

**Garry John Tee** (28 March 1932 – 18 February 2024) was a New Zealand mathematician and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Garry John Tee was born in [Whanganui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whanganui) on 28 March 1932.[^o4][^o5] Tee attended [Seddon Memorial Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_University_of_Technology) (now Auckland University of Technology). In 1954, he was awarded a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) with First Class Honours at the [Auckland University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland) (now the University of Auckland).[^o4][^o5][^o6] Following graduating, Tee worked as a computer in Australia for an oil prospecting team.[^o7]

In 1958, Tee was a mathematician at the [English Electric Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Electric_Company), contributing to the programming of the [DEUCE computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEUCE_computer).[^o5] In 1964, Tee helped establish the Department of Mathematics at the [University of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lancaster) and spent 1965 as a [visiting scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_scholar) at the Department of Computer Science, [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University).[^o4][^o5] By 1968, Tee had returned to the Department of Mathematics at the University of Auckland, playing a key role in founding its Department of Computer Science.[^o4][^o8] Starting in 1969, Tee was also an active member of the Auckland University Underwater Club.[^o4][^o9]

In 1971, Tee pursued further studies under [Richard Bellman](wikipedia://en/730173) at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California).[^o5] However, Bellman's illness and subsequent death led Tee to return to Auckland without completing his doctorate.[^o5] In 2003, Tee received an honorary doctorate from the [Auckland University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_University_of_Technology).[^o4]

Tee died in [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland) on 18 February 2024.[^o4]

## Publications {#Publications}

Tee published widely on numerical analysis, [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), early women in mathematics and computing, and history of mathematics, computer science, and science more broadly.[^o4][^o11] Publications include:

- Tee, Garry J. "A novel finite-difference approximation to the biharmonic operator." *The Computer Journal* 6, no. 2 (1963): 177–192.
- Tee, Garry J. "Evidence for the Chinese Origin of the Jaguar Motif in Chavin Art".[^o12] *Asian Perspectives* 21, no. 1 (1978): 27–29.
- Tee, Garry J. "The Heritage of Charles Babbage in Australasia." *Annals of the History of Computing* 5, no. 1 (1983): 45–60.
- Tee, Garry J. "A Calendar of the Correspondence of Charles Darwin, 1821–1882." *Journal of the Royal Society of New Zealand* 15, no. 3 (1985): 341–343.
- Tee, Garry J. "Mathematics in the Pacific Basin".[^o13] *The British Journal for the History of Science* 21, no. 4 (1988): 401–417.
- Tee, Garry J. "A Note on Bechmann's Approximate Construction of π, Suggested by a Deleted Sketch in Villard de Honnecourt's Manuscript.[^o14]" *The British Journal for the History of Science* 22, no. 2 (1989): 241–242.
- Tee, Garry J. "Prime powers of zeros of monic polynomials with integer coefficients." *The Fibonacci Quarterly* 32, no. 3 (1994): 277–283.
- Tee, Garry J. "Relics of Davy and Faraday in New Zealand".[^o15] *Notes and Records of the Royal Society of London* 52, no. 1 (1998): 93–102.
- Tee, Garry J. "Math Bite: Further Generalizations of a Curiosity That Feynman Remembered All His Life.[^o16]" *Mathematics Magazine* 72, no. 1 (1999): 44.
- Tee, Garry J. "Eigenvectors of block circulant and alternating circulant matrices." *New Zealand Journal of Mathematics* 36, no. 8 (2007): 195–211.
