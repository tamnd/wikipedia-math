# Absorption (logic)

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Absorption"}

If $P$ implies $Q$, then $P$ implies $P$ and $Q$.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Absorption"}

**Absorption** is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [argument form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_form) and [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) of [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154).[^o3][^o4] The rule states that if $P$ implies $Q$, then $P$ implies $P$ and $Q$. The rule makes it possible to introduce [conjunctions](wikipedia://en/18152) to [proofs](wikipedia://en/1197184). It is called the law of absorption because the term $Q$ is "absorbed" by the term $P$ in the [consequent](wikipedia://en/804778).[^o5] The rule can be stated:

:::

$$\frac{P \to Q}{\therefore P \to (P \land Q)}$$

where the rule is that wherever an instance of "$P \to Q$" appears on a line of a proof, "$P \to (P \land Q)$" can be placed on a subsequent line.

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

The *absorption* rule may be expressed as a [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355):

$$P \to Q \vdash P \to (P \land Q)$$

where $\vdash$ is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) symbol meaning that $P \to (P \land Q)$ is a [syntactic consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624#Syntactic_consequence) of $(P \rightarrow Q)$ in some [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102);

and expressed as a truth-functional [tautology](wikipedia://en/4495335) or [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) of [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). The principle was stated as a theorem of propositional logic by [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163) and [Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) in *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)* as:

$$(P \to Q) \leftrightarrow (P \to (P \land Q))$$

where $P$, and $Q$ are propositions expressed in some [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).

## Formal proof {#Formal_proof}

::: {#o13 .table cue=table}

| *Proposition* | *Derivation* |
|---|---|
| $P\rightarrow Q$ | Given |
| $\neg P\lor Q$ | [Material implication](wikipedia://en/244516) |
| $\neg P\lor P$ | [Law of Excluded Middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Excluded_Middle) |
| $(\neg P\lor P)\land (\neg P\lor Q)$ | [Conjunction](wikipedia://en/6563) |
