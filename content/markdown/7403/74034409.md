# Argüman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Argüman"}

**Argüman** is a [free and open source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open_source_software) for collective [structured argumentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_argumentation) and argument analysis via argumentation graphs or [argument maps](wikipedia://en/6190251) in which the type of connections can be specified.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] It allows users to create collaborative "semantic maps" of arguments in well structured [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) formats and share them with an audience and potential participants.[^o9][^o5][^o10] Arguman.org was an open structured social debate platform that implemented the software.[^o11] It is down as of 2023. There also is a mobile version of the tool. The project was started, in 2014, and largely built by developers in Turkey.[^o3]

:::

*Image: Gource visualization video of Argüman development, an argument map software (sped-up).webm.* [Gource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gource) visualization video of the development of Argüman.

Some studies used or investigated excerpts of argumentations on the platform.[^o12][^o13] Unlike the larger and functional alternative [Kialo](wikipedia://en/74010797), which is structured using only 'Pro' and 'Con' relations,[^o4] argüman arguments are structured by three types of premises – 'because', 'but', and 'however'.[^o5] As of the latest version, debates are presented in their entirety as a large tree which may be harder to navigate than other formats – for instance, trees "can become extremely dense, and the interface does not make it obvious which arguments the user should pay attention to".[^o4] Users can also flag arguments for [fallacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacies). Arguman.org also had a Turkish-language subdomain.[^o14]

A researcher suggested the concept of the [Semantic Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_Web)-interoperability could be useful for argumentative structures on the Web, going beyond the conventional flat structures of discussions and lack of characterizations of their components as implemented in argüman.[^o15] There is research into how to automatically use these collaborative argumentation graphs, which is a "very active" topic in [Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence).[^o6] There also is research into applying conclusion-making methods to the debates or their data, such as bipolar weighted argumentation frameworks – this could be a way to find out what the current conclusion of debates like "[Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) is not actually [a science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branches_of_science)" is.[^o16] A study suggests it could be useful for the development of critical thinking skills.[^o17]
