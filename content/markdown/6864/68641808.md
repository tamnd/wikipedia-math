# Extension complexity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Extension complexity"}

In [convex geometry](wikipedia://en/4165882) and [polyhedral combinatorics](wikipedia://en/19696519), the **extension complexity** of a [convex polytope](wikipedia://en/1817885) $P$ is the smallest number of [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) among convex polytopes $Q$ that have $P$ as a projection. In this context, $Q$ is called an **extended formulation** of $P$; it may have much higher dimension than $P$.

:::

The extension complexity depends on the precise shape of $P$, not just on its combinatorial structure. For instance, [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) with $n$ sides have extension complexity $O(\log n)$ (expressed using [big O notation](wikipedia://en/44578)), but some other convex $n$-gons have extension complexity at least proportional to $\sqrt{n}$.

If a polytope describing the feasible solutions to a [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555) problem has low extension complexity, this could potentially be used to devise efficient algorithms for the problem, using [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) on its extended formulation. For this reason, researchers have studied the extension complexity of the polytopes arising in this way. For instance, it is known that the [matching polytope](wikipedia://en/64572404) has exponential extension complexity. On the other hand, the [independence polytope](wikipedia://en/38726681) of [regular matroids](wikipedia://en/13415343) has polynomial extension complexity.

The notion of extension complexity has also been generalized from linear programming to [semidefinite programming](wikipedia://en/4993539), by considering projections of [spectrahedra](wikipedia://en/28733878) in place of projections of polytopes.
