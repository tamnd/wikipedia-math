# Thomas Jones Enright

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas Jones Enright"}

**Thomas Jones Enright** (August 15, 1947 – January 27, 2019) was an American mathematician known for his work in the algebraic theory of [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of real [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Enright received a B.S. from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1969 and a Ph.D. in 1973 from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) under the direction of Ramesh A. Gangolli. From 1973 to 1975 he was the Hedrick Assistant Professor in [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) working with [Veeravalli S. Varadarajan](wikipedia://en/33119851), and spent the 1976-1977 year after in the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) at Princeton, N. J. before starting at [University of California at San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) in 1977. He was chair of the mathematics department of UCSD from 1986 to 1990.[^o4] In 2010 he retired due to symptoms of Parkinson's disease.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In the mid-1970s, Enright introduced new methods that led him to an algebraic way of looking at [discrete series](wikipedia://en/7205312) (which were fundamental representations constructed by [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190) in the early 1960s), and to an algebraic proof of the [Blattner multiplicity formula](wikipedia://en/2964127).

He was known for Enright–Varadarajan modules,[^o6][^o7] Enright resolutions, and the Enright completion [functor](wikipedia://en/10987),[^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11] which has had a lasting influence in algebra.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

- Recipient of [Alfred P. Sloan Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Fellowship), 1978[^o13]
- Enright's work was the subject of a [Bourbaki Seminar](wikipedia://en/2904840) by [Michel Duflo](wikipedia://en/36324838)[^o14]
