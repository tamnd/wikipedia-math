# Lothar Collatz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lothar Collatz"}

**Lothar Collatz** (; July 6, 1910 – September 26, 1990) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

The "3*x* + 1" problem is also known as the [Collatz conjecture](wikipedia://en/37895) and remains unsolved. The Collatz–Wielandt formula for the [Perron–Frobenius eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/1540333) of a positive square matrix was also named after him.

Collatz's 1957 paper with Ulrich Sinogowitz,[^o3] who had been killed in the [bombing of Darmstadt in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Darmstadt_in_World_War_II),[^o4] founded the field of [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914).

## Biography {#Biography}

Collatz studied at universities in Germany including the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald) and the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin), where he was supervised by [Alfred Klose](wikipedia://en/71876602), receiving his doctorate in 1935 for a dissertation entitled *Das Differenzenverfahren mit höherer Approximation für lineare Differentialgleichungen* (The finite difference method with higher approximation for linear differential equations). He then worked as an assistant at the University of Berlin, before moving to the *Technische Hochschule Karlsruhe* (now [Karlsruhe Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology)) in 1935 where he remained through 1937. From 1938 to 1943, he worked as a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in Karlsruhe. In the war years he worked with [Alwin Walther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alwin_Walther) at the Institute for Practical Mathematics of the [Technische Hochschule Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Darmstadt).[^o6]

From 1943 to 1952, Collatz held a chair at the [Technische Hochschule Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hochschule_Hannover) (now [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover)). From 1952 until his retirement in 1978, Collatz worked at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg), where he founded the Institute of Applied Mathematics in 1953. After retirement as professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus), he continued to be very active at mathematical conferences.

For his many contributions to the field, Collatz had many honors bestowed upon him in his lifetime, including:

- election to the [Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina), the [Academy of Sciences of the Institute of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Institute_of_Bologna), and the Academy at Modena in Italy
- honorary member of the [Hamburg Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Mathematical_Society)
- honorary degrees from the [University of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_São_Paulo), the [Vienna University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_University_of_Technology), the [University of Dundee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dundee) in [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), [Brunel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunel_University) in England, the [University of Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hannover) in 1981, and the [Technische Universität Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Dresden).

He died unexpectedly from a heart attack in [Varna, Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria), while attending a mathematics conference.[^o7]
