# Rademacher distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rademacher distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Rademacher distribution** (which is named after [Hans Rademacher](wikipedia://en/1864916)) is a [discrete probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_probability_distribution) where a [random variate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variate) *X* has a 50% chance of being +1 and a 50% chance of being −1.[^o2]

:::

A [series](wikipedia://en/15287) (that is, a sum) of Rademacher distributed variables can be regarded as a simple symmetrical [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) where the step size is 1.

## Mathematical formulation {#Mathematical_formulation}

The [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) of this distribution is

$$f(k) = \left\{\begin{matrix} 1/2 & \text{if }k=-1, \\ 1/2 & \text{if }k=+1, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.$$

## Bounds on sums of independent Rademacher variables {#Bounds_on_sums_of_independent_Rademacher_variables}

There are various results in probability theory around analyzing the sum of [i.i.d.](wikipedia://en/30876902) Rademacher variables, including [concentration inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_inequalities) such as [Bernstein inequalities](wikipedia://en/9075610) as well as [anti-concentration inequalities](wikipedia://en/32023963#Anti-concentration_inequalities) like Tomaszewski's conjecture.

### Concentration inequalities {#Concentration_inequalities}

Let {*x<sub>i</sub>*} be a set of random variables with a Rademacher distribution. Let {*a<sub>i</sub>*} be a sequence of real numbers. Then

$$\Pr\left( \sum_i x_i a_i > t \| a \|_2 \right) \le e^{ -t^2/2 }$$

where ||*a*||<sub>2</sub> is the [Euclidean norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of the sequence {*a*<sub>i</sub>}, *t* > 0 is a real number and Pr(*Z*) is the probability of event *Z*.[^o6]

Let *Y* = Σ *x<sub>i</sub>a<sub>i</sub>* and let *Y* be an almost surely convergent [series](wikipedia://en/15287) in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). The for *t* > 0 and *s* ≥ 1 we have[^o7]

$$\Pr\left( \| Y \| > st \right) \le \left[ \frac{ 1 }{ c } \Pr( \| Y \| > t ) \right]^{ cs^2 }$$

for some constant *c*.

Let *p* be a positive real number. Then the [Khintchine inequality](wikipedia://en/10002357) says that[^o8]

$$c_1 \left[ \sum \left| a_i \right|^2 \right]^\frac{ 1 }{ 2 } \le \left( E\left[ \left| \sum a_i x_i \right|^p \right] \right)^{ \frac{ 1 }{ p } } \le c_2 \left[ \sum \left| a_i \right|^2 \right]^\frac{ 1 }{ 2 }$$

where *c*<sub>1</sub> and *c*<sub>2</sub> are constants dependent only on *p*.

For *p* ≥ 1, $c_2 \le c_1 \sqrt{ p }.$

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Tomaszewski’s conjecture"}

### Tomaszewski’s conjecture {#Tomaszewski’s_conjecture}

In 1986, Bogusław Tomaszewski proposed a question about the distribution of the sum of independent Rademacher variables. A series of works on this question[^o11][^o12] culminated in a proof in 2020 by Nathan Keller and Ohad Klein of the following conjecture.[^o13]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=inline-head name="Conjecture"}

**Conjecture.** Let $X = \sum_{i=1}^n a_i X_i$, where $a_1^2 + \cdots + a_n^2 = 1$ and the $X_i$'s are independent Rademacher variables. Then

$$\Pr[|X| \leq 1] \geq 1/2.$$

For example, when $a_1 = a_2 = \cdots = a_n = 1/\sqrt{n}$, one gets the following bound, first shown by Van Zuijlen.[^o15]

$$\Pr \left( \left| \frac{ \sum_{i = 1}^n X_i } \sqrt n \right| \le 1 \right) \ge 0.5.$$

The bound is sharp and better than that which can be derived from the normal distribution (approximately Pr > 0.31).

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The Rademacher distribution has been used in [bootstrapping](wikipedia://en/6885770). See Chapter 17 of Testing Statistical Hypotheses for example.[^o18] The distribution is particularly useful in high-dimensional statistics.[^o19]

The Rademacher distribution can be used to show that [normally distributed and uncorrelated does not imply independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normally_distributed_and_uncorrelated_does_not_imply_independent).

Random vectors with components sampled independently from the Rademacher distribution are useful for various [stochastic approximations](wikipedia://en/8979437), for example:

- The [Hutchinson trace estimator](wikipedia://en/43270#Stochastic_estimator),[^o20] which can be used to efficiently approximate the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) of which the elements are not directly accessible, but rather implicitly defined via matrix-vector products.
- [SPSA](wikipedia://en/24464148), a computationally cheap, derivative-free, stochastic gradient approximation, useful for [numerical optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_optimization).

Rademacher random variables are used in the [Symmetrization Inequality](wikipedia://en/305843#Symmetrization).

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- [Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/199189): If *X* has a Rademacher distribution, then $(X+1)/2$ has a Bernoulli(1/2) distribution.
- [Laplace distribution](wikipedia://en/1106213): If *X* has a Rademacher distribution and *Y* ~ Exp(*λ*) is independent from *X*, then *XY* ~ Laplace(0, 1/*λ*).
