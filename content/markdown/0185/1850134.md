# Picard group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Picard group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Picard group** of a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) *X*, denoted by Pic(*X*), is the group of [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) classes of [invertible sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_sheaves) (or [line bundles](wikipedia://en/361366)) on *X*, with the [group operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_operation) being [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844). This construction is a global version of the construction of the divisor class group, or [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907), and is much used in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and the theory of [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742).

:::

Alternatively, the Picard group can be defined as the [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) group

$$H^1 (X, \mathcal{O}_X^{*}).\,$$

For integral [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) the Picard group is isomorphic to the class group of [Cartier divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartier_divisor). For complex manifolds the [exponential sheaf sequence](wikipedia://en/1881339) gives basic information on the Picard group.

The name is in honour of [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000)'s theories, in particular of divisors on [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The Picard group of the [spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291) of a [Dedekind domain](wikipedia://en/145132) is its *[ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907)*.
- The invertible sheaves on [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) **P**<sup>*n*</sup>(*k*) for *k* a [field](wikipedia://en/10603), are the [twisting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twisting_sheaf) [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) $\mathcal{O}(m),\,$ so the Picard group of **P**<sup>*n*</sup>(*k*) is isomorphic to **Z**.
- The Picard group of the affine line with two origins over *k* is isomorphic to **Z**.
- The Picard group of the $n$-dimensional [complex affine space](wikipedia://en/4850001): $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb{C}^n)=0$, indeed the [exponential sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_sequence) yields the following long exact sequence in cohomology

$$\dots\to H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^n}) \to H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\mathcal{O}^\star_{\mathbb{C}^n})\to H^2(\mathbb{C}^n,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\to\cdots$$

and since $H^k(\mathbb{C}^n,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq H_{\scriptscriptstyle\rm sing}^k(\mathbb{C}^n;\mathbb{Z})$[^o4] we have $H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq H^2(\mathbb{C}^n,\underline{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq 0$ because $\mathbb{C}^n$ is contractible, then $H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^n}) \simeq H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\mathcal{O}^\star_{\mathbb{C}^n})$ and we can apply the [Dolbeault isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3189601#Dolbeault's_theorem) to calculate $H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^n})\simeq H^1(\mathbb{C}^n,\Omega^0_{\mathbb{C}^n})\simeq H^{0,1}_{\bar{\partial}}(\mathbb{C}^n)=0$ by the [Dolbeault–Grothendieck lemma](wikipedia://en/3189601#Dolbeault–Grothendieck_lemmata).

:::

## Picard scheme {#Picard_scheme}

The construction of a scheme structure on (the [representable functor](wikipedia://en/844461) version of) the Picard group, the **Picard scheme**, is an important step in algebraic geometry, in particular in the [duality theory of abelian varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duality_theory_of_abelian_varieties). It was constructed by (Grothendieck 1962), and also described by (Mumford 1966) and (Kleiman 2005).

In the cases of most importance to classical algebraic geometry, for a [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities) [complete variety](wikipedia://en/1047627) *V* over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) zero, the [connected component](wikipedia://en/6233) of the identity in the Picard scheme is an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) called the **Picard variety** and denoted Pic<sup>0</sup>(*V*). The dual of the Picard variety is the [Albanese variety](wikipedia://en/1814828), and in the particular case where *V* is a curve, the Picard variety is [naturally isomorphic](wikipedia://en/45022) to the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) of *V*. For fields of positive characteristic however, [Igusa](wikipedia://en/33588064) constructed an example of a smooth projective surface *S* with Pic<sup>0</sup>(*S*) non-reduced, and hence not an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152).

The quotient Pic(*V*)/Pic<sup>0</sup>(*V*) is a [finitely-generated abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely-generated_abelian_group) denoted NS(*V*), the **[Néron&ndash;Severi group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Néron&ndash;Severi_group)** of *V*. In other words, the Picard group fits into an [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215)

$$1\to \mathrm{Pic}^0(V)\to\mathrm{Pic}(V)\to \mathrm{NS}(V)\to 1.\,$$

The fact that the rank of NS(*V*) is finite is [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716)'s **theorem of the base**; the rank is the **Picard number** of *V*, often denoted ρ(*V*). Geometrically NS(*V*) describes the [algebraic equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_equivalence) classes of [divisors](wikipedia://en/1693055) on *V*; that is, using a stronger, non-linear [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) in place of [linear equivalence of divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_equivalence_of_divisors), the classification becomes amenable to discrete invariants. Algebraic equivalence is closely related to [numerical equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_equivalence), an essentially topological classification by [intersection numbers](wikipedia://en/384327).

## Relative Picard scheme {#Relative_Picard_scheme}

Let *f*: *X* →*S* be a [morphism of schemes](wikipedia://en/7482797). The **relative Picard functor** (or **relative Picard scheme** if it is a scheme) is given by:[^o11] for any *S*-scheme *T*,

$$\operatorname{Pic}_{X/S}(T) = \operatorname{Pic}(X_T)/f_T^*(\operatorname{Pic}(T))$$

where $f_T: X_T \to T$ is the base change of *f* and *f*<sub>*T*</sub> <sup>\*</sup> is the pullback.

We say an *L* in $\operatorname{Pic}_{X/S}(T)$ has degree *r* if for any geometric point *s* → *T* the pullback $s^*L$ of *L* along *s* has degree *r* as an invertible sheaf over the fiber *X*<sub>*s*</sub> (when the degree is defined for the Picard group of *X*<sub>*s*</sub>.)

:::
