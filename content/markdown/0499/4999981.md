# Cantor cube

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cantor cube"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Cantor cube** is a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) of the form {0, 1}<sup>*A*</sup> for some index set *A*. Its algebraic and topological structures are the [group direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_direct_product) and [product topology](wikipedia://en/24910) over the [cyclic group of order 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_group_of_order_2) (which is itself given the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology)).

:::

If *A* is a [countably infinite set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite_set), the corresponding Cantor cube is a [Cantor space](wikipedia://en/352376). Cantor cubes are special among [compact groups](wikipedia://en/750326) because every compact group is a continuous image of one, although usually not a homomorphic image. (The literature can be unclear, so for safety, assume all spaces are [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637).)

Topologically, any Cantor cube is:

- [homogeneous](wikipedia://en/363325);
- [compact](wikipedia://en/6042);
- [zero-dimensional](wikipedia://en/517682);
- AE(0), an [absolute extensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_extensor) for compact zero-dimensional spaces. (Every map from a closed subset of such a space into a Cantor cube extends to the whole space.)

By a theorem of Schepin, these four properties characterize Cantor cubes; any space satisfying the properties is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to a Cantor cube.

In fact, every AE(0) space is the [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of a Cantor cube, and with some effort one can prove that every [compact group](wikipedia://en/750326) is AE(0). It follows that every zero-dimensional compact group is homeomorphic to a Cantor cube, and every compact group is a continuous image of a Cantor cube.
