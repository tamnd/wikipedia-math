# Ideal point

*Image: Ideal circles.svg.* Three [ideal triangles](wikipedia://en/5532715) in the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836); the [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are ideal points

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ideal point"}

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), an **ideal point**, **omega point**[^o2] or **point at infinity** is a [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) point outside the hyperbolic plane or space. Given a line *l* and a point *P* not on *l*, right- and left-[limiting parallels](wikipedia://en/31547181) to *l* through *P* [converge](wikipedia://en/1743431) to *l* at *ideal points*.

:::

Unlike the projective case, ideal points form a [boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifold_with_boundary), not a [submanifold](wikipedia://en/525095). So, these lines do not intersect at an ideal point and such points, although well-defined, do not belong to the hyperbolic space itself.

The ideal points together form the [Cayley absolute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_absolute) or boundary of a [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291). For instance, the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) forms the Cayley absolute of the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) and the [Klein disk model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_disk_model). The real line forms the Cayley absolute of the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942).[^o3]

[Pasch's axiom](wikipedia://en/5751973) and the [exterior angle theorem](wikipedia://en/19298354) still hold for an omega triangle, defined by two points in hyperbolic space and an omega point.[^o4]

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The hyperbolic distance between an ideal point and any other point or ideal point is infinite.
- The centres of [horocycles](wikipedia://en/11084460) and [horoballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoball) are ideal points; two [horocycles](wikipedia://en/11084460) are [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric) when they have the same centre.

:::

## Polygons with ideal vertices {#Polygons_with_ideal_vertices}

### Ideal triangles {#Ideal_triangles}

if all vertices of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/1283865) are ideal points the triangle is an [ideal triangle](wikipedia://en/5532715).

Some properties of ideal triangles include:

- All ideal triangles are congruent.
- The interior angles of an ideal triangle are all zero.
- Any ideal triangle has an infinite perimeter.
- Any ideal triangle has area $-\pi / K$ where K is the (always negative) curvature of the plane.[^o9]

### Ideal quadrilaterals {#Ideal_quadrilaterals}

if all vertices of a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) are ideal points, the quadrilateral is an ideal quadrilateral.

While all ideal triangles are congruent, not all convex ideal quadrilaterals are. They can vary from each other, for instance, in the angle at which their two diagonals cross each other. Nevertheless all convex ideal quadrilaterals have certain properties in common:

- The interior angles of a convex ideal quadrilateral are all zero.
- Any convex ideal quadrilateral has an infinite perimeter.
- Any convex ideal quadrilateral has area $-2 \pi / K$ where K is the (always negative) curvature of the plane.

### Ideal square {#Ideal_square}

The ideal quadrilateral where the two diagonals are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to each other form an ideal square.

It was used by [Ferdinand Karl Schweikart](wikipedia://en/58742625) in his memorandum on what he called "astral geometry", one of the first publications acknowledging the possibility of [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291).[^o12]

### Ideal *n*-gons {#Ideal_n-gons}

An ideal *n*-gon can be subdivided into (*n* − 2) ideal triangles, with area (*n* − 2) times the area of an ideal triangle.

## Representations in models of hyperbolic geometry {#Representations_in_models_of_hyperbolic_geometry}

In the [Klein disk model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_disk_model) and the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) of the hyperbolic plane the ideal points are on the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) (hyperbolic plane) or [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) (higher dimensions) which is the unreachable boundary of the hyperbolic plane.

When projecting the same hyperbolic line to the [Klein disk model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_disk_model) and the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) both lines go through the same two ideal points (the ideal points in both models are on the same spot).

### Klein disk model {#Klein_disk_model}

Given two distinct points *p* and *q* in the open [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738) the unique straight line connecting them intersects the unit circle in two ideal points, *a* and *b*, labeled so that the points are, in order, *a*, *p*, *q*, *b* so that |aq| > |ap| and |pb| > |qb|. Then the hyperbolic distance between *p* and *q* is expressed as

$$d(p,q) = \frac{1}{2} \log \frac{ \left| qa \right| \left| bp \right| }{ \left| pa \right| \left| bq \right| } ,$$

### Poincaré disk model {#Poincaré_disk_model}

Given two distinct points *p* and *q* in the open unit disk then the unique circle [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometry%29) orthogonal to the boundary connecting them intersects the unit circle in two ideal points, *a* and *b*, labeled so that the points are, in order, *a*, *p*, *q*, *b* so that |aq| > |ap| and |pb| > |qb|. Then the hyperbolic distance between *p* and *q* is expressed as

$$d(p,q) = \log \frac{ \left| qa \right| \left| bp \right| }{ \left| pa \right| \left| bq \right| } ,$$

Where the distances are measured along the (straight line) segments aq, ap, pb and qb.

### Poincaré half-plane model {#Poincaré_half-plane_model}

In the [Poincaré half-plane model](wikipedia://en/461942) the ideal points are the points on the boundary axis. There is also another ideal point that is not represented in the half-plane model (but rays parallel to the positive y-axis approach it).

### Hyperboloid model {#Hyperboloid_model}

In the [hyperboloid model](wikipedia://en/4771189) there are no ideal points.
