# Frederic Fitch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Frederic Fitch"}

**Frederic Brenton Fitch** (September 9, 1908 – September 18, 1987) was an American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), a [Sterling Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Professor) at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).[^o3]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

At Yale, Fitch earned his B.A in 1931 and his Ph.D. in 1934 under the supervision of [F. S. C. Northrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._S._C._Northrop). From 1934 to 1937 Fitch was a postdoc at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia). In 1937 he returned to Yale, where he taught until his retirement in 1977.[^o6]

His doctoral students include [Alan Ross Anderson](wikipedia://en/3793208), [Ruth Barcan Marcus](wikipedia://en/369743), and [William W. Tait](wikipedia://en/17002765).

## Work {#Work}

Fitch was the [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor) of the [Fitch notation](wikipedia://en/7316682) for arranging formal logical proofs as diagrams.[^o8] In his 1963 published paper "A Logical Analysis of Some Value Concepts" he proves "Theorem 5" (originally by [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731)), which later became famous in context of the [knowability paradox](wikipedia://en/1150215).[^o9]

Fitch worked primarily in [combinatory logic](wikipedia://en/149848), authoring an undergraduate-level textbook on the subject (1974), but he also made significant contributions to [intuitionism](wikipedia://en/19513) and [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365). He was interested in the problem of the consistency, completeness, categoricity, and constructivity of logical theories, especially [non-classical logics](wikipedia://en/3192556), and contributed to the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358) and to [inductive probability](wikipedia://en/42579971). He dealt with the [theory of references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_reference) in "The Problem of the Morning Star and the Evening Star" (1949).[^o6]

He also contributed to the philosophy of how logic relates to language.[^o6]
