# Genocchi number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Genocchi number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Genocchi numbers** G<sub>*n*</sub>, named after [Angelo Genocchi](wikipedia://en/9134562), are a [sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28mathematics%29) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) that satisfy the relation

:::

$$\frac{2t}{1+e^{t}}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty G_n\frac{t^n}{n!}$$

The first few Genocchi numbers are 0, 1, −1, 0, 1, 0, −3, 0, 17 , see .

$G_n=2(1-2^n)(-n\zeta(1-n)),$[^o2]

where $\zeta(1-n)$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) definition of the Genocchi numbers implies that they are [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024). In fact, G<sub>*2n+1*</sub> = 0 for *n* ≥ 1 and (−1)<sup>*n*</sup>G<sub>*2n*</sub> is an [odd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_number) positive integer.
- Genocchi numbers *G*<sub>*n*</sub> are related to [Bernoulli numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_numbers) *B*<sub>*n*</sub> by the formula

$$G_{n}=2 \,(1-2^n) \,B_n.$$

:::

## Combinatorial interpretations {#Combinatorial_interpretations}

The [exponential generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_generating_function) for the **signed even Genocchi numbers** (−1)<sup>*n*</sup>G<sub>*2n*</sub> is

$$t\tan \left(\frac{t}{2} \right)=\sum_{n\geq 1} (-1)^n G_{2n}\frac{t^{2n}}{(2n)!}$$

They enumerate the following objects:

- [Permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) in *S*<sub>2*n*−1</sub> with [descents](wikipedia://en/44027#Descents) after the even numbers and [ascents](wikipedia://en/44027#Descents) after the odd numbers.
- Permutations *π* in *S*<sub>2*n*−2</sub> with 1 ≤ *π*(2*i*−1) ≤ 2*n*−2*i* and 2*n*−2*i* ≤ *π*(2*i*) ≤ 2*n*−2.
- Pairs (*a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*−1</sub>) and (*b*<sub>1</sub>,...,*b*<sub>*n*−1</sub>) such that *a*<sub>*i*</sub> and *b*<sub>*i*</sub> are between 1 and *i* and every *k* between 1 and *n*−1 occurs at least once among the *a*<sub>*i*</sub>'s and *b*<sub>*i*</sub>'s.
- Reverse [alternating permutations](wikipedia://en/4813617) *a*<sub>1</sub> \< *a*<sub>2</sub> > *a*<sub>3</sub> \< *a*<sub>4</sub> >...>*a*<sub>2*n*−1</sub> of \[2*n*−1\] whose [inversion table](wikipedia://en/44027#Numbering_permutations) has only even entries.

## Primes {#Primes}

The only known prime numbers which occur in the Genocchi sequence are 17, at n = 8, and −3, at n = 6 (depending on how primes are defined). It has been proven that no other primes occur in the sequence
