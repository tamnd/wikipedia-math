# Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially in the fields of [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635), [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Lyndon spectral sequence** or **Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence** is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) relating the [group cohomology](wikipedia://en/248635) of a normal subgroup *N* and the quotient group *G*/*N* to the [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) of the total group *G*. The spectral sequence is named after [Roger Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Lyndon), [Gerhard Hochschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Hochschild), and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $G$ be a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) and $N$ be a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918). The latter ensures that the [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) $G/N$ is a group, as well. Finally, let $A$ be a [$G$-module](wikipedia://en/26068776). Then there is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894) of cohomological type

$$H^p(G/N,H^q(N,A)) \Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(G,A)$$

and there is a [spectral sequence of homological type](wikipedia://en/803894#homological_spectral_sequence)

$$H_p(G/N,H_q(N,A)) \Longrightarrow H_{p+q}(G,A),$$

where the arrow '$\Longrightarrow$' means [convergence of spectral sequences](wikipedia://en/803894#Terms_of_convergence).

The same statement holds if $G$ is a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266), $N$ is a *closed* normal subgroup and $H^*$ denotes the continuous cohomology.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Homology of the Heisenberg group {#Homology_of_the_Heisenberg_group}

The spectral sequence can be used to compute the homology of the [Heisenberg group](wikipedia://en/448518) *G* with integral entries, i.e., matrices of the form

$$\left ( \begin{array}{ccc} 1 & a & c \\ 0 & 1 & b \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \right ), \ a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}.$$

This group is a [central extension](wikipedia://en/345807#Central_extension)

$$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to G \to \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} \to 0$$

with [center](wikipedia://en/7125) $\mathbb{Z}$ corresponding to the subgroup with $a=b=0$. The spectral sequence for the group homology, together with the analysis of a differential in this spectral sequence, shows that[^o7]

$$H_i (G, \mathbb{Z}) = \left \{ \begin{array}{cc} \mathbb{Z} & i=0, 3 \\ \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z} & i=1,2 \\ 0 & i>3. \end{array} \right.$$

### Cohomology of wreath products {#Cohomology_of_wreath_products}

For a group *G*, the [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) is an extension

$$1 \to G^p \to G \wr \mathbb{Z} / p \to \mathbb{Z} / p \to 1.$$

The resulting spectral sequence of group cohomology with coefficients in a field *k*,

$$H^r(\mathbb{Z}/p, H^s(G^p, k)) \Rightarrow H^{r+s}(G \wr \mathbb{Z}/p, k),$$

is known to degenerate at the $E_2$-page.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The associated [five-term exact sequence](wikipedia://en/15130142) to the group cohomology is the usual [inflation-restriction exact sequence](wikipedia://en/31364787):

$$0 \to H^1(G/N,A^N) \xrightarrow{\text{inf}} H^1(G,A) \xrightarrow{\text{res}} H^1(N,A)^{G/N} \xrightarrow{d} H^2(G/N,A^N) \xrightarrow{\text{inf}} H^2(G,A).$$

The associated [five-term exact sequence](wikipedia://en/15130142) to the group homology is the coinflation-corestriction exact sequence:[^o12]

$$H_2(G, A) \xrightarrow{\text{coinf}} H_2(G/N, A_N) \xrightarrow{d} H_1(N, A)_{G/N} \xrightarrow{\text{cor}} H_1(G, A) \xrightarrow{\text{coinf}} H_1(G/N, A_N) \to 0.$$

:::

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The spectral sequence is an instance of the more general [Grothendieck spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3017363) of the composition of two derived functors. Indeed, $H^{*}(G,-)$ is the [derived functor](wikipedia://en/433875) of $(-)^G$ (i.e., taking *G*-invariants) and the composition of the functors $(-)^N$ and $(-)^{G/N}$ is exactly $(-)^G$.

A similar spectral sequence exists for group homology, as opposed to group cohomology.[^o15]

A similar spectral sequence exists for Lie algebras. [^o16]

:::
