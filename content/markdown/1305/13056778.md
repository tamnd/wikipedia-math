# Vietoris–Rips complex

*Image: VR complex.svg.* A Vietoris–Rips complex of a set of 23 points in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410). This complex has sets of up to four points: the points themselves (shown as red circles), pairs of points (black edges), triples of points (pale blue triangles), and quadruples of points (dark blue tetrahedrons).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vietoris–Rips complex"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **Vietoris–Rips complex**, also called the **Vietoris complex** or **Rips complex**, is a way of forming a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) from distances in a set of points. It is an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) that can be defined from any [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) *M* and distance δ by forming a [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) for every [finite set](wikipedia://en/11742) of points that has [diameter](wikipedia://en/68283333) no more than δ. That is, it is a family of finite subsets of *M*, in which we think of a subset of *k* points as forming a (*k* − 1)-dimensional simplex (an edge for two points, a triangle for three points, a tetrahedron for four points, etc.); if a finite set *S* has the property that the distance between every pair of points in *S* is at most δ, then we include *S* as a simplex in the complex.

:::

## History {#History}

The Vietoris–Rips complex was originally called the Vietoris complex, for [Leopold Vietoris](wikipedia://en/105043), who introduced it as a means of extending [homology theory](wikipedia://en/142432) from simplicial complexes to metric spaces.[^o3] After [Eliyahu Rips](wikipedia://en/673693) applied the same complex to the study of [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817), its use was popularized by (txt), who called it the Rips complex.[^o5] The name "Vietoris–Rips complex" is due to (txt).[^o7]

## Relation to Čech complex {#Relation_to_Čech_complex}

The Vietoris–Rips complex is closely related to the [Čech complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cech_complex) (or [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_of_a_covering)) of a set of [balls](wikipedia://en/160556), which has a simplex for every finite subset of balls with nonempty intersection. In a [geodesically convex space](wikipedia://en/12677098) *Y*, the Vietoris–Rips complex of any subspace *X* ⊂ *Y* for distance δ has the same points and edges as the Čech complex of the set of balls of radius δ/2 in *Y* that are centered at the points of *X*. However, unlike the Čech complex, the Vietoris–Rips complex of *X* depends only on the intrinsic geometry of *X*, and not on any embedding of *X* into some larger space.

As an example, consider the uniform metric space *M*<sub>3</sub> consisting of three points, each at unit distance from each other. The Vietoris–Rips complex of *M*<sub>3</sub>, for δ = 1, includes a simplex for every subset of points in *M*<sub>3</sub>, including a triangle for *M*<sub>3</sub> itself. If we embed *M*<sub>3</sub> as an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), then the Čech complex of the radius-1/2 balls centered at the points of *M*<sub>3</sub> would contain all other simplexes of the Vietoris–Rips complex but would not contain this triangle, as there is no point of the plane contained in all three balls. However, if *M*<sub>3</sub> is instead embedded into a metric space that contains a fourth point at distance 1/2 from each of the three points of *M*<sub>3</sub>, the Čech complex of the radius-1/2 balls in this space would contain the triangle. Thus, the Čech complex of fixed-radius balls centered at *M*<sub>3</sub> differs depending on which larger space *M*<sub>3</sub> might be embedded into, while the Vietoris–Rips complex remains unchanged.

If any metric space *X* is embedded in an [injective metric space](wikipedia://en/7840768) *Y*, the Vietoris–Rips complex for distance δ and *X* coincides with the Čech complex of the balls of radius δ/2 centered at the points of *X* in *Y*. Thus, the Vietoris–Rips complex of any metric space *M* equals the Čech complex of a system of balls in the [tight span](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_span) of *M*.

## Relation to hyperbolic groups {#Relation_to_hyperbolic_groups}

*Image: VR complex Z.svg.* The Vietoris-Rips complex of $\mathbb Z, \{-1,+1\}$, with $\delta = 2$

Equipped with the [word metric](wikipedia://en/1289716) relative to a [generating set](wikipedia://en/99945), a finitely generated group *G* is a metric space. The associated Vietoris-Rips complex is a finite-dimensional locally finite simplicial complex on which *G* acts [geometrically](wikipedia://en/51457085), *i.e.* [properly discontinuously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Remarkable_properties_of_actions) and with compact quotient. This action is faithful, with finite stabilizers. Moreover, if *G* is torsion-free, the action if free.

When *G* is [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/3172817), for *δ* large enough the associated Vietoris-Rips complex is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414). This implies that the group is of [type *F*<sub>∞</sub>](wikipedia://en/60316348), is [finitely presented](wikipedia://en/99494) and of finite [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197)[^o10].

## Relation to unit disk graphs and clique complexes {#Relation_to_unit_disk_graphs_and_clique_complexes}

The Vietoris–Rips complex for δ = 1 contains an edge for every pair of points that are at unit distance or less in the given metric space. As such, its 1-[skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29) is the [unit disk graph](wikipedia://en/3686677) of its points. It contains a simplex for every [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) in the unit disk graph, so it is the [clique complex](wikipedia://en/20751674) or [flag complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_complex) of the unit disk graph.[^o12] More generally, the clique complex of any graph *G* is a Vietoris–Rips complex for the metric space having as points the [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) of *G* and having as its distances the lengths of the [shortest paths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortest_path) in *G*.

## Other results {#Other_results}

If *M* is a closed [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), then for sufficiently small values of δ the Vietoris–Rips complex of *M*, or of spaces sufficiently close to *M*, is [homotopy equivalent](wikipedia://en/212250) to *M* itself.[^o14]

(Chambers Erickson Worah 2008) describe efficient algorithms for determining whether a given cycle is contractible in the Rips complex of any finite point set in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410).

## Applications {#Applications}

As with unit disk graphs, the Vietoris–Rips complex has been applied in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) to model the topology of [ad hoc wireless communication networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_ad_hoc_network). One advantage of the Vietoris–Rips complex in this application is that it can be determined only from the distances between the communication nodes, without having to infer their exact physical locations. A disadvantage is that, unlike the Čech complex, the Vietoris–Rips complex does not directly provide information about gaps in communication coverage, but this flaw can be ameliorated by sandwiching the Čech complex between two Vietoris–Rips complexes for different values of δ.[^o18] An implementation of Vietoris–Rips complexes can be found in the TDAstats [R package](wikipedia://en/376707).[^n1]

Vietoris–Rips complexes have also been applied for feature-extraction in digital image data; in this application, the complex is built from a high-dimensional metric space in which the points represent low-level image features.[^o20]

The collection of all Vietoris–Rips complexes is a commonly applied construction in [persistent homology](wikipedia://en/31069689) and [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009), and is known as the [Rips filtration](wikipedia://en/73475847).[^o21]
