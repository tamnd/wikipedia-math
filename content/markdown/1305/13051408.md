# Giordano Riccati

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giordano Riccati"}

**Giordano Riccati** or **Jordan Riccati** (25 February, 1709 – 20 July, 1790) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist). The son of the mathematician [Jacopo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Riccati), he was the first [experimental physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_physics) to study material elastic moduli.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Giordano Riccati was born in 1709 in [Castelfranco Veneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelfranco_Veneto), a small town about 30 km north of [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua). He was the brother of [Vincenzo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Riccati) and the fifth son of the theoretical mechanician [Jacopo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Riccati).[^n1]

He began his studies at the College of St. Francis Xavier in Bologna, under the guidance of [Francesco Saverio Quadrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Saverio_Quadrio) and Luigi Marchenti, a pupil of the French mathematician [Pierre Varignon](wikipedia://en/1193511).[^n2] In 1727, he returned to Castelfranco, where his father taught him [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261), [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), [statics](wikipedia://en/28767) and [dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_%28mechanics%29). He then moved to the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) and attended [Giovanni Poleni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Poleni)'s lessons on [hydraulics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics) as well as the lectures of the famous physician and naturalist [Antonio Vallisneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vallisneri).[^n2]

A [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) with a vast range of interests, Riccati published treatises on mathematics, [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture), [acoustics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustics), [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics). He made significant contributions in the field of [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) applied to [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), publishing the *Saggio sulle leggi del contrappunto* \[Essay on the laws of counterpoint\], which tried to prove that music is not just an art, but it is a science as well, a *Trattato delle corde, ovvero delle Fibre Elastiche* \[Treaty on chords; on elastic fibers\], and some studies on the works of [Tartini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Tartini) and [Rameau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Philippe_Rameau). Giordano helped with the improvements to the [Cathedral of Treviso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treviso_Cathedral). He died in Treviso on July 20, 1790 and was buried in his family chapel in the Cathedral of Treviso.

Riccati was a member of the [Accademia Galileiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Galileiana) of Padua, of the [Academy of Sciences of the Institute of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Institute_of_Bologna) and of the Italian [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_nazionale_delle_scienze). Riccati corresponded regularly with the finest scholars of the period and cultivated a circle of friends with similar interests – intellectuals, artists, and writers who often gathered at his house. He enjoyed a close friendship with the architect [Francesco Maria Preti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Preti), whose treatise *Elementi di architettura* he edited after Preti's death.[^n3]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Riccati was the first experimental [mechanician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanician) to study material [elastic moduli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elastic_moduli) as we understand them today. His 1782 paper[^o9] on determining the relative [Young's moduli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young's_modulus) of steel and brass using flexural vibrations preceded [Thomas Young's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Young_%28scientist%29) 1807 paper on the subject of moduli.[^o10] The ratio that Riccati found was:[^o11]

$$\frac{E_{\mbox{steel}}}{E_{\mbox{brass}}} = 2.06$$

Even though the experiments were performed more than 200 years ago, this value is remarkably close to accepted values found in engineering handbooks in 2007.
