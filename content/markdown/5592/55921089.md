# Lucien March

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lucien March"}

**Lucien March** (6 December 1859 – 4 April 1933) was a French demographer, statistician, and engineer.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

In 1878 Lucien March enrolled in l'[École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique) and after graduation in 1880[^o4] served in the naval artillery corps.[^o2] He was the director of the Statistique générale de la France (SGF) from 1896 to 1920. In 1896, he introduced [Hollerith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollerith) [punched card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punched_card) [tabulating machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabulating_machine) into France and later invented an improved machine, the classifier-counter-printer, which was used until the 1940s. He also arranged a sorting process using the workplace addresses of the people counted in the French population census to generate valuable economic data and labor statistics.[^o5]

He was an invited speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) (ICM) in Rome (1908), Toronto (1924), and Bologna (1928).[^o6]

In 1912, upon his return from an international congress on eugenics, held in London, March helped to found a French eugenics society, which published in 1922 Eugénique et Sélection, a collection of essays on [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics).[^o7][^o8] In the 1920s he played an important role in the [International Federation of Eugenics Organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Eugenics_Organizations).[^o9]
