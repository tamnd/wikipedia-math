# Conway–Maxwell–binomial distribution

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Conway–Maxwell–binomial distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Conway–Maxwell–binomial (CMB)** distribution is a three parameter discrete probability distribution that generalises the [binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/3876) in an analogous manner to the way that the [Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/15845763) generalises the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144). The CMB distribution can be used to model both positive and negative association among the [Bernoulli](wikipedia://en/199189) summands,.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The [distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) was introduced by Shumeli et al. (2005),[^o3] and the name Conway–Maxwell–binomial distribution was introduced independently by Kadane (2016) [^o4] and Daly and Gaunt (2016).[^o5]

## Probability mass function {#Probability_mass_function}

The Conway–Maxwell–binomial (CMB) distribution has [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725)

$$\Pr(Y=j)=\frac{1}{C_{n,p,\nu}}\binom{n}{j}^\nu p^j(1-p)^{n-j}\,,\qquad j\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\},$$

where $n\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, $0\leq p\leq1$ and $-\infty<\nu<\infty$. The [normalizing constant](wikipedia://en/398189) $C_{n,p,\nu}$ is defined by

$$C_{n,p,\nu}=\sum_{i=0}^n\binom{n}{i}^\nu p^i(1-p)^{n-i}.$$

If a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $Y$ has the above mass function, then we write $Y\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$.

The case $\nu=1$ is the usual binomial distribution $Y\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$.

## Relation to Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution {#Relation_to_Conway–Maxwell–Poisson_distribution}

The following relationship between Conway–Maxwell–Poisson (CMP) and CMB random variables [^o3] generalises a well-known result concerning Poisson and binomial random variables. If $X_1\sim \operatorname{CMP}(\lambda_1,\nu)$ and $X_2\sim \operatorname{CMP}(\lambda_2,\nu)$ are [independent](wikipedia://en/437701#Mathematics), then $X_1\,|\,X_1+X_2=n\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,\lambda_1/(\lambda_1+\lambda_2),\nu)$.

## Sum of possibly associated Bernoulli random variables {#Sum_of_possibly_associated_Bernoulli_random_variables}

The random variable $Y\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$ may be written [^o3] as a sum of [exchangeable](wikipedia://en/6753623) Bernoulli random variables $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$ satisfying

$$\Pr(Z_1=z_1,\ldots,Z_n=z_n)=\frac{1}{C_{n,p,\nu}}\binom{n}{k}^{\nu-1}p^k(1-p)^{n-k},$$

where $k=z_1+\cdots+z_n$. Note that $\operatorname{E}Z_1\not=p$ in general, unless $\nu=1$.

## Generating functions {#Generating_functions}

Let

$$T(x,\nu)=\sum_{k=0}^n x^k\binom{n}{k}^\nu.$$

Then, the [probability generating function](wikipedia://en/236841), [moment generating function](wikipedia://en/194634) and [characteristic function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_function) are given, respectively, by:[^o4]

$$G(t)=\frac{T(tp/(1-p),\nu)}{T(p(1-p),\nu)},$$

$$M(t)=\frac{T(\mathrm{e}^tp/(1-p),\nu)}{T(p(1-p),\nu)},$$

$$\varphi(t)=\frac{T(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t}p/(1-p),\nu)}{T(p(1-p),\nu)}.$$

## Moments {#Moments}

For general $\nu$, there do not exist closed form expressions for the [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the CMB distribution. Having said that, the following mathematical relationship holds:[^o5]

Let $(j)_r=j(j-1)\cdots(j-r+1)$ denote the [falling factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falling_factorial). If $Y\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$, where $\nu>0$, then

$$\operatorname{E}[((Y)_r)^\nu]=\frac{C_{n-r,p,\nu}}{C_{n,p,\nu}}((n)_r)^\nu p^r\,,$$

for $r=1,\ldots,n-1$.

## Mode {#Mode}

Let $Y\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$ and define

$$a=\frac{n+1}{1+\left(\frac{1-p}{p}\right)^{1/\nu}}.$$

Then the [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127) of $Y$ is $\lfloor a\rfloor$ if $a$ is not an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). Otherwise, the modes of $Y$ are $a$ and $a-1$.[^o5]

## Stein characterisation {#Stein_characterisation}

Let $Y\sim\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$, and suppose that $f:\mathbb{Z}^+\mapsto\mathbb{R}$ is such that $\operatorname{E}|f(Y+1)|<\infty$ and $\operatorname{E}|Y^\nu f(Y)|<\infty$. Then [^o5]

$$\operatorname{E}[p(n-Y)^\nu f(Y+1)-(1-p)Y^\nu f(Y)]=0.$$

## Approximation by the Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution {#Approximation_by_the_Conway–Maxwell–Poisson_distribution}

Fix $\lambda>0$ and $\nu>0$ and let $Y_n\sim\mathrm{CMB}(n,\lambda/n^\nu,\nu)$ Then $Y_n$ [converges](wikipedia://en/50723) in distribution to the $\mathrm{CMP}(\lambda,\nu)$ distribution as $n\rightarrow\infty$.[^o5] This result generalises the classical Poisson approximation of the binomial distribution.

## Conway–Maxwell–Poisson binomial distribution {#Conway–Maxwell–Poisson_binomial_distribution}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be Bernoulli random variables with [joint distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_distribution) given by

$$\Pr(X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_n=x_n)=\frac{1}{C_n'}\binom{n}{k}^{\nu-1} \prod_{j=1}^np_j^{x_j}(1-p_j)^{1-x_j},$$

where $k=x_1+\cdots+x_n$ and the normalizing constant $C_n^\prime$ is given by

$$C_n'=\sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k}^{\nu-1} \sum_{A\in F_k} \prod_{i\in A} p_i \prod_{j\in A^c}(1-p_j),$$

where

$$F_k=\left\{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:|A|=k\right\}.$$

Let $W=X_1+\cdots+X_n$. Then $W$ has mass function

$$\Pr(W=k)=\frac{1}{C_n'}\binom{n}{k}^{\nu-1}\sum_{A\in F_k}\prod_{i\in A}p_i\prod_{j\in A^c}(1-p_j),$$

for $k=0,1,\ldots,n$. This distribution generalises the [Poisson binomial distribution](wikipedia://en/28647736) in a way analogous to the CMP and CMB generalisations of the Poisson and binomial distributions. Such a random variable is therefore said [^o5] to follow the Conway–Maxwell–Poisson binomial (CMPB) distribution. This should not be confused with the rather unfortunate terminology Conway–Maxwell–Poisson–binomial that was used by [^o3] for the CMB distribution.

The case $\nu=1$ is the usual Poisson binomial distribution and the case $p_1=\cdots=p_n=p$ is the $\operatorname{CMB}(n,p,\nu)$ distribution.
