# Geraldine Norman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geraldine Norman"}

**Geraldine Lucia Norman**, [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) (born 13 May 1940) is a British art journalist who made a special name for identifying fakes, moving on to work for the great Russian museum, the [Hermitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitage_Museum) in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Born Geraldine Lucia Keen to Harold Hugh Keen and Catherine Eleanor Lyle Cummins, she was educated in [St. Anne's College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anne's_College,_Oxford). She graduated in 1961 with a Masters of Arts in Mathematics, which she followed up by attending the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), from 1961 to 1962 while working as a teaching assistant.[^o4]

## Career {#Career}

Norman got a job as a statistician for *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* newspaper in 1962 and began to write for the paper on statistics and computers. In 1965, she left to work for the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) in Rome but was called back in 1967 to launch *The Times*-Sotheby index of art prices, which ran from 1967 to 1971. In 1969, she become the Sale Room Correspondent of *The Times*.[^o6][^o7] Norman gained a name during that time as [Christie's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christie's) disliked her. She asked awkward questions about secret practices within the industry. After she published an article suggesting it should be revealed when auction lots were unsold, Peter Wilson, chairman of Sotheby's, was offended and discontinued the index.[^o8] She continued to report on sales but also made a speciality of investigating fakes. She uncovered 13 drawings purporting to be by the 19th-century artist [Samuel Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Palmer) and went on to identify the forger as [Tom Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Keating).[^o9] She and her husband wrote a book with him about his life and the many artists he had forged. She also wrote about the faker [Eric Hebborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Hebborn), fakes at the [Getty Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getty_Museum) in California, and investigated the authenticity of [Van Gogh's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Gogh) *[Sunflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflowers_%28Van_Gogh_series%29)* sold at Christie's for a record price.[^o10]

In 1987, she left *The Times*, as she had objected to the [Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch) takeover. Norman joined *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* newspaper as Art Market Correspondent, eventually leaving in 1995 to focus on writing a book called *The Hermitage: The Biography of a Great Museum* (published in 1997 and reissued in 2017).[^o11] She then set up a branch of the Hermitage Museum in Somerset House which ran from 2000 to 2007 and went on to launch the Friends of the Hermitage in 2003 and the *Hermitage Magazine* in 2004.

She was appointed [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_Order_of_the_British_Empire) in 2011 for her service to UK/ Russian relations in fine art.[^o12]

## Family {#Family}

She married playwright and novelist [John Frank Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Norman) on 16 July 1971. They co-wrote *The Fake's Progress: The Keating Story* with Tom Keating in 1977. Frank died in 1980.
