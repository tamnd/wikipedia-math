# Cohn's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohn's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Cohn's theorem**[^o2] states that a *n*th-degree self-inversive [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $p(z)$ has as many [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeroes_of_a_function) in the open unit disk $D =\{z \in \mathbb{C}: |z|<1\}$ as the [reciprocal polynomial](wikipedia://en/1106042) of its [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921).[^o2][^o3][^o4] Cohn's theorem is useful for studying the distribution of the roots of self-inversive and self-reciprocal polynomials in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628).[^o5][^o6]

:::

An *n*th-degree polynomial,

$$p(z) = p_0 + p_1 z + \cdots + p_n z^n$$

is called self-inversive if there exists a *fixed* [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) ( $\omega$ ) of [modulus](wikipedia://en/20087) 1 so that,

$$p(z) = \omega p^*(z),\qquad \left(|\omega|=1\right),$$

where

$$p^*(z)=z^n \bar{p}\left(1 / \bar{z}\right) =\bar{p}_n + \bar{p}_{n-1} z + \cdots + \bar{p}_0 z^n$$

is the [reciprocal polynomial](wikipedia://en/1106042) associated with $p(z)$ and the bar means [complex conjugation](wikipedia://en/173918). Self-inversive polynomials have many interesting properties.[^o7] For instance, its roots are all [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1440207) with respect to the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) and a polynomial whose roots are all on the unit circle is necessarily self-inversive. The [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) of self-inversive polynomials satisfy the relations.

$$p_k = \omega \bar{p}_{n-k}, \qquad 0 \leqslant k \leqslant n.$$

In the case where $\omega = 1,$ a *self-inversive polynomial* becomes a *complex-reciprocal polynomial* (also known as a *self-conjugate polynomial*). If its coefficients are real then it becomes a *real self-reciprocal polynomial*.

The *[formal derivative](wikipedia://en/3832917)* of $p(z)$ is a (*n* − 1)th-degree polynomial given by

$$q(z) =p'(z) = p_1 + 2p_2 z + \cdots + n p_n z^{n-1}.$$

Therefore, Cohn's theorem states that both $p(z)$ and the polynomial

$$q^*(z) =z^{n-1}\bar{q}_{n-1}\left(1 / \bar{z}\right) = z^{n-1} \bar{p}' \left(1 / \bar{z}\right) = n \bar{p}_n + (n-1)\bar{p}_{n-1} z + \cdots + \bar{p}_1 z^{n-1}$$

have the same number of roots in $|z|<1.$
