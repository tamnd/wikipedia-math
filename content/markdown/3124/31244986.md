# Plancherel measure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plancherel measure"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Plancherel measure** is a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) defined on the set of [irreducible unitary representations](wikipedia://en/385982) of a [locally compact group](wikipedia://en/1651204) $G$, that describes how the [regular representation](wikipedia://en/360011) breaks up into irreducible unitary representations. In some cases the term **Plancherel measure** is applied specifically in the context of the group $G$ being the finite symmetric group $S_n$ – see below. It is named after the Swiss mathematician [Michel Plancherel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Plancherel) for his work in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).

:::

## Definition for finite groups {#Definition_for_finite_groups}

Let $G$ be a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707), we denote the set of its [irreducible representations](wikipedia://en/385982) by $G^\wedge$. The corresponding **Plancherel measure** over the set $G^\wedge$ is defined by

$$\mu(\pi) = \frac{(\mathrm{dim}\,\pi)^2}{|G|},$$

where $\pi\in G^\wedge$, and $\mathrm{dim}\pi$ denotes the dimension of the irreducible representation $\pi$. [^o3]

## Definition on the symmetric group {#Definition_on_the_symmetric_group}

An important special case is the case of the finite [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$, where $n$ is a positive integer. For this group, the set $S_n^\wedge$ of irreducible representations is in natural [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) with the set of [integer partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_partitions) of $n$. For an irreducible representation associated with an integer partition $\lambda$, its dimension is known to be equal to $f^\lambda$, the number of [standard Young tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux) of shape $\lambda$, so in this case **Plancherel measure** is often thought of as a measure on the set of integer partitions of given order *n*, given by

$\mu(\lambda) = \frac{(f^\lambda)^2}{n!}.$ [^o5]

The fact that those probabilities sum up to 1 follows from the combinatorial identity

$$\sum_{\lambda \vdash n}(f^\lambda)^2 = n!,$$

which corresponds to the bijective nature of the [Robinson–Schensted correspondence](wikipedia://en/1901026).

::: {#o7 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

**Plancherel measure** appears naturally in combinatorial and probabilistic problems, especially in the study of [longest increasing subsequence](wikipedia://en/4587078) of a random [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\sigma$. As a result of its importance in that area, in many current research papers the term **Plancherel measure** almost exclusively refers to the case of the symmetric group $S_n$.

### Connection to longest increasing subsequence {#Connection_to_longest_increasing_subsequence}

Let $L(\sigma)$ denote the length of a longest increasing subsequence of a random [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\sigma$ in $S_n$ chosen according to the uniform distribution. Let $\lambda$ denote the shape of the corresponding [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368) related to $\sigma$ by the [Robinson–Schensted correspondence](wikipedia://en/1901026). Then the following identity holds:

$$L(\sigma) = \lambda_1,$$

where $\lambda_1$ denotes the length of the first row of $\lambda$. Furthermore, from the fact that the Robinson–Schensted correspondence is bijective it follows that the distribution of $\lambda$ is exactly the Plancherel measure on $S_n$. So, to understand the behavior of $L(\sigma)$, it is natural to look at $\lambda_1$ with $\lambda$ chosen according to the Plancherel measure in $S_n$, since these two random variables have the same probability distribution. [^o9]

### Poissonized Plancherel measure {#Poissonized_Plancherel_measure}

**Plancherel measure** is defined on $S_n$ for each integer $n$. In various studies of the asymptotic behavior of $L(\sigma)$ as $n \rightarrow \infty$, it has proved useful [^o11] to extend the measure to a measure, called the **Poissonized Plancherel measure**, on the set $\mathcal{P}^*$ of all integer partitions. For any $\theta > 0$, the **Poissonized Plancherel measure with parameter $\theta$** on the set $\mathcal{P}^*$ is defined by

$$\mu_\theta(\lambda) = e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{|\lambda|}(f^\lambda)^2}{(|\lambda|!)^2},$$

for all $\lambda \in \mathcal{P}^*$. [^o5]

### Plancherel growth process {#Plancherel_growth_process}

The **Plancherel growth process** is a random sequence of [Young diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_tableaux) $\lambda^{(1)} = (1),~\lambda^{(2)},~\lambda^{(3)},~\ldots,$ such that each $\lambda^{(n)}$ is a random Young diagram of order $n$ whose probability distribution is the *n*th Plancherel measure, and each successive $\lambda^{(n)}$ is obtained from its predecessor $\lambda^{(n-1)}$ by the addition of a single box, according to the [transition probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_probability)

$$p(\nu, \lambda) = \mathbb{P}(\lambda^{(n)}=\lambda~|~\lambda^{(n-1)}=\nu) = \frac{f^{\lambda}}{nf^{\nu}},$$

for any given Young diagrams $\nu$ and $\lambda$ of sizes *n* − 1 and *n*, respectively. [^o13]

So, the **Plancherel growth process** can be viewed as a natural coupling of the different Plancherel measures of all the symmetric groups, or alternatively as a [random walk](wikipedia://en/235451) on [Young's lattice](wikipedia://en/14836297). It is not difficult to show that the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of $\lambda^{(n)}$ in this walk coincides with the **Plancherel measure** on $S_n$. [^o14]

:::

## Compact groups {#Compact_groups}

The Plancherel measure for compact groups is similar to that for finite groups, except that the measure need not be finite. The [unitary dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_dual) is a discrete set of finite-dimensional representations, and the Plancherel measure of an irreducible finite-dimensional representation is proportional to its dimension.

## Abelian groups {#Abelian_groups}

The unitary dual of a locally compact [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) is another locally compact abelian group, and the Plancherel measure is proportional to the [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645) of the dual group.

## Semisimple Lie groups {#Semisimple_Lie_groups}

The Plancherel measure for semisimple [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) was found by [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190). The support is the set of [tempered representations](wikipedia://en/10995628), and in particular not all unitary representations need occur in the support.
