# Core of a category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Core of a category"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the **core** of a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) *C* is the category whose objects are the objects of *C* and whose morphisms are the invertible morphisms in *C*.[^o2][^o3] In other words, it is the largest [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959).

:::

As a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $C \mapsto \operatorname{core}(C)$, the core is a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint) to the inclusion of the category of (small) groupoids into the category of (small) categories.[^o2] On the other hand, the left adjoint to the above inclusion is the [fundamental groupoid functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_groupoid_functor).

For [∞-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category), $\operatorname{core}$ is defined as a right adjoint to the inclusion ∞-Grpd $\hookrightarrow$ ∞-Cat.[^o5] The core of an ∞-category $C$ is then the largest [∞-groupoid](wikipedia://en/40965675) contained in $C$. The core of *C* is also often written as $C^{\simeq}$. The left adjoint to the above inclusion is given by a [localization of an ∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_an_∞-category).

In *Kerodon*, the subcategory of a [2-category](wikipedia://en/2740708) *C* obtained by removing non-invertible morphisms is called the **pith** of *C*.[^o6] It can also be defined for an [(∞, 2)-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28∞,_2%29-category) *C*;[^o7] namely, the pith of *C* is the largest simplicial subset that does not contain [non-thin 2-simplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_2-simplex).
