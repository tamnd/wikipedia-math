# Simplicial vertex

*Image: Bisimplicial vertex.svg.* Vertex 3 (circled red) is **bisimplicial**, as the set of it and its neighbors is the union of 2 [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) (denoted in black).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simplicial vertex"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **simplicial vertex** $v$ is a [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) whose [closed neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28graph_theory%29) $N_{G}[v]$ in a graph $G$ forms a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466), where every pair of neighbors is adjacent to each other.[^o2]

:::

A vertex of a graph is **bisimplicial** if the set of it and its neighbours is the union of two cliques, and is **$k$-simplicial** if the set is the union of $k$ cliques. A vertex is **co-simplicial** if its non-neighbours form an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501).[^o3]

Addario-Berry et al.[^o4] demonstrated that every [even-hole-free graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even-hole-free_graph) (or more specifically, **even-cycle-free graph**, as 4-cycles are also excluded here) contains a bisimplicial vertex, which settled a conjecture by Reed. The proof was later shown to be flawed by Chudnovsky & Seymour,[^o5] who gave a correct proof. Due to this property, the family of all even-cycle-free graphs is [$\chi$-bounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Χ-bounded).
