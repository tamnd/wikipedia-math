# Force chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Force chain"}

*Image: Stress transmision.svg.* Chain of transmission of stress forces in a granular media

In the study of the physics of [granular materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material), a **force chain** consists of a set of particles within a compressed granular material that are held together and [jammed into place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamming_%28physics%29) by a network of mutual compressive forces.[^o2]

:::

Between these chains are regions of low stress whose grains are shielded for the effects of the grains above by [vaulting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_%28architecture%29) and [arching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch). A set of interconnected force chains is known as a **force network**.[^o3] Force networks visualise inter-particle forces, which is particularly informative for spherical particle systems. For non-spherical particle systems force chain networks benefit from being supplemented by **traction chain networks**. **Traction chains** visualise inter-particle tractions, which give additional insight in inter-particle contact not captured by force chains, in particular, the role of contact area over which inter-particle forces act.

Force networks are an [emergent phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergent_phenomenon) that are created by the complex interaction of the individual grains of material and the patterns of pressure applied within the material. Force chains can be shown to have [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) properties.[^o4]

Force chains have been investigated both experimentally, through the construction of specially instrumented physical models,[^o4][^o5] and through computer simulation.[^o4]
