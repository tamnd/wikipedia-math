# Ice-sheet model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ice-sheet model"}

In [climate modelling](wikipedia://en/47513), **ice-sheet models** use [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/21506) to simulate the evolution, dynamics and thermodynamics of [ice sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sheet), such as the [Antarctic ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_ice_sheet), the [Greenland ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenland_ice_sheet) or the large ice sheets on the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) during the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). They are used for a variety of purposes, from studies of the glaciation of Earth over [glacial–interglacial cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age#Glacials_and_interglacials) in the past to projections of ice-sheet decay under future [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change) conditions.

:::

## History {#History}

Beginning in the mid-18th Century, investigation into ice sheet behavior began.[^o3] Since the [Journal of Glaciology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Glaciology)'s founding, physicists have been publishing glacial mechanics.[^o3]

*Image: Barnes Ice Cap 432 pan merge crop 15 (31109130474).jpg.* Barnes Ice Cap

The first 3-D model was applied to the [Barnes Ice Cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes_Ice_Cap).[^o3] In 1988, the first thermodynamically coupled model incorporating [ice-shelves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_shelf), sheet/shelf transition, membrane stress gradients, isostatic bed adjustment and [basal sliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_sliding) using more advanced numerical techniques was developed and applied to the [Antarctic ice sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic_ice_sheet).[^o3] This model had a resolution of 40 km and 10 vertical layers.[^o3]

When the [first IPCC assessment report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_First_Assessment_Report) came out in 1990, ice sheets were not an active part of the climate system model, their evolution was based on a correlation between global temperature and surface mass balance.[^o4] When [the second IPCC assessment report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Second_Assessment_Report) came out in 1996, the beginning of both 2D and 3D modelling was shown with ice sheets.[^o4] The 1990s heralded several more computational models, bringing with it the [European Ice Sheet Modelling Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Ice_Sheet_Modelling_Initiative) (EISMINT).[^o3][^o5] The EISMINT produced several workshops throughout the 1990s of an international collaboration, comparing most models of Greenland, Antarctic, ice-shelf, thermomechanical and grounding-line.[^o5]

The 2000s included integrating [first-order approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_approximation) of full [Stokes](wikipedia://en/25441497) Dynamics into an ice-sheet model.[^o3] [The fourth IPCC assessment report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Fourth_Assessment_Report) showed ice-sheet models with projections of rapid dynamical responses in the ice, which led to evidence of significant ice loss.[^o4]

In 2016, part of the [Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6 (CMIP Phase 6)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupled_Model_Intercomparison_Project#CMIP_Phase_6) was the [Ice Sheet Model Intercomparison Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Sheet_Model_Intercomparison_Project), which defined a protocol for all variables related to ice sheet modelling.[^o6] The project allowed for both improvement in numerical and physical approaches to ice sheets.[^o7]

## Modelling {#Modelling}

### Ice-Flow {#Ice-Flow}

#### Shallow Ice Approximation {#Shallow_Ice_Approximation}

Shallow Ice Approximation (SIA) is a simple method to model ice flow without having to solve full-Stokes equations.[^o11] The approximation is best applied to ice sheet with a small depth-to-width ratio, without many sliding dynamics and a simple bed topography.[^o12] SIA does not include many forces on an ice sheet, and can be considered a ['zero-order'](wikipedia://en/36800603) model.[^o13] The model assumes that ice sheets are mostly split up by [basal sheer stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice-sheet_dynamics#Basal_shear_stress), and it is not necessary to consider the other forces.[^o14] It also assumes that the basal [shear stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_stress) and the gravitational driving stress of the grounded ice balance one another out.[^o15] The method is computationally inexpensive.[^o14]

#### Shallow Shelf Approximation {#Shallow_Shelf_Approximation}

Shallow Shelf Approximation is another method to model ice flow, in particular a membrane-type flow of floating ice, or of sliding grounded ice over a base.[^o17] Also known as a membrane model, they are similar to free-film models in fluid dynamics.[^o18] As opposed to Shallow Ice Approximation, Shallow Shelf Approximation models ice flow when longitudinal forces are strong; sliding and vertical forces.[^o19] SSA can also be considered a 'zero-order' model.[^o20]

#### Full Stokes Equations {#Full_Stokes_Equations}

It is considered advantageous to model ice using [Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-stokes_equations) as ice is a viscous fluid and these capture all forces exerted on the ice.[^o11] As these equations are computationally expensive, it is important to include many approximations to reduce running time.[^o11] Because of their computational expense, they are not easily used at a large scale and can be used in specific sections or scenarios, such as at grounding lines.[^o12]

### Interactions with other climatic components {#Interactions_with_other_climatic_components}

Ice sheets interact with the surrounding atmosphere, ocean and sub-glacial earth.[^o23] All of these interactive components need to be included to be able to have a comprehensive ice-sheet model.[^o23]

**Basal Conditions** play an important role in determining the behavior of ice sheets. The basal thermal state (if the ice is thawed or frozen) and the basal topography are difficult to map.[^o23] The most favored method is to apply mass conservation constraints.[^o23] For long-term projections, it is important to project the topography onto the continental shelf or into the fjords, and this can be difficult when the sub-glacial topography is not well-known.[^o23]

**Summer Insolation** drive temperature responses that have an effect on the rate of melting and mass balance of the ice sheet.[^o24] For example, the dependence of ice volume on summer insolation can be represented with ${d(I) \over d(t)} = {1 \over T} (S - I)$, where I is ice volume, ${d(I) \over d(t)}$ is the rate of change of ice volume per unit of time, T is the response time of the ice sheet and S is the insolation signal.[^o24]

**Air Temperature** is needed in a model as it informs surface melt and runoff rates.[^o25] For example, surface air temperature can be expressed with latitude 'lat', surface elevation *h* and mean temperature to provide an estimate of annual mean temperatures:[^o25] $T_{aml} = temperature - c_1 * max(h, 1000) - c_2 * lat$. This example assumes the ice shelf 's surface is as cold as at 1000m altitude.[^o25]

**Precipitation** is directly tied to air temperature, and also depends on moisture above and around the ice sheet.[^o25] Precipitation plays an important part in ice-sheet melting and accumulation.[^o25]

### Calving {#Calving}

[Calving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_calving) is still an active area of investigation in ice-sheet modelling.[^o23] A total picture of calving will include many different aspects, including but not limited to tides, basal crevasses, collisions with ice bergs, thickness and temperature.[^o27] The recent development of the concepts of [Marine Ice Sheet Instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) and [Marine Ice Cliff Instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Ice_Cliff_Instability) have contributed to more accurate results of ice-sheet calving processes.[^o28]

## Examples {#Examples}

### CISM {#CISM}

The Community Ice Sheet Model is part of the [Community Earth Systems Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Earth_System_Model) funded by the National Science Foundation and models ice dynamics.[^o32][^o33] It is written in [Fortran 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortran) and is [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source).[^o32] The US [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy) has begun to contribute to CISM.[^o33] The CISM project works on other adjacent projects in developing a cirriculum to expand knowledge on ice sheets, and engaging a broader community in ice-sheet modelling.[^o33] Many ice-sheet modelling softwares have influenced CISM, including the [Parallel Ice Sheet Model](https://pism-docs.org/wiki/doku.php) (PSIM) and Glimmer.[^o34][^o35]

### seaRISE {#seaRISE}

Sea-level Response to Ice Sheet Evolution (SeaRISE) is a subcommunity of CISM that sets out to estimate the upper limit of sea level rise from ice sheets.[^o37] The project sets out to develop a set of experiments and assessments for ice sheet and sea level rise modelling, as well as make a unified input dataset for ice sheet models.[^o37]

### Glimmer {#Glimmer}

Glimmer ([GENIE](https://www.ercim.eu/publication/Ercim_News/enw61/price.html) Land Ice Model with Multiply-Enabled Regions) is an ice-sheet model initially made to contribute to a more comprehensive earth system model, GENIE.[^o39]

### PISM {#PISM}

The Parallel Ice Sheet Model is an open-sourced 3D ice sheet model capable of high resolution.[^o41] PISM is written in C++ and Python, and takes [NetCDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetCDF) files as input for the model.[^o42] PISM uses a "SIA+SSA hybrid" model, using both the shallow shelf approximation and shallow ice approximation models as stress balance models and does not solve full Stokes equations.[^o41] The model gets climatic information from an external [General Circulation Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_circulation_model), and needs information like boundary temperature, mass flux into the ice, precipitation and air temperature.[^o43]

A horizontal grid of equal distance is used, with a variable vertical axis, and runs on a year timescale.[^o44][^o45]

## Ice-sheet models on the web {#Ice-sheet_models_on_the_web}

- CISM[^o48] – Community Ice Sheet Model, under development as a land-ice component of the [Community Earth System Model (CESM)](http://www2.cesm.ucar.edu)
- Elmer/Ice[^o49] – a multi-physics finite element code with special modules for full-stress ice dynamics analysis
- ISSM[^o50] – Ice-Sheet and Sea-level System Model, a large-scale thermo-mechanical 2D/3D parallelized multi-purpose finite-element software dedicated to ice sheet and sea-level modeling.
- PISM[^o51] – Parallel Ice Sheet Model, which includes ice shelves and ice streams
- SICOPOLIS[^o52] – SImulation COde for POLythermal Ice Sheets, a 3D ice-sheet model which accounts for polythermal conditions (coexistence of ice at and below the melting point in different parts of an ice sheet)
