# Joel Kleinman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Joel Kleinman"}

**Joel C. Kleinman** (1946 – May 2, 1991) was an American health statistician and epidemiologist specializing on the causes of infant mortality. He was director of analysis at the [National Center for Health Statistics](wikipedia://en/1042734).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Kleinman was born in 1946 in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to Rose and George Kleinman.[^o4][^o5] He was raised in [the Bronx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bronx).[^o5] In 1967, he earned a bachelor's degree from [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York).[^o4][^o5] He completed a master's degree and Ph.D. in statistics (1971) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University).[^o4] He worked there as an assistant professor of biostatistics from 1971 to 1975.[^o4][^o5] In 1975, Kleinman joined the [National Center for Health Statistics](wikipedia://en/1042734) as a visiting service fellow.[^o4][^o5] He became its director of analysis in 1980.[^o5] In 1982, Kleinman won the [Mortimer Spiegelman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortimer_Spiegelman_Award).[^o6] In 1990, he was elected a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o7] A health statistician and epidemiologist, he specialized on the causes of infant mortality.[^o4][^o5]

Kleinman was married to cell biologist [Hynda Kleinman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hynda_Kleinman).[^o4] They had two daughters.[^o4] He died of diabetes complications on May 2, 1991, during a business trip in [Berkeley Springs, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley_Springs,_West_Virginia).[^o4]
