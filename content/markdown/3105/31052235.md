# Mathematics and the Imagination

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mathematics and the Imagination"}

***Mathematics and the Imagination*** is a book written by [Edward Kasner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Kasner) and [James R. Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_R._Newman) and illustrated by [Rufus Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Isaacs_%28game_theorist%29), who provided 169 figures. It was published in New York by [Simon & Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_&_Schuster) in 1940. It rapidly became a best-seller and received several glowing reviews. This book introduced the term [googol](wikipedia://en/18994679) for 10<sup>100</sup>, and [googolplex](wikipedia://en/12365) for 10<sup>googol</sup>. It includes nine chapters, an annotated bibliography of 45 titles, and an index in its 380 pages.

:::

## Reception {#Reception}

[I. Bernard Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._Bernard_Cohen) described it as "the best account of modern mathematics... written in a graceful style, combining clarity of exposition with good humor." Cohen believed it was suitable for both mathematicians and non-mathematicians alike.[^o4] According to T. A. Ryan the book "is not as superficial as one might expect a book at the popular level to be. For instance, the description of the invention of the term *googol* ... is a very serious attempt to show how misused is the term *infinite* when applied to large and finite numbers."[^o5] [G. Waldo Dunnington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Waldo_Dunnington) could note the book had become a [best-seller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-seller) by 1941, writing, "Apparently it has succeeded in communicating to the layman something of the pleasure experienced by the creative mathematician in difficult problem solving."[^o6]

## Contents {#Contents}

**Chapter I** ("New Names for Old") explains the significance of the concept of the [function](wikipedia://en/185427), the notions of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) and [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) from [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the [Problem of Apollonius](wikipedia://en/2642185), radicals, and the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518), or impossibility of solving polynomial equations of the fifth or higher degree by radicals.

**Chapter II** ("Beyond Googol") treats the size or [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of a set, [infinite sets](wikipedia://en/142679), the differences between [countable](wikipedia://en/6026) and [uncountable sets](wikipedia://en/32248), Cantor's theory of [transfinite numbers](wikipedia://en/189734), and the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901), or the set of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$.

**Chapter III** ("$\pi$, $i$, $e$ (Pie)") goes over [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) and [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325), the impossibility of [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110) or [trisecting an angle](wikipedia://en/91111) by a [straightedge and compass](wikipedia://en/61229), the histories of [Euler's number](wikipedia://en/9633) $e$ and $\pi$ ([pi](wikipedia://en/23601)), the exponential function $y = e^x$, the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358) $i$, the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), the geometric interpretation of multiplication by $i$ as rotating by 90° counterclockwise, and [Euler's identity](wikipedia://en/46740) $e^{i \pi} + 1 = 0$.

**Chapter IV** ("Assorted Geometries—Plane and Fancy") examines [Euclid's fifth postulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid's_fifth_postulate), [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610), and [four-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/1364622).

**Chapter V** ("Pastimes of Past and Present Times") discusses various mathematical games or puzzles such as the [Tower of Hanoi](wikipedia://en/56990) and the [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202); the [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) and [binary systems](wikipedia://en/238686); and [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).

**Chapter VI** ("Paradox Lost and Paradox Regained") explores a number of paradoxes or fallacies in mathematics, such as the [Banach–Tarski paradox](wikipedia://en/19759220) in geometry and [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095) in set theory.

**Chapter VII** ("Chance and Chanceablity") is about various topics in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), such as [Buffon's needle problem](wikipedia://en/667479) and the [binomial theorem](wikipedia://en/4677).

**Chapter VIII** ("Rubber-sheet Geometry") concerns concepts in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), such as the [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444), the [Euler characteristics](wikipedia://en/148420), and the [four-color map theorem](wikipedia://en/10949).

**Chapter IX** ("Change and Changeability") elucidates the [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176), [its history](wikipedia://en/746117), and applications.
