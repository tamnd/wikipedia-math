# Dunford–Schwartz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dunford–Schwartz theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Dunford–Schwartz theorem**, named after [Nelson Dunford](wikipedia://en/21022291) and [Jacob T. Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_T._Schwartz), states that the averages of powers of certain norm-[bounded operators](wikipedia://en/455961) on [*L*<sup>1</sup>](wikipedia://en/45194) [converge](wikipedia://en/285773) in a suitable sense.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $T$ be a linear operator from $L^1$ to $L^1$ with $\|T\|_1 \le 1$ and $\|T\|_\infty \le 1$. Then

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^kf$$

exists almost everywhere for all $f\in L^1$.

The statement is no longer true when the boundedness condition is relaxed to even $\|T\|_\infty\le 1+\varepsilon$.[^o5]

:::
