# Shoshichi Kobayashi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shoshichi Kobayashi"}

**Shoshichi Kobayashi**[^o3] was a Japanese [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was the eldest brother of electrical engineer and computer scientist [Hisashi Kobayashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisashi_Kobayashi).[^o4] His research interests were in [Riemannian](wikipedia://en/144652) and [complex](wikipedia://en/509742) [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470), transformation groups of geometric structures, and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kobayashi graduated from the [University of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tokyo) in 1953. In 1956, he earned a Ph.D. from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) under [Carl B. Allendoerfer](wikipedia://en/3466784). His dissertation was *Theory of Connections*.[^o6] He then spent two years at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and two years at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). He joined the faculty of the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1962 as an assistant professor, was awarded tenure the following year, and was promoted to full professor in 1966.

Kobayashi served as chairman of the Berkeley Mathematics Department for a three-year term from 1978 to 1981 and for the 1992 Fall semester. He chose early retirement under the VERIP plan in 1994.

The two-volume book *[Foundations of Differential Geometry](wikipedia://en/36727478)*, which he coauthored with [Katsumi Nomizu](wikipedia://en/37009604), has been known for its wide influence. In 1970 he was an invited speaker for the section on geometry and topology at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).

## Technical contributions {#Technical_contributions}

Kobayashi's earliest work dealt with the geometry of [connections on principal bundles](wikipedia://en/470632). Many of these results, along with others, were later absorbed into *[Foundations of Differential Geometry](wikipedia://en/36727478)*.

As a consequence of the [Gauss–Codazzi equations](wikipedia://en/9633335) and the commutation formulas for [covariant derivatives](wikipedia://en/431848), [James Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844) discovered a formula for the Laplacian of the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078) of a submanifold of a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652).[^o8] As a consequence, one can find a formula for the Laplacian of the norm-squared of the second fundamental form. This ["Simons formula"](wikipedia://en/65644428) simplifies significantly when the [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) of the submanifold is zero and when the Riemannian manifold has constant curvature. In this setting, [Shiing-Shen Chern](wikipedia://en/391905), [Manfredo do Carmo](wikipedia://en/45041862), and Kobayashi studied the algebraic structure of the zeroth-order terms, showing that they are nonnegative provided that the norm of the second fundamental form is sufficiently small.

As a consequence, the case in which the norm of the second fundamental form is constantly equal to the threshold value can be completely analyzed, the key being that all of the matrix inequalities used in controlling the zeroth-order terms become equalities. As such, in this setting, the second fundamental form is uniquely determined. As submanifolds of [space forms](wikipedia://en/1087009) are locally characterized by their first and second fundamental forms, this results in a complete characterization of minimal submanifolds of the round sphere whose second fundamental form is constant and equal to the threshold value. Chern, do Carmo, and Kobayashi's result was later improved by An-Min Li and Jimin Li, making use of the same methods.[^o9]

On a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538), it is natural to consider the restriction of the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) to the two-dimensional planes which are holomorphic, i.e. which are invariant under the [almost-complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost-complex_structure). This is called the *holomorphic sectional curvature*. Samuel Goldberg and Kobayashi introduced an extension of this quantity, called the *holomorphic bisectional curvature*; its input is a pair of holomorphic two-dimensional planes. Goldberg and Kobayashi established the differential-geometric foundations of this object, carrying out many analogies with the sectional curvature. In particular they established, by the [Bochner technique](wikipedia://en/3988777), that the second [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of a connected [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) must equal one if there is a Kähler metric whose holomorphic bisectional curvature is positive. Later, Kobayashi and Takushiro Ochiai proved some [rigidity theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigidity_theorem) for [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538). In particular, if $M$ is a [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) Kähler manifold and there exists $\alpha$ in $H^{1, 1}(M, \mathbb{Z})$ such that

$$c_1(M)\geq(n+1)\alpha,$$

then $M$ must be biholomorphic to [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). This, in combination with the Goldberg–Kobayashi result, forms the final part of [Yum-Tong Siu](wikipedia://en/6085925) and [Shing-Tung Yau](wikipedia://en/303137)'s proof of the [Frankel conjecture](wikipedia://en/65551796).[^o10] Kobayashi and Ochiai also characterized the situation of $c_{1}(M) = n\alpha$ as $M$ being biholomorphic to a quadratic hypersurface of complex projective space.

Kobayashi is also notable for having proved that a hermitian–Einstein metric on a [holomorphic vector bundle](wikipedia://en/4794668) over a compact Kähler manifold has deep algebro-geometric implications, as it implies semistability and decomposability as a direct sum of stable bundles. This establishes one direction of the [Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence](wikipedia://en/37856668). [Karen Uhlenbeck](wikipedia://en/4384796) and Yau proved the converse result, following well-known partial results by [Simon Donaldson](wikipedia://en/976305).

In the 1960s, Kobayashi introduced what is now known as the [Kobayashi metric](wikipedia://en/31025902). This associates a [pseudo-metric](wikipedia://en/68503) to any [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742), in a holomorphically invariant way. This sets up the important notion of *Kobayashi hyperbolicity*, which is defined by the condition that the Kobayashi metric is a genuine metric (and not only a pseudo-metric). With these notions, Kobayashi was able to establish a higher-dimensional version of the [Alhfors–Schwarz lemma](wikipedia://en/1487910) from [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).
