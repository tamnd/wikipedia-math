# Constantin Gonciulea

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Constantin Gonciulea"}

**Constantin Gonciulea** is an American computer scientist and technology executive recognized for his work in advanced computing, quantum technologies, and financial systems. He serves as the Chief Technology Officer for Advanced Technology at [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo) and co-authored *Building Quantum Software with Python*.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gonciulea was born in [Drobeta-Turnu Severin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drobeta-Turnu_Severin), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania).[^o4] He studied at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest) and earned a Bachelor of Science degree in 1992. According to a 2015 regional mathematical society magazine, Gonciulea was awarded in several Romanian mathematics competitions in his youth, including the Junior Mathematics Olympiad (OJM), the National Mathematics Olympiad (ONM), the [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica) Contest, the GMB Solvers competition, and National Scientific Communications Sessions.[^o5] He later moved to the United States to continue his studies at [The Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ohio_State_University), where he received a Master of Science in 1999 and a Ph.D. in Mathematics in 2000.[^o4] His doctoral dissertation, titled *Virtual epimorphisms of Coxeter groups onto free groups*, focused on algebra, geometry, and group theory.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After finishing his academic studies, Gonciulea entered the technology sector, specializing in applied computer science and technology leadership. He has worked in the financial services industry on enterprise software and digital banking systems for server-side, web, and mobile platforms.[^o8] His career includes software engineering, technology architecture, and strategy in areas such as payment systems, digital platforms, [distributed computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_computing), and financial technology.

At [JPMorgan Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPMorgan_Chase), Gonciulea looked into the potential use of quantum computing in financial services.[^o9] His work at the bank involved studying quantum algorithms for various applications, risk analysis, portfolio optimization, and cryptography.[^o9]

In June 2021, Gonciulea joined [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo) to lead the bank’s Advanced Technology organization.[^o9][^o10][^o11] As [Chief Technology Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Technology_Officer) for Advanced Technology, he manages research and development in quantum computing, distributed systems, and other [emerging technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_technologies).[^o12][^o13] He has participated in industry conferences and technology events, such as the [Q2B quantum computing conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QC_Ware#Q2B_conference), where he presented his research and long-term technological direction.[^o14] His role involves collaboration in research, engineering, and technology evaluation in the financial sector.[^o15]

## Research {#Research}

Gonciulea’s research covers algorithmic and software-engineering aspects of quantum computing, with emphasis on optimization, simulation, and applications to financial engineering. He co-authored *Building Quantum Software with Python: A Developer’s Guide* with Charlee Stefanski, published by [Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manning_Publications) in 2025.[^o17] He has discussed quantum computing in public forums, including the [QuEra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QuEra_Computing_Inc.) podcast, The Quantum Insider, and Superposition Guys.[^o18]

In July 2025, he delivered an [ACM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) Learning TechTalk introducing key concepts in quantum computing for software developers.[^o19] His publications include work on quantum algorithms and their applications to financial engineering, including *A generalized quantum inner product and applications to financial engineering*. His works *Challenges and opportunities in quantum optimization* and *Designing a fast and flexible quantum state simulator* introduced Spinoza, an open-source [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_%28programming_language%29)-based quantum state simulator with [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) bindings.[^o20][^o21] Gonciulea is also listed as an inventor on patents related to distributed quantum computing and quantum visualization.[^o22]

His publications address the following topics:

- Building blocks for state preparation and distribution encodings (including [normal distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_distributions)).
- Hash-based generalization of a quantum inner-product approach for weighted sums of [discrete functions](wikipedia://en/8492) ($\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} w_k,f(k)$), enabling restricted evaluations (e.g., [VaR](wikipedia://en/148633)) and comparator-style quantities.
- Oracle construction for [Grover Adaptive Search](wikipedia://en/58498) on constrained binary optimization with near-term resource constraints in mind.
- Automatic oracle generation using a Quantum Dictionary pattern with conditional additions of [polynomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/23000).
- Demonstration on portfolio optimization, using both simulation and real hardware results.
- Spinoza simulator implementation choices and internal representation details.
- [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29)-facing [API](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API) via [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_%28programming_language%29) bindings circuit-style interface.
