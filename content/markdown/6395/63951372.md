# Strong dual space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Strong dual space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **strong dual space** of a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) $X$ is the [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) $X^{\prime}$ of $X$ equipped with the **strong** (**dual**) **topology** or the **topology of uniform convergence on bounded subsets of $X,$** where this topology is denoted by $b\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$ or $\beta\left(X^{\prime}, X\right).$ The [coarsest](wikipedia://en/393069) polar topology is called [weak topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_topology_%28polar_topology%29). The strong dual space plays such an important role in modern functional analysis, that the continuous dual space is usually assumed to have the strong dual topology unless indicated otherwise. To emphasize that the continuous dual space, $X^{\prime},$ has the strong dual topology, $X^{\prime}_b$ or $X^{\prime}_{\beta}$ may be written.

:::

## Strong dual topology {#Strong_dual_topology}

Throughout, all vector spaces will be assumed to be over the field $\mathbb{F}$ of either the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}.$

### Definition from a dual system {#Definition_from_a_dual_system}

Let $(X, Y, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ be a [dual pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_pair) of vector spaces over the field $\mathbb{F}$ of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}.$ For any $B \subseteq X$ and any $y \in Y,$ define

$$|y|_B = \sup_{x \in B}|\langle x, y\rangle|.$$

Neither $X$ nor $Y$ has a topology so say a subset $B \subseteq X$ is said to be **bounded by a subset $C \subseteq Y$** if $|y|_B < \infty$ for all $y \in C.$ So a subset $B \subseteq X$ is called bounded if and only if

$$\sup_{x \in B} |\langle x, y \rangle| < \infty \quad \text{ for all } y \in Y.$$

This is equivalent to the usual notion of [bounded subsets](wikipedia://en/1789812) when $X$ is given the weak topology induced by $Y,$ which is a Hausdorff [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) topology.

Let $\mathcal{B}$ denote the [family](wikipedia://en/723043) of all subsets $B \subseteq X$ bounded by elements of $Y$; that is, $\mathcal{B}$ is the set of all subsets $B \subseteq X$ such that for every $y \in Y,$

$$|y|_B = \sup_{x\in B}|\langle x, y\rangle| < \infty.$$

Then the **strong topology** $\beta(Y, X, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ on $Y,$ also denoted by $b(Y, X, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$ or simply $\beta(Y, X)$ or $b(Y, X)$ if the pairing $\langle \cdot, \cdot\rangle$ is understood, is defined as the [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) topology on $Y$ generated by the seminorms of the form

$$|y|_B = \sup_{x \in B} |\langle x, y\rangle|,\qquad y \in Y, \qquad B \in \mathcal{B}.$$

The definition of the strong dual topology now proceeds as in the case of a TVS. Note that if $X$ is a TVS whose continuous dual space [separates points](wikipedia://en/13972359) on $X,$ then $X$ is part of a canonical dual system $\left(X, X^{\prime}, \langle \cdot , \cdot \rangle\right)$ where $\left\langle x, x^{\prime} \right\rangle := x^{\prime}(x).$ In the special case when $X$ is a [locally convex space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex_space), the **strong topology** on the (continuous) [dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) $X^{\prime}$ (that is, on the space of all continuous linear functionals $f : X \to \mathbb{F}$) is defined as the strong topology $\beta\left(X^{\prime}, X\right),$ and it coincides with the topology of uniform convergence on [bounded sets](wikipedia://en/48258) in $X,$ i.e. with the topology on $X^{\prime}$ generated by the seminorms of the form

$$|f|_B = \sup_{x \in B} |f(x)|, \qquad \text{ where } f \in X^{\prime},$$

where $B$ runs over the family of all [bounded sets](wikipedia://en/1789812) in $X.$ The space $X^{\prime}$ with this topology is called **strong dual space** of the space $X$ and is denoted by $X^{\prime}_{\beta}.$

### Definition on a TVS {#Definition_on_a_TVS}

Suppose that $X$ is a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) over the field $\mathbb{F}.$ Let $\mathcal{B}$ be any fundamental system of [bounded sets](wikipedia://en/1789812) of $X$; that is, $\mathcal{B}$ is a [family](wikipedia://en/723043) of bounded subsets of $X$ such that every bounded subset of $X$ is a subset of some $B \in \mathcal{B}$; the set of all bounded subsets of $X$ forms a fundamental system of bounded sets of $X.$ A basis of closed neighborhoods of the origin in $X^{\prime}$ is given by the [polars](wikipedia://en/1764022):

$$B^{\circ} := \left\{ x^{\prime} \in X^{\prime} : \sup_{x \in B} \left|x^{\prime}(x)\right| \leq 1 \right\}$$

as $B$ ranges over $\mathcal{B}$). This is a locally convex topology that is given by the set of [seminorms](wikipedia://en/292067) on $X^{\prime}$: $\left|x^{\prime}\right|_{B} := \sup_{x \in B} \left|x^{\prime}(x)\right|$ as $B$ ranges over $\mathcal{B}.$

If $X$ is [normable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normable_space) then so is $X^{\prime}_{b}$ and $X^{\prime}_{b}$ will in fact be a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989). If $X$ is a normed space with norm $\| \cdot \|$ then $X^{\prime}$ has a canonical norm (the [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722)) given by $\left\| x^{\prime} \right\| := \sup_{\| x \| \leq 1} \left| x^{\prime}(x) \right|$; the topology that this norm induces on $X^{\prime}$ is identical to the strong dual topology.

## Bidual {#Bidual}

The **bidual** or **second dual** of a TVS $X,$ often denoted by $X^{\prime \prime},$ is the strong dual of the strong dual of $X$:

$$X^{\prime \prime} \,:=\, \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}$$

where $X^{\prime}_b$ denotes $X^{\prime}$ endowed with the strong dual topology $b\left(X^{\prime}, X\right).$ Unless indicated otherwise, the vector space $X^{\prime \prime}$ is usually assumed to be endowed with the strong dual topology induced on it by $X^{\prime}_b,$ in which case it is called the **strong bidual** of $X$; that is,

$$X^{\prime \prime} \,:=\, \left(X^{\prime}_b\right)^{\prime}_b$$

where the vector space $X^{\prime \prime}$ is endowed with the strong dual topology $b\left(X^{\prime\prime}, X^{\prime}_b\right).$

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $X$ be a [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) TVS.

- A convex [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) weakly compact subset of $X^{\prime}$ is bounded in $X^{\prime}_b.$[^n1]
- Every weakly bounded subset of $X^{\prime}$ is strongly bounded.[^n2]
- If $X$ is a [barreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space) then $X$'s topology is identical to the strong dual topology $b\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$ and to the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627) on $X.$
- If $X$ is a metrizable locally convex space, then the strong dual of $X$ is a [bornological space](wikipedia://en/1804457) if and only if it is an [infrabarreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrabarreled_space), if and only if it is a [barreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barreled_space).[^n3]
- If $X$ is Hausdorff locally convex TVS then $\left(X, b\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)\right)$ is [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) if and only if there exists a countable set $\mathcal{B}$ of bounded subsets of $X$ such that every bounded subset of $X$ is contained in some element of $\mathcal{B}.$[^n4]
- If $X$ is locally convex, then this topology is finer than all other [$\mathcal{G}$-topologies](wikipedia://en/41677409) on $X^{\prime}$ when considering only $\mathcal{G}$'s whose sets are subsets of $X.$
- If $X$ is a [bornological space](wikipedia://en/1804457) (e.g. [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable) or [LF-space](wikipedia://en/2314852)) then $X^{\prime}_{b(X^{\prime}, X)}$ is [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856).

If $X$ is a [barrelled space](wikipedia://en/1733592), then its topology coincides with the strong topology $\beta\left(X, X^{\prime}\right)$ on $X$ and with the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627) on generated by the pairing $\left(X, X^{\prime}\right).$

:::

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

If $X$ is a [normed vector space](wikipedia://en/21538), then its [(continuous) dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual) $X^{\prime}$ with the strong topology coincides with the [Banach dual space](wikipedia://en/3989#dual_space) $X^{\prime}$; that is, with the space $X^{\prime}$ with the topology induced by the [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722). Conversely $\left(X, X^{\prime}\right).$-topology on $X$ is identical to the topology induced by the [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) on $X.$
