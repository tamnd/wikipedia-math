# Pollaczek–Khinchine formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pollaczek–Khinchine formula"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Pollaczek–Khinchine formula** states a relationship between the queue length and service time distribution [Laplace transforms](wikipedia://en/18610) for an [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064) (where jobs arrive according to a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) and have general service time distribution). The term is also used to refer to the relationships between the mean queue length and mean waiting/service time in such a model.[^o2]

:::

The formula was first published by [Felix Pollaczek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Pollaczek) in 1930[^o3] and recast in probabilistic terms by [Aleksandr Khinchin](wikipedia://en/1029748)[^o4] two years later.[^o5][^o6] In [ruin theory](wikipedia://en/14993253) the formula can be used to compute the probability of ultimate ruin (probability of an insurance company going bankrupt).[^o7]

## Mean queue length {#Mean_queue_length}

The formula states that the mean number of customers in system *L* is given by[^o9]

$$L = \rho + \frac{\rho^2 + \lambda^2 \operatorname{Var}(S)}{2(1-\rho)}$$

where

- $\lambda$ is the arrival rate of the [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process)
- $1/\mu$ is the mean of the service time distribution *S*
- $\rho=\lambda/\mu$ is the [utilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rental_utilization)
- Var(*S*) is the [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) of the service time distribution *S*.

For the mean queue length to be finite it is necessary that $\rho < 1$ as otherwise jobs arrive faster than they leave the queue. "Traffic intensity," ranges between 0 and 1, and is the mean fraction of time that the server is busy. If the arrival rate $\lambda$ is greater than or equal to the service rate $\mu$, the queuing delay becomes infinite. The variance term enters the expression due to [Feller's paradox](wikipedia://en/31776869).[^o10]

## Mean waiting time {#Mean_waiting_time}

If we write *W* for the mean time a customer spends in the system, then $W=W'+\mu^{-1}$ where $W'$ is the mean waiting time (time spent in the queue waiting for service) and $\mu$ is the service rate. Using [Little's law](wikipedia://en/184920), which states that

$$L=\lambda W$$

where

- *L* is the mean number of customers in system
- $\lambda$ is the arrival rate of the [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process)
- *W* is the mean time spent at the queue both waiting and being serviced,

so

$$W = \frac{L}{\lambda} = \frac{\rho + \lambda \mu \text{Var}(S)}{2(\mu-\lambda)} + \mu^{-1}.$$

We can write an expression for the mean waiting time as[^o12]

$$W' = \frac{L}{\lambda} - \mu^{-1} = \frac{\rho + \lambda \mu \text{Var}(S)}{2(\mu-\lambda)}.$$

## Queue length transform {#Queue_length_transform}

Writing π(*z*) for the [probability-generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability-generating_function) of the number of customers in the queue[^o14]

$$\pi(z) = \frac{(1-z)(1-\rho)g(\lambda(1-z))}{g(\lambda(1-z))-z}$$

where g(*s*) is the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of the service time probability density function.[^o15]

## Waiting time transform {#Waiting_time_transform}

Writing W<sup>\*</sup>(*s*) for the [Laplace–Stieltjes transform](wikipedia://en/365929) of the waiting time distribution,[^o14]

$$W^\ast(s) = \frac{(1-\rho)s}{s-\lambda(1-g(s))}$$

where again g(*s*) is the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) of service time probability density function. Each *n*th moment can be obtained by differentiating the transform *n* times, multiplying by (−1)<sup>*n*</sup> and evaluating at *s* = 0. If one multiplies this W<sup>\*</sup>(*s*) by g(*s*), then one receives the Laplace–Stieltjes transform for the distribution of the sojourn time (waiting plus service).
