# Geoffrey Heyworth, 1st Baron Heyworth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geoffrey Heyworth, 1st Baron Heyworth"}

**Geoffrey Heyworth, 1st Baron Heyworth** (18 October 1894 – 15 June 1974), was a British businessman and [public servant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_servant).

:::

At the outbreak of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), he was employed as an [accountant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountant) in [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), Canada. He served as a [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) in [134th Battalion, CEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/134th_Battalion,_CEF) 1916/1919. He was [wounded in action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wounded_in_action) in France on 14 July, 1918.[^o2]

Heyworth was chairman of [Imperial Chemical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries)[^o3] and of [Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever), a company for which he worked for 48 years until his retirement in 1960.[^o5] He was also a member of the [National Coal Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coal_Board) and the [London Passenger Transport Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Passenger_Transport_Board).[^o7] In 1951, he was appointed to a commission, led by [Sir Lionel Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Cohen,_Baron_Cohen), set up to look into the issue of taxation on income and profits. Having been [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Bachelor) in 1948, he was raised to the peerage as **Baron Heyworth** on 25 July, 1955, of Oxton in the County Palatine of Chester, in recognition of his "... public services".[^o4] He was the lead author of *[The Heyworth Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Heyworth_Report)* (1965), which led to the establishment of the [Social Science Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Science_Research_Council).[^o3] He was also [President of the Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/6524553) from 1949 to 1950.[^o11]

Lord Heyworth died in June 1974, aged 79.[^o12] The barony died with him.
