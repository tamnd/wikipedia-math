# Residue field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residue field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **residue field** is a basic construction in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990). If $R$ is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) and $\mathfrak{m}$ is a [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164), then the residue field is the [quotient ring](wikipedia://en/184635) $k=R/\mathfrak{m}$, which is a [field](wikipedia://en/10603).[^o2] Frequently, $R$ is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) and $\mathfrak{m}$ is then its unique maximal ideal.

:::

In abstract algebra, the [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440) of a polynomial is constructed using residue fields. Residue fields also applied in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), where to every point $x$ of a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) $X$ one associates its residue field $k(x)$.[^o3] One can say a little loosely that the residue field of a point of an abstract [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) is the *natural domain* for the coordinates of the point.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose that $R$ is a commutative [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391), with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. Then the **residue field** is the quotient ring $R/\mathfrak{m}$.

Now suppose that $X$ is a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) and $x$ is a point of $X$. By the definition of a scheme, we may find an affine [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) $\mathcal{U} = \text{Spec}(A)$ of $x$, with some [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) $A$. Considered in the neighbourhood $\mathcal{U}$, the point $x$ corresponds to a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq A$ (see [Zariski topology](wikipedia://en/241744)). The *[local ring](wikipedia://en/153391)* of $X$ at $x$ is by definition the [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ of $A$ by $A\setminus \mathfrak{p}$, and $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p} A_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Applying the construction above, we obtain the **residue field of the point $x$**:

$$k(x) := A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p} A_{\mathfrak{p}}.$$

Since localization is [exact](wikipedia://en/453945), $k(x)$ is the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) of $A/\mathfrak p$ (which is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) as $\mathfrak p$ is a prime ideal).[^o6] One can prove that this definition does not depend on the choice of the affine neighbourhood $\mathcal{U}$.[^o7]

A point is called [$\color{blue}k$-rational](wikipedia://en/2960200) for a certain field $k$, if $k(x)=k$.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider the [affine line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_line) $\mathbb{A}^1(k)=\operatorname{Spec}(k[t])$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $k$. If $k$ is [algebraically closed](wikipedia://en/1018), there are exactly two types of prime ideals, namely

- $$(t-a),\,a \in k$$
- $(0)$, the zero-ideal.

The residue fields are

- $$k[t]_{(t-a)}/(t-a)k[t]_{(t-a)} \cong k$$
- $k[t]_{(0)} \cong k(t)$, the function field over *k* in one variable.

If $k$ is not algebraically closed, then more types arise from the [irreducible polynomials](wikipedia://en/188725) of degree greater than 1. For example if $k=\mathbb{R}$, then the prime ideals generated by quadratic irreducible polynomials (such as $x^2+1$) all have residue field isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}$.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- For a scheme locally of [finite type](wikipedia://en/70728366) over a field $k$, a point $x$ is [closed](wikipedia://en/33086408) if and only if $k(x)$ is a finite extension of the base field $k$. This is a geometric formulation of [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215). In the above example, the points of the first kind are closed, having residue field $k$, whereas the second point is the [generic point](wikipedia://en/3043978), having [transcendence degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcendence_degree) 1 over $k$.
- A [morphism](wikipedia://en/7482797) $\operatorname{Spec}(K) \rightarrow X$, $K$ some field, is equivalent to giving a point $x \in X$ and an [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) $K/k(x)$.
- The [dimension](wikipedia://en/58058216) of a scheme of finite type over a field is equal to the transcendence degree of the residue field of the generic point.
