# Local class field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Local class field theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **local class field theory** (**LCFT**), introduced by [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118),[^o2] is the study of [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375); here, "local field" means a field which is complete with respect to an [absolute value](wikipedia://en/5042667) or a [discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) with a finite [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680): hence every local field is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) (as a [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field)) to the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers) **R**, the [complex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_numbers) **C**, a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of the [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) **Q**<sub>*p*</sub> (where *p* is any [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666)), or the field of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) **F**<sub>*q*</sub>((*T*)) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) **F**<sub>*q*</sub>.

:::

## Approaches to local class field theory {#Approaches_to_local_class_field_theory}

Local class field theory gives a description of the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) *G* of the maximal abelian extension of a local field *K* via the reciprocity map which acts from the multiplicative group *K*<sup>×</sup>=*K*\\{0}. For a finite abelian extension *L* of *K* the reciprocity map induces an isomorphism of the quotient group *K*<sup>×</sup>/*N*(*L*<sup>×</sup>) of *K*<sup>×</sup> by the [norm group](wikipedia://en/32769154) *N*(*L*<sup>×</sup>) of the extension *L*<sup>×</sup> to the Galois group Gal(*L*/*K*) of the extension.[^o4]

The existence theorem in local class field theory establishes a one-to-one correspondence between open subgroups of finite [index](wikipedia://en/230581) in the multiplicative group *K*<sup>×</sup> and finite abelian extensions of the field *K*. For a finite abelian extension *L* of *K* the corresponding open subgroup of finite index is the norm group *N*(*L*<sup>×</sup>). The reciprocity map sends higher groups of units to higher ramification subgroups.[^o4]<sup>Ch. 4</sup>

Using the local reciprocity map, one defines the Hilbert symbol and its generalizations. Finding explicit formulas for it is one of subdirections of the theory of local fields, it has a long and rich history, see e.g. [Sergei Vostokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Vostokov)'s review.[^o5]

There are [cohomological](wikipedia://en/194060) approaches and non-cohomological approaches to local class field theory. Cohomological approaches tend to be non-explicit, since they use the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) of the first [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) groups.

For various approaches to local class field theory see Ch. IV and sect. 7 Ch. IV of.[^o4] They include the Hasse approach of using the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078), cohomological approaches, the explicit methods of [Jürgen Neukirch](wikipedia://en/17477195), [Michiel Hazewinkel](wikipedia://en/40490058), the [Lubin-Tate theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubin-Tate_theory) and others.

## Generalizations of local class field theory {#Generalizations_of_local_class_field_theory}

Generalizations of local class field theory to local fields with quasi-finite residue field were easy extensions of the theory, obtained by G. Whaples in the 1950s.[^o5]<sup>ch. V</sup>

Explicit p-class field theory for local fields with [perfect](wikipedia://en/505088) and imperfect residue fields which are not finite has to deal with the new issue of norm groups of infinite index. Appropriate theories were constructed by [Ivan Fesenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Fesenko).[^o8][^o9] Fesenko's noncommutative local class field theory for arithmetically profinite Galois extensions of local fields studies appropriate local reciprocity cocycle map and its properties.[^o10] This arithmetic theory can be viewed as an alternative to the [representation-theoretical](wikipedia://en/19378200) [local Langlands correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Langlands_correspondence).

:::

## Higher local class field theory {#Higher_local_class_field_theory}

For a [higher-dimensional local field](wikipedia://en/32091871) $K$ there is a higher local reciprocity map which describes abelian extensions of the field in terms of open subgroups of finite index in the [Milnor K-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_K-group) of the field. Namely, if $K$ is an $n$-dimensional local field then one uses $\mathrm{K}^{\mathrm{M}}_n(K)$ or its separated quotient endowed with a suitable topology. When $n=1$ the theory becomes the usual local class field theory. Unlike the classical case, Milnor K-groups do not satisfy Galois module descent if $n>1$. General higher-dimensional local class field theory was developed by [K. Kato](wikipedia://en/4616818) and [I. Fesenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Fesenko).

Higher local class field theory is part of [higher class field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_class_field_theory) which studies abelian extensions (resp. abelian covers) of [rational function fields](wikipedia://en/381301) of [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_scheme) [regular](wikipedia://en/43016199) [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754) [flat](wikipedia://en/542520) over integers.
