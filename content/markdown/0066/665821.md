# Injective hull

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Injective hull"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **injective hull** (or **injective envelope**) of a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) is both the smallest [injective module](wikipedia://en/473775) containing it and the largest [essential extension](wikipedia://en/10119238) of it. Injective hulls were first described in (Eckmann Schopf 1953).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [module](wikipedia://en/276410) $E$ is called the **injective hull** of a module $M$, if $E$ is an [essential extension](wikipedia://en/10119238) of $M$, and $E$ is [injective](wikipedia://en/473775). Here, the base ring is a ring with unity, though possibly non-commutative.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- An injective module is its own injective hull.
- The injective hull of an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) (as a module over itself) is its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) (Lam 1999, Example 3.35).
- The injective hull of a cyclic $p$-group (as $\mathbb Z$-module) is a [Prüfer group](wikipedia://en/4885031) (Lam 1999, Example 3.36).
- The injective hull of a [torsion-free abelian group](wikipedia://en/11630973) $A$ is the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/2993836) $\mathbb Q \otimes_{\mathbb Z} A$.
- The injective hull of $R/\mathfrak{R}$ is $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb k}(R,\mathbb k)$, where $R$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathbb k$-[algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) with [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656) $\mathfrak R$ (Lam 1999, Example 3.41).
- A [simple module](wikipedia://en/29430) is necessarily the [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350) of its injective hull.
- The injective hull of the residue field of a [discrete valuation ring](wikipedia://en/848633) $(R,\mathfrak{m},k)$ where $\mathfrak{m} = x\cdot R$ is $R_x/R$.[^o10]
- In particular, the injective hull of $\mathbb{C}$ in $(\mathbb{C}[[t]],(t),\mathbb{C})$ is the module $\mathbb{C}((t))/\mathbb{C}[[t]]$.

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The injective hull of $M$ is unique up to isomorphisms which are the identity on $M$, however the isomorphism is not necessarily unique. This is because the injective hull's map extension property is not a full-fledged [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245). Because of this uniqueness, the hull can be denoted as $E(M)$.
- The injective hull $E$($M$) is a maximal [essential extension](wikipedia://en/10119238) of $M$ in the sense that if $M\subseteq E(M)\subsetneq B$ for a module $B$, then $M$ is not an essential submodule of $B$.
- The injective hull $E(M)$ is a minimal injective module containing $M$ in the sense that if $M\subseteq B$ for an injective module $B$, then $E(M)$ is (isomorphic to) a submodule of $B$.
- If $N$ is an essential submodule of $M$, then $E(N)=E(M)$.
- Every module $M$ has an injective hull. A construction of the injective hull in terms of homomorphisms $\mathrm{Hom}(I,M)$, where $I$ runs through the ideals of $R$, is given by (Fleischer 1968).
- The dual notion of a [projective cover](wikipedia://en/10273917) does *not* always exist for a module, however a [flat cover](wikipedia://en/34846642) exists for every module.

### Ring structure {#Ring_structure}

In some cases, for $R$ a subring of a self-injective ring $S$, the injective hull of $R$ will also have a ring structure.[^n1] For instance, taking $S$ to be a full [matrix ring](wikipedia://en/487541) over a field, and taking $R$ to be any ring containing every matrix which is zero in all but the last column, the injective hull of the right $R$-module $R$ is $S$. For instance, one can take $R$ to be the ring of all upper triangular matrices. However, it is not always the case that the injective hull of a ring has a ring structure, as an example in (Osofsky 1964) shows.

A large class of rings which do have ring structures on their injective hulls are the [nonsingular rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsingular_ring).[^n2] In particular, for an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), the injective hull of the ring (considered as a module over itself) is the [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271). The injective hulls of nonsingular rings provide an analogue of the ring of quotients for non-commutative rings, where the absence of the [Ore condition](wikipedia://en/3100560) may impede the formation of the [classical ring of quotients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_ring_of_quotients). This type of "ring of quotients" (as these more general "fields of fractions" are called) was pioneered in (Utumi 1956), and the connection to injective hulls was recognized in (Lambek 1963).

:::

## Uniform dimension and injective modules {#Uniform_dimension_and_injective_modules}

An $R$ module $M$ has finite [uniform dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_dimension) (=*finite rank*) $n$ if and only if the injective hull of $M$ is a finite direct sum of $n$ [indecomposable submodules](wikipedia://en/810549).

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

More generally, let $\mathbf C$ be an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063). An [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) $E$ is an **injective hull** of an object $M$ if $M$ → $E$ is an essential extension and $E$ is an [injective object](wikipedia://en/473779).

If $\mathbf C$ is [locally small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_small_category), satisfies [Grothendieck's axiom AB5](wikipedia://en/45063) and has [enough injectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_injectives), then every object in $\mathbf C$ has an injective hull (these three conditions are satisfied by the category of modules over a ring).[^o23] Every object in a [Grothendieck category](wikipedia://en/30051538) has an injective hull.

:::
