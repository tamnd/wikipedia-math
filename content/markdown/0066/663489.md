# Endre Szemerédi

Szemerédi Endre

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Endre Szemerédi"}

**Endre Szemerédi** (; born August 21, 1940) is a Hungarian-American[^o3] mathematician and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist), working in the field of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). He is the State of New Jersey Professor of Computer Science Emeritus at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), after having served in the position since 1986. He also holds a professor emeritus status at the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences).

:::

Szemerédi has won prizes in mathematics and science, including the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2012. He has made a number of discoveries in combinatorics and computer science, including [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703), the [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417), the [Erdős–Szemerédi theorem](wikipedia://en/35021032), the [Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajnal–Szemerédi_theorem) and the [Szemerédi–Trotter theorem](wikipedia://en/1051627).

## Early life {#Early_life}

Szemerédi was born in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest). Since his parents wished him to become a doctor, Szemerédi enrolled at a college of medicine, but he dropped out after six months (in an interview[^o5] he explained it: "I was not sure I could do work bearing such responsibility.").[^o6][^o7][^o8] He studied at the [Faculty of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELTE_Faculty_of_Sciences) of the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University) in Budapest and received his PhD from [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University). His adviser was [Israel Gelfand](wikipedia://en/364643). This stemmed from a misspelling, as Szemerédi originally wanted to study with [Alexander Gelfond](wikipedia://en/4542389).[^o6]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Szemerédi has been the State of New Jersey Professor of computer science at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) since 1986. He has held visiting positions at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) (1974), [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) (1980), the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina) (1981–1983) and the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) (1985–1986).[^o11]

## Work {#Work}

Endre Szemerédi has published over 200 scientific articles in the fields of discrete mathematics, theoretical computer science, arithmetic combinatorics and discrete geometry. He is best known for his proof from 1975 of an old conjecture of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998): if a sequence of natural numbers has positive [upper density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_density) then it contains arbitrarily long [arithmetic progressions](wikipedia://en/168389). This is now known as [Szemerédi's theorem](wikipedia://en/591703). One of the lemmas introduced in his proof is now known as the [Szemerédi regularity lemma](wikipedia://en/1962417), which has become an important lemma in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), being used for instance in [property testing](wikipedia://en/20506304) for graphs and in the theory of [graph limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_limit).

He is also known for the [Szemerédi–Trotter theorem](wikipedia://en/1051627) in [incidence geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidence_geometry_%28structure%29) and the [Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajnal–Szemerédi_theorem) and [Ruzsa–Szemerédi problem](wikipedia://en/59842050) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). [Miklós Ajtai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miklós_Ajtai) and Szemerédi proved the [corners theorem](wikipedia://en/31591175), an important step toward higher-dimensional generalizations of the [Szemerédi theorem](wikipedia://en/591703). With Ajtai and [János Komlós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Komlós_%28mathematician%29) he proved the *ct*<sup>2</sup>/log *t* upper bound for the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) *R*(3,*t*), and constructed a [sorting network](wikipedia://en/562061) of optimal depth. With Ajtai, [Václav Chvátal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Václav_Chvátal), and [Monroe M. Newborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Newborn), Szemerédi proved the famous [crossing number inequality](wikipedia://en/50834770), that a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with *n* vertices and *m* edges, where *m* > 4*n* has at least *m*<sup>3</sup> / 64*n*<sup>2</sup> [crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_lemma). With [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), he proved the [Erdős–Szemerédi theorem](wikipedia://en/35021032) on the number of sums and products in a finite set. With Wolfgang Paul, [Nick Pippenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Pippenger), and [William Trotter](wikipedia://en/41414002), he established a separation between [nondeterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-deterministic_Turing_machine) [linear time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_time) and [deterministic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_computation) linear time,[^o13] in the spirit of the infamous [P versus NP problem](wikipedia://en/6115).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Szemerédi has won numerous awards and honors for his contribution to mathematics and computer science. A few of them are listed here:

- Honorary [John von Neumann Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Professor) (2021) [^o15]
- Grünwald Prize (1967)[^o16]
- Grünwald Prize (1968)[^o16]
- [Rényi Prize](wikipedia://en/26466805) (1973)[^o17]
- [George Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya_Prize) for Achievement in Applied Combinatorics (SIAM),[^o18] (1975)
- Prize of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences (1979)[^o16]
- State of New Jersey Professorship (1986)[^o19]
- The [Leroy P. Steele Prize](wikipedia://en/1252550) for Seminal Contribution to Research (AMS),[^o20] (2008)
- The Rolf Schock Prize in Mathematics for deep and pioneering work from 1975 on arithmetic progressions in subsets of the integers (2008)[^o21]
- The [Széchenyi Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Széchenyi_Prize) of the Hungarian Republic for his many fundamental contributions to mathematics and computer science (2012)[^o25]
- The [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) for his fundamental contributions to discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science (2012)[^o22]
- [Hungarian Order of Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Order_of_Saint_Stephen)[^o23](2020)

Szemerédi is a corresponding member (1982), and member (1987) of the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) and a member (2010) of the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o24] He was elected to the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) in 2022.[^o25] He is also a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) and a permanent research fellow at the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) in Budapest. He was the Fairchild Distinguished Scholar at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1987–88. He is an honorary doctor[^o26] of [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in Prague. He was the lecturer in the Forty-Seventh Annual DeLong Lecture Series[^o27] at the [University of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado). He is also a recipient of the Aisenstadt Chair at CRM,[^o28] [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal). In 2008 he was the Eisenbud Professor at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California).

In 2012, Szemerédi was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) "for his fundamental contributions to discrete mathematics and theoretical computer science, and in recognition of the profound and lasting impact of these contributions on [additive number theory](wikipedia://en/4460333) and [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986)"[^o29] The Abel Prize citation also credited Szemerédi with bringing combinatorics to the centre-stage of mathematics and noted his place in the tradition of Hungarian mathematicians such as [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) who emphasized a problem-solving approach to mathematics.[^o30] Szemerédi reacted to the announcement by saying that "It is not my own personal achievement, but recognition for this field of mathematics and Hungarian mathematicians," that gave him the most pleasure.[^o31]

## Conferences {#Conferences}

*Image: AnIrregularMindBookCoverFairUse.jpg.* An Irregular Mind (2010 book cover)

On August 2–7, 2010, the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670) and the [János Bolyai Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/22777615) organized a conference in honor of the 70th birthday of Endre Szemerédi.[^o33]

Prior to the conference a volume of the Bolyai Society Mathematical Studies Series, *An Irregular Mind*, a collection of papers edited by [Imre Bárány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Bárány) and [József Solymosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/József_Solymosi), was published to celebrate Szemerédi's achievements on the occasion of his 70th birthday.[^o34] Another conference devoted to celebrating Szemerédi's work is the Third Abel Conference: A Mathematical Celebration of Endre Szemerédi.[^o35]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Szemerédi is married to Anna Kepes; they have five children, Andrea, Anita, Peter, Kati, and Zsuzsi.[^o27][^o37]
