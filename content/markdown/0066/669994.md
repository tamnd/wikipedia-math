# Aperiodic semigroup

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aperiodic semigroup"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **aperiodic semigroup** is a [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) *S* such that every element is aperiodic, that is, for each *x* in *S* there exists a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n* such that *x<sup>n</sup>* = *x*<sup>*n*+1</sup>.[^o2] An **aperiodic monoid** is an aperiodic semigroup which is a [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652).

:::

## Finite aperiodic semigroups {#Finite_aperiodic_semigroups}

A finite semigroup is aperiodic [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it contains no nontrivial [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011), so a synonym used (only?) in such contexts is **group-free semigroup**. In terms of [Green's relations](wikipedia://en/1192012), a finite semigroup is aperiodic if and only if its *H*-relation is trivial. These two characterizations extend to [group-bound semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group-bound_semigroup).

A celebrated result of algebraic [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356) due to [Marcel-Paul Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337) asserts that a language is [star-free](wikipedia://en/4922792) if and only if its [syntactic monoid](wikipedia://en/670026) is finite and aperiodic.[^o4]

A consequence of the [Krohn–Rhodes theorem](wikipedia://en/513506) is that every finite aperiodic monoid divides a [wreath product](wikipedia://en/146625) of copies of the [three-element flip-flop monoid](wikipedia://en/45638883), consisting of an identity element and two right zeros. The two-sided Krohn–Rhodes theorem alternatively characterizes finite aperiodic monoids as divisors of iterated block products of copies of the [two-element semilattice](wikipedia://en/22717084).
