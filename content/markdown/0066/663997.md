# Eduard Study

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Study"}

**Christian Hugo Eduard Study** ( ; 23 March 1862, [Coburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coburg) – 6 January 1930, [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for work on [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) of ternary forms (1889) and for the study of [spherical trigonometry](wikipedia://en/650405). He is also known for contributions to space geometry, hypercomplex numbers, and criticism of early physical chemistry.

:::

Study was born in [Coburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coburg) in the Duchy of [Saxe-Coburg-Gotha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxe-Coburg-Gotha).

## Career {#Career}

Eduard Study began his studies at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena), the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg), [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University), and the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München). He loved to study biology, especially entomology. He was awarded the doctorate in mathematics at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) in 1884. [Paul Gordan](wikipedia://en/1059850), an expert in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) was at Leipzig, and Study returned there as Privatdozent. In 1888, he moved to [Marburg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marburg_University) and in 1893 embarked on a speaking tour in the U.S.A. He appeared at a Congress of Mathematicians in Chicago as part of the [World's Columbian Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition)[^o4] and took part in mathematics at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University). Back in Germany, in 1894, he was appointed extraordinary professor at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). Then, he gained the rank of full professor in 1897 at the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald). In 1904, he was called to the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) as the position held by [Rudolf Lipschitz](wikipedia://en/339965) was vacant. There he settled until retirement in 1927.

Study gave a [plenary address at the International Congress of Mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenary_address_at_the_International_Congress_of_Mathematicians) in 1904 at Heidelberg[^o5] and another in 1912 at Cambridge, UK.[^o6]

## Euclidean space group and dual quaternions {#Euclidean_space_group_and_dual_quaternions}

In 1891 Eduard Study published "Of Motions and Translations, in two parts". It treats the [Euclidean group](wikipedia://en/652164) E(3). The second part of his article introduces the [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) of [dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/8730871), that is numbers

$$q = a + bi + cj + dk \!$$

where *a*, *b*, *c*, and *d* are [dual numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_numbers) and {1, *i*, *j*, *k*} multiply as in the [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130). Actually Study uses notation such that

$$e_0 = 1,\ e_1 = i,\ e_2 = j,\ e_3 = k, \!$$

$$\varepsilon _0 = \varepsilon ,\ \varepsilon _1 = \varepsilon i,\ \varepsilon _2 = \varepsilon j,\ \varepsilon _3 = \varepsilon k. \!$$

The multiplication table is found on page 520 of volume 39 (1891) in [Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381) under the title "Von Bewegungen und Umlegungen, I. und II. Abhandlungen". Eduard Study cites [William Kingdon Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820) as an earlier source on these [biquaternions](wikipedia://en/1207070). In 1901 Study published *Geometrie der Dynamen*[^o8] also using dual quaternions. In 1913, he wrote a review article treating both E(3) and [elliptic geometry](wikipedia://en/236020). This article, "Foundations and goals of analytical kinematics"[^o9] develops the field of [kinematics](wikipedia://en/65914), in particular exhibiting an element of E(3) as a [homography of dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/1318037#Homography).

Study's use of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384) was noted in *A History of Algebra* (1985) by [B. L. van der Waerden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._L._van_der_Waerden). On the other hand, Joe Rooney recounts these developments in relation to kinematics.[^o10]

## Hypercomplex numbers {#Hypercomplex_numbers}

Study showed an early interest in systems of complex numbers and their application to transformation groups with his article in 1890.[^o12] He addressed this popular subject again in 1898 in *[Klein's encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein's_encyclopedia)*. The essay explored [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and other hypercomplex number systems.[^o13] This 34-page article was expanded to 138 pages in 1908 by [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274), who surveyed the hypercomplex systems in *Encyclopédie des sciences mathématiques pures et appliqueés*. Cartan acknowledged Eduard Study's guidance, in his title, with the words "after Eduard Study".

In the 1993 biography of Cartan by Akivis and Rosenfeld, one reads:[^o14]

\[Study\] defined the algebra °**H** of '[semiquaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-complex_number)' with the units 1, *i*, *ε*, *η* having the properties $i^2 = -1, \ \varepsilon ^2 = 0, \ i \varepsilon = - \varepsilon i = \eta. \!$

[Semiquaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-complex_number) are often called 'Study's quaternions'.

In 1985 Helmut Karzel and Günter Kist developed "Study's quaternions" as the kinematic algebra corresponding to the [group of motions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_motions) of the Euclidean plane. These quaternions arise in "Kinematic algebras and their geometries" alongside ordinary quaternions and the ring of 2×2 [real matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_matrix) which Karzel and Kist cast as the kinematic algebras of the elliptic plane and hyperbolic plane respectively. See the "Motivation and Historical Review" at page 437 of *Rings and Geometry*, R. Kaya editor.

Some of the other hypercomplex systems in that study are [dual numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_numbers), [dual quaternions](wikipedia://en/8730871), and [split-biquaternions](wikipedia://en/4166765), all being [associative algebras](wikipedia://en/2112) over **R**.

## Ruled surfaces {#Ruled_surfaces}

Study's work with [dual numbers](wikipedia://en/42169) and [line coordinates](wikipedia://en/21252759) was noted by [Heinrich Guggenheimer](wikipedia://en/24089764) in 1963 in his book *Differential Geometry* (see pages 162–5). He cites and proves the following theorem of Study: The oriented lines in **R**<sup>3</sup> are in one-to-one correspondence with the points of the dual unit sphere in **D**<sup>3</sup>. Later he says "A differentiable curve **A**(*u*) on the dual unit sphere, depending on a *real* parameter *u*, represents a differentiable family of straight lines in **R**<sup>3</sup>: a [ruled surface](wikipedia://en/705158). The lines **A**(*u*) are the *generators* or *rulings* of the surface." Guggenheimer also shows the representation of the Euclidean motions in **R**<sup>3</sup> by orthogonal dual matrices.

## Hermitian form metric {#Hermitian_form_metric}

In 1905 Study wrote "Kürzeste Wege im komplexen Gebiet" (Shortest paths in the complex domain) for [Mathematische Annalen](wikipedia://en/1423381) (60:321–378). Some of its contents were anticipated by [Guido Fubini](wikipedia://en/349051) a year before. The distance Study refers to is a [Hermitian form](wikipedia://en/860138#Hermitian_form) on [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). Since then this [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) has been called the [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486). Study was careful in 1905 to distinguish the hyperbolic and elliptic cases in Hermitian geometry.

## Valence theory {#Valence_theory}

Somewhat surprisingly Eduard Study is known by practitioners of [quantum chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_chemistry). Like [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861), [Paul Gordan](wikipedia://en/1059850) believed that invariant theory could contribute to the understanding of [chemical valence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_%28chemistry%29). In 1900 Gordan and his student G. Alexejeff contributed an article on an analogy between the [coupling problem for angular momenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_momentum_coupling) and their work on invariant theory to the *[Zeitschrift für Physikalische Chemie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeitschrift_für_Physikalische_Chemie)* (v. 35, p. 610). In 2006 Wormer and Paldus summarized Study's role as follows:[^o18]

The analogy, lacking a physical basis at the time, was criticised heavily by the **mathematician E. Study** and ignored completely by the chemistry community of the 1890s. After the advent of quantum mechanics it became clear, however, that chemical valences arise from electron–spin couplings ... and that electron spin functions are, in fact, binary forms of the type studied by [Gordan and Clebsch](wikipedia://en/1074990).
