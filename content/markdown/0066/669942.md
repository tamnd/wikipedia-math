# Generalized star-height problem

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

Can all [regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723) be expressed using generalized regular expressions with a limited nesting depth of [Kleene stars](wikipedia://en/16750)?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized star-height problem"}

In [formal language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language_theory), the **generalized star-height problem** is the open question whether all [regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723) can be expressed using generalized regular expressions with a limited nesting depth of [Kleene stars](wikipedia://en/16750). Here, generalized regular expressions are defined like [regular expressions](wikipedia://en/25717#Formal_language_theory), but with a built-in complement operator. For a regular language, its [generalized star height](wikipedia://en/669992) is defined as the minimum nesting depth of Kleene stars needed in order to describe the language by means of a generalized regular expression.

:::

More specifically, it is an open question whether a nesting depth of more than 1 is required, and if so, whether there is an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) to determine the minimum required star height.[^n1]

[Regular languages](wikipedia://en/25723) of generalized star-height 0 are also known as [star-free languages](wikipedia://en/4922792). A theorem of [Schützenberger](wikipedia://en/2817337) provides an algebraic characterization of star-free languages by means of aperiodic [syntactic monoids](wikipedia://en/670026).[^n2] In particular, star-free languages are a proper decidable subclass of regular languages.
