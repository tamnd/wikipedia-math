# Khatri–Rao product

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Khatri–Rao product"}

In mathematics, the **Khatri–Rao product** or **block Kronecker product** of two [partitioned matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioned_matrix) $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ is defined as[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

$$\mathbf{A} \ast \mathbf{B} = \left(\mathbf{A}_{ij} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{ij}\right)_{ij}$$

in which the *ij*-th block is the *m<sub>i</sub>p<sub>i</sub>* × *n<sub>j</sub>q<sub>j</sub>* sized [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430) of the corresponding blocks of **A** and **B**, assuming the number of row and column partitions of both [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) is equal. The size of the product is then (Σ*<sub>i</sub> m<sub>i</sub>p<sub>i</sub>*) × (Σ*<sub>j</sub> n<sub>j</sub>q<sub>j</sub>*).

For example, if **A** and **B** both are 2 × 2 partitioned matrices e.g.:

$$\mathbf{A} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{A}_{11} & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{A}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{22} \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} {c c | c} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ \hline 7 & 8 & 9 \end{array} \right] ,\quad \mathbf{B} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{B}_{11} & \mathbf{B}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{B}_{21} & \mathbf{B}_{22} \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c c} 1 & 4 & 7 \\ \hline 2 & 5 & 8 \\ 3 & 6 & 9 \end{array} \right] ,$$

we obtain:

$$\mathbf{A} \ast \mathbf{B} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{A}_{11} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{11} & \mathbf{A}_{12} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{A}_{21} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{22} \otimes \mathbf{B}_{22} \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} {c c | c c} 1 & 2 & 12 & 21 \\ 4 & 5 & 24 & 42 \\ \hline 14 & 16 & 45 & 72 \\ 21 & 24 & 54 & 81 \end{array} \right].$$

This is a submatrix of the [Tracy–Singh product](wikipedia://en/712430#Tracy–Singh_product)[^o5] of the two matrices (each partition in this example is a partition in a corner of the [Tracy–Singh product](wikipedia://en/712430#Tracy–Singh_product)).

## Column-wise Kronecker product {#Column-wise_Kronecker_product}

The column-wise [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430) of two matrices is a special case of the Khatri-Rao product as defined above, and may also be called *the* Khatri–Rao product. This product assumes the partitions of the matrices are their columns. In this case *m*<sub>1</sub> = *m*, *p*<sub>1</sub> = *p*, *n* = *q* and for each *j*: *n<sub>j</sub>* = *q<sub>j</sub>* = 1. The resulting product is a *mp* × *n* matrix of which each column is the Kronecker product of the corresponding columns of *A* and *B*. Using the matrices from the previous examples with the columns partitioned:

$$\mathbf{C} = \left[ \begin{array} { c | c | c} \mathbf{C}_1 & \mathbf{C}_2 & \mathbf{C}_3 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c | c} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{array} \right] ,\quad \mathbf{D} = \left[ \begin{array} { c | c | c } \mathbf{D}_1 & \mathbf{D}_2 & \mathbf{D}_3 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} { c | c | c } 1 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 & 8 \\ 3 & 6 & 9 \end{array} \right] ,$$

so that:

$$\mathbf{C} \ast \mathbf{D} = \left[ \begin{array} { c | c | c } \mathbf{C}_1 \otimes \mathbf{D}_1 & \mathbf{C}_2 \otimes \mathbf{D}_2 & \mathbf{C}_3 \otimes \mathbf{D}_3 \end{array} \right] = \left[ \begin{array} { c | c | c } 1 & 8 & 21 \\ 2 & 10 & 24 \\ 3 & 12 & 27 \\ 4 & 20 & 42 \\ 8 & 25 & 48 \\ 12 & 30 & 54 \\ 7 & 32 & 63 \\ 14 & 40 & 72 \\ 21 & 48 & 81 \end{array} \right].$$

This column-wise version of the Khatri–Rao product is useful in linear algebra approaches to data analytical processing[^o7] and in optimizing the solution of inverse problems dealing with a diagonal matrix.[^o8][^o9]

In 1996 the column-wise Khatri–Rao product was proposed to estimate the [angles of arrival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_arrival) (AOAs) and delays of multipath signals[^o10] and four coordinates of signals sources[^o11] at a [digital antenna array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_antenna_array).

## Face-splitting product {#Face-splitting_product}

*Image: Face splitting product of matrices.jpg.* Face splitting product of matrices

An alternative concept of the matrix product, which uses row-wise splitting of matrices with a given quantity of rows, was proposed by [V. Slyusar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadym_Slyusar)[^o14] in 1996.[^o11][^o15][^o16][^o17][^o18]

This matrix operation was named the "face-splitting product" of matrices[^o15][^o17] or the "transposed Khatri–Rao product". This type of operation is based on row-by-row [Kronecker products](wikipedia://en/712430) of two matrices. Using the matrices from the previous examples with the rows partitioned:

$$\mathbf{C} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_1 \\\hline \mathbf{C}_2 \\\hline \mathbf{C}_3\\ \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\\hline 4 & 5 & 6 \\\hline 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix} ,\quad \mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{D}_1\\\hline \mathbf{D}_2\\\hline \mathbf{D}_3\\ \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 7 \\\hline 2 & 5 & 8 \\\hline 3 & 6 & 9 \end{bmatrix} ,$$

the result can be obtained:[^o11][^o15][^o17]

$$\mathbf{C} \bullet \mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_1 \otimes \mathbf{D}_1\\\hline \mathbf{C}_2 \otimes \mathbf{D}_2\\\hline \mathbf{C}_3 \otimes \mathbf{D}_3\\ \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 7 & 2 & 8 & 14 & 3 & 12 & 21 \\\hline 8 & 20 & 32 & 10 & 25 & 40 & 12 & 30 & 48 \\\hline 21 & 42 & 63 & 24 & 48 & 72 & 27 & 54 & 81 \end{bmatrix}.$$

:::

## Main properties {#Main_properties}

In the following properties, the operator $\bullet$ denotes the row-wise Kronecker product (face-splitting product) and the operator $\ast$ denotes the column-wise Kronecker product

::: {#o29 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

### Theorem {#Theorem}

Source:[^o22]

If $M = T^{(1)} \bullet \dots \bullet T^{(c)}$, where $T^{(1)}, \dots, T^{(c)}$ are independent components a random matrix $T$ with independent identically distributed rows $T_1, \dots, T_m\in \mathbb R^d$, such that

$E\left[(T_1x)^2\right] = \left\|x\right\|_2^2$ and $E\left[(T_1 x)^p\right]^\frac{1}{p} \le \sqrt{ap}\|x\|_2$,

then for any vector $x$

$$\left| \left\|Mx\right\|_2 - \left\|x\right\|_2 \right| < \varepsilon \left\|x\right\|_2$$

with probability $1 - \delta$ if the quantity of rows

$$m = (4a)^{2c} \varepsilon^{-2} \log 1/\delta + (2ae)\varepsilon^{-1}(\log 1/\delta)^c.$$

In particular, if the entries of $T$ are $\pm 1$ can get

$$m = O\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\log1/\delta + \varepsilon^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{c}\log1/\delta\right)^c\right)$$

which matches the [Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma](wikipedia://en/14855952) of $m = O\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\log1/\delta\right)$ when $\varepsilon$ is small.

:::

## Block face-splitting product {#Block_face-splitting_product}

*Image: Transposed Block Face-Splitting Product.jpg.* Transposed block face-splitting product in the context of a multi-face radar model[^o32]

According to the definition of [V. Slyusar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadym_Slyusar)[^o11][^o17] the block face-splitting product of two [partitioned matrices](wikipedia://en/457464) with a given quantity of rows in blocks

$$\mathbf{A} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{A}_{11} & \mathbf{A}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{A}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{22} \end{array} \right] ,\quad \mathbf{B} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{B}_{11} & \mathbf{B}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{B}_{21} & \mathbf{B}_{22} \end{array} \right] ,$$

can be written as :

$$\mathbf{A} [\bullet] \mathbf{B} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{A}_{11} \bullet \mathbf{B}_{11} & \mathbf{A}_{12} \bullet \mathbf{B}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{A}_{21} \bullet \mathbf{B}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{22} \bullet \mathbf{B}_{22} \end{array} \right] .$$

The **transposed block face-splitting product** (or Block column-wise version of the Khatri–Rao product) of two [partitioned matrices](wikipedia://en/457464) with a given quantity of columns in blocks has a view:[^o11][^o17]

$$\mathbf{A} [\ast] \mathbf{B} = \left[ \begin{array} {c | c} \mathbf{A}_{11} \ast \mathbf{B}_{11} & \mathbf{A}_{12} \ast \mathbf{B}_{12} \\ \hline \mathbf{A}_{21} \ast \mathbf{B}_{21} & \mathbf{A}_{22} \ast \mathbf{B}_{22} \end{array} \right].$$

## Applications {#Applications}

The Face-splitting product and the Block Face-splitting product used in the [tensor](wikipedia://en/29965)-matrix theory of [digital antenna arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_antenna_array). These operations are also used in:

- [Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence) and [Machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) systems to minimization of [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) and [tensor sketch](wikipedia://en/64295245) operations,[^o22]
- A popular [Natural Language Processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Language_Processing) models, and hypergraph models of similarity,[^o35]
- [Generalized linear array model](wikipedia://en/7786819) in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685)[^o25]
- Two- and multidimensional [P-spline](wikipedia://en/21834#P-spline) approximation of data,[^o24]
- Studies of genotype x environment interactions.[^o36]
