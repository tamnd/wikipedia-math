# Description

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Description"}

**Description** is any type of communication that aims to make vivid a place, object, person, group, or other physical entity.[^o2] It is one of four [rhetorical modes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhetorical_modes) (also known as *modes of discourse*), along with [exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_%28literary_technique%29), [argumentation](wikipedia://en/1317383), and [narration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narration).[^o3]

:::

## Fiction writing {#Fiction_writing}

Fiction writing specifically has modes such as [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_%28fiction%29), exposition, description, [dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_in_writing), summary, and transition.[^o5] Author [Peter Selgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Selgin) refers to *methods*, including action, dialogue, thoughts, summary, [scenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scene_%28drama%29), and description.[^o6]

Description is the mode for transmitting a mental image of the particulars of a story. Together with dialogue, narration, exposition, and summarization, it is one of the most widely recognized of the fiction-writing modes. As stated in *Writing from A to Z*, edited by Kirk Polking, it is more than the amassing of details; it is bringing something to life by carefully choosing and arranging words and phrases to produce the desired effect.[^o7]

## Purple prose {#Purple_prose}

A purple patch is an over-written passage in which the writer has strained too hard to achieve an impressive effect, by elaborate figures or other means. The phrase ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): "purpureus pannus") was first used by the Roman poet [Horace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace) in his *[Ars Poetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Poetica_%28Horace%29)* (c. 20 [BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_Christ)) to denote an irrelevant and excessively ornate passage; the sense of irrelevance is normally absent in modern usage, although such passages are usually incongruous. By extension, **purple prose** is lavishly figurative, rhythmic, or otherwise overwrought.[^o9]

## Philosophy {#Philosophy}

In philosophy, the [nature of description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_descriptions) has been an important question since [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)'s classical texts.[^o11]
