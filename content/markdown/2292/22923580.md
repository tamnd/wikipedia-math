# Alfred Brauer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Brauer"}

**Alfred Theodor Brauer** (April 9, 1894 – December 23, 1985) was a German-American mathematician who did work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was born in [Charlottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenburg), and studied at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin). As he served Germany in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), even being injured in the war, he was able to keep his position longer than many other Jewish academics who had been forced out after Hitler's rise to power.[^o3] In 1935 he lost his position and in 1938 he tried to leave Germany, but was not able to until the following year. He initially worked in [the Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_United_States), but in 1942 he settled into a position at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill). A good deal of his works, and the Alfred T. Brauer library, would be linked to this university. He occasionally taught at [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) after he retired from Chapel Hill at 70.[^o4] He died in [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina), aged 91.

:::

He was the brother of the mathematician [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134), who was the founder of [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685).[^o3]
