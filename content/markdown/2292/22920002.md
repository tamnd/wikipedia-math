# Determinantal variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Determinantal variety"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), **determinantal varieties** are spaces of matrices with a given upper bound on their [ranks](wikipedia://en/26561). Their significance comes from the fact that many examples in algebraic geometry are of this form, such as the [Segre embedding](wikipedia://en/1623980) of a product of two [projective spaces](wikipedia://en/242135).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given *m* and *n* and *r* \< min(*m*, *n*), the **determinantal variety** *Y*<sub>*r*</sub> is the set of all *m* × *n* matrices (over a field *k*) with rank ≤ *r*. This is naturally an [algebraic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_set) as the condition that a matrix have rank ≤ *r* is given by the vanishing of all of its (*r* + 1) × (*r* + 1) [minors](wikipedia://en/262107); in fact this is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808), since the ideal generated by these minors is [prime](wikipedia://en/24928). Considering the generic *m* × *n* matrix whose entries are [algebraically independent](wikipedia://en/287808) variables *x*<sub>*i*,*j*</sub>, these minors are polynomials of degree *r* + 1. The ideal of *k*\[*x*<sub>*i*,*j*</sub>\] generated by these polynomials is a **determinantal ideal**. Since the equations defining minors are homogeneous, one can consider *Y*<sub>*r*</sub> either as an [affine variety](wikipedia://en/320468) in *mn*-dimensional [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834), or as a [projective variety](wikipedia://en/320469) in (*mn* − 1)-dimensional [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) defining the determinantal variety is generated by the (*r* + 1) × (*r* + 1) minors of a *generic* square matrix, that is a matrix whose entries are distinct indeterminates. This ideal is a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) in the polynomial ring in these indeterminates (Bruns-Vetter, Theorem 2.10).

Assuming that we consider *Y*<sub>*r*</sub> as an [affine variety](wikipedia://en/320468), its dimension is *r*(*m* + *n* − *r*). One way to see this is as follows: form the product space $\mathbf{A}^{mn} \times \mathbf{Gr}(r,m)$ over $\mathbf{A}^{mn}$ where $\mathbf{Gr}(r,m)$ is the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *r*-planes in an *m*-dimensional vector space, and consider the subspace $Z_r = \{ (A, W) \mid A(k^n) \subseteq W \}$, which is a [desingularization](wikipedia://en/9914115) of $Y_r$ (over the open set of matrices with rank exactly *r*, this map is an isomorphism), and $Z_r$ is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over $\mathbf{Gr}(r,m)$ which is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Hom}(k^n, \mathcal{R})$ where $\mathcal{R}$ is the tautological bundle over the Grassmannian. So $\dim Y_r = \dim Z_r$ since they are [birationally equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birationally_equivalent), and $\dim Z_r = \dim \mathbf{Gr}(r,m) + nr = r(m-r) + nr$ since the fiber of $\mathrm{Hom}(k^n, \mathcal{R})$ has dimension *nr*.

The above shows that the matrices of rank \<*r* contains the [singular locus](wikipedia://en/981915) of $Y_r$, and in fact one has equality. This fact can be verified using that the radical ideal is given by the minors along with the [Jacobian criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_criterion) for nonsingularity.

The variety *Y*<sub>*r*</sub> naturally has an action of $G = \mathbf{GL}(m) \times \mathbf{GL}(n)$, a product of [general linear groups](wikipedia://en/113564). The problem of determining the [syzygies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syzygy_%28mathematics%29) of $Y_r$, when the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the [field](wikipedia://en/10603) is zero, was solved by [Alain Lascoux](wikipedia://en/32555229), using the natural action of *G*.

:::

## Related topics {#Related_topics}

One can "globalize" the notion of determinantal varieties by considering the space of linear maps between two vector bundles on an algebraic variety. Then the determinantal varieties fall into the general study of [degeneracy loci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degeneracy_loci). An expression for the cohomology class of these degeneracy loci is given by the [Thom-Porteous formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom-Porteous_formula), see (Fulton-Pragacz).
