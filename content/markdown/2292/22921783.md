# Du Val singularity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Du Val singularity"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), a **Du Val singularity**, also called **simple surface singularity**, **Kleinian singularity**, or **rational double point**, is an isolated singularity of a complex surface which is modeled on a double branched cover of the plane, with minimal resolution obtained by replacing the singular point with a tree of smooth rational curves, with intersection pattern dual to a Dynkin diagram of [A-D-E singularity](wikipedia://en/648042) type. They are the [canonical singularities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_singularities) (or, equivalently, rational Gorenstein singularities) in dimension 2. They were studied by [Patrick du Val](wikipedia://en/10637467)[^o2][^o3][^o4] and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887).

:::

The Du Val singularities also appear as quotients of $\mathbb{C}^2$ by a finite subgroup of [SL<sub>2</sub>$(\mathbb{C})$](wikipedia://en/314493); equivalently, a finite subgroup of [SU(2)](wikipedia://en/173997), which are known as [binary polyhedral groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_polyhedral_group).[^o5] The rings of [invariant polynomials](wikipedia://en/1798843) of these finite group actions were computed by Klein, and are essentially the coordinate rings of the singularities; this is a classic result in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762).[^o6][^o7]

## Classification {#Classification}

*Image: Simply Laced Dynkin Diagrams.svg.* Du Val singularies are classified by the [simply laced Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_laced_Dynkin_diagram), a form of [ADE classification](wikipedia://en/648042).

The possible Du Val singularities are (up to analytical isomorphism):

- $$A_n: \quad w^2+x^2+y^{n+1}=0$$
- $$D_n: \quad w^2+y(x^2+y^{n-2}) = 0 \qquad (n\ge 4)$$
- $$E_6: \quad w^2+x^3+y^4=0$$
- $$E_7: \quad w^2+x(x^2+y^3)=0$$
- $$E_8: \quad w^2+x^3+y^5=0.$$
