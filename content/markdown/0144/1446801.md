# Ewens's sampling formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ewens's sampling formula"}

In [population genetics](wikipedia://en/219268), **Ewens's sampling formula** describes the [probabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilities) associated with counts of how many different [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) are observed a given number of times in the [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Ewens's sampling formula, introduced by [Warren Ewens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Ewens), states that under certain conditions (specified below), if a random sample of *n* [gametes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete) is taken from a population and classified according to the [gene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene) at a particular [locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_%28genetics%29) then the [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) that there are *a*<sub>1</sub> [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) represented once in the sample, and *a*<sub>2</sub> alleles represented twice, and so on, is

$$\operatorname{Pr}(a_1,\dots,a_n; \theta)={n! \over \theta(\theta+1)\cdots(\theta+n-1)}\prod_{j=1}^n{\theta^{a_j} \over j^{a_j} a_j!},$$

for some positive number *θ* representing the [population mutation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_mutation_rate), whenever $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ is a sequence of nonnegative integers such that

$$a_1+2a_2+3a_3+\cdots+na_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n} i a_i = n.\,$$

The phrase "under certain conditions" used above is made precise by the following assumptions:

- The sample size *n* is small by comparison to the size of the whole population; and
- The population is in statistical equilibrium under [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) and [genetic drift](wikipedia://en/72016) and the role of selection at the locus in question is negligible; and
- Every mutant allele is novel.

This is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) on the set of all [partitions of the integer](wikipedia://en/104790) *n*. Among probabilists and statisticians it is often called the **multivariate Ewens distribution**.

:::

## Mathematical properties {#Mathematical_properties}

When *θ* = 0, the probability is 1 that all *n* genes are the same. When *θ* = 1, then the distribution is precisely that of the integer partition induced by a uniformly distributed [random permutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_permutation). As *θ* → ∞, the probability that no two of the *n* genes are the same approaches 1.

This family of probability distributions enjoys the property that if after the sample of *n* is taken, *m* of the *n* gametes are chosen without replacement, then the resulting probability distribution on the set of all partitions of the smaller integer *m* is just what the formula above would give if *m* were put in place of *n*.

The Ewens distribution arises naturally from the [Chinese restaurant process](wikipedia://en/4482900).
