# Dave Bayer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dave Bayer"}

**David Allen Bayer** (born November 29, 1955) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [symbolic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation) and for his consulting work in the movie industry. He is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College), [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Bayer graduated from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) with highest honors in 1977. As a Swarthmore undergraduate, he attended a course on combinatorial algorithms given by [Herbert Wilf](wikipedia://en/3782688). During that semester, Bayer related several original ideas to Wilf on the subject. These contributions were later incorporated into the second edition of Wilf and [Albert Nijenhuis](wikipedia://en/11222925)' influential book *Combinatorial Algorithms*, with a detailed acknowledgement by its authors.[^o4] Bayer subsequently earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1982 under the direction of [Heisuke Hironaka](wikipedia://en/13822) with a dissertation entitled *The Division Algorithm and the Hilbert Scheme*. He joined [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) thereafter.

Bayer is the son of Joan and [Bryce Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryce_Bayer), the inventor of the [Bayer filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_filter).[^o5]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Bayer has worked in various areas of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [symbolic computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbolic_computation), including [Hilbert functions](wikipedia://en/6612596), [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262), and [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730). He has written a number of highly cited papers in these areas with other notable mathematicians, including [Bernd Sturmfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Sturmfels), [Jeffrey Lagarias](wikipedia://en/4327781), [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467), [Irena Peeva](wikipedia://en/43076151), and [David Eisenbud](wikipedia://en/3649845). Bayer is one of ten individuals cited in the white paper published by the pseudonymous [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto) describing the technological underpinnings of [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265). He is cited as a co-author, along with [Stuart Haber](wikipedia://en/70206006) and [W. Scott Stornetta](wikipedia://en/70149498), of a paper to improve on a system for tamper-proofing [timestamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timestamps) by incorporating [Merkle trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkle_trees).[^o7]

## Consulting {#Consulting}

Bayer was a mathematics [consultant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultant) for the film *[A Beautiful Mind](wikipedia://en/3569822)*, the [biopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biographical_film) of [John Nash](wikipedia://en/102567), and also had a cameo as one of the "Pen Ceremony" professors.[^o9]
