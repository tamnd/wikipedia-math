# Equivariant index theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Equivariant index theorem"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **equivariant index theorem**, of which there are several variants, computes the (graded) trace of an element of a compact [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) acting in given setting in terms of the integral over the [fixed points](wikipedia://en/449738) of the element. If the element is neutral, then the theorem reduces to the usual [index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_theorem).

:::

The classical formula such as the [Atiyah–Bott formula](wikipedia://en/44849824) is a special case of the theorem.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $\pi: E \to M$ be a [clifford module bundle](wikipedia://en/42500406). Assume a compact Lie group *G* acts on both *E* and *M* so that $\pi$ is [equivariant](wikipedia://en/42482272). Let *E* be given a connection that is compatible with the action of *G*. Finally, let *D* be a [Dirac operator](wikipedia://en/1079448) on *E* associated to the given data. In particular, *D* commutes with *G* and thus the kernel of *D* is a finite-dimensional representation of *G*.

The **equivariant index** of *E* is a [virtual character](wikipedia://en/3053436) given by taking the [supertrace](wikipedia://en/1249738):

$$\operatorname{str}(g\mid\ker D) = \operatorname{tr}(g\mid\ker D^+) - \operatorname{tr}(g\mid\ker D^-).$$

:::
