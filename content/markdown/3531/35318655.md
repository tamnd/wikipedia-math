# Coons patch

*Image: Example of coons surface.svg.* Sample Coons patch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coons patch"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Coons patch**, is a type of [surface patch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_patch) or [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) [parametrization](wikipedia://en/453198) used in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics) to smoothly join other [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) together, and in [computational mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_mechanics) applications, particularly in [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581) and [boundary element method](wikipedia://en/1678795), to mesh problem domains into elements.

:::

Coons patches are named after [Steven Anson Coons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Anson_Coons), and date to 1967.[^o2]

## Bilinear blending {#Bilinear_blending}

Given four [space](wikipedia://en/27667) curves *c*<sub>0</sub>(*s*), *c*<sub>1</sub>(*s*), *d*<sub>0</sub>(*t*), *d*<sub>1</sub>(*t*) which meet at four corners *c*<sub>0</sub>(0) = *d*<sub>0</sub>(0), *c*<sub>0</sub>(1) = *d*<sub>1</sub>(0), *c*<sub>1</sub>(0) = *d*<sub>0</sub>(1), *c*<sub>1</sub>(1) = *d*<sub>1</sub>(1); [linear interpolation](wikipedia://en/160010) can be used to interpolate between *c*<sub>0</sub> and *c*<sub>1</sub>, that is

$$L_c(s,t)=(1-t) c_0(s)+ t c_1(s)$$

and between *d*<sub>0</sub>, *d*<sub>1</sub>

$$L_d(s,t)=(1-s) d_0(t)+ s d_1(t)$$

producing two [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158) defined on the unit square.

The [bilinear interpolation](wikipedia://en/674484) on the four corner points is another surface

$$B(s,t) = c_0(0) (1-s)(1-t) + c_0(1) s(1-t) + c_1(0) (1-s)t + c_1(1) s t.$$

A **bilinearly blended Coons patch** is the surface

$$C(s,t)=L_c(s,t)+L_d(s,t)-B(s,t).$$

## Bicubic blending {#Bicubic_blending}

Although the bilinear Coons patch exactly meets its four boundary curves, it does not necessarily have the same [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane) at those curves as the surfaces to be joined, leading to creases in the joined surface along those curves. To fix this problem, the linear interpolation can be replaced with [cubic Hermite splines](wikipedia://en/656586) with the weights chosen to match the partial derivatives at the corners. This forms a **bicubically blended Coons patch**.
