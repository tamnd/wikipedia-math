# Applied mathematics

*Image: Vehicle Routing Problem Example.svg.* Efficient solutions to the [vehicle routing problem](wikipedia://en/7799668) require tools from [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555) and [integer programming](wikipedia://en/411215).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Applied mathematics"}

**Applied mathematics** is the application of [mathematical methods](wikipedia://en/18831) by different fields such as [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [social science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_science), and [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector). Thus, applied mathematics is a combination of [mathematical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_science) and specialized knowledge. The term "applied mathematics" also describes the [professional specialty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profession) in which mathematicians work on practical problems by formulating and studying [mathematical models](wikipedia://en/20590).

:::

In the past, practical applications have motivated the development of mathematical theories, which then became the subject of study in [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) where abstract concepts are studied for their own sake. The activity of applied mathematics is thus intimately connected with research in pure mathematics.

## History {#History}

*Image: Elmer-pump-heatequation.png.* A numerical solution to the [heat equation](wikipedia://en/166415) on a pump casing model using the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581).

Historically, applied mathematics consisted principally of [applied analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), most notably [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations); [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) (broadly construed, to include [representations](wikipedia://en/6355948), [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/641995) methods, [variational methods](wikipedia://en/171882), and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506)); and applied [probability](wikipedia://en/22934). These areas of mathematics related directly to the development of [Newtonian physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_physics), and in fact, the distinction between mathematicians and physicists was not sharply drawn before the mid-19th century. This history left a pedagogical legacy in the United States: until the early 20th century, subjects such as [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) were often taught in applied mathematics departments at American universities rather than in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) departments, and [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) may still be taught in applied mathematics departments.[^o3] [Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) departments have traditionally made use of applied mathematics.

## Divisions {#Divisions}

*Image: HD-Rayleigh-Taylor.gif.* [Computational fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/305924) is often considered a branch of applied mathematics and mechanical engineering.

Today, the term "applied mathematics" is used in a broader sense. It includes the classical areas noted above as well as other areas that have become increasingly important in applications. Even fields such as [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) that are part of [pure mathematics](wikipedia://en/457210) are now important in applications (such as [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432)), though they are not generally considered to be part of the field of applied mathematics *per se*.

There is no consensus as to what the various branches of applied mathematics are. Such categorizations are made difficult by the way mathematics and science change over time, and also by the way universities organize departments, courses, and degrees.

Many mathematicians distinguish between "applied mathematics", which is concerned with mathematical methods, and the "applications of mathematics" within science and engineering. A [biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) using a [population model](wikipedia://en/2985755) and applying known mathematics would not be *doing* applied mathematics, but rather *using* it; however, mathematical biologists have posed problems that have stimulated the growth of pure mathematics. Mathematicians such as [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740) and [Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) deny the existence of "applied mathematics" and claim that there are only "applications of mathematics." Similarly, non-mathematicians blend applied mathematics and applications of mathematics. The use and development of mathematics to solve industrial problems is also called "industrial mathematics".[^o5]

The success of modern numerical mathematical methods and software has led to the emergence of [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833), [computational science](wikipedia://en/1181008), and [computational engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_engineering), which use [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercomputer) for the [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation) of phenomena and the solution of problems in the sciences and engineering. These are often considered interdisciplinary.

### Applicable mathematics {#Applicable_mathematics}

Sometimes, the term **applicable mathematics** is used to distinguish between the traditional applied mathematics that developed alongside physics and the many areas of mathematics that are applicable to real-world problems today, although there is no consensus as to a precise definition.[^o8]

Mathematicians often distinguish between "applied mathematics" on the one hand, and the "applications of mathematics" or "applicable mathematics" both within and outside of science and engineering, on the other.[^o8] Some mathematicians emphasize the term applicable mathematics to separate or delineate the traditional applied areas from new applications arising from fields that were previously seen as pure mathematics.[^o9] For example, from this viewpoint, an ecologist or geographer using population models and applying known mathematics would not be doing applied, but rather applicable, mathematics. Even fields such as number theory that are part of pure mathematics are now important in applications (such as [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432)), though they are not generally considered to be part of the field of applied mathematics *per se*. Such descriptions can lead to *applicable mathematics* being seen as a collection of mathematical methods such as [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), [mathematical modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_modelling), [optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimisation), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), which are useful in areas outside traditional mathematics and not specific to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

Other authors prefer describing *applicable mathematics* as a union of "new" mathematical applications with the traditional fields of applied mathematics.[^o9][^o10][^o11] With this outlook, the terms applied mathematics and applicable mathematics are thus interchangeable.

:::

## Utility {#Utility}

*Image: Market Data Index NYA on 20050726 202628 UTC.png.* [Mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) is concerned with the modelling of financial markets.

Historically, mathematics was most important in the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) and [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering). However, since [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), fields outside the physical sciences have spawned the creation of new areas of mathematics, such as [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924) and [social choice theory](wikipedia://en/1745670), which grew out of economic considerations. Further, the utilization and development of mathematical methods expanded into other areas leading to the creation of new fields such as [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) and [data science](wikipedia://en/35458904).

The advent of the computer has enabled new applications: studying and using the new computer technology itself ([computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science)) to study problems arising in other areas of science (computational science) as well as the mathematics of computation (for example, [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392), [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805),[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506)[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]). [Statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) is probably the most widespread [mathematical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_science) used in the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences).

## Status in academic departments {#Status_in_academic_departments}

Academic institutions are not consistent in the way they group and label courses, programs, and degrees in applied mathematics. At some schools, there is a single mathematics department, whereas others have separate departments for Applied Mathematics and (Pure) Mathematics. It is very common for Statistics departments to be separated at schools with graduate programs, but many undergraduate-only institutions include statistics under the mathematics department.

Many applied mathematics programs (as opposed to departments) consist primarily of cross-listed courses and jointly appointed faculty in departments representing applications. Some Ph.D. programs in applied mathematics require little or no coursework outside mathematics, while others require substantial coursework in a specific area of application. In some respects this difference reflects the distinction between "application of mathematics" and "applied mathematics".

Some universities in the [U.K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom). host departments of *Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics*,[^o22][^o23][^o24] but it is now much less common to have separate departments of pure and applied mathematics. A notable exception to this is the [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](wikipedia://en/3254271) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), housing the [Lucasian Professor of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/147921) whose past holders include [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627), [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698), [James Lighthill](wikipedia://en/946659), [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742), and [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148).

*Image: Henry and Elizabeth Pearce Estate.jpg.* The [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) Division of Applied Mathematics is the oldest applied math program in the U.S.[^o25][^o26]

Schools with separate applied mathematics departments range from [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), which has a large Division of Applied Mathematics that offers degrees through the [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate), to [Santa Clara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_University), which offers only the [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) in applied mathematics.[^o27] Research universities dividing their mathematics department into pure and applied sections include [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). Students in this program also learn another skill (computer science, engineering, physics, pure math, etc.) to supplement their applied math skills.

## Associated mathematical sciences {#Associated_mathematical_sciences}

*Image: Oldfaithful3.png.* Applied mathematics has substantial overlap with statistics.

Applied mathematics is associated with the following mathematical sciences:

### Engineering {#Engineering}

Mathematics is used in all branches of engineering and has subsequently developed as distinct specialties within the engineering profession.

For example, [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918) is foundational to [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering), [Mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) and [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) engineering, with courses in [solid mechanics](wikipedia://en/458866) and [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) being important components of the engineering curriculum. Continuum mechanics is also an important branch of mathematics in its own right. It has served as the inspiration for a vast range of difficult research questions for mathematicians involved in the analysis of [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). Perhaps the most well-known mathematical problem posed by a continuum mechanical system is the question of [Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-Stokes_existence_and_smoothness). Prominent career mathematicians rather than engineers who have contributed to the mathematics of continuum mechanics are [Clifford Truesdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Truesdell), [Walter Noll](wikipedia://en/14964890), [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) and [George Batchelor](wikipedia://en/1763397).

An essential discipline for many fields in engineering is that of [control engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_engineering). The associated mathematical theory of this specialism is [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), a branch of applied mathematics that builds off the mathematics of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). Control theory has played a significant enabling role in modern technology, serving a foundational role in [electrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), mechanical and aerospace engineering. Like continuum mechanics, control theory has also become a field of mathematical research in its own right, with mathematicians such as [Aleksandr Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/65425), [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185), [Lev Pontryagin](wikipedia://en/582075) and [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) recipient [Pierre-Louis Lions](wikipedia://en/1270068) contributing to its foundations.

### Scientific computing {#Scientific_computing}

[Scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing) includes applied mathematics (especially [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506)[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20][^o31]), [computing science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing_science) (especially [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing)[^o32][^o33]), and mathematical modelling in a scientific discipline.

### Computer science {#Computer_science}

[Computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) relies on [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), [discrete mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492) such as [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401),[^o35][^o36] and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

### Operations research and management science {#Operations_research_and_management_science}

[Operations research](wikipedia://en/43476)[^o38] and [management science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_science) are often taught in faculties of engineering, business, and public policy.

### Statistics {#Statistics}

Applied mathematics has substantial overlap with the discipline of statistics. [Statistical theorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statisticians) study and improve statistical procedures with mathematics, and statistical research often raises mathematical questions. Statistical theory relies on [probability](wikipedia://en/23542) and [decision theory](wikipedia://en/16414027), and makes extensive use of scientific computing, analysis, and [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033); for the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), statisticians use [algebra](wikipedia://en/17314682) and [combinatorial design](wikipedia://en/2761968). Applied mathematicians and [statisticians](wikipedia://en/48661) often work in a department of mathematical sciences (particularly at colleges and small universities).

### Actuarial science {#Actuarial_science}

[Actuarial science](wikipedia://en/189373) applies probability, statistics, and economic theory to assess risk in insurance, finance and other industries and professions.[^o41]

### Mathematical economics {#Mathematical_economics}

[Mathematical economics](wikipedia://en/18967255) is the application of mathematical methods to represent theories and analyze problems in economics.[^o43][^o44][^o45] The applied methods usually refer to nontrivial mathematical techniques or approaches. Mathematical economics is based on statistics, probability, mathematical programming (as well as other [computational methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_economics)), operations research, game theory, and some methods from mathematical analysis. In this regard, it resembles (but is distinct from) [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics), another part of applied mathematics.[^o46]

According to the [Mathematics Subject Classification](wikipedia://en/3008091) (MSC), mathematical economics falls into the [Applied mathematics/other](wikipedia://en/3008091#Applied_mathematics_/_other) classification of category 91:

Game theory, economics, social and behavioral sciences

with [MSC2010](http://msc2010.org/mscwiki/index.php?title=MSC2010) classifications for '[Game theory](wikipedia://en/11924)' at codes [91Axx](http://msc2010.org/mscwiki/index.php?title=91Axx) (2015-04-02). <https://web.archive.org/web/20150402092147/http://msc2010.org/mscwiki/index.php?title=91Axx> and for 'Mathematical economics' at codes [91Bxx](http://msc2010.org/mscwiki/index.php?title=91Bxx) (2015-04-02). <https://web.archive.org/web/20150402155902/http://msc2010.org/mscwiki/index.php?title=91Bxx>.

### Other disciplines {#Other_disciplines}

The line between applied mathematics and specific areas of application is often blurred. Many universities teach mathematical and statistical courses outside the respective departments, in departments and areas including [business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business), [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology), [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), [scientific computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computation), [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

## Applied Mathematics Societies {#Applied_Mathematics_Societies}

The [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) is an international applied mathematics organization. As of 2024, the society has 14,000 individual members.[^o49] The [American Mathematics Society](wikipedia://en/198822) has its Applied Mathematics Group.[^o50]
