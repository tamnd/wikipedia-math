# Hopkins–Levitzki theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hopkins–Levitzki theorem"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), in particular [ring theory](wikipedia://en/250424), the **Akizuki–Hopkins–Levitzki theorem** connects the [descending chain condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descending_chain_condition) and [ascending chain condition](wikipedia://en/3189) in [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over semiprimary [rings](wikipedia://en/48404). A ring *R* (with 1) is called **semiprimary** if *R*/*J*(*R*) is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/3876151) and *J*(*R*) is a [nilpotent ideal](wikipedia://en/6389159), where *J*(*R*) denotes the [Jacobson radical](wikipedia://en/57656). The [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) states that if *R* is a semiprimary ring and *M* is an *R*-module, the three module conditions [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/542431), [Artinian](wikipedia://en/542336) and "has a [composition series](wikipedia://en/297493)" are equivalent. Without the semiprimary condition, it is only true that *M* has a composition series if and only if *M* is both Noetherian and Artinian.

:::

The theorem takes its current form from a paper by Charles Hopkins (a former doctoral student of [George Abram Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Abram_Miller)) and a paper by [Jacob Levitzki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Levitzki), both in 1939. For this reason it is often cited as the **Hopkins–Levitzki theorem**. However [Yasuo Akizuki](wikipedia://en/70092474) is sometimes included since he [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) the result[^o2] for [commutative rings](wikipedia://en/61346) a few years earlier, in 1935.

Since it is known that right [Artinian rings](wikipedia://en/489273) are semiprimary, a direct [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of the theorem is: a right Artinian ring is also right [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129). The analogous statement for left Artinian rings holds as well. This is not true in general for Artinian modules, because there are [examples of Artinian modules which are not Noetherian](wikipedia://en/542336#Relation_to_the_Noetherian_condition).

Another direct corollary is that if *R* is right Artinian, then *R* is left Artinian if and only if it is left Noetherian.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Sketch of proof"}

## Sketch of proof {#Sketch_of_proof}

Here is the proof of the following: Let *R* be a semiprimary ring and *M* a left *R*-module. If *M* is either Artinian or Noetherian, then *M* has a composition series.[^o5] (The [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) of this is true over any ring.)

Let *J* be the [radical](wikipedia://en/57656) of *R*. Set $F_i = J^{i-1}M/J^iM$. The *R*-module $F_i$ may then be viewed as an $R/J$-module because *J* is contained in the [annihilator](wikipedia://en/328684) of $F_i$. Each $F_i$ is a [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1001361) $R/J$-module, because $R/J$ is a semisimple ring. Furthermore, since *J* is nilpotent, only finitely many of the $F_i$ are nonzero. If *M* is Artinian (or Noetherian), then $F_i$ has a finite composition series. Stacking the composition series from the $F_i$ end to end, we obtain a composition series for *M*.

:::

## In Grothendieck categories {#In_Grothendieck_categories}

Several generalizations and extensions of the theorem exist. One concerns [Grothendieck categories](wikipedia://en/30051538): if *G* is a Grothendieck category with an Artinian generator, then every Artinian [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) in *G* is Noetherian.[^o7]
