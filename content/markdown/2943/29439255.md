# KR-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="KR-theory"}

In mathematics, **KR-theory** is a variant of [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) defined for spaces with an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450). It was introduced by (Atiyah 1966),[^o3] motivated by applications to the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752) for real [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *real space* is a defined to be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with an involution. A real [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over a real space *X* is defined to be a complex vector bundle *E* over *X* that is also a real space, such that the natural maps from *E* to *X* and from $\mathbb{C}$×*E* to *E* commute with the involution, where the involution acts as complex conjugation on $\mathbb{C}$. (This differs from the notion of a complex vector bundle in the category of **Z**/2**Z** spaces, where the involution acts trivially on $\mathbb{C}$.)

The group *KR*(*X*) is the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of finite-dimensional real vector bundles over the real space *X*.

:::

## Periodicity {#Periodicity}

Similarly to [Bott periodicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_periodicity), the periodicity theorem for *KR* states that *KR*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> = *KR*<sup>*p*+1,*q*+1</sup>, where *KR*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> is suspension with respect to *R*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> = **R**<sup>*q*</sup> + i**R**<sup>*p*</sup> (with a switch in the order of *p* and *q*), given by

$$KR^{p,q}(X,Y) = KR(X\times B^{p,q},X\times S^{p,q}\cup Y\times B^{p,q})$$

and *B*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup>, *S*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup> are the unit ball and sphere in *R*<sup>*p*,*q*</sup>.
