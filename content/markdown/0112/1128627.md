# Augmented tridiminished icosahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Augmented tridiminished icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **augmented tridiminished icosahedron** is a [composite](wikipedia://en/77684519) convex polyhedron with regular faces, a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). It can be obtained by joining a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521) to another Johnson solid, the [tridiminished icosahedron](wikipedia://en/1123559). The resulting polyhedron has seven [equilateral triangular](wikipedia://en/173285) and three [regular pentagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon) faces. It is the only Johnson solid constructed with augmentation and diminishment. Out of 92 solids, the augmented tridiminished icosahedron is listed as 64th Johnson solid $J_{64}$.

*Image: J64 augmented tridiminished icosahedron.stl.* 3D model of an augmented tridiminished icosahedron

:::

The surface area of an augmented tridiminished icosahedron is the sum of the area of seven equilateral triangles and three regular pentagons. Its volume can be obtained by summing the volume of a tridiminished icosahedron and a regular tetrahedron. Let $a$ be its edge length. Then its surface area $A$ and volume $V$ are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{7\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2 + \frac{\sqrt{25 + 10\sqrt{5}}}{4}a^2 \approx 4.751a^2 \\ V &= \frac{15 + 7 \sqrt{5}}{24}a^3 + \frac{\sqrt{2}}{12}a^3 \approx 1.395a^3. \end{aligned}$$
