# Square pyramid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Square pyramid"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **square pyramid** is a [pyramid](wikipedia://en/3307045) with a square base and four triangles, having a total of five faces. If the [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079) of the pyramid is directly above the center of the square, it becomes a form of right pyramid with four [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428). When all of the pyramid's edges are equal in length, its triangles are all [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285), an example of a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

Square pyramids have appeared throughout the history of architecture, with examples being [Egyptian pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid) and many other similar buildings. They also occur in chemistry in [square pyramidal molecular structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_pyramidal_molecular_geometry). Square pyramids are often used in the construction of other [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra) and as the [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) of a four-dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) called [cubic pyramid](wikipedia://en/36537625). [Square pyramidal number](wikipedia://en/542343) is a natural number that counts the number of spheres stacked into a square pyramid. Many mathematicians in ancient times discovered the formula for the volume of a square pyramid with different approaches.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

### As a right pyramid {#As_a_right_pyramid}

A square pyramid has five vertices, eight edges, and five faces. One face, called the base of the pyramid, is a [square](wikipedia://en/659939); the four other faces are [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654).[^n1] Four of the edges make up the square by connecting its four vertices. The other four edges are known as the [lateral edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateral_edge) of the pyramid; they meet at the fifth vertex, called the [apex](wikipedia://en/6022079). If the pyramid's apex lies on a line erected perpendicularly from the center of the square, the square pyramid becomes a right pyramid, and the four triangular faces are [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428). Otherwise, the pyramid has two or more non-isosceles triangular faces.[^n2]

The slant height $s$ of a right square pyramid is defined as the height of one of its isosceles triangles. It can be obtained via the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034):

$$s = \sqrt{b^2 - \frac{l^2}{4}},$$

where $l$ is the length of the triangle's base, also one of the square's edges, and $b$ is the length of the triangle's legs, which are lateral edges of the pyramid. The height $h$ of a right square pyramid can be similarly obtained, with a substitution of the slant height formula giving:[^n3]

$$h = \sqrt{s^2 - \frac{l^2}{4}} = \sqrt{b^2 - \frac{l^2}{2}}.$$

A [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470)'s [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) is the sum of the areas of its faces. The surface area $A$ of a right square pyramid can be expressed as $A = 4T + S$, where $T$ and $S$ are the areas of one of its triangles and its base, respectively. The area of a triangle is half of the product of its base and side, with the area of a square being the length of the side squared. This gives the expression:[^n4]

$$A = 4\left(\frac{1}{2}ls\right) + l^2 = 2ls + l^2.$$

In general, the volume $V$ of a pyramid is equal to one-third of the area of its base multiplied by its height.[^n5] Expressed in a formula for a square pyramid, this is:[^n6]

$$V = \frac{1}{3}l^2h.$$

Many mathematicians discovered the formula for calculating the volume of a square pyramid in ancient times. In the [Moscow Mathematical Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Papyrus), Egyptian mathematicians demonstrated knowledge of the formula for calculating the volume of a [truncated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/89405), suggesting that they were also acquainted with the volume of a square pyramid, but it is unknown how the formula was derived. Beyond the discovery of the volume of a square pyramid, the problem of finding the slope and height of a square pyramid can be found in the [Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](wikipedia://en/9210114).[^n7] The Babylonian mathematicians also considered the volume of a frustum, but gave an incorrect formula for it.[^n8] One Chinese mathematician [Liu Hui](wikipedia://en/202719) also discovered the volume by the method of dissecting a rectangular solid into pieces.[^n9]

Like other right pyramids with a regular polygon as a base, a right square pyramid has [pyramidal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_symmetry), the symmetry of [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_{4\mathrm{v}}$: the pyramid is left invariant by rotations of one-, two-, and three-quarters of a full turn around its [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry), the line connecting the apex to the center of the base; and is also [mirror symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_symmetric) relative to any perpendicular plane passing through a bisector of the base.[^n10] It can be represented as the [wheel graph](wikipedia://en/1655876) $W_4$, meaning its [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_%28topology%29) can be interpreted as a square in which its four vertices connect a vertex in the center called the [universal vertex](wikipedia://en/51352780).[^n11] It is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual_polyhedron), meaning its [dual polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8815) is the square pyramid itself.[^n12]

### As a Johnson solid {#As_a_Johnson_solid}

*Image: J1 square pyramid.stl.* 3D model of a square pyramid as a Johnson solid

If all triangular edges are of equal length, the four triangles are [equilateral](wikipedia://en/173285), and the pyramid's faces are all [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306).[^n13] The [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) between adjacent triangular faces are $\arccos (-\frac{1}{3}) \approx 109.47^\circ$, and that between the base and each triangular face being half of that, $\arctan \left(\sqrt{2}\right) \approx 54.74^\circ$.[^n10] A [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) in which all of the faces are [regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polygons) is called a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). Such a square pyramid is among them, enumerated as the first Johnson solid $J_1$.[^n14]

Because its edges are all equal in length (that is, $b = l$), its slant, height, surface area, and volume can be derived by substituting the formulas of a right pyramid:

$$\begin{aligned} s = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}l \approx 0.866l, &\qquad h = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}l \approx 0.707l,\\ A = (1 + \sqrt{3})l^2 \approx 2.732l^2, &\qquad V = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{6}l^3 \approx 0.236l^3. \end{aligned}$$

An equilateral square pyramid is an [elementary polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_polyhedron). This means it cannot be separated by a plane to create two small convex polyhedra with regular faces.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

In architecture, the [pyramids built in ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramids) are examples of buildings shaped like square pyramids.[^n15] [Pyramidologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidology) have put forward various suggestions for the design of the [Great Pyramid of Giza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza), including a theory based on the [Kepler triangle](wikipedia://en/13938136) and the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386). However, modern scholars favor descriptions using integer ratios, as being more consistent with the knowledge of Egyptian mathematics and proportion.[^o27] The [Mesoamerican pyramids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_pyramids) are also ancient pyramidal buildings similar to the Egyptian; they differ in having flat tops and stairs ascending their faces. Modern buildings whose designs imitate the Egyptian pyramids include the [Louvre Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louvre_Pyramid) and the casino hotel [Luxor Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor_Las_Vegas).

In [stereochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereochemistry), an [atom cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_cluster) can have a [square pyramidal geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_pyramidal_molecular_geometry). A square pyramidal molecule has a [main-group element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main-group_element) with one active [lone pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lone_pair), which can be described by a model that predicts the geometry of molecules known as [VSEPR theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSEPR_theory).[^n16] Examples of molecules with this structure include [chlorine pentafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorine_pentafluoride), [bromine pentafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromine_pentafluoride), and [iodine pentafluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine_pentafluoride).[^n17]

:::

## Related topics {#Related_topics}

The base of a square pyramid can be attached to a square face of another polyhedron to construct new polyhedra, an example of [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29). For example, a [tetrakis hexahedron](wikipedia://en/722554) can be constructed by attaching the base of an equilateral square pyramid onto each face of a cube.[^n18] Attaching [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) or [antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiprisms) to pyramids is known as [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) or [gyroelongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongation), respectively.[^n19] Some of the other Johnson solids can be constructed by either augmenting square pyramids or augmenting other shapes with square pyramids: [elongated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1177939) $J_8$, [gyroelongated square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1123470) $J_{10}$, [elongated square bipyramid](wikipedia://en/1177952) $J_{15}$, [gyroelongated square bipyramid](wikipedia://en/715707) $J_{17}$, [augmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/1177970) $J_{49}$, [biaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/1177972) $J_{50}$, [triaugmented triangular prism](wikipedia://en/728019) $J_{51}$, [augmented pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177974) $J_{52}$, [biaugmented pentagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177977) $J_{53}$, [augmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177979) $J_{54}$, [parabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177980) $J_{55}$, [metabiaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177982) $J_{56}$, [triaugmented hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/1177983) $J_{57}$, and [augmented sphenocorona](wikipedia://en/1170930) $J_{87}$.[^o33]

Square pyramids are the [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29) of a four-dimensional polytope, the [cubic pyramid](wikipedia://en/36537625). This polytope has nine edges, twenty vertices, and eighteen faces (which include twelve triangles and six squares). It has seven cells, consisting of six square pyramids and one cube.[^n20]

*Image: Square pyramidal number.svg.* Geometric representation of the square pyramidal number 1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = 30.

A [square pyramidal number](wikipedia://en/542343) is a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) that counts the number of stacked spheres in a square pyramid. If the pyramid has $n$ layers, then it has $1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + n^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6$ spheres.[^n21] The first few terms of this sequence are: 1, 5, 14, 30, 55, 91, ...
