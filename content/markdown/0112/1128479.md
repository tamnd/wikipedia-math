# Square cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Square cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **square cupola** (sometimes called **lesser dome**) is a [cupola](wikipedia://en/689743) with an [octagonal](wikipedia://en/314575) base. In the case of all edges being equal in length, it is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), a [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron with [regular faces](wikipedia://en/333306). It can be used to construct many other polyhedrons, particularly other Johnson solids.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The square cupola has 4 triangles, 5 squares, and 1 octagon as their faces; the octagon is the base, and one of the squares is the top. If the edges are equal in length, the triangles and octagon become [regular](wikipedia://en/333306), and the edge length of the octagon is equal to the edge length of both triangles and squares. The [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) between both square and triangle is approximately $144.7^\circ$, that between both triangle and octagon is $54.7^\circ$, that between both square and octagon is precisely $45^\circ$, and that between two adjacent squares is $135^\circ$. A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron in which all the faces are regular is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), and the square cupola is enumerated as $J_{4}$, the fourth Johnson solid.

Given that the edge length of $a$, the surface area of a square cupola $A$ can be calculated by adding the area of all faces:

$$A = \left(7+2\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 \approx 11.560a^2.$$

Its height $h$, circumradius $C$, and volume $V$ are:

$$\begin{aligned} h &= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}a \approx 0.707a, \\ C &= \left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{2}}\right)a \approx 1.399a, \\ V &= \left(1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}\right)a^3 \approx 1.943a^3. \end{aligned}$$

*Image: J4 square cupola.stl.* 3D model of a square cupola

It has an [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) passing through the center of its both top and base, which is symmetrical by rotating around it at one-, two-, and three-quarters of a full-turn angle. It is also mirror-symmetric relative to any perpendicular plane passing through a bisector of the base. Therefore, it has [pyramidal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_symmetry), the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_{4v}$ of order 8.

:::

## Related polyhedra and honeycombs {#Related_polyhedra_and_honeycombs}

The square cupola can be found in many constructions of polyhedra. An example is the [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213), which can be seen as eight overlapping cupolae. A construction that involves the attachment of its base to another polyhedron is known as [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29); attaching it to [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) or [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) is known as [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) or [gyroelongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongation). Some of the other Johnson solids are [elongated square cupola](wikipedia://en/1128506) $J_{19}$, [gyroelongated square cupola](wikipedia://en/1128599) $J_{23}$, [square orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1128614) $J_{28}$, [square gyrobicupola](wikipedia://en/1128615) $J_{29}$, [elongated square gyrobicupola](wikipedia://en/1128551) $J_{37}$, [gyroelongated square bicupola](wikipedia://en/1128650) $J_{45}$, [augmented truncated cube](wikipedia://en/1233340) $J_{66}$, and [biaugmented truncated cube](wikipedia://en/1233342) $J_{67}$.

*Image: Crossed square cupola.stl.* 3D model of a crossed square cupola

The [crossed square cupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_square_cupola) is one of the nonconvex [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) isomorphs, being topologically identical to the convex square cupola. It can be obtained as a slice of the [nonconvex great rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/4157120) or quasirhombicuboctahedron, analogously to how the square cupola may be obtained as a slice of the rhombicuboctahedron. As in all [cupolae](wikipedia://en/689743), the base [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) has twice as many [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) and [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) as the top; in this case the base polygon is an [octagram](wikipedia://en/2667613). It may be seen as a cupola with a retrograde square base, so that the squares and triangles connect across the bases in the opposite way to the square cupola, hence intersecting each other.

The square cupola is a component of several nonuniform space-filling lattices:

- with [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606);
- with [cubes](wikipedia://en/6285) and [cuboctahedra](wikipedia://en/6280); and
- with tetrahedra, [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) and various combinations of cubes, [elongated square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1177939) and [elongated square bipyramids](wikipedia://en/1177952).[^o6]
