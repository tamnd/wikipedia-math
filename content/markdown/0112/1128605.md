# Gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda** is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), a convex polyhedron with thirty [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), six [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon), and one [regular decagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_decagon) as its faces.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

To construct, begin by attaching the [pentagonal rotunda](wikipedia://en/1128491)'s base to one of the [pentagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860091)'s decagons. This construction is called [gyroelongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongation). This replaces the [decagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_decagon) with ten [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and six [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon) from the faces of a pentagonal rotunda. Hence, the faces of such a resulting polyhedron have thirty equilateral triangles, six regular pentagons, and one regular decagon, adding from the faces of a pentagonal antiprism, and the total number of faces is thirty-seven. Because both polyhedra are [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) and the faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), the gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), enumerated as 25th Johnson solid $J_{25}$.[^o5]

From this construction, the gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda is a [composite polyhedron](wikipedia://en/77684519).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: J25 gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda.stl.* 3D model of a gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda

A gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda has the same [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525) as the pentagonal rotunda, namely the cyclic group $C_{5\mathrm{v}}$ of order ten. Its [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046), an angle between two polygonal faces, can be obtained from the dihedral angle of a pentagonal rotunda and of a pentagonal antiprism. There are five distinguishing dihedral angles:[^o8]

- the two triangle-to-decagon angles are around \142.6 deg and \158.7 deg,
- the triangle-to-decagon angle is around \95.2 deg, from the angle between the triangular faces and the antiprism's base, and
- the two pentagon-to-triangle angles are around \174.4 deg and \159.2 deg.

The surface area of a gyroelongated pentagonal rotunda is obtained by adding all of the polygonal faces' areas, and the volume is obtained by summing the volume of a pentagonal rotunda and of a pentagonal antiprism. With edge length $a$, the surface area $A$ and volume $V$ are:

$$\begin{aligned} A & = \frac{1}{2}\left( 15\sqrt{3}+\left(5+3\sqrt{5}\right)\sqrt{5+2\sqrt{5}}\right)a^2 \approx 31.007a^2, \\ V &= \left(\frac{45}{12}+\frac{17}{12}\sqrt{5} + \frac{5}{6}\sqrt{2\sqrt{650+290\sqrt{5}}-2\sqrt{5}-2}\right) a^3 \approx 13.667a^3 \end{aligned}$$
