# Neopythagoreanism

*Image: Apollonius of Tyana.jpg.* [Apollonius of Tyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Tyana) ( c. 15?–c. 100? AD), one of the most important representatives of Neopythagoreanism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neopythagoreanism"}

**Neopythagoreanism** (or **neo-Pythagoreanism**) was a school of [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_philosophy) and [Ancient Roman philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Roman_philosophy) which revived [Pythagorean doctrines](wikipedia://en/291170). Neopythagoreanism was influenced by [middle Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Platonism) and in turn influenced [Neoplatonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonism). It originated in the 1st century BC and flourished during the 1st and 2nd centuries AD. The [*Encyclopædia Britannica* Eleventh Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica_Eleventh_Edition) describes Neopythagoreanism as "a link in the chain between the old and the new" within Hellenistic philosophy. Central to Neopythagorean thought was the concept of a [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul) and its inherent desire for a *[unio mystica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unio_mystica)* with the divine.[^o2] The word *Neopythagoreanism* is a modern (19th century) term,[^o3] coined as a parallel of "Neoplatonism".

:::

## History {#History}

In the 1st century BC [Cicero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero)'s friend [Nigidius Figulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigidius_Figulus) made an attempt to revive Pythagorean doctrines, but the most important members of the school were [Apollonius of Tyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Tyana) and [Moderatus of Gades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderatus_of_Gades) in the 1st century AD. Other important Neopythagoreans include mathematician [Nicomachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachus), who wrote about the mystical properties of numbers. In the 2nd century, [Numenius of Apamea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numenius_of_Apamea) sought to fuse additional elements of [Platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism) into Neopythagoreanism, prefiguring the rise of [Neoplatonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonism). ([Iamblichus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iamblichus) in particular was influenced by Neopythagoreanism.)

Neopythagoreanism was an attempt to re-introduce a [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism) religious element into [Hellenistic philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_philosophy) in place of what had come to be regarded as an arid formalism. The founders of the school sought to invest their doctrines with the halo of tradition by ascribing them to [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275) and [Plato](wikipedia://en/22954). They went back to the later period of Plato's thought, the period when Plato endeavoured to combine his [theory of forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_forms) with Pythagorean [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and identified the [good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_of_the_Good) with the [monad](wikipedia://en/9273782) (which would give rise to the Neoplatonic concept of "[the One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplatonism#The_One)"), the source of the duality of the infinite and the measured with the resultant scale of realities from the one down to the objects of the material world.

They emphasized the fundamental distinction between the [soul and the body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind–body_problem). [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God) must be worshipped spiritually by [prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayer) and the [will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_%28philosophy%29) to be good, not in outward [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_%28philosophy%29). The [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul) must be freed from its material surrounding, the "muddy vesture of decay," by an [ascetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asceticism) habit of life. Bodily pleasures and all sensuous impulses must be abandoned as detrimental to the spiritual purity of the soul. God is the principle of good and [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter) the groundwork of [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil). In this system can be distinguished not only the asceticism of Pythagoras and the later mysticism of Plato, but also the influence of [Orphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphism) and of [Oriental philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_philosophy). The Platonic forms are no longer self-subsistent entities but are the elements which constitute the content of spiritual activity. The non-material universe is regarded as the sphere of [mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind) or [spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_%28animating_force%29).

The [Porta Maggiore Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Maggiore_Basilica), where Neopythagoreans held their meetings in the 1st century BC, is believed to have been constructed by the [Statilia gens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statilia_gens).[^o5] It was discovered in 1915 near [Porta Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Maggiore) on [Via Praenestina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Praenestina) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome).[^o6][^o7][^o8]
