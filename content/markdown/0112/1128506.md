# Elongated square cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elongated square cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **elongated square cupola** is a polyhedron constructed from an [octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569) by attaching [square cupola](wikipedia://en/1128479) onto its base. It is an example of [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The elongated square cupola is constructed from an [octagonal prism](wikipedia://en/3026569) by attaching a [square cupola](wikipedia://en/1128479) onto one of its bases, a process known as the [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29). This cupola covers the octagonal face so that the resulting polyhedron has four [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), thirteen [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), and one [regular octagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_octagon). It can also be constructed by removing a [square cupola](wikipedia://en/1128479) from a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213), which would also make it a diminished rhombicuboctahedron. A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) polyhedron in which all of the faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) is the [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). The elongated square cupola is one of them, enumerated as the nineteenth Johnson solid $J_{19}$.

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: J19 elongated square cupola.stl.* 3D model of an elongated square cupola

The [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of an elongated square cupola $A$ is the sum of all polygonal faces' area. Its volume $V$ can be ascertained by dissecting it into both a square cupola and a regular octagon, and then adding their volume. Given the elongated square cupola with edge length $a$, its surface area and volume are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \left(15+2\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}\right)a^2 \approx 19.561a^2, \\ V &= \left(3+\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{3}\right)a^3 \approx 6.771a^3. \end{aligned}$$

Its circumradius $C$ is:

$$C = \frac{1}{2}a \sqrt{5 + 2\sqrt{2}}.$$
