# Triangular cupola

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Triangular cupola"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **triangular cupola** is the [cupola](wikipedia://en/689743) with [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) as its base and [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) as its top. If the edges are equal in length, the triangular cupola is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034). It can be seen as half a [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280). The triangular cupola can be applied to construct many polyhedrons.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The triangular cupola has four [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), three [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), and one [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) as its faces; the hexagon is the base, and one of the four triangles is the top. If all of the edges are equal in length, the [triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and the hexagon become [regular](wikipedia://en/333306). Such a triangular cupola has three [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046): an angle between each triangle and the hexagon is approximately 70.5°, an angle hat between each square and the hexagon is 54.7°, and an angle between square and triangle is 125.3°. The triangular cupola is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), named after American mathematician [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527), who listed the [convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with regular polygonal faces. In the list, the triangular cupola is enumerated as the third Johnson solid $J_{3}$. It is an [elementary polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_polyhedron), meaning that it cannot produce any convex, regular-faced polyhedron after being sliced by a plane.

For a triangular cupola with edge length $a$, its surface area $A$ can be calculated by adding the area of four equilateral triangles, three squares, and one hexagon:

$$A = \left(3+\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) a^2 \approx 7.33a^2.$$

Its height $h$ and volume $V$ are:

$$\begin{aligned} h &= \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3} a\approx 0.82a, \\ V &= \left(\frac{5}{3\sqrt{2}}\right)a^3 \approx 1.18a^3. \end{aligned}$$

*Image: J3 triangular cupola.stl.* 3D model of a triangular cupola

It has a threefold [axis of symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_of_symmetry) passing through the center of both top and base. It is also mirror-symmetric relative to any plane containing this axis and a bisector of the triangular base. Therefore, it has [pyramidal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_symmetry), the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) $C_{3\mathrm{v}}$ of order 6.

:::

## Related polyhedra {#Related_polyhedra}

The triangular cupola can be found in the construction of many polyhedra. An example is the [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280) in which the triangular cupola may be considered as its hemisphere. A construction that involves the attachment of its base to another polyhedron is known as [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29); attaching [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) or [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) to it is known as [elongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elongation_%28geometry%29) or [gyroelongation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroelongation). Some of the other Johnson solids constructed in such a way are the [elongated triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1196215) $J_{18}$, [gyroelongated triangular cupola](wikipedia://en/1196222) $J_{22}$, [triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196250) $J_{27}$, [elongated triangular orthobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196304) $J_{35}$, [elongated triangular gyrobicupola](wikipedia://en/1196309) $J_{36}$, [gyroelongated triangular bicupola](wikipedia://en/1196315) $J_{44}$, and [augmented truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/1200120) $J_{65}$.

The triangular cupola may also be used in constructing the [truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/279651), although it leaves some hollows and a regular tetrahedron as its interior. (Cundy 1956) constructed such a polyhedron in a way similar to the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631) by attaching six [square pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128455) outwards, each of whose apices are in the [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29)'s center. That being said, such truncated tetrahedra are constructed by attaching four triangular cupolas rectangle-by-rectangle; those cupolas in which the alternating sides of both right isosceles triangle and rectangle have the edges in terms of ratio $1 : \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}$. The [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654) can be constructed by attaching eight of those same triangular cupolas triangle-by-triangle.
