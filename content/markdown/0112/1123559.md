# Tridiminished icosahedron

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Tridiminished icosahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **tridiminished icosahedron** is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034) that is constructed by removing three [pentagonal pyramids](wikipedia://en/1128461) from a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968).

*Image: J63 tridiminished icosahedron.stl.* 3D model of a tridiminished icosahedron

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

The tridiminished icosahedron can be constructed by removing three regular-faced [pentagonal pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128461) from a [regular icosahedron](wikipedia://en/14968). The aftereffect of such construction leaves five [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285) and three [regular pentagons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon). Since all of its faces are [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) and the resulting polyhedron remains [convex](wikipedia://en/6292), the tridiminished icosahedron is a [Johnson solid](wikipedia://en/16034), after American mathematician [Norman W. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_W._Johnson) who listed the 92 such polyhedra. It is enumerated as the sixty-third Johnson solid $J_{63}$. This construction is similar to other Johnson solids as in [gyroelongated pentagonal pyramid](wikipedia://en/1123549) and [metabidiminished icosahedron](wikipedia://en/1123562).

One can construct the vertices of a tridiminished icosahedron with the following Cartesian coordinates:

$$(\pm 1, 0, \varphi), (1, 0, -\varphi), (\varphi, \pm 1, 0), (0, \varphi, 1), (-\varphi, -1, 0), (0, -\varphi, \pm 1),$$

where $\varphi = (1-\sqrt{5})/2$ is a [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386), obtained from the equation $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$.

The tridiminished icosahedron is a [non-composite polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-composite_polyhedron). That is, no plane intersects its surface only in edges, so that it cannot be thereby divided into two or more convex, regular-faced polyhedra.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The surface area of a tridiminished icosahedron $A$ is the sum of all polygonal faces' area: five equilateral triangles and three regular pentagons. Its volume $V$ can be ascertained by subtracting the volume of a regular icosahedron from the volume of three pentagonal pyramids. Given that $a$ is the edge length of a tridiminished icosahedron, they are:

$$\begin{aligned} A &= \frac{5 \sqrt{3}+3 \sqrt{5 \left(5+2 \sqrt{5}\right)}}{4} a^2 &\approx 7.3265a^2, \\ V &= \frac{15 + 7 \sqrt{5}}{24}a^3 &\approx 1.2772a^3. \end{aligned}$$

A tridiminished icosahedron has a [three-dimensional symmetry group](wikipedia://en/2694525) $C_{3\mathrm{v}}$ of order six. It has three kinds of [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046). These angles are yielded in the following calculations.

- An angle between two adjacent triangles is around 138.1°, equal to that of a regular icosahedron and that of a pentagonal pyramid.
- A triangle-to-pentagon angle is around 100.8°. The result is obtained by subtracting the pentagon-to-triangle angle of a pentagonal pyramid from the triangle-to-triangle angle of a regular icosahedron.
- An angle between two adjacent pentagons is around 63.4°. The result is obtained by subtracting the pentagon-to-triangle angle of a pentagonal pyramid from the tridiminished icosahedron's triangle-to-pentagon angle.

:::

## As a vertex figure {#As_a_vertex_figure}

The tridiminished icosahedron is a [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of a [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756), a four-dimensional polytope consisting of 120 [regular tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tetrahedra) and 24 [icosahedra](wikipedia://en/14968) as the [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29).[^n1]
