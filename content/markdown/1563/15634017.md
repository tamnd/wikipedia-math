# Ramified forcing

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ramified forcing"}

In the mathematical discipline of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **ramified forcing** is the original form of [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) introduced by (Cohen 1963) to prove the [independence](wikipedia://en/1141208) of the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) from [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214). Ramified forcing starts with a model $M$ of set theory in which the [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472), $V = L$, holds, and then builds up a larger model $M[G]$ of Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory by adding a generic subset $G$ of a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) to $M$, imitating [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)'s [constructible hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructible_hierarchy).

:::

[Dana Scott](wikipedia://en/161875) and [Robert Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110) realized that the use of [constructible sets](wikipedia://en/343335) was an unnecessary complication, and could be replaced by a simpler construction similar to [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942)'s construction of the universe as a union of sets $V_{\alpha}$ for ordinals $\alpha$. Their simplification was originally called "unramified forcing" (Shoenfield 1971), but is now usually just called "forcing". As a result, ramified forcing is only rarely used.
