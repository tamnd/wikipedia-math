# Eutocius of Ascalon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eutocius of Ascalon"}

**Eutocius of Ascalon** (; Εὐτόκιος ὁ Ἀσκαλωνίτης; c. 480s – c. 520s) was a Greek mathematician who wrote commentaries on several [Archimedean](wikipedia://en/1844) treatises and on the [Apollonian](wikipedia://en/257242) *Conics*.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Little is known about the life of Eutocius. He was born in [Ascalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkelon), then in [Palestina Prima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestina_Prima) and lived during the reign of [Justinian.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) Eutocius probably became the head of the [Alexandrian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrian_school) following [Ammonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonius_Hermiae), and he was succeeded in this position by [Olympiodorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiodorus_the_Younger), possibly as early as 525.[^o3] From his testimony, it seems he traveled to other cultural centers of his time to find missing manuscripts.

Eutocius wrote commentaries on [Apollonius](wikipedia://en/257242) and [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844). The surviving commentaries are:

- A [Commentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commentary_%28philology%29) on the first four books of the *[Conics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conics)* of Apollonius.[^o4]
- Commentaries[^o5] on Archimedes' work:
  - *[On the Sphere and Cylinder](wikipedia://en/18084436)* I-II.
  - *[Measurement of the Circle](wikipedia://en/18203202)* (Latin: *In Archimedis Dimensionem Circuli*).
  - [*On the Equilibrium* *of Planes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Equilibrium_of_Planes) I-II.
- An introduction to Book I of [Ptolemy](wikipedia://en/23979)'s *[Almagest](wikipedia://en/148060)*.[^o6]

Historians owe much of their knowledge of Archimedes' solution of a cubic by means of intersecting conics, alluded to in *On the Sphere and Cylinder*, to Eutocius and his commentaries. Eutocius dedicated his commentary on Apollonius' *Conics* to [Anthemius of Tralles](wikipedia://en/1873), also a mathematician and the architect of the [Hagia Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagia_Sophia) in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople).[^o7]
