# Hadwiger–Finsler inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadwiger–Finsler inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hadwiger–Finsler inequality** is a result on the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410). It states that if a triangle in the plane has side lengths $a$, $b$ and $c$ and area $\Delta$, then

:::

$$a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2} \geq (a - b)^{2} + (b - c)^{2} + (c - a)^{2} + 4 \sqrt{3}\, \Delta \quad \mbox{(HF)}.$$

## Related inequalities {#Related_inequalities}

- [Weitzenböck's inequality](wikipedia://en/10777748) is a straightforward [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865) of the Hadwiger–Finsler inequality: if a triangle in the plane has side lendths $a$, $b$ and $c$ and area $\Delta$, then

$$a^{2} + b^{2} + c^{2} \geq 4 \sqrt{3}\, \Delta \quad \mbox{(W)}.$$

Hadwiger–Finsler inequality is actually equivalent to Weitzenböck's inequality. Applying (W) to the circummidarc triangle gives (HF)[^o3]

Weitzenböck's inequality can also be proved using [Heron's formula](wikipedia://en/194123), by which route it can be seen that equality holds in (W) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the triangle is an [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285), i.e. $a=b=c$.

- A version for [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278): Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral with the lengths $a, b, c, d$ and the area $T$ then:[^o4]

$a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2 \ge 4T + \frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum{(a-b)^2}$ with equality only for a [square](wikipedia://en/659939).

Where $\sum{(a-b)^2}=(a-b)^2+(a-c)^2+(a-d)^2+(b-c)^2+(b-d)^2+(c-d)^2$

::: {#o6 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

From the [cosines law](wikipedia://en/19480890) we have: $a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos\alpha$

$\alpha$ being the angle between $b$ and $c$. This can be transformed into: $a^2=(b-c)^2+2bc(1-\cos\alpha)$

Since $\Delta=\frac{1}{2}bc\sin\alpha$ we have: $a^2=(b-c)^2+4\Delta\frac{(1-\cos\alpha)}{\sin\alpha}$

Now remember that $1-\cos\alpha=2\sin^2\frac{\alpha}{2}$

and $\sin\alpha=2\sin\frac{\alpha}{2}\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}$

Using this we get: $a^2=(b-c)^2+4\Delta\tan\frac{\alpha}{2}$

Doing this for all sides of the triangle and adding up we get: $a^2+b^2+c^2=(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2+4\Delta\Big(\tan\frac{\alpha}{2}+\tan\frac{\beta}{2}+\tan\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big)$

$\beta$ and $\gamma$ being the other angles of the triangle. Now since the halves of the triangle’s angles are less than $\pi/2$ the function $\tan$ is [convex](wikipedia://en/245568) we have:

$$\tan\frac{\alpha}{2}+\tan\frac{\beta}{2}+\tan\frac{\gamma}{2}\geq3\tan\frac{\alpha+\beta+\gamma}{6}=3\tan\frac{\pi}{6}=\sqrt{3}$$

Using this we get: $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 \geq (a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2+ 4\sqrt{3}\, \Delta$

This is the Hadwiger-Finsler inequality.

:::

## History {#History}

The Hadwiger–Finsler inequality is named after (txt), who also published in the same paper the [Finsler–Hadwiger theorem](wikipedia://en/27007324) on a square derived from two other squares that share a vertex.
