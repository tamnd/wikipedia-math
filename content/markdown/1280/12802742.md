# Jaffard ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jaffard ring"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a **Jaffard ring** is a type of [ring](wikipedia://en/48404), more general than a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129), for which [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) behaves as expected in polynomial extensions. They are named for [Paul Jaffard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Jaffard) who first studied them in 1960.[^n1]

:::

Formally, a Jaffard ring is a ring *R* such that the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065)

$$\dim R[T_1,\ldots,T_n] = n + \dim R, \,$$

where "dim" denotes Krull dimension. A Jaffard ring that is also an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462) is called a **Jaffard domain**.

The Jaffard property is satisfied by any Noetherian ring *R*, and examples of non-Noetherian rings might appear to be quite difficult to find, however they do arise naturally. For example, the ring of [(all) algebraic integers](wikipedia://en/98981), or more generally, any [Prüfer domain](wikipedia://en/12367835).[^n2] Another example is obtained by "pinching" formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) at the origin along a subfield of infinite extension degree, such as the subring of $\overline{\mathbf{Q}} [[T]]$ consisting of those [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) whose constant term is rational.[^n3]
