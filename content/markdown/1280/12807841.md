# Hurwitz surface

*Image: Order-7 triangular tiling.svg.* Every Hurwitz surface has a triangulation as a quotient of the [order-7 triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/8834365), with the automorphisms of the triangulation equaling the Riemannian and algebraic automorphisms of the surface.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hurwitz surface"}

In [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) theory and [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), a **Hurwitz surface**, named after [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690), is a [compact Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Riemann_surface) with precisely 84(*g* − 1) automorphisms, where *g* is the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) of the surface. This number is maximal by virtue of [Hurwitz's theorem on automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz's_theorem_on_automorphisms) (Hurwitz 1893). They are also referred to as **Hurwitz curves**, interpreting them as complex algebraic curves (complex dimension 1 = real dimension 2).

:::

The [Fuchsian group](wikipedia://en/471365) of a Hurwitz surface is a [finite index](wikipedia://en/230581) [torsionfree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion-free_group) normal subgroup of the (ordinary) [(2,3,7) triangle group](wikipedia://en/12659803). The finite quotient group is precisely the automorphism group.

Automorphisms of complex algebraic curves are *[orientation-preserving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientation-preserving)* automorphisms of the underlying real surface; if one allows orientation-*reversing* isometries, this yields a group twice as large, of order 168(*g* − 1), which is sometimes of interest.

A note on terminology – in this and other contexts, the "(2,3,7) triangle group" most often refers, not to the *full* triangle group Δ(2,3,7) (the [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) with [Schwarz triangle](wikipedia://en/2069325) (2,3,7) or a realization as a hyperbolic [reflection group](wikipedia://en/2422023)), but rather to the *ordinary* triangle group (the [von Dyck group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Dyck_group)) *D*(2,3,7) of orientation-preserving maps (the rotation group), which is index 2. The group of complex automorphisms is a quotient of the *ordinary* (orientation-preserving) triangle group, while the group of (possibly orientation-reversing) isometries is a quotient of the *full* triangle group.

## Classification by genus {#Classification_by_genus}

Only finitely many Hurwitz surfaces occur with each genus. The function $h(g)$ mapping the genus to the number of Hurwitz surfaces with that genus is unbounded, even though most of its values are zero. The sum

$$\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{h(g)}{g^s}$$

converges for $s > 1/3$, implying in an approximate sense that the genus of the $n$th Hurwitz surface grows at least as a cubic function of $n$ (Larsen 2001).

The Hurwitz surface of least genus is the [Klein quartic](wikipedia://en/390976) of genus 3, with automorphism group the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) [PSL(2,7)](wikipedia://en/390426), of order 84(3 − 1) = 168 = 2<sup>3</sup>·3·7, which is a [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600); (or order 336 if one allows orientation-reversing isometries). The next possible genus is 7, possessed by the [Macbeath surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macbeath_surface), with automorphism group PSL(2,8), which is the simple group of order 84(7 − 1) = 504 = 2<sup>3</sup>·3<sup>2</sup>·7; if one includes orientation-reversing isometries, the group is of order 1,008.

An interesting phenomenon occurs in the next possible genus, namely 14. Here there is a triple of distinct Riemann surfaces with the identical automorphism group (of order 84(14 − 1) = 1092 = 2<sup>2</sup>·3·7·13). The explanation for this phenomenon is arithmetic. Namely, in the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of the appropriate [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), the rational prime 13 splits as a product of three distinct [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928). The [principal congruence subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_congruence_subgroup) defined by the triplet of primes produce [Fuchsian groups](wikipedia://en/471365) corresponding to the [first Hurwitz triplet](wikipedia://en/13622736).

The sequence of allowable values for the genus of a Hurwitz surface begins

3, 7, 14, 17, 118, 129, 146, 385, 411, 474, 687, 769, 1009, 1025, 1459, 1537, 2091, ...
