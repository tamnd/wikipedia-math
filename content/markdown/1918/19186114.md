# Roger Wolcott Richardson

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Wolcott Richardson"}

**Roger Wolcott Richardson** (30 May 1930 – 15 June 1993) was a mathematician noted for his work in [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Richardson was born in [Baton Rouge, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_Rouge,_Louisiana), and educated at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University), [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and [University of Michigan, Ann Arbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan,_Ann_Arbor) where he obtained a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1958 under the supervision of [Hans Samelson](wikipedia://en/17290837). After a postdoc appointment at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), he accepted a faculty position at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle). He emigrated to the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) in 1970, taking up a chair at [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University). In 1978 he moved to the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), where he stayed as faculty until his death.

Richardson's best known result states that if *P* is a [parabolic subgroup](wikipedia://en/588356) of a [reductive group](wikipedia://en/1088386), then *P* has a dense orbit on its [nilradical](wikipedia://en/22468313), i.e., one whose closure is the whole space.[^o3] This orbit is now universally known as the [Richardson orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richardson_orbit).[^o4]

He was elected a Fellow of the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 1990.[^o5]

In 1997 the [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) published *Algebraic Groups and Lie Groups: A Volume of Papers in Honour of the Late R. W. Richardson*, which was organized by a committee of 5 mathematicians selected by the [Australian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/4579582). The volume's preface has several paragraphs about Richardson's research.[^o6]
