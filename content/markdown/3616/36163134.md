# Allen Knutson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Allen Knutson"}

**Allen Ivar Knutson** is an American mathematician who is a professor of mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Knutson graduated from Stuyvesant High School and completed his undergraduate studies at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology)[^o11] and received a Ph.D. from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1996 under the joint advisorship of [Victor Guillemin](wikipedia://en/13236636) and [Lisa Jeffrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Jeffrey).

## Career {#Career}

He was on the faculty at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) before moving to the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) in 2005 and then to [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 2009.[^o7] In 2005, he and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) won the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) for their paper "Honeycombs and Sums of Hermitian Matrices".[^o8] He was an invited speaker at the 2022 International Congress of Mathematicians.[^o9]

Knutson is also known for his studies of the [mathematics of juggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_juggling).[^o10] For five years beginning in 1990, he and fellow Caltech student David Morton held a [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juggling_world_records#Balls) for passing 12 balls.[^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Knutson is the son of Donald Ivar Knutson, an American mathematician and financial executive at [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS).[^o13]
