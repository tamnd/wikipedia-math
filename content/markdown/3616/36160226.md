# Octant (solid geometry)

*Image: Cube with balanced ternary labels.svg.* Three axial planes (*x*=0, *y*=0, *z*=0) divide space into eight octants. The eight (±,±,±) coordinates of the cube vertices are used to denote them. The horizontal plane shows the four quadrants between *x*- and *y*-axis. (Vertex numbers are little-endian balanced ternary.)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Octant"}

An **octant** in [solid geometry](wikipedia://en/507960) is one of the eight divisions of a [Euclidean](wikipedia://en/9417) [three-dimensional coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706) defined by the signs of the coordinates. It is analogous to the two-dimensional [*quadrant*](wikipedia://en/18315778) and the one-dimensional [*ray*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%28geometry%29). The generalization of an octant is called *[orthant](wikipedia://en/1283725)* or *hyperoctant*.

:::

## Naming and numbering {#Naming_and_numbering}

A convention for naming an octant is to give its list of signs, e.g. (+,−,−) or (−,+,−). Octant (+,+,+) is sometimes referred to as the *first octant*, although similar ordinal name descriptors are not defined for the other seven octants. The advantages of using the (±,±,±) notation are its unambiguousness, and extensibility for higher dimensions.

The following table shows the sign tuples together with likely ways to enumerate them. A binary enumeration with − as 1 can be easily generalized across dimensions. A binary enumeration with + as 1 defines the same order as [balanced ternary](wikipedia://en/376757). The [Roman](wikipedia://en/25657) enumeration of the [quadrants](wikipedia://en/18315778) is in [Gray code](wikipedia://en/50564) order, so the corresponding Gray code is also shown for the octants.

Verbal descriptions are ambiguous, because they depend on the representation of the coordinate system. In the two depicted representations of a [right-hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-hand_rule) coordinate system, the first octant could be called *right-back-top* or *right-top-front* respectively.
