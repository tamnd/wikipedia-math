# Tangent circles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tangent circles"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **tangent circles** (also known as **kissing circles**) are circles in a common plane that intersect in a single point. There are two types of [tangency](wikipedia://en/31482): internal and external. Many problems and constructions in geometry are related to tangent circles; such problems often have real-life applications, such as [trilateration](wikipedia://en/59675946) and maximizing the use of materials.

:::

### Apollonian gasket {#Apollonian_gasket}

If a circle is iteratively inscribed into the interstitial curved triangles between three mutually tangent circles, an Apollonian gasket results, one of the earliest fractals described in print.

## Malfatti's problem {#Malfatti's_problem}

Malfatti's problem is to carve three cylinders from a triangular block of marble, using as much of the marble as possible. In 1803, [Gian Francesco Malfatti](wikipedia://en/1475987) conjectured that the solution would be obtained by inscribing three mutually tangent circles into the triangle (a problem that had previously been considered by Japanese mathematician [Ajima Naonobu](wikipedia://en/841517)); these circles are now known as the [Malfatti circles](wikipedia://en/1475894), although the conjecture has been proven to be false.

## Six circles theorem {#Six_circles_theorem}

A chain of six circles can be drawn such that each circle is tangent to two sides of a given triangle and also to the preceding circle in the chain. The chain closes; the sixth circle is always tangent to the first circle.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Problems involving tangent circles are often generalized to spheres. For example, the Fermat problem of finding sphere(s) tangent to four given spheres is a generalization of [Apollonius' problem](wikipedia://en/2642185), whereas [Soddy's hexlet](wikipedia://en/7463064) is a generalization of a [Steiner chain](wikipedia://en/18866777).

:::
