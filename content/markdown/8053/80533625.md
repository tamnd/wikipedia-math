# Synergistic system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Synergistic system"}

A **Synergistic system** (or **S-system**)[^o2] is a collection of ordinary [nonlinear differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_differential_equations)

:::

$$\frac{dx_i}{dt}= \alpha_{i} \prod_{j=1}^{n+m} x_i ^{g_{ij}}- \beta_{j} \prod_{j=1}^{n+m} x_i ^{h_{ij}} ~~~~~ (i=1,...,n)$$

where the $x_{i}$ are positive real, $\alpha_{i}$ and $\beta_{i}$ are non-negative real, called the **[rate constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_rate_constant)**(or, **kinetic rates**) and $g_{ij}$ and $h_{ij}$ are real exponential, called **kinetic orders**. These terms are based on the [chemical equilibrium](wikipedia://en/5306)**[^o3]**

## One variable S-system {#One_variable_S-system}

Source:[^o5]

In the case of $n=1 (i=1)$ and $m=0$, the given S-system equation can be written as

$$\ \frac{dx}{dt}= \alpha x^{g}-\beta x^{h} \$$

Under the non-zero steady condition, $\frac{dx_0}{dt}= 0$, the following non-linear equation can be transformed into an [ordinary differential equation](wikipedia://en/32742753)(ODE).

**Transformation one variable S-system into a first-order ODE**

Let $x=e^{\operatorname{log} x}=e^{y}$(with $x_0=e^{y_0}$) Then, given a one-variable S-system is

$$\frac{dy}{dt}=\alpha e^{(g-1)y}-\beta e^{(h-1)y}$$

Apply a non-zero steady condition to the given equation

$0=\frac{dy_{0}}{dt}=\alpha e^{(g-1)y_{0}}-\beta e^{(h-1)y_{0}}$, or equivalently $\alpha e^{(g-1)y_{0}}=\beta e^{(h-1)y_{0}}$

Thus, $y_0 = \frac{\operatorname{log}\beta-\operatorname{log}\alpha}{g-h}$(or, $x_0 = \left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{g-h}}$)

If $\frac{dy}{dt}$ can be approximated around $y=y_0$, remaining the first two terms,

$$\frac{dy}{dt} \simeq \alpha e^{(g-1)y_0}+\alpha e^{(g-1)y_0}(g-1)(y-y_{0})-\beta e^{(g-1)y_0} - \beta e^{(h-1)y_0}(h-1)(y-y_{0})$$

By non-zero steady condition, $\alpha e^{(g-1)y_{0}}=\beta e^{(h-1)y_{0}}$, a nonlinear one-variable S-system can be transformed into [a first-order ODE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_ordinary_differential_equation):

$$\frac{du}{dt} \simeq \left ( \alpha e^{(g-1)y_0} (g-h) \right )u= \left ( Fa \right )u$$

where $F=\alpha e^{(g-1)y_0}$, $a=g-h$, and $u=y-y_0 \simeq \frac{x-x_0}{x_0}$, called a **percentage variation**.

## Two variables S-system {#Two_variables_S-system}

Source:[^o5]

In the case of $n=2 (i=1,2)$ and $m=0$ , the S-system equation can be written as [system of (non-linear) differential equations](wikipedia://en/249617).

$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{dx_1}{dt}= \alpha_{1}x_{1}^{g_{11}}x_2^{g_{21}}-\beta_{1}x_{1}^{h_{11}}x_{2}^{h_{21}} \ \\ \frac{dx_2}{dt}= \alpha_{2}x_{2}^{g_{21}}x_2^{g_{22}}-\beta_{2}x_{1}^{h_{21}}x_{2}^{h_{22}} \end{matrix}\right.$$

Assume non-zero steady condition, $\frac{dx_{i0}}{dt}= 0$.

**Transformation two variables S-system into a second-order ODE**

By putting $x_{i}=e^{\operatorname{log} x_{i}}=e^{y_{i}}$ . The given system of equations can be written as

$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{d u_{1}}{dt}= c_{11}u_{1}+ c_{12}u_{2} \ \\ \frac{d u_{2}}{dt}= c_{21}u_{1}+ c_{22}u_{2} \end{matrix}\right.$$

(where $u_{i}=y_{i}-y_{i0}$, $u_{i}=y_{i}-y_{i0}$ and $c_{ij}(i,j=1,2)$ are constant.

Since $\frac{d^{2}u_{1}}{dt^{2}}=c_{11}\frac{du_{1}}{dt}+c_{12}\frac{du_{2}}{dt}$, the given system of equation can be approximated as a second-order ODE:

$$\frac{d^{2}u_{1}}{dt^{2}}- \left ( c_{11}+c_{22} \right )\frac{du_{1}}{dt}+\left ( c_{11}c_{22}-c_{12}c_{21} \right )u_{1}=0,$$

## Applications {#Applications}

::: {#o10 .statement cue=named-heading name="Mass-action Law"}

### Mass-action Law {#Mass-action_Law}

Source:[^o3]

Consider the following chemical pathway:

$$\ce{A + 2B ->[k_1] C ->[k_2] 3D + E}$$

where $k_{1}$ and $k_{2}$ are [rate constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_constant).

Then the [mass-action law](wikipedia://en/318051) applied to species $\ce{C}$ gives the equation

$$\frac{d[C]}{dt}=k_1[A][B]^{2}-k_{2}[D]^{3}[E]$$

(where $[A]$ is a [concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration) of A etc.)

:::

### Komarova Model ([Bone Remodeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_remodeling)) {#Komarova_Model_(Bone_Remodeling)}

Sources:[^o12][^o13]

Komarova Model is an example of a two-variable system of non-linear differential equations that describes [bone remodeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_remodeling). This equation is regulated by biochemical factors called [paracrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracrine_signaling) and [autocrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocrine_signaling), which quantify the bone mass in each step.

$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \quad \frac{dx_{1}}{dt} = \alpha_{1} x_{1}^{g_{11}}x_{2}^{g_{21}}-\beta_{1}x_{1} \\ \quad \frac{dx_{2}}{dt} = \alpha_{2} x_{1}^{g_{12}}x_{2}^{g_{22}}-\beta_{2}x_{2} \\ \frac{dz}{dt}= -k_{1}y_{1}+k_{2}y_{2} \end{matrix}\right.$$

Where

- $x_1$, $x_2$: The number of [osteoclast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoclast)/[osteoblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoblast)
- $\alpha_{1}$, $\alpha_{2}$: Osteoclast/Osteoblast production rate
- $\beta_{1}$, $\beta_{2}$: Osteoclast/Osteoblast removal rate
- $g_{ij}$: Paracrine factor on the $j$-cell due to the presence of $i$-cell
- $z$: [The bone mass percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_mass)
- $y_{i}$: Let $\bar{x_i}$ be the difference between the number of osteoclasts/osteoblasts and its steady state. Then $y_{i}:=\frac{1}{2} \left [ \left ( x_{i} - \bar{x_{i}})+(x_{i}-\bar{x_{i}}) \right ) \right ]$

### Modified Komarova Model (Bone Remodeling with Tumor affecting, [Bone metastasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_metastasis)) {#Modified_Komarova_Model_(Bone_Remodeling_with_Tumor_affecting,_Bone_metastasis)}

Source:[^o15]

The modified Komarova Model describes the [tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor) effect on the osteoclasts and osteoblasts rate. The following equation can be described as

$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{dx_{1}}{dt} = \widehat{ \alpha_{1}(\omega)}x_{1}^{g_{11}}x_{2}^{g_{21}}-\widehat{\beta_{1}(\omega)}x_{1} \\ \frac{dx_{2}}{dt} = \widehat{ \alpha_{2}(\omega)} x_{2}^{g_{22}}-\widehat{ \beta_{2}(\omega)}x_{2} \\ \frac{d\omega}{dt}= \mu \omega \operatorname{log}\left (\sigma \frac{L_{\omega}}{\omega} \right ) \end{matrix}\right.$$

(with [initial condition](wikipedia://en/1186804) $x_1(0)=x_{10}$, $x_2(0)=x_{20}$, and $\omega(0)=\omega_{0}$)

Where

- $x_1$, $x_2$: The number of [osteoclast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoclast)/[osteoblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoblast).
- $\omega=\omega(t)$ : The tumor representation depending on time $t$
- $\widehat{\alpha_{1}(\omega)}$,$\widehat{\alpha_{2}(\omega)}$: The representation of the activity of cell production
- $\widehat{\beta_{1}(\omega)}$,$\widehat{\beta_{2}(\omega)}$: The representation of the activity of cell removal
- $g_{ij}$: The net effectiveness of osteoclast/osteoblast derived autocrine and paracrine factors
- $\mu$ : The tumor cell proliferation rate
- $L_{\omega}$: The upper limit value for tumor cells
- $\sigma$ : Scaling constant of tumor growth

:::
