# Complete quadrangle

*Image: Complete-quads.svg.* A complete quadrangle (at left) and a complete [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) (at right).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complete quadrangle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [incidence geometry](wikipedia://en/1576323) and especially in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), a **complete quadrangle** is a system of geometric objects consisting of any four [points](wikipedia://en/593693) in a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), no three of which are [on a common line](wikipedia://en/3189581), and of the six [lines](wikipedia://en/946975) connecting the six pairs of points. [Dually](wikipedia://en/539925), a *complete [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278)* is a system of four lines, no three of which pass through the same point, and the six points of [intersection](wikipedia://en/8240558) of these lines. The complete quadrangle was called a **tetrastigm** by (Lachlan 1893), and the complete quadrilateral was called a **tetragram**; those terms are occasionally still used. The complete quadrilateral has also been called a **Pasch configuration**, especially in the context of [Steiner triple systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_triple_system).[^o3]

:::

## Diagonals {#Diagonals}

The six lines of a complete quadrangle meet in pairs to form three additional points called the *diagonal points* of the quadrangle. Similarly, among the six points of a complete quadrilateral there are three pairs of points that are not already connected by lines; the [line segments](wikipedia://en/22634860) connecting these pairs are called *diagonals*. For points and lines in the Euclidean plane, the diagonal points cannot lie on a single line, and the diagonals cannot have a single point of triple crossing. Due to the discovery of the [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404), a [finite geometry](wikipedia://en/267061) in which the diagonal points of a complete quadrangle are [collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinear), some authors have augmented the axioms of projective geometry with *Fano's axiom* that the diagonal points are *not* collinear,[^o5] while others have been less restrictive.

A set of contracted expressions for the parts of a complete quadrangle were introduced by [G. B. Halsted](wikipedia://en/3992708): He calls the vertices of the quadrangle *dots*, and the diagonal points he calls *codots*. The lines of the projective space are called *straights*, and in the quadrangle they are called *connectors*. The "diagonal lines" of Coxeter are called *opposite connectors* by Halsted. Opposite connectors cross at a codot. The configuration of the complete quadrangle is a **tetrastim**.[^o6]

## Projective properties {#Projective_properties}

*Image: pappusharmonic.svg.* KLMN is a complete quadrangle;*D* is the [projective harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/16094600) of *C* with respect to *A* and *B*.

As systems of points and lines in which all points belong to the same number of lines and all lines contain the same number of points, the complete quadrangle and the complete quadrilateral both form [projective configurations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_configuration); in the notation of projective configurations, the complete quadrangle is written as (4<sub>3</sub>6<sub>2</sub>) and the complete quadrilateral is written (6<sub>2</sub>4<sub>3</sub>), where the numbers in this notation refer to the numbers of points, lines per point, lines, and points per line of the configuration. The [projective dual](wikipedia://en/539925) of a complete quadrangle is a complete quadrilateral, and vice versa. For any two complete quadrangles, or any two complete quadrilaterals, there is a unique [projective transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) taking one of the two configurations into the other.[^o8]

[Karl von Staudt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Staudt) reformed mathematical foundations in 1847 with the complete quadrangle when he noted that a "harmonic property" could be based on concomitants of the quadrangle: When each pair of opposite sides of the quadrangle intersect on a line, then the diagonals intersect the line at [projective harmonic conjugate](wikipedia://en/16094600) positions. The four points on the line deriving from the sides and diagonals of the quadrangle are called a [harmonic range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_range). Through perspectivity and projectivity, the harmonic property is stable. Developments of modern geometry and algebra note the influence of von Staudt on [Mario Pieri](wikipedia://en/2452874) and [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) .

## Euclidean properties {#Euclidean_properties}

In the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), the four lines of a complete quadrilateral must not include any pairs of parallel lines, so that every pair of lines has a crossing point.

(Wells 1991) describes several additional properties of complete quadrilaterals that involve metric properties of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410), rather than being purely projective. The midpoints of the diagonals are [collinear](wikipedia://en/60810627), and (as proved by [Isaac Newton](wikipedia://en/14627)) also collinear with the center of a [conic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic) that is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to all four lines of the quadrilateral. Any three of the lines of the quadrilateral form the sides of a triangle; the [orthocenters](wikipedia://en/161244) of the four triangles formed in this way lie on a second line (known as the ortholine, Steiner line or Aubert line[^o11][^o12]), perpendicular to the one through the midpoints. The [circumcircles](wikipedia://en/1926432) of these same four triangles meet in a [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miquel_point). In addition, the three circles having the diagonals as diameters belong to a common [pencil of circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_of_circles)[^o13] the axis of which is the line through the orthocenters.

The [polar circles](wikipedia://en/45079434) of the triangles of a complete quadrilateral form a [coaxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxal_circles) system.[^o14]
