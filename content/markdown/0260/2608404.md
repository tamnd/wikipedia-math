# Artin reciprocity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin reciprocity"}

The **Artin reciprocity law**, which was established by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) in a series of papers (1924; 1927; 1930), is a general theorem in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) that forms a central part of global [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695).[^o2] The term "[reciprocity law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocity_law_%28mathematics%29)" refers to a long line of more concrete number theoretic statements which it generalized, from the [quadratic reciprocity law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_reciprocity_law) and the reciprocity laws of [Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482) and [Kummer](wikipedia://en/319117) to [Hilbert's](wikipedia://en/8302) product formula for the [norm symbol](wikipedia://en/5924217). Artin's result provided a partial solution to [Hilbert's ninth problem](wikipedia://en/1695214).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $L/K$ be a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358), $\text{Gal}(L/K)$ be [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of this extension and $C_K, C_L$ denote the [idèle class groups](wikipedia://en/1168608) of respectively $K$ and $L$. Let:

$$N_{L/K}: C_L \rightarrow C_K$$

denote idele norm map. The Artin reciprocity law states that there exist canonical isomorphism between quotient group and [abelianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianization) of Galois group:[^o5][^o6]

$$\theta: C_K/{N_{L/K}(C_L)} \to \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)^{\text{ab}},$$

that is defined by assembling the local maps:

$$\theta_v: K_v^{\times}/N_{L_v/K_v}(L_v^{\times}) \to \text{Gal}(L/K)^{\text{ab}},$$

for different [places](wikipedia://en/5042667#Places) $v$ of $K$.

### Remarks {#Remarks}

- The map $\theta$ is called **global symbol map** or **Artin symbol**.
- For each place $v$ the local map $\theta_v$ is called **local Artin symbol**, **local reciprocity map** or **norm residue symbol**.
- The local maps $\theta_v$ are isomorphisms themselves, this is the content of local reciprocity law, key result in [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532).

::: {#o9 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

A cohomological proof of the global reciprocity law can be achieved by first establishing that

$$(\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\text{sep}}/K),\varinjlim C_L)$$

constitutes a [class formation](wikipedia://en/6019404) in the sense of Artin and Tate.[^o10] Then one proves that

$$\hat{H}^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K), C_L) \simeq\hat{H}^{-2}(\operatorname{Gal}(L/K), \mathbb{Z}),$$

where $\hat{H}^{i}$ denote the [Tate cohomology groups](wikipedia://en/6070279). Working out the cohomology groups establishes that $\theta$ is an isomorphism.

:::

:::

## Significance {#Significance}

Artin's reciprocity law implies a description of the [abelianization](wikipedia://en/8847#Abelianization) of the absolute [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) *K* which is based on the [Hasse local–global principle](wikipedia://en/489707) and the use of the [Frobenius elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element). Together with the [Takagi existence theorem](wikipedia://en/1099918), it is used to describe the [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) of *K* in terms of the arithmetic of *K* and to understand the behavior of the [nonarchimedean places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_%28mathematics%29) in them. Therefore, the [Artin reciprocity law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_reciprocity_law) can be interpreted as one of the main theorems of global class field theory. It can be used to prove that [Artin L-functions](wikipedia://en/2774171) are [meromorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic), and also to prove the [Chebotarev density theorem](wikipedia://en/535349).[^o12]

Two years after the publication of his general reciprocity law in 1927, Artin rediscovered the [transfer homomorphism](wikipedia://en/43066470) of I. Schur and used the reciprocity law to translate the [principalization problem](wikipedia://en/43103430) for ideal classes of [algebraic number](wikipedia://en/1158) fields into the group theoretic task of determining the kernels of transfers of finite non-abelian groups.[^o13]

## Finite extensions of global fields {#Finite_extensions_of_global_fields}

(See [math.stackexchange.com](https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/4131855/frobenius-elements#:~:text=A%20Frobenius%20element%20for%20P,some%20%CF%84%E2%88%88KP) for an explanation of some of the terms used here)

The definition of the Artin map for a [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) *L*/*K* of [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358) (such as a finite abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$) has a concrete description in terms of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) and [Frobenius elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_element).

If $\mathfrak{p}$ is a prime of *K* then the [decomposition groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decomposition_group) of primes $\mathfrak{P}$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ are equal in Gal(*L*/*K*) since the latter group is [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974). If $\mathfrak{p}$ is [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified) in *L*, then the decomposition group $D_\mathfrak{p}$ is canonically isomorphic to the Galois group of the extension of residue fields $\mathcal{O}_{L,\mathfrak{P}}/\mathfrak{P}$ over $\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}$. There is therefore a canonically defined Frobenius element in Gal(*L*/*K*) denoted by $\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}$ or $\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)$. If Δ denotes the [relative discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_discriminant) of *L*/*K*, the **Artin symbol** (or **Artin map**, or **(global) reciprocity map**) of *L*/*K* is defined on the [group of prime-to-Δ fractional ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_fractional_ideals), $I_K^\Delta$, by linearity:

$$\begin{cases} \left(\frac{L/K}{\cdot}\right):I_K^\Delta \longrightarrow \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\\ \prod_{i=1}^m\mathfrak{p}_i^{n_i} \longmapsto \prod_{i=1}^m\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak{p}_i}\right)^{n_i} \end{cases}$$

The **Artin reciprocity law** (or **global reciprocity law**) states that there is a [modulus](wikipedia://en/18510677) **c** of *K* such that the Artin map induces an isomorphism

$$I_K^\mathbf{c}/i(K_{\mathbf{c},1})\mathrm{N}_{L/K}(I_L^\mathbf{c})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$$

where *K*<sub>**c**,1</sub> is the [ray modulo **c**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_class_group), N<sub>*L*/*K*</sub> is the norm map associated to *L*/*K* and $I_L^\mathbf{c}$ is the fractional ideals of *L* prime to **c**. Such a modulus **c** is called a **defining modulus for *L*/*K***. The smallest defining modulus is called the [conductor of *L*/*K*](wikipedia://en/26355302) and typically denoted $\mathfrak{f}(L/K).$

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

#### Quadratic fields {#Quadratic_fields}

If $d\neq1$ is a [squarefree integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree_integer), $K=\mathbb{Q},$ and $L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, then $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ can be identified with {±1}. The discriminant Δ of *L* over $\mathbb{Q}$ is *d* or 4*d* depending on whether *d* ≡ 1 (mod 4) or not. The Artin map is then defined on primes *p* that do not divide Δ by

$$p\mapsto\left(\frac{\Delta}{p}\right)$$

where $\left(\frac{\Delta}{p}\right)$ is the [Kronecker symbol](wikipedia://en/706301).[^o18] More specifically, the conductor of $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is the principal ideal (Δ) or (Δ)∞ according to whether Δ is positive or negative,[^o19] and the Artin map on a prime-to-Δ ideal (*n*) is given by the Kronecker symbol $\left(\frac{\Delta}{n}\right).$ This shows that a prime *p* is split or inert in *L* according to whether $\left(\frac{\Delta}{p}\right)$ is 1 or −1.

#### Cyclotomic fields {#Cyclotomic_fields}

Let *m* > 1 be either an odd integer or a multiple of 4, let $\zeta_m$ be a [primitive *m*th root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity), and let $L = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ be the *m*th [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462). $\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ can be identified with $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ by sending σ to *a*<sub>σ</sub> given by the rule

$$\sigma(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^{a_\sigma}.$$

The conductor of $L/\mathbb{Q}$ is (*m*)∞,[^o21] and the Artin map on a prime-to-*m* ideal (*n*) is simply *n* (mod *m*) in $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.$[^o22]

:::

### Relation to quadratic reciprocity {#Relation_to_quadratic_reciprocity}

Let *p* and $\ell$ be distinct odd primes. For convenience, let $\ell^* = (-1)^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\ell$ (which is always 1 (mod 4)). Then, quadratic reciprocity states that

$$\left(\frac{\ell^*}{p}\right)=\left(\frac{p}{\ell}\right).$$

The relation between the quadratic and Artin reciprocity laws is given by studying the quadratic field $F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell^*})$ and the cyclotomic field $L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$ as follows.[^o18] First, *F* is a subfield of *L*, so if *H* = Gal(*L*/*F*) and $G= \operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}),$ then $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q}) = G/H.$ Since the latter has order 2, the subgroup *H* must be the group of squares in $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.$ A basic property of the Artin symbol says that for every prime-to-ℓ ideal (*n*)

$$\left(\frac{F/\mathbb{Q}}{(n)}\right)=\left(\frac{L/\mathbb{Q}}{(n)}\right)\pmod H.$$

When *n* = *p*, this shows that $\left(\frac{\ell^*}{p}\right)=1$ if and only if, *p* modulo ℓ is in *H*, i.e. if and only if, *p* is a square modulo ℓ.

## Statement in terms of *L*-functions {#Statement_in_terms_of_L-functions}

An alternative version of the reciprocity law, leading to the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905), connects [Artin L-functions](wikipedia://en/2774171) associated to abelian extensions of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) with Hecke L-functions associated to characters of the idèle class group.[^o25]

A [Hecke character](wikipedia://en/3149443) (or Größencharakter) of a number field *K* is defined to be a [quasicharacter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicharacter) of the idèle class group of *K*. [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986) interpreted Hecke characters as [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146) on the [reductive algebraic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_algebraic_group) *GL*(1) over the [ring of adeles](wikipedia://en/247978) of *K*.[^o26]

Let $E/K$ be an abelian Galois extension with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) *G*. Then for any [character](wikipedia://en/293418) $\sigma: G \to \mathbb{C}^{\times}$ (i.e. one-dimensional complex [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of the group *G*), there exists a Hecke character $\chi$ of *K* such that

$$L_{E/K}^{\mathrm{Artin}}(\sigma, s) = L_{K}^{\mathrm{Hecke}}(\chi, s)$$

where the left hand side is the Artin L-function associated to the extension with character σ and the right hand side is the Hecke L-function associated with χ, Section 7.D of.[^o26]

The formulation of the Artin reciprocity law as an equality of *L*-functions allows formulation of a generalisation to *n*-dimensional representations, though a direct correspondence is still lacking.
