# Peter Whittle (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Whittle"}

**Peter Whittle** (27 February 1927 – 10 August 2021[^o46]) was a mathematician and statistician from New Zealand, working in the fields of [stochastic nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_net), [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) analysis, [stochastic optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_optimisation) and [stochastic dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_dynamics). From 1967 to 1994, he was the [Churchill Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Professor_of_Mathematics_of_Information) of Mathematics for [Operational Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Research) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Whittle was born in [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington). He graduated from the [University of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Zealand) in 1947 with a BSc in mathematics and physics and in 1948 with an MSc in mathematics.[^o47]

He then moved to Uppsala, Sweden in 1950 to study for his PhD with [Herman Wold](wikipedia://en/454834) (at [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University)). His thesis, *Hypothesis Testing in Time Series*, generalised Wold's [autoregressive](wikipedia://en/1434444) [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_theorem) [theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition) for univariate [stationary processes](wikipedia://en/329898) to [multivariate](wikipedia://en/19384) processes. Whittle's thesis was published in 1951. A synopsis of Whittle's thesis also appeared as an appendix to the second edition of Wold's book on time-series analysis. Whittle remained in Uppsala at the Statistics Institute as a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) until 1953, when he returned to New Zealand.

In New Zealand, Whittle worked at the [Department of Scientific and Industrial Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Scientific_and_Industrial_Research_%28New_Zealand%29) (DSIR) in the Applied Mathematics Laboratory (later named the Applied Mathematics Division).

In 1959 Whittle was appointed to a [lectureship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) in Cambridge University. Whittle was appointed Professor of [Mathematical statistics](wikipedia://en/888711) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) in 1961. After six years in [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), Whittle returned to Cambridge as the [Churchill Professor of Mathematics for Operational Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_Professorship_of_Mathematics_for_Operational_Research), a post he held until his retirement in 1994. From 1973, he was also Director of the [Statistical Laboratory, University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Laboratory,_University_of_Cambridge). He was a fellow of [Churchill College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_College,_Cambridge). He died in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Whittle was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1978, and an Honorary Fellow of the [Royal Society of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_New_Zealand) in 1981. The Royal Society awarded him their [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) in 1994 in recognition of his "major distinctive contributions to time series analysis, to optimisation theory, and to a wide range of topics in applied probability theory and the mathematics of operational research". In 1986, the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences) awarded Whittle the Lanchester Prize for his book *Systems in Stochastic Equilibrium* () and the [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize) in 1997 for his "outstanding contributions to the theory of operations research and management science". He was elected to the 2002 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Institute for Operations Research and the Management Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations_Research_and_the_Management_Sciences).[^o48]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1951, Whittle married a Finnish woman, Käthe Blomquist, whom he had met in Sweden. The Whittle family has six children.
