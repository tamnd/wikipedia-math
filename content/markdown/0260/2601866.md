# Stellated octahedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stellated octahedron"}

The **stellated octahedron**, **elevated octahedron**, or **compound of two tetrahedra** is a shape made from two [regular tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/5242521) crossing each other. It is also called the **stella octangula** (Latin for "eight-pointed star"), a name given to it by [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in 1609, though it was known to earlier [geometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer). It appears as a carving in a 13th century Turkish [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), and in modern architectural decorations; it has been depicted by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci) in [Pacioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacioli)'s 1509 *[De Divina Proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)*, and in the works of [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127).

:::

The two tetrahedra of this shape form the simplest of the five regular [polyhedral compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_compound), and the only regular polyhedral compound composed of only two polyhedra. They form the only fully symmetric [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458), and dually the only fully symmetric [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) of the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285). The combinatorial structure of this shape has been considered in multiple variations; these vary by whether the triangular faces of the two crossing tetrahedra are considered as faces themselves or whether they are subdivided into smaller triangular faces, and by whether interior boundaries or only the outer shell are included.

The [stella octangula numbers](wikipedia://en/34110891) are [figurate numbers](wikipedia://en/325260) defined from the stellated octahedron. The two tetrahedra of a stellated octahedron can be extended to form a [desmic system](wikipedia://en/23457340) of three tetrahedra whose edges, extended to [projective lines](wikipedia://en/398578), each cross four other such lines. The stellated octahedron is the second stage of construction of a [geode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geode)-like three-dimensional [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) within a cube, analogous to the two-dimensional [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959). The stellated octahedron can be generalized to compounds of two centrally symmetric [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) in any dimension, forming a family of shapes that also includes the two-dimensional [hexagram](wikipedia://en/302441) or [Star of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David). Applications of the stellated octahedron include the fabrication of [nanoparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoparticle) with distinctive electromagnetic and biological properties, the theoretical understanding of [quantum entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336), and the design of [four-bar linkages](wikipedia://en/1442361) and [auxetic metamaterials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxetics).

## Construction and properties {#Construction_and_properties}

The stellated octahedron can be constructed in multiple different ways, including as a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253), as a [compound polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_polyhedron), as a [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537), and as an [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29).

### Stellation {#Stellation}

*Image: Stellation of octahedron facets.svg.* [Stellation diagram](wikipedia://en/18696734) of a stellated octahedron

The stellated octahedron is constructed by a [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253) of the [regular octahedron](wikipedia://en/22458).[^o3][^o7] In a stellation, the faces of the underlying polyhedron are extended within the same planes to enclose a different volume. It is generally required that the result maintain the same symmetry as the underlying polyhedron,[^o7] and with this restriction the stellated octahedron is the only stellation of the octahedron. Here, the extension in each plane consists of three equilateral triangles, surrounding the original triangular face of the octahedron and having the same size as it. These three triangles and the fourth triangle that they surround together form a larger equilateral triangle, and when constructed in this way, the stellated octahedron has eight of these larger equilateral triangle faces, crossing each other.[^o3][^o7]

Some versions of stellation also take the further step of removing parts of the extended faces to produce a polyhedral surface without self-crossings or interior voids.[^n1] For these versions, there are again eight faces, one in each plane, but each face takes the shape of an equilateral triangle with a hole formed by the removal of its [midpoint triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midpoint_triangle). This is shown in its [stellation diagram](wikipedia://en/18696734), which depicts the plane of a single extended face and shades the outer boundary of the stellation within that plane.[^o3]

### Compound {#Compound}

The stellated octahedron is also a regular [polyhedron compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedron_compound). Here, a compound is a system of two or more polyhedra, and being regular means that it has symmetries that take every two vertices to each other, that take every two edges to each other, and that take every two faces to each other.[^o10] The stellated compound is a compound of two [regular tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_tetrahedra), related to each other by a [central symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry) through the centroid of each tetrahedron. Hence, the stellated octahedron is also called the compound of two tetrahedra.[^o7]

Both tetrahedra can be inscribed in a cube, and each one shares four vertices with the cube.[^o11] The two tetrahedra share a common [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281), making the compound [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual).[^o12] The regular octahedron whose stellation forms this compound can be recovered as the intersection of the two tetrahedra.[^o13] This compound is related to several others: stellating the [compound of five octahedra](wikipedia://en/8010546) produces five stellated octahedra that together form the [compound of ten tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/8010465), and selecting one tetrahedron from each of these five stellated octahedra produces the [compound of five tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/3645795).[^n2]

If the edges of two congruent tetrahedra are arranged in this configuration, it is possible to slide the two tetrahedra against each other, to less symmetric configurations, in such a way that each pair of crossing edges remains [coplanar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coplanar). The relative positions of the two tetrahedra that can be reached in this way form a system of one- and two-dimensional [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) within the [six-dimensional configuration space](wikipedia://en/473596#Rigid_body_in_3D_space) of positions of one tetrahedron relative to the other.[^o15][^o16]

### Faceting {#Faceting}

*Image: CubeAndStel.svg.* Stellated octahedron as a cube faceting

The stellated octahedron is a [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) of the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), meaning that it is a polyhedron or compound polyhedron within a cube that uses only the vertices of the cube. Faceting is the [dual](wikipedia://en/8815) process to stellation. For the faceting that produces the stellated octahedron, the edges of the faceting are the [face diagonals](wikipedia://en/10830506) of the cube, and the faces are equilateral triangles connecting the three neighbors of each cube vertex. These vertices, edges, and faces form two tetrahedra, forming the stellated octahedron as a compound of two tetrahedra.[^o18]

Although the cube can be faceted in multiple ways with lesser symmetry, the stellated octahedron is the only faceting that has the same [three-dimensional point group symmetry](wikipedia://en/2694525) as the cube,[^o18] an [octahedral symmetry](wikipedia://en/2900805).[^n3][^o20]

### Augmentation {#Augmentation}

*Image: Diamant1.gif.* The [triakis octahedron](wikipedia://en/722549), a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) with the same combinatorial structure as the outer boundary of the augmented octahedron

A shape with the same surface geometry as the stellated octahedron, but with a different combinatorial face structure, can be obtained as an [augmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmentation_%28geometry%29) of the regular octahedron, by adding tetrahedral [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045) on each face. In this form, the faces of the augmented octahedron are equilateral triangles, of half the side length and one quarter the area of the faces of the corresponding compound of two tetrahedra.[^o26] The volume of the augmented octahedron is the sum of the volumes of eight tetrahedra and one regular octahedron. For an augmented octahedron with edge length $a$, this gives volume

$$V=8\frac{a^3\sqrt2}{12}+\frac{a^3\sqrt2}{3}=a^3\sqrt2.$$

Equivalently, the volume is 3/2 of the volume of one of the larger tetrahedra forming it as a compound of two tetrahedra, and 1/2 of the volume of the cube in which it is inscribed.[^o22]

When only the outer faces of the added pyramids are used, this construction produces a non-convex polyhedron with the same combinatorial structure as the convex [triakis octahedron](wikipedia://en/722549), a [Catalan solid](wikipedia://en/564393) with much shorter pyramids. Any of these pyramid attachments, convex or non-convex, may be known as the [Kleetope](wikipedia://en/31165912) of an octahedron.[^o23] For another choice of pyramid height, intermediate between the triakis octahedron and stellated octahedron, pairs of adjacent triangle faces from different pyramids lie on the same plane, and the shape [degenerates](wikipedia://en/232952) to the [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631).[^o24]

The outer shell of the augmented octahedron, with 24 equilateral-triangle faces, is an example of a [non-convex deltahedron](wikipedia://en/493995#Non-convex_deltahedron).[^o25][^o26] There are several different ways of arranging the 24 equilateral triangles of this shape into a [net](wikipedia://en/1148129), a planar system of polygons from which this deltahedron can be folded.[^o27][^o28] When the faces of the inner octahedron are retained rather than replaced, the resulting structure (the *elevation* of the octahedron) has 32 equilateral triangles in two shells: the outer augmentation and an inner regular octahedron. Its [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) at the inner six vertices takes the form of two nested polygons, a square within an octagon.[^o29] The [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) obtained from the augmented octahedron, with or without its eight inner triangles, has 14 [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) and 36 [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29); it has been called the *stellated octahedron graph*.[^o30]

As an augmented polyhedron, the stellated octahedron has two [dihedral angles](wikipedia://en/338046) between two equilateral triangles, namely a convex and a concave angle, obtained from the angle of a tetrahedron and an octahedron. The stellated octahedron's convex angle is the same as the dihedral angle of a tetrahedron, whereas its concave angle is the sum of two tetrahedra attached to an octahedron. That is,[^o31]

$$\begin{aligned} \arccos\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) &\approx 70.5^\circ, \\ \arccos\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) + 2 \arccos\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) &\approx 250.5^\circ. \end{aligned}$$

## Appearances {#Appearances}

### Historical {#Historical}

One of the geometric stone carvings in the Sahib Ata (İshaklı) caravanserai, built in the 13th century in [Sultandağı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultandağı), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey), takes the shape of a stellated octahedron.[^o34]

[Luca Pacioli](wikipedia://en/18473) 1509's *[Divina proportione](wikipedia://en/16175342)* introduced the concept of the elevation of a polyhedron, formed by replacing each face of the polyhedron by a [pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid). Pacioli describes the elevated octahedron (named by him in Latin, octaedron elevatum) as having two layers of faces, the outer visible layer having 24 equilateral triangles and the hidden inner layer taking the form of an octahedron with another 8 equilateral triangle faces. [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s illustration of an elevated regular octahedron for the book takes the form of a stellated octahedron.[^o39]

The stellated octahedron subsequently appeared in Renaissance books of illustrated polyhedra including Lorenz Stöer's *Geometria et Perspectiva* (1567),[^o35] [Daniele Barbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniele_Barbaro)'s *La pratica della perspettiva* (1568),[^o36] and [Wenzel Jamnitzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzel_Jamnitzer)'s *[Perspectiva corporum regularium](wikipedia://en/65182964)* (1568).[^o37] The stellated octahedron was rediscovered as a [faceting](wikipedia://en/8613537) of the cube by Frans Cophart of the [Collegium Musicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegium_Musicum) in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden); Cophart claimed that this was a sixth regular polyhedron, because its 24 faces were both regular and symmetric. [Simon Stevin](wikipedia://en/72586) disagreed in his 1583 work *Problemata geometrica*, noting that Cophart's shape had two kinds of vertices, instead of all vertices being symmetric to each other.[^o23][^o38] [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) in his 1609's *[Harmonice Mundi](wikipedia://en/1264842)* introduced the concept of [stellation](wikipedia://en/180253), the extension of the faces of one polyhedron within the same planes to form a different polyhedron, and named the stellated octahedron the "stella octangula", Latin for "eight-pointed star".[^o39]

A 1627 painting by [Gérard Douffet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gérard_Douffet), *Pope Nicholas V's visit to the tomb of Saint Francis of Assisi*, depicts a stellated octahedron hanging from the dome of the church above the tomb, over a scene of an [exorcism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exorcism). Art historian Pierre-Yves Kairis calls out this detail as a "[Mannerist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mannerism) oddity".[^o40]

### In science and technology {#In_science_and_technology}

*Image: 3D model of a Stellated Octahedron.stl.* 3D model of stellated octahedron

Researchers have used various methods to fabricate [nanoparticles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanoparticle) of [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold) and [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium) with the "octopod" shape of a stellated octahedron. The pointed shapes of these particles give them distinctive electromagnetic and biological properties.[^o42][^o43][^o44]

The stellated octahedron has also been used in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202). The [Bell states](wikipedia://en/1007613) of two [qubits](wikipedia://en/25284) can be modeled as the four vertices of a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), consisting of all possible states of the two qubits. A "Peres transformation", the reflection of this tetrahedron, forms a second tetrahedron, crossing the first one to form a stellated octahedron, within which the central octahedron formed by the intersection of the two tetrahedra forms the set of Bell-diagonal [separable states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_states).[^o45][^o46]

In the design of [mechanical linkages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_linkage), the design space of a planar [four-bar linkage](wikipedia://en/1442361) can be studied by normalizing the length of a fixed bar to one, and defining parameters for the lengths of the other three bars. The tangents of the angles made at the two ends of the fixed bar are then related by a [quartic equation](wikipedia://en/251591) whose coefficients involve pairwise products of eight linear combinations of the length parameters. The eight planes in the parameter space where these eight combinations become zero form the faces of a stellated octahedron. A similar analysis, producing a shape resembling a stellated octahedron but with curved surfaces, applies to spherical four-bar linkages.[^o47]

Tanaka et al have investigated [auxetic metamaterials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxetics) in which stretching the material in one direction does not necessarily cause compression in perpendicular directions, obtained by repeating a three-dimensional grid of unit cells, each taking the form of a stellated octahedron whose eight rigid tetrahedra are connected to some but not all neighboring tetrahedra at hinged joints.[^o48]

Investigation of [granular materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granular_material) with particles in the shape of stellated octahedra found them to have an [angle of repose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_repose) intermediate between the shallower angles of convex particles with a shape closer to spheres and the steeper angles of tetrahedra and more extremely non-convex [caltrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltrop)-shaped particles.[^o49]

### In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

The stellated octahedron appears with several other polyhedra and polyhedral compounds in [M. C. Escher](wikipedia://en/20127)'s prints *[Stars](wikipedia://en/1170784)* and *Study for Stars*, in a skeletal style similar to that of da Vinci.[^o52] It provides the central form in Escher's *[Double Planetoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Planetoid)* (1949).[^o53] Escher likely learned of this shape and of other compound and stellated polyhedra from [Max Brückner](wikipedia://en/48703370)'s book *Vielecke und Vielflache* (1900).[^o54] In *Stars*, it has been theorized to represent [crystal twinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_twinning)[^o55] or a [double star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_star).[^o56] In *Double Planetoid*, the two tetrahedra of the compound form two interpenetrating worlds, one entirely covered by architecture, the other a wilderness populated by [saurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurian) creatures.[^o57]

The obelisk in the center of the Plaza de Europa (Zaragoza) in [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain), is surrounded by twelve stellated octahedral lampposts, shaped to form a three-dimensional version of the [Flag of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Europe).[^o58]

The single decoration on the tomb of [André Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/André_Breton), in the [Batignolles Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batignolles_Cemetery) of Paris, is a stellated octahedron.[^o59] French historian Henri Calhiol has documented stone carvings of this shape on several country houses in the [Arrondissement of Mirande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Mirande) of southwestern France.[^o60] They appear also in interior wooden staircase carvings in the [Maison du Val de Villé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_du_Val_de_Villé) in northeastern France.[^o61]

### Mysticism {#Mysticism}

Some modern mystics have associated this shape with the "merkaba":[^o63] a "counterrotating field of light"[^o64] that "transport\[s\] body and soul to other dimensions."[^o65] [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) authors have attributed the merkaba to ancient Egyptian origins[^o64] — traditionally, "mer" stood for [pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid), "[ka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_conception_of_the_soul)" for [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul), and "[ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_conception_of_the_soul#Ba_%28personality%29)" for personality or spiritual essence that guides the soul. In a different tradition, Jewish ["Merkabah" mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkabah_mysticism) details a living *chariot* in the visions of [Ezekiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel) (in Hebrew, [chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariot) is written **מֶרְכָּבָה** and pronounced *merkābâ*, where "rakab" means "to ride" or "to be carried"), used by higher angels for motility.[^o66]

The triangles in the stellated octahedron have been used as a sign of the Christian [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity), copied 24 times for the 24 hours of the day.[^o61] The resemblance between this shape and the two-dimensional [star of David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_David) has also been frequently noted.[^o67]

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

*Image: StellaOctangulaNumber.jpg.* 124 [magnetic balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium_magnet_toys) arranged into the shape of a stellated octahedron

### Stella octangula numbers {#Stella_octangula_numbers}

The [stella octangula numbers](wikipedia://en/34110891) are [figurate numbers](wikipedia://en/325260) that count the number of balls that can be arranged into the shape of a stellated octahedron. These numbers are the form of $n(2n^2 - 1)$ for $n$ being the positive integers; the first ten such numbers are:[^o70]

0, 1, 14, 51, 124, 245, 426, 679, 1016, 1449, 1990, .... .

The only two of these numbers that are also [square numbers](wikipedia://en/213919) are 1 and[^o71]

$$9\,653\,449 = 3107^2 = 169(2\cdot 169^2-1).$$

### Tangent circles {#Tangent_circles}

*Image: Stella octangula circle packing.svg.* Eight circles that, when lifted to a sphere by [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431), lie on the face planes of a stellated octahedron

It is a theorem of [absolute geometry](wikipedia://en/699294) that, when [four circles are mutually tangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes'_theorem), then there exists another set of four mutually tangent circles each crossing at right angles through three of the six points of tangency of the first four circles. When these circles all lie on a sphere, the planes through them form a compound of two tetrahedra, with the same combinatorial structure (but not necessarily the same shape) as a compound of two regular tetrahedra. In this case, the sphere that contains the circles is the [midsphere](wikipedia://en/4709281) of both tetrahedra.[^o73]

### Fractal {#Fractal}

*Image: 01 3D Koch Schneeflocke-5.svg.* The third stage of construction of a three-dimensional [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) analogous to the [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959). The green triangles outline a central tetrahedron, the first stage, and the red triangles outline a second tetrahedron that in the second stage forms a stellated octahedron. The yellow and blue triangles are added in the third stage.

The [Koch snowflake](wikipedia://en/46959), a two-dimensional [fractal](wikipedia://en/10913) resembling a hexagram, can be generalized in three dimensions to a fractal whose first stage of construction is a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521). Each successive stage subdivides each triangle of the previous stage into four smaller equilateral triangles and then attaches a triangular pyramid to the middle triangle of each subdivided triangle. The second stage is a stellated octahedron. In the limit, the outer surface of this three-dimensional fractal forms a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), but unlike a cube it has a [geode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geode)-like interior fractal structure.[^o75][^o76][^o77]

### Desmic tetrahedra {#Desmic_tetrahedra}

The two tetrahedra of the compound view of the stellated octahedron are "desmic", meaning that (when extended into a line in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135)) each edge of one tetrahedron crosses two opposite edges of the other tetrahedron. One of these two crossings is visible in the stellated octahedron; the other crossing occurs at a [point at infinity](wikipedia://en/403139) of the projective space, where each edge of one tetrahedron crosses the parallel edge of the other tetrahedron. These two tetrahedra can be completed to a [desmic system](wikipedia://en/23457340) of three tetrahedra, where the third tetrahedron has as its four vertices the four [centers of perspectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_perspectivity) of the first two tetrahedra: three points at infinity (the three points where [parallel lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_line) extending the edges of its enclosing cube meet) and the centroid of the two finite tetrahedra. Every desmic system is equivalent under [projective transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_transformation) to a system formed from a stellated octahedron in this way.[^o79] The same twelve tetrahedron vertices also form the points of [Reye's configuration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reye's_configuration).[^o80]

### In other dimensions {#In_other_dimensions}

This shape can be seen as a 3D extension of the [hexagram](wikipedia://en/302441):[^o67] the hexagram is a two-dimensional shape formed from two crossing equilateral triangles, [centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_symmetry) to each other, and in the same way, the stellated octahedron can be formed from two centrally symmetric crossing tetrahedra. This can be generalized to any desired number of higher dimensions; the four-dimensional equivalent construction is the [compound of two 5-cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_of_two_5-cells).[^o82]
