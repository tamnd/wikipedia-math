# Graham Higman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Graham Higman"}

**Graham Higman** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o3] (19 January 1917 – 8 April 2008) was a prominent English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his contributions to [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Higman was born in [Louth, Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louth,_Lincolnshire), and attended [Sutton High School, Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_High_School,_Plymouth), winning a scholarship to [Balliol College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College,_Oxford).[^o5] In 1939 he co-founded [The Invariant Society](wikipedia://en/30248689), the student mathematics society,[^o6] and earned his DPhil from the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 1941. His thesis, *The units of group-rings*, was written under the direction of [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179). From 1960 to 1984 he was the [Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waynflete_Professorships#Waynflete_Professors_of_Pure_Mathematics) at [Magdalen College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford).

Higman was awarded the [Senior Berwick Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick_Prizes) in 1962 and the [De Morgan Medal](wikipedia://en/1096532) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1974. He was the founder of the [Journal of Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Algebra) and its editor from 1964 to 1984. Higman had 51 D.Phil. students, including [Jonathan Lazare Alperin](wikipedia://en/25403965), [Rosemary A. Bailey](wikipedia://en/26414007), [Marston Conder](wikipedia://en/38305099), [John Mackintosh Howie](wikipedia://en/2916430), and [Peter M. Neumann](wikipedia://en/7151472).

He was also a [local preacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_local_preacher) in the Oxford Circuit of the [Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodism). During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) he was a [conscientious objector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objector), working at the [Meteorological Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorological_Office) in [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland) and [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar).

He died in Oxford.[^o5]
