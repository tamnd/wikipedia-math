::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="List of k-uniform tilings"}

A ***k*-uniform tiling** is a tiling of [tilings](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane by convex [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306), connected edge-to-edge, with *k* types of vertices. The 1-uniform tiling include 3 regular tilings, and 8 semiregular tilings. A 1-uniform tiling can be defined by its [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745). Higher *k*-uniform tilings are listed by their vertex figures, but are not generally uniquely identified this way.

:::

The complete lists of *k*-uniform tilings have been enumerated up to *k*=6. There are 20 2-uniform tilings, 61 3-uniform tilings, 151 4-uniform tilings, 332 5-uniform tilings, and 673 6-uniform tilings. This article lists all solutions up to *k*=5.

*Image: Halfshift square tiling.svg.* This square tiling is [isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560) and [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120), but not uniform because it is not edge-to-edge.

Other [tilings of regular polygons that are not edge-to-edge](wikipedia://en/1548669#Tilings_that_are_not_edge-to-edge) allow different sized polygons, and continuous shifting positions of contact.

Such periodic tilings of convex polygons may be classified by the number of [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Orbits_and_stabilizers) of vertices, edges and tiles. If there are $k$ orbits of vertices, a tiling is known as $k$-uniform or $k$-[isogonal](wikipedia://en/581560); if there are $t$ orbits of tiles, as $t$-[isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120); if there are $e$ orbits of edges, as $e$-[isotoxal](wikipedia://en/6289189).

*k*-uniform tilings with the same vertex figures can be further identified by their [wallpaper group](wikipedia://en/386214) symmetry.

## Enumeration {#Enumeration}

1-uniform tilings include 3 regular tilings, and 8 semiregular ones, with 2 or more types of regular polygon faces. There are 20 2-uniform tilings, 61 3-uniform tilings, 151 4-uniform tilings, 332 5-uniform tilings and 673 6-uniform tilings. Each can be grouped by the number *m* of distinct vertex figures, which are also called ***m*-Archimedean tilings**.[^o7]

Finally, if the number of types of vertices is the same as the uniformity (*m* = *k* below), then the tiling is said to be *[Krotenheerdt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeis:A068600)*. In general, the uniformity is greater than or equal to the number of types of vertices (*m* ≥ *k*), as different types of vertices necessarily have different orbits, but not vice versa. Setting *m* = *n* = *k*, there are 11 such tilings for *n* = 1; 20 such tilings for *n* = 2; 39 such tilings for *n* = 3; 33 such tilings for *n* = 4; 15 such tilings for *n* = 5; 10 such tilings for *n* = 6; and 7 such tilings for *n* = 7.

## 1-uniform tilings (regular) {#1-uniform_tilings_(regular)}

A tiling is said to be *regular* if the [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) of the tiling [acts transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Types_of_actions) on the *flags* of the tiling, where a flag is a triple consisting of a mutually incident [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417#Of_a_plane_tiling), edge and tile of the tiling. This means that, for every pair of flags, there is a symmetry operation mapping the first flag to the second. This is equivalent to the tiling being an [edge-to-edge tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge-to-edge_tiling) by [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) regular polygons. There must be six [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), four [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) or three regular [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733) at a vertex, yielding the three regular tessellations.

## m-Archimedean and k-uniform tilings {#m-Archimedean_and_k-uniform_tilings}

[Vertex-transitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) means that for every pair of vertices there is a [symmetry operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_operation) mapping the first vertex to the second.[^o15]

If the requirement of flag-transitivity is relaxed to one of vertex-transitivity, while the condition that the tiling is edge-to-edge is kept, there are eight additional tilings possible, known as *Archimedean*, *[uniform](wikipedia://en/12126635)* or *demiregular* tilings. Note that there are two [mirror image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_image) (enantiomorphic or [chiral](wikipedia://en/1170160)) forms of 3<sup>4</sup>.6 (snub hexagonal) tiling, only one of which is shown in the following table. All other regular and semiregular tilings are achiral.

Grünbaum and Shephard distinguish the description of these tilings as *Archimedean* as referring only to the local property of the arrangement of tiles around each vertex being the same, and that as *uniform* as referring to the global property of vertex-transitivity. Though these yield the same set of tilings in the plane, in other spaces there are Archimedean tilings which are not uniform.

## Higher k-uniform tilings {#Higher_k-uniform_tilings}

*k*-uniform tilings have been enumerated up to 6. There are 673 6-uniform tilings of the Euclidean plane. Brian Galebach's search reproduced Krotenheerdt's list of 10 6-uniform tilings with 6 distinct vertex types, as well as finding 92 of them with 5 vertex types, 187 of them with 4 vertex types, 284 of them with 3 vertex types, and 100 with 2 vertex types.
