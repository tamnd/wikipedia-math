# Outline of logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Outline of logic"}

**[Logic](wikipedia://en/46426065)** is the [formal science](wikipedia://en/3694774) of using [reason](wikipedia://en/42446) and is considered a branch of both [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and to a lesser extent [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). Logic investigates and classifies the structure of statements and arguments, both through the study of [formal systems](wikipedia://en/396102) of inference and the study of arguments in [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173). The scope of logic can therefore be very large, ranging from core topics such as the study of [fallacies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallacies) and [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390), to specialized analyses of reasoning such as [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), correct reasoning, and arguments involving [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality). One of the aims of logic is to identify the correct (or [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742)) and incorrect (or [fallacious](wikipedia://en/53986)) [inferences](wikipedia://en/317465). Logicians study the criteria for the evaluation of [arguments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_argument).

### Fallacies {#Fallacies}

- [Fallacy](wikipedia://en/53986) ([list](wikipedia://en/8042940)) – incorrect argumentation in reasoning resulting in a misconception or presumption. By accident or design, fallacies may exploit emotional triggers in the listener or interlocutor (appeal to emotion), or take advantage of social relationships between people (e.g. argument from authority). Fallacious arguments are often structured using rhetorical patterns that obscure any logical argument. Fallacies can be used to win arguments regardless of the merits. There are dozens of types of fallacies.

### Computability theory {#Computability_theory}

[Computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414) – branch of mathematical logic that originated in the 1930s with the study of computable functions and [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405). The field has grown to include the study of generalized computability and definability. The basic questions addressed by recursion theory are "What does it mean for a function from the natural numbers to themselves to be computable?" and "How can noncomputable functions be classified into a hierarchy based on their level of noncomputability?". The answers to these questions have led to a rich theory that is still being actively researched.
