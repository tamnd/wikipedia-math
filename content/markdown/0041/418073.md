# Elias M. Stein

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elias M. Stein"}

**Elias Menachem Stein** (January 13, 1931 – December 23, 2018) was an American mathematician who was a leading figure in the field of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). He was the [Albert Baldwin Dod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Baldwin_Dod) Professor of Mathematics, Emeritus, at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he was a faculty member from 1963 until his death in 2018.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Stein was born in [Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antwerp), Belgium, to Elkan Stein and Chana Goldman, [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) from Belgium.[^o4] After the [German invasion in 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Belgium), the Stein family fled to the United States, first arriving in New York City.[^o4] He graduated from [Stuyvesant High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_High_School) in 1949,[^o4] where he was classmates with future [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) recipient [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen_%28mathematician%29),[^o5] before moving on to the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) for college. In 1955, Stein earned a Ph.D. from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) under the direction of [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477). He began teaching at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) in 1955, moved to the University of Chicago in 1958 as an assistant professor, and in 1963 became a full professor at Princeton.

Stein worked primarily in the field of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), and made contributions in both extending and clarifying [Calderón–Zygmund theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderón–Zygmund_theory). These include *Stein interpolation* (a variable-parameter version of [complex interpolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_interpolation)), the *Stein maximal principle* (showing that under many circumstances, [almost everywhere convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_everywhere_convergence) is equivalent to the boundedness of a [maximal function](wikipedia://en/16358287)), *[Stein complementary series representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stein_complementary_series_representation)*, *Nikishin–Pisier–Stein factorization* in operator theory, the *Tomas–Stein restriction theorem* in [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), the *Kunze–Stein phenomenon* in [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) on [semisimple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_group), the [Cotlar–Stein lemma](wikipedia://en/8203600) concerning the sum of almost orthogonal operators, and the Fefferman–Stein theory of the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) $H^1$ and the space $BMO$ of functions of bounded mean oscillation.

He wrote numerous books on harmonic analysis (see e.g. \[1,3,5\]), which are often cited as the standard references on the subject. His *[Princeton Lectures in Analysis](wikipedia://en/43844668)* series \[6,7,8,9\] were penned for his sequence of undergraduate courses on analysis at Princeton. Stein was also noted as having trained a high number of graduate students. According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), Stein had at least 52 graduate students—including the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) recipients [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831) and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498)—some of whom went on to shape modern Fourier analysis.

His honors included the [Steele Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Prize) (1984 and 2002), the [Schock Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schock_Prize) in Mathematics (1993), the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) (1999), and the [National Medal of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medal_of_Science) (2001). In addition, he had fellowships to [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), [Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation), [Guggenheim Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship), and [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences). Stein was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1982.[^o6] In 2005, Stein was awarded the [Stefan Bergman](wikipedia://en/9395687) prize in recognition of his contributions in real, complex, and harmonic analysis. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1959, he married Elly Intrator.[^o4] They had two children, Karen Stein and [Jeremy C. Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_C._Stein),[^o4] and grandchildren named Alison, Jason, and Carolyn. His son Jeremy is a professor of financial economics at Harvard, former adviser to [Tim Geithner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Geithner) and [Lawrence Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Summers), and served on the [Federal Reserve Board of Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Board_of_Governors) from 2012 to 2014. Elias Stein died of complications of [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma) in 2018, aged 87.[^o9]
