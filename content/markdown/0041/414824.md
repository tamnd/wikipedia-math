# Nonagon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nonagon"}

Regular polygon stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **nonagon** () or **enneagon** () is a [nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine)-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or 9-gon.

:::

The name *nonagon* is a [prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefix) [hybrid formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_word), from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) (*nonus*, "ninth" + *gonon*), used equivalently, attested already in the 16th century in French *nonogone* and in English from the 17th century. The name *enneagon* comes from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) *enneagonon* (εννεα, "nine" + γωνον (from γωνία = "corner")), and is arguably more correct, though less common.

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Regular Nonagon"}

## Regular nonagon {#Regular_nonagon}

A *[regular](wikipedia://en/333306) nonagon* is represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {9} and has [internal angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) of 140°. The area of a regular nonagon of side length *a* is given by

$$A = \frac{9}{4}a^2\cot\frac{\pi}{9}=(9/2)ar = 9r^2\tan(\pi/9)$$

$$= (9/2)R^2\sin(2\pi/9)\simeq6.18182\,a^2,$$

where the radius *r* of the [inscribed circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inscribed_circle) of the regular nonagon is

$$r=(a/2)\cot(\pi/9)$$

and where *R* is the radius of its [circumscribed circle](wikipedia://en/161262):

$$R = \sqrt{(a/2)^2 + r^2 }=r\sec(\pi/9)=(a/2)\csc(\pi/9).$$

## Construction {#Construction}

Although a regular nonagon is not [constructible](wikipedia://en/523430) with [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge) (as 9 = 3<sup>2</sup>, which is not a product of distinct [Fermat primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat_prime)), there are very old methods of construction that produce very close approximations.[^o7]

It can be also constructed using [neusis](wikipedia://en/3752339), or by allowing the use of an [angle trisector](wikipedia://en/91111).

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Regular enneagon symmetries.png.* Symmetries of a regular enneagon. Vertices are colored by their symmetry positions. Blue mirrors are drawn through vertices, and purple mirrors are drawn through edge. Gyration orders are given in the center.

The *regular enneagon* has [Dih<sub>9</sub> symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_symmetry), order 18. There are 2 subgroup dihedral symmetries: Dih<sub>3</sub> and Dih<sub>1</sub>, and 3 [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) symmetries: Z<sub>9</sub>, Z<sub>3</sub>, and Z<sub>1</sub>.

These 6 symmetries can be seen in 6 distinct symmetries on the enneagon. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these by a letter and group order.[^o11] Full symmetry of the regular form is **r18** and no symmetry is labeled **a1**. The dihedral symmetries are divided depending on whether they pass through vertices (**d** for diagonal) or edges (**p** for perpendiculars), and **i** when reflection lines path through both edges and vertices. Cyclic symmetries in the middle column are labeled as **g** for their central gyration orders.

Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g9** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

:::

## Tilings {#Tilings}

The regular enneagon can tessellate the euclidean tiling with gaps. These gaps can be filled with regular hexagons and isosceles triangles. In the notation of [symmetrohedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrohedron) this tiling is called H(\*;3;\*;\[2\]) with H representing \*632 hexagonal symmetry in the plane.

## Graphs {#Graphs}

The K<sub>9</sub> [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) is often drawn as a *regular enneagon* with all 36 edges connected. This graph also represents an [orthographic projection](wikipedia://en/203052) of the 9 vertices and 36 edges of the [8-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-simplex).

## Architecture {#Architecture}

Temples of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith), called [Baháʼí Houses of Worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_House_of_Worship), are required to be nonagonal.

The [U.S. Steel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel_Tower) is an irregular nonagon.

The *[Sound Catcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Catcher)*, a wooden structure in a Lithuanian forest, is also nine-sided.

[Palmanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmanova) in Italy is in the shape of a nonagon.

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

- [They Might Be Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Might_Be_Giants) have a song titled "Nonagon" on their [children's album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_album) *[Here Come the 123s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Here_Come_the_123s)*. It refers to both an attendee at a party at which "everybody at the party is a many-sided polygon", as well as a dance that the polygons perform at this party.[^o18]
- [Slipknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slipknot_%28band%29)'s logo is also a version of a nonagon, being a nine-pointed star made of three triangles, referring to the nine members.
- [King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Gizzard_&_the_Lizard_Wizard) have an album titled *[Nonagon Infinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonagon_Infinity)*, the album art featuring a nonagonal complete graph. The album consists of nine songs and repeats cyclically.
