# Mach wave

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mach wave"}

*Image: Schlierenfoto Mach 1-2 Pfeilflügel - NASA.jpg.* [Schlieren photograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieren_photography) of an attached shock on a sharp-nosed supersonic body. The Mach angle is acute, showing that the body exceeds Mach 1. The angle of the Mach wave (~59 degrees) indicates a velocity of about Mach 1.17.

In [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), a **Mach wave**, also known as a **weak discontinuity**,[^o2][^o3] is a pressure wave traveling with the [speed of sound](wikipedia://en/147853) caused by a slight change of [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) added to a [compressible flow](wikipedia://en/219144). These weak waves can combine in supersonic flow to become a [shock wave](wikipedia://en/146253) if sufficient Mach waves are present at any location. Such a shock wave is called a **Mach stem** or **Mach front**. Thus, it is possible to have shockless compression or expansion in a supersonic flow by having the production of Mach waves sufficiently spaced (*cf.* [isentropic](wikipedia://en/375900) compression in supersonic flows). A Mach wave is the weak limit of an [oblique shock](wikipedia://en/2786041) wave where time averages of flow quantities don't change (a normal shock is the other limit). If the size of the object moving at the speed of sound is near 0, then this domain of influence of the wave is called a **Mach cone**.[^o7][^o4]

:::

## Mach angle {#Mach_angle}

*Image: Sonic boom.svg.* A sonic boom produced by an aircraft moving at M=2.92, calculated from the cone angle of 20 degrees. Observers hear nothing until the shock wave, on the edges of the cone, crosses their location.

A Mach wave propagates across the flow at the **Mach angle** *μ*, which is the angle formed between the Mach wave [wavefront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavefront) and a vector that points opposite to the vector of motion.[^o7][^o8] It is given by

$$\mu = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{M}\right),$$

where *M* is the [Mach number](wikipedia://en/20051).

Mach waves can be used in [schlieren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieren_photography) or shadowgraph observations to determine the local Mach number of the flow. Early observations by [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach) used grooves in the wall of a duct to produce Mach waves in a duct, which were then photographed by the schlieren method, to obtain data about the flow in nozzles and ducts. Mach angles may also occasionally be visualized out of their condensation in air, for example [vapor cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor_cone) around aircraft during [transonic](wikipedia://en/247737) flight.
