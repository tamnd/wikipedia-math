# Algorism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algorism"}

*Image: Houghton Typ 520.03.736 - Margarita philosophica.jpg.* *Calculating-Table* by [Gregor Reisch](wikipedia://en/8809962): *Margarita Philosophica*, 1503. The woodcut shows *Arithmetica* instructing an algorist and an abacist (inaccurately represented as [Boethius](wikipedia://en/195684) and [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275)). There was keen competition between the two from the introduction of the *[Algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing)* into Europe in the 12th century until its triumph in the 16th.[^o2]

**Algorism** is the technique of performing basic [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) by writing numbers in [place value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_value) form and applying a set of memorized rules and [facts](wikipedia://en/239112) to the digits. One who practices algorism is known as an **algorist**. This [positional notation](wikipedia://en/437052) system has largely superseded earlier calculation systems that used a different set of symbols for each numerical [magnitude](wikipedia://en/577301), such as [Roman numerals](wikipedia://en/25657), and in some cases required a device such as an [abacus](wikipedia://en/655).

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

The word *algorism* comes from the name [Al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khwārizmī) (c. 780–850), a [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians)[^o4][^o5] [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [geographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographer) and [scholar](wikipedia://en/772062) in the [House of Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wisdom) in [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad), whose name means "the native of [Khwarezm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarezm)", which is now in modern-day [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan).[^o16][^o14][^o15] He wrote a treatise in Arabic language in the 9th century, which was translated into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) in the 12th century under the title *Algoritmi de numero Indorum*. This title means "Algoritmi on the numbers of the Indians", where "Algoritmi" was the translator's Latinization of Al-Khwarizmi's name.[^o6] Al-Khwarizmi was the most widely read mathematician in Europe in the late Middle Ages, primarily through his other book, the [Algebra](wikipedia://en/1406909).[^o7] In late medieval Latin, *algorismus*, the corruption of his name, simply meant the "decimal number system" that is still the meaning of modern English algorism. During the 17th century, the French form for the word – but not its meaning – was changed to *[algorithm](wikipedia://en/775)*, following the model of the word *[logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860)*, this form alluding to the ancient Greek *arithmos* = number. English adopted the French very soon afterwards, but it wasn't until the late 19th century that "algorithm" took on the meaning that it has in modern English.[^o8] In English, it was first used about 1230 and then by Chaucer in 1391.[^o9] Another early use of the word is from 1240, in a manual titled *Carmen de Algorismo* composed by [Alexandre de Villedieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_de_Villedieu). It begins thus: *Haec algorismus ars praesens dicitur, in qua / Talibus Indorum fruimur bis quinque figuris.* which translates as: This present art, in which we use those twice five Indian figures, is called algorismus.

The word *[algorithm](wikipedia://en/775)* also derives from *algorism*, a generalization of the meaning to any set of rules specifying a computational procedure. Occasionally *algorism* is also used in this generalized meaning, especially in older texts.

## History {#History}

Starting with the [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118) developed in India using [base 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_10) notation, [Al-Khwārizmī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khwārizmī) along with other [mathematicians in medieval Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_in_medieval_Islam), documented new arithmetic methods and made many other contributions to decimal arithmetic (see the articles linked below). These included the concept of the decimal fractions as an extension of the notation, which in turn led to the notion of the [decimal point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_point). This system was popularized in Europe by Leonardo of Pisa, now known as [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949).[^o11]
