# Exploratory data analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exploratory data analysis"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **exploratory data analysis** (EDA) or **exploratory analytics** is an approach of [analyzing](wikipedia://en/2720954) [data sets](wikipedia://en/8495) to summarize their main characteristics, often using [statistical graphics](wikipedia://en/15934463) and other [data visualization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_visualization) methods. A [statistical model](wikipedia://en/27576) can be used or not, but primarily EDA is for seeing what the data can tell beyond the formal modeling and thereby contrasts with traditional hypothesis testing, in which a model is supposed to be selected before the data is seen and tested. Exploratory data analysis has been promoted by [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974) since 1970 to encourage statisticians to explore the data, and possibly formulate hypotheses that could lead to new data collection and experiments. EDA is different from [initial data analysis (IDA)](wikipedia://en/2720954#Initial_data_analysis),[^o2][^o3] which focuses more narrowly on checking assumptions required for model fitting and hypothesis testing, and handling missing values and making transformations of variables as needed. EDA encompasses IDA.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Tukey defined data analysis in 1961 as: "Procedures for analyzing data, techniques for interpreting the results of such procedures, ways of planning the gathering of data to make its analysis easier, more precise or more accurate, and all the machinery and results of (mathematical) statistics which apply to analyzing data."[^o5]

Exploratory data analysis is a technique to analyze and investigate a dataset and summarize its main characteristics. A main advantage of EDA is providing the visualization of data after conducting analysis.

Tukey's championing of EDA encouraged the development of [statistical computing](wikipedia://en/15832717) packages, especially [S](wikipedia://en/919313) at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs).[^o6] The S programming language inspired the systems [S-PLUS](wikipedia://en/3830007) and [R](wikipedia://en/376707). This family of statistical-computing environments featured vastly improved dynamic visualization capabilities, which allowed statisticians to identify [outliers](wikipedia://en/160951), [trends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trend_estimation) and [patterns](wikipedia://en/126706) in data that merited further study.

Tukey's EDA was related to two other developments in [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579): [robust statistics](wikipedia://en/2885691) and [nonparametric statistics](wikipedia://en/223369), both of which tried to reduce the sensitivity of statistical inferences to errors in formulating [statistical models](wikipedia://en/27576). Tukey promoted the use of [five number summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_number_summary) of numerical data—the two [extremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_value) ([maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) and [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum)), the [median](wikipedia://en/18837), and the [quartiles](wikipedia://en/25170)—because these median and quartiles, being functions of the [empirical distribution](wikipedia://en/2690455) are defined for all distributions, unlike the [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_value) and [standard deviation](wikipedia://en/27590). Moreover, the quartiles and median are more robust to [skewed](wikipedia://en/28212) or [heavy-tailed distributions](wikipedia://en/8092200) than traditional summaries (the mean and standard deviation). The packages [S](wikipedia://en/919313), [S-PLUS](wikipedia://en/3830007), and [R](wikipedia://en/376707) included routines using [resampling statistics](wikipedia://en/3763850), such as Quenouille and Tukey's [jackknife](wikipedia://en/3763850#Jackknife) and [Efron](wikipedia://en/7250868) [bootstrap](wikipedia://en/6885770), which are nonparametric and robust (for many problems).

Exploratory data analysis, robust statistics, nonparametric statistics, and the development of statistical programming languages facilitated statisticians' work on scientific and engineering problems. Such problems included the fabrication of semiconductors and the understanding of communications networks, both of which were of interest to Bell Labs. These statistical developments, all championed by Tukey, were designed to complement the [analytic](wikipedia://en/61478) theory of [testing statistical hypotheses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing), particularly the [Laplacian](wikipedia://en/344783) tradition's emphasis on [exponential families](wikipedia://en/339174).[^o7]

Additionally, there are arguments to first visualize data during EDA before modeling in order to avoid misleading conclusions as in [Anscombe's Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anscombe's_Quartet).

## Development {#Development}

*Image: Data visualization process v1.png.* Data science process flowchart

[John W. Tukey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Tukey) wrote the book *Exploratory Data Analysis* in 1977.[^o9] Tukey held that too much emphasis in statistics was placed on [statistical hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_hypothesis_testing) (confirmatory data analysis); more emphasis needed to be placed on using [data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data) to suggest hypotheses to test. In particular, he held that confusing the two types of analyses and employing them on the same set of data can lead to [systematic bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_error) owing to the issues inherent in [testing hypotheses suggested by the data](wikipedia://en/416831).

The objectives of EDA are to:

- Enable unexpected discoveries in the data
- Suggest hypotheses about the [causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) of observed [phenomena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenomenon)
- Assess assumptions on which [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) will be based
- Support the selection of appropriate statistical tools and techniques
- Provide a basis for further data collection through [surveys](wikipedia://en/27578) or [experiments](wikipedia://en/9541)[^o10]

Many EDA techniques have been adopted into [data mining](wikipedia://en/42253). They are also being taught to young students as a way to introduce them to statistical thinking.[^o11]

There are a number of tools that are useful for EDA, but EDA is characterized more by the attitude taken than by particular techniques.[^o13]

Typical [graphical techniques](wikipedia://en/15934463) used in EDA are:

- [Multi-vari chart](wikipedia://en/17702675)

## History {#History}

Many EDA ideas can be traced back to earlier authors, for example:

- [Francis Galton](wikipedia://en/49072) emphasized [order statistics](wikipedia://en/160986) and [quantiles](wikipedia://en/25184).
- [Arthur Lyon Bowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Lyon_Bowley) used precursors of the stemplot and [five-number summary](wikipedia://en/160963) (Bowley actually used a "[seven-figure summary](wikipedia://en/18083379)", including the extremes, [deciles](wikipedia://en/1577298) and [quartiles](wikipedia://en/25170), along with the median—see his *Elementary Manual of Statistics* (3rd edn., 1920), p. 62[^o16]– he defines "the maximum and minimum, median, quartiles and two deciles" as the "seven positions").
- [Andrew Ehrenberg](wikipedia://en/7177077) articulated a philosophy of [data reduction](wikipedia://en/23789529) (see his book of the same name).

The [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) course *Statistics in Society* (MDST 242), took the above ideas and merged them with [Gottfried Noether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Noether)'s work, which introduced [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577) via coin-tossing and the [median test](wikipedia://en/236641).

## Example {#Example}

Findings from EDA are orthogonal to the primary analysis task. To illustrate, consider an example from Cook et al. where the analysis task is to find the variables which best predict the tip that a dining party will give to the waiter.[^o19] The variables available in the data collected for this task are: the tip amount, total bill, payer gender, smoking/non-smoking section, time of day, day of the week, and size of the party. The primary analysis task is approached by fitting a regression model where the tip rate is the response variable. The fitted model is

([tip rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tip_rate)) = 0.18 - 0.01 × (party size)

which says that as the size of the dining party increases by one person (leading to a higher bill), the tip rate will decrease by 1%, on average.

However, exploring the data reveals other interesting features not described by this model.

What is learned from the plots is different from what is illustrated by the regression model, even though the experiment was not designed to investigate any of these other trends. The patterns found by exploring the data suggest hypotheses about tipping that may not have been anticipated in advance, and which could lead to interesting follow-up experiments where the hypotheses are formally stated and tested by collecting new data.

## Software {#Software}

- [JMP](wikipedia://en/1979375), an EDA package from [SAS Institute](wikipedia://en/194872).
- [KNIME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNIME), Konstanz Information Miner – Open-Source data exploration platform based on Eclipse.
- [Minitab](wikipedia://en/1773375), an EDA and general statistics package widely used in industrial and corporate settings.
- [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_%28software%29), an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) [data mining](wikipedia://en/42253) and [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488) software suite.
- [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29), an open-source programming language widely used in data mining and machine learning.
- Matplotlib & Seaborn are the Python libraries used in today's world for EDA and Plotting/Data Visualization. (point updated: 2025)
- [R](wikipedia://en/376707), an open-source programming language for statistical computing and graphics. Together with Python one of the most popular languages for data science.
- [TinkerPlots](wikipedia://en/16939285) an EDA software for upper elementary and middle school students.
- [Weka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weka_%28machine_learning%29) an open source data mining package that includes visualization and EDA tools such as [targeted projection pursuit](wikipedia://en/32924592).
