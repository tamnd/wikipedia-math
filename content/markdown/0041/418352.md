# Vector operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector operator"}

A **vector operator** is a [differential operator](wikipedia://en/263886) used in [vector calculus](wikipedia://en/32640).[^o2] Vector operators include:

:::

- [Gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) is a vector operator that operates on a [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879), producing a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641).
- [Divergence](wikipedia://en/8328) is a vector operator that operates on a vector field, producing a [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879).
- [Curl](wikipedia://en/6123) is a vector operator that operates on a vector field, producing a vector field.

Defined in terms of [del](wikipedia://en/151925):

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{grad} &\equiv \nabla \\ \operatorname{div} &\equiv \nabla \cdot \\ \operatorname{curl} &\equiv \nabla \times \end{aligned}$$

The [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian) operates on a scalar field, producing a scalar field:

$$\nabla^2 \equiv \operatorname{div}\ \operatorname{grad} \equiv \nabla \cdot \nabla$$

Vector operators must always come right before the [scalar field](wikipedia://en/161879) or [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on which they operate, in order to produce a result. E.g.

$$\nabla f$$

yields the gradient of *f*, but

$$f \nabla$$

is just another vector operator, which is not operating on anything.

A vector operator can operate on another vector operator, to produce a compound vector operator, as seen above in the case of the Laplacian.
