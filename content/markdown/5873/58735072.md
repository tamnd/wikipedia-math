# Real radical

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real radical"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **real radical** of an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* in a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) with [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [coefficients](wikipedia://en/51203) is the largest ideal containing *I* with the same (real) vanishing locus. It plays a similar role in [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198) that the [radical of an ideal](wikipedia://en/252009) plays in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018). More specifically, [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215) says that when *I* is an ideal in a polynomial ring with coefficients coming from an algebraically closed field, the radical of *I* is the set of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) vanishing on the vanishing locus of *I*. In real algebraic geometry, the Nullstellensatz fails as the real numbers are not algebraically closed. However, one can recover a similar theorem, the *real Nullstellensatz*, by using the real radical in place of the (ordinary) radical.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **real radical** of an ideal *I* in a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $\mathbb{R}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ over the real numbers, denoted by $\sqrt[\mathbb{R}]{I}$, is defined as

$$\sqrt[\mathbb{R}]{I} = \Big\{ f \in \mathbb{R}[x_1,\dots,x_n] \left|\, -f^{2m} = \textstyle{\sum_i} h_i^2 + g \right.\text{ where }\ m \in \mathbb{Z}_+,\, h_i \in \mathbb{R}[x_1,\dots,x_n], \,\text{and } g \in I\Big\}.$$

The [Positivstellensatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positivstellensatz) then implies that $\sqrt[\mathbb{R}]{I}$ is the set of all polynomials that vanish on the real variety[^o4] defined by the vanishing of $I$.

:::
