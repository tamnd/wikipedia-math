# Alexander Kiselev (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Kiselev"}

**Alexander A. Kiselev** (born 1969) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), and [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988).[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Alexander Kiselev received his bachelor's degree in 1992 from [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) and his PhD in 1997 from [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) under supervision of [Barry Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Simon). In 1997-1998 he was a postdoctoral fellow at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute), where he co-authored a paper on [Christ–Kiselev maximal inequality](wikipedia://en/42730692). Between 1998 and 2002 he was an E. Dickson Instructor and then assistant professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) where he worked with [Peter Constantin](wikipedia://en/82683896) on reaction-diffusion equations and fluid mechanics. In 2001, Kiselev solved one of the [Simon problems](wikipedia://en/57577573), on existence of imbedded singular continuous spectrum of the Schrödinger operator with slowly decaying potential.[^o5][^o6] [^o7][^o8] He taught at the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin-Madison) from 2002 to 2013, as an associate and full professor. He was a member of the [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) faculty between 2013 and 2017. Since 2018, Kiselev is a William T. Laprade Professor of Mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University). His research has been profiled by Science Watch,[^o9] [Institute for Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/10324088),[^o10] Duke Today [^o11] and Quanta Magazine [^o12]
