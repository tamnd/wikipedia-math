# Shmuel Friedland

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Shmuel Friedland"}

**Shmuel Friedland** (שמואל פרידלנד; born 1944 in [Tashkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent), [Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_Soviet_Socialist_Republic))[^o3] is an Israeli-American mathematician.

:::

Friedland studied at the [Technion – Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology), graduating in 1967 with [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) and in 1971 with doctorate of science under the supervision of Binjamin Schwarz. As a postdoc Friedland was in 1972/73 at the [Weizmann Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute), in 1973/74 at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and in 1974/75 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). Then he taught at the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), where he became in 1982 a full professor. In 1985 he became a professor at the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago).[^o5]

Besides linear algebra (matrix theory), Friedland does research on a wide variety of mathematics, including [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518) and applied mathematics. With Elizabeth Gross, he proved a set-theoretic version of the *salmon conjecture* posed by [Elizabeth S. Allman](wikipedia://en/44749903).[^o6]

With [Miroslav Fiedler](wikipedia://en/5539109) and [Israel Gohberg](wikipedia://en/19685973), Friedland shared in the first [Hans Schneider Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Schneider_Prize), awarded by the [International Linear Algebra Society](wikipedia://en/63337149) in 1993. He was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (Class of 2019). Also, he was selected as a 2021 [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow), "for deep and varied contributions to mathematics, especially linear algebra, matrix theory, and matrix computations".[^o7]
