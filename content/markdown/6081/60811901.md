# Laurent Cesalli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laurent Cesalli"}

**Laurent Cesalli** (born 1968) is a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) historian of philosophy who focuses on [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), philosophy of mind and metaphysics.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Laurent Cesalli was born in [Vevey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vevey) in 1968. After graduating from high school in 1987, he studied at the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg) and graduated in 1993 under the supervision of Ruedi Imbach. Upon graduation he focused on non-academic activities: in 1995–1996, he cycled from Geneva to Beijing and, in 1997, he became a mountain guide. In 1998, he began his Ph.D. in [medieval philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_philosophy) at the [University of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Geneva) under the supervision of Alain de Libera, completing it in 2003. From 2004 to 2008 he worked as *Wissenschaftlicher Assistent* at the [Albert-Ludwigs-Universität](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) in Freiburg i. Br. From 2008 to 2014 he has been teaching at the universities of [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne), Freiburg, Geneva, Fribourg, and [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University). From 2011 to 2014 he was a researcher at the [Centre national de la recherche scientifique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_national_de_la_recherche_scientifique) in Lille (UMR 8163, Savoirs, textes, langage). Since 2014 he is full professor of medieval philosophy at the University of Geneva. Since 2018 he is editor-in-chief of the Grundriss der Geschichte der Philosophie|de (Ueberweg), together with Gerald Hartung (Wuppertal).

## Areas of specialization {#Areas_of_specialization}

His research focuses on philosophy of language, philosophy of mind and metaphysics – both in the Middle Ages and within the so-called “Austro-German” tradition ([Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano) and his pupils).
