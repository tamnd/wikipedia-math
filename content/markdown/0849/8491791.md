# Minkowski's bound

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Minkowski's bound"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), **Minkowski's bound** gives an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) of the norm of ideals to be checked in order to determine the [class number](wikipedia://en/150907) of a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *K*. It is named for the mathematician [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *D* be the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of the field, *n* be the degree of *K* over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $2 r_2 = n - r_1$ be the number of [complex embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_embedding) where $r_1$ is the number of [real embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_embedding). Then every class in the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) of *K* contains an [integral ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_ideal) of [norm](wikipedia://en/460672) not exceeding Minkowski's bound

$$M_K = \sqrt{|D|} \left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^{r_2} \frac{n!}{n^n} \ .$$

**Minkowski's constant** for the field *K* is this bound *M*<sub>*K*</sub>.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Since the number of integral ideals of given norm is finite, the finiteness of the class number is an immediate consequence,[^o4] and further, the [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907) is generated by the [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of norm at most *M*<sub>*K*</sub>.

Minkowski's bound may be used to derive a lower bound for the discriminant of a field *K* given *n*, *r*<sub>1</sub> and *r*<sub>2</sub>. Since an integral ideal has norm at least one, we have 1 ≤ *M*<sub>*K*</sub>, so that

$$\sqrt{|D|} \ge \left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^{r_2} \frac{n^n}{n!} \ge \left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^{n/2} \frac{n^n}{n!} \ .$$

For *n* at least 2, it is easy to show that the lower bound is greater than 1, so we obtain **Minkowski's Theorem**, that the discriminant of every number field, other than **Q**, is non-trivial. This implies that the field of rational numbers has no [unramified extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified_extension).

:::

::: {#o8 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

The result is a consequence of [Minkowski's theorem](wikipedia://en/53455).

:::
