# Phillips–Perron test

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Phillips–Perron test"}

Academics In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Phillips–Perron test** (named after [Peter C. B. Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_C._B._Phillips) and [Pierre Perron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Perron)) is a [unit root](wikipedia://en/2398969) test.[^o2] That is, it is used in [time series](wikipedia://en/406624) analysis to test the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) that a time series is [integrated of order](wikipedia://en/10088188) 1. It builds on the [Dickey–Fuller test](wikipedia://en/2473516) of the null hypothesis $\rho = 1$ in $\Delta y_{t}= (\rho -1)y_{t-1}+u_{t}\,$, where $\Delta$ is the [first difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_difference) [operator](wikipedia://en/22213). Like the [augmented Dickey–Fuller test](wikipedia://en/3767048), the Phillips–Perron test addresses the issue that the process generating data for $y_{t}$ might have a higher order of autocorrelation than is admitted in the test equation—making $y_{t-1}$ endogenous and thus invalidating the Dickey–Fuller [t-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-test). Whilst the augmented Dickey–Fuller test addresses this issue by introducing lags of $\Delta y_{t}$ as regressors in the test equation, the Phillips–Perron test makes a [non-parametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics) correction to the t-test statistic. The test is robust with respect to unspecified [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) and [heteroscedasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heteroscedasticity) in the disturbance process of the test equation.

:::

Davidson and MacKinnon (2004) report that the Phillips–Perron test performs worse in finite samples than the augmented Dickey–Fuller test.[^o3]
