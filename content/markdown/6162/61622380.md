# Sommer Gentry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sommer Gentry"}

**Sommer Elizabeth Gentry** is an American mathematician who works as a professor of mathematics at the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) and as a research associate in surgery at the [Johns Hopkins School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_School_of_Medicine). Her research concerns [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476) and its applications to the optimization of [organ transplants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplant), and has led to the discovery of geographic inequities in organ allocation. She is also interested in dancing, teaches [swing dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_%28dance%29) at the Naval Academy, and wrote her doctoral dissertation on the mathematics and robotics of dance.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Gentry is originally from California. As a girl, she was inspired to continue in mathematics by the [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446) columns of [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) and [Ivars Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivars_Peterson). In 1993, as a senior at [Thousand Oaks High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Oaks_High_School), Gentry had the highest individual score at the [Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California) county-level [Academic Decathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Academic_Decathlon). She graduated from [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1998, with both a bachelor's degree in mathematical and computational sciences and a master's degree in engineering-economic systems and operations research.

She completed her Ph.D. in electrical engineering and computer science at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 2005. Her dissertation, *Dancing cheek to cheek: haptic communication between partner dancers and swing as a finite state machine*, was supervised by Eric Feron. In her doctoral research, she modeled the language and notation of dance mathematically using [finite-state machines](wikipedia://en/10931), programmed a robot to dance, and used her model to improve [haptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haptic_technology) communications between humans and robots, with the goal of eventually producing human-machine surgical collaborations that could be more effective than human or robotic surgeons working alone.

## Other activities {#Other_activities}

Gentry is married to Israeli surgeon [Dorry Segev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorry_Segev). She met him at a [Lindy Hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Hop) dance competition in 1999, and a few years later they won the British Championship in Lindy Hop. She has also worked with Segev on the [Kidney Paired Donation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_Paired_Donation) program.

In 2017, Gentry was a competitor on the [Fox Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_programs_broadcast_by_Fox) game show *Superhuman*.

She has also been a vocal critic of the airport passenger screening procedures of the US [Transportation Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_Security_Administration), which she characterizes as [sexual assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_assault).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Gentry won the [Henry L. Alder Award](wikipedia://en/65434512) for Distinguished Teaching by a Beginning Faculty Member of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2009. She won the [Navy Meritorious Civilian Service Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Meritorious_Civilian_Service_Award) in 2014, and in the same year was a finalist for the [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS) Daniel H. Wagner Prize for Excellence in Operations Research Practice. She will deliver a [Mathematical Association of America (MAA)](wikipedia://en/198772) invited lecture at [MathFest](wikipedia://en/14984993) 2021.[^o5]
