# Bimatrix game

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bimatrix game"}

In [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), a **bimatrix game** is a [simultaneous game](wikipedia://en/23992863) for two players in which each player has a finite number of possible actions. The name comes from the fact that the [normal form](wikipedia://en/1863612) of such a game can be described by two [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) - matrix $A$ describing the payoffs of player 1 and matrix $B$ describing the payoffs of player 2.[^o2]

:::

Player 1 is often called the "row player" and player 2 the "column player". If player 1 has $m$ possible actions and player 2 has $n$ possible actions, then each of the two matrices has $m$ rows by $n$ columns. When the row player selects the $i$-th action and the column player selects the $j$-th action, the payoff to the row player is $A[i,j]$ and the payoff to the column player is $B[i,j]$.

The players can also play [mixed strategies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_strategies). A mixed strategy for the row player is a non-negative vector $x$ of length $m$ such that: $\sum_{i=1}^m x_i = 1$. Similarly, a mixed strategy for the column player is a non-negative vector $y$ of length $n$ such that: $\sum_{j=1}^n y_j = 1$. When the players play mixed strategies with vectors $x$ and $y$, the expected payoff of the row player is: $x^\mathsf{T} A y$ and of the column player: $x^\mathsf{T} B y$.

## Nash equilibrium in bimatrix games {#Nash_equilibrium_in_bimatrix_games}

Every bimatrix game has a [Nash equilibrium](wikipedia://en/45337) in (possibly) mixed strategies. Finding such a Nash equilibrium is a special case of the [Linear complementarity problem](wikipedia://en/1470767) and can be done in finite time by the [Lemke–Howson algorithm](wikipedia://en/38282904).[^o2]

There is a [reduction](wikipedia://en/848067) from the problem of finding a Nash equilibrium in a bimatrix game to the problem of finding a competitive equilibrium in an economy with [Leontief utilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leontief_utilities).[^o4]

## Related terms {#Related_terms}

A [zero-sum game](wikipedia://en/34417) is a special case of a bimatrix game in which $A+B = 0$.
