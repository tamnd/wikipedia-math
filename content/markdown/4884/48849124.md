# Florence Lewis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Florence Lewis"}

**Florence Parthenia Lewis** (September 24, 1877 – March 10, 1964[^o2]) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in [Fort Scott, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Scott,_Kansas), Lewis attended the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin) for her [undergraduate degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate_degree), which she received in 1897, and [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College) for a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree), which she received in 1906. She earned her [Ph.D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy). at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o3][^o5]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Lewis was a professor at [Goucher College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goucher_College) for her entire teaching career, from 1908 when she was appointed as an instructor to 1938 when she retired as an emeritus professor. She was known for her studies of [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) interpretation of [algebraic invariants](wikipedia://en/363762).[^o3]

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

A longtime member of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), she served on its council from 1919 to 1922. She was also a member of the [American Astronomical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Astronomical_Society) and the [Astronomical Society of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Society_of_the_Pacific).[^o3]
