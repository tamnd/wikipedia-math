# Statistical field theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Statistical field theory"}

In [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), **statistical field theory** (**SFT**) is a theoretical framework that describes systems with many degrees of freedom, particularly near [phase transitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_transitions).[^o2] It does not denote a single theory but encompasses many models, including for [magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetism), [superconductivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superconductivity), [superfluidity](wikipedia://en/35607283),[^o3] [topological phase transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_phase_transition), [wetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetting)[^o4][^o5] as well as non-equilibrium phase transitions.[^o6] A SFT is any model in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) where the [degrees of freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_%28physics_and_chemistry%29) comprise a [field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) or fields. In other words, the [microstates](wikipedia://en/3066350) of the system are expressed through field configurations. It is closely related to [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), which describes the [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202) of fields, and shares with it many techniques, such as the [path integral formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) and [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453). If the system involves polymers, it is also known as [polymer field theory](wikipedia://en/19668009).

:::

In fact, by performing a [Wick rotation](wikipedia://en/293629) from [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) to [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), many results of statistical field theory can be applied directly to its quantum equivalent. The [correlation functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation_function_%28quantum_field_theory%29) of a statistical field theory are called [Schwinger functions](wikipedia://en/1577800), and their properties are described by the [Osterwalder–Schrader axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osterwalder–Schrader_axioms).

Statistical field theories are widely used to describe systems in [polymer physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer_physics) or [biophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biophysics), such as [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer) films, nanostructured block [copolymers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copolymers)[^o7] or [polyelectrolytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyelectrolyte).[^o8]
