# Enneper surface

*Image: EnneperSurfaceAnimated.gif.* A portion of the Enneper surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Enneper surface"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Enneper surface** is a self-intersecting surface that can be described [parametrically](wikipedia://en/25065) by:

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \tfrac{1}{3} u \left(1 - \tfrac{1}{3}u^2 + v^2\right), \\ y &= \tfrac{1}{3} v \left(1 - \tfrac{1}{3}v^2 + u^2\right), \\ z & = \tfrac{1}{3} \left(u^2 - v^2\right). \end{aligned}$$

:::

It was introduced by [Alfred Enneper](wikipedia://en/2996246) in 1864 in connection with [minimal surface](wikipedia://en/276734) theory.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

The [Weierstrass–Enneper parameterization](wikipedia://en/2996193) is very simple, $f(z)=1, g(z)=z$, and the real parametric form can easily be calculated from it. The surface is [conjugate](wikipedia://en/37016612) to itself.

Implicitization methods of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) can be used to find out that the points in the Enneper surface given above satisfy the degree-9 [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) equation

$$\begin{aligned} & 64 z^9 - 128 z^7 + 64 z^5 - 702 x^2 y^2 z^3 - 18 x^2 y^2 z + 144 (y^2 z^6 - x^2 z^6)\\ & {} + 162 (y^4 z^2 - x^4 z^2) + 27 (y^6 - x^6) + 9 (x^4 z + y^4 z) + 48 (x^2 z^3 + y^2 z^3)\\ & {} - 432 (x^2 z^5 + y^2 z^5) + 81 (x^4 y^2 - x^2 y^4) + 240 (y^2 z^4 - x^2 z^4) - 135 (x^4 z^3 + y^4 z^3) = 0. \end{aligned}$$

Dually, the [tangent plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_plane) at the point with given parameters is $a + b x + c y + d z = 0,\$ where

$$\begin{aligned} a &= -\left(u^2 - v^2\right) \left(1 + \tfrac{1}{3}u^2 + \tfrac{1}{3}v^2\right), \\ b &= 6 u, \\ c &= 6 v, \\ d &= -3\left(1 - u^2 - v^2\right). \end{aligned}$$

Its coefficients satisfy the implicit degree-6 polynomial equation

$$\begin{aligned} &162 a^2 b^2 c^2 + 6 b^2 c^2 d^2 - 4 (b^6 + c^6) + 54 (a b^4 d - a c^4 d) + 81 (a^2 b^4 + a^2 c^4)\\ &{} + 4 (b^4 c^2 + b^2 c^4) - 3 (b^4 d^2 + c^4 d^2) + 36 (a b^2 d^3 - a c^2 d^3) = 0. \end{aligned}$$

The [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351), [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) and [mean curvature](wikipedia://en/684210) are

$$\begin{aligned} J &= \frac{1}{81}(1 + u^2 + v^2)^4, \\ K &= -\frac{4}{9}\frac{1}{J}, \\ H &= 0. \end{aligned}$$

The [total curvature](wikipedia://en/19172363) is $-4\pi$. [Osserman](wikipedia://en/19663336) proved that a complete minimal surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ with total curvature $-4\pi$ is either the [catenoid](wikipedia://en/273329) or the Enneper surface.[^o6]

The curves obtained by setting $u=0$ or $v=0$ are [geodesics](wikipedia://en/91096) of the Enneper surface, and are copies of the [Tschirnhausen cubic](wikipedia://en/10475041).[^o7]

Another property is that all bicubical minimal [Bézier surfaces](wikipedia://en/285274) are, up to an [affine transformation](wikipedia://en/38449), pieces of the surface.[^o8]

It can be generalized to higher order rotational symmetries by using the Weierstrass–Enneper parameterization $f(z) = 1, g(z) = z^k$ for integer k>1.[^o4] It can also be generalized to higher dimensions; Enneper-like surfaces are known to exist in $\mathbb{R}^n$ for n up to 7.[^o9]

See also [^o10] [^o11] for higher order algebraic Enneper surfaces.
