# Sphere theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sphere theorem"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), the **sphere theorem**, also known as the **quarter-pinched sphere theorem**, strongly restricts the topology of manifolds admitting metrics with a particular curvature bound. The precise statement of the theorem is as follows. If $M$ is a [complete](wikipedia://en/6875557), [simply-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply-connected), *n*-dimensional [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) taking values in the interval $(1,4]$ then $M$ is [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [*n*-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). (To be precise, we mean the sectional curvature of every tangent 2-plane at each point must lie in $(1,4]$.) Another way of stating the result is that if $M$ is not homeomorphic to the sphere, then it is impossible to put a metric on $M$ with quarter-pinched curvature.

:::

Note that the conclusion is false if the sectional curvatures are allowed to take values in the *closed* interval $[1,4]$. The standard counterexample is [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) with the [Fubini–Study metric](wikipedia://en/2574486); sectional curvatures of this metric take on values between $1$ and $4$, with endpoints included. Other counterexamples may be found among the rank one [symmetric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_symmetric_space).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Differentiable sphere theorem"}

## Differentiable sphere theorem {#Differentiable_sphere_theorem}

The original proof of the sphere theorem did not conclude that $M$ was necessarily [diffeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffeomorphic) to the *n*-sphere. This complication is because spheres in higher dimensions admit [smooth structures](wikipedia://en/1542238) that are not diffeomorphic. (For more information, see the article on [exotic spheres](wikipedia://en/1114931).) However, in 2007 [Simon Brendle](wikipedia://en/36493319) and [Richard Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Schoen) utilized [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291) to prove that with the above hypotheses, $M$ is necessarily diffeomorphic to the *n*-sphere with its standard smooth structure. Moreover, the proof of Brendle and Schoen only uses the weaker assumption of pointwise rather than global pinching. This result is known as the **differentiable sphere theorem**.

:::

## History of the sphere theorem {#History_of_the_sphere_theorem}

[Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) conjectured that a simply connected manifold with pinched sectional curvature is a sphere.[^o5] In 1951, [Harry Rauch](wikipedia://en/37090856) showed that a simply connected manifold with curvature in $[3/4,1]$ is homeomorphic to a sphere.[^o6] In 1960, both [Marcel Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) and [Wilhelm Klingenberg](wikipedia://en/33387331) proved the topological version of the sphere theorem with the optimal pinching constant.[^o7][^o8] [Berger](wikipedia://en/12149363) discusses the history of the theorem in his book *A Panoramic View of Riemannian Geometry*, originally published in 2003.[^o9]
