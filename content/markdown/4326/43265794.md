# Choquet game

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Choquet game"}

The **Choquet game** is a [topological game](wikipedia://en/15334997) named after [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193), who was in 1969 the first to investigate such games.[^o2] A closely related game is known as the **strong Choquet game**.

:::

Let $X$ be a non-empty [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). The Choquet game of $X$, $G(X)$, is defined as follows: Player I chooses $U_0$, a non-empty [open subset](wikipedia://en/39358) of $X$, then Player II chooses $V_0$, a non-empty open subset of $U_0$, then Player I chooses $U_1$, a non-empty open subset of $V_0$, etc. The players continue this process, constructing a sequence $U_0 \supseteq V_0 \supseteq U_1 \supseteq V_1 \supseteq U_2 ...$.

If $\bigcap\limits_{i=0}^{\infty} U_i = \emptyset$ then Player I wins, otherwise Player II wins.

It was proven by [John C. Oxtoby](wikipedia://en/47435441) that a non-empty topological space $X$ is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550) if and only if Player I has no [winning strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winning_strategy). A nonempty topological space $X$ in which Player II has a winning strategy is called a **Choquet space**. (Note that it is possible that neither player has a winning strategy.) Thus every Choquet space is Baire. On the other hand, there are Baire spaces (even [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrizable) ones) that are not Choquet spaces, so the converse fails.

The strong Choquet game of $X$, $G^s(X)$, is defined similarly, except that Player I chooses $(x_0,U_0)$, then Player II chooses $V_0$, then Player I chooses $(x_1,U_1)$, etc, such that $x_i \in U_i, V_i$ for all $i$. A topological space $X$ in which Player II has a winning strategy for $G^s(X)$ is called a **strong Choquet space**. Every strong Choquet space is a Choquet space, although the converse does not hold.

All nonempty [complete metric spaces](wikipedia://en/7832) and [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [T<sub>2</sub> spaces](wikipedia://en/13637) are strong Choquet. (In the first case, Player II, given $(x_i,U_i)$, chooses $V_i$ such that $\operatorname{diam}(V_i)<1/i$ and $\operatorname{cl}(V_i) \subseteq V_{i-1}$. Then the sequence $\left \{ x_i \right \} \to x \in V_i$ for all $i$.) Any subset of a strong Choquet space that is a [$G_\delta$ set](wikipedia://en/252076) is strong Choquet. Metrizable spaces are [completely metrizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_metrizable) if and only if they are strong Choquet.[^o3][^o4]
