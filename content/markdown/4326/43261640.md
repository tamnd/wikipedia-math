# Atlantic City algorithm

Algorithme d'Atlantic City

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atlantic City algorithm"}

In computing, an **Atlantic City algorithm** is a [randomized algorithm](wikipedia://en/495383) that answers correctly at least 75% of the time[^o2]. In some variant definitions, the correctness threshold may be any value greater than 50%[^o3]. In either sense, Atlantic City algorithms are types of [Monte Carlo algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_algorithm).

:::

There exists a polynomial-time Atlantic City algorithm for any problem belonging to the class [BPP](wikipedia://en/4079) of bounded probabilistic polynomial-time problems[^o2].

[Las Vegas algorithms](wikipedia://en/537519), which never return an incorrect answer, are a [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_%28mathematics%29) of Monte Carlo algorithms and, therefore, of Atlantic City algorithms.

The name refers to [Atlantic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey) in the [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state) of [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey). Much like [Monte Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo) and [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas), Atlantic City is widely associated with casinos and gambling culture.
