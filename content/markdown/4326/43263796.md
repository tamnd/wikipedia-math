# Hilbert–Kunz function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Kunz function"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923), the **Hilbert–Kunz function** of a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) (*R*, *m*) of [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p* is the [function](wikipedia://en/185427)

:::

$$f(q) = \operatorname{length}_R(R/m^{[q]})$$

where *q* is a power of *p* and *m*<sup>\[*q*\]</sup> is the [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) generated by the *q*-th powers of elements of the [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m*.[^o2]

The notion was introduced by [Ernst Kunz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kunz_%28mathematician%29), who used it to characterize a [regular ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_ring) as a [Noetherian ring](wikipedia://en/56129) in which the [Frobenius morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_morphism) is [flat](wikipedia://en/492616). If d is the [dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of the local ring, [Monsky](wikipedia://en/12673586) showed that f(q)/(q^d) is c+O(1/q) for some real constant c. This constant, the "Hilbert-Kunz multiplicity", is greater than or equal to 1. Watanabe and Yoshida strengthened some of Kunz's results, showing that in the unmixed case, the ring is [regular](wikipedia://en/506959) precisely when c=1.

Hilbert–Kunz functions and multiplicities have been studied for their own sake. Brenner and Trivedi have treated local rings coming from the homogeneous co-ordinate rings of smooth projective curves, using techniques from [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). Han, Monsky, and Teixeira have treated [diagonal hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/3028504) and various related [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101). But there is no known technique for determining the Hilbert–Kunz function or c in general. In particular the question of whether c is always rational wasn't settled until recently (by Brenner—it needn't be, and indeed can be transcendental). [Hochster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Hochster) and [Huneke](wikipedia://en/41178067) related Hilbert-Kunz multiplicities to "[tight closure](wikipedia://en/6921893)" and Brenner and Monsky used Hilbert–Kunz functions to show that localization need not preserve tight closure. The question of how c behaves as the characteristic goes to infinity (say for a hypersurface defined by a polynomial with [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) coefficients) has also received attention; once again open questions abound.

A comprehensive overview is to be found in Craig Huneke's article "Hilbert-Kunz multiplicities and the F-signature" arXiv:1409.0467. This article is also found on pages 485-525 of the Springer volume "Commutative Algebra: Expository Papers Dedicated to [David Eisenbud](wikipedia://en/3649845) on the Occasion of His 65th Birthday", edited by [Irena Peeva](wikipedia://en/43076151).
