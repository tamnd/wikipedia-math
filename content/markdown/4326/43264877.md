# Bipartite realization problem

*Image: Bipartite realization.svg.* The sequences above are able to be the degree sequence for the [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) below it, where each vertex has a [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) equal to the number assigned to it.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bipartite realization problem"}

The **bipartite realization problem** is a classical [decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). Given two finite sequences $(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ and $(b_1,\dots,b_m)$ of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) with $m\leq n$, the problem asks whether there is a labeled simple [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) such that $(a_1,\dots,a_n), (b_1,\dots,b_m)$ is the [degree sequence](wikipedia://en/244431) of this bipartite graph.

:::

## Solutions {#Solutions}

The problem belongs to the complexity class [P](wikipedia://en/658550). This can be [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) using the [Gale–Ryser theorem](wikipedia://en/42442683), i.e., one has to validate the correctness of $n$ [inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489).

## Other notations {#Other_notations}

The problem can also be stated in terms of [zero-one matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0-1_matrix). The connection can be seen if one realizes that each bipartite graph has a [biadjacency matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjacency_matrix_of_a_bipartite_graph) where the column sums and row sums correspond to $(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ and $(b_1,\ldots,b_m)$. The problem is then often denoted by *0-1-matrices for given row and column sums*. In the classical literature the problem was sometimes stated in the context of [contingency tables](wikipedia://en/935515) by *contingency tables with given marginals*. A third formulation is in terms of degree sequences of simple [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986) with at most one [loop](wikipedia://en/1449083) per [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899). In this case the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) is interpreted as the adjacency matrix of such a directed graph. When are pairs of non-negative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) ((*a*<sub>1</sub>,*b*<sub>1</sub>), ..., (*a<sub>n</sub>*,*b<sub>n</sub>*)) the indegree-outdegree pairs of a labeled directed graph with at most one loop per vertex?

## Related problems {#Related_problems}

Similar problems describe the [degree sequences](wikipedia://en/1374448) of [simple graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) and simple directed graphs. The first problem is the so-called [graph realization problem](wikipedia://en/43304622), and the second is known as the [digraph realization problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digraph_realization_problem). The bipartite realization problem is equivalent to the question, if there exists a labeled bipartite subgraph of a [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) to a given degree sequence. The *hitchcock problem* asks for such a subgraph minimizing the sum of the costs on each edge which are given for the complete bipartite graph. A further generalization is the [f-factor problem for bipartite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-matching), i.e. for a given bipartite graph one searches for a subgraph possessing a certain degree sequence. The problem *uniform sampling a bipartite graph to a fixed degree sequence* is to construct a solution for the bipartite realization problem with the additional constraint that each such solution comes with the same probability. This problem was shown to be in [FPTAS](wikipedia://en/666431) for [regular sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28graph_theory%29) by [Catherine Greenhill](wikipedia://en/56687309)[^o5] (for regular bipartite graphs with a forbidden [1-factor](wikipedia://en/3298854)) and for [half-regular sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_%28graph_theory%29) by [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) et al.[^o6] The general problem is still unsolved.
