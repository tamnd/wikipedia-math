# Eduard Selling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eduard Selling"}

*Image: Eduard Selling by Otto Patzig (Kunstbibliothek Berlin) - crop.jpg.* Portrait of Eduard Selling

**Eduard Selling** (5 November 1834 in [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach) – 31 January 1920 in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and inventor of [calculating machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculating_machine).

:::

Selling studied mathematics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) (under [Philipp Ludwig von Seidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Ludwig_von_Seidel)). He obtained the [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) at the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München in 1859, under the supervision of [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980).[^o2] On recommendation of [Leopold Kronecker](wikipedia://en/318450), he became professor extraordinarius of mathematics at the [University of Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Würzburg) in 1860 – against the will of the philosophical faculty and the mathematics professor Aloys Mayr.[^o4] There, he also taught astronomy and became [conservator-restorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservator-restorer) at the astronomical department in 1879. In 1873[^o5] he wrote an important paper on binary and ternary [quadratic forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_forms) which was also translated into French and cited by [Henri Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740), [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000) and [Paul Gustav Heinrich Bachmann](wikipedia://en/641951).[^o6] Beginning with 1877, he also became concerned with [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance), and participated in the reorganization of the pensions in [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria) on behalf of the Bavarian government. His application for a promotion to professor ordinarius was declined in 1891. In 1906, he became [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus).

For his own extensive computations (for instance, [signed-digit representation](wikipedia://en/1181818)), he initially used computational machines by [Thomas de Colmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Colmar) with which he was not satisfied. Therefore, he built multiplication machines after the model of a [Pantograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantograph),[^o7] for which he got a patent in 1886, and a prize at the [Chicago World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Columbian_Exposition) in 1893. However, the machine was complicated to use and to produce, so it didn't gain much importance. Some 30 to 40 devices were produced until 1898. He also built a few copies of an improved version and designed a third electrical machine (patent in 1894). The later inventor of computational machines, [Christel Hamann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christel_Hamann), participated in those constructions. Some copies of Selling's machine can be seen, for instance, in the [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum) in Munich.
