# Double category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Double category"}

In mathematics, especially [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a **double category** is a generalization of a category where instead of morphisms, we have vertical morphisms, horizontal morphisms and 2-morphisms. Introduced by [Ehresmann](wikipedia://en/605108) in 1960s,[^o2][^o3] the notion may be compared with that of a [bicategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategory); namely, the notion of a bicategory is obtained by [enrichment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrichment_%28category_theory%29), while the notion of a double category is obtained by [internalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internalization_%28category_theory%29).[^o4] Precisely, a double category is a category [internal](wikipedia://en/31813104) to **Cat** (roughly meaning a category object).[^o5]

:::

Just as iterating the process of obtaining the notion of a 2-category leads to that of an *n*-category, iterating the process for a double category leads to that of an [*n*-fold category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-fold_category).

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Double categories can be understood as being made from the following data

- A class of objects
- A class of vertical morphisms between objects
- A class of horizontal morphisms between objects
- A class of 2-morphisms between horizontal morphisms filling in squares

In this presentation both the vertical and horizontal morphisms give a category structure to the objects. These categories are called edge categories.
