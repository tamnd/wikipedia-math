# LibreSSL

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="LibreSSL"}

**LibreSSL** is an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) implementation of the [Transport Layer Security](wikipedia://en/187813) (TLS) protocol. The implementation is named after [Secure Sockets Layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Sockets_Layer) (SSL), the deprecated predecessor of TLS, for which support was removed in release 2.3.0. The [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD) project [forked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_%28software%29) LibreSSL from [OpenSSL](wikipedia://en/438819) 1.0.1g in April 2014 as a response to the [Heartbleed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbleed) security [vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29),[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8] with the goals of modernizing the [codebase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codebase), improving [security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security), and applying development [best practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_practice).[^o9][^o10][^o11]

:::

## History {#History}

After the [Heartbleed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbleed) security [vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29) was discovered in [OpenSSL](wikipedia://en/438819), the [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD) team [audited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_audit) the codebase and decided it was necessary to [fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_%28software_development%29) OpenSSL to remove dangerous code.[^o5] The libressl.org domain was registered on 11 April 2014; the project announced the name on 22 April 2014. In the first week of development, more than 90,000 lines of C code were removed.[^o10][^o13] Unused code was removed, and support for obsolete [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) ([Classic Mac OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Mac_OS), [NetWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetWare), [OS/2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OS/2), [16-bit Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16-bit_Windows)) and some older [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system) ([OpenVMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVMS)) was removed.[^o14]

LibreSSL was initially developed as an intended replacement for OpenSSL in OpenBSD 5.6, and was ported to other platforms once a stripped-down version of the library was stable.[^o15] , the project was seeking a "stable commitment" of external funding.[^o13] On 17 May 2014, Bob Beck presented "LibreSSL: The First 30 Days, and What The Future Holds" during the 2014 BSDCan conference, in which he described the progress made in the first month.[^o16] On 5 June 2014, several OpenSSL bugs became public. While several projects were notified in advance,[^o17] LibreSSL was not; [Theo de Raadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_de_Raadt) accused the OpenSSL developers of intentionally withholding this information from OpenBSD and LibreSSL.[^o18]

On 20 June 2014, [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) created another fork of OpenSSL called [BoringSSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoringSSL), and promised to exchange fixes with LibreSSL.[^o19][^o20] Google has already [relicensed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_relicensing) some of its contributions under the [ISC license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISC_license), as it was requested by the LibreSSL developers.[^o19][^o21] On 21 June 2014, Theo de Raadt welcomed BoringSSL and outlined the plans for LibreSSL-portable.[^o22] Starting on 8 July, code porting for [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS) and [Solaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solaris_%28operating_system%29) began,[^o23] while the initial porting to [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux) began on 20 June.[^o24]

As of 2021, OpenBSD uses LibreSSL as the primary TLS library. [Alpine Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Linux) supported LibreSSL as its primary TLS library for three years, until release 3.9.0 in January 2019. [Gentoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentoo_Linux) supported LibreSSL until February 2021.[^o25] [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) 3.10 dropped LibreSSL[^o26] after being supported since Python 3.4.3 (2015).[^o27]

### Adoption {#Adoption}

LibreSSL is the default provider of TLS for:

- [Dragonfly BSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly_BSD)[^o29]
- [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD)[^o14]
- [Hyperbola GNU/Linux-libre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbola_GNU/Linux-libre)[^o30]
- [OpenSSH on Windows](https://github.com/powershell/Win32-OpenSSH)

LibreSSL was the default provider of TLS for these now-discontinued systems:

- [OpenELEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenELEC)[^o31]
- [TrueOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrueOS) packages[^o32][^o33]

LibreSSL is a selectable provider of TLS for:

- [FreeBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeBSD) packages[^o34]
- [Gentoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentoo_Linux) packages[^o35] (support dropped as of February 2021[^o36][^o37][^o38])
- [OPNsense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPNsense) packages [^o39] (will be dropped after 22.7[^o40])
- [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS)

## Changes {#Changes}

### Memory-related {#Memory-related}

Changes include replacement of custom memory calls to ones in a [standard library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_library) (for example, `[[strlcpy]]`, `[[calloc]]`, `[[asprintf]]`, `[[reallocarray]]`, etc.).[^o43][^o44] This process may help later on to catch [buffer overflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_overflow) errors with more advanced [memory analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_debugger) tools or by observing program crashes (via [ASLR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Address_space_layout_randomization), use of the [NX bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NX_bit), [stack canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stack_canary), etc.).

Fixes for potential [double free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_dynamic_memory_allocation#Common_errors) scenarios have also been cited in the [VCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Version_control_system) commit logs (including explicit assignments of [null pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_pointer) values).[^o45] There have been extra [sanity checks](wikipedia://en/39893) also cited in the [commit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commit_%28version_control%29) logs related to ensuring length arguments, unsigned-to-signed [variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_%28computer_science%29) assignments, [pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointer_%28computer_programming%29) values, and method returns.

### Proactive measures {#Proactive_measures}

In order to maintain good programming practice, a number of compiler options and flags designed for safety have been enabled by default to help in spotting potential issues so they can be fixed earlier (-Wall, -Werror, -Wextra, -Wuninitialized). There have also been [code readability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_readability) updates which help future contributors in verifying program correctness (KNF, white-space, line-wrapping, etc.). Modification or removal of unneeded method wrappers and macros also help with code readability and auditing (Error and [I/O abstraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I/O_abstraction) library references).

Changes were made to ensure that LibreSSL will be [year 2038 compatible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_2038_problem) along with maintaining [portability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portability_%28computer_science%29) for other similar platforms. In addition, `explicit_bzero` and `bn_clear` calls were added to prevent the compiler from optimizing them out and prevent attackers from reading previously allocated memory.

### Cryptographic {#Cryptographic}

There were changes to help ensure proper [seeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_seed) of [random number generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_number_generator)-based methods via replacements of insecure seeding practices (taking advantage of features offered by the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28operating_system%29) itself natively).[^o48][^o49] In terms of notable additions made, OpenBSD has added support for newer and more reputable algorithms ([ChaCha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChaCha_%28cipher%29) stream cipher and [Poly1305](wikipedia://en/2017690) message authentication code) along with a safer set of [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography) (brainpool curves from RFC 5639, up to 512 bits in strength).

### Added features {#Added_features}

The initial release of LibreSSL added a number of features: the [ChaCha](wikipedia://en/2701329#ChaCha_variant) and [Poly1305](wikipedia://en/2017690) algorithm, the [Brainpool](https://tools.ietf.org/html/rfc7027) and [ANSSI](http://www.ssi.gouv.fr/agence/publication/publication-dun-parametrage-de-courbe-elliptique-visant-des-applications-de-passeport-electronique-et-de-ladministration-electronique-francaise/) elliptic curves, and the [AES-GCM](wikipedia://en/5897139) and [ChaCha20-Poly1305](wikipedia://en/69611898) [AEAD](wikipedia://en/2244296) modes.

Later versions added the following:[^o62]

- \2.1.0: Automatic [ephemeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemeral_key) EC keys.[^o51]
- \2.1.2: Built-in [arc4random](wikipedia://en/25831#RC4-based_random_number_generators) implementation on macOS and FreeBSD.[^o52]
- \2.1.2: Reworked [GOST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOST) cipher suite support.
- \2.1.3: [ALPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application-Layer_Protocol_Negotiation) support.[^o53]
- \2.1.3: Support for [SHA-256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-256) and [Camellia](wikipedia://en/532475) cipher suites.
- \2.1.4: [TLS\_FALLBACK\_SCSV](wikipedia://en/187813#POODLE_attack) server-side support.[^o54]
- \2.1.4: certhash as a replacement of the c\_rehash script.
- \2.1.4: X509\_STORE\_load\_mem API for loading certificates from memory (enhance chroot support).
- \2.1.4: Experimental [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows) [binaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binaries).
- \2.1.5: Minor update mainly for improving Windows support, first working 32- and [64-bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/64-bit_computing) binaries.[^o55]
- \2.1.6: libtls declared stable and enabled by default.[^o56]
- \2.2.0: [AIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIX) and [Cygwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygwin) support.[^o57]
- \2.2.1: Addition of EC\_curve\_nid2nist and EC\_curve\_nist2nid[^o58] from OpenSSL, initial [Windows XP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_XP)/2003 support.
- \2.2.2: Defines LIBRESSL\_VERSION\_NUMBER,[^o59] added TLS\_\*methods as a replacement for the SSLv23\_\*method calls, [cmake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMake) build support.

### Old insecure features {#Old_insecure_features}

The initial release of LibreSSL disabled a number of features by default.[^o14] Some of the code for these features was later removed, including [Kerberos](wikipedia://en/16947), [US-Export ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Export_of_cryptography_in_the_United_States), TLS compression, [DTLS heartbeat](wikipedia://en/438819#SSL,_TLS_and_DTLS_plaintext_recovery_attack), [SSL v2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSL_v2) and [SSL v3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSL_v3).

Later versions disabled more features:

- \2.1.1: Following the discovery of the [POODLE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POODLE) vulnerability in the legacy [SSL 3.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSL_3.0) protocol, LibreSSL now disables the use of SSL 3.0 by default.[^o61]
- \2.1.3: [GOST R 34.10-94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOST) signature authentication.[^o62][^o53]
- \2.2.1: Removal of Dynamic Engine and MDC-2DES support[^o58]
- \2.2.2: Removal of SSL 3.0 from the openssl binary, removal of [Internet Explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Explorer) 6 workarounds, RSAX engine.[^o59]
- \2.3.0: Complete removal of SSL 3.0, [SHA-0](wikipedia://en/26672#SHA-0) and [DTLS](wikipedia://en/1500599)1\_BAD\_VER.

### Code removal {#Code_removal}

The initial release of LibreSSL has removed a number of features that were deemed insecure, unnecessary or deprecated as part of [OpenBSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenBSD) 5.6.

- In response to [Heartbleed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbleed), the heartbeat functionality[^o64] was one of the first features to be removed.
- Support for obsolete platforms ([Classic Mac OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Mac_OS), [NetWare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NetWare), [OS/2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OS/2), [16-bit Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16-bit_Windows)) were removed.
- Support for some older platforms ([OpenVMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenVMS)) was removed.
- Support for platforms that do not exist, such as [big-endian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-endian) [i386](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I386) and [amd64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amd64).[^o65]
- Support for old compilers.
- The [IBM 4758](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_4758), Broadcom ubsec, Sureware, Nuron, GOST, GMP, CSwift, CHIL, CAPI, Atalla and AEP engines were removed due to irrelevance of hardware or dependency on [non-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-free_software) libraries.
- The OpenSSL [PRNG](wikipedia://en/45524) was removed (and replaced with [ChaCha20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChaCha20)-based implementation of arc4random).
- [Preprocessor macros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_preprocessor) that have been deemed unnecessary or insecure or had already been deprecated in OpenSSL for a long time (e.g. des\_old.h).
- Older unneeded files for [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language), [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29), and [Perl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perl) (e.g. Entropy Gathering Daemon).
- [MD2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD2_%28cryptography%29), [SEED](wikipedia://en/1063358) functionality.
- SSL 3.0, SHA-0, DTLS1\_BAD\_VER

The [Dual EC DRBG](wikipedia://en/14259066) algorithm, which is suspected of having a [back door](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backdoor_%28computing%29),[^o66] was cut along with support for the [FIPS 140-2](wikipedia://en/885948) standard that required it. Unused protocols and insecure algorithms have also been removed, including the support for [FIPS 140-2](wikipedia://en/885948),[^o67] [MD4](wikipedia://en/578106)/[MD5](wikipedia://en/18826)[^o62] [J-PAKE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Password_Authenticated_Key_Exchange_by_Juggling),[^o14] and [SRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Remote_Password_protocol).[^o68]

### Bug backlog {#Bug_backlog}

One of the complaints of OpenSSL was the number of open [bugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_bug) reported in the bug tracker that had gone unfixed for years. Older bugs are now being fixed in LibreSSL.[^o70]
