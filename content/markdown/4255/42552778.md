# Simpson's rules (ship stability)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Simpson's rules"}

**Simpson's rules** are a set of rules used in ship stability and [naval architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_architecture), to calculate the [areas](wikipedia://en/1209) and [volumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumes) of irregular figures.[^o2] This is an application of [Simpson's rule](wikipedia://en/219962) for finding the values of an [integral](wikipedia://en/15532), here interpreted as the area under a curve.

:::

*Image: Simpsonrule1.jpg.* Simpson's First Rule

## Simpson's 1st rule {#Simpson's_1st_rule}

Also known as the **1–4–1 rule** (after the multipliers used ).[^o4]

$$\text{Area} = \frac{h}{3}( a + 4b + c).$$

## Simpson's 2nd rule {#Simpson's_2nd_rule}

*Image: Simpson2rule.jpg.* Simpson's Second Rule

Also known as the **1–3–3–1** rule, Simpson's second rule is a simplified version of [Simpson's 3/8 rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_Rule).[^o6]

$$\text{Area} = \frac{3h} 8 (a + 3b + 3c + d).$$

## Simpson's 3rd rule {#Simpson's_3rd_rule}

Also known as the **5–8–1 rule**,[^o8] SImpson's third rule is used to find the area between two consecutive ordinates when three consecutive ordinates are known.[^o9]

$$\text{Area} = \frac h {12} (5a + 8b - c ).$$

This estimates the area in the left half of the figure for Simpson's 1st Rule while using all three pieces of data.

## Use of Simpsons rules {#Use_of_Simpsons_rules}

Simpson's rules are used to calculate the volume of [lifeboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeboat_%28shipboard%29),[^o11] and by surveyors to calculate the volume of [sludge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sludge) in a ship's oil tanks. For instance, in the latter, Simpson's 3rd rule is used to find the volume between two co-ordinates. To calculate the entire area / volume, Simpson's first rule is used.[^o12]

Simpson's rules are used by a ship's officers to check that the area under the ship's GZ curve complies with IMO stability criteria.
