# National Survey of Family Growth

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="National Survey of Family Growth"}

The **National Survey of Family Growth** (**NSFG**) is a [survey](wikipedia://en/277315) conducted by the [National Center for Health Statistics](wikipedia://en/1042734) division of the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centers_for_Disease_Control_and_Prevention) to understand trends related to fertility, family structure, and demographics in the United States.[^o2]

:::

## History and details {#History_and_details}

The National Survey of Family Growth is conducted in five-year cycles. In each cycle, surveys are administered via personal interviews with people at homes. The interviewees generally comprise only the civilian, non-institutionalized population.[^o4]

The cycles so far have been:[^o5]

- Cycle 1, started 1973[^o6]
- Cycle 2, started 1976[^o7]
- Cycle 3, started 1982[^o8]
- Cycle 4, started 1988[^o9]
- Cycle 5, started 1995[^o10]
- Cycle 6, started 2002[^o11]
- 2006–2010 NSFG[^o12]
- 2011–2013 NSFG[^o13]
- 2013–2015 NSFG[^o13]
- 2015–2019 NSFG[^o13]

While Cycles 1–5 surveyed only women, Cycle 6 and later surveyed both men and women and used households as the unit of analysis. Cycle 6 surveyed 12,571 respondents 15–44 years of age: 7,643 females and 4,928 males. The 2006–2010 NSFG surveyed 22,682 interviews: over 10,000 interviews with men and more than 12,000 interviews with women.[^o4] In 2006, the NSFG shifted from periodic surveys to continuous interviewing.[^o13] For Cycle 6 onward, the surveys were conducted in person by female interviewers who are hired and managed by the [University of Michigan Institute for Social Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan_Institute_for_Social_Research).[^o4] The survey samples are intended to be nationally representative but not necessarily representative at subnational levels (such as individual states, ethnicities, or religions).[^o4]

NSFG resumed data collection in January 2022 with a multi-mode, continuous survey design. Under this survey design, data collection will continue through December 2029.[^o14]

## Data {#Data}

For the survey cycles that have been completed, data is available both in the form of [portable document format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_document_format) summaries and as full data files. In addition, program statements are available in SAS, SPSS, and STATA.[^o6]

Key statistics are also browsable online.[^o16]

NSFG data is also mirrored on the website of the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan)'s [Inter-university Consortium for Political and Social Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-university_Consortium_for_Political_and_Social_Research).[^o17]

## Reception and impact {#Reception_and_impact}

### Claims {#Claims}

Data collected by the NSFG are used as follows:[^o4]

- By scholars in the [behavioral sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_science) (e.g., [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), [demography](wikipedia://en/45642), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics)) to study [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage), [divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce), [fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility), and [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family) life
- By scholars in public health to study reproductive, maternal and infant health topics
- By agencies of the US [Department of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_and_Human_Services), to brief senior officials and to inform program decision-making in research programs and in health and social service programs
- By state and local governments to plan health and social service programs
- By private-sector research organizations which distribute the information to the public and to policy makers
- By the press, to prepare articles on a number of topics related to health and family life

### Academic research {#Academic_research}

The NSFG has been cited in more than 1,300 journal articles, NHCS reports, and book chapters.[^o4] Research citing the NSFG is concentrated on topics related to family planning, contraception, abortion, and fertility.[^o21][^o22][^o23]
