# Jamie Whyte

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jamie Whyte"}

**Jamie Whyte** is a New Zealand [classical-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical-liberal) academic and politician who was the Leader of [ACT New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_New_Zealand) in 2014. He unsuccessfully contested the [Pakuranga electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakuranga_%28New_Zealand_electorate%29) in the [2014 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_New_Zealand_general_election).[^o3] At the election, Whyte held the first position on the party list, but ACT did not achieve enough party votes to secure any list seats.[^o4] Soon after the 2014 general election, he resigned from the leadership of ACT.[^o5]

:::

Whyte is a former philosophy lecturer, currency trader and management consultant. He has written books and newspaper columns about reasoning and politics.

## Early life and academia {#Early_life_and_academia}

Whyte was born in [Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland), New Zealand. He received his undergraduate degree from the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland).[^o7] He then moved to the UK to study for an M.Phil and Ph.D at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge).

Upon graduation, Whyte remained at Cambridge University for three years as a research fellow at [Corpus Christi College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Cambridge) and temporarily lectured in the philosophy faculty and at the University of Reading. He won the *Analysis* journal prize for the best article by a philosopher under the age of 30.[^o8]

## Early career {#Early_career}

Whyte then took up a job with the New York-based management consultancy firm [Oliver Wyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Wyman).[^o10] He also worked as a foreign currency trader.

## Writing and media {#Writing_and_media}

Since 2003, Whyte has written books for general audiences and newspaper columns, aiming to expose shoddy reasoning, especially by politicians. In 2006 he won the [Reason Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reason_Foundation) Bastiat Prize for journalism (jointly with Tim Harford of the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)*) and in 2010 he was named runner-up.[^o12] In June 2014, Whyte won the [Institute of Economic Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Economic_Affairs)' Arthur Seldon Memorial Award for Excellence for *Quack Policy*.[^o13]

Whyte is the author of *Crimes Against Logic* (titled *Bad Thoughts: A Guide to Clear Thinking* in the UK; 2003), *A Load of Blair* (2005), *Free Thoughts* (2012) and *Quack Policy* (2013). He has also written columns for many notable publications, including *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)*, [City A.M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_A.M.)., [Standpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standpoint_%28magazine%29), *Financial Times* and *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*.

Whyte has also occasionally broadcast on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4) and television.

## Political career {#Political_career}

In his youth, Whyte was a campaign volunteer for [Bob Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Jones_%28businessman%29)' [New Zealand Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Party) in the [1984 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_New_Zealand_general_election).[^o15]

Despite no prior experience in political office, Whyte was elected to the ACT party's leadership at a board meeting in February 2014, beating former MP, [John Boscawen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boscawen). At the same meeting, [David Seymour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Seymour) was chosen as ACT's candidate for the [Epsom electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsom_%28New_Zealand_electorate%29)[^o16]; Kenneth Wang was elected as Whyte's deputy leader in April 2014.[^o17]

During his tenure as leader, Whyte drew controversy when he stated his opinion that incestuous relationships between consenting adults should not be illegal.[^o18]

In the 2014 general election, ACT only won enough votes to be represented by David Seymour in the [Epsom electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsom_%28New_Zealand_electorate%29). After the election, on 3 October 2014 Whyte resigned from the leadership of the party, saying: "Clearly, I make this announcement with regret, however the election result is clear, and I must now turn to my career and my family." He was replaced as ACT leader by Seymour on the day of his resignation.[^o5]

In February 2017, Whyte joined the [Institute of Economic Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Economic_Affairs) as Director of Research.[^o19] He left the think tank on 1 January 2019.[^o20]

Whyte's political philosophy is [classical liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism), in the tradition of [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek).[^o21]
