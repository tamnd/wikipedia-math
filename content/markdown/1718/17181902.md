# Everything

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Everything"}

*Image: Hubble ultra deep field.jpg.* In [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe) (depending on its definition) and all it comprises, *is everything*. ([Hubble Ultra-Deep Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Ultra-Deep_Field) image of distant galaxies pictured)

**Everything**, **every-thing**, or **every thing**, is all that exists. As this encompasses the totality of things relevant to some [subject matter](wikipedia://en/30746), it can be understood in many different ways.

:::

In an [anthropocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropocentric) [worldview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldview),[^o2] it may be understood as the sum of human experience, history, and the [human condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_condition) in general, or that of all living beings. Other worldviews may view every [object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28philosophy%29) and [entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entity) as part of everything, including all [physical bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_body) and in some cases all [abstract objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object).

To describe or know of everything as a spatial consideration in a local environment, such as the world in which humans mostly live, is possible. The determination of all things in the universe is unknown because of the physics beyond the [observed universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observed_universe) and the problem of knowing physics at the range [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824). To know everything universally as a temporal and spatial consideration isn't possible because of the unavailability of information at a certain time before the beginning of the universe and because of the problem of [eternal](wikipedia://en/217472) [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality).

## Scope {#Scope}

In ordinary conversation, *everything* usually refers only to the totality of things relevant to the subject matter.[^o2] When there is no expressed limitation, *everything* may refer to the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe), or the [world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World).

The universe is most commonly defined as everything that physically exists: the entirety of [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time), all forms of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) and [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431), and the [physical laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_law) and [constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_constant) that govern them. However, the term "universe" may be used in slightly different contextual senses, denoting such concepts as the *[cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos)*, the *[world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_%28philosophy%29)*, or *[nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature)*. According to some speculations, this universe may be one of many disconnected universes collectively denoted as the [multiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiverse). In the [bubble universe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_universe_theory), there are an infinite variety of universes, each with different [physical constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_constant). In the [many-worlds hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-worlds_hypothesis), new universes are spawned with every [quantum measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_measurement). By definition, these speculations cannot currently be tested experimentally, yet, if multiple universes do exist, they would still be part of everything.

Especially in a [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) context, *World* may refer to everything that constitutes [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality) and the [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe): see [World (philosophy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_%28philosophy%29). However, world may only refer to *[Earth](wikipedia://en/9228)* envisioned from an [anthropocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropocentric) or human [worldview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldview), as a place by [human beings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_beings).

## In theoretical physics {#In_theoretical_physics}

In [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), a *theory of everything* (TOE) is a hypothetical [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) that fully explains and links together all known physical phenomena. Initially, the term was used with an ironic connotation to refer to various overgeneralized theories. For example, a great-grandfather of [Ijon Tichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijon_Tichy)—a character from a cycle of [Stanisław Lem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Lem)'s [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) stories of the 1960s—was known to work on the "General Theory of Everything". Over time, the term stuck in popularizations of [quantum physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_physics) to describe a theory that would unify or explain through a single model the theories of all [fundamental interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_interaction) in nature.

There have been many theories of everything proposed by [theoretical physicists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_physicists) over the last century, but none have been confirmed experimentally. The primary problem in producing a TOE is that the accepted theories of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), and [special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) are hard to combine. Theories exploring quantum mechanics and string theory are easier to combine.

Based on theoretical [holographic principle](wikipedia://en/14286) arguments from the 1990s, many physicists believe that 11-dimensional [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406), which is described in many sectors by [matrix string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_string_theory), and in many other sectors by [perturbative string theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_resonance_model), is the complete theory of everything. Other physicists disagree.

## In philosophy {#In_philosophy}

In philosophy, a *theory of everything* or *TOE* is an ultimate, all-encompassing explanation of [nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature) or [reality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality).[^o6][^o7][^o8] Adopting the term from physics, where the search for a [theory of everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_everything) is ongoing, [philosophers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophers) have discussed the viability of the concept and analyzed its properties and implications.[^o6][^o7][^o8] Among the questions to be addressed by a [philosophical theory](wikipedia://en/17645570) of everything are: "Why is reality understandable ?", "Why are the laws of nature as they are?", and "[Why is there anything at all?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_is_there_anything_at_all?)".[^o6]
