# Polytree

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polytree"}

*Image: Polytree.svg.* A polytree

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **polytree**[^n1] (also called **directed tree**,[^n2] **oriented tree**[^o4] or **singly connected network**[^o5]) is a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002) whose underlying undirected graph is a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560). In other words, a polytree is formed by assigning an orientation to each edge of a [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) and [acyclic](wikipedia://en/168609) undirected graph.

:::

A **polyforest** (or **directed forest** or **oriented forest**) is a directed acyclic graph whose underlying undirected graph is a [forest](wikipedia://en/48560#Forest). In other words, if we replace its directed edges with undirected edges, we obtain an undirected graph that is acyclic.

A polytree is an example of an [oriented graph](wikipedia://en/36619818).

The term *polytree* was coined in 1987 by Rebane and [Pearl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_Pearl).[^o6]

## Related structures {#Related_structures}

- An [arborescence](wikipedia://en/13251077) is a directed rooted [tree](wikipedia://en/48560), i.e. a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002) in which there exists a single source node that has a unique path to every other node. Every arborescence is a polytree, but not every polytree is an arborescence.
- A [multitree](wikipedia://en/27131455) is a directed acyclic graph in which the subgraph reachable from any node forms a tree. Every polytree is a [multitree](wikipedia://en/27131455).
- The [reachability](wikipedia://en/2833097) relationship among the nodes of a polytree forms a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) that has [order dimension](wikipedia://en/3121852) at most three. If the order dimension is three, there must exist a subset of seven elements $x$, $y_i$, and $z_i$ (for $i=0,1,2$) such that, for each $i$, either $x\le y_i\ge z_i$ or $x\ge y_i\le z_i$, with these six inequalities defining the polytree structure on these seven elements.[^n3]
- A [fence](wikipedia://en/21644788) or zigzag poset is a special case of a polytree in which the underlying tree is a path and the edges have orientations that alternate along the path. The [reachability](wikipedia://en/2833097) ordering in a polytree has also been called a *generalized fence*.[^n4]

## Sumner's conjecture {#Sumner's_conjecture}

[Sumner's conjecture](wikipedia://en/28866227), named after [David Sumner](wikipedia://en/47676305), states that [tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502) are [universal graphs](wikipedia://en/1452141) for polytrees, in the sense that every tournament with $2n-2$ vertices contains every polytree with $n$ vertices as a subgraph. Although it remains unsolved, it has been proven for all sufficiently large values of $n$.[^n5]

## Applications {#Applications}

Polytrees have been used as a [graphical model](wikipedia://en/447298) for [probabilistic reasoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_reasoning).[^n1] If a [Bayesian network](wikipedia://en/203996) has the structure of a polytree, then [belief propagation](wikipedia://en/800010) may be used to perform inference efficiently on it.[^o5][^o6]

The [contour tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_tree) of a real-valued function on a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) is a polytree that describes the [level sets](wikipedia://en/559622) of the function. The nodes of the contour tree are the level sets that pass through a [critical point](wikipedia://en/1951424) of the function and the edges describe contiguous sets of level sets without a critical point. The orientation of an edge is determined by the comparison between the function values on the corresponding two level sets.[^n6]
