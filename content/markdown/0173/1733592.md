# Barrelled space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Barrelled space"}

In [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and related areas of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **barrelled space** (also written **barreled space**) is a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) for which every [barrelled set](wikipedia://en/1733629) in the space is a [neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhood_%28topology%29) for the [zero vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_vector). A **barrelled set** or a **barrel** in a topological vector space is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) that is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292), [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238), [absorbing](wikipedia://en/1728179), and [closed](wikipedia://en/47279). Barrelled spaces are studied because a form of the [Banach–Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach–Steinhaus_theorem) still holds for them. Barrelled spaces were introduced by (txt).

:::

## Barrels {#Barrels}

A [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) and [balanced](wikipedia://en/1734238) [subset](wikipedia://en/26691) of a real or complex vector space is called a disk and it is said to be disked, [absolutely convex](wikipedia://en/1734292), or convex balanced.

A **barrel** or a **[barrelled set](wikipedia://en/1733629)** in a [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) is a subset that is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) [absorbing](wikipedia://en/1728179) disk; that is, a barrel is a convex, balanced, closed, and absorbing subset.

Every barrel must contain the origin. If $\dim X \geq 2$ and if $S$ is any subset of $X,$ then $S$ is a convex, balanced, and absorbing set of $X$ if and only if this is all true of $S \cap Y$ in $Y$ for every $2$-dimensional vector subspace $Y;$ thus if $\dim X > 2$ then the requirement that a barrel be a [closed subset](wikipedia://en/47279) of $X$ is the only defining property that does not depend solely on $2$ (or lower)-dimensional vector subspaces of $X.$

If $X$ is any TVS then every closed convex and balanced [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of the origin is necessarily a barrel in $X$ (because every neighborhood of the origin is necessarily an absorbing subset). In fact, every [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) has a [neighborhood basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_basis) at its origin consisting entirely of barrels. However, in general, there might exist barrels that are not neighborhoods of the origin; "barrelled spaces" are exactly those TVSs in which every barrel is necessarily a neighborhood of the origin. Every finite dimensional topological vector space is a barrelled space so examples of barrels that are not neighborhoods of the origin can only be found in infinite dimensional spaces.

### Examples of barrels and non-barrels {#Examples_of_barrels_and_non-barrels}

The closure of any convex, balanced, and absorbing subset is a barrel. This is because the closure of any convex (respectively, any balanced, any absorbing) subset has this same property.

*A family of examples*: Suppose that $X$ is equal to $\mathbb{C}$ (if considered as a complex vector space) or equal to $\mathbb{R}^2$ (if considered as a real vector space). Regardless of whether $X$ is a real or complex vector space, every barrel in $X$ is necessarily a neighborhood of the origin (so $X$ is an example of a barrelled space). Let $R : [0, 2\pi) \to (0, \infty]$ be any function and for every angle $\theta \in [0, 2 \pi),$ let $S_{\theta}$ denote the closed line segment from the origin to the point $R(\theta) e^{i \theta} \in \mathbb{C}.$ Let $S := \bigcup_{\theta \in [0, 2 \pi)} S_{\theta}.$ Then $S$ is always an absorbing subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (a real vector space) but it is an absorbing subset of $\mathbb{C}$ (a complex vector space) if and only if it is a [neighborhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of the origin. Moreover, $S$ is a balanced subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ if and only if $R(\theta) = R(\pi + \theta)$ for every $0 \leq \theta < \pi$ (if this is the case then $R$ and $S$ are completely determined by $R$'s values on $[0, \pi)$) but $S$ is a balanced subset of $\mathbb{C}$ if and only it is an open or closed ball centered at the origin (of radius $0 < r \leq \infty$). In particular, barrels in $\mathbb{C}$ are exactly those closed balls centered at the origin with radius in $(0, \infty].$ If $R(\theta) := 2 \pi - \theta$ then $S$ is a closed subset that is absorbing in $\mathbb{R}^2$ but not absorbing in $\mathbb{C},$ and that is neither convex, balanced, nor a neighborhood of the origin in $X.$ By an appropriate choice of the function $R,$ it is also possible to have $S$ be a balanced and absorbing subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ that is neither closed nor convex. To have $S$ be a balanced, absorbing, and closed subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ that is neither convex nor a neighborhood of the origin, define $R$ on $[0, \pi)$ as follows: for $0 \leq \theta < \pi,$ let $R(\theta) := \pi - \theta$ (alternatively, it can be any positive function on $[0, \pi)$ that is continuously differentiable, which guarantees that $\lim_{\theta \searrow 0} R(\theta) = R(0) > 0$ and that $S$ is closed, and that also satisfies $\lim_{\theta \nearrow \pi} R(\theta) = 0,$ which prevents $S$ from being a neighborhood of the origin) and then extend $R$ to $[\pi, 2 \pi)$ by defining $R(\theta) := R(\theta - \pi),$ which guarantees that $S$ is balanced in $\mathbb{R}^2.$

### Properties of barrels {#Properties_of_barrels}

In any [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) $X,$ every barrel in $X$ [absorbs](wikipedia://en/1728179) every compact convex subset of $X.$[^n1] In any [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) Hausdorff TVS $X,$ every barrel in $X$ absorbs every convex bounded complete subset of $X.$[^n1] If $X$ is locally convex then a subset $H$ of $X^{\prime}$ is $\sigma\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$-bounded if and only if there exists a barrel $B$ in $X$ such that $H \subseteq B^{\circ}.$[^n1] Let $(X, Y, b)$ be a [pairing](wikipedia://en/63735167) and let $\nu$ be a locally convex topology on $X$ consistent with duality. Then a subset $B$ of $X$ is a barrel in $(X, \nu)$ if and only if $B$ is the [polar](wikipedia://en/1764022) of some $\sigma(Y, X, b)$-bounded subset of $Y.$[^n1] Suppose $M$ is a vector subspace of finite codimension in a locally convex space $X$ and $B \subseteq M.$ If $B$ is a barrel (resp. [bornivorous](wikipedia://en/64267682) barrel, bornivorous disk) in $M$ then there exists a barrel (resp. bornivorous barrel, bornivorous disk) $C$ in $X$ such that $B = C \cap M.$[^n2]

## Characterizations of barreled spaces {#Characterizations_of_barreled_spaces}

Denote by $L(X; Y)$ the space of continuous linear maps from $X$ into $Y.$

If $(X, \tau)$ is a [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) (TVS) with [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) $X^{\prime}$ then the following are equivalent:

$X$ is barrelled. **Definition**: Every barrel in $X$ is a neighborhood of the origin.

This definition is similar to a characterization of Baire TVSs proved by Saxon \[1974\], who proved that a TVS $Y$ with a topology that is not the [indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology) is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550) if and only if every absorbing balanced subset is a neighborhood of some point of $Y$ (not necessarily the origin).[^n2] For any Hausdorff TVS $Y$ every pointwise bounded subset of $L(X; Y)$ is equicontinuous.[^n3] For any [F-space](wikipedia://en/200440) $Y$ every pointwise bounded subset of $L(X; Y)$ is equicontinuous.[^n3]

An F-space is a complete [metrizable TVS](wikipedia://en/64278078). Every [closed linear operator](wikipedia://en/64713671) from $X$ into a complete metrizable TVS is continuous.[^n4]

A linear map $F : X \to Y$ is called **closed** if its [graph is a closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_graph) subset of $X \times Y.$ Every Hausdorff TVS topology $\nu$ on $X$ that has a neighborhood basis of the origin consisting of $\tau$-closed set is coarser than $\tau.$[^n5]

If $(X, \tau)$ is [locally convex](wikipedia://en/518844) space then this list may be extended by appending:

There exists a TVS $Y$ not carrying the [indiscrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiscrete_topology) (so in particular, $Y \neq \{0\}$) such that every pointwise bounded subset of $L(X; Y)$ is equicontinuous.[^n2] For any locally convex TVS $Y,$ every pointwise bounded subset of $L(X; Y)$ is equicontinuous.[^n2]

It follows from the above two characterizations that in the class of locally convex TVS, barrelled spaces are exactly those for which the uniform boundedness principle holds. Every $\sigma\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$-bounded subset of the continuous dual space $X$ is equicontinuous (this provides a partial converse to the [Banach-Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach-Steinhaus_theorem)).[^n2][^o20] $X$ carries the [strong dual topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_dual_topology) $\beta\left(X, X^{\prime}\right).$[^n2] Every [lower semicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semicontinuous) [seminorm](wikipedia://en/292067) on $X$ is continuous.[^n2] Every linear map $F : X \to Y$ into a locally convex space $Y$ is [almost continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_continuous).[^n2]

A linear map $F : X \to Y$ is called **almost continuous** if for every neighborhood $V$ of the origin in $Y,$ the closure of $F^{-1}(V)$ is a neighborhood of the origin in $X.$ Every surjective linear map $F : Y \to X$ from a locally convex space $Y$ is [almost open](wikipedia://en/65738568).[^n2]

This means that for every neighborhood $V$ of 0 in $Y,$ the closure of $F(V)$ is a neighborhood of 0 in $X.$ If $\omega$ is a locally convex topology on $X$ such that $(X, \omega)$ has a neighborhood basis at the origin consisting of $\tau$-closed sets, then $\omega$ is weaker than $\tau.$[^n2]

If $X$ is a Hausdorff locally convex space then this list may be extended by appending:

**[Closed graph theorem](wikipedia://en/497834)**: Every [closed linear operator](wikipedia://en/64713671) $F : X \to Y$ into a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) $Y$ is [continuous](wikipedia://en/1802169).[^n6]

The linear operator is called closed if its [graph is a closed subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_graph) of $X \times Y.$ For every subset $A$ of the continuous dual space of $X,$ the following properties are equivalent: $A$ is[^o20]

equicontinuous; relatively weakly compact; strongly bounded; weakly bounded.

The 0-neighborhood bases in $X$ and the fundamental families of bounded sets in $X_{\beta}^{\prime}$ correspond to each other by [polarity](wikipedia://en/1764022).[^o20]

If $X$ is [metrizable topological vector space](wikipedia://en/64278078) then this list may be extended by appending:

For any complete metrizable TVS $Y$ every pointwise bounded sequence in $L(X; Y)$ is equicontinuous.[^n3]

If $X$ is a locally convex [metrizable topological vector space](wikipedia://en/64278078) then this list may be extended by appending:

(Property S): The [weak\* topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak*_topology) on $X^{\prime}$ is [sequentially complete](wikipedia://en/64126151).[^n7] (Property C): Every weak\* bounded subset of $X^{\prime}$ is $\sigma\left(X^{\prime}, X\right)$-relatively [countably compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_compact).[^n7] (𝜎-barrelled): Every countable weak\* bounded subset of $X^{\prime}$ is equicontinuous.[^n7] (Baire-like): $X$ is not the union of an increase sequence of [nowhere dense](wikipedia://en/48634) [disks](wikipedia://en/1734292).[^n7]

## Examples and sufficient conditions {#Examples_and_sufficient_conditions}

Each of the following topological vector spaces is barreled:

TVSs that are [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550).

Consequently, every topological vector space that is of the [second category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_category) in itself is barrelled. [F-spaces](wikipedia://en/200440), [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801), [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989), and [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932).

However, there exist [normed vector spaces](wikipedia://en/21538) that are not barrelled. For example, if the [$L^p$-space](wikipedia://en/45194) $L^2([0, 1])$ is topologized as a subspace of $L^1([0, 1]),$ then it is not barrelled. [Complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) [pseudometrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) TVSs.[^n8]

Consequently, every finite-dimensional TVS is barrelled. [Montel spaces](wikipedia://en/1796319). [Strong dual spaces](wikipedia://en/63951372) of Montel spaces (since they are necessarily Montel spaces). A locally convex [quasi-barrelled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-barrelled_space) that is also a [σ-barrelled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σ-barrelled_space).[^n9] A sequentially complete [quasibarrelled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasibarrelled_space). A [quasi-complete](wikipedia://en/63948433) Hausdorff locally convex [infrabarrelled space](wikipedia://en/63950387).[^n2]

A TVS is called **quasi-complete** if every closed and bounded subset is complete. A TVS with a dense barrelled vector subspace.[^n2]

Thus the completion of a barreled space is barrelled. A Hausdorff locally convex TVS with a dense [infrabarrelled](wikipedia://en/63950387) vector subspace.[^n2]

Thus the completion of an infrabarrelled Hausdorff locally convex space is barrelled.[^n2] A vector subspace of a barrelled space that has countable codimensional.[^n2]

In particular, a finite codimensional vector subspace of a barrelled space is barreled. A locally convex ultrabarelled TVS.[^n10] A Hausdorff locally convex TVS $X$ such that every weakly bounded subset of its continuous dual space is equicontinuous.[^n11] A locally convex TVS $X$ such that for every Banach space $B,$ a closed linear map of $X$ into $B$ is necessarily continuous.[^n12] A product of a family of barreled spaces.[^n13] A locally convex direct sum and the inductive limit of a family of barrelled spaces.[^n14] A quotient of a barrelled space.[^n15][^n14] A Hausdorff [sequentially complete](wikipedia://en/64126151) [quasibarrelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasibarrelled) boundedly summing TVS.[^n16] A locally convex Hausdorff [reflexive space](wikipedia://en/210091) is barrelled.

### Counterexamples {#Counterexamples}

A barrelled space need not be [Montel](wikipedia://en/1796319), [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856), [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078), unordered Baire-like, nor the inductive limit of Banach spaces. Not all normed spaces are barrelled. However, they are all infrabarrelled.[^n2] A closed subspace of a barreled space is not necessarily [countably quasi-barreled](wikipedia://en/64267707) (and thus not necessarily barrelled).[^n17] There exists a dense vector subspace of the [Fréchet](wikipedia://en/233801) barrelled space $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$ that is not barrelled.[^n2] There exist complete locally convex TVSs that are not barrelled.[^n2] The [finest locally convex topology](wikipedia://en/518844#Finest_and_coarsest_locally_convex_topology) on an infinite-dimensional vector space is a Hausdorff barrelled space that is a [meagre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meager_set) subset of itself (and thus not a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550)).[^n2]

:::

## Properties of barreled spaces {#Properties_of_barreled_spaces}

### Banach–Steinhaus generalization {#Banach–Steinhaus_generalization}

The importance of barrelled spaces is due mainly to the following results.

::: {#o58 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem[^n18].**

Let $X$ be a barrelled TVS and $Y$ be a locally convex TVS. Let $H$ be a subset of the space $L(X ;Y)$ of continuous linear maps from $X$ into $Y$. The following are equivalent:

$H$ is bounded for the topology of pointwise convergence; $H$ is bounded for the topology of bounded convergence; $H$ is [equicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equicontinuous).

:::

The [Banach-Steinhaus theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach-Steinhaus_theorem) is a corollary of the above result.[^n19] When the vector space $Y$ consists of the complex numbers then the following generalization also holds.

::: {#o61 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem[^n20].**

If $X$ is a barrelled TVS over the complex numbers and $H$ is a subset of the continuous dual space of $X$, then the following are equivalent:

$H$ is weakly bounded; $H$ is strongly bounded; $H$ is equicontinuous; $H$ is relatively compact in the weak dual topology.

:::

Recall that a linear map $F : X \to Y$ is called **closed** if its graph is a closed subset of $X \times Y.$

::: {#o63 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Closed Graph Theorem"}

**Closed Graph Theorem[^n21].**

Every closed linear operator from a Hausdorff barrelled TVS into a complete metrizable TVS is continuous.

:::

### Other properties {#Other_properties}

Every Hausdorff barrelled space is [quasi-barrelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-barrelled).[^n22] A linear map from a barrelled space into a locally convex space is almost continuous. A linear map from a locally convex space onto a barrelled space is [almost open](wikipedia://en/65738568). A [separately continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separately_continuous) bilinear map from a product of barrelled spaces into a locally convex space is [hypocontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypocontinuous).[^n23] A linear map with a closed graph from a barreled TVS into a $B_r$-complete TVS is necessarily continuous.[^n12]
