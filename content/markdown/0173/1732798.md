# Naimark's problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Naimark's problem"}

**Naimark's problem** is a question in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) asked by (txt). It asks whether every [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) that has only one [irreducible $*$-representation](wikipedia://en/385982) up to [unitary equivalence](wikipedia://en/187408#Spectral_theorem) is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to the $*$-algebra of [compact operators](wikipedia://en/6690773) on some (not necessarily separable) [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

:::

The problem has been solved in the affirmative for special cases (specifically for separable and Type-I C\*-algebras). (Akemann Weaver 2004) used the diamond principle to construct a [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) with $\aleph_{1}$ generators that serves as a counterexample to Naimark's problem. More precisely, they showed that the existence of a counterexample generated by $\aleph_{1}$ elements is independent of the axioms of [Zermelo–Fraenkel set theory](wikipedia://en/152214) and the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) ($\mathsf{ZFC}$).

Whether Naimark's problem itself is independent of $\mathsf{ZFC}$ remains unknown.
