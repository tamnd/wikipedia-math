# Federigo Enriques

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Federigo Enriques"}

**Abramo Giulio Umberto Federigo Enriques** (5 January 1871 – 14 June 1946) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), now known principally as the first to give a [classification of algebraic surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_of_algebraic_surfaces) in [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), and other contributions in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Enriques was born in [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno), and brought up in [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa), in a [Sephardi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jew) family of [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) descent. His younger brother was zoologist [Paolo Enriques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Enriques) who was also the father of Enzo Enriques Agnoletti and [Anna Maria Enriques Agnoletti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Maria_Enriques_Agnoletti). He became a student of [Guido Castelnuovo](wikipedia://en/1558891) (who later became his brother-in-law after marrying his sister Elbina), and became an important member of the [Italian school of algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/383424). He also worked on [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He collaborated with Castelnuovo, [Corrado Segre](wikipedia://en/2422061) and [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716). He had positions at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), and then the [University of Rome La Sapienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza). In 1931, he swore allegiance to fascism, and in 1933 he became a member of the PNF. Despite this, he lost his position in 1938, when the [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) government enacted the "leggi razziali" (racial laws), which in particular banned Jews from holding professorships in Universities.

The Enriques classification, of complex [algebraic surfaces](wikipedia://en/648311) up to birational equivalence, was into five main classes, and was background to further work until [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326) reconsidered the matter in the 1950s. The largest class, in some sense, was that of [surfaces of general type](wikipedia://en/2368356): those for which the consideration of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) provides [linear systems](wikipedia://en/722503) that are large enough to make all the geometry visible. The work of the Italian school had provided enough insight to recognise the other main birational classes. [Rational surfaces](wikipedia://en/2380148) and more generally [ruled surfaces](wikipedia://en/705158) (these include [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570) and [cubic surfaces](wikipedia://en/526126) in projective 3-space) have the simplest geometry. [Quartic surfaces](wikipedia://en/20133775) in 3-spaces are now classified (when [non-singular](wikipedia://en/253260#Singularities)) as cases of [K3 surfaces](wikipedia://en/646116); the classical approach was to look at the [Kummer surfaces](wikipedia://en/647758), which are singular at 16 points. [Abelian surfaces](wikipedia://en/974181) give rise to Kummer surfaces as quotients. There remains the class of [elliptic surfaces](wikipedia://en/3073462), which are [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526) over a curve with [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225) as fiber, having a finite number of modifications (so there is a bundle that is [locally trivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_trivial) actually over a curve less some points). The question of classification is to show that any surface, lying in [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) of any dimension, is in the birational sense (after [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) and blowing down of some curves, that is) accounted for by the models already mentioned.

No more than other work in the Italian school would the proofs by Enriques now be counted as complete and [rigorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigorous). Not enough was known about some of the technical issues: the geometers worked by a mixture of inspired guesswork and close familiarity with examples. [Oscar Zariski](wikipedia://en/359352) started to work in the 1930s on a more refined theory of birational mappings, incorporating [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) methods. He also began work on the question of the classification for [characteristic p](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_p), where new phenomena arise. The schools of Kunihiko Kodaira and [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815) had put Enriques' work on a sound footing by about 1960.
