# Michael Stifel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Stifel"}

*Image: Michael Stifel's Arithmetica Integra (1544) p225.tif.* Michael Stifel's Arithmetica Integra (1544), p. 225.

**Michael Stifel** or **Styfel** (1487 – April 19, 1567) was a German monk, [Protestant reformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_reformer) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was an [Augustinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustinians) who became an early supporter of [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther). He was later appointed [professor of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_of_mathematics) at [Jena University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena_University).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Stifel was born in [Esslingen am Neckar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esslingen_am_Neckar) in southern Germany. He joined the [Order of Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine) and was ordained a priest in 1511. Tensions in the abbey grew after he published the poem *Von der Christförmigen, rechtgegründeten leer Doctoris Martini Luthers* (1522, i.e. On the Christian, righteous doctrine of Doctor [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther)) and came into conflict with [Thomas Murner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Murner). Stifel then left for [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt), and soon went to [Mansfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfeld), where he began his mathematical studies. In 1524, upon a recommendation by Luther, Stifel was called by the [Jörger family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jörger_von_Tollet) to serve at their residence, Tollet Castle in [Tollet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollet) (close to [Grieskirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grieskirchen), [Upper Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Austria)).[^o4] Due to the tense situation in the [Archduchy of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduchy_of_Austria) in the wake of the execution of [Leonhard Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Kaiser) in [Schärding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schärding), Stifel returned to [Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittenberg) in 1527. At this time Stifel started writing a book collecting letter transcripts of Martin Luther, completed in 1534.[^o5]

By intercession of Martin Luther, Stifel became minister in [Lochau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaburg) (now Annaburg). Luther also confirmed his marriage to the widow of his predecessor in the ministry. Michael Stifel was fascinated regarding the properties and possibilities of numbers; he studied [number theory](wikipedia://en/3118) and [numerology](wikipedia://en/21137). He also performed the "Wortrechnung" (i.e. word-calculation), studying the statistical properties of letters and words in the bible (a common method at that time). In 1532, Stifel published anonymously his "*Ein Rechenbuchlin vom EndChrist. [Apocalyps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse) in Apocalypsim*" (A Book of Arithmetic about the AntiChrist. A Revelation in the Revelation). This predicted that [Judgement Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Judgement) would occur and the world would end at 8am on October 19, 1533. The German saying "to talk a Stiefel" or "to calculate a Stiefel" (Stiefel is the German word for boot), meaning to say or calculate something based on an unusual track, can be traced back to this incident.[^o6] When this prediction failed, he did not make any other predictions.

In 1535 he became minister in [Holzdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holzdorf) near Wittenberg and stayed there for 12 years. He studied "Die Coss" (the first algebra book written in German) by [Christoph Rudolff](wikipedia://en/23511221) and [Euclid's *Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) in the Latin edition by [Campanus of Novara](wikipedia://en/3926883). [Jacob Milich](wikipedia://en/12993364) supported his scientific development and encouraged him to write a comprehensive work on arithmetic and algebra.[^o7][^o8] In 1541 he registered for mathematics at the [University of Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg)[^o9] to extend his mathematical knowledge. In 1558 Stifel became first professor of mathematics at the new founded [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Schiller_University_Jena).[^o10]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Stifel's most important work *Arithmetica integra* (1544) contained important innovations in [mathematical notation](wikipedia://en/277184). It has the first use of [multiplication by juxtaposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplication_by_juxtaposition) (with no symbol between the terms) in Europe. He is the first to use the term "[exponent](wikipedia://en/99491)" and also included the following rules for calculating powers: $q^m q^n = q^{m+n}$ and $\tfrac{q^m}{q^n} = q^{m-n}$.[^n1]

The book contains a table of integers and powers of 2 that some have considered to be an early version of a [logarithmic](wikipedia://en/17860) table. Stifel explicitly points out, that multiplication and division operations in the (lower) geometric series can be mapped by addition and subtraction in the (upper) arithmetic series. On the following page 250, he shows examples also using negative exponents. He also realized that this would create a lot of work. So he wrote, that regarding this issue marvelous books could be written, but he himself will refrain and keep his eyes shut.[^n2][^o14][^o15]

Stifel was the first, who had a standard method to solve [quadratic equations](wikipedia://en/25175). He was able to reduce the different cases known to one case, because he uses both, positive and negative coefficients. He called his method/rule AMASIAS. The letters A, M, A/S, I, A/S each are representing a single operation step when solving a quadratic equation. Stifel, however avoided showing the negative results.[^n3][^o17]

Another topic dealt with in the *Arithmetica integra* are [negative numbers](wikipedia://en/154616) (which Stifel calls *numeri absurdi*). Negative numbers were refused and considered as preposterous by the authorities at that time. Stifel however, used negative numbers in the same manner as the other numbers. He also discussed the properties of [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689) and if the irrationals are real numbers, or only fictitious (AI page 103). Stifel found them very useful for mathematics, and not dispensable. Further issues were a method of calculating roots of higher order by using [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668)[^o18] and sequences.
