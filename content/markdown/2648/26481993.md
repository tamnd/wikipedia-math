# László Pyber

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="László Pyber"}

Pyber László ***László Pyber*** (born 8 May 1960 in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest)) is a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is a researcher at the [Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/31714670), Budapest. He works in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pyber received his Ph.D. from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1989 under the direction of [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) and [Gyula O.H. Katona](wikipedia://en/9211532) with the thesis *Extremal Structures and Covering Problems.*[^o3]

In 2007, he was awarded the Academics Prize by the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.[^o4]

In 2017, he was the recipient of an [ERC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Research_Council) Advanced Grant.[^o5]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Pyber has solved a number of [conjectures](wikipedia://en/6138) in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). In 1985, he proved the conjecture of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Tibor Gallai](wikipedia://en/1651618) that edges of a [simple graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_graph) with *n* vertices can be covered with at most *n*−1 circuits and edges.[^o7] In 1986, he proved the conjecture of [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) that a graph with *n* vertices and its complement can be covered with *n*<sup>2</sup>/4 + 2 [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466).[^o8]

He has also contributed to the study of [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634). In 1993, he provided an upper bound for the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of a 2-transitive group of degree *n* not containing *[A<sub>n</sub>](wikipedia://en/49726)* avoiding the use of the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445).[^o9] Together with [Tomasz Łuczak](wikipedia://en/32126054), Pyber proved the conjecture of [McKay](wikipedia://en/4583624) that for every *ε*>0, there is a constant *C* such that *C* randomly chosen elements invariably generate the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>*n*</sub> with probability greater than 1−*ε*.[^o10]

Pyber has made fundamental contributions in enumerating [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) of a given order *n*. In 1993, he proved[^o11] that if the [prime decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_decomposition) of *n* is *n*=*p*<sub>1</sub><sup>*g*<sub>1</sub></sup> ⋯ *p*<sub>*k*</sub><sup>*g*<sub>*k*</sub></sup> and *μ=*max(*g*<sub>1</sub>,...,*g*<sub>k</sub>), then the number of groups of order *n* is at most$n^{(\frac{2}{27}+o(1))\mu^2}.$In 2004, Pyber settled several questions in [subgroup growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgroup_growth) by completing the investigation of the spectrum of possible subgroup growth types.[^o12]

In 2011, Pyber and Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain obtained a surprisingly explicit formula for the number of random elements needed to generate a finite *d*-generator group with high probability.[^o13] They also explored related questions for [profinite groups](wikipedia://en/24266) and settled several [open problems](wikipedia://en/1333335).

In 2016, Pyber and Endre Szabó proved that in a [finite simple group](wikipedia://en/1749665) *L* of Lie type, a generating set *A* of *L* either grows, i.e., |*A*<sup>3</sup>| ≥ |*A*|<sup>1+*ε*</sup> for some *ε* depending only on the Lie rank of *L*, or *A*<sup>3</sup>=*L*.[^o14] This implies that diameters of [Cayley graphs](wikipedia://en/358277) of finite simple groups of bounded rank are polylogarithmic in the size of the group, partially resolving a well-known conjecture of [László Babai](wikipedia://en/3461604).
