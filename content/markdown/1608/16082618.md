# Michael J. D. Powell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Michael J. D. Powell"}

**Michael James David Powell** [^o3] (29 July 1936 – 19 April 2015) was a British mathematician, who worked in the [Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Applied_Mathematics_and_Theoretical_Physics) (DAMTP) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o6][^o7][^o8]

:::

## Education and early life {#Education_and_early_life}

Born in London, Powell was educated at [Frensham Heights School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frensham_Heights_School) and [Eastbourne College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_College).[^o3] He earned his Bachelor of Arts degree followed by a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) (DSc) degree in 1979 at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o10]

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

Powell was known for his extensive work in [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506), especially [nonlinear optimisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear_optimization) and [approximation](wikipedia://en/336271). He was a founding member of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) and a founding editor-in-chief of *IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis*.[^o12] His mathematical contributions include [quasi-Newton methods](wikipedia://en/7883127), particularly the [Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula](wikipedia://en/11231265) and the Powell's Symmetric Broyden formula, [augmented Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/30898832) function (also called Powell–[Rockafellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockafellar) penalty function), [sequential quadratic programming](wikipedia://en/7415899) method (also called as Wilson–Han–Powell method), [trust region](wikipedia://en/2975766) algorithms ([Powell's dog leg method](wikipedia://en/63648795)), [conjugate direction method](wikipedia://en/24106975) (also called [Powell's method](wikipedia://en/24106975)), and [radial basis function](wikipedia://en/2310753). He had been working on [derivative-free optimization](wikipedia://en/41680875) algorithms in recent years, the resultant algorithms including COBYLA, UOBYQA, NEWUOA, BOBYQA, and LINCOA.[^o13] He was the author of numerous scientific papers[^o5] and of several books, most notably *Approximation Theory and Methods*.[^o14]

### Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Powell won several awards, including the [George B. Dantzig Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Dantzig_Prize) from the Mathematical Programming Society/[Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) and the [Naylor Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naylor_Prize) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097). Powell was elected a [Foreign Associate of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Associate_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences) of the United States in 2001 and as a corresponding fellow to the [Australian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Academy_of_Science) in 2007.[^o10][^o16][^o17][^o18]
