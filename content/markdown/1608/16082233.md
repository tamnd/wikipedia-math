# Donald W. Fiske

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald W. Fiske"}

**Donald Winslow Fiske** (August 27, 1916 – April 6, 2003) was an American psychologist.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Fiske was born in [Lincoln, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska).[^o4] He grew up in [Medford, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medford,_Massachusetts).[^o4] He graduated from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and, in 1948, earned a PhD from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan).[^o4][^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Fiske was a professor of psychology at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o4]

Fiske specialized in methodological issues in [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_psychology), ability, and trait research. He was, with [Donald T. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_T._Campbell), co-author of a seminal paper regarding the [multitrait-multimethod approach](wikipedia://en/16082332) to evaluating [construct validity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construct_validity).[^o7]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Fiske had a wife, Barbara Page, a son, [Alan Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Fiske) (who became a professor of anthropology at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles)), and a daughter, [Susan Fiske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Fiske) (who became a professor of Psychology and Public Affairs at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University)).[^o4] He resided in [Hyde Park, Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Chicago),[^o4] where he died on April 6, 2003.
