# Topological pair

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological pair"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), a pair $(X,A)$ is shorthand for an inclusion of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) $i\colon A \hookrightarrow X$. Sometimes $i$ is assumed to be a [cofibration](wikipedia://en/1697216). A morphism from $(X,A)$ to $(X',A')$ is given by two maps $f\colon X\rightarrow X'$ and $g\colon A \rightarrow A'$ such that $i' \circ g =f \circ i$.

:::

A **pair of spaces** is an ordered pair $(X, A)$ where $X$ is a topological space and $A$ a [subspace](wikipedia://en/910926). The use of pairs of spaces is sometimes more convenient and technically superior to taking a [quotient space](wikipedia://en/237213) of $X$ by $A$. Pairs of spaces occur centrally in [relative homology](wikipedia://en/1248647),[^o2] [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) and [cohomology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohomology_theory), where chains in $A$ are made equivalent to 0, when considered as chains in $X$.

Heuristically, one often thinks of a pair $(X,A)$ as being akin to the quotient space $X/A$.

There is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051) to the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of pairs of spaces, which sends a space $X$ to the pair $(X, \varnothing)$.

A related concept is that of a triple $(X, A, B)$, with $B \subset A \subset X$. Triples are used in [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). Often, for a [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666) with basepoint at $x_{0}$, one writes the triple as $(X, A, B, x_{0})$, where $x_{0} \in B \subset A \subset X$.[^o2]
