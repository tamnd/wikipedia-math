# Terminal digit preference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Terminal digit preference"}

**Terminal digit preference**, **terminal digit bias**, or **end-digit preference** is a commonly-observed statistical phenomenon whereby humans recording numbers have a bias or preference for a specific final digit in a number. In [medical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine), this is often seen when recording measurements such as [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure) by hand, where those taking measurements will round to the nearest 5 or 0.[^o2] The phenomenon has been blamed for misdiagnoses.[^o3] Terminal digit bias has been used to identify errors in research,[^o2][^o4][^o5][^o6] and is one method used in the identification of scientific fraud.[^o7] Severe terminal digit bias has been found in datasets for scientific papers that were later retracted [^o8][^o9]

:::
