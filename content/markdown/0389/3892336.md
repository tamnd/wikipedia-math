# Tropical semiring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tropical semiring"}

In [idempotent analysis](wikipedia://en/59158118), the **tropical semiring** is a [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) of [extended real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_real_numbers) with the operations of [minimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) (or [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum)) and addition replacing the usual ("classical") operations of addition and multiplication, respectively.

:::

The tropical semiring has various applications (see [tropical analysis](wikipedia://en/59158120)), and forms the basis of [tropical geometry](wikipedia://en/867041). The name *tropical* is a reference to the Hungarian-born computer scientist [Imre Simon](wikipedia://en/23579726), so named because he lived and worked in Brazil.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The *min tropical semiring* (or **min-plus semiring** or **min-plus algebra**) is the [semiring](wikipedia://en/371468) (or [semifield](wikipedia://en/10417428)) ($\mathbb{R} \cup \{+\infty\}$, $\oplus$, $\otimes$), with the operations:

$$x \oplus y = \min\{x, y \},$$

$$x \otimes y = x + y.$$

The operations $\oplus$ and $\otimes$ are referred to as *tropical addition* and *tropical multiplication* respectively. The identity element for $\oplus$ is $+\infty$, and the identity element for $\otimes$ is 0.

Similarly, the *max tropical semiring* (or **max-plus semiring** or **max-plus algebra** or **arctic semiring**[^o5]) is the semiring ($\mathbb{R} \cup \{-\infty\}$, $\oplus$, $\otimes$), with operations:

$$x \oplus y = \max\{x, y \},$$

$$x \otimes y = x + y.$$

The identity element unit for $\oplus$ is $-\infty$, and the identity element unit for $\otimes$ is 0.

The two semirings are isomorphic under negation $x \mapsto -x$, and generally one of these is chosen and referred to simply as the *tropical semiring*. Conventions differ between authors and subfields: some use the *min* convention, some use the *max* convention.

The two tropical semirings are the limit ("[tropicalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropicalization)", "dequantization") of the [log semiring](wikipedia://en/37563991) as the base goes to infinity $b \to \infty$ (max-plus semiring) or to zero $b \to 0$ (min-plus semiring).

Tropical addition is [idempotent](wikipedia://en/14972), thus a tropical semiring is an example of an [idempotent semiring](wikipedia://en/371468#idempotent_semiring).

A tropical semiring is also referred to as a **tropical algebra**,[^o6] though this should not be confused with an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over a tropical semiring.

Tropical [exponentiation](wikipedia://en/99491) is defined in the usual way as iterated tropical products.

:::

## Valued fields {#Valued_fields}

The tropical semiring operations model how [valuations](wikipedia://en/347049) behave under addition and multiplication in a [valued field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valued_field). A real-valued field $K$ is a field equipped with a function

$$v:K \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}$$

which satisfies the following properties for all $a$, $b$ in $K$:

$v(a) = \infty$ if and only if $a = 0,$

$$v(ab) = v(a) + v(b) = v(a) \otimes v(b),$$

$v(a + b) \geq \min\{v(a), v(b) \} = v(a) \oplus v(b),$ with equality if $v(a) \neq v(b).$

Therefore the valuation *v* is almost a semiring homomorphism from *K* to the tropical semiring, except that the homomorphism property can fail when two elements with the same valuation are added together.

Some common valued fields:

- $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ with the trivial valuation, $v(a)=0$ for all $a\neq 0$,
- $\mathbb{Q}$ with the [p-adic valuation](wikipedia://en/16047618), $v(p^na/b)=n$ for $a,b\ne0$ and coprime to $p$,
- a non-Archimedean [local field](wikipedia://en/145375), such as the [p-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with the p-adic valuation extending the one on $\mathbb{Q}$,
- the field of [formal Laurent series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_Laurent_series) $K((t))$ (integer powers), or the field of [Puiseux series](wikipedia://en/2604429) $K\{\{t\}\}$, or the field of [Hahn series](wikipedia://en/21368973), with valuation returning the smallest exponent of $t$ appearing in the series.
