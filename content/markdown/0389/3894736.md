# Samarendra Nath Roy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Samarendra Nath Roy"}

**Samarendra Nath Roy** (11 December 1906 – 23 July 1964) was an Indian-born American mathematician and an applied statistician.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Roy was the first of three children of [Kali Nath Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Nath_Roy) and Suniti Bala Roy.[^o4] His father, was a freedom fighter and the Chief Editor of the newspaper [The Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tribune_%28Chandigarh%29), then publishing from [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore).[^o5] Roy and The Tribune published [seven allegedly seditious articles from April 3, 1919 through April 11, 1919](https://kalinathroy.wordpress.com/kali-nath-roys-seditious-articles-1919/), leading up to the April 13th massacre of the Indians at the hands of the British in the infamous [Jallianwala Bagh massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre). For this "offence" Kali Nath Roy was sentenced to imprisonment of two years along with a fine of one thousand rupees.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9]

Roy secured first division in the Matriculation Examination in 1923 from the [Khulna District School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna_Zilla_School).[^o9] He was the topper in the Intermediate Science (Higher Secondary) Examinations in 1925 from the [Daulatpur Hindu Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brajalal_College).[^o9] He obtained first class and was the topper in both the BSc Mathematics (Honours) from [Presidency College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_College,_Kolkata) of the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) in 1928 and the MSc in Mixed Mathematics - which later was renamed to Applied Mathematics - (with The Theory of Relativity as the elective) from the renowned [Rajabazar Science College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajabazar_Science_College), [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) in 1931.[^o4][^o9]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

In 1931, when Roy joined the Department of Applied Mathematics of [Rajabazar Science College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajabazar_Science_College) at the University of Calcutta as a research associate, he used computing facilities at the newly established [Indian Statistical Institute](wikipedia://en/2238418),[^o9] which was founded by Professor [P. C. Mahalanobis](wikipedia://en/359931). Roy along with several talented young scholars including [J. M. Sengupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._M._Sengupta), [H. C. Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._C._Sinha), [Raj Chandra Bose](wikipedia://en/1162266), K. R. Nair, [K. Kishen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Kishen) and [C. R. Rao](wikipedia://en/1418009), joined to form an active group of statisticians under Mahalanobis. Roy was one of the very early students of Mahalanobis, who initiated some of the early works in Statistics.[^o11] He was well known for his pioneering contribution to multivariate statistical analysis, mainly that of the Jacobians of complicated transformations for various exact distributions, rectangular coordinates and the Bartlett decomposition.[^o12] His dissertation included the Post master's work at the Indian Statistical Institute where he worked under Mahalanobis.

It was Bose who first went to the United States as a visiting professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and then joined the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) in 1947. Roy followed suit by later joining him at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill) in the spring of 1950, after initially travelling to the United States to take up a Visiting Professorship of Statistics at [Columbia University in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_in_New_York) in the spring of 1949. In between this Roy returned to India and became Head of the Department of Statistics at the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) during the academic year 1949–50.[^o9] Roy joined Bose as full Professor of Statistics in the Statistics Department at the [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill). S. N. Roy had 15 doctorate students there from 1950 till 1963.[^o14] To commemorate his Birth Centenary an International Conference on "Multivariate Statistical Methods in the 21st Century: The Legacy of Prof. S.N. Roy" was held at Kolkata, India during 28–29 December 2006.[^o15] The Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference published a special issue for celebrating the Centennial of Birth of S. N. Roy.[^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Roy was married to [Bani Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_Roy) and had four children, Prabir, Subir, Tapon and Sunanda. He died while on holiday in Jasper, Canada.
