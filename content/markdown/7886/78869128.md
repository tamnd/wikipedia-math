# Hilbert–Arnold problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert–Arnold problem"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is there a [uniform bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_bounded) on [limit cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_cycles) in generic finite-parameter families of [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_fields) on a sphere?

:::

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the **Hilbert–Arnold problem** is an [unsolved problem](wikipedia://en/183091) concerning the estimation of [limit cycles](wikipedia://en/452950) of the dynamics of a flow on a sphere, and whether the number of such cycles can be bounded. A flow on a sphere means that one can imagine that the [velocities](wikipedia://en/20491903) of particles are prescribed, and a limit cycle is a limit of those velocities, like the [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream) on the globe.[^o3] The problem asks whether in a [generic](wikipedia://en/12886758) family of smooth vector fields, smoothly parameterized over a [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) in finite dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), the number of limit cycles is uniformly bounded across all parameter values. Thus, under perturbations of climate conditions, it asks whether there is a bounded number of "Gulf Streams". The problem is historically related to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750) and was first formulated by Russian [mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematicians) [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490) and [Yulij Ilyashenko](wikipedia://en/56631455) in the 1980s.[^o4][^o5] In *[Arnold's Problems](wikipedia://en/77999374)* there are many questions related to the Hilbert–Arnold problem: 1978–6, 1979–16, 1980–1, 1983–11, 1989–17, 1990–24, 1990–25, 1994–51 and 1994–52.[^o6]

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

*Image: Saddle\_separatrix\_loop\_polycycle.svg.* Polycycle

The problem arises from considering modern approaches to [Hilbert's sixteenth problem](wikipedia://en/381750). While Hilbert's original question focused on [polynomial](wikipedia://en/373065) [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_fields), mathematical attention shifted toward properties of generic families within certain classes. Unlike polynomial systems, typical smooth systems on a sphere can have arbitrarily many hyperbolic limit cycles that persist under small [perturbations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perturbation_%28mathematics%29). However, the question of uniform boundedness across parameter families remains meaningful and forms the basis of the Hilbert–Arnold problem.[^o8]

Due to the compactness of both the parameter base and phase space, the Hilbert–Arnold problem can be reduced to a local problem studying [bifurcations](wikipedia://en/2326042) of special [degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) vector fields. This leads to the concept of polycycles—[cyclically ordered](wikipedia://en/32131620) sets of [singular points](wikipedia://en/4230456) connected by [phase curve](wikipedia://en/29298399) arcs—and their cyclicity, which measures the number of limit cycles born in bifurcations.

::: {#o10 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Bifurcation number"}

### Local Hilbert–Arnold problem {#Local_Hilbert–Arnold_problem}

The local version of the Hilbert–Arnold problem asks whether the maximum cyclicity of nontrivial polycycles in generic k-parameter families (known as the **bifurcation number** $B(k)$) is finite, and seeks explicit upper bounds.[^o11] The local Hilbert–Arnold problem has been solved for $k=1$ and $k=2$, with $B(1) = 1$ and $B(2) = 2$. For $k=3$, a solution strategy exists but remains incomplete. A simplified version considering only elementary polycycles (where all vertices are elementary singular points with at least one nonzero [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue)) has been more thoroughly studied. Ilyashenko and Yakovenko proved in 1995 that the elementary bifurcation number $E(k)$ is finite for all $k>0$.[^o12]

In 2003, mathematician [Vadim Kaloshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Kaloshin) established the explicit bound $E(k) < 25^{k^2}$.[^o13]
