# Raymond Flood (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Raymond Flood"}

**Raymond Gerard Flood** is Emeritus Fellow and a member of the Continuing Education Department at [Kellogg College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_College,_Oxford),[^o3] and has been a [Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052) at [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College).[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Flood achieved a Bachelor of Science degree at [Queens University, Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_University,_Belfast), and a master's degree at [Linacre College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linacre_College,_Oxford). He obtained his PhD from [University College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Dublin).[^o6] Flood obtained his doctorate through part-time study, as he had already acquired a family and a job.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

In 1990, Flood was made a Founding Fellow of [Kellogg College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_College,_Oxford), formally [Rewley House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewley_House). [Kellogg College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kellogg_College,_Oxford) was created to look after the interests of mature and part-time students. Flood primarily teaches those students who are either mature, or who study part-time.[^o7] He has held numerous positions at the College and the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), including Curator of the University Libraries and as a University lecturer at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o3]

Flood has dedicated much of his academic career promoting mathematics and computing to adult audiences. He has been President of the [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793) from 2006 until 2009,[^o9] and also Research Associate in the School of Theoretical Physics, [Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Institute_for_Advanced_Studies).[^o3] On [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College), Flood has said "Gresham College comes from a long tradition of liberal adult education. Allowing people from a variety of backgrounds... to get access to current thinking on the major issues of the day. Gresham College ethos is very similar to my own ethos"[^o10]

In August 2012, Flood was appointed [Gresham Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052) at [Gresham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham_College) for a period of three years, replacing [John D. Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Barrow).[^o11] During his term at the College he delivered series of free public lectures on *Shaping Modern Mathematics*,[^o12] *Applying Modern Mathematics*,[^o13] and *Great Mathematicians, Great Mathematics*.[^o14]

## Other research work and publications {#Other_research_work_and_publications}

Aside from his academic work, Flood is active in communicating mathematics and its history to non-specialist audiences. He has appeared on [BBC Radio 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_4)'s [In Our Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Our_Time_%28BBC_Radio_4%29)[^o16] and has lectured on transatlantic voyages with Queen Mary 2.[^o4]

Flood has produced and co-produced many publications and books on mathematics. Some of the most recent books with which he has been involved are *James Clerk Maxwell: Perspectives on his Life and Work* (Oxford University Press, 2014),[^o17][^o18][^o19] *The Great Mathematicians* (Arcturus, 2011),[^o20][^o21] and *Mathematics in Victorian Britain* (Oxford University Press, 2011).[^o22][^o23][^o24]
