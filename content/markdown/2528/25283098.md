# K-distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="K-distribution"}

In [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **generalized K-distribution** is a three-parameter family of continuous probability distributions. The distribution arises by compounding two [gamma distributions](wikipedia://en/207079). In each case, a re-parametrization of the usual form of the family of gamma distributions is used, such that the parameters are:

:::

- the mean of the distribution,
- the usual shape parameter.

K-distribution is a special case of [variance-gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/6534096), which in turn is a special case of [generalised hyperbolic distribution](wikipedia://en/3420188). A simpler special case of the generalized K-distribution is often referred as *the* K-distribution.

## Density {#Density}

Suppose that a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) $X$ has gamma distribution with mean $\sigma$ and shape parameter $\alpha$, with $\sigma$ being treated as a random variable having another gamma distribution, this time with mean $\mu$ and shape parameter $\beta$. The result is that $X$ has the following [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (pdf) for $x>0$:[^n1]

$$f_X(x; \mu, \alpha, \beta)= \frac{2}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)} \, \left( \frac{\alpha \beta}{\mu} \right)^{\frac{\alpha + \beta}{2}} \, x^{ \frac{\alpha + \beta}{2} - 1} K_{\alpha - \beta} \left( 2 \sqrt{\frac{\alpha \beta x}{\mu}} \right),$$

where $K$ is a [modified Bessel function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Bessel_function) of the second kind. Note that for the modified Bessel function of the second kind, we have $K_{\nu} = K_{-\nu}$. In this derivation, the K-distribution is a [compound probability distribution](wikipedia://en/28897820). It is also a [product distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_distribution):[^n1] it is the distribution of the product of two independent random variables, one having a gamma distribution with mean 1 and shape parameter $\alpha$, the second having a gamma distribution with mean $\mu$ and shape parameter $\beta$.

A simpler two parameter formalization of the K-distribution can be obtained by setting $\beta = 1$ as[^n2][^n3]

$$f_X(x; b, v)= \frac{2b}{\Gamma(v)} \left( \sqrt{bx} \right)^{v-1} K_{v-1} (2 \sqrt{bx} ),$$

where $v = \alpha$ is the shape factor, $b = \alpha/\mu$ is the scale factor, and $K$ is the modified Bessel function of second kind. The above two parameter formalization can also be obtained by setting $\alpha = 1$, $v = \beta$, and $b = \beta/\mu$, albeit with different physical interpretation of $b$ and $v$ parameters. This two parameter formalization is often referred to as *the* K-distribution, while the three parameter formalization is referred to as the generalized K-distribution.

This distribution derives from a paper by [Eric Jakeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Jakeman) and [Peter Pusey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Pusey) (1978) who used it to model microwave sea echo.[^n4] Jakeman and Tough (1987) derived the distribution from a biased random walk model.[^n5] Keith D. Ward (1981) derived the distribution from the product for two random variables, *z* = *a* *y*, where *a* has a [chi distribution](wikipedia://en/1901933) and *y* a complex Gaussian distribution. The modulus of *z*, *\|z|*, then has K-distribution.[^n6]

## [Moments](wikipedia://en/368684) {#Moments}

The moment generating function is given by[^n7]

$$M_X(s) = \left(\frac{\xi}{s}\right)^{\beta/2} \exp \left( \frac{\xi}{2s} \right) W_{-\delta/2,\gamma/2} \left(\frac{\xi}{s}\right),$$

where $\gamma = \beta - \alpha,$ $\delta = \alpha + \beta - 1,$ $\xi = \alpha \beta/\mu,$ and $W_{-\delta/2,\gamma/2}(\cdot)$ is the [Whittaker function](wikipedia://en/3036816).

The n-th moments of K-distribution is given by[^n1]

$$\mu_n = \xi^{-n} \frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n)\Gamma(\beta+n)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}.$$

So the mean and variance are given by[^n1]

$$\operatorname{E}(X)= \mu$$

$$\operatorname{var}(X)= \mu^2 \frac{\alpha+\beta+1}{\alpha \beta} .$$

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

All the properties of the distribution are symmetric in $\alpha$ and $\beta.$[^n1]

::: {#o17 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

K-distribution arises as the consequence of a statistical or probabilistic model used in [synthetic-aperture radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic-aperture_radar) (SAR) imagery. The K-distribution is formed by [compounding](wikipedia://en/28897820) two separate [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543), one representing the [radar cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross-section), and the other representing speckle that is a characteristic of coherent imaging. It is also used in wireless communication to model composite fast fading and shadowing effects.

:::
