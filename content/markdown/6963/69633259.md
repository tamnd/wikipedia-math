# Hugo Krawczyk

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hugo Krawczyk"}

**Hugo Mario Krawczyk** is an Argentine-Israeli cryptographer best known for co-inventing the [HMAC](wikipedia://en/17112) message authentication algorithm and contributing in fundamental ways to the cryptographic architecture of central Internet standards, including [IPsec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPsec), [IKE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Key_Exchange), and [SSL/TLS](wikipedia://en/187813). In particular, both IKEv2 and TLS 1.3 use Krawczyk's SIGMA protocol[^o3] as the cryptographic core of their key exchange procedures. He has also contributed foundational work in the areas of [threshold](wikipedia://en/6866642) and [proactive](wikipedia://en/27716438) cryptosystems and [searchable symmetric encryption](wikipedia://en/65133209), among others.

:::

## Education {#Education}

Krawczyk acquired a Bachelor of Arts in mathematics from the [University of Haifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Haifa). Later he received his Master of Science and Ph.D. in computer science from [Technion - Israel Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology) with [Oded Goldreich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oded_Goldreich) as doctoral thesis advisor.

## Career {#Career}

Hugo Krawczyk is a senior principal scientist at [Amazon Web Services (AWS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services). Between 2019 and 2023 he was a Principal Researcher at the [Algorand](wikipedia://en/67291815) Foundation and part of its founding team. Prior to that, he was an [IBM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Fellow) and Distinguished Research Staff Member at the [IBM T.J. Watson Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_T.J._Watson_Research_Center) in New York as a member of the Cryptography Research group from 1992 to 1997, and again from 2004 to 2019. He was an associate professor at the [Department of Electrical Engineering at the Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_Faculty_of_Electrical_Engineering) in Israel from 1997 until 2004.

Krawczyk has published over 100 papers with [more than 35,000 citations](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=-coqiSIAAAAJ), and is an inventor in 30 issued patents.

His research includes both theoretical and applied elements of cryptography, with a focus on internet security, privacy, and authentication. His most recent projects in the area include: [TLS](wikipedia://en/187813) 1.3, the new-generation SSL/TLS; [HKDF](wikipedia://en/34278662), the standard for key derivation embraced by TLS 1.3, [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28messaging_app%29), [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867), [Facebook Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Messenger), and others; and [OPAQUE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Password-authenticated_key_agreement), a password authentication protocol being standardized by the [IRTF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Research_Task_Force) and recently deployed by Facebook in its implementation of end-to-end encrypted chat backups for WhatsApp.[^o6]

Krawczyk is the author of many other cryptographic algorithms and protocols including the [HMQV](wikipedia://en/1586447) key-exchange protocol, the LFSR-based [Toeplitz Hash Algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toeplitz_Hash_Algorithm), the [Shrinking Generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrinking_generator) encryption scheme, the [UMAC](wikipedia://en/2005331) message authentication code, and the [randomized hashing](wikipedia://en/439526) scheme for strengthening digital signatures.

Other influential work includes [threshold](wikipedia://en/6866642) and [proactive](wikipedia://en/27716438) cryptosystems (including [distributed key generation](wikipedia://en/6870523)), [searchable symmetric encryption](wikipedia://en/65133209), and theoretical contributions to secure cryptographic communications, password protocols, zero knowledge and pseudorandomness.

## Awards {#Awards}

Krawczyk, a [Fellow of the International Association of Cryptologic Research (IACR)](wikipedia://en/87043) and 2017 [IBM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Fellow), has won the [RSA Conference Award for Excellence in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Award_for_Excellence_in_Mathematics) in 2015, the [Levchin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levchin_Prize)[^o8] for Contributions to Real-World Cryptography in 2018, the [ACM Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Award) in 2025,[^o9] and two IBM corporate awards (2005 and 2008). He is the recipient of two Test-of-Time awards: In 2019 for his 1996 NDSS paper,[^o10] “SKEME: A versatile secure key exchange mechanism for internet”, a precursor of KEM-based key exchange protocols, central to developing post-quantum key exchange standards, and in 2025 for his CRYPTO 2010 "Cryptographic Extraction and Key Derivation: The HKDF Scheme" paper "for formalizing key derivation and introducing HKDF, a widely adopted and standardized extract-then-expand scheme."[^o11]
