# Conjunction elimination

::: {#o1 .statement cue=infobox-mathematical-statement name="Conjunction elimination"}

**Conjunction elimination.**

If the conjunction $A$ and $B$ is true, then $A$ is true, and $B$ is true.

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Conjunction elimination"}

In [propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus), **conjunction elimination** (also called ***and*** **elimination**, **∧ elimination**,[^o3] or **simplification**)[^o4][^o5][^o6] is a [valid](wikipedia://en/21304742) [immediate inference](wikipedia://en/25379366), [argument form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argument_form) and [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311) which makes the [inference](wikipedia://en/317465) that, if the [conjunction](wikipedia://en/18152) *A and B* is true, then *A* is true, and *B* is true. The rule makes it possible to shorten longer [proofs](wikipedia://en/1197184) by deriving one of the conjuncts of a conjunction on a line by itself.

:::

An example in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language):

It's raining and it's pouring.

Therefore it's raining.

The rule consists of two separate sub-rules, which can be expressed in [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939) as:

$$\frac{P \land Q}{\therefore P}$$

and

$$\frac{P \land Q}{\therefore Q}$$

The two sub-rules together mean that, whenever an instance of "$P \land Q$" appears on a line of a proof, either "$P$" or "$Q$" can be placed on a subsequent line by itself. The above example in English is an application of the first sub-rule.

## Formal notation {#Formal_notation}

The *conjunction elimination* sub-rules may be written in [sequent](wikipedia://en/252355) notation:

$$(P \land Q) \vdash P$$

and

$$(P \land Q) \vdash Q$$

where $\vdash$ is a [metalogical](wikipedia://en/387934) symbol meaning that $P$ is a [syntactic consequence](wikipedia://en/37531624) of $P \land Q$ and $Q$ is also a syntactic consequence of $P \land Q$ in [logical system](wikipedia://en/396102);

and expressed as truth-functional [tautologies](wikipedia://en/4495335) or [theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorems) of propositional logic:

$$(P \land Q) \to P$$

and

$$(P \land Q) \to Q$$

where $P$ and $Q$ are propositions expressed in some [formal system](wikipedia://en/396102).
