# Bitwarden

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Bitwarden"}

**Bitwarden** is a [freemium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemium) [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) [password management service](wikipedia://en/4696394) that is used to store sensitive information, such as website credentials, in an [encrypted](wikipedia://en/10294) vault. It is owned and developed by Bitwarden, Inc.[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Functionalities {#Functionalities}

Bitwarden uses client-side [zero-knowledge encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-knowledge_encryption), meaning the company cannot see its users' data.[^o8] This is achieved by [end-to-end encrypting](wikipedia://en/1065362) data with [AES-CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AES-CBC) 256-bit and by using [PBKDF2](wikipedia://en/1568244) [SHA-256](wikipedia://en/1638777)/[Argon2id](wikipedia://en/48963187) to derive the encryption key.[^o9][^o10]

To [log in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Login), a user can use an email-address and password combination, [biometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric) authentication, [two-factor authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-factor_authentication) (2FA), [passkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passkey_%28authentication%29), [single sign-on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_sign-on), or [passwordless login](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passwordless_login) via notification approval on a mobile/desktop device.[^o14][^o11][^o12]

Additional client functionality includes: import of data from more than 50 password managers (such as [LastPass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LastPass), [1Password](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1Password), and [Keeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keeper_%28password_manager%29)) passkey management; export to [JSON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSON), encrypted JSON, and [CSV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comma-separated_values) formats;[^o13] a [random password generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_password_generator); [autofill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autofill) of login and other forms; integration with [email alias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Email_alias) services; ability to sync across unlimited platforms and devices; storage of an unlimited number of items; and storing a variety of information beyond [username](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_%28computing%29)-and-[password](wikipedia://en/24304) pairs, including passkeys, [TOTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-based_one-time_password) seeds, [debit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debit_card) and [credit card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_card) numbers, billing data and other identity information, and secure notes (free-form text). Each item type can be extended by custom fields and file attachments, though these are restricted by file size depending on the subscription plan.[^o14][^o15] A feature called "Send" allows sharing of end-to-end encrypted [text messages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_message) (free version) and files (paid versions). Any sent item optionally can be set with an expiration date, a maximum access limit, and a password.[^o16][^o17][^o18][^o19] The Password Checkup tool uses zxcvbn to assess [password strength](wikipedia://en/4459886).[^o20] It detects credential breaches by querying the [Have I Been Pwned?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Have_I_Been_Pwned?) database.[^o20]

Bitwarden implements credential sharing through collections within an organization. As of 2024 collections in a given organization are encrypted using the same organizational secret key, meaning that privilege separation is enforced at the authorization layer rather than through distinct encryption keys for each collection.[^o21]

## Availability {#Availability}

The platform hosts multiple client applications, including a [web interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browser_user_interface), [desktop applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desktop_application), [browser extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browser_extension), [mobile apps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app), and a [command-line interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface).[^o23] Bitwarden can be operated on web interfaces, desktop applications ([Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows), [MacOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux)), browser extensions ([Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome), [Firefox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox), [Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari_%28web_browser%29), [Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Edge), [Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_%28web_browser%29), [Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivaldi_%28web_browser%29), [Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28web_browser%29), [Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brave_%28web_browser%29) and [Tor](wikipedia://en/20556944#Tor_Browser)), or mobile apps ([Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29), [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS), [iPadOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPadOS) and [watchOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WatchOS)).[^o14] 50 languages and dialects are supported, although not all of them are available on all clients.[^o24]

The platform also offers a free US or European [cloud-hosted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_computing) synchronisation service, as well as the ability to [self-host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-hosting_%28web_services%29).[^o25][^o26][^o27][^o28]

## Compliance {#Compliance}

Bitwarden's codebases of the computer clients, the mobile apps, and the server are [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_source).[^o30] In August 2020, Bitwarden achieved [SOC 2 Type 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_and_Organization_Controls) and SOC 3 certification.[^o31][^o32] Bitwarden is compliant with [HIPAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_Insurance_Portability_and_Accountability_Act),[^o33] [GDPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Data_Protection_Regulation), [CCPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Consumer_Privacy_Act), [SOC 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOC_2), [SOC 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOC_3), and the [EU-US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU–US_Privacy_Shield) and Swiss–US Privacy Shield[^o34] frameworks.[^o35][^o36]

### Security audits {#Security_audits}

Third-party security audits are conducted annually and a [vulnerability disclosure program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_bounty_program) is also established.[^o61][^o35]

In June 2018, [Cliqz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliqz_GmbH) performed a privacy and security review of the Bitwarden for Firefox browser extension and concluded that it would not negatively impact their users.[^o69] In October of that year, Bitwarden completed a security assessment, [code audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_audit), and cryptographic analysis from third-party security auditing firm [Cure53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cure53).[^o38][^o39][^o40][^o41]

In July 2020, Bitwarden completed another security audit from security firm Insight Risk Consulting to evaluate the security of the Bitwarden network perimeter as well as penetration testing and vulnerability assessments against Bitwarden web services and applications. In August 2021, Bitwarden announced that network assessment (security assessment and penetration testing) for 2021 had been completed again by Insight Risk Consulting.[^o13][^o42]

In February 2023, Bitwarden released network security assessment and security assessment reports that were conducted by Cure53 again in May and October 2022 respectively.[^o43] The first related to penetration testing and security assessment across Bitwarden IPs, servers, and web applications.[^o44] The second related to penetration testing and source code audit against all Bitwarden password manager software components, including the core application, browser extension, desktop application, web application, and TypeScript library.[^o45] [Ghacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghacks) reported that:[^o46] No critical issues were discovered during the two audits. Two security issues that Cure53 rated high were discovered during the source code audit and penetration testing. These were fixed quickly by Bitwarden and the third-party [HubSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HubSpot). All other issues were either rated low or informational only.

## Reception {#Reception}

In January 2021, in its first password-protection program comparison, *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)* selected Bitwarden as "Best Password Manager".[^o48] A month later, with Bitwarden competitor [LastPass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LastPass) about to remove a feature from its free version, *[CNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNet)* recommended Bitwarden as the best free app for password synchronization across multiple devices,[^o49] while *[Lifehacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifehacker)* recommended it as "the best password manager for most people".[^o50]

Reviewers have praised the features offered in the software's free version, and (mostly) the low price of the premium tier compared to other managers.[^o49][^o51][^o52][^o53] The product was named the best "budget pick" in a *[Wirecutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirecutter_%28website%29)* password manager comparison.[^o54] Bitwarden's secure open-source implementation was also praised by reviewers.[^o51][^o53]

Nevertheless, *[Tom's Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom's_Guide)* found some features to be less intuitive than they could be,[^o51] while *[PC Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_Magazine)* criticized the price of the business tier as too high.[^o55]

Bitwarden was highlighted by [Süddeutsche Zeitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Süddeutsche_Zeitung) in its 2025 overview of leading self-hosted password managers as a solution that offers users increased security and full control by allowing the entire vault to be operated on their own server.[^o56]

## History {#History}

### 2016–2017 {#2016–2017}

Bitwarden debuted in August 2016 with an initial release of mobile applications for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) and [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29), browser extensions for [Chrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome) and [Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_%28web_browser%29), and a Web-based "vault" (encrypted database). The browser extension for [Firefox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefox) was later launched in February 2017.[^o59] The same month, the [Brave web browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brave_%28web_browser%29) began including the Bitwarden extension as an optional replacement password manager.[^o60] In September 2017, Bitwarden launched a [bug bounty program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_bounty_program) at [HackerOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HackerOne).[^o61][^o35]

### 2018 {#2018}

In January 2018, the Bitwarden browser extension was adapted to and released for Apple's [Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari_%28web_browser%29) browser through the Safari Extensions Gallery.[^o63] In February, Bitwarden debuted as a stand-alone desktop application for [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux), and [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows). It was built as a [web app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_application) variant of the browser extension, built with the [Electron framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_%28software_framework%29).[^o64] The Windows app was released alongside the Bitwarden extension for [Microsoft Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Edge) in the [Microsoft Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Store_%28digital%29) a month later.[^o65][^o66] In May, Bitwarden released a [command-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface) application enabling users to write scripted applications using data from their Bitwarden vaults.[^o23][^o67][^o68] In June 2018, following a review, Bitwarden was made available as an optional password manager in the [Cliqz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliqz) browser[^o69] (discontinued in 2020).

### 2022 {#2022}

In September 2022, the company announced \$100M [series B financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital_financing); the lead investor was PSG (an affiliate of [Providence Equity Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Equity_Partners)), with the existing investor [Battery Ventures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Ventures) participating.[^o71][^o72] The investment would be used to accelerate [product development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_development) and company growth to support its users and customers worldwide.[^o71][^o72]

### 2023 {#2023}

*Image: Bitwarden Passkey window screenshot.png.* Example of passwordless authentication with Bitwarden

In January, Bitwarden announced the acquisition of Swedish startup Passwordless.dev for an undisclosed amount.[^o74] Passwordless.dev provided an open-source solution allowing developers to easily implement [passwordless authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passwordless_authentication) based on the standards [WebAuthn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebAuthn) and [FIDO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDO2).[^o74][^o75]

Bitwarden also launched a [beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_release_life_cycle#Beta) software service allowing third-party developers the use of biometric sign-in technologies – including Apple's [Touch ID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch_ID) and [Face ID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_ID), and Microsoft's [Windows Hello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows_Hello) – in their apps.[^o74]

### 2024 {#2024}

On 1 May, Bitwarden launched its own [multi-factor authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-factor_authentication) app, Bitwarden Authenticator.[^o77] In October of that year, Bitwarden introduced changes to the dependencies of its desktop application to include a restricted-use [SDK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development_kit) that may prevent some members of the public from [compiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) the application from [source code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_code), provoking concerns that Bitwarden is moving away from open-source principles. Bitwarden [CTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_technical_officer) Kyle Spearrin stated in response that it is an issue they plan to resolve, and is "merely a bug".[^o78]

### 2026 {#2026}

Bitwarden raised the price of its "Premium" subscription in February 2026 from \$10 to \$20 per year, which was criticized by *[Fast Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Company)* and [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit) users for being announced in a section of a blog post covering feature updates instead of being communicated more transparently.[^o80] Bitwarden Inc. CEOs Michael Crandell and Stephen Morrison stepped down from their roles in February and April 2026, and were replaced Michael Sullivan and Michael Shenkman, respectively. In May 2026, *Fast Company* reported that Bitwarden had removed the phrase "Always free" from its marketing page for its free plan and replaced the terms "inclusion" and "transparency" from its list of principles on its hiring page with "innovation" and "trust". Sullivan's [LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn) profile touted his experience with [mergers and acquisitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mergers_and_acquisitions), particularly involving [private equity firms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_equity_firm), and prior to his appointment, he served as CEO at two companies that were involved in private equity transactions valued at \$1 billion each. After being contacted by *Fast Company*, Bitwarden restored "Always free" to the description of its free plan.[^o81]

## Security {#Security}

### 2024 evaluation of password checkup tools {#2024_evaluation_of_password_checkup_tools}

A 2024 study by Hutchinson et al. examined the “password checkup” features of 14 password managers, including Bitwarden, using weak, breached, and randomly generated passwords. The authors found that the evaluated products reported weak and compromised passwords inconsistently and sometimes incompletely. No manager successfully flagged all known breached passwords. The study concludes that such inconsistencies may give users a false sense of security.[^o20]

### 2025 DOM-based extension clickjacking {#2025_DOM-based_extension_clickjacking}

Security researcher Marek Tóth presented a vulnerability in browser extensions of several password managers (including Bitwarden) at [DEF CON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEF_CON) 33 on August 9, 2025. In their default configurations, these extensions were shown to be exposed to a DOM-based extension clickjacking technique, allowing attackers to exfiltrate user data with just a single click.[^o85] The affected password manager vendors were notified in April 2025. According to Tóth, Bitwarden version 2025.8.2 (August 31, 2025) addressed the issue.[^o86]

### 2026 Vault Compromise Under Malicious Server {#2026_Vault_Compromise_Under_Malicious_Server}

Researchers from [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) and [Università della Svizzera italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Università_della_Svizzera_italiana) found 12 attacks work against Bitwarden, 7 attacks work against [LastPass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LastPass), and 6 attacks work against [Dashlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashlane), all Cloud-based[^o88] Password Managers.[^o89][^o90][^o91]
