# Combinatorial matrix theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Combinatorial matrix theory"}

**Combinatorial matrix theory** is a branch of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) that studies [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) in terms of the patterns of nonzeros and of positive and negative values in their coefficients.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

Concepts and topics studied within combinatorial matrix theory include:

- [(0,1)-matrix](wikipedia://en/4109196), a matrix whose coefficients are all 0 or 1
- [Permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849), a (0,1)-matrix with exactly one nonzero in each row and each column
- The [Gale–Ryser theorem](wikipedia://en/42442683), on the existence of (0,1)-matrices with given row and column sums
- [Hadamard matrix](wikipedia://en/507553), a square matrix of 1 and −1 coefficients with each pair of rows having matching coefficients in exactly half of their columns
- [Alternating sign matrix](wikipedia://en/1740155), a matrix of 0, 1, and −1 coefficients with the nonzeros in each row or column alternating between 1 and −1 and summing to 1
- [Sparse matrix](wikipedia://en/341015), is a matrix with few nonzero elements, and sparse matrices of special form such as [diagonal matrices](wikipedia://en/174080) and [band matrices](wikipedia://en/1451476)
- [Sylvester's law of inertia](wikipedia://en/1127460), on the invariance of the number of negative diagonal elements of a matrix under changes of basis

Researchers in combinatorial matrix theory include [Richard A. Brualdi](wikipedia://en/22929637) and [Pauline van den Driessche](wikipedia://en/47807735).
