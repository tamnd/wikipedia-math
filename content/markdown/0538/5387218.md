# Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy"}

***Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy*** is a book (1919 first edition[^o3]) by philosopher [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163), in which the author seeks to create an accessible introduction to various topics within the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358). According to the preface, the book is intended for those with only limited knowledge of mathematics and no prior experience with the [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) it deals with.[^o4] Accordingly, it is often used in introductory [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) courses at institutions of higher education.[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Background {#Background}

*Introduction to Mathematical Philosophy* was written while Russell was serving time in [Brixton Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Prison_Brixton) due to his anti-war activities.[^o8]

## Contents {#Contents}

The book deals with a wide variety of topics within the philosophy of mathematics and mathematical logic including the logical basis and definition of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474), [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), [limits](wikipedia://en/26551602) and [continuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuity_%28mathematics%29), and [classes](wikipedia://en/20696).[^o10]
