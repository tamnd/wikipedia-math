# Albert Ingham

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Ingham"}

**Albert Edward Ingham** FRS (3 April 1900 – 6 September 1967) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o4]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Ingham was born in [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton). He went to [King Edward VI Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_High_School,_Stafford) in [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford) and began his studies at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge) in January 1919 after service in the British Army in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I).[^o3] Ingham received a distinction as a Wrangler in the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) at Cambridge. He was elected a fellow of Trinity in 1922. He also received an [1851 Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_Research_Fellowship).[^o7]

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Ingham was appointed a [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) at the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) in 1926 and returned to Cambridge as a fellow of [King's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College,_Cambridge) and lecturer in 1930. Ingham was appointed after the death of [Frank Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Ramsey_%28mathematician%29).

Ingham supervised the [PhDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) of [C. Brian Haselgrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Brian_Haselgrove), [Wolfgang Fuchs](wikipedia://en/5397705) and [Christopher Hooley](wikipedia://en/5380265).

Ingham proved in 1937[^o10] that if

$$\zeta\left(1/2+it\right)=O\left(t^c\right)$$

for some positive constant *c*, then

$$\pi\left(x+x^\theta\right)-\pi(x)\sim\frac{x^\theta}{\log x},$$

for any θ > (1+4c)/(2+4c). Here ζ denotes the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809) and π the [prime-counting function](wikipedia://en/331826).

Using the best published value for *c* at the time, an immediate consequence of his result was that

*g*<sub>*n*</sub> \< *p*<sub>*n*</sub><sup>5/8</sup>,

where *p*<sub>*n*</sub> the *n*-th [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) and *g*<sub>*n*</sub> = *p*<sub>*n*+1</sub> − *p*<sub>*n*</sub> denotes the *n*-th [prime gap](wikipedia://en/5075259).

Ingham retired from teaching in 1959.[^o7]

## Honours {#Honours}

Ingham was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 1945.[^o7]

## Marriage and children {#Marriage_and_children}

Ingham married [Rose Marie "Jane" TupperCarey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Ingham) in 1932. They had two sons.

## Death {#Death}

Ingham died in Switzerland in 1967, aged 67.[^o7]

## Publications {#Publications}

Ingham's sole book, *On the Distribution of Prime Numbers*, was published in 1932.[^o7]
