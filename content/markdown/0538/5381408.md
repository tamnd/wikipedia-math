# Engineering mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Engineering mathematics"}

**Engineering Mathematics** is a branch of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), concerning [mathematical methods and techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_models) that are typically used in [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing). Along with fields like [engineering physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_physics) and [engineering geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_geology), both of which may belong in the wider category [engineering science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_science), engineering mathematics is an [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinary) subject motivated by engineers' needs both for [practical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical), [theoretical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical) and other considerations outside their specialization, and to deal with constraints to be effective in their work.

:::

## Description {#Description}

Historically, engineering mathematics consisted mostly of [applied analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), most notably: [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations); [real](wikipedia://en/26478) and [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) (including [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_analysis) and [tensor analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_analysis)); [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) (broadly construed, to include [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/641995), [variational](wikipedia://en/171882), and [perturbative methods](wikipedia://en/102140), [representations](wikipedia://en/6355948), [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506)); [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659); [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342); as well as [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) and applied [probability](wikipedia://en/22934), outside of analysis. These areas of mathematics were intimately tied to the development of [Newtonian physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newtonian_physics), and the [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) of that period. This history also left a legacy: until the early 20th century subjects such as [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) were often taught in applied mathematics departments at American universities, and [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988) may still be taught in (applied) mathematics as well as engineering departments.[^o3]

The success of modern numerical computer methods and software has led to the emergence of [computational mathematics](wikipedia://en/10433833), [computational science](wikipedia://en/1181008), and [computational engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_engineering) (the last two are sometimes lumped together and abbreviated as **CS&E**), which occasionally use [high-performance computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-performance_computing) for the [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation) of phenomena and the solution of problems in the sciences and engineering. These are often considered interdisciplinary fields, but are also of interest to engineering mathematics.[^o4]

Specialized branches include [engineering optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_optimization) and [engineering statistics](wikipedia://en/9546).

Engineering mathematics in [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) typically consists of mathematical methods and models courses.[^o5]
