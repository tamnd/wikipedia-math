# Skew polygon

*Image: Disphenoid tetrahedron.png.* The red edges of this [tetragonal disphenoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragonal_disphenoid) represent a regular zig-zag skew quadrilateral.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skew polygon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **skew polygon** is a closed [polygonal chain](wikipedia://en/12056032) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). It is a [figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_%28geometry%29) similar to a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) except its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are not all [coplanar](wikipedia://en/1508434).[^n1] While a polygon is ordinarily defined as a [plane figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_figure), the [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) and vertices of a skew polygon form a [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve). Skew polygons must have at least four vertices. The *interior* [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28geometry%29) and corresponding area measure of such a polygon is not uniquely defined.

:::

[Skew infinite polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skew_infinite_polygon) (apeirogons) have vertices which are not all colinear.

A **zig-zag skew polygon** or **[antiprismatic](wikipedia://en/1851) polygon**[^o3] has vertices which alternate on two parallel planes, and thus must be even-sided.

**Regular skew polygons** in 3 dimensions (and regular skew apeirogons in two dimensions) are always zig-zag.

## Skew polygons in three dimensions {#Skew_polygons_in_three_dimensions}

*Image: Dodecagonal antiprism.png.* A uniform *n*-gonal [antiprism](wikipedia://en/1851) has a 2*n*-sided regular skew polygon defined along its side edges.

A **regular skew polygon** is a faithful symmetric realization of a polygon in dimension greater than 2. In 3 dimensions a regular skew polygon has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A regular skew $n$-gon can be given a [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $p\#$ as a *blend* of a [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) $p\}$ and an orthogonal [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) { }.[^o5] The symmetry operation between sequential vertices is [glide reflection](wikipedia://en/412984).

Examples are shown on the uniform square and pentagon antiprisms. The [star antiprisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_antiprism) also generate regular skew polygons with different connection order of the top and bottom polygons. The filled top and bottom polygons are drawn for structural clarity, and are not part of the skew polygons.

[Petrie polygons](wikipedia://en/18571869) are regular skew polygons defined within regular polyhedra and polytopes. For example, the five [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) have 4-, 6-, and 10-sided regular skew polygons, as seen in these [orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) with red edges around their respective [projective envelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_envelope). The tetrahedron and the octahedron include all the vertices in their respective zig-zag skew polygons, and can be seen as a digonal antiprism and a triangular antiprism respectively.

## Regular skew polygon as vertex figure of regular skew polyhedron {#Regular_skew_polygon_as_vertex_figure_of_regular_skew_polyhedron}

A [regular skew polyhedron](wikipedia://en/19193037) has regular polygon faces, and a regular skew polygon [vertex figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_figures).

Three infinite regular skew polyhedra are [space-filling](wikipedia://en/45592828) in 3-space; others [exist in 4-space](wikipedia://en/19193037#Finite_regular_skew_polyhedra_of_4-space), some within the [uniform 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1645899).

## Regular skew polygons in four dimensions {#Regular_skew_polygons_in_four_dimensions}

In 4 dimensions, a regular skew polygon can have vertices on a [Clifford torus](wikipedia://en/7950934) and related by a [Clifford displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_displacement). Unlike zig-zag skew polygons, skew polygons on double rotations can include an odd-number of sides.

The [Petrie polygons](wikipedia://en/18571869) of the [regular 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/18801624) define regular zig-zag skew polygons. The [Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_number) for each [coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004) symmetry expresses how many sides a Petrie polygon has. This is 5 sides for a [5-cell](wikipedia://en/513821), 8 sides for a [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) and [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422), 12 sides for a [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485), and 30 sides for a [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) and [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447).

When orthogonally projected onto the [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane), these regular skew polygons appear as regular polygon envelopes in the plane.
