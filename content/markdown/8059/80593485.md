# Markov Processes and Potential Theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov Processes and Potential Theory"}

***Markov Processes and Potential Theory*** is a mathematics book written by [Robert McCallum Blumenthal](wikipedia://en/56586935) and [Ronald Getoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Getoor). It was first published in 1968 by [Academic Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Press), and remained an influential reference work for several decades. Getoor, in his obituary of Blumenthal, referred to the book as "undoubtedly, our best known work".

:::

## Background {#Background}

*Markov Processes and Potential Theory* is based on, and extends, [Gilbert Hunt](wikipedia://en/41911457)'s work on [probabilistic potential theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_potential_theory), in particular his three-part paper "Markoff Processes and Potentials", which was published in the *[Illinois Journal of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/61381311)* in 1957–1958. Blumenthal wrote his doctoral thesis with Hunt at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1956, and that year started working at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), where he met Getoor.

In a 1980 interview with [Eugene Dynkin](wikipedia://en/1351540), Getoor said that he and Blumenthal began studying Hunt's papers together in 1960, and by 1965 had decided to write a book on the topic. They had written a draft by 1966, and finished the book by correspondence in 1966–1967 while Blumenthal was working in Germany.

## Content {#Content}

The main object of study in *Markov Processes and Potential Theory* is the standard process, a slight generalisation of the [Hunt process](wikipedia://en/4888399), which [Joanna Mitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Mitro) describes as "the largest class of Markov processes for which there was well-developed associated potential theory at that time." The chapters are: Markov processes; Excessive functions; Multiplicative functionals and subprocesses; Additive functionals and their potentials; Further properties of continuous additive functionals; Dual processes and potential theory

Chapter I covers the [Markov property](wikipedia://en/306422) and [strong Markov property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_Markov_property), [Markov kernels](wikipedia://en/21180014), standard and Hunt processes, and [measurability](wikipedia://en/44055) of [hitting times](wikipedia://en/7059985). Theorem 9.4 contains the fact that a [Feller semigroup](wikipedia://en/6891551) induces a Hunt process, and Theorem 10.6 is [Gustave Choquet](wikipedia://en/17301193)'s capacity theorem. The chapter introduces the notation $(\Omega, \mathcal F, \mathcal F_t, X_t, \theta_t, P_x)$ for a Markov process, which was widely adopted.

Chapter II addresses the content of Hunt's "Markoff Processes and Potentials I", and includes [excessive functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excessive_function) and the [fine topology](wikipedia://en/5170051). Chapter III covers Hunt's "Markoff Processes and Potentials II", and includes what [Paul-André Meyer](wikipedia://en/3653083) calls the "main [balayage](wikipedia://en/21337035) theorem" (Theorem 6.12), as well as subprocesses and [multiplicative functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_functional). Chapter IV introduces [additive functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_functional), and Chapter V applies various contemporary theorems to them, including the work of Minoru Motoo, Edward S. Boylan, and [Henry McKean](wikipedia://en/35051081). Chapter VI is concerned with Hunt's "Markoff Processes and Potentials III", and covers [dual processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_process_%28probability%29), [potentials of measures](wikipedia://en/814816), and [capacity](wikipedia://en/14368398).

## Reception {#Reception}

### Contemporary reception {#Contemporary_reception}

*Markov Processes and Potential Theory* was positively received in contemporary reviews. In a 1970 review for the *[Annals of Mathematical Statistics](wikipedia://en/23772805)*, [Harry Dym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dym) described the book as "impressive and important" and wrote that it "will surely serve as a basic reference on Markov processes and potential theory for years to come." [Paul-André Meyer](wikipedia://en/3653083) wrote in a 1969 review for the *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)* that "every mathematician interested in time continuous Markov processes should know this book." Statistician [M. S. Bartlett](wikipedia://en/865429) was critical of the level of abstraction, writing in his 1972 review for *[the Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)* that "many branches of pure mathematics, including measure-theoretic probability, are becoming too rarefied to have any very discernible connection with the applied problems which generated them in the first instance".

### Legacy {#Legacy}

In 1989 [Chris Rogers](wikipedia://en/22806119) wrote: "Some 21 years ago, the celebrated volume *Markov processes and potential theory* by Blumenthal and Getoor was published. Since then, it has become one of the most frequently cited books in the subject". The book is often referenced in later probability theory books; [Kai Lai Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Lai_Chung) and John B. Walsh call it "the standard reference on duality" in their book *Markov Processes, Brownian Motion, and Time Symmetry*, and in *Continuous Martingales and Brownian Motion*, [Daniel Revuz](wikipedia://en/61125654) and [Marc Yor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Yor) write that "the basic reference for additive functionals is the book of Blumenthal and Getoor from which most of our proofs \[for the chapter\] are borrowed".

*Markov Processes and Potential Theory* was in part superseded by Michael Sharpe's 1988 book *General Theory of Markov Processes*, which treated the more general [Borel right process](wikipedia://en/12086637) and also covered topics that Blumenthal and Getoor had not, including the Ray–Knight compactification, Lévy systems, characteristic measures, the [Martin boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_boundary), and excursion theory.
