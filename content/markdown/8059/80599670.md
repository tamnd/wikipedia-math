# Ron Doney

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ron Doney"}

**Ronald Arthur Doney** is a British mathematician. He is Emeritus Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester) and a specialist in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).[^o3]

:::

Doney completed his PhD at [Durham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University) in 1964 under the supervision of G. E. H. Reuter.[^o4] He worked briefly at the [University of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_East_Anglia), before joining [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) as a lecturer in 1965. In 1970, he moved to the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester), where he spent the remainder of his academic career.[^o5]

In the mid-1970s, Doney published a series of papers on the growth properties of general branching processes, and often collaborated with [Nicholas Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Bingham).[^o5] From 1977 onward, he returned primarily to the study of [random walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_walks).[^o5]

During the 1990s, he had a 'relatively intense' collaboration with French probabilist [Jean Bertoin](wikipedia://en/56969293). This emerged from Bertoin noticing Doney’s 1991 paper on [Lévy processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lévy_processes) in the *[Journal of the London Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_London_Mathematical_Society)*. They eventually co-authored seven papers on conditioned random walks, six of which appeared between 1994 and 1997.[^o5]
