# Klaus Wilhelm Roggenkamp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Klaus Wilhelm Roggenkamp"}

**Klaus Wilhelm Roggenkamp** (24 December 1940 – 23 July 2021[^o2]) was a German mathematician, specializing in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

As an undergraduate, Roggenkamp studied mathematics from 1960 to 1964 at the [University of Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen).[^o4] There in 1967 he received his PhD. His thesis *Darstellungen endlicher Gruppen in Polynombereichen* (*Representations of finite groups in polynomial integral domains*) was written under the supervision of [Hermann Boerner](wikipedia://en/44021499). As a postdoc, Roggenkamp was at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign), where he studied under [Irving Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Reiner), and at the [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal). After four years as a professor at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), he was appointed to the chair of algebra at the [University of Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Stuttgart).[^o4]

Roggenkamp and Leonard Lewy Scott collaborated on a long series of papers on the [groups of units](wikipedia://en/289666) of [integral](wikipedia://en/14563) [group rings](wikipedia://en/349223), dealing with problems connected with the "integral isomorphism problem", which was proposed by [Graham Higman](wikipedia://en/2600152) in his 1940 doctoral dissertation at the University of Oxford.[^o6][^o7] In 1986 Roggenkamp and Scott [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) their most famous theorem (published in 1987 in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)*). Their theorem states that given two [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) $G$ and $H$, if $Z$$G$ is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to $Z$$H$ then $G$ is isomorphic to $H$, in the case where $G$ and $H$ are finite [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) over the [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer), and also in the case where $G$ and $H$ are finite [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569). Their 1987 paper also established a very strong form of a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) made by [Hans Zassenhaus](wikipedia://en/1376825). The papers of Roggenkamp and Scott were the basis for most developments which followed in the study of finite groups of units of integral group rings.[^o4]

In 1988 Roggenkamp and Scott found a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to another conjecture by Hans Zassenhaus — the conjecture was a somewhat strengthened form of the conjecture that the "integral isomorphism problem" always has an affirmative solution.[^o8] Martin Hertweck, partly building on the techniques introduced by Roggenkamp and Scott for their counterexample, published a counterexample to the conjecture that the "integral isomorphism problem" can always be solved affirmatively.[^o9][^o10]

A series of joint papers of Klaus Roggenkamp and [Karl Gruenberg](wikipedia://en/57806841) centers around [homological](wikipedia://en/139410) considerations of groups and connections to homological questions of group rings. In particular, the authors studied the relation module of a group, i.e. the [abelianised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelianisation) kernel of a minimal [presentation](wikipedia://en/99494) of a group. Various applications were given, among others, to questions about units in integral group rings. Klaus Roggenkamp managed to clarify completely the structure of blocks of *p*-adic group rings with [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) defect group, thus establishing an integral analogue of the celebrated theory of [Brauer tree algebras](wikipedia://en/29573353). Many applications are known and more are on the way, from equivalences between derived categories to the inverse problem of [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316). A new branch of [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) is created by Klaus Roggenkamp’s most recent research on higher-dimensional orders. Motivated by recent developments in the representation theory of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924), [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582), [Hecke algebras](wikipedia://en/55907189) and [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678), Klaus Roggenkamp had started to study orders over two and higher-dimensional coefficient domains.[^o4]

Roggenkamp was elected a member of the *Akademie gemeinnütziger Wissenschaften zu Erfurt* (Erfurt Academy of Sciences) and was made an honorary member of [Ovidius University of Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovidius_University) in Romania.
