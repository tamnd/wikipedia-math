# Signature matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signature matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **signature matrix** is a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) whose diagonal elements are either 1 or -1, that is, any matrix of the form:[^o2]

:::

$$A=\begin{pmatrix} \pm 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \pm 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \pm 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & \pm 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Any signature matrix is its own [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_matrix), hence it is an [involutory matrix](wikipedia://en/7917643). It is consequently a [square root](wikipedia://en/2929701) of the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718).

Since signature matrices are both [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) and involutory, they are [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/105620). Consequently, any [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) corresponding to a signature matrix constitutes an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777). Geometrically, signature matrices represent a [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829) in each of the axes corresponding to the negated rows or columns.

If $A$ is an $\times$ signature matrix, then:

- The [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of $A$ is either 1 or -1 (since it is diagonal), and
- $-n \leq \operatorname{tr}(A)\leq n$ (since the diagonal values are either -1 or 1).

:::
