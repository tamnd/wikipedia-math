# Metzler matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Metzler matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Metzler matrix** is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) in which all the off-diagonal components are nonnegative (equal to or greater than zero):

:::

$$\forall_{i\neq j}\, x_{ij} \geq 0.$$

It is named after the American economist [Lloyd Metzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Metzler).

Metzler matrices appear in stability analysis of [time delayed differential equations](wikipedia://en/3941333) and positive [linear dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/4786318). Their properties can be derived by applying the properties of [nonnegative matrices](wikipedia://en/3945884) to matrices of the form *M* + *aI*, where *M* is a Metzler matrix.

## Definition and terminology {#Definition_and_terminology}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), especially [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) is called **Metzler**, **quasipositive** (or **quasi-positive**) or **essentially nonnegative** if all of its elements are [non-negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-negative) except for those on the main diagonal, which are unconstrained. That is, a Metzler matrix is any matrix *A* which satisfies

$$A=(a_{ij});\quad a_{ij}\geq 0, \quad i\neq j.$$

Metzler matrices are also sometimes referred to as $Z^{(-)}$-matrices, as a [*Z*-matrix](wikipedia://en/7838302) is equivalent to a negated quasipositive matrix.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The [exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) of a Metzler (or quasipositive) matrix is a [nonnegative matrix](wikipedia://en/3945884) because of the corresponding property for the exponential of a nonnegative matrix. This is natural, once one observes that the generator matrices of [continuous-time Markov chains](wikipedia://en/1125342) are always Metzler matrices, and that probability distributions are always non-negative.

A Metzler matrix has an [eigenvector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvector) in the nonnegative [orthant](wikipedia://en/1283725) because of the corresponding property for nonnegative matrices.
