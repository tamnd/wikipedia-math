# Jean Pedersen

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jean Pedersen"}

*Image: PedersenJeanJ MFO3251.jpg.* Jean J. Pedersen

**Jean J. Pedersen** (Sep 17, 1934–Jan 1, 2016) was an American mathematician and author particularly known for her works on the [mathematics of paper folding](wikipedia://en/232840).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Pedersen was born Thelma Jean Jorgenson in [Salt Lake City, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City,_Utah), the daughter of an ophthalmologist and a teacher. She studied [home economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_economics) changing to a double major in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) as an undergraduate at [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University), before becoming a graduate student in mathematics at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah) under the supervision of E. Allen Davis.

After completing her [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree), she moved to [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California), following her husband who worked for [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM). She joined the faculty at the [Santa Clara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_University) on a part-time basis in 1966, but shifted to full-time and was promoted to full professor in 1996. She was the first woman to teach mathematics at the university, and the first to be tenured as a mathematics professor.

Her discovery that the [platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids) could be braided from strips of paper led to [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435) writing about it in the September, 1971 [Mathematical Games column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Games_column) in [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American).[^o3]

## Books {#Books}

Pedersen's books include:

- *Geometric Playthings* (With Kent Pedersen, Dale Seymour Publications Secondary, 1973, )
- *Fear No More: An Adult Approach to Mathematics* (with [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), Dale Seymour Publications, 1982 )
- *Build Your Own Polyhedra* (with [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), Addison-Wesley, 1988)
- *Mathematical Reflections: In a Room with Many Windows* (with [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286) and Derek Holton, Springer, 1996)
- *Mathematical Vistas: From a Room with Many Windows* (with [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286) and Derek Holton, Springerl 2002)
- *[99 Points of Intersection: Examples—Pictures—Proofs](wikipedia://en/63044352)* (by Hans Walser, translated with Peter Hilton, Mathematical Association of America, 2006)
- *A Mathematical Tapestry: Demonstrating the Beautiful Unity of Mathematics* (with [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), illustrated by Sylvie Donmoyer, Cambridge University Press, 2010)

She and [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286) also translated *The Golden Section* and *Symmetry* by Hans Walser from German into English. Both translations were published by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) in 2001.
