::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Rhombohedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rhombohedron** (also called a **rhombic hexahedron**[^o4][^o5] or, inaccurately, a **rhomboid**) is a special case of a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) in which all six faces are congruent [rhombi](wikipedia://en/183884).[^o6] It can be used to define the [rhombohedral lattice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombohedral_lattice_system), a [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) with rhombohedral cells. A rhombohedron has two opposite [apices](wikipedia://en/6022079) at which all face angles are equal; a **prolate rhombohedron** has this common angle acute, and an **oblate rhombohedron** has an obtuse angle at these vertices. A [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) is a special case of a rhombohedron with all sides [square](wikipedia://en/659939).

:::

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

The common angle at the two apices is here given as $\theta$. There are two general forms of the rhombohedron: oblate (flattened) and prolate (stretched).

| *Image: Rhombohedron-oblate.svg.* | *Image: Prolate rhombohedron.svg.* |
|---|---|
| *Oblate rhombohedron* | *Prolate rhombohedron* |

In the oblate case $\theta > 90^\circ$ and in the prolate case $\theta < 90^\circ$. For $\theta = 90^\circ$ the figure is a cube.

Certain proportions of the rhombs give rise to some well-known special cases. These typically occur in both prolate and oblate forms.

## Solid geometry {#Solid_geometry}

For a unit (i.e.: with side length 1) rhombohedron,[^o11] with rhombic acute angle $\theta~$, with one vertex at the origin (0, 0, 0), and with one edge lying along the x-axis, the three generating vectors are

***e<sub>1</sub>*** : $\biggl(1, 0, 0\biggr),$

***e<sub>2</sub>*** : $\biggl(\cos\theta, \sin\theta, 0\biggr),$

***e<sub>3</sub>*** : $\biggl(\cos\theta, {\cos\theta-\cos^2\theta\over \sin\theta}, {\sqrt{1-3\cos^2\theta+2\cos^3\theta} \over \sin\theta} \biggr).$

The other coordinates can be obtained from vector addition[^o12] of the 3 direction vectors: ***e<sub>1</sub>*** + ***e<sub>2</sub>*** , ***e<sub>1</sub>*** + ***e<sub>3</sub>*** , ***e<sub>2</sub>*** + ***e<sub>3</sub>*** , and ***e<sub>1</sub>*** + ***e<sub>2</sub>*** + ***e<sub>3</sub>*** .

The volume $V$ of a rhombohedron, in terms of its side length $a$ and its rhombic acute angle $\theta~$, is a simplification of the volume of a [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975), and is given by

$$V = a^3(1-\cos\theta)\sqrt{1+2\cos\theta} = a^3\sqrt{(1-\cos\theta)^2(1+2\cos\theta)} = a^3\sqrt{1-3\cos^2\theta+2\cos^3\theta}~.$$

We can express the volume $V$ another way :

$$V = 2\sqrt{3} ~ a^3 \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \sqrt{1-\frac{4}{3}\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}~.$$

As the area of the (rhombic) base is given by $a^2\sin\theta~$, and as the height of a rhombohedron is given by its volume divided by the area of its base, the height $h$ of a rhombohedron in terms of its side length $a$ and its rhombic acute angle $\theta$ is given by

$$h = a~{(1-\cos\theta)\sqrt{1+2\cos\theta} \over \sin\theta} = a~{\sqrt{1-3\cos^2\theta+2\cos^3\theta} \over \sin\theta}~.$$

Note:

$h = a~z$<sub>*3*</sub> , where $z$<sub>*3*</sub> is the third coordinate of ***e<sub>3</sub>*** .

The body diagonal between the acute-angled vertices is the longest. By rotational symmetry about that diagonal, the other three body diagonals, between the three pairs of opposite obtuse-angled vertices, are all the same length.

### Relation to orthocentric tetrahedra {#Relation_to_orthocentric_tetrahedra}

Four points forming non-adjacent vertices of a rhombohedron necessarily form the four vertices of an [orthocentric tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/34286384), and all orthocentric tetrahedra can be formed in this way.[^o14]
