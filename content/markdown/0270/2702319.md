# Nuclear operators between Banach spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nuclear operators between Banach spaces"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **nuclear operators between Banach spaces** are a [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) between [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) in infinite dimensions that share some of the properties of their counter-part in finite dimension. In [Hilbert spaces](wikipedia://en/20598932) such operators are usually called [trace class operators](wikipedia://en/192266) and one can define such things as the [trace](wikipedia://en/43270). In Banach spaces this is no longer possible for general nuclear operators, it is however possible for $\tfrac{2}{3}$-nuclear operator via the [Grothendieck trace theorem](wikipedia://en/73169693).

:::

The general definition for [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) was given by [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck). This article presents both cases but concentrates on the general case of nuclear operators on Banach spaces.

## Nuclear operators on Hilbert spaces {#Nuclear_operators_on_Hilbert_spaces}

An operator $\mathcal L$ on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\mathcal H$

$$\mathcal{L} : \mathcal{H} \to \mathcal{H}$$

is [compact](wikipedia://en/577441) if it can be written in the form

$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{n=1}^N \rho_n \langle f_n, \cdot \rangle g_n,$$

where $1 \leq N \leq \infty,$ and $\{f_1, \ldots, f_N\}$ and $\{g_1, \ldots, g_N\}$ are (not necessarily complete) orthonormal sets. Here $\{\rho_1, \ldots, \rho_N\}$ is a set of real numbers, the set of [singular values](wikipedia://en/709116) of the operator, obeying $\rho_n \to 0$ if $N = \infty.$

The bracket $\langle\cdot, \cdot\rangle$ is the scalar product on the Hilbert space; the sum on the right hand side must converge in norm.

An operator that is compact as defined above is said to be nuclear or trace-class if

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty |\rho_n| < \infty.$$

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

A nuclear operator on a Hilbert space has the important property that a [trace](wikipedia://en/192266) operation may be defined. Given an orthonormal basis $\{\psi_n\}$ for the Hilbert space, the trace is defined as

$$\operatorname{Tr} \mathcal {L} = \sum_n \langle \psi_n, \mathcal{L} \psi_n \rangle.$$

Obviously, the sum converges absolutely, and it can be proven that the result is independent of the basis. It can be shown that this trace is identical to the sum of the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{L}$ (counted with multiplicity).

:::

## Nuclear operators on Banach spaces {#Nuclear_operators_on_Banach_spaces}

The definition of trace-class operator was extended to [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in 1955.

Let $A$ and $B$ be Banach spaces, and $A^{\prime}$ be the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) of $A,$ that is, the set of all [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28topology%29) or (equivalently) [bounded linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_functional) on $A$ with the usual norm. There is a canonical evaluation map

$$A^{\prime} \otimes B \to \operatorname{Hom}(A, B)$$

(from the [projective tensor product](wikipedia://en/63566617) of $A$ and $B$ to the Banach space of continuous linear maps from $A$ to $B$). It is determined by sending $f \in A^{\prime}$ and $b \in B$ to the linear map $a \mapsto f(a) \cdot b.$ An operator $\mathcal L \in \operatorname{Hom}(A,B)$ is called nuclear if it is in the image of this evaluation map.[^o6]

### $q$-nuclear operators {#-nuclear_operators}

An operator

$$\mathcal{L} : A \to B$$

is said to be nuclear of order $q$ if there exist sequences of vectors $\{g_n\} \in B$ with $\Vert g_n \Vert \leq 1,$ functionals $\left\{f^*_n\right\} \in A^{\prime}$ with $\Vert f^*_n \Vert \leq 1$ and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\{\rho_n\}$ with

$$\sum_n |\rho_n|^q < \infty,$$

such that the operator may be written as

$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_n \rho_n f^*_n(\cdot) g_n$$

with the sum converging in the operator norm.

Operators that are nuclear of order 1 are called nuclear operators: these are the ones for which the series $\sum \rho_n$ is absolutely convergent. Nuclear operators of order 2 are called [Hilbert–Schmidt operators](wikipedia://en/976673).

### Relation to trace-class operators {#Relation_to_trace-class_operators}

With additional steps, a trace may be defined for such operators when $A = B.$

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

The trace and determinant can no longer be defined in general in Banach spaces. However they can be defined for the so-called $\tfrac{2}{3}$-nuclear operators via [Grothendieck trace theorem](wikipedia://en/73169693).

:::

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

### Generalizations {#Generalizations}

More generally, an operator from a [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) $A$ to a Banach space $B$ is called nuclear if it satisfies the condition above with all $f_n^*$ bounded by 1 on some fixed neighborhood of 0.

An extension of the concept of nuclear maps to arbitrary [monoidal categories](wikipedia://en/330604) is given by (Stolz Teichner 2012). A monoidal category can be thought of as a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) equipped with a suitable notion of a tensor product. An example of a monoidal category is the category of Banach spaces or alternatively the category of locally convex, complete, Hausdorff spaces; both equipped with the projective tensor product. A map $f : A \to B$ in a monoidal category is called thick if it can be written as a composition

$$A \cong I \otimes A \stackrel{t \otimes \operatorname{id}_A} \longrightarrow B \otimes C \otimes A \stackrel{\operatorname{id}_B \otimes s} \longrightarrow B \otimes I \cong B$$

for an appropriate object $C$ and maps $t: I \to B \otimes C, s: C \otimes A \to I,$ where $I$ is the monoidal unit.

In the monoidal category of Banach spaces, equipped with the projective tensor product, a map is thick if and only if it is nuclear.[^o14]

:::

::: {#o16 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Suppose that $f : H_1 \to H_2$ and $g : H_2 \to H_3$ are [Hilbert-Schmidt operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert-Schmidt_operator) between Hilbert spaces. Then the composition $g \circ f : H_1 \to H_3$ is a [nuclear operator](wikipedia://en/63684284).[^n1]

:::
