# Composition operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Composition operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **composition operator** $C_\phi$ with symbol $\phi$ is a [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) defined by the rule

$$C_\phi (f) = f \circ \phi$$

where $f \circ \phi$ denotes [function composition](wikipedia://en/195947). It is also encountered in composition of [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) in [permutation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_groups).

:::

The study of composition operators is covered by [AMS category 47B33](https://web.archive.org/web/20090418144227/https://www.ams.org/msc/47Bxx.html).

## In physics {#In_physics}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and especially the area of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the composition operator is usually referred to as the **Koopman operator**, named after [Bernard Koopman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Koopman).[^o3][^o4] Its wild surge in popularity[^o5] is sometimes jokingly called "Koopmania"[^o6] - a popularity that has begun spreading in Machine Learning as well.[^o7] It is the [left-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-adjoint) of the [transfer operator](wikipedia://en/1346096) of Frobenius–Perron.

## In Borel functional calculus {#In_Borel_functional_calculus}

Using the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the composition operator is a [pull-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pull-back) on the space of [measurable functions](wikipedia://en/44055); it is adjoint to the [transfer operator](wikipedia://en/1346096) in the same way that the pull-back is adjoint to the [push-forward](wikipedia://en/9412979); the composition operator is the [inverse image functor](wikipedia://en/7086661).

Since the domain considered here is that of [Borel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_function), the above describes the Koopman operator as it appears in [Borel functional calculus](wikipedia://en/876294).

## In holomorphic functional calculus {#In_holomorphic_functional_calculus}

The [domain](wikipedia://en/50263) of a composition operator can be taken more narrowly, as some [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), often consisting of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110): for example, some [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) or [Bergman space](wikipedia://en/3205927). In this case, the composition operator lies in the realm of some [functional calculus](wikipedia://en/965348), such as the [holomorphic functional calculus](wikipedia://en/3131706).

Interesting questions posed in the study of composition operators often relate to how the [spectral properties](wikipedia://en/206137) of the operator depend on the [function space](wikipedia://en/340630). Other questions include whether $C_\phi$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/577441) or [trace-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace-class); answers typically depend on how the function $\varphi$ behaves on the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of some domain.

When the transfer operator is a left-[shift operator](wikipedia://en/467384), the Koopman operator, as its adjoint, can be taken to be the right-shift operator. An appropriate basis, explicitly manifesting the shift, can often be found in the [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718). When these are orthogonal on the real number line, the shift is given by the [Jacobi operator](wikipedia://en/28868152).[^o10] When the polynomials are orthogonal on some region of the complex plane (viz, in [Bergman space](wikipedia://en/3205927)), the Jacobi operator is replaced by a [Hessenberg operator](wikipedia://en/519204).[^o11]

## Applications {#Applications}

In mathematics, composition operators commonly occur in the study of [shift operators](wikipedia://en/467384), for example, in the [Beurling–Lax theorem](wikipedia://en/14104402) and the [Wold decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wold_decomposition). Shift operators can be studied as one-dimensional [spin lattices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_lattice). Composition operators appear in the theory of [Aleksandrov–Clark measures](wikipedia://en/13895181).

The [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) equation of the composition operator is [Schröder's equation](wikipedia://en/5834541), and the principal [eigenfunction](wikipedia://en/228601) $f(x)$ is often called [Schröder's function](wikipedia://en/5834541) or [Koenigs function](wikipedia://en/33973912).

The composition operator has been used in data-driven techniques for dynamical systems in the context of [dynamic mode decomposition](wikipedia://en/19858814) algorithms, which approximate the modes and eigenvalues of the composition operator.
