# Fredholm kernel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fredholm kernel"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Fredholm kernel** is a certain type of a [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_%28integral_operator%29) on a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), associated with [nuclear operators](wikipedia://en/63684284) on the Banach space. They are an abstraction of the idea of the [Fredholm integral equation](wikipedia://en/363377) and the [Fredholm operator](wikipedia://en/279722), and are one of the objects of study in [Fredholm theory](wikipedia://en/4355120). Fredholm kernels are named in honour of [Erik Ivar Fredholm](wikipedia://en/669963). Much of the abstract theory of Fredholm kernels was developed by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and published in 1955.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *B* be an arbitrary [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), and let *B*<sup>\*</sup> be its dual, that is, the space of [bounded linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_linear_functional) on *B*. The [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) $B^*\otimes B$ has a [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) under the norm

$$\Vert X \Vert_\pi = \inf \sum_{\{i\}} \Vert e^*_i\Vert \Vert e_i \Vert$$

where the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) is taken over all finite representations

$$X=\sum_{\{i\}} e^*_i \otimes e_i \in B^*\otimes B$$

The completion, under this norm, is often denoted as

$$B^* \widehat{\,\otimes\,}_\pi B$$

and is called the **[projective topological tensor product](wikipedia://en/63566617)**. The elements of this space are called **Fredholm kernels**.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Every Fredholm kernel has a representation in the form

$$X=\sum_{\{i\}} \lambda_i e^*_i \otimes e_i$$

with $e_i \in B$ and $e^*_i \in B^*$ such that $\Vert e_i \Vert = \Vert e^*_i \Vert = 1$ and

$$\sum_{\{i\}} \vert \lambda_i \vert < \infty. \,$$

Associated with each such kernel is a linear operator

$$\mathcal {L}_X : B \to B$$

which has the canonical representation

$$\mathcal{L}_X f =\sum_{\{i\}} \lambda_i e^*_i(f) e_i. \,$$

Associated with every Fredholm kernel is a trace, defined as

$$\mbox{tr} X = \sum_{\{i\}} \lambda_i e^*_i(e_i). \,$$

:::

## *p*-summable kernels {#p-summable_kernels}

A Fredholm kernel is said to be ***p*-summable** if

$$\sum_{\{i\}} \vert \lambda_i \vert^p < \infty.$$

A Fredholm kernel is said to be of **order q** if *q* is the [infimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infimum) of all $0<p\le 1$ for all *p* for which it is *p*-summable.

## Nuclear operators on Banach spaces {#Nuclear_operators_on_Banach_spaces}

An operator L : $B\to B$ is said to be a [nuclear operator](wikipedia://en/63684284) if there exists an $X$ ∈ $B^* \widehat{\,\otimes\,}_\pi B$ such that L = L<sub>$X$</sub>. Such an operator is said to be $p$-summable and of order $q$ if $X$ is. In general, there may be more than one $X$ associated with such a nuclear operator, and so the trace is not uniquely defined. However, if the order $q$ ≤ 2/3, then there is a unique trace, as given by a theorem of Grothendieck.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Grothendieck's theorem"}

## Grothendieck's theorem {#Grothendieck's_theorem}

If $\mathcal{L}:B\to B$ is an operator of order $q \le 2/3$ then a trace may be defined, with

$$\mbox{Tr} \mathcal {L} = \sum_{\{i\}} \rho_i$$

where $\rho_i$ are the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of $\mathcal{L}$. Furthermore, the [Fredholm determinant](wikipedia://en/2685562)

$$\det \left( 1-z\mathcal{L}\right)= \prod_i \left(1-\rho_i z \right)$$

is an [entire function](wikipedia://en/10372) of *z*. The formula

$$\det \left( 1-z\mathcal{L}\right)= \exp \mbox{Tr} \log\left( 1-z\mathcal{L}\right)$$

holds as well. Finally, if $\mathcal{L}$ is parameterized by some [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued parameter *w*, that is, $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_w$, and the parameterization is [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) on some [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643), then

$$\det \left( 1-z\mathcal{L}_w\right)$$

is holomorphic on the same domain.

:::

::: {#o11 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

An important example is the Banach space of holomorphic functions over a domain $D\subset \mathbb{C}^k$. In this space, every nuclear operator is of order zero, and is thus of [trace-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace-class).

:::

## Nuclear spaces {#Nuclear_spaces}

The idea of a nuclear operator can be adapted to [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801). A **[nuclear space](wikipedia://en/2702957)** is a Fréchet space where every bounded map of the space to an arbitrary Banach space is nuclear.
