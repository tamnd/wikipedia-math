# Twiddle factor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Twiddle factor"}

A **twiddle factor**, in [fast Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/11512) (FFT) algorithms, is any of the [trigonometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) constant coefficients that are multiplied by the data in the course of the algorithm. This term was apparently coined by Gentleman & Sande in 1966, and has since become widespread in thousands of papers of the FFT literature.

:::

More specifically, "twiddle factors" originally referred to the [root-of-unity](wikipedia://en/171950) [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) multiplicative constants in the [butterfly](wikipedia://en/2707212) operations of the [Cooley–Tukey FFT algorithm](wikipedia://en/352702), used to [recursively](wikipedia://en/25407) combine smaller [discrete Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/8811). This remains the term's most common meaning, but it may also be used for any data-independent multiplicative constant in an FFT.

The [prime-factor FFT algorithm](wikipedia://en/241490) is one unusual case in which an FFT can be performed without twiddle factors, albeit only for restricted factorizations of the transform size.

For example, W<sub>8</sub><sup>2</sup> is a twiddle factor used in 8-point radix-2 FFT.
