# Predation rates

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Predation rates"}

The term **predation rate** refers to the frequency with which an [organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism) captures and consumes its [prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prey) in an ecosystem. Coupled with the [kill rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_rate), the predation rate drives the population dynamics of predation.[^o2] This statistic is related to [Predator–prey dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator–prey_dynamics) and may be influenced by several factors.

:::

In order for predation to occur, a [predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predator) and its prey must encounter one another. A low concentration of prey decreases the likelihood of such encounters. The prey encounter rate is determined by the abundance of organisms and a predator’s ability to locate its prey.[^o3] Covering more territory increases the likelihood that a predator will meet its prey. In areas of low prey density, predators are adapted to be more motile, engage in [filter feeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_feeding), or use attractants such as chemical lures.[^o4]

If predation increased simply with prey concentration, the relationship would be linear until a limit is reached. This scenario is represented by [Holling's type I functional response](wikipedia://en/8019439#Type_I), which is rarely observed in nature.[^o5] Several factors affect this relationship, including handling time (the time required for a predator to consume its prey), selective feeding behaviors, and learning.[^o6] In contrast, [Holling's type II](wikipedia://en/8019439#Type_II) and [type III functional responses](wikipedia://en/8019439#Type_III) account for the time predators spend handling prey and the reduced efficiency in locating prey at low densities.[^o7]

Predation rate is also influenced by spatial and temporal mismatch. An extreme example occurred in the [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic) in May of 2021 and 2022, when large blooms of [Phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton) were observed alongside low concentrations of grazers.[^o8] As the phytoplankton bloomed and died, the energy was not transferred into the [Food web](wikipedia://en/145772). Although primary production was high, the food web experienced an energy deficit. Spatial mismatch is particularly concerning under [Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change), as changing environmental parameters—such as rising [Sea surface temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature) and alterations in terrestrial habitats (e.g., loss of [Tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) and melting [Sea ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_ice))—can create conditions that are no longer conducive to the populations they once supported[^o9]
