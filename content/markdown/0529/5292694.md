# Elkies trinomial curves

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Elkies trinomial curves"}

*Image: C168.svg.* Elkies trinomial curve C<sub>168</sub>

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **Elkies trinomial curves** are certain [hyperelliptic curves](wikipedia://en/499361) constructed by [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) which have the property that rational points on them correspond to trinomial polynomials giving an extension of **Q** with particular [Galois groups](wikipedia://en/12673).

:::

One curve, C<sub>168</sub>, gives Galois group [PSL(2,7)](wikipedia://en/390426) from a polynomial of degree seven, and the other, C<sub>1344</sub>, gives Galois group AL(8), the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of a [2-elementary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-elementary_group) of order eight acted on by PSL(2, 7), giving a transitive permutation subgroup of the symmetric group on eight roots of order 1344.

The equation of the curve C<sub>168</sub> is

$$y^2 = x(81x^5+396x^4+738x^3+660x^2+269x+48).$$

The curve is a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) model for a [Galois resolvent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_resolvent) for the trinomial polynomial equation $x^{7} + bx + c = 0$. If there exists a point $(x, y)$ on the (projectivized) curve, there is a corresponding pair $(b, c)$ of rational numbers, such that the trinomial either factors or has Galois group PSL(2,7), the finite simple group of order 168. The curve has [genus](wikipedia://en/3115807) two, so by [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199) there are only a finite number of rational points on it. These rational points were proved by Nils Bruin using the computer program [Kash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KANT_%28mathematics%29) to be the only ones on C<sub>168</sub>, and they give only four distinct trinomials with Galois group PSL(2,7): $x^{7} - 7x + 3$ (the Trinks polynomial), $x^{7}/11 - 14x + 3^{2}$ (the Erbach-Fisher-McKay polynomial), and two new polynomials with Galois group PSL(2,7),

$$37^2x^7-28x+3^2$$

and

$$\frac{499^2}{113}x^7-212x+3^4.$$

On the other hand, the equation of curve C<sub>1344</sub> is

$$y^2 = 2 x^6 + 4 x^5 + 36 x^4 + 16 x^3 - 45 x^2 + 190 x + 1241.$$

Once again the genus is two, and by [Faltings' theorem](wikipedia://en/172199) the list of rational points is finite. It is thought the only rational points on it correspond to polynomials $x^{8} + 16x + 28$, $x^{8} + 576x + 1008$, $19^{4}53x^{8} + 19x + 2$ which have Galois group AL(8), and $x^{8} + 324x + 567$, which comes from two different rational points and has Galois group PSL(2,7) again, this time as the Galois group of a polynomial of degree eight.
