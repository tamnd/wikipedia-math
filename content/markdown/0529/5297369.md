# Gain graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gain graph"}

A **gain graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose edges are labelled "invertibly", or "orientably", by elements of a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G*. This means that, if an edge *e* in one direction has label *g* (a group element), then in the other direction it has label *g*<sup>−1</sup>. The label function *φ* therefore has the property that it is defined differently, but not independently, on the two different orientations, or directions, of an edge *e*. The group *G* is called the **gain group**, *φ* is the **gain function**, and the value *φ*(*e*) is the **gain** of *e* (in some indicated direction). A gain graph is a generalization of a [signed graph](wikipedia://en/2472618), where the gain group *G* has only two elements. See Zaslavsky (1989, 1991).

:::

A gain should not be confused with a **weight** on an edge, whose value is independent of the orientation of the edge.

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Some reasons to be interested in gain graphs are their connections to [network flow](wikipedia://en/645676) theory in [combinatorial optimization](wikipedia://en/420555), to [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and to [physics](wikipedia://en/22939).

- The mathematics of a [network with gains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_with_gains), or [generalized network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_network), is connected with the [frame matroid](wikipedia://en/2472880) of the gain graph.
- Suppose we have some [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) in *R <sup>n</sup>* given by equations of the form *x<sub>j</sub>* = *g x<sub>i</sub>* . The geometry of the hyperplanes can be treated by using the following gain graph: The vertex set is {1,2,...,*n*}. There is an edge *ij* with gain *g* (in the direction from *i* to *j*) for each hyperplane with equation *x<sub>j</sub> = g x<sub>i</sub>* . These hyperplanes are treated through the frame matroid of the gain graph (Zaslavsky 2003).
- Or, suppose we have hyperplanes given by equations of the form *x<sub>j</sub>* = *x<sub>i</sub>* + *g*. The geometry of these hyperplanes can be treated by using the gain graph with the same vertex set and an edge *ij* with gain *g* (in the direction from *i* to *j*) for each hyperplane with equation *x<sub>j</sub>* = *x<sub>i</sub>* + *g*. These hyperplanes are studied via the [lift matroid](wikipedia://en/2472880) of the gain graph (Zaslavsky 2003).
- Suppose the gain group has an [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a set *Q*. Assigning an element *s<sub>i</sub>* of *Q* to each vertex gives a **state** of the gain graph. An edge is **satisfied** if, for each edge *ij* with gain *g* (in the direction from *i* to *j*), the equation *s<sub>j</sub>* = *s<sub>i</sub> g* is satisfied; otherwise it is **frustrated**. A state is *satisfied* if every edge is satisfied. In physics this corresponds to a [ground state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_state) (a state of lowest energy), if such a state exists. An important problem in physics, especially in the theory of [spin glasses](wikipedia://en/148555), is to determine a state with the fewest frustrated edges.

:::

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

Gain graphs used in [topological graph theory](wikipedia://en/1631772) as a means to construct [graph embeddings](wikipedia://en/8149170) in surfaces are known as "[voltage graphs](wikipedia://en/8802504)" (Gross 1974; Gross and Tucker 1977). The term "gain graph" is more usual in other contexts, e.g., [biased graph](wikipedia://en/2472880) theory and [matroid theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matroid_theory). The term **group-labelled graph** has also been used, but it is ambiguous since "group labels" may be—and have been—treated as weights.

Since much of the theory of gain graphs is a special case of that of biased graphs (and much of the theory of biased graphs is a generalization of that of gain graphs), the reader should refer to the article on [biased graphs](wikipedia://en/2472880) for more information and examples.
