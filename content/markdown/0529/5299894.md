# Daniel Kleitman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Kleitman"}

**Daniel J. Kleitman** (born October 4, 1934)[^o3][^o4][^o5] is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT). His research interests include [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomics), and [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kleitman was born in 1934 in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29),[^o3] the younger of Bertha and Milton Kleitman's two sons. His father was a lawyer who after WWII became a commodities trader and investor. In 1942 the family moved to [Morristown, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morristown,_New_Jersey),[^o7] and he graduated from [Morristown High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morristown_High_School) in 1950.[^o3]

Kleitman then attended [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), from which he graduated in 1954,[^o3] and received his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in [Physics](wikipedia://en/22939) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1958 under Nobel Laureates [Julian Schwinger](wikipedia://en/269980) and [Roy Glauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Glauber). He is the "k" in [G. W. Peck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._W._Peck), a pseudonym for a group of six mathematicians that includes Kleitman. Formerly a physics professor at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University),[^o9] Kleitman was encouraged by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) to change his field of study to mathematics. Perhaps humorously, Erdős once asked him, "Why are you *only* a physicist?"[^o3]

Kleitman joined the applied mathematics faculty at MIT in 1966, and was promoted to professor in 1969.[^o9]

Kleitman coauthored at least six papers with Erdős, giving him an [Erdős number](wikipedia://en/9742) of 1.

He was a math advisor and extra for the film *[Good Will Hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting).*[^o10] Since [Minnie Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Driver), who appeared in *[Good Will Hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting)*, also appeared in *[Sleepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepers_%28film%29)* with [Kevin Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Bacon), Kleitman has a [Bacon number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon_number) of 2. Adding the two numbers results in an [Erdős–Bacon number](wikipedia://en/2625993) of 3, which is a tie with [Bruce Reznick](wikipedia://en/61232693) for the lowest number anyone has.[^o11]

In 1973 Kleitman was elected as a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o12]

In 2024 Kleitman was elected as a member of the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o13]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On July 26, 1964 Kleitman married Sharon Ruth Alexander. They have three children.[^o3]
