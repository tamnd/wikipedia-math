# Roger J-B Wets

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger J-B Wets"}

**Roger Jean-Baptiste Robert Wets** (February 1937 – April 1, 2025) was a Belgian [stochastic programming](wikipedia://en/501898)[^o4] and a leader in [variational analysis](wikipedia://en/171882) who published as **Roger J-B Wets**. His research, expositions, graduate students, and his collaboration with [R. Tyrrell Rockafellar](wikipedia://en/26300644) have had a profound influence on optimization theory, computations, and applications.[^o4][^o14][^o13] Since 2009, Wets has been a distinguished research professor at the mathematics department of the [University of California, Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Davis).[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Schooling and positions {#Schooling_and_positions}

Roger Wets attended high school in Belgium, after which he worked for his family while earning his *[Licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licenciate#Belgium)* in applied economics from [Université de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Bruxelles) (Brussels, Belgium) in 1961.[^o8] He was encouraged by [Jacques&nbsp;H. Drèze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques&nbsp;H._Drèze) to study [optimization](wikipedia://en/52033) with [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111) at the program in [operations&nbsp;research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations&nbsp;research) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o9] Dantzig and mathematician–statistician [David&nbsp;Blackwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David&nbsp;Blackwell) jointly supervised Wets's dissertation.[^o6][^o10] In 1965 Wets befriended [R.&nbsp;Tyrrell Rockafellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.&nbsp;Tyrrell_Rockafellar), whom Wets introduced to stochastic optimization, starting a collaboration of many decades.[^o11]

*Image: Blauer Hof Laxenburg 3-4.JPG.* Wets helped to lead a project on decision-making under uncertainty at the [International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Applied_Systems_Analysis) *(pictured)*.

He worked at Boeing Scientific Research Labs, 1964–1970 and was Ford Professor at the University of Chicago, 1970–1972 before being appointed Professor at the Mathematics Department of the University of Kentucky and then University Research Professor (1977–78).[^o5] While at the [International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Applied_Systems_Analysis) (IIASA) in Austria, during 1980–1984,[^o5] he led research in decision-making in uncertainty, returning as an acting leader in 1985–1987; during that time, Wets and Rockafellar developed the progressive-hedging algorithm for stochastic programming.[^o4][^o13] The University of California, Davis named him Professor (1984–1997), Distinguished Professor, and Distinguished Research Professor of Mathematics (2009–).[^o5]

### Awards and contributions {#Awards_and_contributions}

*Image: Rockafellar.jpg.* Since 1965 [R. Tyrrell Rockafellar](wikipedia://en/26300644) *(pictured)* and Wets have collaborated in stochastic programming, earning the Dantzig prize for their progressive-hedging algorithm and theory of [epigraphic](wikipedia://en/5744061) convergence.

Wets was awarded a [George&nbsp;B. Dantzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George&nbsp;B._Dantzig) Prize for "original research that has had a major impact on the field of mathematical programming" by the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) (SIAM) and the Mathematical Programming Society (MPS, now the [Mathematical&nbsp;Optimization Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical&nbsp;Optimization_Society)).[^o13] In 1994, the Dantzig Prize was awarded to Wets and also to the French pioneer in nonsmooth computational-optimization, [Claude Lemaréchal](wikipedia://en/26313585).[^o13]

Wets's contributions included developing [set-valued analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-valued_analysis), including [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) of sets, which he used to study the [convergence](wikipedia://en/19719#Convergent_filter_bases) of [epigraphs](wikipedia://en/5744061); Wets's ideas of epigraphical convergence was used to study the convergence [iterative methods](wikipedia://en/15237) of stochastic optimization and has had applications in the [approximation theory of statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_statistics).[^o4][^o13][^o6][^o15] A metric theory of finite-dimensional epigraphical convergence ("cosmic convergence") appears in *Variational analysis*.[^o15] Wets and his coauthor R. Tyrrell Rockafellar were awarded the 1997 [Frederick&nbsp;W. Lanchester Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick&nbsp;W._Lanchester_Prize) by the [Institute for Operations&nbsp;Research and the Management&nbsp;Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Operations&nbsp;Research_and_the_Management&nbsp;Sciences) (INFORMS) for their monograph *Variational Analysis*, which was published in November 1997 and copyrighted in 1998.[^o14][^o15]

With Rockafellar, Wets proposed, studied, and implemented the progressive-hedging algorithm for stochastic programming. Besides his theoretical and computational contributions, Wets has worked with applications on lake ecology (IIASA), finance (Frank Russel investment system), and developmental economics (World Bank). He also consulted with the development of professional stochastic-optimization software (IBM).[^o13]

Wets died on 1 April, 2025, at the age of 88.[^o16]
