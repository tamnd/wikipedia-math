# George H. Mealy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="George H. Mealy"}

**George H. Mealy** (December 31, 1927 – June 21, 2010, in [Scituate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scituate,_Massachusetts), [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts))[^o2] was an American mathematician and computer scientist who invented the namesake [Mealy machine](wikipedia://en/331512), a type of [finite state transducer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_state_transducer). He was also a pioneer of [modular programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_programming),[^o3][^o4] one of the lead designers of the [IPL-V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Processing_Language) programming language,[^o5] and an early advocate of [macro processors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General-purpose_macro_processor) in [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language) programming.[^o6]

:::

Mealy went to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he was active in [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) as business manager for [WHRB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHRB).[^o7] He graduated in 1951 with an A.B., and at that time began working for [Bell Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Laboratories).[^o8] He later worked at the [RAND Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_Corporation)[^o9] then [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)[^o10] and taught at Harvard.

## Other contributions {#Other_contributions}

In a 2024 article in *[Data & Knowledge Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_&_Knowledge_Engineering)*, researchers described Mealy's 1967 paper "Another Look at Data" as containing the first mention of the term "[ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology_%28information_science%29)" in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), quoting his statement: "Data are fragments of a theory of the real world, and data processing juggles representations of these fragments of theory ... The issue is one of ontology, or the question of what exists."
