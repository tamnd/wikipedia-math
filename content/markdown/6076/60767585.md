# Hazel Perfect

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hazel Perfect"}

**Hazel Perfect** (c. 1927 – 8 July 2015) was a British mathematician specialising in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Perfect was known for inventing [gammoids](wikipedia://en/26952253),AMG for her work with [Leon Mirsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Mirsky) on [doubly stochastic matrices](wikipedia://en/2906180),SP2 for her three books *Topics in Geometry*,TIG *Topics in Algebra*,TIA and *[Independence Theory in Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/68703246)*,ITC and for her work as a translator (from an earlier German translation) of [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245)'s book *An Introduction to the Theory of Groups* (Hafner, 1959).ITG

The [Perfect–Mirsky conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect–Mirsky_conjecture), named after Perfect and [Leon Mirsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Mirsky), concerns the region of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) formed by the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of doubly stochastic matrices. Perfect and Mirsky conjectured that for $n\times n$ matrices this region is the union of regular polygons of up to $n$ sides, having the [roots of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) of each degree up to $n$ as vertices. Perfect and Mirsky proved their conjecture for $n\le 3$; it was subsequently shown to be true for $n=4$ and false for $n=5$, but remains open for larger values of $n$.SP2

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Perfect earned a master's degree through [Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_College) (a constituent college for women in the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London)) in 1949, with a thesis on *The Reduction of Matrices to Canonical Form*. In the 1950s, Perfect was a lecturer at [University College of Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea_University); she collaborated with Gordon Petersen, a visitor to Swansea at that time, on their translation of Alexandrov's book. She completed her Ph.D. at the University of London in 1969; her dissertation was *Studies in Transversal Theory with Particular Reference to Independence Structures and Graphs*. She became a reader in mathematics at the [University of Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sheffield).
