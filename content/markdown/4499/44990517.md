# Amari distance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amari distance"}

The **Amari distance**,[^o2][^o3] also known as **Amari index**[^o4] and **Amari metric**[^o5] is a [similarity measure](wikipedia://en/1004743) between two [invertible matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible_matrices), useful for checking for convergence in [independent component analysis](wikipedia://en/598031) algorithms and for comparing solutions. It is named after Japanese information theorist [Shun'ichi Amari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shun'ichi_Amari) and was originally introduced as a performance index for [blind source separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_source_separation).[^o6]

:::

For two invertible matrices $A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$, it is defined as:

$$d(A, B) = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(\sum_{j=1}^n\frac{|p_{ij}|}{\max_{k} |p_{ik}|} - 1\right) + \sum_{j=1}^n \left(\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{|p_{ij}|}{\max_{k} |p_{kj}|} - 1\right), P = A^{-1}B$$

It is non-negative and cancels if and only if $A^{-1}B$ is a scale and permutation matrix, i.e. the product of a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) and a [permutation matrix](wikipedia://en/238849). The Amari distance is invariant to permutation and scaling of the columns of $A$ and $B$.[^o7]
