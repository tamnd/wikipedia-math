# Malmquist's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Malmquist's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Malmquist's theorem**, is the name of any of the three theorems proved by (txt).[^o3][^o4][^o5] These theorems restrict the forms of first order algebraic [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) which have transcendental [meromorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meromorphic) or algebroid solutions.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorems"}

## Statement of the theorems {#Statement_of_the_theorems}

Theorem (1913). If the differential equation

$$\frac{dw}{dz}=R(z,w)$$

where *R*(*z*,*w*) is a [rational function](wikipedia://en/361210), has a transcendental [meromorphic](wikipedia://en/80857) solution, then *R* is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) of degree at most 2 with respect to *w*; in other words the differential equation is a [Riccati equation](wikipedia://en/304999), or linear.

Theorem (1920). If an irreducible differential equation

$$F\left(\frac{dw}{dz},w,z\right)=0$$

where *F* is a polynomial, has a transcendental meromorphic solution, then the equation has no [movable singularities](wikipedia://en/7664719). Moreover, it can be algebraically reduced either to a Riccati equation or to

$$\left(\frac{dw}{dz}\right)^2=a(z)P(z,w),$$

where *P* is a polynomial of degree *3* with respect to *w*.

Theorem (1941). If an irreducible differential equation

$$F\left(\frac{dw}{dz},w,z\right)=0,$$

where *F* is a polynomial, has a transcendental [algebroid](wikipedia://en/49367970) solution, then it can be algebraically reduced to an equation that has no movable singularities.

A modern account of theorems 1913, 1920 is given in the paper of [A. Eremenko](wikipedia://en/34534867) (1982).[^o8]
