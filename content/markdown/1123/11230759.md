# Retarded potential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Retarded potential"}

In [electrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrodynamics), the **retarded potentials** are the [electromagnetic potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_potential) for the [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field) generated by [time-varying](wikipedia://en/1291342) [electric current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current) or [charge distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_distribution) in the past. The fields propagate at the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light) *c*, so the delay of the fields connecting [cause and effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality_%28physics%29) at earlier and later times is an important factor: the signal takes a finite time to propagate from a point in the charge or current distribution (the point of cause) to another point in space (where the effect is measured), see figure below.[^o2]

:::

## In the Lorenz gauge {#In_the_Lorenz_gauge}

*Image: Universal charge distribution.svg.* Position vectors **r** and **r′** used in the calculation

The starting point is [Maxwell's equations in the potential formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maths_of_EM_field#Potential_field_approach) using the [Lorenz gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenz_gauge):

$$\Box \varphi = \dfrac{\rho}{\epsilon_0} \,,\quad \Box \mathbf{A} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$$

where φ(**r**, *t*) is the [electric potential](wikipedia://en/59615) and **A**(**r**, *t*) is the [magnetic vector potential](wikipedia://en/1191067), for an arbitrary source of [charge density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_density) ρ(**r**, *t*) and [current density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_density) **J**(**r**, *t*), and $\Box$ is the [D'Alembert operator](wikipedia://en/293511).[^o4] Solving these gives the retarded potentials below (all in [SI units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SI_units)).

### For time-dependent fields {#For_time-dependent_fields}

For time-dependent fields, the retarded potentials are:[^o6][^o7]

$$\mathrm\varphi (\mathbf r , t) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int \frac{\rho (\mathbf r' , t_r)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\, \mathrm{d}^3\mathbf r'$$

$$\mathbf A (\mathbf r , t) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{\mathbf J (\mathbf r' , t_r)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\, \mathrm{d}^3\mathbf r'\,.$$

where **r** is a [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Position_vector) in space, *t* is time,

$$t_r = t-\frac{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}{c}$$

is the [retarded time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retarded_time), and d<sup>3</sup>**r'** is the [integration measure](wikipedia://en/709567) using **r'**.

From φ(**r**, t) and **A**(**r**, *t*), the fields **E**(**r**, *t*) and **B**(**r**, *t*) can be calculated using the definitions of the potentials:

$$-\mathbf{E} = \nabla\varphi +\frac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial t}\,,\quad \mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf A\,.$$

and this leads to [Jefimenko's equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefimenko's_equations). The corresponding advanced potentials have an identical form, except the advanced time

$$t_a = t+\frac{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}{c}$$

replaces the retarded time.

### In comparison with static potentials for time-independent fields {#In_comparison_with_static_potentials_for_time-independent_fields}

In the case the fields are time-independent ([electrostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostatic) and [magnetostatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetostatic) fields), the time derivatives in the $\Box$ operators of the fields are zero, and Maxwell's equations reduce to

$$\nabla^2 \varphi =-\dfrac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}\,,\quad \nabla^2 \mathbf{A} =- \mu_0 \mathbf{J}\,,$$

where ∇<sup>2</sup> is the [Laplacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian), which take the form of [Poisson's equation](wikipedia://en/193735) in four components (one for φ and three for **A**), and the solutions are:

$$\mathrm\varphi (\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int \frac{\rho (\mathbf r' )}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\, \mathrm{d}^3\mathbf r'$$

$$\mathbf A (\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int \frac{\mathbf J (\mathbf r' )}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\, \mathrm{d}^3\mathbf r'\,.$$

These also follow directly from the retarded potentials.

## In the Coulomb gauge {#In_the_Coulomb_gauge}

In the [Coulomb gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulomb_gauge), Maxwell's equations are[^o10]

$$\nabla^2 \varphi =-\dfrac{\rho}{\epsilon_0}$$

$$\nabla^2 \mathbf{A} - \dfrac{1}{c^2}\dfrac{\partial^2 \mathbf{A}}{\partial t^2}=- \mu_0 \mathbf{J} +\dfrac{1}{c^2}\nabla\left(\dfrac{\partial \varphi}{\partial t}\right)\,,$$

although the solutions contrast the above, since **A** is a retarded potential yet φ changes *instantly*, given by:

$$\varphi(\mathbf{r}, t) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\int \dfrac{\rho(\mathbf{r}',t)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{r}'$$

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi \varepsilon_0} \nabla\times\int \mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{r'} \int_0^{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|/c} \mathrm{d}t_r \dfrac{ t_r \mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r'}, t-t_r)}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'|^3}\times (\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}') \,.$$

This presents an advantage and a disadvantage of the Coulomb gauge - φ is easily calculable from the charge distribution ρ but **A** is not so easily calculable from the current distribution **j**. However, provided we require that the potentials vanish at infinity, they can be expressed neatly in terms of fields:

$$\varphi(\mathbf{r}, t) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int \dfrac{\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}',t)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{r}'$$

$$\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r},t) = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\int \dfrac{\nabla \times \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}',t)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|}\mathrm{d}^3\mathbf{r}'$$

## In linearized gravity {#In_linearized_gravity}

The retarded potential in [linearized general relativity](wikipedia://en/2865024) is closely analogous to the electromagnetic case. The trace-reversed tensor $\tilde h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \frac 1 2 \eta_{\mu\nu} h$ plays the role of the four-vector potential, the [harmonic gauge](wikipedia://en/7203375) $\tilde h^{\mu\nu}{}_{,\mu} = 0$ replaces the electromagnetic Lorenz gauge, the field equations are $\Box \tilde h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$, and the retarded-wave solution is[^o12]

$$\tilde h_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r, t) = 4 G \int \frac{T_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf r', t_r)}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r'|} \mathrm d^3 \mathbf r'.$$

Using SI units, the expression must be divided by $c^4$, as can be confirmed by dimensional analysis.

## Occurrence and application {#Occurrence_and_application}

A many-body theory which includes an average of retarded and *advanced* [Liénard–Wiechert potentials](wikipedia://en/9999827) is the [Wheeler–Feynman absorber theory](wikipedia://en/3062954) also known as the Wheeler–Feynman time-symmetric theory.

In [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation), there are application examples for calculating deviations in [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit) of [satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite),[^o14] [moons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_of_the_Moon)[^o15] or [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbits_of_planets).[^o16] The anomalies in the [rotation curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_curve) of more than one hundred [spiral galaxies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_galaxies) of different [types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_morphological_classification) could also be explained. The data of the “SPARC (Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves) Galaxy collection”, which were recorded with the [Spitzer Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitzer_Space_Telescope), were used for this purpose. In this way, neither the assumption of [dark matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter) nor a modification of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) is required to explain the observations.[^o17]

In 2026 the German physicist Andreas Malcherek published about a new theory of [gravitoelectromagnetism](wikipedia://en/24794048) that builds on the basic work of [Oliver Heaviside](wikipedia://en/22831) und [Oleg D. Jefimenko](wikipedia://en/4344332).[^o18] The new theory applies retarded gravitational potentials within [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488) and is consistent with all the predictions of [Albert Einstein's](wikipedia://en/736) [general theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), without taking into account the [equivalence principle](wikipedia://en/857235) or for [curved space](wikipedia://en/2557627).[^o19]

## Example {#Example}

The potential of charge with uniform speed on a straight line has [inversion in a point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_in_a_point) that is in the recent position. The potential is not changed in the direction of movement.[^o22]
