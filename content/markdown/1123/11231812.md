# Category of modules

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of modules"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), given a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $R$, the **category of left modules** over $R$ is the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are all left [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over $R$ and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are all [module homomorphisms](wikipedia://en/474118) between left $R$-modules. For example, when $R$ is the ring of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $\mathbb{Z}$, it is the same thing as the [category of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/454748). The **category of right modules** is defined in a similar way.

:::

One can also define the category of [bimodules](wikipedia://en/492612) over a ring $R$ but that category is equivalent to the category of left (or right) modules over the [enveloping algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enveloping_algebra_of_an_associative_algebra) of $R$ (or over the opposite of that).

**Note:** Some authors use the term **module category** for the category of modules. This term can be ambiguous since it could also refer to a category with a [monoidal-category action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoidal-category_action).[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The categories of left and right modules are [abelian categories](wikipedia://en/45063). These categories have [enough projectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_projectives)[^o5] and [enough injectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_injectives).[^o6] [Mitchell's embedding theorem](wikipedia://en/453755) states every abelian category arises as a [full subcategory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategory) of the category of modules over some ring.

[Projective limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_limit) and [inductive limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_limit) exist in the categories of left and right modules.[^o7]

Over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), together with the [tensor product of modules](wikipedia://en/2993836) $\otimes$, the category of modules is a [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249).

:::

## Objects {#Objects}

A [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) of the category of modules over a commutative ring $R$ is exactly an [associative algebra](wikipedia://en/2112) over $R$.

A [compact object](wikipedia://en/57633087) in $R$-$\mathbf{Mod}$ is exactly a finitely presented module.

## Category of vector spaces {#Category_of_vector_spaces}

The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ (some authors use $\mathbf{Vect}_K$) has all [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ as objects, and [$K$-linear maps](wikipedia://en/18102) as morphisms. Since vector spaces over $K$ (as a field) are the same thing as [modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) over the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $K$, $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ is a special case of $R$-$\mathbf{Mod}$ (some authors use $\mathbf{Mod}_R$), the category of left $R$-modules.

Much of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) concerns the description of $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$. For example, the [dimension theorem for vector spaces](wikipedia://en/346992) says that the [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) in $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ correspond exactly to the [cardinal numbers](wikipedia://en/6173), and that $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) of $K\text{-}\mathbf{Vect}$ which has as its objects the vector spaces $K_n$, where $n$ is any cardinal number.

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The category of [sheaves of modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaves_of_modules) over a [ringed space](wikipedia://en/248014) also has enough injectives (though not always enough projectives).

:::
