# Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula"}

The **Davidon–Fletcher–Powell formula** (or **DFP**; named after [William C. Davidon](wikipedia://en/41578324), [Roger Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Fletcher_%28mathematician%29), and [Michael J. D. Powell](wikipedia://en/16082618)) finds the solution to the secant equation that is closest to the current estimate and satisfies the curvature condition. It was the first [quasi-Newton method](wikipedia://en/7883127) to generalize the [secant method](wikipedia://en/640746) to a multidimensional problem. This update maintains the symmetry and positive definiteness of the [Hessian matrix](wikipedia://en/412108).

:::

Given a [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) function $f(x)$, its [gradient](wikipedia://en/12461) ($\nabla f$), and [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix) [Hessian matrix](wikipedia://en/412108) $B$, the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) is

$$f(x_k+s_k) = f(x_k) + \nabla f(x_k)^T s_k + \frac{1}{2} s^T_k {B} s_k + \dots,$$

and the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) of the gradient itself (secant equation)

$$\nabla f(x_k+s_k) = \nabla f(x_k) + B s_k + \dots$$

is used to update $B$.

The DFP formula finds a solution that is symmetric, positive-definite and closest to the current approximate value of $B_k$:

$$B_{k+1}= (I - \gamma_k y_k s_k^T) B_k (I - \gamma_k s_k y_k^T) + \gamma_k y_k y_k^T,$$

where

$$y_k = \nabla f(x_k+s_k) - \nabla f(x_k),$$

$$\gamma_k = \frac{1}{y_k^T s_k},$$

and $B_k$ is a symmetric and [positive-definite matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_matrix).

The corresponding update to the inverse Hessian approximation $H_k = B_k^{-1}$ is given by

$$H_{k+1} = H_k - \frac{H_k y_k y_k^T H_k}{y_k^T H_k y_k} + \frac{s_k s_k^T}{y_k^{T} s_k}.$$

$B$ is assumed to be positive-definite, and the vectors $s_k^T$ and $y$ must satisfy the curvature condition

$$s_k^T y_k = s_k^T B s_k > 0.$$

The DFP formula is quite effective, but it was soon superseded by the [Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno formula](wikipedia://en/1926409), which is its [dual](wikipedia://en/609737) (interchanging the roles of *y* and *s*).[^o2]

## Compact representation {#Compact_representation}

By unwinding the matrix recurrence for $B_k$, the DFP formula can be expressed as a [compact matrix representation](wikipedia://en/72865936). Specifically, defining

$$S_k = \begin{bmatrix} s_0 & s_1 & \ldots & s_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

$$Y_k = \begin{bmatrix} y_0 & y_1 & \ldots & y_{k-1} \end{bmatrix},$$

and upper triangular and diagonal matrices

$$\big(R_k\big)_{ij} := \big(R^{\text{SY}}_k\big)_{ij} = s^T_{i-1}y_{j-1}, \quad \big(R^{\text{YS}}_k\big)_{ij} = y^T_{i-1}s_{j-1}, \quad (D_k)_{ii} := \big(D^{\text{SY}}_k\big)_{ii} = s^T_{i-1}y_{i-1} \quad \quad \text{ for } 1 \le i \le j \le k$$

the DFP matrix has the equivalent formula

$$B_k = B_0 + J_k N^{-1}_k J^T_k,$$

$$J_k = \begin{bmatrix} Y_k & Y_k - B_0 S_k \end{bmatrix}$$

$$N_k = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{k \times k} & R^{\text{YS}}_k \\ \big( R^{\text{YS}}_k \big)^T & R_k+R^T_k-(D_k+S^T_k B_0 S_k) \end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse compact representation can be found by applying the [Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury inverse](wikipedia://en/430493) to $B_k$. The compact representation is particularly useful for limited-memory and constrained problems.[^o4]
