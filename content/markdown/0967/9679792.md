# Benjamin Kagan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Kagan"}

**Veniamin Fyodorovich Kagan** (Вениами́н Фёдорович Ка́ган; 9 March 1869 – 8 May 1953) was a Russian and Soviet mathematician and expert in non-Euclidean [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).[^o3] He is the maternal grandfather of mathematicians [Yakov Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_G._Sinai) and [Grigory Barenblatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Barenblatt).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kagan was born in Shavli, in the [Kovno Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kovno_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) (now [Šiauliai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Šiauliai), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania)) in 1869, to a poor [Lithuanian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Lithuania) family. In 1871, his family moved to Yekaterinoslav (now [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro)), where he grew up. Kagan entered the [Imperial Novorossiya University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_University) in [Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odessa) in 1887, but was expelled for revolutionary activities in 1889. He was put on [probation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probation) and sent back to [Yekaterinoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate). He studied mathematics on his own and, in 1892, passed the state exam at [Kiev University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv).

In 1894, Kagan moved to [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg), where he continued his studies with [Andrey Markov](wikipedia://en/915) and [Konstantin Posse](wikipedia://en/34441519). They tried to help him to obtain an academic position, but Kagan's Jewish background was an obstacle. Only in 1897 was he allowed to become a [dozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at the Imperial Novorossiya University, where he continued to work until 1923. His students in the [theory of relativity](wikipedia://en/30001) class he taught in 1921-22 included [Nikolai Papaleksi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Papaleksi), [Alexander Frumkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Frumkin), and [Igor Tamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Tamm).

Kagan worked at the [Imperial Moscow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Moscow_University), where he held the Geometry Chair from 1923 to 1952.

In 1924, he joined [Otto Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Schmidt) in drawing up plans for the *[Great Soviet Encyclopedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Soviet_Encyclopedia)*.

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

He published over 100 mathematical papers in different parts of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), particularly on [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) and on [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243). He received the [Stalin Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_State_Prize) in 1943. He founded the science publisher *Mathesis* in Odesa. He was a director of the mathematics and natural sciences department of the Great Soviet Encyclopedia. He wrote a definitive biography of [Nikolai Lobachevsky](wikipedia://en/648527) and edited his collected works (5 volumes, 1946–1951).

Kagan's doctoral students include [Viktor Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Wagner) and [Isaak Yaglom](wikipedia://en/11606627).
