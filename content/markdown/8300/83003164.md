# Richard Montgomery (mathematician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Montgomery"}

**Richard Montgomery** (born 1990) is a British mathematician and professor at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). His research is principally in [extremal](wikipedia://en/2675917) and [probabilistic combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probabilistic_combinatorics).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Montgomery studied at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), receiving a BA and MMath in 2011 and completing his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 2015 under the supervision of [Andrew Thomason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Thomason). His thesis was titled *Minors and Spanning Trees in Graphs*.[^o3][^o6]

He was a postdoctoral fellow at the [University of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Birmingham) in 2015 and a [Junior Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Research_Fellow) at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), from 2015 to 2019.[^o7] At Birmingham, he was a Birmingham Fellow from 2018 to 2020, a Senior Birmingham Fellow from 2020 to 2021 and a [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_%28academic_rank%29) in combinatorics from 2021 to 2022. In 2022 he joined the University of Warwick as an associate professor. He became a reader there in 2024 and a full professor in 2025.[^o3]

## Research {#Research}

Montgomery works on extremal and probabilistic combinatorics, particularly problems concerning subgraphs, [graph decompositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_decomposition) and [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477).[^o9]

He resolved a conjecture of [Jeff Kahn](wikipedia://en/26919476) about spanning trees in random graphs. Montgomery proved that, for every fixed $\Delta>0$, there is a constant $C=C(\Delta)$ such that the [binomial random graph](wikipedia://en/10477221) $G(n,C\log n/n)$ contains a copy of every tree on $n$ vertices with maximum degree at most $\Delta$, [with high probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_high_probability).[^o10]

With [Alexey Pokrovskiy](wikipedia://en/83165784) and [Benny Sudakov](wikipedia://en/35862803), Montgomery studied decompositions of edge-coloured complete bipartite graphs into [rainbow perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/42366612), which correspond to decompositions of [Latin squares](wikipedia://en/37447) into transversals. They also proved that every properly edge-coloured complete graph can be nearly decomposed into rainbow spanning trees.[^o11]

The same authors proved [Ringel's conjecture](wikipedia://en/8978968#Ringel's_conjecture) for all sufficiently large $n$. The conjecture, posed by [Gerhard Ringel](wikipedia://en/1571968) in 1963, states that, for every tree $T$ with $n$ edges, the edges of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) $K_{2n+1}$ can be decomposed into $2n+1$ copies of $T$.[^o12][^o13]

With Hong Liu, Montgomery solved a problem posed by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [András Hajnal](wikipedia://en/21765779) in 1981 concerning the odd cycle lengths of graphs with infinite chromatic number. They proved an asymptotically sharp lower bound on the sum of the reciprocals of the odd cycle lengths in a graph in terms of its chromatic number.[^o14]

In 2023, Montgomery proved the Ryser–Brualdi–Stein conjecture for all sufficiently large even $n$. This conjecture states that every Latin square of even order $n$ has a transversal containing $n-1$ cells.[^o15]

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Together with Alexey Pokrovskiy, Montgomery received the 2019 [European Prize in Combinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Prize_in_Combinatorics). The prize recognised their "deep contributions to extremal and probabilistic combinatorics".[^o17]

In 2020, Montgomery received a [Philip Leverhulme Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Leverhulme_Prize).[^o18]

The [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) awarded him an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Mathematical_Society_Prize) in 2024, citing his work on Ringel's conjecture, distributive absorption and sublinear expanders.[^o19]

In 2025, he received a [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) from the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) for his work on the absorption method and sublinear expanders in extremal and probabilistic combinatorics.[^o20]

In 2026, Montgomery and [Julian Sahasrabudhe](wikipedia://en/76040232) jointly received the [Adams Prize](wikipedia://en/1098110) for work in discrete mathematics.[^o21]
