# Base (geometry)

*Image: Pyramid coloured base (geometry).png.* A skeletal [pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid) with its **base** highlighted

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Base"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **base** is a [side](wikipedia://en/12158034) of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) or a [face](wikipedia://en/149926) of a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), particularly one oriented [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to the direction in which [height](wikipedia://en/239972#In_mathematics) is measured, or on what is considered to be the "bottom" of the figure.[^o2] This term is commonly applied in plane geometry to [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654), [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496), [trapezoids](wikipedia://en/201826), and in solid geometry to [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29), [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29), [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), [parallelepipeds](wikipedia://en/23975), [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), and [frustums](wikipedia://en/89405).

:::

The side or point opposite the base is often called the *[apex](wikipedia://en/6022079)* or *summit* of the shape.

## Of a triangle {#Of_a_triangle}

*Image: Projection formula (3).png.* The [altitude](wikipedia://en/161241) from A intersects the **extended base** at D (a point outside the triangle).

In a triangle, any arbitrary side can be considered the *base*. The two endpoints of the base are called *base vertices* and the corresponding angles are called *base angles*. The third vertex opposite the base is called the *apex*.

The **extended base** of a triangle (a particular case of an [extended side](wikipedia://en/43958459)) is the [line](wikipedia://en/946975) that contains the base. When the triangle is [obtuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle) and the base is chosen to be one of the sides adjacent to the [obtuse angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_angle), then the [altitude](wikipedia://en/161241) dropped [perpendicularly](wikipedia://en/76944) from the apex to the base [intersects](wikipedia://en/41780044) the extended base outside of the triangle.

The [area of a triangle](wikipedia://en/9194858) is its half of the product of the base times the height (length of the altitude). For a triangle $\triangle ABC$ with opposite sides $a, b, c,$ if the three altitudes of the triangle are called $h_a, h_b, h_c,$ the area is:

$$\Delta = \tfrac12 a h_a = \tfrac12 b h_b = \tfrac12 c h_c.$$

Given a fixed base side and a fixed area for a triangle, the [locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_%28geometry%29) of apex points is a straight line parallel to the base.

## Of a trapezoid or parallelogram {#Of_a_trapezoid_or_parallelogram}

Any of the sides of a parallelogram, or either (but typically the longer) of the parallel sides of a trapezoid can be considered its *base*. Sometimes the parallel opposite side is also called a *base*, or sometimes it is called a *top*, *apex*, or *summit*. The other two edges can be called the *sides*.

## Role in area and volume calculation {#Role_in_area_and_volume_calculation}

Bases are commonly used (together with heights) to calculate the [areas](wikipedia://en/1209) and [volumes](wikipedia://en/32498) of figures. In speaking about these processes, the measure (length or area) of a figure's base is often referred to as its "base."

By this usage, the area of a parallelogram or the volume of a [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) or cylinder can be calculated by multiplying its "base" by its height; likewise, the areas of triangles and the volumes of cones and pyramids are fractions of the products of their bases and heights. Some figures have two parallel bases (such as trapezoids and frustums), both of which are used to calculate the extent of the figures.[^o6]
