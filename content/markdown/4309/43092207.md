# Formal ball

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formal ball"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **formal ball** is an extension of the notion of [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) to allow unbounded and negative radius. The concept of formal ball was introduced by Weihrauch and Schreiber in 1981 and the negative radius case (the **generalized formal ball**) by Tsuiki and Hattori in 2008.

:::

Specifically, if $(X,d)$ is a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) then an element of $B^+(X,d) = X\times\mathbb{R}^{+}$ is a formal ball, where $\mathbb{R}^{+}$ is the set of nonnegative [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). Elements of $B(X,d)=X\times\mathbb{R}$ are known as generalized formal balls.

Formal balls possess a [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) $\leq$ defined by $(x,r) \leq (y,s)$ if $d(x,y) \leq r-s$.

Generalized formal balls are interesting because this partial order works just as well for $B(X,d)$ as for $B^+(X,d)$, even though a generalized formal ball with negative radius does not correspond to a subset of $X$.

Formal balls possess the [Lawson topology](wikipedia://en/23600625) and the [Martin topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_topology).
