# Johannes Dryander

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johannes Dryander"}

*Image: Johann Dryander (11498078).jpg.* Johannes Dryander portrait

**Johannes Dryander**, also known as **Johannes Eichmann** (1500-1560), was an academic.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Dryander was born in Wetter, [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) as Johannes Eichmann. He studied [anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy) and [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine) at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and the [University of Erfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erfurt) from 1528 to 1534 before becoming a professor of medicine at the [University of Marburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Marburg) in 1535.[^o3][^o4]

Dryander held two public dissections in the following year and authored the first text illustrating a [Galenic dissection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galenic_corpus) of the [human brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_brain) in 1536.[^o5] His *Anatomiae pars prior*, an expanded edition published in 1537, marked a significant transition from medieval scholasticism to the precise observations of [Andreas Vesalius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Vesalius).[^o5]

Dryander's books featured detailed illustrations of the brain, skull, and cranial sutures, reflecting his personal use of dissections.[^o5] His translation of Mundinus' anatomy in 1542 drew the ire of the anatomist as it plagiarized a portion of Vesalius' *Tabulae sex*.[^o5] He continued his work on [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) until his death in 1560.[^o5]

His early books, as products of rational scientific thought, contributed significantly to the development of modern anatomy.[^o5]
