# Compact closed category

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Compact closed category"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **compact closed categories** are a general context for treating [dual objects](wikipedia://en/5560194). The idea of a dual object generalizes the more familiar concept of the [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) of a [finite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). So, the motivating example of a compact closed category is [**FdVect**](wikipedia://en/72243857), the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) having finite-dimensional vector spaces as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and [linear maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_maps) as [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_%28category_theory%29), with [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) as the [monoidal](wikipedia://en/330604) structure. Another example is [**Rel**](wikipedia://en/5930730), the category having [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) as objects and [relations](wikipedia://en/3931) as morphisms, with [Cartesian monoidal structure](wikipedia://en/43676277).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/3865249) $(\mathbf{C},\otimes,I)$ is **compact closed** if every object $A \in \mathbf C$ has a [dual object](wikipedia://en/5560194). If this holds, the dual object is unique up to [canonical isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_isomorphism), and is denoted $A^*$.

In a bit more detail, an object $A^*$ is called the **[dual](wikipedia://en/5560194)** of $A$ if it is equipped with two morphisms called the **[unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_%28category_theory%29)** $\eta_A:I\to A^*\otimes A$ and the **counit** $\varepsilon_A:A\otimes A^*\to I$, satisfying the equations

$$\lambda_A\circ(\varepsilon_A\otimes id_A)\circ\alpha_{A,A^*,A}^{-1}\circ(id_A\otimes\eta_A)\circ\rho_A^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_A$$

and

$$\rho_{A^*}\circ(id_{A^*}\otimes\varepsilon_A)\circ\alpha_{A^*,A,A^*}\circ(\eta_A\otimes id_{A^*})\circ\lambda_{A^*}^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{A^*},$$

where $\lambda,\rho$ are the introduction of the unit on the left and right, respectively, and $\alpha$ is the associator.

For clarity, we rewrite the above compositions diagrammatically. In order for $(\mathbf{C},\otimes,I)$ to be compact closed, we need the following composites to equal $\mathrm{id}_A$:

$$A\xrightarrow{\cong} A\otimes I\xrightarrow{A\otimes\eta}A\otimes (A^*\otimes A)\xrightarrow{\cong} (A\otimes A^*)\otimes A\xrightarrow{\epsilon\otimes A} I\otimes A\xrightarrow{\cong} A$$

and $\mathrm{id}_{A^*}$:

$$A^*\xrightarrow{\cong} I\otimes A^*\xrightarrow{\eta\otimes A^*}(A^*\otimes A)\otimes A^*\xrightarrow{\cong} A^*\otimes (A\otimes A^*)\xrightarrow{A^* \otimes\epsilon} A^*\otimes I\xrightarrow{\cong} A^*$$

:::

## Non-symmetric compact closed categories {#Non-symmetric_compact_closed_categories}

Some authors, especially in [computational linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_linguistics), allow for the monoidal operation $\otimes$ to be non-symmetric.

Suppose $(\mathbf{C},\otimes,I)$ is a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604), not necessarily symmetric, such as in the case of a [pregroup grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregroup_grammar). The above notion of having a dual $A^*$ for each object *A* is replaced by that of having both a left and a right [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint), $A^l$ and $A^r$, with a corresponding left unit $\eta^l_A:I\to A\otimes A^l$, right unit $\eta^r_A:I\to A^r\otimes A$, left counit $\varepsilon^l_A:A^l\otimes A\to I$, and right counit $\varepsilon^r_A:A\otimes A^r\to I$. These must satisfy the four **yanking conditions**, namely that each of the following compositions are identity morphisms:[^o5]

$$A\to A\otimes I\xrightarrow{\eta^r}A\otimes (A^r\otimes A)\to (A\otimes A^r)\otimes A\xrightarrow{\epsilon^r} I\otimes A\to A$$

$$A\to I\otimes A\xrightarrow{\eta^l}(A\otimes A^l)\otimes A\to A\otimes (A^l \otimes A)\xrightarrow{\epsilon^l} A\otimes I\to A$$

and

$$A^r\to I\otimes A^r\xrightarrow{\eta^r}(A^r\otimes A)\otimes A^r\to A^r\otimes (A\otimes A^r)\xrightarrow{\epsilon^r} A^r\otimes I\to A^r$$

$$A^l\to A^l\otimes I\xrightarrow{\eta^l}A^l\otimes (A\otimes A^l)\to (A^l\otimes A)\otimes A^l \xrightarrow{\epsilon^l} I\otimes A^l\to A^l$$

That is, in the general case, a compact closed category is both left and right-[rigid](wikipedia://en/25383437), and [biclosed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biclosed_monoidal_category).

Non-symmetric compact closed categories find applications in [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), in the area of [categorial grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorial_grammar) and specifically in [pregroup grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregroup_grammar), where the distinct left and right adjoints are required to capture word-order in sentences. In this context, compact closed monoidal categories are called ([Lambek](wikipedia://en/1250498)) **[pregroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregroup)**.[^o5]

## Properties {#Properties}

Compact closed categories are a special case of [monoidal closed categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoidal_closed_category), which in turn are a special case of [closed categories](wikipedia://en/1397197).

Compact closed categories are precisely the [symmetric](wikipedia://en/3865249) [autonomous categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_category). They are also [\*-autonomous](wikipedia://en/3865506).

Every compact closed category **C** admits a [trace](wikipedia://en/7984007). Namely, for every morphism $f:A\otimes C\to B\otimes C$, one can define

$$\mathrm{Tr_{A,B}^C}(f)=\rho_B\circ(id_B\otimes\varepsilon_C)\circ\alpha_{B,C,C^*}\circ(f\otimes C^*)\circ\alpha_{A,C,C^*}^{-1}\circ(id_A\otimes\eta_{C^*})\circ\rho_A^{-1}:A\to B$$

which can be shown to be a proper trace. It helps to draw this diagrammatically: $A\xrightarrow{\cong}A\otimes I\xrightarrow{A\otimes\eta_{C^*}}A\otimes (C\otimes C^*)\xrightarrow{\cong}(A\otimes C)\otimes C^* \xrightarrow{\;\;f\otimes C^*\;\;}(B\otimes C)\otimes C^*\xrightarrow{\cong}B\otimes(C\otimes C^*)\xrightarrow{B\otimes\varepsilon_C}B\otimes I\xrightarrow{\cong}B.$

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The canonical example is the category **FdVect** with finite-dimensional [vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_spaces) as objects and [linear maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_maps) as morphisms. Here $A^*$ is the usual dual of the vector space $A$.

The category of finite-dimensional [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) of any group is also compact closed.

The category **Vect**, with *all* vector spaces as objects and linear maps as morphisms, is not compact closed; it is symmetric monoidal closed.

### Simplex category {#Simplex_category}

The [simplex category](wikipedia://en/1028841) can be used to construct an example of non-symmetric compact closed category. The **simplex category** is the category of non-zero [finite ordinals](wikipedia://en/21474) (viewed as [totally ordered sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_ordered_set)); its morphisms are order-preserving ([monotone](wikipedia://en/4719864)) maps. We make it into a monoidal category by moving to the [arrow category](wikipedia://en/840950#Arrow_category), so the objects are morphisms of the original category, and the morphisms are [commuting squares](wikipedia://en/138484#Examples). Then the tensor product of the arrow category is the original composition operator. The left and right adjoints are the min and max operators; specifically, for a monotone map *f* one has the right adjoint

$$f^r(n) = \sup \{m \in \mathbb{N} \mid f(m) \le n\}$$

and the left adjoint

$$f^l(n) = \inf \{m \in \mathbb{N} \mid n \le f(m)\}$$

The left and right units and counits are:

$$\mbox {id} \le f \circ f^l\qquad\mbox{(left unit)}$$

$$\,\mbox {id} \le f^r \circ f\quad\ \ \ \mbox{(right unit)}$$

$$f^l \circ f \le \mbox {id}\qquad\mbox{(left counit)}$$

$$f \circ f^r \le \mbox {id}\qquad\mbox{(right counit)}$$

One of the yanking conditions is then

$$f = f \circ \mbox {id} \le f \circ (f^r \circ f) = (f \circ f^r) \circ f \le \mbox {id} \circ f = f.$$

The others follow similarly. The correspondence can be made clearer by writing the arrow $\to$ instead of $\le$, and using $\otimes$ for function composition $\circ$.

### Dagger compact category {#Dagger_compact_category}

A [dagger symmetric monoidal category](wikipedia://en/7156065) which is compact closed is a [dagger compact category](wikipedia://en/7155145).

:::

## Rigid category {#Rigid_category}

A monoidal category that is not symmetric, but otherwise obeys the duality axioms above, is known as a [rigid category](wikipedia://en/25383437). A monoidal category where every object has a left (resp. right) dual is also sometimes called a **left** (resp. right) **autonomous** category. A monoidal category where every object has both a left and a right dual is sometimes called an [autonomous category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_category). An autonomous category that is also [symmetric](wikipedia://en/3865249) is then a compact closed category.
