# Topological module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **topological module** is a [module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Module_%28algebra%29) over a [topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097) such that [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) and addition are [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A module topology is the finest topology such that [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) and addition are [continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29). A finitely generated module topology is a [topological ring](wikipedia://en/60097). Note that this general definition of a module topology does not need to have a ring structure, it merely needs the existence of addition and scalar multiplication. [^o4]

A [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) is a topological module over a [topological field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_field).

An [abelian](wikipedia://en/2974) [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) can be considered as a topological module over $\mathbb{Z},$ where $\mathbb{Z}$ is the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology).

A topological ring is a topological module over each of its [subrings](wikipedia://en/24873145).

A more complicated example is the $I$-[adic topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adic_topology) on a ring and its modules. Let $I$ be an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) of a ring $R.$ The sets of the form $x + I^n$ for all $x \in R$ and all positive integers $n,$ form a [base](wikipedia://en/59220) for a topology on $R$ that makes $R$ into a topological ring. Then for any left $R$-module $M,$ the sets of the form $x + I^n M,$ for all $x \in M$ and all positive integers $n,$ form a base for a topology on $M$ that makes $M$ into a topological module over the topological ring $R.$
