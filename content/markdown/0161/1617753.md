# Giuseppe Veronese

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giuseppe Veronese"}

**Giuseppe Veronese** (7 May 1854 – 17 July 1917) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was born in [Chioggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chioggia), near [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Veronese earned his [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) in mathematics from the Istituto Tecnico di Venezia in 1872.

## Work {#Work}

Although Veronese's work was severely criticised as unsound by [Peano](wikipedia://en/13043), he is now recognised as having priority on many ideas that have since become parts of [transfinite numbers](wikipedia://en/189734) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), and as one of the respected authorities of the time, his work served to focus Peano and others on the need for greater rigor.

He is particularly noted for his hypothesis of relative continuity which was the foundation for his development of the first non-[Archimedean](wikipedia://en/264158) [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear) [continuum](wikipedia://en/24758677).

Veronese produced several significant monographs. The most famous appeared in 1891, *Fondamenti di geometria a più dimensioni e a più specie di unità rettilinee esposti in forma elementare*, normally referred to as *Fondamenti di geometria* to distinguish it from Veronese' other works also styled *Fondamenti*. It was this work that was most severely criticised by both Peano and [Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216), however [Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180) described it as *masterful* and [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) as *profound*.
