# Reinhold Baer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reinhold Baer"}

**Reinhold Baer** (22 July 1902 – 22 October 1979) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is most known for his work in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923). He introduced the mathematical concept of [injective modules](wikipedia://en/473775) in 1940. [Baer rings](wikipedia://en/7455223), [Baer groups](wikipedia://en/34959071), and [Baer subplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baer_subplane) are all named, [eponymously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eponym), for him.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Reinhold Baer studied [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) for a year at [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover), before he decided to study [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) at [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg) in 1921. While he was at [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) in 1922 he was influenced by [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) and [Hellmuth Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368525). In 1924 he won a scholarship for specially gifted students. Baer wrote up his doctoral dissertation and it was published in [Crelle's Journal](wikipedia://en/973547) in 1927.

Baer accepted a post at [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt) in 1928. There, he published [Ernst Steinitz](wikipedia://en/3966685)'s "Algebraische Theorie der Körper" with [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118), first published in Crelle's Journal in 1910.

While Reinhold Baer was with his wife, Marianne, in Austria, [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler) and the [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) came into power.[^o5] Both of Baer's parents were Jewish; he was for this reason informed that his services at Halle were no longer required. [Louis Mordell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mordell) invited him to go to [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester), and Baer accepted.

Baer stayed at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and was a visiting scholar at the nearby [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1935 to 1937.[^o6] For a short while he lived in [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina). From 1938 to 1956 he worked at the [University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana-Champaign). He returned to Germany in 1956.

According to biographer K. W. Gruenberg,

The rapid development of [lattice theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_theory) in the mid-thirties suggested that [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) should be viewed as a special kind of lattice, the [lattice](wikipedia://en/469304) of all [subspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspace_%28linear_algebra%29) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370)... \[*Linear Algebra and Projective Geometry* (1952)\] is an account of the representation of vector spaces over [division rings](wikipedia://en/9067), of projectivities by semi-linear transformations and of dualities by semi-bilinear forms.[^o7]

Baer's most important work, however, was in group theory, including on the extension problem for groups, finiteness conditions, soluble and nilpotent groups.[^o5]

He died of heart failure on 22 October, 1979, in Zürich, Switzerland.

In 2016 the [Reinhold Baer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_Baer_Prize) for the best Ph.D. thesis in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) was set up in his honour.[^o8]
