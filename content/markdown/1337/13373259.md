# Smooth coarea formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Smooth coarea formula"}

In [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243), the **smooth coarea formulas** relate integrals over the domain of certain mappings with integrals over their codomains.

:::

Let $\scriptstyle M,\,N$ be smooth [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) of respective dimensions $\scriptstyle m\,\geq\, n$. Let $\scriptstyle F:M\,\longrightarrow\, N$ be a smooth [surjection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjection) such that the [pushforward (differential)](wikipedia://en/1558510) of $\scriptstyle F$ is surjective almost everywhere. Let $\scriptstyle\varphi:M\,\longrightarrow\, [0,\infty)$ a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055). Then, the following two equalities hold:

$$\int_{x\in M}\varphi(x)\,dM = \int_{y\in N}\int_{x\in F^{-1}(y)}\varphi(x)\frac{1}{N\!J\;F(x)}\,dF^{-1}(y)\,dN$$

$$\int_{x\in M}\varphi(x)N\!J\;F(x)\,dM = \int_{y\in N}\int_{x\in F^{-1}(y)} \varphi(x)\,dF^{-1}(y)\,dN$$

where $\scriptstyle N\!J\;F(x)$ is the [normal Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of $\scriptstyle F$, i.e. the determinant of the derivative restricted to the orthogonal complement of its kernel.

Note that from [Sard's lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sard's_lemma) almost every point $\scriptstyle y\,\in\, N$ is a regular point of $\scriptstyle F$ and hence the set $\scriptstyle F^{-1}(y)$ is a Riemannian submanifold of $\scriptstyle M$, so the integrals in the right-hand side of the formulas above make sense.
