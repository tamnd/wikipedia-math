# Frey curve

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frey curve"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Frey curve** or **Frey–Hellegouarch** curve is the [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225)

$$y^2 = x(x - \alpha)(x + \beta)$$

associated with an ABC triple $\alpha+\beta=\gamma$. This relates properties of solutions of equations to elliptic curves. This curve was popularized in its application to [Fermat’s Last Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat’s_Last_Theorem) where one investigates a (hypothetical) solution of [Fermat's equation](wikipedia://en/19021953)

:::

$$a^\ell + b^\ell = c^\ell.$$

The curve is named after [Gerhard Frey](wikipedia://en/1021312) and (sometimes) Yves Hellegouarch.

## History {#History}

(txt) came up with the idea of associating solutions $(a,b,c)$ of Fermat's equation with a completely different mathematical object: an elliptic curve.[^n1] If ℓ is an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) and *a*, *b*, and *c* are positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) such that

$$a^\ell + b^\ell = c^\ell,$$

then a corresponding Frey curve is an algebraic curve given by the equation

$$y^2 = x(x - a^\ell)(x + b^\ell),$$

or, equivalently

$$y^2 = x(x - a^\ell)(x - c^\ell).$$

This is a nonsingular algebraic curve of [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_of_an_algebraic_curve) one defined over **Q**, and its [projective completion](wikipedia://en/21861125) is an elliptic curve over **Q**.

(txt) called attention to the unusual properties of the same curve as Hellegouarch, which became called a Frey curve. This provided a bridge between Fermat and Taniyama by showing that a [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to Fermat's Last Theorem would create such a curve that would not be modular. The conjecture attracted considerable interest when (Frey 1986) suggested that the [Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura–Weil_conjecture) implies Fermat's Last Theorem. However, his argument was not complete. In 1985, [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) proposed that a Frey curve could not be modular and provided a partial proof of this. This showed that a proof of the semistable case of the Taniyama–Shimura conjecture would imply Fermat's Last Theorem. Serre did not provide a complete proof and what was missing became known as the [epsilon conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon_conjecture) or ε-conjecture. In the summer of 1986, (Ribet 1990) proved the epsilon conjecture, thereby proving that the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil conjecture implies Fermat's Last Theorem.[^n2]
