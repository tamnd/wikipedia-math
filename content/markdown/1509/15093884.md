# Self-complementary graph

*Image: Self-complementary NZ graph.svg.* solid #2878BD dashed red Graph $A$ is isomorphic to its complement.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Self-complementary graph"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **self-complementary graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) which is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to its [complement](wikipedia://en/1175649). The simplest non-trivial self-complementary graphs are the 4-vertex [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) and the 5-vertex [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Every [Paley graph](wikipedia://en/5719307) is self-complementary.[^o9] For example, the 3 × 3 [rook's graph](wikipedia://en/7324959) (the Paley graph of order nine) is self-complementary, by a symmetry that keeps the center vertex in place but exchanges the roles of the four side midpoints and four corners of the grid.[^o4] All [strongly regular](wikipedia://en/1399856) self-complementary graphs with fewer than 37 vertices are Paley graphs; however, there are strongly regular graphs on 37, 41, and 49 vertices that are not Paley graphs.[^o5]

The [Rado graph](wikipedia://en/7785594) is an infinite self-complementary graph.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

An $n$-vertex self-complementary graph has exactly half as many edges of the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816), i.e., $n(n - 1)/4$ edges, and (if there is more than one vertex) it must have [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) either 2 or 3.[^o9] Since $n(n - 1)$ must be divisible by 4, $n$ must be [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to 0 or 1 modulo 4; for instance, a 6-vertex graph cannot be self-complementary.

:::

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

The problems of checking whether two self-complementary graphs are isomorphic and of checking whether a given graph is self-complementary are [polynomial-time equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time_equivalent) to the general [graph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/1950766).[^o11]
