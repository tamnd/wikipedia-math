# Graph automorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph automorphism"}

In the mathematical field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **automorphism** of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is a form of [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741) in which the graph is [mapped](wikipedia://en/516931) onto itself while preserving the edge–[vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) connectivity.

:::

Formally, an automorphism of a graph $G = (V, E)$ is a [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) $\sigma$ of the vertex set $V$, such that the pair of vertices $(u, v)$ form an edge [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the pair $(\sigma(u), \sigma(v))$ also form an edge. That is, it is a [graph isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577) from $G$ to itself. Automorphisms may be defined in this way both for [directed graphs](wikipedia://en/19721986) and for [undirected graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph). The [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of two automorphisms is another automorphism, and the set of automorphisms of a given graph, under the composition operation, forms a [group](wikipedia://en/19447), the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the graph. In the opposite direction, by [Frucht's theorem](wikipedia://en/26259599), all groups can be represented as the automorphism group of a connected graph – indeed, of a [cubic graph](wikipedia://en/980508).[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Graph automorphism problem"}

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Constructing the automorphism group of a graph, in the form of a list of generators, is polynomial-time equivalent to the [graph isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/1950766), and therefore solvable in [quasi-polynomial time](wikipedia://en/25269049), that is with running time $2^{O((\log n)^c)}$ for some fixed $c > 0$.[^o6][^o7] Consequently, like the graph isomorphism problem, the problem of finding a graph's automorphism group is known to belong to the [complexity class](wikipedia://en/502426) [NP](wikipedia://en/21562), but not known to be in [P](wikipedia://en/658550) nor to be [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete), and therefore may be [NP-intermediate](wikipedia://en/4637081).

*Image: Petersen1 tiny.svg.* This drawing of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) displays a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of its symmetries, isomorphic to the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) *D*<sub>5</sub>, but the graph has additional symmetries that are not present in the drawing. For example, since the graph is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/1577896), all edges are equivalent.

The easier problem of testing whether a graph has any symmetries (nontrivial automorphisms), known as the **graph automorphism problem**, also has no known [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) solution.[^o8] There is a [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) algorithm for solving the graph automorphism problem for graphs where vertex degrees are bounded by a constant.[^o9] The graph automorphism problem is polynomial-time [many-one reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-one_reducible) to the graph isomorphism problem, but the converse reduction is unknown.[^o6][^o10][^o11] By contrast, hardness is known when the automorphisms are constrained in a certain fashion; for instance, determining the existence of a fixed-point-free automorphism (an automorphism that fixes no vertex) is NP-complete, and the problem of counting such automorphisms is [♯P-complete](wikipedia://en/27925).[^o8][^o11]

:::

## Algorithms, software and applications {#Algorithms,_software_and_applications}

While no *worst-case* polynomial-time algorithms are known for the general Graph Automorphism problem, finding the automorphism group (and printing out an irredundant set of generators) for many large graphs arising in applications is rather easy. Several open-source software tools are available for this task, including [NAUTY](http://cs.anu.edu.au/people/bdm/nauty/),[^o13] [BLISS](http://www.tcs.hut.fi/Software/bliss/)[^o14] and [SAUCY](http://vlsicad.eecs.umich.edu/BK/SAUCY/).[^o15][^o16] SAUCY and BLISS are particularly efficient for sparse graphs, e.g., SAUCY processes some graphs with millions of vertices in mere seconds. However, BLISS and NAUTY can also produce [Canonical Labeling](wikipedia://en/20199287), whereas SAUCY is currently optimized for solving Graph Automorphism. An important observation is that for a graph on $n$ vertices, the automorphism group can be specified by no more than $n - 1$ generators, and the above software packages are guaranteed to satisfy this bound as a side-effect of their algorithms (minimal sets of generators are harder to find and are not particularly useful in practice). It also appears that the total support (i.e., the number of vertices moved) of all generators is limited by a linear function of $n$, which is important in runtime analysis of these algorithms. However, this has not been established for a fact, as of March 2012.

Practical applications of Graph Automorphism include [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) and other visualization tasks, solving structured instances of [Boolean Satisfiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_Satisfiability) arising in the context of [Formal verification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_verification) and [Logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain). [Molecular symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_symmetry) can predict or explain chemical properties.

## Symmetry display {#Symmetry_display}

Several [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813) researchers have investigated algorithms for drawing graphs in such a way that the automorphisms of the graph become visible as symmetries of the drawing. This may be done either by using a method that is not designed around symmetries, but that automatically generates symmetric drawings when possible,[^o18] or by explicitly identifying symmetries and using them to guide vertex placement in the drawing.[^o19] It is not always possible to display all symmetries of the graph simultaneously, so it may be necessary to choose which symmetries to display and which to leave unvisualized.

## Graph families defined by their automorphisms {#Graph_families_defined_by_their_automorphisms}

Several families of graphs are defined by having certain types of automorphisms:

- An [asymmetric graph](wikipedia://en/20249418) is an undirected graph with only the trivial automorphism.
- A [vertex-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/581175) is an undirected graph in which every vertex may be mapped by an automorphism into any other vertex.
- An [edge-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/584406) is an undirected graph in which every edge may be mapped by an automorphism into any other edge.
- A [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896) is a graph such that every pair of adjacent vertices may be mapped by an automorphism into any other pair of adjacent vertices.
- A [distance-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/7768222) is a graph such that every pair of vertices may be mapped by an automorphism into any other pair of vertices that are the same distance apart.
- A [semi-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/2554240) is a graph that is edge-transitive but not vertex-transitive.
- A [half-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/24229305) is a graph that is vertex-transitive and edge-transitive but not symmetric.
- A [skew-symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/17283742) is a directed graph together with a permutation σ on the vertices that maps edges to edges but reverses the direction of each edge. Additionally, σ is required to be an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450).
