# Horst Sachs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Horst Sachs"}

**Horst Sachs** (27 March 1927 – 25 April 2016) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), an expert in [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a recipient of the [Euler Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Medal) (2000).[^o3]

:::

He earned the degree of [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) (Dr. rer. nat.) from the [Martin-Luther-Universität Halle-Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin-Luther-Universität_Halle-Wittenberg) in 1958.[^o4] Following his retirement in 1992, he was [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at the Institute of Mathematics of the [Technische Universität Ilmenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Ilmenau).[^o5]

His encyclopedic book in [spectral graph theory](wikipedia://en/534914), *Spectra of Graphs. Theory and Applications* (with Dragos Cvetković and Michael Doob) has several editions and was translated in several languages.[^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11]

Two theorems in graph theory bear his name. One of them relates the coefficients of the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of a graph to certain structural features of the graph. Another one is a simple relation between the characteristic polynomials of a graph and its [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231). [Sachs subgraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachs_subgraph) are also named after Sachs.
