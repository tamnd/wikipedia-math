# Hamming ball

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hamming ball"}

*Image: Hamming ball.svg.* Hamming balls centered at the string "cab" with radius 1 (orange vertices and center), 2 (previous ball plus yellow vertices) and 3 (previous plus gray vertices).

In [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), a **Hamming ball** is a [metric ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_ball) for [Hamming distance](wikipedia://en/41227). The Hamming ball of radius $r$ centered at a [string](wikipedia://en/27701) $x$ over some [alphabet](wikipedia://en/4292269) (often the alphabet {0,1}) is the set of all strings of the same length that differ from $x$ in at most $r$ positions. This may be denoted using the standard notation for metric balls, $B(x,r)$. For an alphabet $X$ and a string $x$, the Hamming ball is a subset of the [Hamming space](wikipedia://en/4566542) $X^{|x|}$ of strings of the same length as $x$, and it is a proper subset whenever $r<|x|$. The name *Hamming ball* comes from [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869), where [error correction codes](wikipedia://en/4237207) can be defined as having disjoint Hamming balls around their codewords, and [covering codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_code) can be defined as having Hamming balls around the codeword whose union is the whole Hamming space.

:::

Some [local search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_search_%28constraint_satisfaction%29) algorithms for [SAT solvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT_solver) such as [WalkSAT](wikipedia://en/2853246) operate by using random guessing or covering codes to find a Hamming ball that contains a desired solution, and then searching within this Hamming ball to find the solution.

A version of [Helly's theorem](wikipedia://en/1409506) for Hamming balls is known: For Hamming balls of radius $r$ (in Hamming spaces of dimension greater than $r$), if a family of balls has the property that every subfamily of at most $2^{r+1}$ balls has a common intersection, then the whole family has a common intersection.
