# Monsky's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monsky's theorem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **Monsky's theorem** states that it is not possible to dissect a [square](wikipedia://en/659939) into an odd number of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) of equal area.[^o2] In other words, a square does not have an odd [equidissection](wikipedia://en/36643448).

:::

The problem was posed by Fred Richman in the *[American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly)* in 1967, after being solved for rational coordinates by John Thomas, and the solution was extended to all coordinates by [Paul Monsky](wikipedia://en/12673586) in 1970.[^o3][^o4][^o5] For more on the history of the problem, see [Equidissection#Monsky's\_theorem](wikipedia://en/36643448#Monsky's_theorem).

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

*Image: Square\_triangulation.svg.* A square can be divided into an even number of triangles of equal area (left), but into an odd number of only *approximately* equal area triangles (right).

Monsky's proof combines [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) and [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) techniques and in outline is as follows:

1. Take the square to be the unit square with vertices at (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0) and (1, 1). If there is a dissection into *n* triangles of equal area, then the area of each triangle is 1/*n*.
1. Colour each point in the square with one of three colours, depending on the [2-adic valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_norm) of its coordinates.
1. Show that a straight line can contain points of only two colours.
1. Use [Sperner's lemma](wikipedia://en/465067) to show that every [triangulation](wikipedia://en/1440970) of the square into triangles meeting edge-to-edge must contain at least one triangle whose vertices have three different colours.
1. Conclude from the straight-line property that a tricolored triangle must also exist in every dissection of the square into triangles, not necessarily meeting edge-to-edge.
1. Use Cartesian geometry to show that the 2-adic valuation of the area of a triangle whose vertices have three different colours is greater than 1. So every dissection of the square into triangles must contain at least one triangle whose area has a 2-adic valuation greater than 1.
1. If *n* is odd, then the 2-adic valuation of 1/*n* is 1, so it is impossible to dissect the square into triangles all of which have area 1/*n*.[^o8]

:::

## Optimal dissections {#Optimal_dissections}

By Monsky's theorem, it is necessary to have triangles with different areas to dissect a square into an odd number of triangles. [Lower bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) for the area differences that must occur to dissect a square into an odd numbers of triangles and the optimal dissections have been studied.[^o10][^o11][^o12]

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Because [affine transformations](wikipedia://en/38449) preserve equidissections, it follows more generally that [parallelograms](wikipedia://en/133496) (the affine images of squares) also do not have odd equidissections. [Centrally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrally_symmetric) polygons, more generally, do not have odd equidissections, nor do [polyominos](wikipedia://en/72634).[^o5]

The theorem can be generalized to higher dimensions: an *n*-dimensional [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) can only be divided into [simplices](wikipedia://en/39781) of equal volume if the number of simplices is a multiple of *n*!.[^o15]

:::
