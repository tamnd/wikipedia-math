# Partial differential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Partial differential"}

the partial differential

The [character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_%28symbol%29) **∂** ([Unicode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicode): U+2202) is a stylized [cursive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cursive) *[d](wikipedia://en/8123)* mainly used as a [mathematical symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_mathematical_symbols), usually to denote a [partial derivative](wikipedia://en/52565) such as ${\partial z}/{\partial x}$ (read as "the partial derivative of *z* with respect to *x*").[^o3][^o4] It is also used for [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of a set, the boundary operator in a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404), and the conjugate of the [Dolbeault operator](wikipedia://en/3189601) on smooth differential forms over a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742).

:::

It should be distinguished from other similar-looking symbols such as lowercase [Greek letter delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_letter_delta) (δ) or the lowercase Latin letter [eth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eth) (ð).

## History {#History}

The symbol was introduced originally in 1770 by [Nicolas de Condorcet](wikipedia://en/37749), who used it for a partial [differential](wikipedia://en/23997203), and adopted for the partial derivative by [Adrien-Marie Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695) in 1786.[^o6] It represents a specialized cursive type of the letter *d*, just as the [integral sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_sign) originates as a specialized type of a [long s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_s) (first used in print [by Leibniz](wikipedia://en/410712) in 1686). Use of the symbol was discontinued by Legendre, but it was taken up again by [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340) in 1841,[^o7] whose usage became widely adopted.[^o8]

## Names and coding {#Names_and_coding}

The symbol is variously referred to as "partial", "curly d"[^o10] or "Jacobi's delta",[^o8] or as "del"[^o11] (but this name is also used for the "nabla" symbol [∇](wikipedia://en/151925)). It may also be pronounced simply "dee",[^o12] "partial dee",[^o13][^o14] "doh",[^o15][^o16] "dow", "die",[^o17] or "diddly".

The Unicode character 2202 is accessed by [HTML entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTML_entities) `&#8706;` or `&part;`, and the equivalent [LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) symbol ([Computer Modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Modern) glyph: **$\partial$**) is accessed by `\partial`.

::: {#o19 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

**∂** is also used to denote the following:

- The [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) $\frac{\partial(x, y, z)}{\partial(u, v, w)}$.
- The [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of a set in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954).
- The boundary operator on a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404) in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).
- The boundary operator of a [differential graded algebra](wikipedia://en/2415863).
- The conjugate of the [Dolbeault operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbeault_operator) on [complex differential forms](wikipedia://en/2497815).
- The boundary *∂(S)* of a set of vertices *S* in a [graph](wikipedia://en/12401) is the set of edges leaving *S*, which defines a [cut](wikipedia://en/2180494).
