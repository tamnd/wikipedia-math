# Geometric topology (object)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geometric topology"}

*Image: CkMove.png.*

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **geometric topology** is a [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) one can put on the set *H* of [hyperbolic 3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1237700) of finite volume.

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

## Use {#Use}

Convergence in this topology is a crucial ingredient of [hyperbolic Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/4450009), a fundamental tool in the theory of hyperbolic 3-manifolds.

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The following is a definition due to [Troels Jorgensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troels_Jorgensen):

A sequence $\{M_i\}$ in *H* converges to *M* in *H* if there are

- a sequence of positive real numbers $\epsilon_i$ converging to 0, and
- a sequence of $(1+\epsilon_i)$-bi-Lipschitz [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) $\phi_i: M_{i, [\epsilon_i, \infty)} \rightarrow M_{[\epsilon_i, \infty)},$

where the domains and ranges of the maps are the $\epsilon_i$-thick parts of either the $M_i$'s or *M*.

:::

## Alternate definition {#Alternate_definition}

There is an alternate definition due to [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29). Gromov's topology utilizes the [Gromov-Hausdorff metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gromov-Hausdorff_metric) and is defined on *pointed* hyperbolic 3-manifolds. One essentially considers better and better bi-Lipschitz [homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) on larger and larger balls. This results in the same notion of convergence as above as the thick part is always connected; thus, a large ball will eventually encompass all of the thick part.

### On framed manifolds {#On_framed_manifolds}

As a further refinement, Gromov's metric can also be defined on *framed* hyperbolic 3-manifolds. This gives nothing new but this space can be explicitly identified with torsion-free [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) with the [Chabauty topology](wikipedia://en/7683674).
