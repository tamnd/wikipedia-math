# Differentiable stack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differentiable stack"}

A **differentiable stack** is the analogue in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) of an [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). It can be described either as a [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) over [differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219) which admits an atlas, or as a [Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923) up to [Morita equivalence](wikipedia://en/3296134).[^o2]

:::

Differentiable stacks are particularly useful to handle spaces with [singularities](wikipedia://en/54240) (i.e. orbifolds, leaf spaces, quotients), which appear naturally in differential geometry but are not differentiable manifolds. For instance, differentiable stacks have applications in [foliation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foliation_theory),[^o3] [Poisson geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_geometry)[^o4] and [twisted K-theory](wikipedia://en/4297226).[^o5]

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Definition 1 (via groupoid fibrations) {#Definition_1_(via_groupoid_fibrations)}

Recall that a [category fibred in groupoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_fibered_in_groupoids) (also called a **groupoid fibration**) consists of a category $\mathcal{C}$ together with a functor $\pi: \mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Mfd}$ to the [category of differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/10059981) such that

1. $\mathcal{C}$ is a [fibred category](wikipedia://en/2086749), i.e. for any object $u$ of $\mathcal{C}$ and any arrow $V \to U$ of $\mathrm{Mfd}$ there is an arrow $v \to u$ lying over $V \to U$;
1. for every [commutative triangle](wikipedia://en/138484) $W \to V \to U$ in $\mathrm{Mfd}$ and every arrows $w \to u$ over $W \to U$ and $v \to u$ over $V \to U$, there exists a unique arrow $w \to v$ over $W \to V$ making the triangle $w \to v \to u$ commute.

These properties ensure that, for every object $U$ in $\mathrm{Mfd}$, one can define its **fibre**, denoted by $\pi^{-1}(U)$ or $\mathcal{C}_U$, as the [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959) of $\mathcal{C}$ made up by all objects of $\mathcal{C}$ lying over $U$ and all morphisms of $\mathcal{C}$ lying over $id_U$. By construction, $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is a [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543), thus explaining the name. A **stack** is a groupoid fibration satisfied further glueing properties, expressed in terms of [descent](wikipedia://en/361449).

Any manifold $X$ defines its [slice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slice_category) $F_X = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Mfd}} (-, X)$, whose objects are pairs $(U,f)$ of a manifold $U$ and a smooth map $f: U \to X$; then $F_X \to \mathrm{Mfd}, (U,f) \mapsto U$ is a groupoid fibration which is actually also a stack. A morphism $\mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ of groupoid fibrations is called a **representable submersion** if

- for every manifold $U$ and any morphism $F_U \to \mathcal{D}$, the [fibred product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibred_product) $\mathcal{C} \times_{\mathcal{D}} F_U$ is representable, i.e. it is isomorphic to $F_V$ (for some manifold $V$) as groupoid fibrations;
- the induced smooth map $V \to U$ is a [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413).

A **differentiable stack** is a stack $\pi: \mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Mfd}$ together with a special kind of representable submersion $F_X \to \mathcal{C}$ (every submersion $V \to U$ described above is asked to be [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873)), for some manifold $X$. The map $F_X \to \mathcal{C}$ is called atlas, presentation or cover of the stack $X$.[^o9][^o10]

### Definition 2 (via 2-functors) {#Definition_2_(via_2-functors)}

Recall that a **[prestack](wikipedia://en/39547490)** (of groupoids) on a category $\mathcal{C}$, also known as a 2-[presheaf](wikipedia://en/4840944), is a [2-functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-functor) $X: \mathcal{C}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$, where $\mathrm{Grp}$ is the [2-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_category) of (set-theoretical) [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543), their morphisms, and the natural transformations between them. A [stack](wikipedia://en/13185596) is a prestack satisfying further glueing properties (analogously to the glueing properties satisfied by a sheaf). In order to state such properties precisely, one needs to define (pre)stacks on a [site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_%28mathematics%29), i.e. a category equipped with a [Grothendieck topology](wikipedia://en/12910).

Any object $M \in \mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{C})$ defines a stack $\underline{M} := \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,M)$, which associated to another object $N \in \mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{C})$ the groupoid $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(N,M)$ of [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) from $N$ to $M$. A stack $X: \mathcal{C}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$ is called **geometric** if there is an object $M \in \mathrm{Obj}(\mathcal{C})$ and a morphism of stacks $\underline{M} \to X$ (often called atlas, presentation or cover of the stack $X$) such that

- the morphism $\underline{M} \to X$ is representable, i.e. for every object $Y$ in $\mathcal{C}$ and any morphism $Y \to X$ the [fibred product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibred_product) $\underline{M} \times_X \underline{Y}$ is isomorphic to $\underline{Z}$ (for some object $Z$) as stacks;
- the induces morphism $Z \to Y$ satisfies a further property depending on the category $\mathcal{C}$ (e.g., for manifold it is asked to be a [submersion](wikipedia://en/356413)).

A **differentiable stack** is a stack on $\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Mfd}$, the [category of differentiable manifolds](wikipedia://en/10059981) (viewed as a site with the usual open covering topology), i.e. a 2-functor $X: \mathrm{Mfd}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$, which is also geometric, i.e. admits an atlas $\underline{M} \to X$ as described above.[^o12][^o13]

Note that, replacing $\mathrm{Mfd}$ with the category of [affine schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme), one recovers the standard notion of [algebraic stack](wikipedia://en/717377). Similarly, replacing $\mathrm{Mfd}$ with the category of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), one obtains the definition of topological stack.

### Definition 3 (via Morita equivalences) {#Definition_3_(via_Morita_equivalences)}

Recall that a **[Lie groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923)** consists of two differentiable manifolds $G$ and $M$, together with two [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873) [submersions](wikipedia://en/356413) $s,t: G \to M$, as well as a partial multiplication map $m: G \times_M G \to G$, a unit map $u: M \to G$, and an inverse map $i: G \to G$, satisfying group-like compatibilities.

Two Lie groupoids $G \rightrightarrows M$ and $H \rightrightarrows N$ are **Morita equivalent** if there is a principal bi-bundle $P$ between them, i.e. a principal right $H$-bundle $P \to M$, a principal left $G$-bundle $P \to N$, such that the two actions on $P$ commutes. Morita equivalence is an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) between Lie groupoids, weaker than isomorphism but strong enough to preserve many geometric properties.

A **differentiable stack**, denoted as $[M/G]$, is the Morita equivalence class of some Lie groupoid $G \rightrightarrows M$.[^o9][^o15]

### Equivalence between the definitions 1 and 2 {#Equivalence_between_the_definitions_1_and_2}

Any fibred category $\mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Mdf}$ defines the 2-sheaf $X: \mathrm{Mdf}^{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}, U \mapsto \pi^{-1}(U)$. Conversely, any prestack $X: \mathrm{Mdf}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$ gives rise to a category $\mathcal{C}$, whose objects are pairs $(U,x)$ of a manifold $U$ and an object $x \in X(U)$, and whose morphisms are maps $\phi: (U,x) \to (V,y)$ such that $X (\phi) (y) = x$. Such $\mathcal{C}$ becomes a fibred category with the functor $\mathcal{C} \to \mathrm{Mdf}, (U,x) \mapsto U$.

The glueing properties defining a stack in the first and in the second definition are equivalent; similarly, an atlas in the sense of Definition 1 induces an atlas in the sense of Definition 2 and vice versa.[^o9]

### Equivalence between the definitions 2 and 3 {#Equivalence_between_the_definitions_2_and_3}

Every Lie groupoid $G \rightrightarrows M$ gives rise to the differentiable stack $BG: \mathrm{Mfd}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$, which sends any manifold $N$ to the category of $G$-[torsors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsor) on $N$ (i.e. $G$-[principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950)). Any other Lie groupoid in the Morita class of $G \rightrightarrows M$ induces an isomorphic stack.

Conversely, any differentiable stack $X: \mathrm{Mfd}^\text{opp} \to \mathrm{Grp}$ is of the form $BG$, i.e. it can be represented by a Lie groupoid. More precisely, if $\underline{M} \to X$ is an atlas of the stack $X$, then one defines the Lie groupoid $G_X:= M \times_{X} M \rightrightarrows M$ and checks that $BG_X$ is isomorphic to $X$.

A theorem by [Dorette Pronk](wikipedia://en/77120772) states an equivalence of [bicategories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicategories) between differentiable stacks according to the first definition and Lie groupoids up to Morita equivalence.[^o18]

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any manifold $M$ defines a differentiable stack $\underline{M} := \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Hom}}(-,M)$, which is trivially presented by the identity morphism $\underline{M} \to \underline{M}$. The stack $\underline{M}$ corresponds to the Morita equivalence class of the [unit groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923#Trivial_and_extreme_cases) $u(M) \rightrightarrows M$.
- Any [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ defines a differentiable stack $BG$, which sends any manifold $N$ to the category of $G$-principal bundle on $N$. It is presented by the trivial stack morphism $\underline{pt} \to BG$, sending a point to the [universal $G$-bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570) over the [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) of $G$. The stack $BG$ corresponds to the Morita equivalence class of $G \rightrightarrows \{ *\}$ seen as a Lie groupoid over a point (i.e., the Morita equivalence class of any transitive Lie groupoids with isotropy $G$).
- Any [foliation](wikipedia://en/371227) $\mathcal{F}$ on a manifold $M$ defines a differentiable stack via its leaf spaces. It corresponds to the Morita equivalence class of the [holonomy groupoid](wikipedia://en/310923#Examples_from_differential_geometry) $\mathrm{Hol} (\mathcal{F}) \rightrightarrows M$.
- Any [orbifold](wikipedia://en/293504) is a differentiable stack, since it is the Morita equivalence class of a proper Lie groupoid with [discrete](wikipedia://en/56061) isotropies (hence [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_space), since isotropies of proper Lie groupoids are [compact](wikipedia://en/6042)).

### Quotient differentiable stack {#Quotient_differentiable_stack}

Given a [Lie group action](wikipedia://en/39904936) $a: M \times G \to M$ on $M$, its **quotient (differentiable) stack** is the differential counterpart of the [quotient (algebraic) stack](wikipedia://en/40579087) in algebraic geometry. It is defined as the stack $[M/G]$ associating to any manifold $X$ the category of principal $G$-bundles $P \to X$ and $G$-equivariant maps $\phi: P \to M$. It is a differentiable stack presented by the stack morphism $\underline{M} \to [M/G]$ defined for any manifold $X$ as

$$\underline{M}(X) = \mathrm{Hom}(X,M) \to [M/G](X), \quad f \mapsto (X \times G \to X, \phi_f)$$

where $\phi_f: X \times G \to M$ is the $G$-equivariant map $\phi_f = a \circ (f \circ \mathrm{pr}_1, \mathrm{pr}_2): (x,g) \mapsto f(x) \cdot g$.[^o12]

The stack $[M/G]$ corresponds to the Morita equivalence class of the action groupoid $M \times G \rightrightarrows M$. Accordingly, one recovers the following particular cases:

- if $M$ is a point, the differentiable stack $[M/G]$ coincides with $BG$
- if the action is [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_action) and [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_action) (and therefore the quotient $M/G$ is a manifold), the differentiable stack $[M/G]$ coincides with $\underline{M/G}$
- if the action is proper (and therefore the quotient $M/G$ is an orbifold), the differentiable stack $[M/G]$ coincides with the stack defined by the orbifold

:::

## Differential space {#Differential_space}

A **differentiable space** is a differentiable stack with trivial stabilizers. For example, if a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) [acts](wikipedia://en/39904936) freely but not necessarily properly on a manifold, then the quotient by it is in general not a manifold but a differentiable space.

## With Grothendieck topology {#With_Grothendieck_topology}

A differentiable stack *$X$* may be equipped with [Grothendieck topology](wikipedia://en/12910) in a certain way (see the reference). This gives the notion of a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) over *$X$*. For example, the sheaf $\Omega_X^p$ of differential $p$-forms over *$X$* is given by, for any *$x$* in *$X$* over a manifold *$U$*, letting $\Omega_X^p(x)$ be the space of *$p$*-forms on *$U$*. The sheaf $\Omega_X^0$ is called the [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf) on *$X$* and is denoted by $\mathcal{O}_X$. $\Omega_X^*$ comes with [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) and thus is a [complex of sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_of_sheaves) of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) over *$X$*: one thus has the notion of [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of *$X$*.

## Gerbes {#Gerbes}

An [epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539) between differentiable stacks $G \to X$ is called a [gerbe](wikipedia://en/822043) over *$X$* if $G \to G \times_X G$ is also an epimorphism. For example, if *$X$* is a stack, $BS^1 \times X \to X$ is a gerbe. A theorem of [Giraud](wikipedia://en/572753) says that $H^2(X, S^1)$ corresponds one-to-one to the set of gerbes over *$X$* that are locally isomorphic to $BS^1 \times X \to X$ and that come with trivializations of their [bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_%28geometry%29).[^o25]
