# Volkenborn integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Volkenborn integral"}

In mathematics, in the field of [p-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), the **Volkenborn integral** is a method of [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) for p-adic functions.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let :$f:\mathbb{Z}_p\to \mathbb{C}_p$ be a function from the [p-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) integers taking values in the p-adic numbers. The Volkenborn integral is defined by the limit, if it exists:

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} f(x) \, {\rm d}x = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{p^n} \sum_{x=0}^{p^n-1} f(x).$$

More generally, if

$$R_n = \left\{\left. x = \sum_{i=r}^{n-1} b_i p^i \right | b_i=0, \ldots, p-1 \text{ for } r<n \right\}$$

then

$$\int_K f(x) \, {\rm d}x = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{p^n} \sum_{x \in R_n \cap K} f(x).$$

This integral was defined by Arnt Volkenborn.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} 1 \, {\rm d}x = 1$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} x \, {\rm d}x = -\frac{1}{2}$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} x^2 \, {\rm d}x = \frac{1}{6}$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} x^k \, {\rm d}x = B_k$$

where $B_k$ is the k-th [Bernoulli number](wikipedia://en/4964).

The above four examples can be easily checked by direct use of the definition and [Faulhaber's formula](wikipedia://en/6738151).

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} {x \choose k} \, {\rm d}x = \frac{(-1)^k}{k+1}$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} (1 + a)^x \, {\rm d}x = \frac{\log(1+a)}{a}$$

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} e^{a x} \, {\rm d}x = \frac{a}{e^a-1}$$

The last two examples can be formally checked by expanding in the [Taylor series](wikipedia://en/30448) and integrating term-wise.

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \log_p(x+u) \, {\rm d}u = \psi_p(x)$$

with $\log_p$ the p-adic logarithmic function and $\psi_p$ the p-adic [digamma function](wikipedia://en/406935).

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

$$\int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} f(x+m) \, {\rm d}x = \int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} f(x) \, {\rm d}x+ \sum_{x=0}^{m-1} f'(x)$$

From this it follows that the Volkenborn-integral is not translation invariant.

If $P^t = p^t \mathbb{Z}_p$ then

$$\int_{P^t} f(x) \, {\rm d}x = \frac{1}{p^t} \int_{\mathbb{Z}_p} f(p^t x) \, {\rm d}x$$

:::

## Origin {#Origin}

The idea of integrating p-adic functions was initially proposed by F. Thomas and [François Bruhat](wikipedia://en/9169813). However, the definition of their translation-invariant p-adic integral proved too restrictive for analytical and number-theoretical purposes. Arnt Volkenborn developed the generalized p-adic integral, later named after him, in his 1971 dissertation at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne). The Volkenborn integral allows integration of all locally analytic functions, such as [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213). It is used in the computation of the generalized p-adic Bernoulli numbers (like in the examples above) and other p-adic functions.
