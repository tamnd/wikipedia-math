::: {#o11 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 9-polytope"}

In nine-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **nine-dimensional polytope** or **9-polytope** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) contained by 8-polytope facets. Each [7-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-polytope) [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29) being shared by exactly two [8-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-polytope) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29).

:::

A **uniform 9-polytope** is one which is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), and constructed from [uniform 8-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821715) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647).

## Regular 9-polytopes {#Regular_9-polytopes}

Regular 9-polytopes can be represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s,t,u,v,w}, with **w** {p,q,r,s,t,u,v} 8-polytope [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_%28geometry%29).

There are exactly three such [convex regular 9-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_4):

1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [9-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-simplex)
1. {4,3,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [9-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-cube)
1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,3,4} - [9-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9-orthoplex)

There are no nonconvex regular 9-polytopes.

## Euler characteristic {#Euler_characteristic}

The topology of any given 9-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o14]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o14]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o14]

## Regular and uniform honeycombs {#Regular_and_uniform_honeycombs}

*Image: Coxeter diagram affine rank9 correspondence.png.* Coxeter-Dynkin diagram correspondences between families and higher symmetry within diagrams. Nodes of the same color in each row represent identical mirrors. Black nodes are not active in the correspondence.

### Regular and uniform hyperbolic honeycombs {#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs}

There are no compact hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 9, groups that can generate honeycombs with all finite facets, and a finite [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). However, there are [4 paracompact hyperbolic Coxeter groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram#Rank_4_to_10) of rank 9, each generating uniform honeycombs in 8-space as permutations of rings of the Coxeter diagrams.
