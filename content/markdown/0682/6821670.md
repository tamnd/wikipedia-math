In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **five-dimensional polytope** (or **5-polytope** or **polyteron**) is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) in [five-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/2851410), bounded by ([4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979)) [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647), pairs of which share a [polyhedral cell](wikipedia://en/149926#k-face).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A 5-polytope is a closed five-dimensional figure with [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034), [faces](wikipedia://en/149926), and [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29), and [4-faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-face). A vertex is a [point](wikipedia://en/593693) where five or more edges meet. An edge is a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860) where four or more faces meet, and a face is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) where three or more cells meet. A cell is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), and a 4-face is a [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979). Furthermore, the following requirements must be met:

1. Each cell must join exactly two 4-faces.
1. Adjacent 4-faces are not in the same four-dimensional [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862).
1. The figure is not a compound of other figures which meet the requirements.

:::

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The topology of any given 5-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o8]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o8]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o8]

## Classification {#Classification}

5-polytopes may be classified based on properties like "[convexity](wikipedia://en/6292)" and "[symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741)".

- A 5-polytope is *[convex](wikipedia://en/1817885)* if its boundary (including its cells, faces and edges) does not intersect itself and the line segment joining any two points of the 5-polytope is contained in the 5-polytope or its interior; otherwise, it is *non-convex*. Self-intersecting 5-polytopes are also known as [star polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_polytope), from analogy with the star-like shapes of the non-convex [Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot_polyhedra).
- A **uniform** 5-polytope has a [symmetry group](wikipedia://en/28186) under which all vertices are equivalent, and its facets are [uniform 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/1645899). The faces of a uniform polytope must be [regular](wikipedia://en/333306).
- A **semi-regular 5-polytope** contains two or more types of regular 4-polytope facets. There is only one such figure, called a [demipenteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demipenteract).
- A **regular 5-polytope** has all identical regular 4-polytope facets. All regular 5-polytopes are convex.
- A **prismatic 5-polytope** is constructed by a [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of two lower-dimensional polytopes. A prismatic 5-polytope is uniform if its factors are uniform. The [hypercube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penteract) is prismatic (product of a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) and a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)), but is considered separately because it has symmetries other than those inherited from its factors.
- A *4-space [tessellation](wikipedia://en/321671)* is the division of four-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) into a regular grid of polychoral facets. Strictly speaking, tessellations are not polytopes as they do not bound a "5D" volume, but we include them here for the sake of completeness because they are similar in many ways to polytopes. A *uniform 4-space tessellation* is one whose vertices are related by a [space group](wikipedia://en/463721) and whose facets are uniform 4-polytopes.

## Regular 5-polytopes {#Regular_5-polytopes}

Regular 5-polytopes can be represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) *p*,*q*,*r*,*s*, with *s* *p*,*q*,*r* polychoral [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [face](wikipedia://en/149926).

There are exactly three such [convex regular 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_4):

1. {3,3,3,3} – [5-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-simplex)
1. {4,3,3,3} – [5-cube](wikipedia://en/7184215)
1. {3,3,3,4} – [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615)

## Pyramids {#Pyramids}

Pyramidal 5-polytopes, or **5-pyramids**, can be generated by a 4-polytope **base** in a 4-space hyperplane connected to a point off the hyperplane. The 5-simplex is the simplest example with a 4-simplex base.
