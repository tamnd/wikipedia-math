::: {#o14 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 8-polytope"}

In [eight-dimensional](wikipedia://en/25555117) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **eight-dimensional polytope** or **8-polytope** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) contained by 7-polytope facets, each [6-polytope](wikipedia://en/26021427) [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29) being shared by exactly two [7-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-polytope) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29).

:::

A **uniform 8-polytope** is one which is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), and constructed from [uniform 7-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821719) facets.

## Regular 8-polytopes {#Regular_8-polytopes}

Regular 8-polytopes can be represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s,t,u,v}, with **v** {p,q,r,s,t,u} 7-polytope [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each [peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_%28geometry%29).

There are exactly three such [convex regular 8-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex):

1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [8-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-simplex)
1. {4,3,3,3,3,3,3} - [8-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cube)
1. {3,3,3,3,3,3,4} - [8-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-orthoplex)

There are no nonconvex regular 8-polytopes.

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The topology of any given 8-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o17]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, and is zero for all 8-polytopes, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o17]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o17]

## Uniform 8-polytopes by fundamental Coxeter groups {#Uniform_8-polytopes_by_fundamental_Coxeter_groups}

Uniform 8-polytopes with reflective symmetry can be generated by these four Coxeter groups, represented by permutations of rings of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram):

1. [Simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) family: A<sub>8</sub> \[3<sup>7</sup>\] -

   - 255 uniform 8-polytopes as permutations of rings in the group diagram, including two regular ones:
     1. {4,3<sup>6</sup>} - [8-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cube) or *octeract* -
     1. {3<sup>6</sup>,4} - [8-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-orthoplex) or *octacross* -

   - 191 uniform 8-polytopes as permutations of rings in the group diagram, including:

     1. {3,3,3,3,3,3<sup>1,1</sup>} - [8-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-orthoplex), **5<sub>11</sub>** -
1. [E-polytope family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiregular_E-polytope) E<sub>8</sub> family: \[3<sup>4,1,1</sup>\] -
   - 255 uniform 8-polytopes as permutations of rings in the group diagram, including:
     1. {3,3,3,3,3<sup>2,1</sup>} - [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset)'s semiregular **[4<sub>21</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_4_21_polytope)**,
     1. {3,3<sup>4,2</sup>} - the uniform **[1<sub>42</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_1_42_polytope)**, ,
     1. {3,3,3<sup>4,1</sup>} - the uniform **[2<sub>41</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosset_2_41_polytope)**,

### The A<sub>8</sub> family {#The_A8_family}

The A<sub>8</sub> family has symmetry of order 362880 (9 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)).

There are 135 forms based on all permutations of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) with one or more rings (128 + 8 − 1 cases). These are all enumerated below. Bowers-style acronym names are given in parentheses for cross-referencing.

See also a [list of 8-simplex polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_8-simplex_polytopes) for symmetric [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) graphs of these polytopes.

### The D<sub>8</sub> family {#The_D8_family}

The D<sub>8</sub> family has symmetry of order 5,160,960 (8 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) × 2<sup>7</sup>).

This family has 191 Wythoffian uniform polytopes, from 3 × 64 − 1 permutations of the D<sub>8</sub> [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) with one or more rings. 127 (2 × 64 − 1) are repeated from the B<sub>8</sub> family and 64 are unique to this family, all listed below.

### Regular and uniform hyperbolic honeycombs {#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs}

There are no compact hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 8, groups that can generate honeycombs with all finite facets, and a finite [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719).
