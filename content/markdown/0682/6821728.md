::: {#o14 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 6-polytope"}

In [six-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-dimensional_space) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **uniform [6-polytope](wikipedia://en/26021427)** is a six-dimensional [uniform polytope](wikipedia://en/6842392). A uniform polypeton is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive), and all [facets](wikipedia://en/7186647) are [uniform 5-polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338).

:::

The complete set of **convex uniform 6-polytopes** has not been determined, but most can be made as [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777) from a small set of [symmetry groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_groups). These construction operations are represented by the [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) of [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). Each combination of at least one ring on every connected group of nodes in the diagram produces a uniform 6-polytope.

The simplest uniform polypeta are [regular polypeta](wikipedia://en/518693): the [6-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-simplex) {3,3,3,3,3}, the [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462) (hexeract) {4,3,3,3,3}, and the [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518) (hexacross) {3,3,3,3,4}.

## History of discovery {#History_of_discovery}

- **[Regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693)**: (convex faces)
  - **1852**: [Ludwig Schläfli](wikipedia://en/1452408) proved in his manuscript *Theorie der vielfachen Kontinuität* that there are exactly 3 regular polytopes in 5 or more [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398).
- **Convex [semiregular polytopes](wikipedia://en/11684875)**: (Various definitions before Coxeter's **uniform** category)
  - **1900**: [Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) enumerated the list of nonprismatic semiregular convex polytopes with regular facets (convex regular polytera) in his publication *On the Regular and Semi-Regular Figures in Space of n Dimensions*.[^o16]
- **Convex uniform polytopes**:
  - **1940**: The search was expanded systematically by [H.S.M. Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Scott_MacDonald_Coxeter) in his publication *Regular and Semi-Regular Polytopes*.
- **Nonregular uniform star polytopes**: (similar to the [nonconvex uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2850448#Nonconvex_forms_with_convex_faces))
  - **Ongoing**: [Jonathan Bowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Bowers) and other researchers search for other non-convex uniform 6-polytopes, with a current count of 41348 known uniform 6-polytopes outside infinite families (convex and non-convex), excluding the prisms of the uniform 5-polytopes. The list is not proven complete.[^o17][^o18]

### The A<sub>6</sub> family {#The_A6_family}

There are 32+4−1=35 forms, derived by marking one or more nodes of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). All 35 are enumerated below. They are named by [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) from the Wythoff construction operations upon regular 6-simplex (heptapeton). Bowers-style acronym names are given in parentheses for cross-referencing.

The A<sub>6</sub> family has symmetry of order 5040 (7 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)).

The coordinates of uniform 6-polytopes with 6-simplex symmetry can be generated as permutations of simple integers in 7-space, all in hyperplanes with [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) (1,1,1,1,1,1,1).

### Non-Wythoffian 6-polytopes {#Non-Wythoffian_6-polytopes}

In 6 dimensions and above, there are an infinite amount of non-Wythoffian convex [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392): the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of the [grand antiprism](wikipedia://en/4058047) in 4 dimensions and any [regular polygon](wikipedia://en/333306) in 2 dimensions. It is not yet proven whether or not there are more.

## Notes on the Wythoff construction for the uniform 6-polytopes {#Notes_on_the_Wythoff_construction_for_the_uniform_6-polytopes}

Construction of the reflective 6-dimensional [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) are done through a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) process, and represented through a [Coxeter–Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/5358554), where each node represents a mirror. Nodes are ringed to imply which mirrors are active. The full set of uniform polytopes generated are based on the unique permutations of ringed nodes. Uniform 6-polytopes are named in relation to the [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) in each family. Some families have two regular constructors and thus may have two ways of naming them.

Here's the primary operators available for constructing and naming the uniform 6-polytopes.

The prismatic forms and bifurcating graphs can use the same truncation indexing notation, but require an explicit numbering system on the nodes for clarity.

::: {#o50 .table cue=table}

| Operation | Extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) | [Coxeter- Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent | t<sub>0</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Any regular 6-polytope |
| [Rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) | t<sub>1</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | The edges are fully truncated into single points. The 6-polytope now has the combined faces of the parent and dual. |
| Birectified | t<sub>2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Birectification reduces [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) to their [duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope). |
| [Truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) | t<sub>0,1</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Each original vertex is cut off, with a new face filling the gap. Truncation has a degree of freedom, which has one solution that creates a uniform truncated 6-polytope. The 6-polytope has its original faces doubled in sides, and contains the faces of the dual. *Image: Cube truncation sequence.svg.* |
| [Bitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated) | t<sub>1,2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Bitrunction transforms cells to their dual truncation. |
| Tritruncated | t<sub>2,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Tritruncation transforms 4-faces to their dual truncation. |
| [Cantellated](wikipedia://en/6317514) | t<sub>0,2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | In addition to vertex truncation, each original edge is *beveled* with new rectangular faces appearing in their place. A uniform cantellation is halfway between both the parent and dual forms. *Image: Cube cantellation sequence.svg.* |
| Bicantellated | t<sub>1,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | In addition to vertex truncation, each original edge is *beveled* with new rectangular faces appearing in their place. A uniform cantellation is halfway between both the parent and dual forms. |
| [Runcinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcination_%28geometry%29) | t<sub>0,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Runcination reduces cells and creates new cells at the vertices and edges. |
| Biruncinated | t<sub>1,4</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Runcination reduces cells and creates new cells at the vertices and edges. |
| [Stericated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterication_%28geometry%29) | t<sub>0,4</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Sterication reduces 4-faces and creates new 4-faces at the vertices, edges, and faces in the gaps. |
| Pentellated | t<sub>0,5</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | Pentellation reduces 5-faces and creates new 5-faces at the vertices, edges, faces, and cells in the gaps. ([expansion](wikipedia://en/6341070) operation for polypeta) |
| [Omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncation_%28geometry%29) | t<sub>0,1,2,3,4,5</sub>{p,q,r,s,t} |  | All five operators, truncation, cantellation, runcination, sterication, and pentellation are applied. |
