# Root datum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Root datum"}

In mathematical [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), the **root datum** of a connected split [reductive](wikipedia://en/1088386) [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) over a field is a generalization of a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) that determines the group up to isomorphism. They were introduced by [Michel Demazure](wikipedia://en/32535595) in [SGA III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck's_Séminaire_de_géométrie_algébrique), published in 1970.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **root datum** consists of a quadruple

$$(X^\ast, \Phi, X_\ast, \Phi^\vee),$$

where

- $X^\ast$ and $X_\ast$ are [free abelian groups](wikipedia://en/247296) of finite [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) together with a [perfect pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_pairing) between them with values in $\mathbb{Z}$ which we denote by ( , ) (in other words, each is identified with the dual of the other).
- $\Phi$ is a finite subset of $X^\ast$ and $\Phi^\vee$ is a finite subset of $X_\ast$ and there is a bijection from $\Phi$ onto $\Phi^\vee$, denoted by $\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee$.
- For each $\alpha\in\Phi$, $(\alpha, \alpha^\vee)=2$.
- For each $\alpha\in\Phi$, the map $x\mapsto x-(x,\alpha^\vee)\alpha$ induces an automorphism of the root datum (in other words it maps $\Phi$ to $\Phi$ and the induced action on $X_\ast$ maps $\Phi^\vee$ to $\Phi^\vee$)

The elements of $\Phi$ are called the **roots** of the root datum, and the elements of $\Phi^\vee$ are called the **coroots**.

If $\Phi$ does not contain $2\alpha$ for any $\alpha\in\Phi$, then the root datum is called **reduced**.

:::

## The root datum of an algebraic group {#The_root_datum_of_an_algebraic_group}

If $G$ is a reductive algebraic group over an [algebraically closed field](wikipedia://en/1018) $K$ with a split maximal torus $T$ then its **root datum** is a quadruple

$$(X^*, \Phi, X_*, \Phi^{\vee}),$$

where

- $X^*$ is the lattice of characters of the maximal torus,
- $X_*$ is the dual lattice (given by the 1-parameter subgroups),
- $\Phi$ is a set of roots,
- $\Phi^{\vee}$ is the corresponding set of coroots.

A connected split reductive algebraic group over $K$ is uniquely determined (up to isomorphism) by its root datum, which is always reduced. Conversely for any root datum there is a reductive algebraic group. A root datum contains slightly more information than the [Dynkin diagram](wikipedia://en/277109), because it also determines the center of the group.

For any root datum $(X^*, \Phi, X_*, \Phi^{\vee})$, we can define a **dual root datum** $(X_*, \Phi^{\vee},X^*, \Phi)$ by switching the characters with the 1-parameter subgroups, and switching the roots with the coroots.

If $G$ is a connected reductive algebraic group over the algebraically closed field $K$, then its [Langlands dual group](wikipedia://en/6789133) ${}^L G$ is the complex connected reductive group whose root datum is dual to that of $G$.
