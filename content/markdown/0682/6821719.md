::: {#o12 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform 7-polytope"}

In [seven-dimensional](wikipedia://en/25546470) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **7-polytope** is a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) contained by 6-polytope facets. Each [5-polytope](wikipedia://en/6821670) [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_%28geometry%29) being shared by exactly two [6-polytope](wikipedia://en/26021427) [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29).

:::

A **uniform 7-polytope** is one whose symmetry group is [transitive on vertices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) and whose facets are [uniform 6-polytopes](wikipedia://en/6821728).

::: {#o14 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Regular 7-polytope"}

## Regular 7-polytopes {#Regular_7-polytopes}

Regular 7-polytopes are represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {p,q,r,s,t,u} with **u** {p,q,r,s,t} 6-polytopes [facets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facet_%28mathematics%29) around each 4-face.

There are exactly three such [convex regular 7-polytopes](wikipedia://en/505253#Convex_4):

1. {3,3,3,3,3,3} - [7-simplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-simplex)
1. {4,3,3,3,3,3} - [7-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-cube)
1. {3,3,3,3,3,4} - [7-orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-orthoplex)

There are no nonconvex regular 7-polytopes.

:::

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

The topology of any given 7-polytope is defined by its [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) and [torsion coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_coefficient_%28topology%29).[^o16]

The value of the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) used to characterise polyhedra does not generalize usefully to higher dimensions, whatever their underlying topology. This inadequacy of the Euler characteristic to reliably distinguish between different topologies in higher dimensions led to the discovery of the more sophisticated Betti numbers.[^o16]

Similarly, the notion of orientability of a polyhedron is insufficient to characterise the surface twistings of toroidal polytopes, and this led to the use of torsion coefficients.[^o16]

## The A<sub>7</sub> family {#The_A7_family}

The A<sub>7</sub> family has symmetry of order 40320 (8 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606)).

There are 71 (64 + 8 − 1) forms based on all permutations of the [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) with one or more rings. All 71 are enumerated below. [Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527)'s truncation names are given. Bowers names and acronym are also given for cross-referencing.

See also a [list of A7 polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_A7_polytopes) for symmetric [Coxeter plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) graphs of these polytopes.

## The D<sub>7</sub> family {#The_D7_family}

The D<sub>7</sub> family has symmetry of order 322560 (7 [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) × 2<sup>6</sup>).

This family has 3 × 32 − 1 = 95 Wythoffian uniform polytopes, generated by marking one or more nodes of the D<sub>7</sub> [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram). Of these, 63 (2 × 32 − 1) are repeated from the B<sub>7</sub> family and 32 are unique to this family, listed below. Bowers names and acronym are given for cross-referencing.

See also [list of D7 polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_D7_polytopes) for Coxeter plane graphs of these polytopes.

### Regular and uniform hyperbolic honeycombs {#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs}

There are no compact hyperbolic Coxeter groups of rank 7, groups that can generate honeycombs with all finite facets, and a finite [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719). ## Notes on the Wythoff construction for the uniform 7-polytopes {#Notes_on_the_Wythoff_construction_for_the_uniform_7-polytopes}

The reflective 7-dimensional [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392) are constructed through a [Wythoff construction](wikipedia://en/2950777) process, and represented by a [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), where each node represents a mirror. An active mirror is represented by a ringed node. Each combination of active mirrors generates a unique uniform polytope. Uniform polytopes are named in relation to the [regular polytopes](wikipedia://en/518693) in each family. Some families have two regular constructors and thus may be named in two equally valid ways.

Here are the primary operators available for constructing and naming the uniform 7-polytopes.

The prismatic forms and bifurcating graphs can use the same truncation indexing notation, but require an explicit numbering system on the nodes for clarity.

::: {#o36 .table cue=table}

| Operation | Extended [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) | [Coxeter- Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram) | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent | t<sub>0</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Any regular 7-polytope |
| [Rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) | t<sub>1</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | The edges are fully truncated into single points. The 7-polytope now has the combined faces of the parent and dual. |
| Birectified | t<sub>2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Birectification reduces [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) to their [duals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_polytope). |
| [Truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) | t<sub>0,1</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Each original vertex is cut off, with a new face filling the gap. Truncation has a degree of freedom, which has one solution that creates a uniform truncated 7-polytope. The 7-polytope has its original faces doubled in sides, and contains the faces of the dual. *Image: Cube truncation sequence.svg.* |
| [Bitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitruncated) | t<sub>1,2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Bitrunction transforms cells to their dual truncation. |
| Tritruncated | t<sub>2,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Tritruncation transforms 4-faces to their dual truncation. |
| [Cantellated](wikipedia://en/6317514) | t<sub>0,2</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | In addition to vertex truncation, each original edge is *beveled* with new rectangular faces appearing in their place. A uniform cantellation is halfway between both the parent and dual forms. *Image: Cube cantellation sequence.svg.* |
| Bicantellated | t<sub>1,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | In addition to vertex truncation, each original edge is *beveled* with new rectangular faces appearing in their place. A uniform cantellation is halfway between both the parent and dual forms. |
| [Runcinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runcination_%28geometry%29) | t<sub>0,3</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Runcination reduces cells and creates new cells at the vertices and edges. |
| Biruncinated | t<sub>1,4</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Runcination reduces cells and creates new cells at the vertices and edges. |
| [Stericated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterication) | t<sub>0,4</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Sterication reduces 4-faces and creates new 4-faces at the vertices, edges, and faces in the gaps. |
| Pentellated | t<sub>0,5</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Pentellation reduces 5-faces and creates new 5-faces at the vertices, edges, faces, and cells in the gaps. |
| Hexicated | t<sub>0,6</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | Hexication reduces 6-faces and creates new 6-faces at the vertices, edges, faces, cells, and 4-faces in the gaps. ([expansion](wikipedia://en/6341070) operation for 7-polytopes) |
| [Omnitruncated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnitruncation_%28geometry%29) | t<sub>0,1,2,3,4,5,6</sub>{p,q,r,s,t,u} |  | All six operators, truncation, cantellation, runcination, sterication, pentellation, and hexication are applied. |

:::
