# E7½

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="E7½"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) **E<sub>7½</sub>** is a subalgebra of [E<sub>8</sub>](wikipedia://en/647994) containing [E<sub>7</sub>](wikipedia://en/649115) defined by Landsberg and Manivel in order to fill the "hole" in a dimension formula for the [exceptional series E<sub>*n*</sub>](wikipedia://en/5351858) of simple Lie algebras. This hole was observed by [Cvitanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predrag_Cvitanović), [Deligne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deligne), Cohen and de Man. E<sub>7½</sub> has dimension 190, and is not simple: as a representation of its subalgebra E<sub>7</sub>, it splits as E<sub>7</sub> ⊕ (56) ⊕ **R**, where (56) is the 56-dimensional [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of E<sub>7</sub>. This representation has an invariant [symplectic form](wikipedia://en/292852), and this symplectic form equips (56) ⊕ **R** with the structure of a [Heisenberg algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_algebra); this Heisenberg algebra is the [nilradical](wikipedia://en/22468313) in E<sub>7½</sub>.
