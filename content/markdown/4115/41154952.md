# Philip Wolfe (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Philip Wolfe"}

**Philip Starr "Phil" Wolfe** (August 11, 1927 – December 29, 2016) was an American mathematician and one of the founders of [convex optimization](wikipedia://en/1674411) theory and [mathematical programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_programming).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Wolfe received his bachelor's degree, masters, and Ph.D. degrees from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o4] He and his wife, Hallie, lived in [Ossining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossining_%28town%29,_New_York), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

In 1954, he was offered an instructorship at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he worked on generalizations of [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730), such as [quadratic programming](wikipedia://en/40324) and general [non-linear programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_programming), leading to the [Frank–Wolfe algorithm](wikipedia://en/3152055)[^o7] in joint work with [Marguerite Frank](wikipedia://en/42133630), then a visitor at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). When [Maurice Sion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Sion) was on sabbatical at the Institute for Advanced Study, Sion and Wolfe published in 1957 an example of [a zero-sum game without a minimax value](wikipedia://en/19073258).[^o8] Wolfe joined [RAND corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_corporation) in 1957, where he worked with [George Dantzig](wikipedia://en/13111), resulting in the now well known [Dantzig–Wolfe decomposition method](wikipedia://en/17885310).[^o9] In 1965, he moved to [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)'s Thomas J. Watson Research Center in Yorktown Heights, New York.

## Honors and awards {#Honors_and_awards}

He received the [John von Neumann Theory Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_von_Neumann_Theory_Prize) in 1992, jointly with [Alan Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hoffman_%28mathematician%29).
