# Order-4 apeirogonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order-4 apeirogonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **order-4 apeirogonal tiling** is a [regular](wikipedia://en/505253#Hyperbolic_tilings) [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of the [hyperbolic plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_plane). It covers the hyperbolic plane, which is a [non-Euclidean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean) surface with constant negative [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770), with a repeating pattern of [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) shapes that fill the plane completely without gaps or overlaps.

:::

This tiling is made from [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261), which are [polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygons) with infinitely many sides. In this pattern, four [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261) meet at each vertex. It can be understood as the hyperbolic analogue of the [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809) of the Euclidean plane, where four squares meet at each vertex. Its [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) is {∞,4}, meaning that each face has infinitely many sides and four faces meet at every vertex.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

This tiling shows the mirror lines of the symmetry group written in [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/293504) as \*2∞. Its dual tiling corresponds to the fundamental domains of the symmetry group written as \*∞∞∞∞. In that case, the fundamental domain is a square with four ideal vertices.

:::

## Uniform colorings {#Uniform_colorings}

As in the Euclidean square tiling, this tiling has nine uniform colorings. Three of these arise from [triangular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular) reflective symmetry domains. A fourth comes from square symmetry written as \*∞∞∞∞, with four different colors meeting at each vertex. The [checkerboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkerboard) coloring, denoted r{∞,∞}, defines the fundamental domains of the symmetry group written as \[(∞,4,4)\] or \*∞44, and is usually shown as alternating black and white regions corresponding to reflections.

## Related polyhedra and tiling {#Related_polyhedra_and_tiling}

This tiling is also related to a sequence of regular [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra) and tilings where four faces meet at each vertex. The sequence starts with the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), which has the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {n,4}, and a corresponding [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , and continues with larger tilings as *n* increases toward infinity.
