# Georgia Benkart

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Georgia Benkart"}

**Georgia McClure Benkart** (December 30, 1947 – April 29, 2022)[^o3][^o4][^o5] was an American mathematician who was known for her work in the structure and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) and related [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364). She published over 130 journal articles[^o10][^o13] and co-authored three [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) memoirs[^o6][^o7][^o8] in four broad categories: [modular Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/10198685); [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) of [Lie algebra representations](wikipedia://en/318712); [graded algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) and [superalgebras](wikipedia://en/469950); and [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) and related structures.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Benkart received her BS degree in mathematics summa cum laude from the [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University)[^o10] in 1970 and an MPhil in mathematics from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1973. She completed her doctoral work at Yale under [Nathan Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Jacobson) and wrote a dissertation entitled *Inner Ideals and the Structure of Lie Algebras.* She was awarded a PhD in mathematics from the Yale University in 1974.[^o11]

Upon completing her doctoral degree, Benkart began her long career at the [University of Wisconsin&ndash;Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin&ndash;Madison), first as a MacDuffee Instructor and eventually as a E. B. Van Vleck Professor of Mathematics until she retired from teaching in 2006.[^o12]

She held visiting positions at the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), California, the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey), New Jersey, the [Aspen Center for Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspen_Center_for_Physics), and the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia).[^o13] During her career, Benkart delivered over 350 invited talks including 3 plenary lectures at the Joint Mathematics Meetings and the [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896) Lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Seoul, South Korea in 2014.[^o10]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Benkart was born on December 30, 1947, in Youngstown, Ohio,[^o5] to George Benkart II and Florence K. Benkart.[^o4] Her father served in the [Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) and her mother was a teacher in Youngstown's "ethnically rich south side."[^o15]

Benkart died on April 29, 2022, aged 74, from undisclosed causes in [Madison, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin). Her survivors included her sister, Paula Kaye Benkart.[^o3][^o15]

## Research {#Research}

Benkart made a contribution to the classification of [simple](wikipedia://en/59500649) modular Lie algebras. Her work with J. Marshall Osborn on toroidal rank-one Lie algebras[^o17] became one of the building blocks of the classification.[^o40] The complete description of Hamiltonian Lie Algebras (with Gregory, Osborn, Strade, Wilson) can stand alone, and also has applications in the theory of [pro-*p* groups](wikipedia://en/1842075).[^o40]

In 2009, she published, jointly with Thomas Gregory and Alexander Premet,[^o8] the first complete [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the recognition theorem for graded Lie algebras in [characteristics](wikipedia://en/1066621) at least 5.[^o18]

In the early 1990s, Benkart and [Efim Zelmanov](wikipedia://en/1104849) started to work on classification of root-graded Lie algebras and intersection matrix algebras. The latter were introduced by [Peter Slodowy](wikipedia://en/35104673) in his work on singularities. Berman and Moody recognized that these algebras (generalizations of affine [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292)) are universal root graded Lie algebras and classified them for simply laced [root systems](wikipedia://en/277087). Benkart and Zelmanov tackled the remaining cases involving the [Freudenthal magic square](wikipedia://en/13796065) and extended this square to exceptional [Lie superalgebras](wikipedia://en/297501).[^o40]

Later Benkart extended these results in two directions. In a series of papers with Alberto Elduque she developed the theory of root graded Lie superalgebras.[^o19] In a second series of works with Bruce Allison, Arturo Pianzola, Erhard Neher, et al. she determined the universal central covers of these algebras.[^o20]

One of the pillars of the representation theory of [quantum groups](wikipedia://en/340678) (and applications to combinatorics) is [Masaki Kashiwara](wikipedia://en/4376209)'s theory of [crystal bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_basis). These are highly invariant bases which are well suited for decompositions of tensor products. In a paper with Seok-Jin Kang and Kashiwara, Benkart extended the theory of crystal bases to quantum superalgebras.[^o40][^o21]

Benkart's work on [noncommutative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncommutative_algebra) related to [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) became a basic tool in the construction of [tensor categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoidal_categories).[^o40]

## Service to the profession {#Service_to_the_profession}

Benkart served on several editorial boards including the boards of the American Mathematical Society for Surveys and Monographs and Abstracts,[^o23] *Communications in Algebra*,[^o24] and the *Journal of Algebra*.[^o25] She served as the associate secretary of the American Mathematical Society for the Central Section from 2010 to 2020,[^o26] and was a member of the governing council in 1995 and from 2010 to 2021.[^o27] Benkart was active in the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) (AWM) over many years, served as chair of the membership portfolio and the nominating committee, and helped plan the first AWM Research Symposium, which was also a celebration of the 40th anniversary of AWM.[^o28]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Benkart received a Woodrow Wilson Fellowship from the [Woodrow Wilson National Fellowship Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson_National_Fellowship_Foundation). Her work at Wisconsin was recognized by a Romnes Fellowship in 1985, a Distinguished Teaching Award in 1987, and a WARF Mid-Career Faculty Research Award in 1996.[^o30] In 2008 the University of California Lie Groups and Lie Algebras meeting was held in Benkart's honor. Her talks and lectures include two invited lectures at the Joint Mathematics Meetings[^o31] and a plenary lecture at a meeting of the Canadian Mathematical Society.[^o32]

In 2000–2002 Benkart was named a Polya Lecturer[^o33] by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772). She was elected a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS)[^o34] in the inaugural class of 2013.

She was elected and served as president of the Association for Women in Mathematics from 2009 to 2011.[^o35] In 2014 she was selected to deliver the AWM-AMS [Noether Lecture](wikipedia://en/11566873).[^o36] The title of her talk was Walking on Graphs the Representation Theory Way.[^o37]

In 2014 at the International Congress of Mathematicians held in Seoul, she delivered the ICM Emmy Noether Lecture.[^o38]

She was selected as a fellow of the Association for Women in Mathematics in the inaugural 2018 class.[^o39]

In 2022, a tribute to her contributions to her field, *"Gems from the Work of Georgia Benkart"*, appeared in the *Notices of the American Mathematical Society*.[^o40]

The [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o41] and the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison) mathematics department[^o42] posted remembrances of Benkart.
