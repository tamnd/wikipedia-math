# Wait/walk dilemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wait/walk dilemma"}

*Image: Waiting for the bus 2765.jpg.* Waiting girl sculpture at a bus stop in [Aachen, Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachen,_Germany)

The **wait/walk dilemma** occurs when waiting for a [bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus) at a [bus stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_stop), when the duration of the wait may exceed the time needed to arrive at a destination by another means, especially [walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking). Some work on this problem was featured in the 2008 "Year in Ideas" issue of *The New York Times Magazine*.[^o2]

:::

## Research {#Research}

The dilemma has been studied in an unpublished report entitled "Walk Versus Wait: The Lazy Mathematician Wins."[^o4][^o5] Anthony B. Morton's paper "A Note on Walking Versus Waiting" supports and extends Chen et al.'s results.[^o6] Ramnik Arora's "A Note on Walk versus Wait: Lazy Mathematician Wins" discusses what he believes to be some of the errors in Chen et al.'s argument; the result of Chen et al.'s paper still holds following Arora's alleged corrections.[^o7] As early as 1990, writer Tom Parker had observed that "walking is faster than waiting for a bus if you're going less than a mile".[^o8]

As an undergraduate mathematics major at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard), Scott D. Kominers first began fixating on the problem while walking from [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT) to [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard),[^o4] which are more than a mile apart in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts) along [MBTA bus route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_1_%28MBTA%29). He enlisted the help of [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltech) physics major Justin G. Chen and [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard) statistics major Robert W. Sinnott to perform the analysis.[^o4]

Their paper concludes that it is usually mathematically quicker to wait for the bus, at least for a little while. But once made, the decision to walk should be final instead of waiting again at subsequent stops.[^o4][^o2] The paper also showed potential applications to the field of cryptography.

## Interstellar travel {#Interstellar_travel}

The corresponding problem in interstellar travel is called the [wait calculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wait_calculation), which tries to determine the optimal time to wait for technological progress to improve spaceship speeds before committing to the journey.
