# Bergman kernel

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bergman kernel"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) study of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), the **Bergman kernel**, named after [Stefan Bergman](wikipedia://en/9395687), is the [reproducing kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproducing_kernel) for the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) ([RKHS](wikipedia://en/651196)) of all [square integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_integrable) [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) on a domain *D* in **C**<sup>*n*</sup>.

:::

In detail, let [L<sup>2</sup>(*D*)](wikipedia://en/45194) be the Hilbert space of square integrable functions on *D*, and let *L*<sup>2,*h*</sup>(*D*) denote the subspace consisting of holomorphic functions in L<sup>2</sup>(*D*): that is,

$$L^{2,h}(D) = L^2(D)\cap H(D)$$

where *H*(*D*) is the space of holomorphic functions in *D*. Then *L*<sup>2,*h*</sup>(*D*) is a Hilbert space: it is a [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) linear subspace of *L*<sup>2</sup>(*D*), and therefore [complete](wikipedia://en/7832) in its own right. This follows from the fundamental estimate, that for a holomorphic square-integrable function *ƒ* in *D*

::: {#o2 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\sup_{z\in K} |f(z)| \le C_K\|f\|_{L^2(D)} \tag{1}$$

:::

for every [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) subset *K* of *D*. Thus convergence of a sequence of holomorphic functions in *L*<sup>2</sup>(*D*) implies also [compact convergence](wikipedia://en/7442564), and so the limit function is also holomorphic.

Another consequence of ((1)) is that, for each *z* ∈ *D*, the evaluation

$$\operatorname{ev}_z : f\mapsto f(z)$$

is a [continuous linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) on *L*<sup>2,*h*</sup>(*D*). By the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452), this functional can be represented as the inner product with an element of *L*<sup>2,*h*</sup>(*D*), which is to say that

$$\operatorname{ev}_z f = \int_D f(\zeta)\overline{\eta_z(\zeta)}\,d\mu(\zeta).$$

The Bergman kernel *K* is defined by

$$K(z,\zeta) = \overline{\eta_z(\zeta)}.$$

The kernel *K*(*z*,ζ) is holomorphic in *z* and antiholomorphic in ζ, and satisfies

$$f(z) = \int_D K(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta).$$

One key observation about this picture is that *L*<sup>2,*h*</sup>(*D*) may be identified with the space of $L^2$ holomorphic (n,0)-forms on D, via multiplication by $dz^1\wedge \cdots \wedge dz^n$. Since the $L^2$ inner product on this space is manifestly invariant under [biholomorphisms](wikipedia://en/6077963) of D, the Bergman kernel and the associated [Bergman metric](wikipedia://en/12457310) are therefore automatically invariant under the automorphism group of the domain.

The Bergman kernel for the [unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc) *D* is the function

$$K(z,\zeta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(1-z\bar\zeta)^2}.$$
