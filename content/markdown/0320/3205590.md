# Gustav Bergmann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gustav Bergmann"}

**Gustav Bergmann** (; May 4, 1906 – April 21, 1987) was an Austrian-American philosopher. He studied at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) and was a member of the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle). Bergmann was influenced by the philosophers [Moritz Schlick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_Schlick), [Friedrich Waismann](wikipedia://en/1724365), and [Rudolf Carnap](wikipedia://en/57987), who were members of the Circle.[^o5] In the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), he was a professor of [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bergmann was born in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). He earned his Ph.D. in mathematics at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) in 1928. His dissertation, directed by [Walther Mayer](wikipedia://en/6004027), was titled *Zwei Beiträge zur mehrdimensionalen Differentialgeometrie*. While studying for his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate), he was invited to join the [Vienna Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Circle), a group of philosophers, mathematicians, scientists, and others committed to a scientific worldview under the name of [logical positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_positivism). In 1930–31, he worked with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) in Berlin. Unable as a [Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) to find academic employment, Bergmann obtained a [J.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) degree from the University of Vienna in 1935, and practiced [corporate law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_law) until he and his family fled to the United States in 1938. Settling at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa) in [Iowa City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_City) in 1939, Bergmann eventually became professor of both philosophy and psychology.

He died in Iowa City.
