# Predicate abstraction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Predicate abstraction"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), **predicate abstraction** is the result of creating a [predicate](wikipedia://en/285109) from a [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29). If Q is any formula then the predicate abstract formed from that sentence is (λx.Q), where λ is an [abstraction operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstraction_operator) and in which every occurrence of x that is free in Q is bound by λ in (λx.Q). The resultant predicate (λx.Q(x)) is a monadic predicate capable of taking a [term](wikipedia://en/24885593) t as argument as in (λx.Q(x))(t), which says that the object denoted by 't' has the property of being such that Q.

:::

The **law of abstraction** states ( λx.Q(x) )(t) ≡ Q(t/x) where Q(t/x) is the result of replacing all free occurrences of x in Q by t. This law is shown to fail in general in at least two cases: (i) when t is irreferential and (ii) when Q contains [modal operators](wikipedia://en/3832714).

In [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) the "*[de re*/*de dicto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_re_and_de_dicto)* distinction" is stated as

\1. (DE DICTO): $\Box A(t)$

\2. (DE RE): $(\lambda x.\Box A(x))(t)$.

In (1) the modal operator applies to the formula A(t) and the term t is within the scope of the modal operator. In (2) t is *not* within the scope of the modal operator.
