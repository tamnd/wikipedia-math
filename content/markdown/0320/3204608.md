# Müntz–Szász theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Müntz–Szász theorem"}

The **Müntz–Szász theorem** is a basic result of [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713), proved by [Herman Müntz](wikipedia://en/3204487) in 1914 and [Otto Szász](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Szász) in 1916. Roughly speaking, the theorem shows to what extent the [Weierstrass theorem on polynomial approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_theorem_on_polynomial_approximation) can be extended by restricting certain coefficients in the polynomials to be zero. The form of the result had been conjectured by [Sergei Bernstein](wikipedia://en/976516) before it was proved.

:::

The theorem, in a special case, states that a necessary and sufficient condition for the [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416)

$$x^n,\quad n\in S\subset\mathbb N$$

to span a [dense subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_subset) of the [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *C*\[*a*,*b*\] of all [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) with [complex number](wikipedia://en/5826) values on the [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) \[*a*,*b*\] with *a* > 0, with the [uniform norm](wikipedia://en/349755), is that the sum

$$\sum_{n\in S}\frac{1}{n}\$$

of the reciprocals, taken over *S*, should [diverge](wikipedia://en/876428), i.e. *S* is a [large set](wikipedia://en/3152856). For an interval \[0, *b*\], the [constant functions](wikipedia://en/320861) are necessary: assuming therefore that 0 is in *S*, the condition on the other exponents is as before.

More generally, one can take exponents from any [strictly increasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_increasing) sequence of positive real numbers, and the same result holds. Szász showed that for complex number exponents, the same condition applied to the sequence of [real parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part).

There are also versions for the [*L*<sub>*p*</sub> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194).
