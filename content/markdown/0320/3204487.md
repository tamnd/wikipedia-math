# Herman Müntz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Herman Müntz"}

*Image: Chaim Muntz.jpg.*

**(Chaim) Herman Müntz** (28 August 1884, in [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź) – 17 April 1956, in [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden)) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), now remembered for the [Müntz approximation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Müntz_approximation_theorem).[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łódź) (then in the [Piotrków Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piotrków_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), now in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)) in a secular [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) family, who had adopted a German spelling of the surname *Minc*. He was educated there and at the [Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) in Berlin, graduating in 1906. He wrote a doctoral dissertation there on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and the [Plateau problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateau_problem), in 1910, supervised by [H. A. Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._A._Schwarz).

In 1911 he moved to [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich). In the following years he published on [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849), [iterative methods](wikipedia://en/15237), and [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713).

In 1914 he took a teaching position in a school near [Heppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heppenheim), and a year later another in [Hochwaldhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochwaldhausen). He became a German citizen in 1919. At around that time he suffered a breakdown, and moved back to Poland with his wife. He shortly began publishing mathematics again. The couple moved to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen) in 1921, and Müntz became involved in editorial, reviewing and translation work, as well as research. At this period, and from 1924 in Berlin, he was unsuccessfully trying to get an academic position, hampered because he had not [habilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitated). In 1927 he worked closely with [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736).

In 1929 he took a professorial position at the [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University), where he was active in teaching, research, administration and as an editor of [Lyapunov](wikipedia://en/65425). Though he left Germany before the [Nazis seized power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Enabling_Act_of_1933) in 1933, some scholars consider him an early Jewish emigrant from Nazi Germany; his career was negatively impacted by anti-semitism and he was not able to return to Germany, unlike early non-Jewish emigrants such as [Eberhard Hopf](wikipedia://en/6021730) and Wilhelm Maier.[^o5][^o6]

In 1932 he was an official Soviet delegate to the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842), with [N. G. Chebotarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._G._Chebotarev), [P. S. Alexandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._S._Alexandrov), and [E. Y. Kolman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kolman), an ideological Marxist. While in Russia, Müntz was influential in helping several other mathematicians escape from the Nazis.[^o7] In 1937 Müntz, who remained a German citizen, was expelled from the USSR. He went to Sweden, where he supported himself by teaching. He obtained Swedish citizenship in 1953.

He wrote extensively on [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism) and related topics. In 1907 his book *Wir Juden* was published in Berlin. He was a correspondent of [Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber), and wrote much for Buber's journal *Der Jude*.
