# Methoden der mathematischen Physik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Methoden der mathematischen Physik"}

***Methoden der mathematischen Physik*** (translated into English with the title **Methods of Mathematical Physics**) is a 1924 book, in two volumes totalling around 1000 pages, published under the names of [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238) and [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302). It was a comprehensive treatment of the "methods of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416)" of the time. The second volume is devoted to the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). It contains presages of the [finite element method](wikipedia://en/18233581), on which Courant would work subsequently, and which would eventually become basic to [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506).

:::

The material of the book was worked up from the content of Hilbert's lectures. While Courant played the major editorial role, many at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) were involved in the writing-up, and in that sense it was a collective production.

On its appearance in 1924 it apparently had little direct connection to the [quantum theory](wikipedia://en/25202) questions at the centre of the [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) of the time. That changed within two years, since the formulation of the [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874) made the Hilbert–Courant techniques of immediate relevance to the new [wave mechanics](wikipedia://en/59874#Particles_as_waves).

There was a second edition (1931/7), wartime edition in the USA (1943), and a third German edition (1968). The English version *Methods of Mathematical Physics* (1953) was revised by Courant, and the second volume had extensive work done on it by the faculty of the [Courant Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute). The books quickly gained the reputation as classics, and are among most highly referenced books in advanced mathematical physics courses.
