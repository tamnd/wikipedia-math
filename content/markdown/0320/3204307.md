# Stefan Cohn-Vossen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Stefan Cohn-Vossen"}

**Stefan Cohn-Vossen** (28 May 1902 – 25 June 1936) was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He is best known for his collaboration with [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) on the 1932 book *Anschauliche Geometrie*, translated into [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as *[Geometry and the Imagination](wikipedia://en/55419796)*.[^o3] Both [Cohn-Vossen's inequality](wikipedia://en/40410426) and the [Cohn-Vossen transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cohn-Vossen_transformation) are named after him. He also proved the first version of the [splitting theorem](wikipedia://en/931034).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Stefan Cohn-Vossen was born 28 May, 1902 to Emanuel Cohn, a lawyer, and Hedwig (née Vossen) in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau) (then a city in the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia); now [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław) in [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland)). He attended [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University) in 1920; his notes from Hilbert's lectures on geometry at that time would form the basis for *Anschauliche Geometrie*. He wrote a 1924 doctoral dissertation at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau) (now the [University of Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wrocław)) under the supervision of [Adolf Kneser](wikipedia://en/1368530). In 1929 he completed his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) at Göttingen with his thesis *Non-rigid closed surfaces*[^n1] under [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238).[^o8]

Cohn-Vossen became a professor at the [University of Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cologne) in 1930.[^o9] In 1931, Cohn-Vossen married Dr. Margot Maria Elfriede Ranft. *Anschauliche Geometrie* was published in 1932; the book was well reviewed[^o10][^o11] and by association with Hilbert, Cohn-Vossen became well known.[^o12] He was barred from lecturing in 1933 under [Nazi racial legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service), because he was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew).[^o13]

Unable to work in Germany, Cohn-Vossen moved to Switzerland in 1934, first to [Locarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locarno) and then to [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich), where he taught [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29). His son, Richard Cohn-Vossen, was born in Zurich in September 1934. Both Courant and [Karl Löwner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Löwner) recommended Cohn-Vossen for positions abroad, at [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_University) and [Dartmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College) respectively; ultimately he emigrated to the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR), with support from [Herman Müntz](wikipedia://en/3204487), [Fritz Houtermans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Houtermans), [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245), and [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082),[^o14] where he was appointed to the [Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_the_Soviet_Union) and worked at [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_State_University) and the [Steklov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steklov_Institute).[^o15][^o16]

Cohn-Vossen died in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow) from [pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonia) in 1936.[^o17] Despite his short time in the USSR, Cohn-Vossen had a significant impact on the development of differential geometry "in the large" in Soviet mathematics.[^n2][^o20][^n3]

Following Cohn-Vossen's death, his widow, Dr. Elfriede Cohn-Vossen, remarried [Alfred Kurella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kurella), returned to Germany in 1954, and died in 1957. His son, Richard, became a filmmaker. In 1946, unaware of his death, the University of Cologne offered Cohn-Vossen his professorship back.[^n4]
