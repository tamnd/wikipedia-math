# F. and M. Riesz theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="F. and M. Riesz theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **F. and M. Riesz theorem** is a result of the brothers [Frigyes Riesz](wikipedia://en/1073126) and [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz), on **analytic measures**. It states that for a [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) μ on the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220), any part of μ that is not [absolutely continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolutely_continuous) with respect to the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) *d*θ can be detected by means of [Fourier coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_coefficient). More precisely, it states that if the Fourier–Stieltjes coefficients of $\mu$ satisfy

:::

$$\hat\mu_n=\int_0^{2\pi}{\rm e}^{-in\theta}\frac{ \mu(d\theta)}{2\pi}=0,\$$

for all $n<0$, then μ is absolutely continuous with respect to *d*θ.

The original statements are rather different (see Zygmund, *[Trigonometric Series](wikipedia://en/35279846)*, VII.8). The formulation here is as in [Walter Rudin](wikipedia://en/5491766), *Real and Complex Analysis*, p. 335. The proof given uses the [Poisson kernel](wikipedia://en/2214583) and the existence of boundary values for the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) *H*<sup>1</sup>.

Expansions to this theorem were made by James E. Weatherbee in his 1968 dissertation: *Some Extensions Of The F. And M. Riesz Theorem On Absolutely Continuous Measures.*
