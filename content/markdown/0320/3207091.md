# Fekete polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fekete polynomial"}

*Image: Polynome de fekete 43.svg.* Roots of the Fekete polynomial for p = 43

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Fekete polynomial** is a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000)

:::

$$f_p(t):=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1} \left (\frac{a}{p}\right )t^a\,$$

where $\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)\,$ is the [Legendre symbol](wikipedia://en/18567) modulo some integer *p* > 1.

These polynomials were known in nineteenth-century studies of [Dirichlet L-functions](wikipedia://en/400092), and indeed to [Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) himself. They have acquired the name of [Michael Fekete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fekete), who observed that the absence of real zeroes *t* of the Fekete polynomial with 0 \< *t* \< 1 implies an absence of the same kind for the [L-function](wikipedia://en/245283)

$$L\left(s,\dfrac{x}{p}\right).\,$$

This is of considerable potential interest in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), in connection with the hypothetical [Siegel zero](wikipedia://en/1967197) near *s* = 1. While numerical results for small cases had indicated that there were few such real zeroes, further analysis reveals that this may indeed be a 'small number' effect.
