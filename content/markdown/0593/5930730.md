# Category of relations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of relations"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) **Rel** has the class of [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and [binary relations](wikipedia://en/3931) as [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphism_%28category_theory%29).

:::

A morphism (or arrow) *R* : *A* → *B* in this category is a relation between the sets *A* and *B*, so *R* ⊆ *A* × *B*.

The [composition of two relations](wikipedia://en/7472170) *R*: *A* → *B* and *S*: *B* → *C* is given by[^o2]

$$(a, c) \in S \circ R \iff \text{for some } b \in B, (a, b) \in R \text{ and } (b, c) \in S.$$

**Rel** has also been called the "category of correspondences of sets".[^o3]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The category **Rel** has the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) **Set** as a (wide) [subcategory](wikipedia://en/310959), where the arrow *f* : *X* → *Y* in **Set** corresponds to the relation *F* ⊆ *X* × *Y* defined by (*x*, *y*) ∈ *F* ⇔ *f*(*x*) = *y*.[^o6][^o7]

A morphism in **Rel** is a relation, and the corresponding morphism in the [opposite category](wikipedia://en/372399) to **Rel** has arrows reversed, so it is the [converse relation](wikipedia://en/3941020). Thus **Rel** contains its opposite and is [self-dual](wikipedia://en/310885).[^o8]

The [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) represented by taking the converse relation provides the **dagger** to make **Rel** a [dagger category](wikipedia://en/7152070).

The category has two [functors](wikipedia://en/10987) into itself given by the [hom functor](wikipedia://en/2741037): A [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) *R* ⊆ *A* × *B* and its transpose *R*<sup>T</sup> ⊆ *B* × *A* may be composed either as *R R*<sup>T</sup> or as *R*<sup>T</sup> *R*. The first composition results in a [homogeneous relation](wikipedia://en/3931#Definition) on *A* and the second is on *B*. Since the images of these hom functors are in **Rel** itself, in this case hom is an [internal hom functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_hom_functor). With its internal hom functor, **Rel** is a [closed category](wikipedia://en/1397197), and furthermore a [dagger compact category](wikipedia://en/7155145).

The category **Rel** can be obtained from the category **Set** as the [Kleisli category](wikipedia://en/3957200) for the [monad](wikipedia://en/355140) whose functor corresponds to [power set](wikipedia://en/23799), interpreted as a covariant functor.

Perhaps a bit surprising at first sight is the fact that [product](wikipedia://en/237868) in **Rel** is given by the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946)[^o8] (rather than the [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) as it is in **Set**), and so is the [coproduct](wikipedia://en/310889).

**Rel** is [monoidal closed](wikipedia://en/1396924), if one defines both the monoidal product *A* ⊗ *B* and the [internal hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_hom) *A* ⇒ *B* by the [cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) of sets. It is also a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) if one defines the monoidal product by the disjoint union of sets.[^o9]

The category **Rel** was the prototype for the algebraic structure called an [allegory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory_%28category_theory%29) by [Peter J. Freyd](wikipedia://en/4593361) and Andre Scedrov in 1990.[^o10] Starting with a [regular category](wikipedia://en/9790950) and a functor *F*: *A* → *B*, they note properties of the induced functor Rel(*A,B*) → Rel(*FA, FB*). For instance, it preserves composition, conversion, and intersection. Such properties are then used to provide axioms for an allegory.

:::

## Relations as objects {#Relations_as_objects}

David Rydeheard and [Rod Burstall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Burstall) consider **Rel** to have objects that are homogeneous relations. For example, *A* is a set and *R* ⊆ *A* × *A* is a binary relation on *A*. The morphisms of this category are functions between sets that preserve a relation: Say *S* ⊆ *B* × *B* is a second relation and *f*: *A* → *B* is a function such that $xRy \implies f(x)Sf(y),$ then *f* is a morphism.[^o12]

The same idea is advanced by Adamek, Herrlich and Strecker, where they designate the objects (*A, R*) and (*B, S*), set and relation.[^o13]
