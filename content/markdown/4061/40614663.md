# Georg Feigl

*Image: Wechselburg-Barockschloss.jpg.* [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) castle, [Wechselburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wechselburg) (2007)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Georg Feigl"}

**Georg Feigl** (13 October 1890 – 20 April 1945) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Georg Feigl started studying [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) in 1909. In 1918, he obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) under [Paul Koebe](wikipedia://en/4313643). From 1928 he was editor of the *Jahrbuch über die Fortschritte der Mathematik* ("Yearbook on the progress of mathematics"). In 1935 he became a full professor at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau). In 1937—1941, he was an editor of the journal *[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)*.

Feigl's main areas of work were the [foundations of geometry](wikipedia://en/21317755) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), where he studied [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738) theorems for *n*-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).

Feigl was one of the initial authors of the *Mathematisches Wörterbuch* ("Mathematical dictionary"). Because of the impending [siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Breslau) by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) he was forced to leave [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau) in January 1945 with his family and other members of the Mathematical Institute. His wife Maria was distantly related to the lord of the manor of [Wechselburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wechselburg) castle and prepared the castle to receive the mathematicians. Feigl brought his previously developed materials for the *Mathematisches Wörterbuch* and asked his students to further refine it in the castle. They did not have access to books, lecture notes, calculators, or typewriters in the castle. [Johann Radon](wikipedia://en/339331) (1887–1956) and Feigl were willing and able to continue lectures started in Breslau for one hour a day at Wechselburg castle, without any documents. Feigl had a severe stomach ailment and died after a few months without medication in Wechselburg. The *Mathematisches Wörterbuch* did not appear until 1961, when [Hermann Ludwig Schmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Ludwig_Schmid) (1908–1956) and [Joseph Naas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Naas) (1906–1993) published it.
