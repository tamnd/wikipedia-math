# CWC mode

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CWC mode"}

In [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), **CWC Mode** (Carter–Wegman + [CTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_cipher_modes_of_operation) mode) is an [AEAD block cipher mode of operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AEAD_block_cipher_modes_of_operation) that provides both encryption and built-in message integrity, similar to CCM and OCB modes. It combines the use of CTR mode with a 128-bit block cipher for encryption with an efficient polynomial [Carter–Wegman MAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter–Wegman_MAC) with a tag length of at most 128 bits and is designed by [Tadayoshi Kohno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadayoshi_Kohno), [John Viega](wikipedia://en/25786581) and [Doug Whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Whiting).[^o2]

:::

CWC mode was submitted to [NIST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology)[^o3] for standardization, but NIST opted for the similar [GCM mode](wikipedia://en/5897139) instead.[^o4]

Although GCM has weaknesses compared to CWC,[^o5] the GCM authors successfully argued for GCM.[^o6]

CWC allows the payload and associated data to be at most 2<sup>32</sup> - 1 blocks or nearly 550 GB.[^o2]
