# Donald G. Higman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald G. Higman"}

**Donald G. Higman** (September 20, 1928 in [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver) – February 13, 2006) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his discovery, in collaboration with [Charles C. Sims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_C._Sims), of the [Higman–Sims group](wikipedia://en/403178).[^o2]

:::

Higman did his undergraduate studies at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia),[^o2] and received his Ph.D. in 1952 from the [University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) under [Reinhold Baer](wikipedia://en/1612114). He served on the faculty of mathematics at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) from 1956 to 1998.[^o2]

His work on [homological](wikipedia://en/142432) aspects of [group representation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_representation_theory) established the concept of a relatively-[projective](wikipedia://en/364488) [module](wikipedia://en/276410) and explained its role in the theory of module decompositions. He developed a characterization of rank-2 [permutation groups](wikipedia://en/24634), and a theory of rank-3 permutation groups; several of the later-discovered [sporadic simple groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporadic_simple_group) were of this type, including the Higman–Sims group which he and Sims constructed in 1967.[^o2]
