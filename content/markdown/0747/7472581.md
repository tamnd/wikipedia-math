# A History of Pi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="A History of Pi"}

***A History of Pi*** (also titled ***A History of $\pi$***) is a 1970 non-fiction book by [Petr Beckmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Beckmann) that presents a layman's introduction to the concept of the [mathematical constant](wikipedia://en/48779551) [pi](wikipedia://en/23601) ($\pi$).[^o3]

:::

## Author {#Author}

Beckmann was a [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) who fled the [Communist government of Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Czechoslovakia_%281948–1989%29) to go to the United States. His dislike of authority gives *A History of Pi* a style that belies its dry title. For example, his chapter on the era following the classical age of [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) is titled "The Roman Pest";[^o5] he calls the Catholic [Inquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisition) the act of "insane religious fanatic"; and he says that people who question public spending on scientific research are "intellectual cripples who drivel about 'too much technology' because technology has wounded them with the ultimate insult: 'They can't understand it any more.'"

Beckmann was a prolific scientific author who wrote several electrical engineering textbooks and non-technical works, founded Golem Press, which published most of his books, and published his own monthly newsletter, *Access to Energy*. In his self-published book *Einstein Plus Two* and in Internet [flame wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaming_%28Internet%29), he claimed that the theory of relativity is incorrect.[^o6]

## Bibliography {#Bibliography}

*A History of Pi* was originally published as *A History of $\pi$* in 1970 by Golem Press. This edition did not cover any [approximations of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximations_of_π) $\pi$ calculated after 1946. A second edition, printed in 1971, added material on the calculation of $\pi$ by electronic computers, but still contained historical and mathematical errors, such as an incorrect proof that there exist infinitely many [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666).[^o8] A third edition was published as *A History of Pi* in 1976 by [St. Martin's Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin's_Press). It was published as *A History of Pi* by Hippocrene Books in 1990.[^o9] The title is given as *A History of Pi* by both Amazon[^o10] and by WorldCat.[^o11]
