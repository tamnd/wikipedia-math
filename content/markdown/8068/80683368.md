# Harish-Chandra integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harish-Chandra integral"}

The **Harish-Chandra integral** is a concept from [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus) that originated in the study of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) on [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). Closely related is the **Harish-Chandra formula** which is used to evaluate the integral.

:::

The integrals are named after the Indian mathematician [Harish-Chandra](wikipedia://en/373190), who proved in 1957 the so-called Harish-Chandra formula.[^o2] Today, the integral and its associated formula find applications in many fields, such as [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory) and [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).

A special case is the formula for integrals over the [unitary group](wikipedia://en/173993) which was independently discovered in 1980 by [Claude Itzykson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Itzykson) and [Jean-Bernard Zuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Bernard_Zuber) and applied to quantum field theory. The integral over the unitary group is also referred to as the **Harish-Chandra–Itzykson–Zuber integral**.[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a connected, semisimple [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group) and let $\mathrm{d}g$ be the [Haar probability measure](wikipedia://en/40645). Let $\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Lie}(G)$ be its [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) and $\mathfrak{t} \subset \mathfrak{g}$ be the [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) (or its complexification $\mathfrak{t} \subset \mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}$).

The **Harish-Chandra integral** is the function[^o6][^o7]

$$H(x,y):=\int_G e^{\langle \operatorname{Ad}_g x, y \rangle} \mathrm{d}g$$

for $x, y \in \mathfrak{t}$, where $\operatorname{Ad}$ denotes the adjoint representation, and $\langle \cdot {,} \cdot \rangle$ is the [Killing form](wikipedia://en/982386).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement cue=named-heading name="Harish-Chandra formula"}

## Harish-Chandra formula {#Harish-Chandra_formula}

Let $G$ be connected and semisimple, and let $R^+$ denote the positive root system of $\mathfrak{t}$. Then[^o6]

$$\Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}(x)\Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}(y)\int_G e^{\langle \operatorname{Ad}_gx,y\rangle}\mathrm{d}g=\frac{[\![\Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}, \Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}]\!]}{|W|}\sum\limits_{w\in W}\varepsilon(w)e^{\langle w(x),y \rangle},$$

where

- $W$ is the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) acting on $\mathfrak{t}$,
- $\Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}(x) := \prod_{\alpha \in R^+} \langle \alpha, x \rangle$ is a polynomial function called the discriminant,
- $\varepsilon(w) := (-1)^{|w|}$ is the signature,
- $[\![\Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}, \Delta_{\mathfrak{t}}]\!]$ is an inner product that extends the killing form to polynomial functions defined in the following way: If $p$ and $q$ are polynomial functions on the real Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, write $p(x) = \sum{\beta} c_{\beta} x^{\beta}$ in real coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$, where $n = \dim \mathfrak{g}$. The associated differential operator is

$$p(\partial) = \sum_{\beta} c_{\beta} \frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial x^{\beta}}.$$

The bilinear form $[[p, q]]$ is defined by applying the differential operator $p(\partial)$ to $q(x)$ then evaluating the result at the zero, i.e.

$$[[p, q]] := p(\partial)q(x) \bigg|_{x = 0}.$$

:::
