# Brute Force: Cracking the Data Encryption Standard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brute Force: Cracking the Data Encryption Standard"}

***Brute Force: Cracking the Data Encryption Standard*** (2005, Copernicus Books ) is a book by [Matt Curtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Curtin) about [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

In this book, Curtin recounts his involvement in the [DESCHALL Project](wikipedia://en/18473453), mobilizing thousands of personal computers in 1997 in order to meet the challenge to crack a single message [encrypted](wikipedia://en/10294) with [DES](wikipedia://en/7978).[^o3]

This was and remains one of the largest [collaborations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration) of any kind on a single project in history.

The message was unencrypted on June 18 and was found to be "Strong cryptography makes the world a safer place."

This is also the message of Curtin's book where he uses a personal account to reveal to the uninitiated reader some insight into a topic of growing importance which is both technically and politically complicated.
