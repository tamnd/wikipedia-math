# Cohen–Daubechies–Feauveau wavelet

*Image: Jpeg2000 2-level wavelet transform-lichtenstein.png.* An example of the 2D wavelet transform that is used in [JPEG 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG_2000)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cohen–Daubechies–Feauveau wavelet"}

**Cohen–Daubechies–Feauveau wavelets** are a family of [biorthogonal wavelets](wikipedia://en/4555949) that was made popular by [Ingrid Daubechies](wikipedia://en/1430989).[^o2][^o3] These are not the same as the orthogonal [Daubechies wavelets](wikipedia://en/554121), and also not very similar in shape and properties. However, their construction idea is the same.

:::

The [JPEG 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG_2000) [compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_compression) standard uses the biorthogonal Le Gall–Tabatabai (LGT) 5/3 wavelet (developed by D. Le Gall and Ali J. Tabatabai)[^o4][^o5][^o6] for [lossless compression](wikipedia://en/18209) and a CDF 9/7 wavelet for [lossy compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lossy_compression).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- The [primal generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primal_generator) is a [B-spline](wikipedia://en/21834) if the simple factorization $q_\text{prim}(X) = 1$ (see below) is chosen.
- The [dual generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_generator) has the highest possible number of smoothness factors for its length.
- All generators and wavelets in this family are symmetric.

:::

::: {#o10 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

For every positive integer *A* there exists a unique polynomial $Q_A(X)$ of degree *A* − 1 satisfying the identity

$$\left(1 - \frac{X}{2}\right)^A\,Q_A(X) + \left(\frac{X}{2}\right)^A\,Q_A(2 - X) = 1.$$

This is the same polynomial as used in the construction of the [Daubechies wavelets](wikipedia://en/554121). But, instead of a spectral factorization, here we try to factor

$$Q_A(X) = q_\text{prim}(X)\,q_\text{dual}(X),$$

where the factors are polynomials with real coefficients and constant coefficient 1. Then

$$a_\text{prim}(Z) = 2 Z^d\,\left(\frac{1 + Z}2\right)^A\,q_\text{prim}\left(1 - \frac{Z + Z^{-1}}{2}\right)$$

and

$$a_\text{dual}(Z) = 2 Z^d\,\left(\frac{1 + Z}2\right)^A\,q_\text{dual}\left(1 - \frac{Z + Z^{-1}}{2}\right)$$

form a biorthogonal pair of scaling sequences. *d* is some integer used to center the symmetric sequences at zero or to make the corresponding discrete filters causal.

Depending on the roots of $Q_A(X)$, there may be up to $2^{A-1}$ different factorizations. A simple factorization is $q_\text{prim}(X) = 1$ and $q_\text{dual}(X) = Q_A(X)$, then the primary scaling function is the [B-spline](wikipedia://en/21834) of order *A* − 1. For *A* = 1 one obtains the orthogonal **[Haar wavelet](wikipedia://en/50905)**.

:::

## Numbering {#Numbering}

There are two concurring numbering schemes for wavelets of the CDF family:

- the number of smoothness factors of the lowpass filters, or equivalently the number of [vanishing moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the highpass filters, e.g. "2, 2";
- the sizes of the lowpass filters, or equivalently the sizes of the highpass filters, e.g. "5, 3".

The first numbering was used in Daubechies' book *Ten lectures on wavelets*. Neither of this numbering is unique. The number of vanishing moments does not tell about the chosen factorization. A [filter bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_bank) with filter sizes 7 and 9 can have 6 and 2 vanishing moments when using the trivial factorization, or 4 and 4 vanishing moments as it is the case for the JPEG 2000 wavelet. The same wavelet may therefore be referred to as "CDF 9/7" (based on the filter sizes) or "biorthogonal 4, 4" (based on the vanishing moments). Similarly, the same wavelet may therefore be referred to as "CDF 5/3" (based on the filter sizes) or "biorthogonal 2, 2" (based on the vanishing moments).

## Lifting decomposition {#Lifting_decomposition}

For the trivially factorized filterbanks a [lifting decomposition](wikipedia://en/4555411) can be explicitly given.[^o17]

### Even number of smoothness factors {#Even_number_of_smoothness_factors}

Let $n$ be the number of smoothness factors in the B-spline lowpass filter, which shall be even.

Then define recursively

$$\begin{aligned} a_0 &= \frac{1}{n}, \\ a_m &= \frac{1}{(n^2 - 4\cdot m^2)\cdot a_{m - 1}}. \end{aligned}$$

The lifting filters are

$$s_{m}(z) = a_m\cdot(2\cdot m + 1)\cdot(1 + z^{(-1)^m}).$$

Conclusively, the interim results of the lifting are

$$\begin{aligned} x_{-1}(z) &= z, \\ x_{0}(z) &= 1, \\ x_{m + 1}(z) &= x_{m - 1}(z) + a_m\cdot(2\cdot m + 1)\cdot(z + z^{-1}) \cdot z^{(-1)^m} \cdot x_{m}(z), \end{aligned}$$

which leads to

$$x_{n/2}(z) = 2^{-n/2} \cdot (1 + z)^n \cdot z^{n/2 \bmod 2 - n/2}.$$

The filters $x_{n/2}$ and $x_{n/2-1}$ constitute the CDF-*n*,0 filterbank.

### Odd number of smoothness factors {#Odd_number_of_smoothness_factors}

Now, let $n$ be odd.

Then define recursively

$$\begin{aligned} a_0 &= \frac{1}{n}, \\ a_m &= \frac{1}{(n^2 - (2\cdot m - 1)^2)\cdot a_{m - 1}}. \end{aligned}$$

The lifting filters are

$$s_{m}(z) = a_m\cdot((2\cdot m + 1) + (2\cdot m - 1)\cdot z) / z^{m \bmod 2}.$$

Conclusively, the interim results of the lifting are

$$\begin{aligned} x_{-1}(z) &= z, \\ x_{0}(z) &= 1, \\ x_{1}(z) &= x_{-1}(z) + a_0\cdot x_0(z), \\ x_{m + 1}(z) &= x_{m - 1}(z) + a_m\cdot((2\cdot m + 1)\cdot z + (2\cdot m - 1)\cdot z^{-1}) \cdot z^{(-1)^m} \cdot x_{m}(z), \end{aligned}$$

which leads to

$$x_{(n + 1)/2}(z) \sim (1 + z)^n,$$

where we neglect the translation and the constant factor.

The filters $x_{(n + 1)/2}$ and $x_{(n - 1)/2}$ constitute the CDF-*n*,1 filterbank.

## Applications {#Applications}

The Cohen–Daubechies–Feauveau wavelet and other biorthogonal wavelets have been used to compress [fingerprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerprint) scans for the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI).[^o22] A standard for compressing fingerprints in this way was developed by Tom Hopper (FBI), Jonathan Bradley ([Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory)) and Chris Brislawn (Los Alamos National Laboratory).[^o22] By using wavelets, a compression ratio of around 20 to 1 can be achieved, meaning a 10 MB image could be reduced to as little as 500 kB while still passing recognition tests.[^o22]

:::
