# GEOS circle

*Image: GEOS circle overview.svg.*

*Image: GEOS circle.svg.*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="GEOS circle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **GEOS circle** is derived from the intersection of four lines that are associated with a generalized [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654): the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622), the [Soddy line](wikipedia://en/72046251), the [orthic axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_axis) and the [Gergonne line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_line). Note that the Euler line is [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) to the orthic axis and that the Soddy line is orthogonal to the Gergonne line.

:::

These four lines provide six points of intersection of which two points occur at line intersections that are orthogonal. Consequently, the other four points form an [orthocentric system](wikipedia://en/161263).

The GEOS circle is that circle centered at a point equidistant from *X*650 (the intersection of the orthic axis with the Gergonne line) and *X*20 (the intersection of the Euler line with the Soddy line and is known as the [de Longchamps point](wikipedia://en/14593776)) and passes through these points as well as the two points of orthogonal intersection.

The orthogonal intersection points are *X*468 (the intersection of the orthic axis with the Euler line) and *X*1323 (the [Fletcher point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletcher_point), the intersection of the Gergonne line with the Soddy line).

The orthocentric system comprises *X*650, *X*20, *X*1375 (the intersection of the Euler line with the Gergonne line and is known as the [Evans point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evans_point)) and *X*3012 (the intersection of the Soddy line and the orthic axis).

The *X*(*i*) point notation is the Clark Kimberling [ETC](wikipedia://en/15003593) classification of [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) centers.
