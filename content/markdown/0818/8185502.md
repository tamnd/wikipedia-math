# Tudor Ganea

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tudor Ganea"}

**Tudor Ganea** (October 17, 1922 –August 1971)[^o3] was a [Romanian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Americans) mathematician, known for his work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), especially [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) theory. Ganea left [Communist Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) to settle in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) in the early 1960s.[^o4] He taught at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

He studied mathematics at the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), and then started his research as a member of [Simion Stoilow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Stoilow)'s seminar on complex functions. His papers from 1949–1952 were on [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323), [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315), [symmetric products](wikipedia://en/31338286), and the [Lusternik–Schnirelmann category](wikipedia://en/764433). During this time, he earned his candidate thesis in topology under the direction of Stoilow.[^o6]

In 1957, Ganea published in the *[Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785)* a short, yet influential paper with [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501), in which the [Eilenberg–Ganea theorem](wikipedia://en/35679624) was proved and the celebrated [Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture](wikipedia://en/8211999) was formulated. The conjecture is still open.

By 1958, Ganea and his mentee, Israel Berstein, were the two leading algebraic topologists in Romania.[^o7] Later that year at an international conference on geometry and topology in [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași), the two met [Peter Hilton](wikipedia://en/8951286), starting long mathematical collaborations. Ganea left for France in 1961, where he obtained in 1962 his Ph.D. from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) under [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536),[^o6] with thesis *Sur quelques invariants numeriques du type d'homotopie*.[^o8] He then emigrated to the United States. After spending a year at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) in [West Lafayette, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lafayette,_Indiana), he joined the faculty at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle).[^o6]

During this time, he tried to get [Aurora Cornu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_Cornu) (his fiancée at the time) out of Romania, but did not succeed.[^o4] In 1962, he gave an invited talk at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), titled *On some numerical homotopy invariants.*

Just before he died, Ganea attended the Symposium on Algebraic Topology, held February 22–26, 1971 at the Battelle Seattle Research Center, in Seattle.[^o9] At the symposium, he was not able to give a talk, but he did distribute a preprint containing a list of unsolved problems. One of these problems, regarding the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category, came to be known as *[Ganea's conjecture](wikipedia://en/8186879)*. A version of this conjecture for rational spaces was proved by [Kathryn Hess](wikipedia://en/53728761) in her 1989 [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) Ph.D. thesis.[^o10] Many particular cases of Ganea's original conjecture were proved, until [Norio Iwase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norio_Iwase) provided a counterexample in 1998.[^o11] A minimum dimensional counterexample to Ganea’s conjecture was constructed by Don Stanley and Hugo Rodríguez Ordóñez in 2010.[^o12]

Ganea is buried at [Lake View Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_View_Cemetery_%28Seattle%29) in Seattle.
