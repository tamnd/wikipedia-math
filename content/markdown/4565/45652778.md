# Frederick Harcourt Kitchin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frederick Harcourt Kitchin"}

**Frederick Harcourt Kitchin**, (c. 1867–1932) was a British journalist, statistician and author.

:::

## Journalism {#Journalism}

Kitchin was the publisher of *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times) Financial and Commercial Supplement* from 1904 to 1908 and was an internationally recognised statistician in the field of nutrition. In 1918, Kitchin edited the *[Board of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Trade) Journal* and in 1925, he co-wrote the autobiography of managing director of *The Times*, [Charles Frederic Moberly Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Frederic_Moberly_Bell): *Moberly Bell and his Times*. Kitchin also wrote a number of books about the [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Navy): *The Secret of the Navy; What It Is and What We Owe to It* (1918) and *The Silent Watchers; England's Navy During the Great War; What It Is, and What We Owe to It*. (1918)

## Novels {#Novels}

Kitchin wrote *The Diversions of Dawson: a novel* using his own name. Under the pseudonym of **Bennet Copplestone**, he wrote a number of adventures including *The Lost Naval Papers* (1917), *The Last of the Grenvilles* (1919), *Madame Gilbert's Cannibal* (1920), *The Treasure of Golden Cap* (1922) and *Dead Men's Tales* (1926)

Kitchin set many of his stories in and around North [Devon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon), [Lundy Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lundy_Island) and the [Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset) town of [Bridport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridport).[^o4] The family of [Sir Richard Grenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Richard_Grenville) features heavily in his writing.

F. Harcourt Kitchin died in August 1932. His funeral was held at the Woking Crematorium on 10 August.[^o5]
