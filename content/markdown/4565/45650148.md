# Saddlepoint approximation method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Saddlepoint approximation method"}

The saddlepoint approximation method, initially proposed by [Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Daniels_%28statistician%29) (1954)[^o2] is a specific example of the mathematical [saddlepoint](wikipedia://en/27082137) technique applied to [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), in particular to the distribution of the sum of $N$ independent random variables. It provides a highly accurate approximation formula for any [PDF](wikipedia://en/43487) or [probability mass function](wikipedia://en/154725) of a distribution, based on the [moment generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment-generating_function). There is also a formula for the [CDF](wikipedia://en/5793) of the distribution, proposed by Lugannani and Rice (1980).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If the moment generating function of a random variable $X = \sum_{i=1}^{N} X_i$ is written as $M(t)=E\left[e^{tX}\right] = E\left[e^{t\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_i}\right]$ and the [cumulant generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulant_generating_function) as $K(t) = \log(M(t)) = \sum_{i=1}^{N}\log E\left[e^{tX_i}\right]$ then the saddlepoint approximation to the [PDF](wikipedia://en/43487) of the distribution $X$ is defined as:[^o2]

$$\hat{f}_X (x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \pi K''(\hat{s})}} \exp(K(\hat{s}) - \hat{s}x) \,\left(1+\mathcal{R}\right)$$

where $\mathcal{R}$ contains higher order terms to refine the approximation[^o2] and the saddlepoint approximation to the CDF is defined as:[^o2]

$$\hat{F}_X (x) = \begin{cases} \Phi(\hat{w}) + \phi(\hat{w})\left(\frac{1}{\hat{w}} - \frac{1}{\hat{u}}\right) & \text{for } x \neq \mu \\ \frac{1}{2} + \frac{K'''(0)}{6 \sqrt{2\pi} K''(0)^{3/2}} & \text{for } x = \mu \end{cases}$$

where $\hat{s}$ is the solution to $K'(\hat{s}) = x$, $\hat{w} = \operatorname{sgn}{\hat{s}}\sqrt{2(\hat{s}x - K(\hat{s}))}$ ,$\hat{u} = \hat{s}\sqrt{K''(\hat{s})}$, and $\Phi(t)$ and $\phi(t)$ are the [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) and the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), respectively, and $\mu$ is the mean of the random variable $X$:

$$\mu \triangleq E \left[X\right] = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} x f_X(x) \,\text{d}x = \sum_{i=1}^{N} E \left[X_i\right]= \sum_{i=1}^{N} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} x_i f_{X_i}(x_i) \,\text{d}x_i.$$

When the distribution is that of a sample mean, Lugannani and Rice's saddlepoint expansion for the cumulative distribution function $F(x)$ may be differentiated to obtain Daniels' saddlepoint expansion for the probability density function $f(x)$ (Routledge and Tsao, 1997). This result establishes the derivative of a truncated Lugannani and Rice series as an alternative asymptotic approximation for the density function $f(x)$. Unlike the original saddlepoint approximation for $f(x)$, this alternative approximation in general does not need to be renormalized.

:::
