# Ernst August Weiß

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst August Weiß"}

*Image: ErnstAugustWeissPortrait.jpg.* From Strubecker's obituary[^o2]

**Ernst August Weiß**[^o3] (or **Weiss**;[^o2] 5 May 1900 in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg) – 9 February 1942 in a Nazi [field hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hospital) near [Lake Ilmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ilmen)) was a German mathematician.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Since 1906, he attended the [Lyceum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyceum) in [Metz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metz), the Luther school and (1910–1912) Schiller [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster), and 1912–1917 the [Mommsen-Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mommsen-Gymnasium) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), where he passed the [Notabitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notabitur). He volunteered for the German [military engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineers) in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I), participated in the [attrition warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare) near [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims) and [Soissons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soissons) and was captured 26 September 1918 during the tank battle near [Cheppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheppy) by American soldiers.[^o2] After his release in September 1919 he studied mathematics at [Leibniz University Hannover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_University_Hannover) and (1920–1924) [Bonn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_University), where he obtained a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.#Germany) in March 1924[^o2] and a [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation#Germany) in May 1926.[^o2] In August 1926, he married the physicist Eva Renate Bidder.[^o5]

## Work in SA {#Work_in_SA}

Weiß joined an [SA storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) in 1933, and got promoted up to an [Obertruppführer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA-Obertruppführer). He directed the Bonn [Studentenwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studentenwerk).[^o2] He created the Mathematical Work Camps ("Mathematische Arbeitslager") in [Kronenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronenburg_%28Eifel%29) "as a new form of mathematical university teaching and collaboration of professors and students",[^o2] which took place 1—15 March 1934, 15—29 October 1934, 17 February–3 March 1935,[^o7] 23 February–7 March 1936,[^o8] and 17—31 October 1938.[^o9] Segal (1992) examined Weiß' camps from a pedagogical point of view, "as one example of how mathematics and Nazi ideology would interact".[^o10] Weiß was a permanent editor of the journal *[Deutsche Mathematik](wikipedia://en/31018614)*. A list of his publications is given in Blaschke (1942).[^o3]

## Death {#Death}

In 1941, Weiß took part in [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) as [company commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_commander) of an [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineering) unit; during the [Demyansk Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demyansk_Pocket) he was mortally wounded in close combat in Lukinn.[^o2]
