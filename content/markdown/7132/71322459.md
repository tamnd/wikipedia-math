# Kaniadakis distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kaniadakis distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **Kaniadakis distribution** (also known as **κ-distribution**) is a statistical distribution that emerges from the [Kaniadakis statistics](wikipedia://en/70596336).[^o2] There are several families of Kaniadakis distributions related to different constraints used in the maximization of the [Kaniadakis entropy](wikipedia://en/70596336#Kaniadakis_entropy), such as the [κ-Exponential distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Exponential_distribution), [κ-Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Gaussian_distribution), [Kaniadakis κ-Gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Gamma_distribution) and [κ-Weibull distribution](wikipedia://en/71231546). The κ-distributions have been applied for modeling a vast phenomenology of experimental statistical distributions in natural or artificial [complex systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system), such as, in [epidemiology](wikipedia://en/66997),[^o3] [quantum statistics](wikipedia://en/712450),[^o4][^o5][^o6] in [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology),[^o7][^o8][^o9] in [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics),[^o10][^o11][^o12] in [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy),[^o13][^o14][^o15] in [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488).[^o16]

:::

The κ-distributions are written as function of the κ-deformed exponential, taking the form

$$f_i=\exp_{\kappa}(-\beta E_i+\beta \mu)$$

enables the power-law description of complex systems following the consistent [κ-generalized statistical theory](wikipedia://en/70596336).,[^o17][^o18] where $\exp_{\kappa}(x)=(\sqrt{1+ \kappa^2 x^2}+\kappa x)^{1/\kappa}$ is the Kaniadakis κ-exponential function.

The κ-distribution becomes the common Boltzmann distribution at low energies, while it has a power-law tail at high energies, the feature of high interest of many researchers.

### Supported on semi-infinite intervals, usually \[0,∞) {#Supported_on_semi-infinite_intervals,_usually_[0,∞)}

*Image: Kaniadakis Gamma Distribution pdf.png.* Plot of the κ-Gamma distribution for typical κ-values.

- The [Kaniadakis Exponential distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Exponential_distribution), also called the κ-Exponential distribution. The [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) is a particular case when $\kappa \rightarrow 0.$
- The [Kaniadakis Gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Gamma_distribution), also called the κ-Gamma distribution, which is a four-parameter ($\kappa, \alpha, \beta, \nu$) deformation of the [generalized Gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/24553589).
  - The [κ-Gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Gamma_distribution) becomes a ...
    - [κ-Exponential distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Exponential_distribution) of Type I when $\alpha = \nu = 1$.
    - κ-Erlang distribution when $\alpha = 1$ and $\nu = n =$ positive integer.
    - [*κ*-Half-Normal distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Half-Normal_distribution), when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu = 1/2$.
    - [Generalized Gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/24553589), when $\alpha = 1$;
  - In the limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$, the [κ-Gamma distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaniadakis_Gamma_distribution) becomes a ...
    - [Erlang distribution](wikipedia://en/199028), when $\alpha = 1$ and $\nu = n =$ positive integer;
    - [Chi-Squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424), when $\alpha = 1$ and $\nu =$ half integer;
    - [Nakagami distribution](wikipedia://en/9492642), when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu > 0$;
    - [Rayleigh distribution](wikipedia://en/262296), when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu = 1$;
    - [Chi distribution](wikipedia://en/1901933), when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu =$ half integer;
    - Maxwell distribution, when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu = 3/2$;
    - [Half-Normal distribution](wikipedia://en/15906455), when $\alpha = 2$ and $\nu = 1/2$;
    - [Weibull distribution](wikipedia://en/206948), when $\alpha > 0$ and $\nu = 1$;
    - [Stretched Exponential distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stretched_Exponential_distribution), when $\alpha > 0$ and $\nu = 1/\alpha$;

### κ-Distribution Type IV {#κ-Distribution_Type_IV}

The **Kaniadakis distribution of Type IV** (or **κ-Distribution Type IV**) is a three-parameter family of [continuous statistical distributions](wikipedia://en/23543).[^o2]

The κ-Distribution Type IV distribution has the following [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487):

$$f_{_{\kappa}}(x) = \frac{\alpha}{\kappa} (2\kappa \beta )^{1/\kappa} \left(1 - \frac{\kappa \beta x^\alpha}{\sqrt{1+\kappa^2\beta^2x^{2\alpha} } } \right) x^{ -1 + \alpha / \kappa} \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha)$$

valid for $x \geq 0$, where $0 \leq |\kappa| < 1$ is the entropic index associated with the [Kaniadakis entropy](wikipedia://en/70596336#Kaniadakis_entropy), $\beta > 0$ is the scale parameter, and $\alpha > 0$ is the shape parameter.

The [cumulative distribution](wikipedia://en/5793) function of κ-Distribution Type IV assumes the form:

$$F_\kappa(x) = (2\kappa \beta )^{1/\kappa} x^{\alpha / \kappa} \exp_\kappa(-\beta x^\alpha)$$

The κ-Distribution Type IV does not admit a classical version, since the probability function and its cumulative reduces to zero in the classical limit $\kappa \rightarrow 0$.

Its [moment](wikipedia://en/32960532) of order $m$ given by

$$\operatorname{E}[X^m] = \frac{(2 \kappa \beta)^{-m/\alpha} }{ 1 + \kappa \frac{ m }{ 2\alpha } } \frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa} + \frac{m}{\alpha}\Big) \Gamma\Big(1 - \frac{m}{2\alpha}\Big)}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa} + \frac{m}{2\alpha}\Big)}$$

The moment of order $m$ of the κ-Distribution Type IV is finite for $m < 2\alpha$.
