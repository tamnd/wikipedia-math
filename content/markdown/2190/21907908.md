# Danny Calegari

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Danny Calegari"}

**Danny Matthew Cornelius Calegari** is a mathematician and, , a professor of mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago). His research interests include [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), and [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

In 1994, Calegari received a B.A. in Mathematics from the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne) with honors. He received his Ph.D. in 2000 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) under the joint supervision of [Andrew Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Casson) and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190); his dissertation concerned [foliations](wikipedia://en/371227) of three-dimensional [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470).[^o4]

From 2000–2002 he was Benjamin Peirce Assistant Professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), after which he joined the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) faculty; he became Merkin Professor in 2007. He was a University Professor of Pure Mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) in 2011–2012, and has been a Professor of Mathematics at the University of Chicago since 2012.[^o5]

Calegari is also an author of short fiction, published in *[Quadrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrant_%28magazine%29)*, *Southerly*, and *[Overland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overland_%28literary_journal%29)*. His story *A Green Light* was a winner of a 1992 [The Age Short Story Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age_Short_Story_Award).[^o6]

## Awards {#Awards}

Calegari was one of the recipients of the 2009 [Clay Research Award](wikipedia://en/664167) for his solution to the [Marden Tameness Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marden_tameness_conjecture) and the [Ahlfors Measure Conjecture](wikipedia://en/31197103).[^o8] In 2011 he was awarded a [Royal Society Wolfson Research Merit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_Wolfson_Research_Merit_Award),[^o9] and in 2012, he became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] In 2012 he delivered the Namboodiri Lectures[^o11] at the University of Chicago, and in 2013 he delivered the Blumenthal Lectures[^o12] at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University). In 2022 he gave an invited lecture[^o13] at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) and in 2024 he gave the Floer Lectures[^o14] in Bochum and the Roever Lecture[^o15] at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis).
