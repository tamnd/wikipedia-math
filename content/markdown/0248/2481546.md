# Guesstimate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Guesstimate"}

***Guesstimate*** is an informal [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) [portmanteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) of *guess* and *[estimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimate)*, first used by American [statisticians](wikipedia://en/48661) in 1934[^o2] or 1935.[^o3] It is defined as an estimate made without using adequate or complete information,[^o5] or, more strongly, as an estimate arrived at by [guesswork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guess) or [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138).[^o3][^o6][^o7] Like the words estimate and guess, guesstimate may be used as a verb or a noun (with the same change in [pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronunciation) as estimate). A guesstimate may be a first rough [approximation](wikipedia://en/336271) pending a more accurate estimate, or it may be an educated guess at something for which no better information will become available.

:::

The word may be used in a [pejorative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pejorative) sense if information for a better estimate is available but ignored.[^o8][^o9]

Guesstimation techniques are used:

- in [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), where the use of guesstimation techniques to solve [Fermi problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_problem) is taught as a useful skill to science students;[^o10]
- in [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology), where the [Drake equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_equation) is a well-known guesstimation method;[^o11]
- in [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), where economic forecasts and statistics are often based on guesstimates;[^o12] and
- in [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering), where new development of features and release timelines are based on effort guesstimates of tasks.

Lawrence Weinstein and [John Adam's](wikipedia://en/69469821) 2009 book *Guesstimation: Solving the World's Problems on the Back of a Cocktail Napkin*, based on the course "Physics on the Back of an Envelope" at [Old Dominion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dominion_University), promotes guesstimation techniques as a useful life skill. It includes many worked examples of guesstimation, including estimating the total number of miles that Americans drive in a year (about 2 trillion)[^o13] and the amount of high-level nuclear waste that a 1 [GW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigawatt) nuclear power plant produces in a year (about 60 tons).[^o14]
