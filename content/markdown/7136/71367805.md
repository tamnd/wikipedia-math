# Burnett equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Burnett equations"}

In [continuum mechanics](wikipedia://en/5918), a branch of mathematics, the **Burnett equations** are a set of higher-order continuum equations for non-equilibrium flows and the transition regimes where the [Navier–Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier–Stokes_equation) do not perform well.[^o2][^o3][^o4]

:::

They were derived by the English mathematician D. Burnett.[^o5]

## Series expansion {#Series_expansion}

### Series expansion approach {#Series_expansion_approach}

The series expansion technique used to derive the Burnett equations involves expanding the distribution function $f$ in the [Boltzmann equation](wikipedia://en/1026522) as a power series in the [Knudsen number](wikipedia://en/401314) $\mathrm{Kn}$:

$$f(r,c,t) = f^{(0)}(c|n,u,T) \left[1 + \mathrm{Kn} \phi^{(1)}(c|n,u,T) + \mathrm{Kn}^2 \phi^{(2)}(c|n,u,T) + \cdots \right]$$

Here, $f^{(0)}(c|n,u,T)$ represents the [Maxwell-Boltzmann equilibrium distribution function](wikipedia://en/19830), dependent on the [number density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_density) $n$, [macroscopic velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_velocity) $u$, and temperature $T$. The terms $\phi^{(1)}, \phi^{(2)},\dots$ are higher-order corrections that account for [non-equilibrium effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-equilibrium_effects), with each subsequent term incorporating higher powers of the Knudsen number.

### Derivation {#Derivation}

The first-order term $f^{(1)}$ in the expansion gives the [Navier-Stokes equations](wikipedia://en/48395), which include terms for viscosity and thermal conductivity. To obtain the Burnett equations, one must retain terms up to second order, corresponding to $\phi^{(2)}$. The Burnett equations include additional second-order derivatives of velocity, temperature, and density, representing more subtle effects of non-equilibrium gas dynamics.

The Burnett equations can be expressed as:

$$\mathbf{u}_t + (\mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{u} + \nabla p = \nabla \cdot (\nu \nabla \mathbf{u}) + \text{higher-order terms}$$

Here, the "**higher-order terms**" involve second-order gradients of velocity and temperature, which are absent in the Navier-Stokes equations. These terms become significant in situations with high Knudsen numbers, where the assumptions of the Navier-Stokes framework break down.

## Extensions {#Extensions}

The Onsager-Burnett Equations, commonly referred to as OBurnett, which form a superset of the [Navier-Stokes equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier-Stokes_equations) and are second-order accurate for [Knudsen number](wikipedia://en/401314).[^o11][^o12]

::: {#o13 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$\sqrt{\tau} \frac{du^s}{ds} - \frac{9}{8} \alpha_1 u^* {\left(\frac{du^*}{ds}\right)}^2 = \frac{\tau}{u^*} - \tau_0 -1 + u^* \tag{1}$$

:::

::: {#o14 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$\frac{45}{16} \sqrt{\tau} \frac{d \tau}{ds} + \frac{9}{4} \gamma_1 \tau {\left(\frac{du^*}{ds}\right)}^2 - \frac{9}{4} \Psi u^* \frac{d \tau}{ds} \frac{du^*}{ds} = \frac{3}{2} (\tau - \tau_0) - \frac{1}{2} {\left(1 - u^*\right)}^2 - \tau_0(1-u^*) \tag{2}$$

:::
