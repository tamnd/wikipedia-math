# Brauer's three main theorems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer's three main theorems"}

**Brauer's main theorems** are three [theorems](wikipedia://en/30977) in [representation theory of finite groups](wikipedia://en/874400) linking the [blocks](wikipedia://en/1110685) of a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) (in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) *p*) with those of its [*p*-local subgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-local_subgroup), that is to say, the [normalizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer) of its nontrivial *p*-subgroups.

:::

The second and third main theorems allow refinements of [orthogonality relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonality_relations) for [ordinary characters](wikipedia://en/691741) which may be applied in [finite group theory](wikipedia://en/41890#Finite_group_theory). These do not presently admit a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) purely in terms of ordinary characters. All three main theorems are stated in terms of the **Brauer correspondence**.

## Brauer correspondence {#Brauer_correspondence}

There are many ways to extend the definition which follows, but this is close to the early treatments by Brauer. Let *G* be a finite group, *p* be a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), *F* be a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) of characteristic *p*. Let *H* be a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G* which contains

$$QC_G(Q)$$

for some *p*-subgroup *Q* of *G*, and is contained in the [normalizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalizer)

$$N_G(Q),$$

where $C_G(Q)$ is the [centralizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) of *Q* in *G*.

The **Brauer homomorphism** (with respect to *H*) is a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) from the [center](wikipedia://en/53796787) of the [group algebra](wikipedia://en/349223) of *G* over *F* to the corresponding algebra for *H*. Specifically, it is the [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) to $Z(FG)$ of the (linear) projection from $FG$ to $FC_G(Q)$ whose [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) is spanned by the elements of *G* outside $C_G(Q)$. The [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of this map is contained in $Z(FH)$, and it transpires that the map is also a [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411).

Since it is a ring homomorphism, for any block *B* of *FG*, the Brauer homomorphism sends the identity element of *B* to an [idempotent element](wikipedia://en/361940) (possibly to 0). If the idempotent is non-zero, it may be decomposed as a sum of (mutually [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/361940#Types_of_ring_idempotents)) [primitive](wikipedia://en/361940#Types_of_ring_idempotents) idempotents of *Z*(*FH*). Each of these primitive idempotents is the multiplicative identity of some block of *FH.* The block *b* of *FH* is said to be a **Brauer correspondent** of *B* if its identity element occurs in this decomposition of the image of the identity of *B* under the Brauer homomorphism.

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Brauer's first main theorem"}

## Brauer's first main theorem {#Brauer's_first_main_theorem}

Brauer's first main theorem () states that if $G$ is a finite group and $D$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$, then there is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between the set of (characteristic *p*) blocks of $G$ with [defect group](wikipedia://en/1110685#Defect_groups) $D$ and blocks of the normalizer $N_G(D)$ with defect group *D*. This bijection arises because when $H = N_G(D)$, each block of *G* with defect group *D* has a unique Brauer correspondent block of *H*, which also has defect group *D*.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Brauer's second main theorem"}

## Brauer's second main theorem {#Brauer's_second_main_theorem}

Brauer's second main theorem () gives, for an element *t* whose [order](wikipedia://en/493527) is a power of a prime *p*, a criterion for a (characteristic *p*) block of $C_G(t)$ to correspond to a given block of $G$, via *generalized decomposition numbers*. These are the coefficients which occur when the restrictions of ordinary characters of $G$ (from the given block) to elements of the form *tu*, where *u* ranges over elements of order prime to *p* in $C_G(t)$, are written as linear combinations of the irreducible [Brauer characters](wikipedia://en/1110685) of $C_G(t)$. The content of the theorem is that it is only necessary to use Brauer characters from blocks of $C_G(t)$ which are Brauer correspondents of the chosen block of *G*.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Brauer's third main theorem"}

## Brauer's third main theorem {#Brauer's_third_main_theorem}

Brauer's third main theorem (Brauer 1964, theorem3) states that when *Q* is a *p*-subgroup of the finite group *G*, and *H* is a subgroup of *G* containing $QC_G(Q)$ and contained in $N_G(Q)$, then the [principal block](wikipedia://en/1110685) of *H* is the only Brauer correspondent of the principal block of *G* (where the blocks referred to are calculated in characteristic *p*).

:::
