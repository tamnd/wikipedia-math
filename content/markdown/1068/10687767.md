# Schatten norm

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schatten norm"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **[Schatten](wikipedia://en/25778498) norm** (or **Schatten–von-Neumann norm**) arises as a generalization of *p*-integrability similar to the [trace class](wikipedia://en/192266) [norm](wikipedia://en/990534) and the [Hilbert–Schmidt](wikipedia://en/976673) norm.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $H_1$, $H_2$ be Hilbert spaces, and $T$ a (linear) [bounded operator](wikipedia://en/455961) from $H_1$ to $H_2$. For $p\in [1,\infty)$, define the Schatten *p*-norm of $T$ as

$$\|T\| _p = [\operatorname{Tr} (|T|^p)]^{1/p},$$

where $|T|:=\sqrt{(T^*T)}$, using the [operator square root](wikipedia://en/2929701#Square_roots_of_positive_operators).

If $T$ is compact and $H_1,\,H_2$ are separable, then

$$\|T\| _p := \bigg( \sum_{n\ge 1} s^p_n(T)\bigg)^{1/p}$$

for $s_1(T) \ge s_2(T) \ge \cdots \ge s_n(T) \ge \cdots \ge 0$ the [singular values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_values) of $T$, i.e. the eigenvalues of the Hermitian operator $|T|:=\sqrt{(T^*T)}$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

### Special cases {#Special_cases}

- The Schatten 1-norm is the **[nuclear norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_norm)** (also known as the **[trace norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_norm)**, or the **[Ky Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_Value_Decomposition#Ky_Fan_norms) *n*-norm**[^o6]).
- The Schatten 2-norm is the **[Frobenius norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_norm)**.
- The Schatten ∞-norm is the **[spectral norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_norm)** (also known as the **operator norm**, or the largest singular value).

:::

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In the following we formally extend the range of $p$ to $[1,\infty]$ with the convention that $\|\cdot\|_{\infty}$ is the operator norm. The dual index to $p=\infty$ is then $q=1$.

- The Schatten norms are unitarily invariant: for unitary operators $U$ and $V$ and $p\in [1,\infty]$,

$$\|U T V\|_p = \|T\|_p.$$

- They satisfy [Hölder's inequality](wikipedia://en/191538): for all $p\in [1,\infty]$ and $q$ such that $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$, and operators $S\in\mathcal{L}(H_2,H_3), T\in\mathcal{L}(H_1,H_2)$ defined between Hilbert spaces $H_1, H_2,$ and $H_3$ respectively,

$$\|ST\|_1 \leq \|S\|_p \|T\|_q.$$

If $p,q,r\in [1,\infty]$ satisfy $\tfrac{1}{p} + \tfrac{1}{q} = \tfrac{1}{r}$, then we have

$$\|ST\|_r \leq \|S\|_p \|T\|_q.$$

The latter version of Hölder's inequality is proven in higher generality (for noncommutative $L^p$ spaces instead of Schatten-p classes) in.[^o9] (For matrices the latter result is found in[^o10].)

- Sub-multiplicativity: For all $p\in [1,\infty]$ and operators $S\in\mathcal{L}(H_2,H_3), T\in\mathcal{L}(H_1,H_2)$ defined between Hilbert spaces $H_1, H_2,$ and $H_3$ respectively,

$$\|ST\|_p \leq \|S\|_p \|T\|_p .$$

- Monotonicity: For $1\leq p\leq p'\leq\infty$,

$$\|T\|_1 \geq \|T\|_p \geq \|T\|_{p'} \geq \|T\|_\infty.$$

- Duality: Let $H_1, H_2$ be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, $p\in [1,\infty]$ and $q$ such that $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$, then

$$\|S\|_p = \sup\lbrace |\langle S,T\rangle | \mid \|T\|_q = 1\rbrace,$$

where $\langle S,T\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(S^*T)$ denotes the [Hilbert–Schmidt inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert–Schmidt_inner_product).

- Let $(e_k)_k,(f_{k'})_{k'}$ be two orthonormal basis of the Hilbert spaces $H_1, H_2$, then for $p=1$

$$\|T\|_1 \leq \sum_{k,k'}\left|T_{k,k'}\right|.$$

:::

## Remarks {#Remarks}

Notice that $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm (see [Hilbert–Schmidt operator](wikipedia://en/976673)), $\|\cdot\|_1$ is the trace class norm (see [trace class](wikipedia://en/192266)), and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is the operator norm (see [operator norm](wikipedia://en/246722)).

Note that the [matrix p-norm](wikipedia://en/1543735) is often also written as $\|\cdot\|_p$, but it is not the same as Schatten norm. In fact, we have $\|A\|_{\text{matrix p-norm}, 2} = \|A\|_{\text{Schatten}, \infty}$.

For $p\in(0,1)$ the function $\|\cdot\|_p$ is an example of a [quasinorm](wikipedia://en/6652599).

An operator which has a finite Schatten norm is called a [Schatten class operator](wikipedia://en/10698304) and the space of such operators is denoted by $S_p(H_1,H_2)$. With this norm, $S_p(H_1,H_2)$ is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989), and a Hilbert space for *p* = 2.

Observe that $S_p(H_1,H_2) \subseteq \mathcal{K} (H_1,H_2)$, the algebra of [compact operators](wikipedia://en/577441). This follows from the fact that if the sum is finite the spectrum will be finite or countable with the origin as [limit point](wikipedia://en/226505), and hence a compact operator (see [compact operator on Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/6690773)).
