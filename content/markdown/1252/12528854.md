# Hermite constant

*Image: Hermite constant.svg.* A hexagonal lattice with unit covolume (the area of the quadrilateral is 1). Both arrows are minimum non-zero elements for $n-1$ with length $\lambda_n=\sqrt{\gamma_n}=\sqrt{2/\sqrt{3}}$.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hermite constant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hermite constant**, named after [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422), determines how long a shortest element of a [lattice](wikipedia://en/469246) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) can be.

:::

The constant $\gamma_n$ for integers $n>0$ is defined as follows. For a lattice $L$ in Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ with unit covolume, i.e. $\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{R}^n/L)=1$, let $\lambda_1(L)$ denote the least length of a nonzero element of $L$. Then $\sqrt{\gamma_n}$ is the maximum of $\lambda_1(L)$ over all such lattices $L$.

The [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) in the definition of the Hermite constant is a matter of historical convention.

Alternatively, the Hermite constant $\gamma_n$ can be defined as the square of the maximal [systole](wikipedia://en/2545455) of a flat $n$-dimensional [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) of unit volume.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The Hermite constant is known in dimensions 1–8 and 24.

::: {#o4 .table cue=table}

| $n$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\gamma_n$ | $1$ | $\frac 2 {\sqrt 3}$ | $\sqrt[3]2$ | $\sqrt 2$ | $\sqrt[5]8$ | $\frac 2 {\sqrt[6]3}$ | $\sqrt[7]{64}$ | $2$ | $4$ |

:::

For $n=2$, one has $\gamma_2=2/\sqrt{3}$. This value is attained by the [hexagonal lattice](wikipedia://en/2868762) of the [Eisenstein integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_integers), scaled to have a [fundamental parallelogram](wikipedia://en/1605743) with unit area.[^o5]

:::

## Estimates {#Estimates}

It is known that[^o7]

$$\gamma_n \le \left( \frac 4 3 \right)^\frac{n-1}{2}.$$

A stronger estimate due to [Hans Frederick Blichfeldt](wikipedia://en/33390154)[^o8] is[^o9]

$$\gamma_n \le \left( \frac 2 \pi \right)\Gamma\left(2 + \frac n 2\right)^\frac{2}{n},$$

where $\Gamma(x)$ is the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316).
