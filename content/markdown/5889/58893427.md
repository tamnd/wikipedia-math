# Zofia Szmydt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zofia Szmydt"}

**Zofia Szmydt** (29 July 1923 – 26 November 2010) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in the areas of [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) and the [theory of distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_distributions). She was a winner of the [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791) for mathematics in 1956.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Zofia Szmydt was born in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) on 29 July 1923. Her mother, Zofia Szmydtowa (née Gąsiorowska), was a [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) and [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philologist).[^n1]

Szmydt studied at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in clandestine classes during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II). Following the [Warsaw Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Uprising), she and her family were deported to [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków).[^n1]

In 1946, Szmydt graduated in mathematics from the [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University). She defended her doctoral thesis in 1949, written under the direction of [Tadeusz Ważewski](wikipedia://en/1028319).[^n2]

Szmydt died on 27 November 2010.[^n3]

## Career {#Career}

Until 1952, Szmydt worked at the Jagiellonian University. She was a member of the Mathematical Institute of the [Polish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) between 1949 and 1971. In 1971, she joined the University of Warsaw where she became a professor in 1984. She retired in 1993.

### Contributions {#Contributions}

In her 1951 paper, *Sur l’allure asymptotique des intégrales des équations différentielles ordinaires*, Szmydt applied the topological method by Ważewski to generalizations of Perron's classic results on the asymptotics of systems of solutions of ordinary differential equations.[^n4]

Szmydt's work on hyperbolic differential equations *Sur un problème concernant un systèmes d’équations différentielles hyperboliques d’ordre arbitraire à deux variables indépendantes* (1957) proposed a generalised solution for the functional differential equation, which subsumed the Darboux, Cauchy, Picard and Goursat problems as special cases.[^n5] This was in later literature referred to as the Szmydt problem.[^n6]

Szmydt's textbook *Fourier Transformation and Linear Differential Equations* (1971) was the first on the topic to be published in the [Polish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language).[^n7] Her motivation was to present the basics of the theory of partial differential equations with a particular emphasis on distributions in limit problems of the classical equations (the heat equation, [Schrödinger equation](wikipedia://en/59874), and the Laplace and Poisson equations).[^n8]

In *Paley–Wiener theorems for the Mellin transformations* (1990), Szmydt gave a full characterization of the space of multipliers for Mellin's distribution in terms of the [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) (equivalent to the [Paley–Wiener theorem](wikipedia://en/632992)) and established relationships between Schwartz and Mellin distribution spaces.[^n9]

## Honours {#Honours}

In 1956, Szmydt won the [Stefan Banach Prize](wikipedia://en/2121791#Stefan_Banach_Prize) of the Polish Mathematical Society for her research into topological methods in nonlinear ordinary differential equations.[^n2] In 1973, she awarded the Commander's Cross of the [Order of Polonia Restituta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Polonia_Restituta) for her services to mathematical education.[^n7]
