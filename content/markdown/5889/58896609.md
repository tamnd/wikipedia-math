# Klavdiya Latysheva

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Klavdiya Latysheva"}

Yakovlevna

**Klavdiya Yakovlevna Latysheva** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Клавдия Яковлевна Латышева; [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Клавдія Яківна Латишева; born 14 March 1897, [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv), [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) – 11 May 1956, Kyiv, [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (now Kyiv, Ukraine)) was a Soviet mathematician known for her contributions to the theory of differential equations, electrodynamics and probability. She was honoured with the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin) and the [Medal "For Valiant Labour in the Great Patriotic War 1941–1945"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_"For_Valiant_Labour_in_the_Great_Patriotic_War_1941–1945").

:::

## Life {#Life}

Klavdiya Yakovlevna Latysheva was born in Kyiv, Russian Empire (now Kyiv, Ukraine) on 14 March 1897[^n1] in a [Russian military family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army). She completed her high school in 1916, and obtained a degree from the Physico-Mathematical division of the Kyiv higher women's educational institution in 1921. The rest of her education and career was at the [Mykhailo Drahomanov University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko_National_University_of_Kyiv). Her teachers were [Boris Bukreev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yakovlevich_Bukreev), [Dmitry Grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Grave), and [Georgy Pfeiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgii_Yurii_Pfeiffer). From 1925 to 1928, she was in postgraduate studies, working on finding solutions to differential and integral equations using [Mykhailo Kravchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykhailo_Kravchuk)'s method of moments. Kravchuk was her doctoral adviser.[^n2] She was the first Ukrainian woman to obtain a doctorate in the mathematical and physical sciences, with her dissertation on approximate solutions of linear differential equations with singular coefficients (1936).[^n3]

Latysheva was an organiser of the first All-Ukrainian Mathematical Olympiad in 1936.

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Latysheva transferred to [Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov).[^n3] She worked at the automotive and highway faculty of the [Saratov State Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_State_Technical_University).[^n4]

In 1946, she established a scientific group in the Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics of the Taras Shevchenko University to study the analytical theory of differential equations. Between 1953 and 1956, Latysheva headed this group. She was the dean of the Faculty between 1952 and 1954.[^n5]

For her contributions to mathematics, she was awarded the [Order of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Lenin) in 1954,[^n6] as well as the [Medal "For Valiant Labour in the Great Patriotic War 1941–1945"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_"For_Valiant_Labour_in_the_Great_Patriotic_War_1941–1945").

Latysheva died on 11 May 1956,[^n3] and was interred in the [Lukyanivka cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukianivka_%28neighborhood%29) in Kyiv.

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

Latysheva developed an effective method for the construction of solutions of linear ordinary differential equations around regular and irregular points by expanding on [Poincaré](wikipedia://en/48740)'s concept of rank and L. Tome's concept of anti-rank. This is now known as the Frobenius-Latysheva method. As part of this work, she determined a new type of normal and normal-regular solutions, and provided necessary and sufficient conditions for their existence.[^n7] This was a major contribution, providing for the existence of closed-form solutions of [linear differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_equations) with [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) coefficients.[^n3] A series of twelve articles, published between 1946–1952, established the full results, and also simplified and extended related theorems in the analytic theory of differential equations by Poincaré, Cayley and others.[^n6]
