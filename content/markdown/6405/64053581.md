# Virasoro group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Virasoro group"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), the **Virasoro group** or **Bott–Virasoro group** (often denoted by *Vir*)[^n1] is an [infinite-dimensional Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945#Infinite-dimensional_Lie_groups) defined as the universal [central extension](wikipedia://en/345807#Central_extension) of the [group of diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564#Diffeomorphism_group) of the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220). The corresponding [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) is the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029), which has a key role in [conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/364774) (CFT) and [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::

The group is named after [Miguel Ángel Virasoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Ángel_Virasoro_%28physicist%29) and [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457).

## Background {#Background}

An [orientation-preserving](wikipedia://en/187446) [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of the circle $S^1$, whose points are labelled by a real coordinate $x$ subject to the identification $x\sim x+2\pi$, is a [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) map $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:x\mapsto f(x)$ such that $f(x+2\pi)=f(x)+2\pi$ and $f'(x)>0$. The set of all such maps spans a group, with [multiplication](wikipedia://en/3948) given by the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of diffeomorphisms. This group is the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of the circle, denoted as $\widetilde{\text{Diff}}{}^+(S^1)$.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Virasoro group is the universal central extension of $\widetilde{\text{Diff}}{}^+(S^1)$.[^n2] The extension is defined by a specific [two-cocycle](wikipedia://en/248635#Definitions), which is a real-valued function $\mathsf{C}(f,g)$ of pairs of diffeomorphisms. Specifically, the extension is defined by the Bott–Thurston cocycle:

$$\mathsf{C}(f,g) \equiv -\frac{1}{48\pi}\int_0^{2\pi} \log\big[(f\circ g)'(x)\big] \frac{g''(x)\,\text{d}x}{g'(x)}.$$

In these terms, the Virasoro group is the set $\widetilde{\text{Diff}}{}^+(S^1)\times\mathbb{R}$ of all pairs $(f,\alpha)$, where $f$ is a diffeomorphism and $\alpha$ is a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438), endowed with the binary operation

$$(f,\alpha)\cdot(g,\beta) = \big(f\circ g,\alpha+\beta+\mathsf{C}(f,g)\big).$$

This operation is an associative group operation. This extension is the only central extension of the universal cover of the group of circle diffeomorphisms, up to trivial extensions.[^n3] The Virasoro group can also be defined without the use explicit coordinates or an explicit choice of [cocycle](wikipedia://en/248635) to represent the central extension, via a description the [universal cover](wikipedia://en/250323#Universal_covering) of the group.[^n3]

:::

## Virasoro algebra {#Virasoro_algebra}

The [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of the Virasoro group is the [Virasoro algebra](wikipedia://en/348029). As a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), the Lie algebra of the Virasoro group consists of pairs $(\xi,\alpha)$, where $\xi=\xi(x)\partial_x$ is a [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) on the circle and $\alpha$ is a real number as before. The vector field, in particular, can be seen as an [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990) diffeomorphism $f(x)=x+\epsilon\xi(x)$. The Lie bracket of pairs $(\xi,\alpha)$ then follows from the multiplication defined above, and can be shown to satisfy[^n4]

$$\big[(\xi,\alpha),(\zeta,\beta)\big]=\bigg([\xi,\zeta],-\frac{1}{24\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\text{d}x\,\xi(x)\zeta'''(x)\bigg)$$

where the bracket of vector fields on the right-hand side is the [usual one](wikipedia://en/10282799): $[\xi,\zeta]=(\xi(x)\zeta'(x)-\zeta(x)\xi'(x))\partial_x$. Upon defining the complex generators

$$L_m\equiv\Big(-ie^{imx}\partial_x,-\frac{i}{24}\delta_{m,0}\Big), \qquad Z\equiv (0,-i),$$

the Lie bracket takes the standard textbook form of the Virasoro algebra:[^n5]

$$[L_m,L_n] = (m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{Z}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n}.$$

The generator $Z$ [commutes](wikipedia://en/294390) with the whole algebra. Since its presence is due to a central extension, it is subject to a [superselection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superselection) rule which guarantees that, in any [physical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_system) having Virasoro symmetry, the operator representing $Z$ is a multiple of the identity. The coefficient in front of the identity is then known as a [central charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge).

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Since each diffeomorphism $f$ must be specified by infinitely many parameters (for instance the [Fourier modes](wikipedia://en/59038) of the periodic function $f(x)-x$), the Virasoro group is infinite-dimensional.

### Coadjoint representation {#Coadjoint_representation}

The Lie bracket of the Virasoro algebra can be viewed as a differential of the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of the Virasoro group. Its dual, the [coadjoint representation](wikipedia://en/3226011) of the Virasoro group, provides the transformation law of a CFT [stress tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) under conformal transformations. From this perspective, the [Schwarzian derivative](wikipedia://en/555745) in this transformation law emerges as a consequence of the Bott–Thurston cocycle; in fact, the Schwarzian is the so-called Souriau cocycle (referring to [Jean-Marie Souriau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Souriau)) associated with the Bott–Thurston cocycle.[^n3]
