# William Vann Parker

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Vann Parker"}

**William "Vann" Parker** (December 22, 1901 – November 25, 1987) was an American mathematician who worked primarily in [classical algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).[^o3] He was chair of the mathematics department at both [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University) in the 1940s and [Auburn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University) in the 1950s. He was also the Graduate Dean at Auburn during [integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_integration_in_the_United_States) in Alabama.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

On December 22, 1901, Parker was born in [Monroe, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe,_North_Carolina) to Benjamin Franklin Parker and Sarah Della Cox.[^o24][^o5] He obtained a Bachelor of Arts degree from University of North Carolina in 1923[^o6] followed by a Master of Arts degree the following year.[^o7] While at the University of North Carolina, he was a member of the Di Society, Math Club, Elisha Mitchell Scientific Society, and Union Country Club. He was also an Assistant Instructor in Mathematics during his senior year.[^o8] He attended [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) between 1926 and 1927; and eventually obtain a PhD at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) in 1931 under his advisor [Albert Arnold Bennett](wikipedia://en/80380836).[^o9][^o25]

## Career {#Career}

In 1924, he began teaching at the University of the South until 1925. He would then teach at the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina) between 1925 and 1931. During his attendance at Princeton University he taught there as well. In 1931, he started teaching at [Mississippi Woman's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Woman's_College) until 1934. He then started teaching at [Georgia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) in 1934 until 1936 where he was one of the first people hired there to have a doctorate degree.[^o12] In 1936, he became an associate professor at [Louisiana State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_State_University) where he advised Anthony Aucoin (1940) and John Currie (1948).[^o11][^o12] In 1943, he would become the head of the math department at Louisiana State University until leaving in 1947. He then became a regents professor at [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) between 1947 and 1950.[^o13][^o25]

### Years at Auburn {#Years_at_Auburn}

In 1950 he became the head of the mathematics department at [Alabama Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_University), now known as Auburn University, a role he would hold until 1965.[^o15][^o16] He is credited for creating the mathematics program at Auburn.[^o17] In 1953, he took on the additional role of Graduate Dean at Auburn until his retirement on July 1, 1972.[^o18] From 1953 to 1971 he was a council representative of Auburn University for the [Oak Ridge Associated Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge_Associated_Universities).[^o19] At Auburn he advised, Robert Ackerson (1955) and Marion Wicht Sr. (1957).[^o11] As head of the Math Department in 1960, he traveled to near [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) to interviewed [Emillie Haynsworth](wikipedia://en/61816794) by having her work on a research problem with him in matrix theory until the end of his trip when he stated that she could have a job at Auburn.[^o20]

While he was the dean of the graduate school he oversaw the first doctorate programs at Auburn. As dean of the graduate school, he was the defendant in the court case Franklin v. Parker. Parker rejected [Harold Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Franklin)'s application to the graduate school at Auburn for not graduating from a college that was accredited with the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools. The court found that this is a reasonable policy in an ideal world. However, given that Alabama only accredited the white schools and let the black schools accreditations to expire, Parker was found to have discriminated against Franklin by following the policy that he was instructed to follow.[^o21] In January 1964, Parker and Malcolm McMillan, a history professor, helped Franklin register for classes at the library while the [national guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_%28United_States%29) ensured their safety from the crowds.[^o22]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was a member of the [Mathematics Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_Association_of_America). He helped start the Alabama Association of College Teachers of Mathematics.[^o24] He was a member of [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi) and [Pi Mu Epsilon](wikipedia://en/1565972).[^o25]

He was listed in Who's Who in America for 1976–1977.[^o24][^o26] He was also included in Who's Who in the South and Southwest, Who's Who Education, Who's Who Atoms, American Men of Science, and Leaders in American Science.[^o26]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

He married the genealogist and writer Carolyn Adele Edwards on July 3, 1926.[^o24][^o13] In February 1960, Carolyn became a member of the [United Daughters of the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Daughters_of_the_Confederacy), [Admiral Semmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Semmes) Chapter in Auburn, Al.[^o28] Carolyn and Vann Parker had a son and two daughters.[^o13] For more than 60 years, he was a part of the [University Masonic Lodge at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge).[^o13]

His daughter Emily "Anne" Parker Battle followed in his footsteps studying mathematics and then teaching mathematics at the [University of Alabama at Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama_at_Birmingham) and the [University of Montevallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montevallo).[^o29]

## Death and legacy {#Death_and_legacy}

In his honor, the Auburn Board of Trustees decided to name the mathematics building Parker Hall.[^o24][^o31] Parker died on November 25, 1987, in Birmingham, Alabama, and was buried at Auburn Memorial Park Cemetery.[^o13][^o5] The Alabama Association of College Teachers of Mathematics holds a lecture named after Parker and [Fred A. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_A._Lewis) of the University of Alabama at their annual meeting called the "Lewis-Parker Lecture" to honor a mathematician from the state of [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama).[^o32]
