# Three-twist knot

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Three-twist knot"}

In [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), the **three-twist knot** is the [twist knot](wikipedia://en/26701261) with three-half twists. It is listed as the **5<sub>2</sub> knot**[^o3] in the [Alexander-Briggs notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander-Briggs_notation), and is one of two knots with [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298598) five, the other being the [cinquefoil knot](wikipedia://en/16856240).

:::

The 5<sub>2</sub> knot can be represented with the following [parametric equation](wikipedia://en/576108): [^o4]

$$\begin{aligned} x &= \cos(2\phi) \\ y &= \cos(5\phi + \pi/4) \\ z &= \cos(2\phi + \pi/2) + \cos(3\phi + \pi/4) \end{aligned}$$

with $0\leq\phi\leq 2\pi$.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The three-twist knot is a [prime knot](wikipedia://en/315030), and it is [invertible](wikipedia://en/17351839) but not [amphichiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphichiral_knot). Its [Alexander polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) is

$$\Delta(t) = 2t-3+2t^{-1}, \,$$

since $\begin{pmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & 2\end{pmatrix}$ is a possible [Seifert matrix](wikipedia://en/1343951), or because of its [Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647#Alexander–Conway_polynomial), which is

$$\nabla(z) = 2z^2+1, \,$$

and its [Jones polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107642) is

$V(q) = q^{-1} - q^{-2} + 2q^{-3} - q^{-4} + q^{-5} - q^{-6}. \,$[^o7]

Because the Alexander polynomial is not [monic](wikipedia://en/314730), the three-twist knot is not [fibered](wikipedia://en/1358592).

The three-twist knot is a [hyperbolic knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_knot), with its complement having a [volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_volume_%28knot%29) of approximately 2.82812.

If the fibre of the knot in the initial image of this page were cut at the bottom right of the image, and the ends were pulled apart, it would result in a single-stranded [figure-of-nine knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure-of-nine_loop#Figure-of-nine_knot) (not the figure-of-nine loop).
