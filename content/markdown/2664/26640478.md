# György Elekes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="György Elekes"}

**György Elekes** (19 May 1949 – 29 September 2008)[^o3] was a [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) who specialized in [Combinatorial geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_geometry) and [Combinatorial set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_set_theory). He may be best known for his work in the field that would eventually be called [Additive Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/4460333). Particularly notable was his "ingenious"[^o4] application of the [Szemerédi–Trotter theorem](wikipedia://en/1051627) to improve the best known lower bound for the [sum-product problem](wikipedia://en/35021032).[^o5] He also proved that any [polynomial-time algorithm](wikipedia://en/405944#Polynomial_time) approximating the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of [convex bodies](wikipedia://en/8652020) must have a [multiplicative error](wikipedia://en/233807), and the error grows [exponentially](wikipedia://en/191933) on the dimension.[^o10] With [Micha Sharir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha_Sharir) he set up a framework which eventually led [Guth](wikipedia://en/31875947) and [Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nets_Hawk_Katz) to the solution of the [Erdős distinct distances problem](wikipedia://en/30193394).[^o6] (See below.)

:::

## Life {#Life}

After graduating from the mathematics program at [Fazekas Mihály Gimnázium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazekas_Mihály_Gimnázium_%28Budapest%29) (i.e., "[Fazekas Mihály](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazekas_Mihály) high school" in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest), which is known for its excellence, especially in mathematics), Elekes studied mathematics at the [Eötvös Loránd University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eötvös_Loránd_University). Upon completing his degree, he joined the faculty in the Department of [Analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) at the university. In 1984, he joined the newly forming Department of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science), which was being headed by [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938). Elekes was promoted to [full professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor#Tenured_and_tenure-track_positions) in 2005. He received the *[Doctor of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doktor_nauk)* title from the [Hungarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2001.[^o3]

## Work {#Work}

Elekes started his mathematical work in [combinatorial set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_set_theory), answering some questions posed by [Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) and [Hajnal](wikipedia://en/21765779). One of his results states that if the set of infinite subsets of the set of natural numbers is split into countably many parts, then in one of them, there is a solution of the equation *A*∪*B*=*C*.[^o3][^o9] His interest later switched to another favorite topic of Erdős, [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468) and [geometric algorithm theory](wikipedia://en/176927). In 1986 he proved that if a deterministic polynomial algorithm computes a number *V*(*K*) for every convex body *K* in any Euclidean space given by a separation oracle such that *V*(*K*) always at least vol(*K*), the volume of *K*, then for every large enough dimension *n*, there is a convex body in the *n*-dimensional Euclidean space such that *V*(*K*)>2<sup>\0.99*n*</sup>vol(*K*). That is, any polynomial-time estimator of volume over *K* must be inaccurate by at least an exponential factor.[^o3][^o10]

Not long before his death he developed new tools in [Algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and used them to obtain results in [Discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), proving [Purdy's Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_B._Purdy#Purdy's_Conjecture). [Micha Sharir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha_Sharir) organized, extended and published Elekes's posthumous notes on these methods.[^o11] Then [Nets Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nets_Katz) and [Larry Guth](wikipedia://en/31875947) used them to solve (apart from a factor of (log n) <sup>1/2</sup>) the [Erdős distinct distances problem](wikipedia://en/30193394), posed in 1946.[^o6][^o12]
