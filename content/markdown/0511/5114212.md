# Goursat's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Goursat's lemma"}

**Goursat's lemma**, named after the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Édouard Goursat](wikipedia://en/1674255), is an [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) about [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of two [groups](wikipedia://en/19447).

:::

It can be stated more generally in a [Goursat variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goursat_variety) (and consequently it also holds in any [Maltsev variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltsev_variety)), from which one recovers a more general version of [Zassenhaus' butterfly lemma](wikipedia://en/877209). In this form, Goursat's lemma also implies the [snake lemma](wikipedia://en/87089) .

## Groups {#Groups}

Goursat's lemma for groups can be stated as follows.

Let $G$, $G'$ be groups, and let $H$ be a subgroup of $G\times G'$ such that the two [projections](wikipedia://en/3909097) $p_1: H \to G$ and $p_2: H \to G'$ are [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) (i.e., $H$ is a [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of $G$ and $G'$). Let $N$ be the kernel of $p_2$ and $N'$ the [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of $p_1$. One can identify $N$ as a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of $G$, and $N'$ as a normal subgroup of $G'$. Then the image of $H$ in $G/N \times G'/N'$ is the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/12397) $G/N \cong G'/N'$. One then obtains a bijection between:

1. Subgroups of $G\times G'$ which project onto both factors,
1. Triples $(N, N', f)$ with $N$ normal in $G$, $N'$ normal in $G'$ and $f$ isomorphism of $G/N$ onto $G'/N'$.

An immediate consequence of this is that the subdirect product of two groups can be described as a [fiber product](wikipedia://en/3706015#Fiber_products) and vice versa.

Notice that if $H$ is *any* subgroup of $G\times G'$ (the projections $p_1: H \to G$ and $p_2: H \to G'$ need not be surjective), then the projections from $H$ onto $p_1(H)$ and $p_2(H)$ *are* surjective. Then one can apply Goursat's [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) to $H \leq p_1(H)\times p_2(H)$.

To motivate the proof, consider the slice $S = \{g\} \times G'$ in $G \times G'$, for any arbitrary $g \in G$. By the surjectivity of the projection map to $G$, this has a non trivial intersection with $H$. Then essentially, this intersection represents exactly one particular coset of $N'$. Indeed, if we have elements $(g,a), (g,b) \in S \cap H$ with $a \in pN' \subset G'$ and $b \in qN' \subset G'$, then $H$ being a group, we get that $(e, ab^{-1}) \in H$, and hence, $(e, ab^{-1}) \in N'$. It follows that $(g,a)$ and $(g,b)$ lie in the same coset of $N'$. Thus the intersection of $H$ with every "horizontal" slice isomorphic to $G' \in G\times G'$ is exactly one particular coset of $N'$ in $G'$. By an identical argument, the intersection of $H$ with every "vertical" slice isomorphic to $G \in G\times G'$ is exactly one particular coset of $N$ in $G$.

All the cosets of $N,N'$ are present in the group $H$, and by the above argument, there is an exact 1:1 correspondence between them. The proof below further shows that the map is an isomorphism.

::: {#o4 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Before proceeding with the [proof](wikipedia://en/82285), $N$ and $N'$ are shown to be normal in $G \times \{e'\}$ and $\{e\} \times G'$, respectively. It is in this sense that $N$ and $N'$ can be identified as normal in *G* and *G**, respectively.***

Since $p_2$ is a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396), its kernel *N* is normal in *H*. Moreover, given $g \in G$, there exists $h=(g,g') \in H$, since $p_1$ is surjective. Therefore, $p_1(N)$ is normal in *G*, viz:

$$gp_1(N) = p_1(h)p_1(N) = p_1(hN) = p_1(Nh) = p_1(N)g.$$

It follows that $N$ is normal in $G \times \{e'\}$ since

$$(g,e')N = (g,e')(p_1(N) \times \{e'\}) = gp_1(N) \times \{e'\} = p_1(N)g \times \{e'\} = (p_1(N) \times \{e'\})(g,e') = N(g,e').$$

The proof that $N'$ is normal in $\{e\} \times G'$ proceeds in a similar manner.

Given the identification of $G$ with $G \times \{e'\}$, we can write $G/N$ and $gN$ instead of $(G \times \{e'\})/N$ and $(g,e')N$, $g \in G$. Similarly, we can write $G'/N'$ and $g'N'$, $g' \in G'$.

On to the proof. Consider the map $H \to G/N \times G'/N'$ defined by $(g,g') \mapsto (gN, g'N')$. The image of $H$ under this map is $\{(gN,g'N') \mid (g,g') \in H \}$. Since $H \to G/N$ is surjective, this [relation](wikipedia://en/22517558) is the graph of a [well-defined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-defined) [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $G/N \to G'/N'$ provided $g_1N = g_2N \implies g_1'N' = g_2'N'$ for every $(g_1,g_1'),(g_2,g_2') \in H$, essentially an application of the [vertical line test](wikipedia://en/6883069).

Since $g_1N=g_2N$ (more properly, $(g_1,e')N = (g_2,e')N$), we have $(g_2^{-1}g_1,e') \in N \subset H$. Thus $(e,g_2'^{-1}g_1') = (g_2,g_2')^{-1}(g_1,g_1')(g_2^{-1}g_1,e')^{-1} \in H$, whence $(e,g_2'^{-1}g_1') \in N'$, that is, $g_1'N'=g_2'N'$.

Furthermore, for every $(g_1,g_1'),(g_2,g_2')\in H$ we have $(g_1g_2,g_1'g_2')\in H$. It follows that this function is a group [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564).

By symmetry, $\{(g'N',gN) \mid (g,g') \in H \}$ is the graph of a well-defined homomorphism $G'/N' \to G/N$. These two homomorphisms are clearly [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) to each other and thus are indeed [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828).

:::

## Goursat varieties {#Goursat_varieties}

As a consequence of Goursat's theorem, one can derive a very general version on the [Jordan–Hölder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan–Hölder_theorem)–[Schreier theorem](wikipedia://en/947167) in Goursat varieties.
