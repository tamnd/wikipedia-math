# Space-filling polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Space-filling polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **space-filling polyhedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) that can be used to fill all of [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853) via [translations](wikipedia://en/204682), [rotations](wikipedia://en/410009) and/or [reflections](wikipedia://en/295829), where *filling* means that; taken together, all the instances of the polyhedron constitute a [partition](wikipedia://en/340240) of three-space. Any [periodic tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_tiling) or [honeycomb](wikipedia://en/6008654) of three-space can, in fact, be generated by translating a [primitive cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_cell) polyhedron.

:::

## Families and their examples {#Families_and_their_examples}

If a polygon can tile the plane, its [prism](wikipedia://en/283007) is space-filling; examples include the [cube](wikipedia://en/6285), [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), and the [hexagonal prism](wikipedia://en/2859668). Any [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975) tessellates [Euclidean 3-space](wikipedia://en/9697), as do the five [parallelohedra](wikipedia://en/25207334) (the cube, hexagonal prism, [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), [elongated dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3515524), and [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631)). Other space-filling polyhedra include the [square pyramid](wikipedia://en/1128455), [plesiohedra](wikipedia://en/53396858), and [stereohedra](wikipedia://en/53413889), polyhedra whose tilings have symmetries taking every tile to every other tile, including the [gyrobifastigium](wikipedia://en/1177963), the [triakis truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/37100405), and the [trapezo-rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/3521213).

The cube is the only [Platonic solid](wikipedia://en/23905) that can fill space, although a tiling that combines tetrahedra and octahedra (the [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260)) is possible. Although the regular tetrahedron cannot fill space, other tetrahedra can, including the [Goursat tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/24104450) derived from the cube, and the [Hill tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/20700599).

## Characteristics {#Characteristics}

Every polyhedron with [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051) of zero that can space-fill. Relatedly, two different polyhedra can have Dehn invariants of zero, which may be able to tile each other.
