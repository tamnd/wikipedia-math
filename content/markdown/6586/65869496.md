# Joubert's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joubert's theorem"}

In [polynomial algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_algebra) and [field theory](wikipedia://en/10603), **Joubert's theorem** states that if $K$ and $L$ are [fields](wikipedia://en/10603), $L$ is a [separable field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separable_field_extension) of $K$ of [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) 6, and the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of $K$ is not equal to 2, then $L$ is generated over $K$ by some element λ in $L$, such that the [minimal polynomial](wikipedia://en/9667106) $p$ of λ has the form $p(t)$ = $t^6 + c_4 t^4 + c_2 t^2 + c_1 t + c_0$, for some constants $c_4, c_2, c_1, c_0$ in $K$.[^o2] The theorem is named in honor of Charles Joubert, a French mathematician, *[lycée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycée)* professor, and Jesuit priest.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

In 1867 Joubert published his theorem in his paper *Sur l'équation du sixième degré* in *tome* 64 of [*Comptes rendus hebdomadaires des séances de l'Académie des sciences*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptes_rendus_de_l'Académie_des_Sciences).[^o8] He seems to have made the assumption that the fields involved in the theorem are subfields of the complex field.[^o2]

Using arithmetic properties of [hypersurfaces](wikipedia://en/525101), Daniel F. Coray gave, in 1987, a proof of Joubert's theorem (with the assumption that the characteristic of $K$ is neither 2 nor 3).[^o2][^o9] In 2006 Hanspeter Kraft gave a proof of Joubert's theorem[^o10] "based on an enhanced version of Joubert’s argument".[^o2] In 2014 [Zinovy Reichstein](wikipedia://en/31869776) proved that the condition characteristic($K$) ≠ 2 is necessary in general to prove the theorem, but the theorem's conclusion can be proved in the characteristic 2 case with some additional assumptions on $K$ and $L$.[^o2]
