# Binary regression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binary regression"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), specifically [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), a **binary regression** estimates a relationship between one or more [explanatory variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explanatory_variable) and a single output [binary variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_variable). Generally the probability of the two alternatives is modeled, instead of simply outputting a single value, as in [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386).

:::

Binary regression is usually analyzed as a special case of [binomial regression](wikipedia://en/4916857), with a single outcome ($n = 1$), and one of the two alternatives considered as "success" and coded as 1: the value is the [count](wikipedia://en/15690807) of successes in 1 trial, either 0 or 1. The most common binary regression models are the [logit model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logit_model) ([logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631)) and the [probit model](wikipedia://en/1611247) ([probit regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probit_regression)).

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Binary regression is principally applied either for prediction ([binary classification](wikipedia://en/205393)), or for estimating the [association](wikipedia://en/157057) between the explanatory variables and the output. In economics, binary regressions are used to model [binary choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_choice).

:::

## Interpretations {#Interpretations}

Binary regression models can be interpreted as [latent variable models](wikipedia://en/4237572), together with a measurement model; or as probabilistic models, directly modeling the probability.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Binary response model with latent variable"}

### Latent variable model {#Latent_variable_model}

The latent variable interpretation has traditionally been used in [bioassay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioassay), yielding the [probit model](wikipedia://en/1611247), where normal variance and a cutoff are assumed. The latent variable interpretation is also used in [item response theory](wikipedia://en/420159) (IRT).

Formally, the latent variable interpretation posits that the outcome *y* is related to a vector of explanatory variables *x* by

$$y=1 [y^*>0]$$

where $y^*=x\beta +\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon \mid x\sim G$, $\beta$ is a vector of [parameters](wikipedia://en/413291) and *G* is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543).

This model can be applied in many economic contexts. For instance, the outcome can be the decision of a manager whether invest to a program, $y^*$ is the expected net [discounted cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discounted_cash_flow) and *x* is a vector of variables which can affect the cash flow of this program. Then the manager will invest only when she expects the net discounted cash flow to be positive.[^o7]

Often, the [error term](wikipedia://en/461509) $\varepsilon$ is assumed to follow a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462) conditional on the explanatory variables *x*. This generates the standard [probit model](wikipedia://en/1611247).[^o8]

:::

### Probabilistic model {#Probabilistic_model}

The simplest direct probabilistic model is the [logit model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logit_model), which models the [log-odds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Log-odds) as a linear function of the explanatory variable or variables. The logit model is "simplest" in the sense of [generalized linear models](wikipedia://en/747122) (GLIM): the log-odds are the natural parameter for the [exponential family](wikipedia://en/339174) of the Bernoulli distribution, and thus it is the simplest to use for computations.

Another direct probabilistic model is the [linear probability model](wikipedia://en/1611124), which models the probability itself as a linear function of the explanatory variables. A drawback of the linear probability model is that, for some values of the explanatory variables, the model will predict probabilities less than zero or greater than one.
