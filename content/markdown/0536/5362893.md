# Perron number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perron number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Perron number** is an [algebraic integer](wikipedia://en/98981) α which is [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) and greater than 1, but such that its [conjugate elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_element) are all less than α in [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991). For example, the larger of the two [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_polynomial) of the [irreducible polynomial](wikipedia://en/188725) $x^2 - 3x + 1$ is a Perron number.

:::

Perron numbers are named after [Oskar Perron](wikipedia://en/1485469); the [Perron–Frobenius theorem](wikipedia://en/1540333) asserts that, for a real [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) with positive algebraic entries whose largest [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) is greater than one, this eigenvalue is a Perron number. (Conversely, it was shown in the late 20th century by Douglas Lind that the Perron numbers are in fact the "closure" of the natural numbers, realized after only one iteration of taking spectral radii of aperiodic matrices, where entries are drawn from this set.) As a closely related case, the Perron number of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) is defined to be the [spectral radius](wikipedia://en/386169) of its [adjacency matrix](wikipedia://en/244463).

Any [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number) or [Salem number](wikipedia://en/1066694) is a Perron number, as is the [Mahler measure](wikipedia://en/1088425) of a [monic](wikipedia://en/314730) [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) polynomial.
