# Leibniz algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leibniz algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **(right) Leibniz algebra**, named after [Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281), sometimes called a **Loday algebra**, after [Jean-Louis Loday](wikipedia://en/36183327), is a [module](wikipedia://en/276410) *L* over a commutative ring *R* with a bilinear product \[ \_ , \_ \] satisfying the **Leibniz identity**

:::

$$[[a,b],c] = [a,[b,c]]+ [[a,c],b]. \,$$

In other words, right multiplication by any element *c* is a [derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29). If in addition the bracket is alternating (\[*a*, *a*\] = 0) then the Leibniz algebra is a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944). Indeed, in this case \[*a*, *b*\] = −\[*b*, *a*\] and the Leibniz identity is equivalent to Jacobi's identity (\[*a*, \[*b*, *c*\]\] + \[*c*, \[*a*, *b*\]\] + \[*b*, \[*c*, *a*\]\] = 0). Conversely any Lie algebra is obviously a Leibniz algebra.

In this sense, Leibniz algebras can be seen as a non-commutative generalization of [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). The investigation of which theorems and properties of Lie algebras are still valid for Leibniz algebras is a recurrent theme in the literature.[^o2] For instance, it has been shown that [Engel's theorem](wikipedia://en/1341657) still holds for Leibniz algebras[^o3][^o4] and that a weaker version of the [Levi–Malcev theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi–Malcev_theorem) also holds.[^o5]

The [tensor](wikipedia://en/2993836) module, $T(V)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^\infty V^{\otimes i}$, of any vector space *V* can be turned into a Loday algebra such that

$$[a_1\otimes \cdots \otimes a_n,x]=a_1\otimes \cdots\otimes a_n\otimes x\quad \text{for }a_1,\ldots, a_n,x\in V.$$

This is the free Loday algebra over *V*.

Leibniz algebras were discovered in 1965 by A. Bloh, who called them D-algebras. They attracted interest after Jean-Louis Loday noticed that the classical [Chevalley–Eilenberg boundary map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley–Eilenberg_boundary_map) in the exterior module of a Lie algebra can be lifted to the tensor module which yields a new [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404). In fact this complex is well-defined for any Leibniz algebra. The homology *HL*(*L*) of this chain complex is known as [Leibniz homology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibniz_homology). If *L* is the Lie algebra of (infinite) matrices over an associative *R*-algebra A then the Leibniz homology of *L* is the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628) over the [Hochschild homology](wikipedia://en/10499629) of *A*.

A *[Zinbiel algebra](wikipedia://en/36166886)* is the [Koszul dual](wikipedia://en/42478011) concept to a Leibniz algebra. It has as defining identity:

$$( a \circ b ) \circ c = a \circ (b \circ c) + a \circ (c \circ b) .$$
