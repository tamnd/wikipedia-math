# Evelyn Prescott Wiggin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Evelyn Prescott Wiggin"}

**Evelyn Prescott Wiggin** (1900–1964) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and university professor. She was one of the few women to earn a PhD in mathematics in the United States before World War II.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Evelyn Prescott Wiggin was born March 1, 1900, in [Stratham, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratham,_New_Hampshire),[^o6] to Margaret Prescott Green and George Herbert Wiggin. Her mother died shortly after giving birth to her second daughter in 1905. She attended the Robinson Seminary public secondary school in [Exeter, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter,_New_Hampshire), and then enrolled in [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College) in 1917 where she was a Durant scholar. She graduated as a mathematics major in 1921 and immediately began teaching mathematics at the [Massena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massena,_New_York) High School in New York.[^o3]

On March 28, 1922, Wiggin received a letter from professor [Roland George Dwight Richardson](wikipedia://en/63563391) at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University), who said, "at the suggestion of my good friends, Misses \[Helen A.\] Merrill and \[Clara E.\] Smith at Wellesley," invited her to apply for a graduate assistant position in the mathematics department at Brown University. He noted that he usually had "four or five students studying for master's degrees each year, half of whom were women." Wiggin took the position and began work toward her M.A. degree in the autumn of 1922. As Richardson's assistant, she also taught classes of girls who were deficient in algebra and geometry. She completed her master's degree in 1924.[^o3][^o6]

For the next three years, Wiggin taught mathematics at [Hood College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hood_College) in [Frederick, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick,_Maryland), and in 1927, she received another note from Richardson encouraging her to continue graduate work saying, "If you care to take some course for credit in absentia here at Brown, I think it could be arranged. I know you will want to keep on with your studies in some sort of way. Possibly you can go to Chicago some summer." She enrolled at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) that same year.[^o3]

## Career {#Career}

In 1929, Wiggin joined the faculty of Randolph-Macon Woman's College (R-MWC) (now [Randolph College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_College)) in [Lynchburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia), as an associate professor.[^o8] On March 31, 1931, her Randolph colleague [Gillie Larew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillie_Larew) wrote to [Gilbert Ames Bliss](wikipedia://en/3011234), who had been Larew's own doctoral advisor at the University of Chicago, saying, "I want to thank you again for sending us Miss Wiggin and express the hope that we may keep her for a long, long time. She is everything she should be both as teacher and as a person."[^o3]

In 1935, Wiggin returned to the University of Chicago for a year and finished her dissertation, titled *A Boundary Value Problem of the Calculus of Variations*, supervised by Gilbert Ames Bliss and William Thomas Reid.[^o4] She was awarded her doctorate in 1936[^o6] and rejoined Randolph-Macon, where, in 1941, she was promoted to full professor.[^o9] She remained there until her retirement, though she did take several leaves to teach elsewhere, including at Wellesley College, [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University) and the University of Chicago.[^o3]

According to [Judy Green](wikipedia://en/55756466), Wiggin belonged to several professional societies.[^o3]

- [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), elected 1923[^o10]
- [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)
- [Sigma Delta Epsilon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_Women_in_Science)
- [American Association of University Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Women)
- [American Association of University Professors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_University_Professors)
- [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa)

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

On June 20, 1956, at age 56, Evelyn Wiggin married Sidney Casner, a retired lawyer, in Chicago, and took the name Evelyn Wiggin Casner. Sidney Casner died around 1962.[^o3] Wiggin died at the University of Virginia Hospital in Charlottesville, Virginia, at the age of 64 on November 5, 1964.[^o6]
