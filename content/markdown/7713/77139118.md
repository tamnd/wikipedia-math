# Eleven-dimensional supergravity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eleven-dimensional supergravity"}

In [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636), **eleven-dimensional supergravity** is the theory of [supergravity](wikipedia://en/432632) in the highest number of [dimensions](wikipedia://en/8398) allowed for a supersymmetric theory. It contains a [graviton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviton), a [gravitino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitino), and a [3-form gauge field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-form_electrodynamics), with their interactions uniquely fixed by supersymmetry. Discovered in 1978 by [Eugène Cremmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugène_Cremmer), [Bernard Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Julia), and [Joël Scherk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joël_Scherk), it quickly became a popular candidate for a [theory of everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_everything) during the 1980s.[^o2] However, interest in it soon faded due to numerous difficulties that arise when trying to construct physically realistic models. It came back to prominence in the mid-1990s when it was found to be the [low energy limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_field_theory) of [M-theory](wikipedia://en/20406), making it crucial for understanding various aspects of [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305).

:::

## History {#History}

Supergravity was discovered in 1976 through the construction of [pure four-dimensional supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_4D_N_=_1_supergravity) with one gravitino. One important direction in the supergravity program was to try to construct [four-dimensional $\mathcal N = 8$ supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_=_8_supergravity) since this was an attractive candidate for a theory of everything, stemming from the fact that it unifies [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle) of all physically admissible [spins](wikipedia://en/19593829) into a single [multiplet](wikipedia://en/12416124). The theory may additionally be UV finite. [Werner Nahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Nahm) showed in 1978 that supersymmetry with spin less than or equal to two is only possible in eleven dimensions or lower.[^o4] Motivated by this, eleven-dimensional supergravity was constructed by Eugène Cremmer, Bernard Julia, and Joël Scherk later the same year,[^o2] with the aim of [dimensionally reducing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensional_reduction) it to four dimensions to acquire the $\mathcal N = 8$ theory, which was done in 1979.[^o5]

During the 1980s, 11D supergravity was of great interest in its own right as a possible fundamental theory of nature. This began in 1980 when [Peter Freund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Freund) and Mark Ruben showed that supergravity [compactifies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactification_%28physics%29) preferentially to four or seven dimensions when using a background where the [field strength tensor](wikipedia://en/1912367) is turned on.[^o6] Additionally, [Edward Witten](wikipedia://en/54894) argued in 1981 that eleven dimensions are also the minimum number of dimensions needed to acquire the [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641) [gauge group](wikipedia://en/39240530), assuming that this arises as subgroup of the [isometry group](wikipedia://en/1101694) of the [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470).[^o7]

The main area of study was understanding how 11D supergravity compactifies down to [four dimensions](wikipedia://en/1364622).[^o8] While there are many ways to do this, depending on the choice of the compact manifold, the most popular one was using the [7-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782). However, a number of problems were quickly identified with these approaches which eventually caused the program to be abandoned.[^o9] One of the main issues was that many of the well-motivated manifolds could not yield the Standard Model gauge group. Another problem at the time was that standard [Kaluza–Klein compactification](wikipedia://en/17070) made it hard to acquire [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_%28physics%29) [fermions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermion) needed to build the Standard Model. Additionally, these compactifications generally yielded very large negative [cosmological constants](wikipedia://en/38992) which could be hard to remove. Lastly, [quantizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29) the theory gave rise to [quantum anomalies](wikipedia://en/364731) which were difficult to eliminate. Some of these problems can be overcome with more modern methods which were unknown at the time.[^o10] For example, chiral fermions can be acquired by using [singular manifolds](wikipedia://en/581763), using noncompact manifolds, utilising the end-of-world 9-brane of the theory, or by exploiting [string dualities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_duality) that relate the 11D theory to chiral string theories. Similarly, the presence of [branes](wikipedia://en/26010282) can also be used to build larger gauge groups.

Due to these issues, 11D supergravity was abandoned in the late 1980s, although it remained an intriguing theory. Indeed, in 1988 [Michael Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Green_%28physicist%29), [John Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Schwarz), and Edward Witten wrote of it that[^o11]

In 1995, Edward Witten discovered M-theory,[^o12] whose low-energy limit is 11D supergravity, bringing the theory back into the forefront of physics and giving it an important place in string theory.

## Theory {#Theory}

In supersymmetry, the maximum number of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [supercharges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercharge) that give [supermultiplets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermultiplet) containing particles of spin less than or equal to two, is 32.[^o14] Supercharges with more components result in supermultiplets that necessarily include [higher spin states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-spin_theory), making such theories unphysical. Since supercharges are [spinors](wikipedia://en/29276), supersymmetry can only be realized in dimensions that admit [spinoral representations](wikipedia://en/10160091) with no more than 32 components, which only occurs in eleven or fewer dimensions.

Eleven-dimensional supergravity is uniquely fixed by supersymmetry, with its structure being relatively simple compared to [supergravity theories in other dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher-dimensional_supergravity). The only free parameter is the [Planck mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_units), setting the scale of the theory. It has a single multiplet consisting of the graviton, a [Majorana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorana_equation) gravitino, and a 3-form gauge field. The necessity of the 3-form field is seen by noting that it provides the missing 84 bosonic [degrees of freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrees_of_freedom_%28physics_and_chemistry%29) needed to complete the multiplet since the graviton has 44 degrees of freedom while the gravitino has 128.

### Superalgebra {#Superalgebra}

The maximally-extended [algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersymmetry_algebra) for supersymmetry in eleven dimensions is given by[^o14]

$$\{Q_\alpha,Q_\beta\} = (C\gamma)^\mu_{\alpha \beta}P_\mu + (C\gamma)^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha \beta}Z_{\mu\nu} + (C\gamma)^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\gamma}_{\alpha \beta}Z_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\gamma},$$

where $C$ is the [charge conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-symmetry) operator which ensures that the combination $C\gamma^{\mu_1\cdots \mu_n}$ is either [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474) or [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/174055). Since the [anticommutator](wikipedia://en/7193) is symmetric, the only admissible entries on the right-hand side are those which are symmetric on their spinor indices, which in eleven dimensions only occurs for one, two, and five [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) indices, with the rest being equivalent up to [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732).[^o16] The corresponding coefficients $Z_{\mu\nu}$ and $Z_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma\gamma}$ are known as quasi-central charges. They aren't regular [central charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_charge) in the [group theoretic](wikipedia://en/41890) sense since they are not [Lorentz scalars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_scalar) and so do not commute with the [Lorentz generators](wikipedia://en/18404), but their interpretation is the same. They indicate that there are extended objects that preserve some amount of supersymmetry, these being the [M2-brane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2-brane) and the [M5-brane](wikipedia://en/53072781).[^o17] Additionally, there is no [R-symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-symmetry) group.[^o16]

### Supergravity action {#Supergravity_action}

The [action](wikipedia://en/312881) for eleven-dimensional supergravity is given by[^o16]

$$S = \frac{1}{2\kappa_{11}^2}\int d^{11} x \ e \bigg[ R(\omega) -\bar \psi_\mu \gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_\nu(\tfrac{1}{2}(\omega+\hat \omega))\psi_\rho - \frac{1}{24}F_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$$

$$-\frac{\sqrt 2}{192} (\bar \psi_\nu \gamma^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta \nu \rho}\psi_\rho+12 \bar \psi^\gamma \gamma^{\alpha \beta}\psi^\delta)(F_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}+\hat F_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta})$$

$$-\frac{2\sqrt 2}{(144)^2}\epsilon^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta \alpha'\beta'\gamma'\delta'\mu\nu\rho}F_{\alpha\beta \gamma \delta}F_{\alpha'\beta'\gamma'\delta'}A_{\mu \nu \rho}\bigg].$$

Here [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) is described using the [vielbein formalism](wikipedia://en/11141222) $e^a_\mu$ with an eleven-dimensional gravitational coupling constant $\kappa_{11}$ and

$$\omega_{\mu a b} = \omega_{\mu a b}(e)+K_{\mu ab},$$

$$\hat \omega_{\mu a b} = \omega_{\mu ab} - \tfrac{1}{8}\bar \psi_\nu \gamma^{\nu \rho}{}_{\mu ab} \psi_\rho,$$

$$K_{\mu ab} = -\tfrac{1}{4}(\bar \psi_\mu \gamma_b \psi_a -\bar \psi_a \gamma_\mu \psi_b + \bar \psi_b \gamma_a \psi_\mu)+\tfrac{1}{8} \bar \psi_\nu \gamma^{\nu \rho}{}_{\mu ab}\psi_\rho,$$

$$\hat F_{\mu \nu \rho \sigma} = F_{\mu\nu\rho \sigma} +\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt 2 \bar \psi_{[\mu}\gamma_{\nu \rho}\psi_{\sigma]}.$$

The [torsion-free connection](wikipedia://en/249176) is given by $\omega_{\mu ab}(e)$, while $K_{\mu ab}$ is the [contorsion tensor](wikipedia://en/1561997). Meanwhile, $D_\nu(\omega)$ is the [covariant derivative](wikipedia://en/431848) with a [spin connection](wikipedia://en/2915190) $\omega$, which acting on spinors takes the form

$$D_\mu(\omega)\psi_\nu = \partial_\mu\psi_\nu+\tfrac{1}{4}\omega_\mu^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\psi_\nu,$$

where $\gamma_{ab} = \gamma_{[a}\gamma_{b]}$. The regular [gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966) satisfying the [Dirac algebra](wikipedia://en/10290343) are denote by $\gamma_a$, while $\gamma_\mu = e_\mu^a\gamma_a$ are position-dependent [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29). The first line in the action contains the covariantized [kinetic terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_term) given by the [Einstein–Hilbert action](wikipedia://en/855776), the [Rarita–Schwinger equation](wikipedia://en/1531781), and the [gauge kinetic action](wikipedia://en/23912155). The second line corresponds to cubic graviton-gauge field terms along with some [quartic](wikipedia://en/1382023) gravitino terms. The last line in the [Lagrangian](wikipedia://en/40752010) is a [Chern–Simons term](wikipedia://en/391908).

The supersymmetry transformation rules are given by[^o14]

$$\delta_s e^a_\mu = \tfrac{1}{2}\bar \epsilon \gamma^a \psi_\mu,$$

$$\delta_s \psi_\mu = D_\mu(\hat \omega)\epsilon + \tfrac{\sqrt 2}{288}(\gamma^{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}{}_\mu - 8 \gamma^{\beta \gamma \delta}\delta^\alpha_\mu) \hat F_{\alpha \beta \gamma \delta}\epsilon,$$

$$\delta_s A_{\mu \nu \rho} = -\tfrac{3\sqrt2}{4}\bar \epsilon \gamma_{[\mu\nu}\psi_{\rho]},$$

where $\epsilon$ is the supersymmetry Majorana gauge parameter. All hatted variables are supercovariant in the sense that they do not depend on the derivative of the supersymmetry parameter $\partial_\mu \epsilon$. The action is additionally invariant under [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29), with the gauge field transforming as a [pseudotensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudotensor) $A\rightarrow -A$. The [equations of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_motion) for this supergravity also have a rigid symmetry known as the trombone symmetry under which $g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow \alpha^2 g_{\mu\nu}$ and $A_{\mu\nu\rho}\rightarrow \alpha^3 A_{\mu\nu\rho}$.[^o19]

### Special solutions {#Special_solutions}

There are a number of special solutions in 11D supergravity, with the most notable ones being the [pp-wave](wikipedia://en/1447921), M2-branes, M5-branes, KK-monopoles, and the M9-brane. Brane solutions are [solitonic](wikipedia://en/51654) objects within supergravity that are the low-energy limit of the corresponding M-theory branes. The 3-form gauge field [couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_%28physics%29) electrically to M2-branes and magnetically to M5-branes.[^o10] Explicit supergravity solitonic solutions for the M2-branes and M5-branes are known.

M2-branes and M5-branes have a regular [non-degenerate](wikipedia://en/232952) [event horizon](wikipedia://en/29320146) whose constant [time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time) [cross-sections](wikipedia://en/1337370) are [topologically](wikipedia://en/29954) 7-spheres and 4-spheres, respectively.[^o17] The [near-horizon limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-horizon_metric) of the [extreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremal_black_hole) M2-brane is given by an $AdS_4 \times S^7$ [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) while for the extreme M5-brane it is given by $AdS_7 \times S^4$. These extreme-limit solutions preserve half of the supersymmetry of the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) solution, meaning that both the extreme M2-branes and the M5-branes can be seen as solitons interpolating between two maximally supersymmetric [Minkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) vacua at infinity, with an $AdS_4\times S^7$ or $AdS_6 \times S^4$ horizon, respectively.

## Compactification {#Compactification}

The [Freund–Rubin compactification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freund–Rubin_compactification) of 11D supergravity shows that it preferentially compactifies to seven and four dimensions, the latter of which led to it being extensively studied throughout the 1980s.[^o6] This compactification is most easily achieved by demanding that the compact and noncompact manifolds have a [Ricci tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci_tensor) that is proportional to the [metric](wikipedia://en/195795), meaning that they are [Einstein manifolds](wikipedia://en/644814). One additionally demands that the solution is [stable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_vacuum) against fluctuations, which in [anti-de Sitter spacetimes](wikipedia://en/301670) requires that the Bretenlohner–Freedman bound is satisfied. Stability is guaranteed if there is some unbroken supersymmetry, although there also exist classically stable solutions that fully break supersymmetry.

One of the main compactification manifolds studied was the 7-sphere.[^o8] The manifold has 8 [Killing spinors](wikipedia://en/871687), meaning that the resulting four dimensional theory has $\mathcal N = 8$ supersymmetry. Additionally, it also results in an [$\text{SO}(8)$ gauge group](wikipedia://en/683116), corresponding to the isometry group of the sphere. A similar widely studied compactification was using a squashed 7-sphere, which can be acquired by [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) the 7-sphere in a [quaternionic projective space](wikipedia://en/2808931), with this giving a gauge group of $\text{SO}(5)\times \text{SU}(2)$.

A key property of 7-sphere Kaluza-Klein compactifications is that their truncation is consistent, which is not necessarily the case for other Einstein manifolds besides the [7-torus](wikipedia://en/74800). An inconsistent truncation means that the resulting four dimensional theory is not consistent with the [higher dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_dimensions) field equations. Physically this needs not be a problem in compactifications to Minkowski spacetimes as the inconsistent truncation merely results in additional [irrelevant operators](wikipedia://en/291462) in the action. However, most Einstein manifold compactifications are to anti-de Sitter spacetimes which have a relatively large cosmological constant. In this case irrelevant operators can be converted to relevant ones through the equation of motion.

## Related theories {#Related_theories}

While eleven-dimensional supergravity is the unique supergravity in eleven dimensions at the level of an action, a related theory can be acquired at the level of the equations of motion, known as modified 11D supergravity. This is done by replacing the spin connection by one that is [conformally](wikipedia://en/50627) related to the original.[^o19] Such a theory is inequivalent to standard 11D supergravity only in spaces that are not [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879). An action for a [massive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass) 11D theory can also be acquired by introducing an [auxiliary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_field) nondynamical [Killing vector field](wikipedia://en/1025455), with this theory reducing to [massive type IIA supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIA_supergravity#Massive_type_IIA_supergravity) upon dimensional reduction.[^o23] This is not a proper eleven-dimensional theory since the fields explicitly do not depend on one of the coordinates, but it is nonetheless useful for studying massive branes.

Dimensionally reducing 11D supergravity to ten dimensions gives rise to [type IIA supergravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_IIA_supergravity), while dimensionally reducing it to four dimensions can give $\mathcal N = 8$ supergravity, which was one of the original motivations for constructing the theory.[^o24] While eleven-dimensional supergravity is not UV finite, it is the low energy limit of M-theory. The supergravity also receives corrections at the [quantum level](wikipedia://en/25202), where these corrections sometimes playing an important role in various compactification mechanisms.[^o10]

Unlike for supergravity in other dimensions, an extension to eleven dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime does not exist.[^o25] While the theory is the supersymmetric theory in the highest number of dimensions, the caveat is that this only holds for spacetime signatures with one temporal dimension. If arbitrary spacetime signatures are allowed, then there also exists a supergravity in twelve dimensions with [two temporal dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_time_dimensions).
