# Howard Levi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Howard Levi"}

**Howard Levi** (November 9, 1916 – September 11, 2002) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked mainly in [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [mathematical education](wikipedia://en/326471).[^o3] Levi was very active during the educational reforms in the United States, having proposed several new courses to replace the traditional ones.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Levi was born in New York City in 1916.[^o3] He earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1942 as a student of [Joseph Fels Ritt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fels_Ritt). Soon after obtaining his degree, he became a researcher on the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project).[^o6][^o7]

At [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) he led a group that developed a course of geometry for high school students that treated [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) as a special case of [affine geometry](wikipedia://en/243890).[^o8][^o9] Much of the Wesleyan material was based on his book *Foundations of Geometry and Trigonometry*.[^o10]

His book *Polynomials, Power Series, and Calculus*, written to be a textbook for a first course in [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176),[^o11] presented an innovative approach, and received favorable reviews by [Leonard Gillman](wikipedia://en/15123686), who wrote "\[...\] this book, with its wealth of imaginative ideas, deserves to be better known."[^o12][^o13]

[Levi's reduction process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi's_reduction_process) is named after him.[^o14]

In his last years, he tried to find a proof of the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949) that did not rely on computers.[^o6]

He died in New York City in 2002.[^o3]
