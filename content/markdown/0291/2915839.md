# Dissection problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dissection problem"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **dissection problem** is the problem of partitioning a geometric [figure](wikipedia://en/169191) (such as a [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) or [ball](wikipedia://en/160556)) into smaller pieces that may be rearranged into a new figure of equal content. In this context, the partitioning is called simply a **dissection** (of one polytope into another). It is usually required that the dissection use only a finite number of pieces. Additionally, to avoid [set-theoretic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set-theoretic) issues related to the [Banach–Tarski paradox](wikipedia://en/19759220) and [Tarski's circle-squaring problem](wikipedia://en/216811), the pieces are typically required to be [well-behaved](wikipedia://en/372742). For instance, they may be restricted to being the [closures](wikipedia://en/42309) of disjoint [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358).

:::

## Polygon dissection problem {#Polygon_dissection_problem}

*Image: visual\_proof\_determinant\_parallelogram\_area.svg.* [Proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) that the area of the parallelogram is the absolute value of the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the 2×2 matrix formed by the vectors representing the parallelogram's sides, by dissection and rearrangement

The [Bolyai–Gerwien theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolyai–Gerwien_theorem) states that any [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) may be dissected into any other polygon of the same area, using interior-disjoint polygonal pieces. It is not true, however, that any [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) has a dissection into any other polyhedron of the same volume using polyhedral pieces (see [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051)). This process *is* possible, however, for any two [honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654) (such as [cube](wikipedia://en/6285)) in three dimension and any two [zonohedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zonohedra) of equal volume (in any dimension).

A partition into [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) of equal area is called an [equidissection](wikipedia://en/36643448). Most polygons cannot be equidissected, and those that can often have restrictions on the possible numbers of triangles. For example, [Monsky's theorem](wikipedia://en/35098575) states that there is no odd equidissection of a [square](wikipedia://en/659939).[^o3]

## Equilateral-triangle squaring problem {#Equilateral-triangle_squaring_problem}

*Image: Hinged haberdasher square.svg.* Four-piece hinged dissection of an equilateral triangle into a square

Among [dissection puzzles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_puzzle), an example is the Haberdasher's Puzzle, posed by puzzle writer [Henry Dudeney](wikipedia://en/465317) in 1902.[^o5] It seeks a dissection from [equilateral triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) into a [square](wikipedia://en/659939). Dudeney provided a [hinged dissection](wikipedia://en/41414139) with four pieces. In 2024, [Erik Demaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Demaine), Tonan Kamata, and Ryuhei Uehara published a preprint claiming to prove that no dissection with fewer pieces exists.[^o6][^o7] This work was selected as one of [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)'s "The 10 Biggest Math Breakthroughs of 2025."[^o8]。
