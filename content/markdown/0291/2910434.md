# Andrew Granville

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Andrew Granville"}

**Andrew James Granville** (born 7 September 1962) is a British mathematician, working in the field of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Granville received his Bachelor of Arts (Honours) (1983) and his [Certificate of Advanced Studies (Distinction)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_III_of_the_Mathematical_Tripos) (1984) from [Trinity College, Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). He received his PhD from [Queen's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_at_Kingston) in 1987 and was inducted into the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) in 2006.

## Career {#Career}

He has been a faculty member at the [Université de Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_de_Montréal) since 2002. Before moving to Montreal he was a mathematics professor at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia) (UGA) from 1991 until 2002. During his postdoctoral years, he worked at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). He was a section speaker in the 1994 [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) together with [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159) from UGA.[^o4]

### Research {#Research}

Granville's work is mainly in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), in particular [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513). Along with [Carl Pomerance](wikipedia://en/2134159) and [W. R. (Red) Alford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._R._%28Red%29_Alford) he proved the infinitude of [Carmichael numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmichael_numbers) in 1994.[^o7] This proof was based on a conjecture given by [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).

### Awards {#Awards}

Granville was the first recipient of the [Ribenboim Prize](wikipedia://en/57933020) of the Canadian Number Theory Association, in 1999. Granville won a [Lester R. Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_R._Ford_Award) in 2007[^o9] and again in 2009.[^o10] In 2008, he won the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) for expository writing from the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for his paper *"It is easy to determine whether a given integer is prime"*.[^o11][^o12] In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o13]

### Other {#Other}

Andrew Granville, in collaboration with his sister Jennifer Granville, a film writer,[^o15] wrote *[Prime Suspects: The Anatomy of Integers and Permutations](wikipedia://en/63035870)*, a graphic novel that is a "mathematical detective story"[^o15] and investigates key concepts in mathematics.[^o16]
