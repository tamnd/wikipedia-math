# Tautological bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tautological bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **tautological bundle** is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) occurring over a [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) in a natural tautological way: for a Grassmannian of $k$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/38267) [subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357) of $V$, given a point in the Grassmannian corresponding to a $k$-dimensional vector subspace $W \subseteq V$, the fiber over $W$ is the subspace $W$ itself. In the case of [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) the tautological bundle is known as the **tautological line bundle.**

:::

The tautological bundle is also called the **[universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570)** since any vector bundle (over a compact space[^o2]) is a pullback of the tautological bundle; this is to say a Grassmannian is a [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) for vector bundles. Because of this, the tautological bundle is important in the study of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530).

Tautological bundles are constructed both in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and in algebraic geometry. In algebraic geometry, the tautological line bundle (as [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859)) is

$$\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(-1),$$

the [dual](wikipedia://en/7824691) of the **hyperplane bundle** or [Serre's twisting sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_twisting_sheaf) $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1)$. The hyperplane bundle is the line bundle corresponding to the hyperplane ([divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055)) $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ in $\mathbb{P}^n$. The tautological line bundle and the hyperplane bundle are exactly the two generators of the [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of the projective space.[^o3]

In [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)'s "K-theory", the tautological line bundle over a [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977) is called the **standard line bundle**. The sphere bundle of the standard bundle is usually called the [Hopf bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopf_bundle). (cf. [Bott generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bott_generator).)

More generally, there are also tautological bundles on a [projective bundle](wikipedia://en/35183882) of a vector bundle, as well as a [Grassmann bundle](wikipedia://en/50406093).

The older term *canonical bundle* has dropped out of favour, on the grounds that *[canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_%28disambiguation%29)* is already a heavily overloaded term, in both mathematical terminology and confusion with the [canonical class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_class) in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) could scarcely be avoided.

## Intuitive definition {#Intuitive_definition}

Grassmannians by definition are the parameter spaces for [linear subspaces](wikipedia://en/56357), of a given dimension, in a given [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $W$. If $G$ is a Grassmannian, and $V_g$ is the subspace of $W$ corresponding to $g$ in $G$, this is already almost the data required for a vector bundle: namely a vector space for each point $g$, varying continuously. All that can stop the definition of the tautological bundle from this indication, is the difficulty that the $V_g$ are going to intersect. Fixing this up is a routine application of the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) device, so that the bundle projection is from a [total space](wikipedia://en/276526) made up of identical copies of the $V_g$, that now do not intersect. With this, we have the bundle.

The projective space case is included. By convention $P(V)$ may usefully carry the tautological bundle in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) sense. That is, with $V^*$ the dual space, points of $P(V)$ carry the vector subspaces of $V^*$ that are their kernels, when considered as (rays of) [linear functionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) on $V^*$. If $V$ has dimension $n+1$, the tautological [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) is one tautological bundle, and the other, just described, is of rank $n$.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ be the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *n*-dimensional vector subspaces in $\mathbb{R}^{n+k};$ as a set it is the set of all *n*-dimensional vector subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^{n+k}.$ For example, if *n* = 1, it is the real projective *k*-space.

We define the tautological bundle γ<sub>*n*, *k*</sub> over $G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ as follows. The total space of the bundle is the set of all pairs (*V*, *v*) consisting of a point *V* of the Grassmannian and a vector *v* in *V*; it is given the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) of the Cartesian product $G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k}) \times \mathbb{R}^{n+k}.$ The projection map π is given by π(*V*, *v*) = *V*. If *F* is the pre-image of *V* under π, it is given a structure of a vector space by *a*(*V*, *v*) + *b*(*V*, *w*) = (*V*, *av* + *bw*). Finally, to see local triviality, given a point *X* in the Grassmannian, let *U* be the set of all *V* such that the orthogonal projection *p* onto *X* maps *V* isomorphically onto *X*,[^o7] and then define

$$\begin{cases} \phi: \pi^{-1}(U) \to U\times X\subseteq G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k}) \times X \\ \phi(V,v) = (V, p(v)) \end{cases}$$

which is clearly a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660). Hence, the result is a vector bundle of rank *n*.

The above definition continues to make sense if we replace $\mathbb{R}$ with the [complex field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_field) $\mathbb{C}.$

By definition, the infinite Grassmannian $G_n$ is the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of $G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k})$ as $k\to\infty.$ Taking the direct limit of the bundles γ<sub>*n*, *k*</sub> gives the tautological bundle γ<sub>*n*</sub> of $G_n.$ It is a universal bundle in the sense: for each compact space *X*, there is a natural bijection

$$\begin{cases} [X, G_n] \to \operatorname{Vect}^{\mathbb{R}}_n(X) \\ f \mapsto f^*(\gamma_n) \end{cases}$$

where on the left the bracket means homotopy class and on the right is the set of isomorphism classes of real vector bundles of rank *n*. The inverse map is given as follows: since *X* is compact, any vector bundle *E* is a [subbundle](wikipedia://en/2517151) of a trivial bundle: $E \hookrightarrow X \times \mathbb{R}^{n+k}$ for some *k* and so *E* determines a map

$$\begin{cases}f_E: X \to G_n \\ x \mapsto E_x \end{cases}$$

unique up to homotopy.

::: {#o8 .remark cue=inline-head name="Remark"}

**Remark**: In turn, one can define a tautological bundle as a universal bundle; suppose there is a natural bijection

$$[X, G_n] = \operatorname{Vect}^{\mathbb{R}}_n(X)$$

for any [paracompact space](wikipedia://en/48631) *X*. Since $G_n$ is the direct limit of compact spaces, it is paracompact and so there is a unique vector bundle over $G_n$ that corresponds to the identity map on $G_n.$ It is precisely the tautological bundle and, by restriction, one gets the tautological bundles over all $G_n(\mathbb{R}^{n+k}).$

:::

## Hyperplane bundle {#Hyperplane_bundle}

The **hyperplane bundle** *H* on a real projective *k*-space is defined as follows. The total space of *H* is the set of all pairs (*L*, *f*) consisting of a line *L* through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^{k+1}$ and *f* a linear functional on *L*. The projection map π is given by π(*L*, *f*) = *L* (so that the fiber over *L* is the dual vector space of *L*.) The rest is exactly like the tautological line bundle.

In other words, *H* is the [dual bundle](wikipedia://en/7824691) of the tautological line bundle.

In algebraic geometry, the hyperplane bundle is the line bundle (as [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859)) corresponding to the **hyperplane divisor**

$$H = \mathbb{P}^{n-1} \subset \mathbb{P}^{n}$$

given as, say, *x*<sub>0</sub> = 0, when *x<sub>i</sub>* are the [homogeneous coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_coordinate). This can be seen as follows. If *D* is a [(Weil) divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_divisor) on $X=\mathbb{P}^n,$ one defines the corresponding line bundle *O*(*D*) on *X* by

$$\Gamma(U, O(D)) = \{ f \in K | (f) + D \ge 0 \text{ on } U \}$$

where *K* is the field of rational functions on *X*. Taking *D* to be *H*, we have:

$$\begin{cases}O(H) \simeq O(1)\\ f \mapsto f x_0\end{cases}$$

where *x*<sub>0</sub> is, as usual, viewed as a global section of the twisting sheaf *O*(1). (In fact, the above isomorphism is part of the usual correspondence between Weil divisors and Cartier divisors.) Finally, the dual of the twisting sheaf corresponds to the tautological line bundle (see below).

:::

## Tautological line bundle in algebraic geometry {#Tautological_line_bundle_in_algebraic_geometry}

In algebraic geometry, this notion exists over any field *k*. The concrete definition is as follows. Let $A = k[y_0, \dots, y_n]$ and $\mathbb{P}^n = \operatorname{Proj}A$. Note that we have:

$$\mathbf{Spec} \left (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}[x_0, \ldots, x_n] \right ) = \mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{P}^n} = \mathbb{A}^{n+1} \times_k {\mathbb{P}^n}$$

where **Spec** is [relative Spec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_Spec). Now, put:

$$L = \mathbf{Spec} \left (\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}[x_0, \dots, x_n]/I \right )$$

where *I* is the [ideal sheaf](wikipedia://en/9508138) generated by global sections $x_iy_j-x_jy_i$. Then *L* is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{P}^n}$ over the same base scheme $\mathbb{P}^n$; moreover, the closed points of *L* are exactly those (*x*, *y*) of $\mathbb{A}^{n+1} \times_k \mathbb{P}^n$ such that either *x* is zero or the image of *x* in $\mathbb{P}^n$ is *y*. Thus, *L* is the tautological line bundle as defined before if *k* is the field of real or complex numbers.

In more concise terms, *L* is the [blow-up](wikipedia://en/1817790) of the origin of the affine space $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$, where the locus *x* = 0 in *L* is the [exceptional divisor](wikipedia://en/14271694). (cf. Hartshorne, Ch. I, the end of § 4.)

In general, $\mathbf{Spec}(\operatorname{Sym} \check{E})$ is the [algebraic vector bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_vector_bundle) corresponding to a locally free sheaf *E* of finite rank.[^o12] Since we have the [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215):

$$0 \to I \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}[x_0, \ldots, x_n] \overset{x_i \mapsto y_i}{\longrightarrow} \operatorname{Sym} \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(1) \to 0,$$

the tautological line bundle *L*, as defined above, corresponds to the dual $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n}(-1)$ of [Serre's twisting sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre's_twisting_sheaf). In practice both the notions (tautological line bundle and the dual of the twisting sheaf) are used interchangeably.

Over a field, its dual line bundle is the line bundle associated to the [hyperplane divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplane_divisor) *H*, whose global sections are the [linear forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_forms). Its [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) is −*H*. This is an example of an anti-[ample line bundle](wikipedia://en/1186896). Over $\mathbb{C},$ this is equivalent to saying that it is a negative line bundle, meaning that minus its Chern class is the [de Rham class](wikipedia://en/221530) of the standard [Kähler form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_form).

## Facts {#Facts}

- The tautological line bundle γ<sub>1, *k*</sub> is [locally trivial](wikipedia://en/276526) but not [trivial](wikipedia://en/276526#Examples), for *k* ≥ 1. This remains true over other fields.

In fact, it is straightforward to show that, for *k* = 1, the real tautological line bundle is none other than the well-known bundle whose [total space](wikipedia://en/276526) is the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817). For a full proof of the above fact, see.[^o15]

- The [Picard group](wikipedia://en/1850134) of line bundles on $\mathbb{P}(V)$ is [infinite cyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_cyclic), and the [tautological line bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tautological_line_bundle) is a generator.
- In the case of projective space, where the tautological bundle is a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366), the associated [invertible sheaf](wikipedia://en/581859) of sections is $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, the tensor inverse (*ie* the dual vector bundle) of the hyperplane bundle or [Serre twist sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proj#The_twisting_sheaf) $\mathcal{O}(1)$; in other words the hyperplane bundle is the generator of the Picard group having positive degree (as a [divisor](wikipedia://en/1693055)) and the tautological bundle is its opposite: the generator of negative degree.

:::
