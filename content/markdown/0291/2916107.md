# Olivier Ramaré

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Olivier Ramaré"}

*Image: OR2017-Triglav.png.*

**Olivier Ramaré** is a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who works as Senior researcher for the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS). He is currently attached to [Aix-Marseille Université](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aix-Marseille_Université).

:::

Ramaré earned a doctorate in 1991 from the [University of Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bordeaux) with a dissertation *Contribution au problème de Goldbach : tout entier >1 est somme d'au plus treize nombres premiers* (*Contribution to Goldbach's problem: any integer >1 is the sum of at most thirteen prime numbers*) supervised by [Jean-Marc Deshouillers](wikipedia://en/40678456).

In 1995, he sharpened earlier work on [Schnirelmann's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnirelmann's_theorem) by [proving](wikipedia://en/82285) that every even number is a sum of at most six [primes](wikipedia://en/23666). This result may be compared with [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758), which states that every even number except 2 is the sum of two primes. The truth of Ramaré's result for all sufficiently large even numbers is a consequence of [Vinogradov's theorem](wikipedia://en/4633030), whereas the full result follows from [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164). In turn, Ramaré's result was strengthened by [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) who proved in 2014 that every odd number is the sum of at most five primes. [Harald Helfgott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Helfgott)'s claimed a proof of [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164).[^o3]
