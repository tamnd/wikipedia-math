# Topological K-theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Topological K-theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **topological $K$-theory** is a branch of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). It was founded to study [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) on [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450), by means of ideas now recognised as (general) [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748) that were introduced by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042). The early work on topological $K$-theory is due to [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Let $X$ be a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637) and $k= \mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. Then $K_k(X)$ is defined to be the [Grothendieck group](wikipedia://en/1634790) of the [commutative monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_monoid) of [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of finite-dimensional $k$-vector bundles over $X$ under [Whitney sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum). [Tensor product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum#Operations_on_vector_bundles) of bundles gives $K$-theory a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) structure. Without subscripts, $K(X)$ usually denotes complex $K$-theory whereas real $K$-theory is sometimes written as $KO(X)$. The remaining discussion is focused on complex $K$-theory.

As a first example, note that the $K$-theory of a point is the integers. This is because vector bundles over a point are trivial and thus classified by their rank and the Grothendieck group of the natural numbers is the integers.

There is also a reduced version of $K$-theory, $\widetilde{K}(X)$, defined for $X$ a compact [pointed space](wikipedia://en/1236666) (cf. [reduced homology](wikipedia://en/3110036)). This reduced theory is intuitively $K(X)$ modulo [trivial bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_bundle). It is defined as the group of stable equivalence classes of bundles. Two bundles $E$ and $F$ are said to be **stably isomorphic** if there are trivial bundles $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$, so that $E \oplus \varepsilon_1 \cong F\oplus \varepsilon_2$. This equivalence relation results in a group since every vector bundle can be completed to a trivial bundle by summing with its orthogonal complement. Alternatively, $\widetilde{K}(X)$ can be defined as the [kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kernel_of_a_homomorphism) of the map $K(X)\to K(x_0) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ induced by the inclusion of the base point $x_{0}$ into $X$.

$K$-theory forms a multiplicative (generalized) [cohomology theory](wikipedia://en/194060#Cohomology_theories) as follows. The [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) of a pair of pointed spaces $(X, A)$

$$\widetilde{K}(X/A) \to \widetilde{K}(X) \to \widetilde{K}(A)$$

extends to a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence)

$$\cdots \to \widetilde{K}(SX) \to \widetilde{K}(SA) \to \widetilde{K}(X/A) \to \widetilde{K}(X) \to \widetilde{K}(A).$$

Let $S^{n}$ be the $n$-th [reduced suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702) of a space and then define

$$\widetilde{K}^{-n}(X):=\widetilde{K}(S^nX), \qquad n\geq 0.$$

Negative indices are chosen so that the [coboundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coboundary) maps increase dimension.

It is often useful to have an unreduced version of these groups, simply by defining:

$$K^{-n}(X)=\widetilde{K}^{-n}(X_+).$$

Here $X_+$ is $X$ with a disjoint basepoint labeled '+' adjoined.[^o4]

Finally, the [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099) as formulated below extends the theories to positive integers.

## Properties {#Properties}

- $K^n$ (respectively, $\widetilde{K}^n$) is a [contravariant functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contravariant_functor) from the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756) of (pointed) spaces to the category of commutative rings. Thus, for instance, the $K$-theory over [contractible spaces](wikipedia://en/1752414) is always $\mathbb{Z}.$
- The [spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29) of $K$-theory is $BU\times\mathbb{Z}$ (with the discrete topology on $\mathbb{Z}$), i.e. $K(X) \cong [ X_+, \mathbb{Z} \times BU ],$ where $[ , ]$ denotes pointed homotopy classes and $BU$ is the [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) of the classifying spaces of the [unitary groups](wikipedia://en/173993): $BU(n) \cong \operatorname{Gr} (n, \mathbb{C}^{\infty} ).$ Similarly, $$\widetilde{K}(X) \cong [X, \mathbb{Z} \times BU].$$ For real $K$-theory use $BO$.
- There is a [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_homomorphism) [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $K^0(X) \to H^{2*}(X, \mathbb{Q}),$ the [Chern character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_character), such that $K^0(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \to H^{2*}(X, \mathbb{Q})$ is an isomorphism.
- The equivalent of the [Steenrod operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steenrod_operation) in $K$-theory are the [Adams operations](wikipedia://en/1226666). They can be used to define characteristic classes in topological $K$-theory.
- The [Splitting principle](wikipedia://en/15958803) of topological $K$-theory allows one to reduce statements about arbitrary vector bundles to statements about sums of line bundles.
- The [Thom isomorphism theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom_isomorphism_theorem) in topological $K$-theory is $$K(X)\cong\widetilde{K}(T(E)),$$ where $T(E)$ is the [Thom space](wikipedia://en/1243451) of the vector bundle $E$ over $X$. This holds whenever $E$ is a spin-bundle.
- The [Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Hirzebruch_spectral_sequence) allows computation of $K$-groups from ordinary cohomology groups.
- Topological $K$-theory can be generalized vastly to a functor on [C\*-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C*-algebras), see [operator K-theory](wikipedia://en/13314265) and [KK-theory](wikipedia://en/13314087).

:::

## Bott periodicity {#Bott_periodicity}

The phenomenon of [periodicity](wikipedia://en/10779) named after [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) (see [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099)) can be formulated this way:

- $K(X \times \mathbb{S}^2) = K(X) \otimes K(\mathbb{S}^2),$ and $K(\mathbb{S}^2) = \mathbb{Z}[H]/(H-1)^2$ where *H* is the class of the [tautological bundle](wikipedia://en/2913502) on $\mathbb{S}^2 = \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}),$ i.e. the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799).
- $$\widetilde{K}^{n+2}(X)=\widetilde{K}^n(X).$$
- $$\Omega^2 BU \cong BU \times \mathbb{Z}.$$

In real $K$-theory there is a similar periodicity, but modulo 8.

## Applications {#Applications}

Topological $K$-theory has been applied in [John Frank Adams’](wikipedia://en/1223959) proof of the “[Hopf invariant](wikipedia://en/4417198) one” problem via [Adams operations](wikipedia://en/1226666).[^o10] Adams also proved an upper bound for the number of linearly-independent [vector fields on spheres](wikipedia://en/3100908).[^o11]

:::

## Chern character {#Chern_character}

[Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) proved a theorem relating the topological K-theory of a finite CW complex $X$ with its rational cohomology. In particular, they showed that there exists a homomorphism

$$ch : K^*_{\text{top}}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q} \to H^*(X;\mathbb{Q})$$

such that

$$\begin{aligned} K^0_{\text{top}}(X)\otimes \mathbb{Q} & \cong \bigoplus_k H^{2k}(X;\mathbb{Q}) \\ K^1_{\text{top}}(X)\otimes \mathbb{Q} & \cong \bigoplus_k H^{2k+1}(X;\mathbb{Q}) \end{aligned}$$

There is an algebraic analogue relating the Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves and the Chow ring of a smooth projective variety $X$.
