# Roy's identity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roy's identity"}

**Roy's identity** (named after [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) [René Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/René_Roy_%28economist%29)) is a major result in [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics) having applications in [consumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer) choice and the [theory of the firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_the_firm). The [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) relates the ordinary (Marshallian) [demand function](wikipedia://en/1018783) to the derivatives of the [indirect utility function](wikipedia://en/1998025). Specifically, denoting the indirect utility function as $v(p,w),$ the Marshallian demand function for good $i$ can be calculated as

:::

$$x_{i}^{m}(p,w)=-\frac{\frac{\partial v}{\partial p_{i}}}{\frac{\partial v}{\partial w}}$$

where $p$ is the price vector of goods and $w$ is [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income),[^o2] and where the superscript ${}^m$ indicates Marshallian demand. The result holds for continuous [utility functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_function) representing [locally non-satiated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_non-satiation) and [strictly convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strict_convexity) preference relations on a convex [consumption set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumption_set), under the additional requirement that the indirect utility function is differentiable in all arguments.

Roy's identity is akin to the result that the price derivatives of the [expenditure function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expenditure_function) give the [Hicksian demand functions](wikipedia://en/1998026). The additional step of dividing by the wealth derivative of the indirect utility function in Roy's identity is necessary since the indirect utility function, unlike the expenditure function, has an ordinal interpretation: any strictly increasing transformation of the original utility function represents the same preferences.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Derivation of Roy's identity"}

## Derivation of Roy's identity {#Derivation_of_Roy's_identity}

Roy's identity reformulates [Shephard's lemma](wikipedia://en/2190008) in order to get a [Marshallian demand function](wikipedia://en/1018783) for an individual and a good ($i$) from some indirect utility function.

The first step is to consider the trivial identity obtained by substituting the [expenditure function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expenditure_function) for [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) or [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income) $w$ in the [indirect utility function](wikipedia://en/1998025) $v (p, w)$, at a utility of $u$:

$$v ( p, e(p, u)) = u$$

This says that the indirect utility function evaluated in such a way that minimizes the cost for achieving a certain utility given a set of prices (a vector $p$) is equal to that utility when evaluated at those prices.

Taking the derivative of both sides of this equation with respect to the price of a single good $p_i$ (with the utility level held constant) gives:

$$\frac{ \partial v [p, e(p,u)]}{\partial w} \frac{\partial e(p,u)}{\partial p_i} + \frac{\partial v [p, e(p,u)]}{\partial p_i} = 0.$$

Rearranging gives the desired result:

$$-\frac{\frac{\partial v [p, e(p,u)]}{\partial p_i}}{\frac{\partial v [p, e(p,u)]}{\partial w}}=\frac{\partial e(p,u)}{\partial p_i}=h_i(p, u)=x_i(p, e(p,u))$$

with the second-to-last equality following from [Shephard's lemma](wikipedia://en/2190008) and the last equality from a basic property of [Hicksian demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hicksian_demand).

:::

## Alternative proof using the envelope theorem {#Alternative_proof_using_the_envelope_theorem}

For expositional ease, consider the two-goods case. The indirect utility function $v(p_{1},p_{2},w)$ is the value function of the constrained optimization problem characterized by the following Lagrangian:[^o7]

$$\mathcal{L}=u(x_{1},x_{2})+\lambda(w-p_{1}x_{1}-p_{2}x_{2})$$

By the [envelope theorem](wikipedia://en/3394695), the derivatives of the value function $v(p_{1},p_{2},w)$ with respect to the parameters are:

$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial p_{1}}=-\lambda x_{1}^{m}$$

$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial w}=\lambda$$

where $x_{1}^{m}$ is the maximizer (i.e. the Marshallian demand function for good 1). Hence:

$$-\frac{\frac{\partial v}{\partial p_{1}}}{\frac{\partial v}{\partial w}}=-\frac{-\lambda x_{1}^{m}}{\lambda}=x_{1}^{m}$$

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Application"}

## Application {#Application}

This gives a method of deriving the [Marshallian demand function](wikipedia://en/1018783) of a good for some consumer from the indirect utility function of that consumer. It is also fundamental in deriving the [Slutsky equation](wikipedia://en/1838280).

:::
