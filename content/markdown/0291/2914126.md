# Milnor conjecture (K-theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Milnor conjecture"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Milnor conjecture** was a proposal by (txt) of a description of the [Milnor K-theory](wikipedia://en/2914128) (mod 2) of a general [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *F* with [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) different from 2, by means of the [Galois](wikipedia://en/1111315) (or equivalently [étale](wikipedia://en/396231)) cohomology of *F* with coefficients in **Z**/2**Z**. It was proved by (txt).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *F* be a field of characteristic different from 2. Then there is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828)

$$K_n^M(F)/2 \cong H_{\acute{e}t}^n(F, \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$$

for all *n* ≥ 0, where *K<sup>M</sup>* denotes the [Milnor ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milnor_ring).

:::

## About the proof {#About_the_proof}

The proof of this theorem by [Vladimir Voevodsky](wikipedia://en/104482) uses several ideas developed by Voevodsky, [Alexander Merkurjev](wikipedia://en/31902178), [Andrei Suslin](wikipedia://en/7111820), [Markus Rost](wikipedia://en/6842771), [Fabien Morel](wikipedia://en/28977644), [Eric Friedlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Friedlander), and others, including the newly minted theory of [motivic cohomology](wikipedia://en/809314) (a kind of substitute for [singular cohomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_cohomology) for [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties)) and the [motivic Steenrod algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivic_Steenrod_algebra).

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The analogue of this result for [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) other than 2 was known as the [Bloch–Kato conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloch–Kato_conjecture). Work of Voevodsky and [Markus Rost](wikipedia://en/6842771) yielded a complete proof of this conjecture in 2009; the result is now called the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](wikipedia://en/27126857).

:::
