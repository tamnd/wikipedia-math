# Routh–Hurwitz matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Routh–Hurwitz matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Routh–Hurwitz matrix**,[^o2] or more commonly just **Hurwitz matrix**, corresponding to a polynomial is a particular matrix whose nonzero entries are coefficients of the polynomial.

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Hurwitz matrix and the Hurwitz stability criterion"}

## Hurwitz matrix and the Hurwitz stability criterion {#Hurwitz_matrix_and_the_Hurwitz_stability_criterion}

Namely, given a real polynomial

$$p(z)=a_{0}z^n+a_{1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}z+a_n$$

the $n\times n$ [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022)

$$H= \begin{pmatrix} a_1 & a_3 & a_5 & \dots & \dots & \dots & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ a_0 & a_2 & a_4 & & & & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & a_1 & a_3 & & & & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ \vdots & a_0 & a_2 & \ddots & & & 0 & \vdots & \vdots \\ \vdots & 0 & a_1 & & \ddots & & a_n & \vdots & \vdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & a_0 & & & \ddots & a_{n-1} & 0 & \vdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & 0 & & & & a_{n-2} & a_n & \vdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & & & a_{n-3} & a_{n-1} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & \dots & \dots & a_{n-4} & a_{n-2} & a_n \end{pmatrix}.$$

is called Hurwitz matrix corresponding to the polynomial $p$. It was established by [Adolf Hurwitz](wikipedia://en/771690) in 1895 that a real polynomial with $a_0 > 0$ is [stable](wikipedia://en/2057699) (that is, all its roots have strictly negative real part) if and only if all the leading principal [minors](wikipedia://en/262107) of the matrix $H(p)$ are positive:

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta_1(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} a_{1} \end{vmatrix} &&=a_{1} > 0 \\[2mm] \Delta_2(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} a_{1} & a_{3} \\ a_{0} & a_{2} \\ \end{vmatrix} &&= a_2 a_1 - a_0 a_3 > 0\\[2mm] \Delta_3(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} a_{1} & a_{3} & a_{5} \\ a_{0} & a_{2} & a_{4} \\ 0 & a_{1} & a_{3} \\ \end{vmatrix} &&= a_3 \Delta_2 - a_1 (a_1 a_4 - a_0 a_5 ) > 0 \end{aligned}$$

and so on. The minors $\Delta_k(p)$ are called the [Hurwitz determinants](wikipedia://en/26569158). Similarly, if $a_0 < 0$ then the polynomial is stable if and only if the principal minors have alternating signs starting with a negative one.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

As an example, consider the matrix

$$M= \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix},$$

and let

$$\begin{aligned} p(x)&=\det(xI-M)\\ &= \begin{vmatrix} x+1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & x+1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & x+1 \end{vmatrix}\\ &=(x+1)^3-(1)(-1)(x+1)\\ &=x^3+3x^2+4x+2 \end{aligned}$$

be the [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of $M$. The Routh–Hurwitz matrixBoth Routh–Hurwitz and Hurwitz-stable matrices are more commonly referred to simply as Hurwitz matrices. To reduce the risk of confusion, this section avoids that terminology. associated to $p$ is then

$$H= \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The leading principal minors of $H$ are

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta_1(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} 3\end{vmatrix} &&=3>0\\[2mm] \Delta_2(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 1 & 4 \\ \end{vmatrix} &&= 12 - 2 = 10 > 0\\[2mm] \Delta_3(p) &= \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 2 \\ \end{vmatrix} &&= 2\Delta_2(p)=20 > 0. \end{aligned}$$

Since the leading principal minors are all positive, all of the roots of $p$ have negative real part. Moreover, since $p$ is the characteristic polynomial of $M$, it follows that all the eigenvalues of $M$ have negative real part, and hence $M$ is a [Hurwitz-stable matrix](wikipedia://en/78291990).
