# Brauer's k(B) conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brauer's k(B) conjecture"}

**[Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134)'s k(B) Conjecture** is a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) in [modular representation theory](wikipedia://en/1110685) of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707) relating the number of [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) irreducible [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) in a [Brauer block](wikipedia://en/1110685) and the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of its [defect groups](wikipedia://en/1110685#Defect_groups). It was first announced in 1946.[^o2] It is Problem 20 in Brauer's list of problems.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666). The set ${\rm Irr}(G)$ of irreducible complex [characters](wikipedia://en/691741) can be partitioned into $p$-[blocks](wikipedia://en/1110685#Blocks_and_the_structure_of_the_group_algebra). To each $p$-block $B$ is canonically associated a conjugacy class of [$p$-subgroups](wikipedia://en/24664), called the [defect groups](wikipedia://en/1110685#Defect_groups) of $B$. The set of irreducible characters belonging to $B$ is denoted by $\mathrm{Irr}(B)$.

The **k(B) Conjecture** asserts that

$$|{\rm Irr}(B)| \leq |D|.$$

:::

## The k(GV) problem {#The_k(GV)_problem}

In the case of blocks of $p$-[solvable groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvable_groups#p-solvable), the conjecture is equivalent to the following question.[^o7] Let $V$ be an [elementary abelian group](wikipedia://en/4843202) of order $p^d$, let $G$ be a finite group of order non-divisible by $p$ and [acting faithfully](wikipedia://en/12781#Notable_properties_of_actions) on $V$ by group automorphisms. Let $GV$ denote the associated semidirect product and let $k(GV)$ be its number of conjugacy classes. Then

$$k(GV) \leq |V|.$$

This was [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) by [John Thompson](wikipedia://en/862189) and Geoffrey Robinson,[^o8] except for finitely many prime numbers. A proof of the last open cases was published in 2004.[^o9][^o10]
