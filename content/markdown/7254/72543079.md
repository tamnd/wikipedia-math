# Brownian snake

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brownian snake"}

A **Brownian snake** is a [stochastic Markov process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_process) on the space of stopped paths. It has been extensively studied.,[^o2][^o3] and was in particular successfully used as a representation of [superprocesses](wikipedia://en/11748718).

:::

Informally, superprocesses are the scaling limit of [branching processes](wikipedia://en/377202), except each particle splits and dies at infinite rates. The Brownian snake is a stochastic object that enables the representation of the genealogy of a superprocess, providing a link between [super-Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/11748718) and [Brownian trees](wikipedia://en/298408). In other words, even though infinitely many particles are constantly born, we can still keep track of individual trajectories in space, or of when two given present-day particles have split from a common ancestor in the past.

## History {#History}

The Brownian snake approach was originally developed by [Jean-François Le Gall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-François_Le_Gall).[^o3][^o5] It has since been applied in fragmentation theory,[^o6] [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564)[^o7] or [planar map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_map)[^o8][^o9]

## The simplest setting {#The_simplest_setting}

Let $D(\mathbb{R}_+,\mathbb{R})$ be the space of [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) functions from $\mathbb{R}_+$ to $\mathbb{R}$, equipped with a metric $d$ compatible with the [Skorokhod topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skorokhod_topology). We define a **stopped path** as a couple $(w,z)$ where $w\in D(\mathbb{R}_+,\mathbb{R})$ and $z\in \mathbb{R}_+$ are such that $w(t)=w(t\wedge z)$. In other words, $w$ is constant after $z$.

Now, we consider a [jump process](wikipedia://en/2180750) $(J_s^N)_{s\geq 0}$ with states $\{+1,-1\}$ and jump rate $N$, such that $J^N_0 = +1$. We set:

$$\hat{\beta}^N_s := \int_0^s J^N_{s'}ds'$$

and then $\beta^N_s := |\hat{\beta}_s^N|$ to be the process *reflected on 0.*

In words, $\beta^N_s$ increases with speed 1, until $J^N_s$ jumps, in which case it decreases with speed 1, and so on. We define the [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) $\sigma_N$ to be the $N$-th hitting time of 0 by $\beta^N$. We now define a stochastic process $(\eta^N_s,\beta^N_s)_{s\in \mathbb{R}_+}$ on the set of stopped paths as follows:

- $$\eta_0^N = 0$$
- if $J^N_s = +1$ for $s\in [s_1,s_2]$ then:
  - $\eta_{s_1}(t)=\eta_{s_2}(t)$ for $t\leq \beta_{s_1}$
  - $\Big(\eta_{s_2}(t-\beta_{s_1})-\eta_{s_1}(\beta_{s_1})\Big)_{0\leq t\leq \beta_{s_2}-\beta_{s_1}}$ is distributed as a Brownian motion independent from $\eta_{s_1}$
- if $J_s^N = -1$ for $s\in [s_1,s_2]$ then $\eta_{s_1}(t)=\eta_{s_2}(t)$ for $t\leq \beta_{s_2}$

See animation for an illustration. We call this process a **snake** and $\beta_s^N$ the **head of the snake**. The path is erased when the snake head moves backwards, and is created anew when it moves forward.

### Duality with a branching Brownian motion {#Duality_with_a_branching_Brownian_motion}

We now consider a measure-valued [branching process](wikipedia://en/377202) $(X^N_t)_{t\geq 0}$ starting with $N$ particles, such that each particle dies with rate $N$, and upon its death gives birth to two offspring with probability $1/2$.

On the other hand, we may define from our process $(\eta_s^N,\beta_s^N)_{0\leq s\leq \sigma^N}$ a measure-valued random process $\hat{X}_t$ as follows: note that for any $t\in \mathbb{R}_+$, there will almost surely be finitely many times $s_1,s_2,\dots,s_n\in [0,\sigma^N]$ such that $\beta_{s_i}=t$. We then set for any [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$:

$$\hat{X}^N_t(f):= \sum\limits_{i=1}^nf(\eta^N_s(t))$$

Then $X$ and $\hat{X}$ are equal in distribution.

## The Brownian snake {#The_Brownian_snake}

We take the limit of the previous system as $N\to \infty$. In this setting, the head of the snake keeps jittering. In fact, the process $\beta_s^N$ tends towards a [reflected Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/35046943) $\beta_s$. The definitions are no longer valid for a number of reasons, in particular because $\beta_s$ is almost surely never monotonous on any interval.

However, we may define a probability $R_{a,b}((u,y),d(w,z))$ on stopped paths such that:

- $R_{a,b}$-almost surely $z=b$ and $w(t)=u(t)$ for $0\leq t\leq a$
- The law of $(w(a+t))_{0\leq t\leq b-a}$ is the law of a standard Brownian motion.

We may also define $\gamma_s^y(da,db)$ to be the distribution of $(\inf_{0\leq r\leq s}\beta_r,\beta_s)$ if $\beta_0=y$. Finally, define the [transition semigroup](wikipedia://en/60876) on the set of stopped paths:

$$Q_s((u,y),d(w,z)) = \int \gamma_s^y(da,db)R_{a,b}((u,y),d(w,z))$$

A [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with this semigroup is called a **Brownian snake**.

We may again find a duality between this process and a branching process. Here the branching process will be a [super-Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/11748718) $(X_t)_{t\in \mathbb{R}_+}$ with branching mechanism $\phi(z)=z^2$, started on a [Dirac](wikipedia://en/37021) in 0.

However, unlike the previous case, we must be more careful in the definition of the process $\hat{X}$. Indeed, for $t\in \mathbb{R}_+$ we cannot just list the times $s_1,s_2,\dots$ such that $\beta_s=t$. Instead we use the [local time](wikipedia://en/5678101) $l_s(t)$ associated with $\beta_s$: we first define the [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) $\sigma = \inf\{s\geq 0, l_s(0)\geq u\}$. Then we define for any measurable $f$:

$$\hat{X}_t(f):= \int_0^\sigma f(\eta_s(t))dl_s(t)$$

Then, as before, we obtain that $X$ and $\hat{X}$ are equal in distribution. See the animation for the construction of the branching process from the Brownian snake. Animation for the branching process associated with the Brownian snake

## Generalisation {#Generalisation}

The previous example can be generalized in many ways:

- We may consider $D(\mathbb{R}_+,E)$ where $(E,d)$ is a complete separable [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018).
- Instead of a Brownian motion, the underlying movement of the snake can be very general class of Markov processes (see [Superprocess](wikipedia://en/11748718)).

## Link with genealogy and the Brownian tree {#Link_with_genealogy_and_the_Brownian_tree}

The Brownian snake can be seen as a way to represent the genealogy of a superprocess, the same way a [Galton-Watson tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton-Watson_tree) may encode the hidden genealogy of a [Galton–Watson process](wikipedia://en/350596).[^o3] Indeed, for two points of the Brownian snake, their common ancestor will be the infimum of the snake's head position between them.

If we take a Brownian snake and construct a [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) from it, we obtain a [Brownian tree](wikipedia://en/298408).[^o3]
