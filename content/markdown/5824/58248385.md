# Walsh–Lebesgue theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Walsh–Lebesgue theorem"}

The **Walsh–Lebesgue theorem** is a famous result from [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) proved by the American mathematician [Joseph L. Walsh](wikipedia://en/14002828) in 1929, using results proved by [Lebesgue](wikipedia://en/49514) in 1907.[^o2][^o3][^o4] The theorem states the following:

:::

Let $K$ be a [compact subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) $\mathbb{R}^2$ such the [relative complement](wikipedia://en/54347) of $K$ with respect to $\mathbb{R}^2$ is [connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_set). Then, every real-valued [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) on $\partial{K}$ (*i.e.* the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of *$K$*) can be [approximated uniformly](wikipedia://en/50652) on $\partial{K}$ by (real-valued) [harmonic polynomials](wikipedia://en/5755288) in the real variables $x$ and $y$.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The Walsh–Lebesgue theorem has been generalized to [Riemann surfaces](wikipedia://en/173181)[^o8] and to $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([real coordinate space](wikipedia://en/1482083)).

This Walsh-Lebesgue theorem has also served as a catalyst for entire chapters in the theory of [function algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_algebra) such as the theory of [Dirichlet algebras](wikipedia://en/8984724) and logmodular algebras.[^o9]

In 1974 Anthony G. O'Farrell gave a generalization of the Walsh–Lebesgue theorem by means of the 1964 Browder–Wermer theorem[^o10] with related techniques.[^o11][^o12][^o13]

:::
