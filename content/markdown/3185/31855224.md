# Rodion Kuzmin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rodion Kuzmin"}

**Rodion Osievich Kuzmin** (Родион Осиевич Кузьмин, 9 November 1891, Riabye village in the [Haradok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haradok) district – 24 March 1949, [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad)) was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his works in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).[^o3] His name is sometimes transliterated as Kusmin. He was an Invited Speaker of the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in 1928 in Bologna.[^o4]

:::

## Selected results {#Selected_results}

- In 1928, Kuzmin solved[^o6] the following problem due to [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (see [Gauss&ndash;Kuzmin distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss&ndash;Kuzmin_distribution)): if *x* is a random number chosen uniformly in (0, 1), and

$$x = \frac{1}{k_1 + \frac{1}{k_2 + \cdots}}$$

is its [continued fraction](wikipedia://en/739199) expansion, find a bound for

$$\Delta_n(s) = \mathbb{P} \left\{ x_n \leq s \right\} - \log_2(1+s),$$

where

$$x_n = \frac{1}{k_{n+1} + \frac{1}{k_{n+2} + \cdots}} .$$

Gauss showed that *Δ*<sub>*n*</sub> tends to zero as *n* goes to infinity, however, he was unable to give an explicit bound. Kuzmin showed that

$$|\Delta_n(s)| \leq C e^{- \alpha \sqrt{n}}~,$$

where *C*,*α* > 0 are numerical constants. In 1929, the bound was improved to *C* 0.7<sup>*n*</sup> by [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462).

- In 1930, Kuzmin proved[^o7] that numbers of the form *a*<sup>*b*</sup>, where *a* is [algebraic](wikipedia://en/1158) and *b* is a real [quadratic irrational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_irrational), are [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325). In particular, this result implies that [Gelfond–Schneider constant](wikipedia://en/886866)

$$2^{\sqrt{2}}=2.6651441426902251886502972498731\ldots$$

is transcendental. See [Gelfond–Schneider theorem](wikipedia://en/609487) for later developments.

- He is also known for the [Kusmin-Landau inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusmin-Landau_inequality): If $f$ is continuously differentiable with monotonic derivative $f'$ satisfying $\Vert f'(x) \Vert \geq \lambda > 0$ (where $\Vert \cdot \Vert$ denotes the [Nearest integer function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function)) on a finite interval $I$, then

$$\sum_{n\in I} e^{2\pi if(n)}\ll \lambda^{-1}.$$
