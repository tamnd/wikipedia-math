# Dinostratus

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dinostratus"}

**Dinostratus** (Δεινόστρατος; c. 390 – c. 320 BCE) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [geometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometer), and the brother of [Menaechmus](wikipedia://en/1944319). He is known for using the [quadratrix](wikipedia://en/2885016) to solve the problem of [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Dinostratus' chief contribution to mathematics was his solution to the problem of squaring the circle. To solve this problem, Dinostratus made use of the [trisectrix](wikipedia://en/2909893) of [Hippias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippias), for which he proved a special property ([Dinostratus' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinostratus'_theorem)) that allowed him the squaring of the circle. Due to his work the trisectrix later became known as the [quadratrix](wikipedia://en/2885016) of Dinostratus as well.[^o4] Although Dinostratus solved the problem of squaring the circle, he did not do so using [ruler and compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge_constructions) alone, and so it was clear to the Greeks that his solution violated the foundational principles of their mathematics.[^o4] Over 2,200 years later [Ferdinand von Lindemann](wikipedia://en/335736) would prove that it is impossible to square a circle using straight edge and compass alone.
