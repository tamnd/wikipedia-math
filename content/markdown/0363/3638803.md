# Constant curvature

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Constant curvature"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **constant curvature** is a concept from [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). Here, curvature refers to the [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618) of a space (more precisely a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219)) and is a single number that determines its local geometry.[^o2] The sectional curvature is said to be constant if it has the same value at every point and for every two-dimensional tangent plane at that point. For example, a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) is a surface of constant positive curvature.

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

The classifications here are based on the [universal covering space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_covering_space). There may be more than one space that has the same universal covering space.

The [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652) of constant curvature can be classified into the following three classes:

- [Elliptic geometry](wikipedia://en/236020) – constant positive sectional curvature
- [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417) – constant vanishing sectional curvature
- [Hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291) – constant negative sectional curvature.

The [Lorentzian manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentzian_manifold) of constant curvature can be classified into the following three classes:

- [De Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301666) – constant positive sectional curvature
- [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) (generalized to any dimension) – constant vanishing sectional curvature
- [Anti-de Sitter space](wikipedia://en/301670) – constant negative sectional curvature.

The de Sitter and anti-de Sitter spaces of dimension 2 are the same (the sign of the curvature depends on which direction is referenced as "space-like").

For every signature, dimension and curvature, a similar classification exists.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Every space of constant curvature is [locally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_symmetric), i.e. its [curvature tensor](wikipedia://en/130526) is [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497) $\nabla \mathrm{R}=0$.
- Every space of dimension $n$ of constant curvature is locally [maximally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximally_symmetric), i.e. it has $\textstyle \frac{1}{2} n (n+1)$ [local isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector).
- Conversely, there exists a similar but stronger statement: every [maximally symmetric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximally_symmetric) space, i.e. a space that has $\textstyle \frac{1}{2} n (n+1)$ (global) [isometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometries), has constant curvature.
- ([Killing–Hopf theorem](wikipedia://en/37711150)) The [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of a Riemannian manifold of constant sectional curvature is one of the model spaces:
  - [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) (positive sectional curvature)
  - [plane](wikipedia://en/12756616) (zero sectional curvature)
  - [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/318742) (negative sectional curvature)
- A space of constant curvature that is [geodesically complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesically_complete) is called a [space form](wikipedia://en/1087009). The study of space forms is intimately related to generalized crystallography (see the article *[Space form](wikipedia://en/1087009)* for more details).
- Two space forms are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) if and only if they have the same dimension, their metrics possess the same [signature](wikipedia://en/463225) and their sectional curvatures are equal.

:::
