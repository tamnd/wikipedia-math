# Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Atiyah–Bott fixed-point theorem**, proven by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) in the 1960s, is a general form of the [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530) for [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*, which uses an [elliptic complex](wikipedia://en/3189496) on *M*. This is a system of [elliptic differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_differential_operator) on [vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524), generalizing the [de Rham complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_complex) constructed from smooth [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) which appears in the original Lefschetz fixed-point theorem.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

The idea is to find the correct replacement for the [Lefschetz number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_number), which in the classical result is an integer counting the correct contribution of a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738) of a smooth mapping

$$f\colon M \to M.$$

Intuitively, the fixed points are the points of intersection of the [graph](wikipedia://en/87352) of *f* with the diagonal (graph of the identity mapping) in $M\times M$, and the Lefschetz number thereby becomes an [intersection number](wikipedia://en/384327). The Atiyah–Bott theorem is an equation in which the [LHS](wikipedia://en/166352) must be the outcome of a global topological (homological) calculation, and the [RHS](wikipedia://en/166352) a sum of the local contributions at fixed points of *f*.

Counting [codimensions](wikipedia://en/525149) in $M\times M$, a [transversality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transversality_%28mathematics%29) assumption for the graph of *f* and the diagonal should ensure that the fixed point set is zero-dimensional. Assuming *M* a [closed manifold](wikipedia://en/669475) should ensure then that the set of intersections is finite, yielding a finite summation as the RHS of the expected formula. Further data needed relates to the elliptic complex of vector bundles $E_j$, namely a [bundle map](wikipedia://en/7220589)

$$\varphi_j \colon f^{-1}(E_j) \to E_j$$

for each *j*, such that the resulting maps on [sections](wikipedia://en/367621) give rise to an [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of an [elliptic complex](wikipedia://en/3189496) $T$. Such an endomorphism $T$ has *Lefschetz number*

$$L(T),$$

which by definition is the [alternating sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_sum) of its [traces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_of_an_endomorphism) on each graded part of the homology of the elliptic complex.

The form of the theorem is then

$$L(T) = \sum_x \left(\sum_j (-1)^j \mathrm{trace}\, \varphi_{j,x}\right)/\delta(x).$$

Here trace $\varphi_{j,x}$ means the trace of $\varphi_{j}$ at a fixed point *x* of *f*, and $\delta(x)$ is the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the endomorphism $I -Df$ at *x*, with $Df$ the derivative of *f* (the non-vanishing of this is a consequence of transversality). The outer summation is over the fixed points *x*, and the inner summation over the index *j* in the elliptic complex.

Specializing the Atiyah–Bott theorem to the de Rham complex of smooth differential forms yields the original Lefschetz fixed-point formula. A famous application of the Atiyah–Bott theorem is a simple proof of the [Weyl character formula](wikipedia://en/5011916) in the theory of [Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_groups).

:::

## History {#History}

The early history of this result is entangled with that of the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752). There was other input, as is suggested by the alternate name *Woods Hole fixed-point theorem* that was used in the past (referring properly to the case of isolated fixed points).[^o6] A 1964 meeting at [Woods Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woods_Hole) brought together a varied group:

> [Eichler](wikipedia://en/4224478) started the interaction between fixed-point theorems and [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146). [Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724) played an important part in this development by explaining this to Bott at the Woods Hole conference in 1964.[^o7]

As Atiyah puts it:[^o8]

> \[at the conference\]...Bott and I learnt of a conjecture of Shimura concerning a generalization of the Lefschetz formula for holomorphic maps. After much effort we convinced ourselves that there should be a general formula of this type \[...\]; .

and they were led to a version for elliptic complexes.

In the recollection of [William Fulton](wikipedia://en/9642149), who was also present at the conference, the first to produce a proof was [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893).

## Proofs {#Proofs}

In the context of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the statement applies for smooth and proper varieties over an algebraically closed field. This variant of the Atiyah–Bott fixed point formula was proved by (Kondyrev Prikhodko 2018) by expressing both sides of the formula as appropriately chosen [categorical traces](wikipedia://en/62338906).
