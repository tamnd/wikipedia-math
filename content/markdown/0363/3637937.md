# Formal fallacy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formal fallacy"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [philosophy](wikipedia://en/304939), a **formal fallacy** is a pattern of [reasoning](wikipedia://en/42446) with a flaw in its logical structure (the logical relationship between the premises and the conclusion). A formal fallacy is contrasted with an [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450). A formal fallacy must have an [invalid](wikipedia://en/21304742) logical [form](wikipedia://en/3331706) and thus be [unsound](wikipedia://en/26819). An informal fallacy, however, may have a valid logical form and yet be unsound because one or more [premises](wikipedia://en/7993337) are false. An [argument](wikipedia://en/13018310) can be both a formal fallacy and an informal fallacy. In everyday conversation, the term *logical fallacy* usually refers to a formal fallacy.

:::

## Related concept {#Related_concept}

[Propositional logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus)[^o6] is concerned with the meanings of sentences and the relationships between them. It focuses on the role of logical operators, called propositional connectives, in determining whether a sentence is true. An error in the sequence will result in a deductive argument that is invalid. The argument itself could have true [premises](wikipedia://en/7993337), but still have a false conclusion.[^o7] Thus, a formal fallacy is a fallacy in which deduction goes wrong, and is no longer a logical process. This may not affect the truth of the conclusion, since validity and truth are separate in formal logic.

## Common examples {#Common_examples}

*Image: Logical fallacy Venn diagram.svg.* Euler diagram showing how axioms of "most animals in this zoo are birds" and "most birds can fly" need not mean that "most animals in this zoo can fly"

In the strictest sense, a logical fallacy is the incorrect application of a valid logical principle or an application of a nonexistent principle, such as reasoning that:

1. Most animals in this zoo are birds.
1. Most birds can fly.
1. Therefore, most animals in this zoo can fly.

This is fallacious: a zoo could have a large proportion of flightless birds. There is no logical principle that states:

1. For some x, P(x).
1. For some x, Q(x).
1. Therefore, for some x, P(x) and Q(x).

An easy way to show the above inference as invalid is by using [Euler diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_diagrams). In logical parlance, the inference is invalid, since under at least one interpretation of the predicates it is not validity preserving.

People often have difficulty applying the rules of logic. For example, a person may say the following [syllogism](wikipedia://en/48046) is valid, when in fact it is not:

1. All [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird) have beaks.
1. That creature has a beak.
1. Therefore, that creature is a bird.

"That creature" may well be a bird, but the [conclusion](wikipedia://en/804778) does not follow from the premises. Certain other animals also have beaks, such as [turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtles). Errors of this type occur because people reverse a premise.[^o9] In this case, "All birds have beaks" is converted to "All beaked creatures are birds." The reversed premise is plausible because few people are aware of any instances of *beaked creatures* besides birds—but this premise is not the one that was given. In this way, the deductive fallacy is formed by points that may individually appear logical, but when placed together are shown to be incorrect.

## Special example {#Special_example}

A special case is a [mathematical fallacy](wikipedia://en/254364), an intentionally invalid [mathematical proof](wikipedia://en/82285), often with the error subtle and somehow concealed. Mathematical fallacies are typically crafted and exhibited for educational purposes, usually taking the form of spurious proofs of obvious [contradictions](wikipedia://en/75807).

## Non Sequitur fallacy {#Non_Sequitur_fallacy}

In everyday speech, a non sequitur is a statement in which the final part is totally unrelated to the first part, for example: Life is life and fun is fun, but it's all so quiet when the goldfish die.
