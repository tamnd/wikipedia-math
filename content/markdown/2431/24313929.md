# Slothful induction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Slothful induction"}

**Slothful induction**, also called **appeal to coincidence**, is a [fallacy](wikipedia://en/228851) in which an [inductive](wikipedia://en/393736) argument is denied its proper conclusion, despite strong evidence for [inference](wikipedia://en/317465). An example of slothful induction might be that of a careless man who has had twelve accidents in the last six months, and it is strongly evident that they were due to his negligence or rashness, yet he keeps insisting that it is just a coincidence and not his fault.[^o2] Its logical form is: evidence suggests X results in Y, yet the person in question insists Y was caused by something else.[^o3]

:::

Its opposite fallacy (which perhaps occurs more often) is called [hasty generalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasty_generalization).
