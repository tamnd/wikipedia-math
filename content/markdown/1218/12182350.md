# Remez inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Remez inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Remez inequality**, discovered by the Soviet mathematician [Evgeny Yakovlevich Remez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Yakovlevich_Remez) (Remez 1936), gives a [bound](wikipedia://en/42693) on the [sup norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sup_norm) of certain [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000), the bound being attained by the [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="The inequality"}

## The inequality {#The_inequality}

Let *σ* be an arbitrary fixed positive number. Define the class of polynomials π<sub>*n*</sub>(*σ*) to be those polynomials *p* of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) *n* for which

$$|p(x)| \le 1$$

on some set of [measure](wikipedia://en/19873) ≥ 2 contained in the [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) \[−1, 1+*σ*\]. Then the **Remez inequality** states that

$$\sup_{p \in \pi_n(\sigma)} \left\|p\right\|_\infty = \left\|T_n\right\|_\infty$$

where *T*<sub>*n*</sub>(*x*) is the [Chebyshev polynomial](wikipedia://en/184539) of degree *n*, and the [supremum norm](wikipedia://en/349755) is taken over the interval \[−1, 1+*σ*\].

Observe that *T*<sub>*n*</sub> is increasing on $[1, +\infty]$, hence

$$\|T_n\|_\infty = T_n(1+\sigma).$$

The R.i., combined with an estimate on Chebyshev polynomials, implies the following [corollary](wikipedia://en/168865): If *J* ⊂ **R** is a finite interval, and *E* ⊂ *J* is an arbitrary [measurable set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_set), then

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="⁎"}

$$\max_{x \in J} |p(x)| \leq \left( \frac{4 \,\, \operatorname{mes}J}{\operatorname{mes}E} \right)^n \sup_{x \in E} |p(x)| \tag{⁎}$$

:::

for any polynomial *p* of degree *n*.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Extensions: Nazarov–Turán lemma"}

## Extensions: Nazarov–Turán lemma {#Extensions:_Nazarov–Turán_lemma}

[Inequalities](wikipedia://en/89489) similar to ((⁎)) have been [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) for different classes of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), and are known as Remez-type inequalities. One important example is [Nazarov](wikipedia://en/21079027)'s inequality for [exponential sums](wikipedia://en/468937) (Nazarov 1993):

**Nazarov's inequality**. Let

$$p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^n a_k e^{\lambda_k x}$$

be an exponential sum (with arbitrary *λ*<sub>*k*</sub> ∈**C**), and let *J* ⊂ **R** be a finite interval, *E* ⊂ *J*—an arbitrary measurable set. Then

$$\max_{x \in J} |p(x)| \leq e^{\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes}J} \left( \frac{C \,\, \operatorname{mes}J}{\operatorname{mes}E} \right)^{n-1} \sup_{x \in E} |p(x)|~,$$

where *C* > 0 is a numerical constant.

In the special case when *λ<sub>k</sub>* are pure imaginary and integer, and the subset *E* is itself an interval, the inequality was proved by [Pál Turán](wikipedia://en/614998) and is known as Turán's lemma.

This inequality also extends to $L^p(\mathbb{T}),\ 0\leq p\leq2$ in the following way

$$\|p\|_{L^p(\mathbb{T})} \leq e^{A(n-1) \operatorname{mes}(\mathbb{T} \setminus E)} \|p\|_{L^p(E)}$$

for some *A* > 0 independent of *p*, *E*, and *n*. When

$$\operatorname{mes} E <1-\frac{\log n}{n}$$

a similar inequality holds for *p* > 2. For *p* = ∞ there is an extension to multidimensional polynomials.

::: {#o9 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof:** Applying Nazarov's lemma to $E = E_\lambda = \{x : |p(x)|\leq\lambda\},\ \lambda>0$ leads to

$$\max_{x \in J} |p(x)| \leq e^{\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes} J} \left( \frac{C \,\, \operatorname{mes} J}{\operatorname{mes} E_\lambda} \right)^{n-1} \sup_{x \in E_\lambda} |p(x)| \leq e^{\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes} J} \left( \frac{C \,\, \operatorname{mes} J}{\operatorname{mes} E_\lambda} \right)^{n-1} \lambda$$

thus

$$\operatorname{mes} E_\lambda \leq C \,\, \operatorname{mes} J\left(\frac{\lambda e^{\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes}J}}{\max_{x \in J} |p(x)|} \right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}$$

Now fix a set $E$ and choose $\lambda$ such that $\operatorname{mes} E_\lambda\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{mes}E$, that is

$$\lambda = \left(\frac{\operatorname{mes} E}{2C \operatorname{mes}J}\right)^{n-1}e^{-\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes}J}\max_{x \in J} |p(x)|$$

Note that this implies:

1. $$\operatorname{mes}E\setminus E_{\lambda}\ge \tfrac{1}{2} \operatorname{mes}E .$$
1. $$\forall x \in E \setminus E_{\lambda} : |p(x)| > \lambda .$$

Now

$$\begin{aligned} \int_{x\in E}|p(x)|^p\,\mbox{d}x &\geq \int_{x\in E \setminus E_\lambda}|p(x)|^p\,\mbox{d}x \\[6pt] &\geq \lambda^p\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mes}E \\[6pt] &= \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mes}E \left(\frac{\operatorname{mes}E}{2C \operatorname{mes}J}\right)^{p(n-1)}e^{-p\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes}J}\max_{x \in J} |p(x)|^p \\[6pt] &\geq \frac{1}{2} \frac{\operatorname{mes}E}{\operatorname{mes}J}\left(\frac{\operatorname{mes} E}{2C \operatorname{mes}J}\right)^{p(n-1)}e^{-p\max_k |\Re \lambda_k| \, \operatorname{mes}J}\int_{x \in J} |p(x)|^p\,\mbox{d}x, \end{aligned}$$

which completes the proof.

:::

::: {#o11 .statement cue=named-heading name="Pólya inequality"}

## Pólya inequality {#Pólya_inequality}

One of the corollaries of the Remez inequality is the **Pólya inequality**, which was proved by [George Pólya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pólya) (Pólya 1928), and states that the [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198) of a sub-level set of a polynomial *p* of degree *n* is bounded in terms of the leading [coefficient](wikipedia://en/51203) LC(*p*) as follows:

$$\operatorname{mes} \left\{ x \in \mathbb{R} : \left|P(x)\right| \leq a \right\} \leq 4 \left(\frac{a}{2 \mathrm{LC}(p)}\right)^{1/n}, \quad a>0~.$$

:::
