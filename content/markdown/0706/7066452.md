# Casson invariant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Casson invariant"}

In [3-dimensional topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-dimensional_topology), a part of the mathematical field of [geometric topology](wikipedia://en/621732), the **Casson invariant** is an integer-valued invariant of oriented integral [homology 3-spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_3-sphere), introduced by [Andrew Casson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Casson).

:::

Kevin Walker (1992) found an extension to [rational homology 3-spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_homology_3-sphere), called the **Casson–Walker invariant**, and Christine Lescop (1995) extended the invariant to all [closed](wikipedia://en/669475) oriented [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Casson invariant is a surjective map λ from oriented integral homology 3-spheres to **Z** satisfying the following properties:

- λ(**S**<sup>3</sup>) = 0.
- Let Σ be an integral homology 3-sphere. Then for any knot *K* and for any integer *n*, the difference

$$\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot K\right)-\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{n}\cdot K\right)$$

is independent of *n*. Here $\Sigma+\frac{1}{m}\cdot K$ denotes $\frac{1}{m}$ [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) on Σ by *K*.

- For any boundary link *K* ∪ *L* in Σ the following expression is zero:

$$\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{m+1}\cdot K+\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot L\right) -\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{m}\cdot K+\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot L\right)-\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{m+1}\cdot K+\frac{1}{n}\cdot L\right) +\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{m}\cdot K+\frac{1}{n}\cdot L\right)$$

The Casson invariant is unique (with respect to the above properties) up to an overall multiplicative constant.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If K is the trefoil then

$$\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot K\right)-\lambda\left(\Sigma+\frac{1}{n}\cdot K\right)=\pm 1.$$

- The Casson invariant is 1 (or −1) for the [Poincaré homology sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincaré_homology_sphere).
- The Casson invariant changes sign if the orientation of *M* is reversed.
- The [Rokhlin invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rokhlin_invariant) of *M* is equal to the Casson invariant mod 2.
- The Casson invariant is additive with respect to [connected summing](wikipedia://en/490134) of homology 3-spheres.
- The Casson invariant is a sort of [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) for [Floer homology](wikipedia://en/2672856).
- For any integer *n*

$$\lambda \left ( M + \frac{1}{n+1}\cdot K\right ) - \lambda \left ( M + \frac{1}{n}\cdot K\right ) = \phi_1 (K),$$

where $\phi_1 (K)$ is the coefficient of $z^2$ in the [Alexander–Conway polynomial](wikipedia://en/1107647) $\nabla_K(z)$, and is congruent (mod 2) to the [Arf invariant](wikipedia://en/6950659) of *K*.

- The Casson invariant is the degree 1 part of the [Le–Murakami–Ohtsuki invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le–Murakami–Ohtsuki_invariant).
- The Casson invariant for the integer [Homology Sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_Sphere) $\Sigma(p,q,r)$ is given by the formula:

$$\lambda(\Sigma(p,q,r))=-\frac{1}{8}\left[1-\frac{1}{3pqr}\left(1-p^2q^2r^2+p^2q^2+q^2r^2+p^2r^2\right) -d(p,qr)-d(q,pr)-d(r,pq)\right]$$

where

$$d(a,b)=-\frac{1}{a}\sum_{k=1}^{a-1}\cot\left(\frac{\pi k}{a}\right)\cot\left(\frac{\pi bk}{a}\right)$$

:::

## The Casson invariant as a count of representations {#The_Casson_invariant_as_a_count_of_representations}

Informally speaking, the Casson invariant counts half the number of conjugacy classes of representations of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a homology 3-sphere *M* into the group [SU(2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%282%29). This can be made precise as follows.

The representation space of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) oriented 3-manifold *M* is defined as $\mathcal{R}(M)=R^{\mathrm{irr}}(M)/SU(2)$ where $R^{\mathrm{irr}}(M)$ denotes the space of irreducible SU(2) representations of $\pi_1 (M)$. For a [Heegaard splitting](wikipedia://en/864418) $\Sigma=M_1 \cup_F M_2$ of $M$, the Casson invariant equals $\frac{(-1)^g}{2}$ times the algebraic intersection of $\mathcal{R}(M_1)$ with $\mathcal{R}(M_2)$.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalizations"}

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

### Rational homology 3-spheres {#Rational_homology_3-spheres}

Kevin Walker found an extension of the Casson invariant to [rational homology 3-spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_homology_3-sphere). A Casson-Walker invariant is a surjective map λ<sub>*CW*</sub> from oriented rational homology 3-spheres to **Q** satisfying the following properties:

**\1.** λ(**S**<sup>3</sup>) = 0.

**\2.** For every 1-component [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) presentation (*K*, μ) of an oriented rational homology sphere *M*′ in an oriented rational homology sphere *M*:

$$\lambda_{CW}(M^\prime)=\lambda_{CW}(M)+\frac{\langle m,\mu\rangle}{\langle m,\nu\rangle\langle \mu,\nu\rangle}\Delta_{W}^{\prime\prime}(M-K)(1)+\tau_{W}(m,\mu;\nu)$$

where:

- *m* is an oriented meridian of a knot *K* and μ is the characteristic curve of the surgery.
- ν is a generator the kernel of the natural map *H*<sub>1</sub>(∂*N*(*K*), **Z**) → *H*<sub>1</sub>(*M*−*K*, **Z**).
- $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the intersection form on the tubular neighbourhood of the knot, *N*(*K*).
- Δ is the Alexander polynomial normalized so that the action of *t* corresponds to an action of the generator of $H_1(M-K)/\text{Torsion}$ in the infinite [cyclic cover](wikipedia://en/46987327) of *M*−*K*, and is symmetric and evaluates to 1 at 1.
- $$\tau_{W}(m,\mu;\nu)= -\mathrm{sgn}\langle y,m\rangle s(\langle x,m\rangle,\langle y,m\rangle)+\mathrm{sgn}\langle y,\mu\rangle s(\langle x,\mu\rangle,\langle y,\mu\rangle)+\frac{(\delta^2-1)\langle m,\mu\rangle}{12\langle m,\nu\rangle\langle \mu,\nu\rangle}$$

where *x*, *y* are generators of *H*<sub>1</sub>(∂*N*(*K*), **Z**) such that $\langle x,y\rangle=1$, *v* = δ*y* for an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) δ and *s*(*p*, *q*) is the [Dedekind sum](wikipedia://en/1122242).

Note that for integer homology spheres, the Walker's normalization is twice that of Casson's: $\lambda_{CW}(M) = 2 \lambda(M)$.

### Compact oriented 3-manifolds {#Compact_oriented_3-manifolds}

Christine Lescop defined an extension λ<sub>*CWL*</sub> of the Casson-Walker invariant to oriented compact [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifolds). It is uniquely characterized by the following properties:

- If the first [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) of *M* is zero,

$$\lambda_{CWL}(M)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left\vert H_1(M)\right\vert\lambda_{CW}(M).$$

- If the first Betti number of *M* is one,

$$\lambda_{CWL}(M)=\frac{\Delta^{\prime\prime}_M(1)}{2}-\frac{\mathrm{torsion}(H_1(M,\mathbb{Z}))}{12}$$

where Δ is the Alexander polynomial normalized to be symmetric and take a positive value at 1.

- If the first Betti number of *M* is two,

$$\lambda_{CWL}(M)=\left\vert\mathrm{torsion}(H_1(M))\right\vert\mathrm{Link}_M (\gamma,\gamma^\prime)$$

where γ is the oriented curve given by the intersection of two generators $S_1,S_2$ of $H_2(M;\mathbb{Z})$ and $\gamma^\prime$ is the [parallel curve](wikipedia://en/912825) to γ induced by the trivialization of the tubular neighbourhood of γ determined by $S_1, S_2$.

- If the first Betti number of *M* is three, then for *a*,*b*,*c* a basis for $H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$, then

$$\lambda_{CWL}(M)=\left\vert\mathrm{torsion}(H_1(M;\mathbb{Z}))\right\vert\left((a\cup b\cup c)([M])\right)^2.$$

- If the first Betti number of *M* is greater than three, $\lambda_{CWL}(M)=0$.

The Casson–Walker–Lescop invariant has the following properties:

- When the orientation of *M* changes the behavior of $\lambda_{CWL}(M)$ depends on the first Betti number $b_1(M) = \operatorname{rank} H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$of *M*: if $\overline{M}$ is *M* with the opposite orientation, then

$$\lambda_{CWL}(\overline{M}) = (-1)^{b_1(M)+1}\lambda_{CWL}(M).$$

That is, if the first Betti number of *M* is odd the Casson–Walker–Lescop invariant is unchanged, while if it is even it changes sign.

- For [connect-sums](wikipedia://en/490134) of manifolds

$$\lambda_{CWL}(M_1\#M_2)=\left\vert H_1(M_2)\right\vert\lambda_{CWL}(M_1)+\left\vert H_1(M_1)\right\vert\lambda_{CWL}(M_2)$$

### [SU(N)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%28N%29) {#SU(N)}

In 1990, C. Taubes showed that the SU(2) Casson invariant of a 3-homology sphere *M* has a gauge theoretic interpretation as the [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) of $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$, where $\mathcal{A}$ is the space of SU(2) connections on *M* and $\mathcal{G}$ is the group of gauge transformations. He regarded the [Chern–Simons invariant](wikipedia://en/391908) as a $S^1$-valued [Morse function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_function) on $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$ and used invariance under perturbations to define an invariant which he equated with the SU(2) Casson invariant. ((Taubes 1990))

H. Boden and C. Herald (1998) used a similar approach to define an [SU(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SU%283%29) Casson invariant for integral homology 3-spheres.
