# Marie Rognes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marie Rognes"}

**Marie Elisabeth Rognes** (born 7 October 1982) is a Norwegian applied mathematician specializing in [scientific computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_computing) and [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/1678715) for [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). She works at the [Simula Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simula_Research_Laboratory), as one of their chief research scientists.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Rognes was a student in applied mathematics at the [University of Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oslo), earning a master's degree in 2005 and completing a Ph.D. in 2009. Her dissertation, *Mixed finite element methods with applications to [viscoelasticity](wikipedia://en/711288) and gels*, was jointly supervised by [Ragnar Winther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Winther) and [Hans Petter Langtangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Petter_Langtangen).

After postdoctoral research at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) and the Simula Research Laboratory, she joined the University of Oslo as a lecturer in 2012, and in the same year became a senior researcher for Simula Research. She remained affiliated on a part-time basis with the University of Oslo until 2016, when she became a chief research scientist for Simula Research.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Rognes became one of 20 founding members of the Young Academy of Norway in 2015. In the same year she was part of a team that won the [J. H. Wilkinson Prize for Numerical Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._H._Wilkinson_Prize_for_Numerical_Software), given every four years at the [International Congress on Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13606431). The award cited their work on dolfin-adjoint, a software package for adjoint and tangent linear equations.

In 2018 she was the winner of the [Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Norwegian_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters) Prize for Young Researchers in the Natural Sciences.
