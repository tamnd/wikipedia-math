# Algebraic expression

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Algebraic expression"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **algebraic expression** is an [expression](wikipedia://en/609125) built up from [constants](wikipedia://en/24758132) (usually, [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158)), [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109), and the basic [algebraic operations](wikipedia://en/5438924): [addition](wikipedia://en/61338) (+), [subtraction](wikipedia://en/74141) (−), [multiplication](wikipedia://en/20845) (×), [division](wikipedia://en/53696) (÷), whole number [powers](wikipedia://en/99491#Integer), and [roots](wikipedia://en/235029) ([fractional](wikipedia://en/1704824) powers), without any relational signs such as = or \<.[^o2][^o3][^o4]. For example, $3x^2 - 2xy + c$ is an algebraic expression. Since taking the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) is the same as raising to the power $\tfrac{1}{2}$, the following is also an algebraic expression:

:::

$$\sqrt{\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}}$$

An *[algebraic equation](wikipedia://en/404001)* is an [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) involving [polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomials), for which algebraic expressions may be [solutions](wikipedia://en/1601833).

If the set of constants is restricted to [numbers](wikipedia://en/21690), any algebraic expression can be called an [arithmetic expression](wikipedia://en/3118). However, algebraic expressions can be used on more abstract objects such as in [Abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384). If the constants are restricted to [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers), the set of numbers that can be described with an algebraic expression are called [Algebraic numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_numbers).

By contrast, [transcendental numbers](wikipedia://en/30325) like $\pi$ and $e$ are not algebraic, since they are not derived from integer constants and algebraic operations. Usually, $\pi$ is constructed as a geometric relationship, and the definition of $e$ requires an *infinite number* of algebraic operations. More generally, expressions which are [algebraically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraically_independent) from their constants and/or variables are called [transcendental](wikipedia://en/297021).

## Conventions {#Conventions}

### Variables {#Variables}

By convention, letters at the beginning of the alphabet (e.g. $a, b, c$) are typically used to represent [constants](wikipedia://en/48779551), and those toward the end of the alphabet (e.g. $x, y$ and $z$) are used to represent [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109).[^o10] They are usually written in italics.[^o11]

### Exponents {#Exponents}

By convention, terms with the highest power ([exponent](wikipedia://en/99491)), are written on the left, for example, $x^2$ is written to the left of $x$. When a coefficient is one, it is usually omitted (e.g. $1x^2$ is written $x^2$).[^o13] Likewise when the exponent (power) is one, (e.g. $3x^1$ is written $3x$),[^o14] and, when the exponent is zero, the result is always 1 (e.g. $3x^0$ is written $3$, since $x^0$ is always $1$).[^o15]

:::

## In roots of polynomials {#In_roots_of_polynomials}

The [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) of a polynomial expression of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) *n*, or equivalently the solutions of a [polynomial equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_equation), can always be written as algebraic expressions if *n* \< 5 (see [quadratic formula](wikipedia://en/59217), [cubic function](wikipedia://en/243709), and [quartic equation](wikipedia://en/251591)). Such a solution of an equation is called an [algebraic solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_solution). But the [Abel–Ruffini theorem](wikipedia://en/152518) states that algebraic solutions do not exist for all such equations (just for some of them) if *n* $\ge$ 5.

## Rational expressions {#Rational_expressions}

Given two polynomials $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ , their [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640) is called a *rational expression* or simply *[rational fraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_fraction)*.[^o18][^o19][^o20] A rational expression $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ is called *proper* if $\deg P(x) < \deg Q(x)$, and *improper* otherwise. For example, the fraction $\tfrac{2x}{x^2-1}$ is proper, and the fractions $\tfrac{x^3+x^2+1}{x^2-5x+6}$ and $\tfrac{x^2-x+1}{5x^2+3}$ are improper. Any improper rational fraction can be expressed as the sum of a polynomial (possibly constant) and a proper rational fraction. In the first example of an improper fraction one has

$$\frac{x^3+x^2+1}{x^2-5x+6} = (x+6) + \frac{24x-35}{x^2-5x+6},$$

where the second term is a proper rational fraction. The sum of two proper rational fractions is a proper rational fraction as well. The reverse process of expressing a proper rational fraction as the sum of two or more fractions is called resolving it into [partial fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_fraction). For example,

$$\frac{2x}{x^2-1} = \frac{1}{x-1} + \frac{1}{x+1}.$$

Here, the two terms on the right are called partial fractions.

### Irrational fraction {#Irrational_fraction}

An *irrational fraction* is one that contains the variable under a fractional exponent.[^o22] An example of an irrational fraction is

$$\frac{x^{1/2} - \tfrac13 a}{x^{1/3} - x^{1/2}}.$$

The process of transforming an irrational fraction to a rational fraction is known as [rationalization](wikipedia://en/16458571). Every irrational fraction in which the radicals are [monomials](wikipedia://en/357416) may be rationalized by finding the [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961) of the indices of the roots, and substituting the variable for another variable with the least common multiple as exponent. In the example given, the least common multiple is 6, hence we can substitute $x = z^6$ to obtain

$$\frac{z^3 - \tfrac13 a}{z^2 - z^3}.$$
