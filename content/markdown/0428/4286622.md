# Navya-Nyāya

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Navya-Nyāya"}

The **Navya-Nyāya** ([sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit): नव्य-न्याय) or **Neo-Logical** *[darśana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darshana)* (view, system, or school) of [Indian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_logic) and [Indian philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_philosophy) was founded in the 13th century [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era) by the philosopher [Gangeśa Upādhyāya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangesha_Upadhyaya) of [Mithila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithila_%28ancient%29) and continued by [Raghunatha Śiromaṇi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunatha_Śiromaṇi) of [Nabadwipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabadwipa) in [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal).[^o12] It was a development of the classical [Nyāya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyāya) *darśana*. Other influences on Navya-Nyāya were the work of earlier philosophers [Vācaspati Miśra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vācaspati_Miśra) (900–980 CE) and [Udayana](wikipedia://en/10997784) (late 10th century). It remained active in India through to the 18th century.

:::

Gangeśa's book *[Tattvacintāmaṇi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattvacintāmaṇi)* ("Thought-Jewel of Reality") was written partly in response to Śrīharśa's *Khandanakhandakhādya*, a defence of [Advaita Vedānta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta), which had offered a set of thorough criticisms of Nyāya theories of thought and language. In his book, Gangeśa both addressed some of those criticisms and – more important – critically examined the Nyāya *darśana* itself. He held that, while Śrīharśa had failed to successfully challenge the Nyāya realist [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology), his and Gangeśa's own criticisms brought out a need to improve and refine the logical and linguistic tools of Nyāya thought, to make them more rigorous and precise.

*Tattvacintāmani* dealt with all the important aspects of Indian philosophy, [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) and especially [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), which Gangeśa examined rigorously, developing and improving the Nyāya scheme, and offering examples. The results, especially his analysis of [cognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognition), were taken up and used by other *darśanas*.

Navya-Nyāya developed a sophisticated language and conceptual scheme that allowed it to raise, analyze, and solve problems in logic and epistemology. It involves naming each object to be analyzed, identifying a distinguishing characteristic for the named object, and verifying the appropriateness of the defining characteristic using *pramanas*.[^o2] It systematized all the Nyāya concepts into four main categories which are (sense-) perception (*pratyakşa*), inference (*anumāna*), comparison or similarity (*upamāna*), and testimony (sound or word; *[śabda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Śabda)*). Great stalwarts like Basudev Sarvabhauma,[^o3] [Raghunath Shiromani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunatha_Siromani),[^o4] Jagadish Tarkalankar,[^o5] Gadadhar Bhattacharya[^o6] and Mathuranatha Tarkavagisha have contributed further in the development of the subject. Prof [John Vattanky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vattanky) has contributed significantly to the modern understanding of Navya-Nyāya.[^o7]
