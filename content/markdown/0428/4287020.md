# Poisson ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Poisson ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Poisson ring** is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) on which an [anticommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutativity) and [distributive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributivity) [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) $[\cdot,\cdot]$ satisfying the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370) and the [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988) is defined. Such an operation is then known as the [Poisson bracket](wikipedia://en/294340) of the Poisson ring.

:::

Many important operations and results of [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298) and [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319) may be formulated in terms of the Poisson bracket and, hence, apply to [Poisson algebras](wikipedia://en/345704) as well. This observation is important in studying the [classical limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_limit) of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202)—the [non-commutative algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_algebra) of [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) has the Poisson algebra of functions on a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) as a singular limit, and properties of the non-commutative algebra pass over to corresponding properties of the Poisson algebra.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Poisson bracket must satisfy the identities

- $[f,g] = -[g,f]$ (skew symmetry)
- $[f + g, h] = [f,h] + [g,h]$ (distributivity)
- $[fg,h] = f[g,h] + [f,h]g$ ([derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29))
- $[f,[g,h]] + [g,[h,f]] + [h,[f,g]] = 0$ ([Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370))

for all $f,g,h$ in the ring.

A [Poisson algebra](wikipedia://en/345704) is a Poisson ring that is also an [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788). In this case, add the extra requirement

$$[sf,g] = s[f,g]$$

for all scalars *s*.

For each *g* in a Poisson ring *A*, the operation $ad_g$ defined as $ad_g(f) = [f,g]$ is a [derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29). If the set $\{ ad_g | g \in A \}$ generates the set of derivations of *A*, then *A* is said to be **non-degenerate**.

If a non-degenerate Poisson ring is [isomorphic as a commutative ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_isomorphism) to the [algebra of smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_of_smooth_functions) on a manifold *M*, then *M* must be a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356) and $[\cdot,\cdot]$ is the Poisson bracket defined by the symplectic form.
