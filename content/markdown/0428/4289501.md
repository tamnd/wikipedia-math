# Biconjugate gradient method

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biconjugate gradient method"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), more specifically in [numerical linear algebra](wikipedia://en/7330660), the **biconjugate gradient method** is an [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) to solve [systems of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087)

:::

$$A x= b.\,$$

Unlike the [conjugate gradient method](wikipedia://en/1448821), this algorithm does not require the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) $A$ to be [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint), but instead one needs to perform multiplications by the [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) $A^{*}$.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="The Algorithm"}

## The Algorithm {#The_Algorithm}

1. Choose initial guess $x_0\,$, two other vectors $x_0^*$ and $b^*\,$ and a [preconditioner](wikipedia://en/5165246) $M\,$
1. $$r_0 \leftarrow b-A\, x_0\,$$
1. $$r_0^* \leftarrow b^*-x_0^*\, A^*$$
1. $$p_0 \leftarrow M^{-1} r_0\,$$
1. $$p_0^* \leftarrow r_0^*M^{-1}\,$$
1. for $k=0, 1, \ldots$ do
   1. $$\alpha_k \leftarrow {r_k^* M^{-1} r_k \over p_k^* A p_k}\,$$
   1. $$x_{k+1} \leftarrow x_k + \alpha_k \cdot p_k\,$$
   1. $$x_{k+1}^* \leftarrow x_k^* + \overline{\alpha_k}\cdot p_k^*\,$$
   1. $$r_{k+1} \leftarrow r_k - \alpha_k \cdot A p_k\,$$
   1. $$r_{k+1}^* \leftarrow r_k^*- \overline{\alpha_k} \cdot p_k^*\, A^*$$
   1. $$\beta_k \leftarrow {r_{k+1}^* M^{-1} r_{k+1} \over r_k^* M^{-1} r_k}\,$$
   1. $$p_{k+1} \leftarrow M^{-1} r_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot p_k\,$$
   1. $$p_{k+1}^* \leftarrow r_{k+1}^*M^{-1} + \overline{\beta_k}\cdot p_k^*\,$$

In the above formulation, the computed $r_k\,$ and $r_k^*$ satisfy

$$r_k = b - A x_k,\,$$

$$r_k^* = b^* - x_k^*\, A^*$$

and thus are the respective [residuals](wikipedia://en/6773580) corresponding to $x_k\,$ and $x_k^*$, as approximate solutions to the systems

$$A x = b,\,$$

$$x^*\, A^* = b^*\,;$$

$x^*$ is the [adjoint](wikipedia://en/667175), and $\overline{\alpha}$ is the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918).

### Unpreconditioned version of the algorithm {#Unpreconditioned_version_of_the_algorithm}

1. Choose initial guess $x_0\,$,
1. $$r_0 \leftarrow b-A\, x_0\,$$
1. $$\hat{r}_0 \leftarrow \hat{b} - \hat{x}_0A^*$$
1. $$p_0 \leftarrow r_0\,$$
1. $$\hat{p}_0 \leftarrow \hat{r}_0\,$$
1. for $k=0, 1, \ldots$ do
   1. $$\alpha_k \leftarrow {\hat{r}_k r_k \over \hat{p}_k A p_k}\,$$
   1. $$x_{k+1} \leftarrow x_k + \alpha_k \cdot p_k\,$$
   1. $$\hat{x}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{x}_k + \alpha_k \cdot \hat{p}_k\,$$
   1. $$r_{k+1} \leftarrow r_k - \alpha_k \cdot A p_k\,$$
   1. $$\hat{r}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{r}_k- \alpha_k \cdot \hat{p}_k A^*$$
   1. $$\beta_k \leftarrow {\hat{r}_{k+1} r_{k+1} \over \hat{r}_k r_k}\,$$
   1. $$p_{k+1} \leftarrow r_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot p_k\,$$
   1. $$\hat{p}_{k+1} \leftarrow \hat{r}_{k+1} + \beta_k \cdot \hat{p}_k\,$$

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

The biconjugate gradient method is [numerically unstable](wikipedia://en/233807) (compare to the [biconjugate gradient stabilized method](wikipedia://en/25694537)), but very important from a theoretical point of view. Define the iteration steps by

$$x_k:=x_j+ P_k A^{-1}\left(b - A x_j \right),$$

$$x_k^*:= x_j^*+\left(b^*- x_j^* A \right) P_k A^{-1},$$

where $j<k$ using the related [projection](wikipedia://en/519182)

$$P_k:= \mathbf{u}_k \left(\mathbf{v}_k^* A \mathbf{u}_k \right)^{-1} \mathbf{v}_k^* A,$$

with

$$\mathbf{u}_k=\left[u_0, u_1, \dots, u_{k-1} \right],$$

$$\mathbf{v}_k=\left[v_0, v_1, \dots, v_{k-1} \right].$$

These related projections may be iterated themselves as

$$P_{k+1}= P_k+ \left( 1-P_k\right) u_k \otimes {v_k^* A\left(1-P_k \right) \over v_k^* A\left(1-P_k \right) u_k}.$$

A relation to [Quasi-Newton methods](wikipedia://en/7883127) is given by $P_k= A_k^{-1} A$ and $x_{k+1}= x_k- A_{k+1}^{-1}\left(A x_k -b \right)$, where

$$A_{k+1}^{-1}= A_k^{-1}+ \left( 1-A_k^{-1}A\right) u_k \otimes {v_k^* \left(1-A A_k^{-1} \right) \over v_k^* A\left(1-A_k^{-1}A \right) u_k}.$$

The new directions

$$p_k = \left(1-P_k \right) u_k,$$

$$p_k^* = v_k^* A \left(1- P_k \right) A^{-1}$$

are then orthogonal to the residuals:

$$v_i^* r_k= p_i^* r_k=0,$$

$$r_k^* u_j = r_k^* p_j= 0,$$

which themselves satisfy

$$r_k= A \left( 1- P_k \right) A^{-1} r_j,$$

$$r_k^*= r_j^* \left( 1- P_k \right)$$

where $i,j<k$.

The biconjugate gradient method now makes a special choice and uses the setting

$$u_k = M^{-1} r_k,\,$$

$$v_k^* = r_k^* \, M^{-1}.\,$$

With this particular choice, explicit evaluations of $P_k$ and $A^{-1}$ are avoided, and the algorithm takes the form stated above.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $A= A^*\,$ is [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/217548), $x_0^*= x_0$ and $b^*=b$, then $r_k= r_k^*$, $p_k= p_k^*$, and the [conjugate gradient method](wikipedia://en/1448821) produces the same sequence $x_k= x_k^*$ at half the computational cost.
- The sequences produced by the algorithm are [biorthogonal](wikipedia://en/1090253), i.e., $p_i^*Ap_j=r_i^*M^{-1}r_j=0$ for $i \neq j$.
- if $P_{j'}\,$ is a polynomial with $\deg\left(P_{j'}\right)+j<k$, then $r_k^*P_{j'}\left(M^{-1}A\right)u_j=0$. The algorithm thus produces projections onto the [Krylov subspace](wikipedia://en/2542615).
- if $P_{i'}\,$ is a polynomial with $i+\deg\left(P_{i'}\right)<k$, then $v_i^*P_{i'}\left(AM^{-1}\right)r_k=0$.

:::
