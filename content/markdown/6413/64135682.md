# Karen D. King

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Karen D. King"}

**Karen Denise King** (July 6, 1971 – December 24, 2019)[^o3] was an African-American mathematics educator, a program director at [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation), and a 2012 [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer).

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Karen Denise King was born on July 6, 1971, in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.) She was selected for the [National Aeronautics and Space Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Administration) Women in Science and Engineering (WISE) Scholars Program at [Spelman College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spelman_College) as an undergraduate and finished her degree in mathematics [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude) in 3 years. King next attended University of Maryland with a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) fellowship and earned her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in Mathematics Education in 1997. Her dissertation advisor was Patricia F. Campbell.[^o3]

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

King began her career as an assistant professor at [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University) in 1997. In 1999 she moved to [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University). In 2006 King relocated to [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) as an associate professor. While a professor, King focused her research and publications on urban mathematics reform, the mathematical preparation of K-12 teachers, and mathematics professional development policies.[^o6] King became the Director of Research for [National Council of Teachers of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1561691) in 2011 where she co-authored the book Disrupting Tradition: Research and Practice in Mathematics.[^o7] She later became a program director at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) of Education and Human Resources. In 2012 she was honored as an [AWM/MAA Falconer Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWM/MAA_Falconer_Lecturer)[^o8] and served on the writing team that wrote The Mathematical Education of Teachers II.[^o9] King also served as an associate editor for the *[Journal for Research in Mathematics Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_for_Research_in_Mathematics_Education)* from 2001 to 2004.[^o10]

King's work earned her recognition by *[Mathematically Gifted & Black](wikipedia://en/64552006),* where she was featured as a [Black History Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_History_Month) 2020 Honoree.[^o3]

## Memorial Award {#Memorial_Award}

The Association of Mathematics Teacher Educators established an award in Dr. King's memory, the Karen D. King Excellence in Advocacy Award. Given every 4 years, the award recognizes unique contributions in advocating for ideas, plans, or decisions that enhance math teacher preparation.
