# Eristic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eristic"}

In [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447), **eristic** (from *[Eris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eris_%28mythology%29)*, the ancient Greek [goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goddess) of chaos, strife, and discord) refers to an [argument](wikipedia://en/13018310) that aims to successfully dispute another's argument, rather than searching for [truth](wikipedia://en/29920). According to [T.H. Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terence_Irwin), "It is characteristic of the eristic to think of some arguments as a way of defeating the other side, by showing that an opponent must assent to the negation of what he initially took himself to believe."[^o2] Eristic is defined by Rankin as arguing for the sake of conflict, as opposed to resolving conflict.[^o3]

:::

## Use in education {#Use_in_education}

Eristic was a type of "question-and-answer"[^o5] teaching method popularized by the [Sophists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophism), such as [Euthydemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthydemus_of_Chios) and [Dionysiodoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysodorus_%28sophist%29). Students learned eristic arguments to "refute their opponent, no matter whether he \[said\] yes or no in answer to their initial question".[^o6]

[Plato](wikipedia://en/22954) contrasted this type of argument with [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813) and other more [reasonable](wikipedia://en/42446) and [logical](wikipedia://en/46426065) methods (e.g., at [*Republic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republic_%28Plato%29) 454a). In the dialogue [*Euthydemus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthydemus_%28dialogue%29), Plato satirizes eristic. It is more than persuasion, and it is more than discourse. It is a combination that wins an argument without regard to truth. Plato believed that the eristic style "did not constitute a method of argument" because to argue eristically is to consciously use fallacious arguments, which therefore weakens one's position.[^o7]

Unlike Plato, [Isocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isocrates) (often considered a Sophist) did not distinguish eristic from dialectic.[^o8] He held that both lacked a "'useful application' ... that created responsible citizens",[^o9] which unscrupulous teachers used for "enriching themselves at the expense of the youth."[^o10]

## Philosophical eristic {#Philosophical_eristic}

[Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schopenhauer) considers that only logic pursues truth. For him, [dialectic](wikipedia://en/52813), [sophistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophistry), and eristic have no objective truth in view, but only the appearance of it; he believed that they do not seek truth itself but, rather, victory. He names these three last methods as "eristic dialectic (contentious argument)."[^o12]

According to [Schopenhauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schopenhauer), [*Eristic Dialectic*](wikipedia://en/13564651) is mainly concerned to tabulate and analyze dishonest stratagems,[^o13] so that they may at once be recognized and defeated, in order to continue with a productive dialectic debate. It is for this very reason that Eristic Dialectic must admittedly take victory, and not objective truth, for its selfish aim and purpose.

## Argumentation theory {#Argumentation_theory}

[Argumentation theory](wikipedia://en/1317383) is a field of study that asks critical questions about eristic arguments and the other types of dialogue.
