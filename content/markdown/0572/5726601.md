# Family of curves

*Image: Apollonian circles.svg.* The [Apollonian circles](wikipedia://en/5023947), two orthogonal families of circles

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Family of curves"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **family of curves** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of [curves](wikipedia://en/89246), each of which is given by a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) or [parametrization](wikipedia://en/576108) in which one or more of the [parameters](wikipedia://en/25065) is variable. In general, the parameter(s) influence the shape of the curve in a way that is more complicated than a simple [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation). Sets of curves given by an [implicit relation](wikipedia://en/250896) may also represent families of curves.

:::

Families of curves appear frequently in solutions of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309); when an additive [constant of integration](wikipedia://en/147939) is introduced, it will usually be manipulated algebraically until it no longer represents a simple linear transformation.

Families of curves may also arise in other areas. For example, all non-degenerate [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_sections) can be represented using a single [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation) with one parameter, the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472) of the curve:

$$r(\theta) = {l \over 1+e \cos \theta}$$

as the value of $e$ changes, the appearance of the curve varies in a relatively complicated way.

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

Families of curves may arise in various topics in geometry, including the [envelope](wikipedia://en/880235) of a set of curves and the [caustic](wikipedia://en/3498698) of a given curve.

In [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), [neural networks](wikipedia://en/21523) are families of curves with parameters chosen by an [optimization algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_algorithm) e.g. to minimize the value of a loss function on a given training dataset.

:::
