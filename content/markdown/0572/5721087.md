# Ernst Sigismund Fischer

Ernst Sigismund Fischer

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ernst Sigismund Fischer"}

**Ernst Sigismund Fischer** (12 July 1875 – 14 November 1954) was a mathematician born in [Vienna, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna,_Austria). He worked alongside [Franz Mertens](wikipedia://en/41999) and [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) at the universities of [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) and [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), respectively. He later became professor at the [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen), where he worked with [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).

:::

His main area of research was [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), specifically [orthonormal sequences](wikipedia://en/194743) of functions, which laid groundwork for the emergence of the concept of a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).

The [Riesz–Fischer theorem](wikipedia://en/1836188) in [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration) is named in his honour.

He is the grandson of composer [Karl Graedener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Graedener).[^o3][^o4]
