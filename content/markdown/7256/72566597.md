# Nullary relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nullary relation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **nullary relation**, **0-ary relation**, or **relation of degree zero** is a [relation](wikipedia://en/19509) with [zero attributes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullary). There are exactly two relations of degree zero. One has [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) zero; that is, contains no [tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) at all. The other has cardinality 1 and contains only the unique 0-tuple.[^o2]<sup>:56</sup>

:::

The zero-degree relations represent true and false in [relational algebra](wikipedia://en/175285).[^o2]<sup>:57</sup> Under the [closed-world assumption](wikipedia://en/2526582), an *n*-ary relation is interpreted as the [extension](wikipedia://en/39095) of some *n*-adic [predicate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29): all and only those *n*-tuples whose values, substituted for corresponding [free variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) in the predicate, yield [propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) that hold true, appear in the relation. A zero-degree relation is therefore interpreted as the extension of the 0-adic predicate $P() \to \operatorname{true}$. The zero-degree relation with cardinality zero therefore represents false because it contains no tuples that yield a true proposition, and the zero-degree relation with cardinality 1 represents true because it contains the unique 0-tuple that yields a true proposition.

The zero-degree relations are also significant as [identities](wikipedia://en/14962) for certain operators in the relational algebra. The zero-degree relation of cardinality 1 is the identity with respect to [join](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_join) (⋈); that is, when it is joined with any other relation $R$, the result is $R$. Defining an identity with respect to join makes it possible to extend the [binary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_operator) join operator into a *n*-ary join operator.[^o2]<sup>:89</sup>

Since the relational [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/175285#Set_operators) is a special case of join, the zero-degree relation of cardinality 1 is also the identity with respect to the Cartesian product.[^o2]<sup>:89</sup>

A [projection](wikipedia://en/2702833) of a relation over no attributes yields one of the relations of degree zero. If the projected relation has cardinality 0, the projection will have cardinality 0; if the projected relation has positive cardinality, the result will have cardinality 1.

[Hugh Darwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Darwen) refers to the zero-degree relation with cardinality 0 as TABLE\_DUM and the relation with cardinality 1 as TABLE\_DEE, alluding to the characters [Tweedledum and Tweedledee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweedledum_and_Tweedledee).[^o3]
