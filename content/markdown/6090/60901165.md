# Q-Gaussian process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Q-Gaussian process"}

**q-Gaussian processes** are deformations of the usual [Gaussian distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_distribution). There are several different versions of this; here we treat a multivariate deformation, also addressed as q-Gaussian process, arising from [free probability theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_probability_theory) and corresponding to deformations of the [canonical commutation relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_commutation_relations). For other deformations of Gaussian distributions, see [q-Gaussian distribution](wikipedia://en/32185446) and [Gaussian q-distribution](wikipedia://en/25178047).

:::

## History {#History}

The q-Gaussian process was formally introduced in a paper by Frisch and Bourret[^o3] under the name of *parastochastics*, and also later by Greenberg[^o4] as an example of *infinite statistics*. It was mathematically established and investigated in papers by Bozejko and Speicher[^o5] and by Bozejko, Kümmerer, and Speicher[^o6] in the context of non-commutative probability.

It is given as the distribution of sums of creation and annihilation operators in a q-deformed [Fock space](wikipedia://en/230491). The calculation of moments of those operators is given by a q-deformed version of a [Wick formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wick_theorem) or [Isserlis formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isserlis'_theorem). The specification of a special covariance in the underlying [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) leads to the [q-Brownian motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Brownian_motion),[^o6] a special non-commutative version of classical [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436).

## q-Fock space {#q-Fock_space}

In the following $q\in[-1,1]$ is fixed. Consider a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. On the algebraic full Fock space

$$\mathcal{F}_\text{alg}(\mathcal{H})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n},$$

where $\mathcal{H}^0=\mathbb{C}\Omega$ with a norm one vector $\Omega$, called *vacuum*, we define a q-deformed inner product as follows:

$$\langle h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_n,g_1\otimes\cdots\otimes g_m\rangle_q = \delta_{nm}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\prod^n_{r=1}\langle h_r,g_{\sigma(r)}\rangle q^{i(\sigma)},$$

where $i(\sigma)=\#\{(k,\ell)\mid 1\leq k<\ell\leq n; \sigma(k)>\sigma(\ell)\}$ is the number of inversions of $\sigma\in S_n$.

The *q-Fock space*[^o8] is then defined as the completion of the algebraic full Fock space with respect to this inner product

$$\mathcal{F}_q(\mathcal{H})=\overline{\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_q}.$$

For $-1 < q < 1$ the q-inner product is strictly positive.[^o5][^o9] For $q=1$ and $q=-1$ it is positive, but has a kernel, which leads in these cases to the symmetric and anti-symmetric Fock spaces, respectively.

For $h\in\mathcal{H}$ we define the *q-creation operator* $a^*(h)$, given by

$$a^*(h)\Omega=h,\qquad a^*(h)h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_n=h\otimes h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_n.$$

Its adjoint (with respect to the q-inner product), the *q-annihilation operator* $a(h)$, is given by

$$a(h)\Omega=0,\qquad a(h)h_1\otimes\cdots\otimes h_n=\sum_{r=1}^n q^{r-1} \langle h,h_r\rangle h_1\otimes \cdots \otimes h_{r-1}\otimes h_{r+1}\otimes\cdots \otimes h_n.$$

## q-commutation relations {#q-commutation_relations}

Those operators satisfy the q-commutation relations[^o11]

$$a(f)a^*(g)-q a^*(g)a(f)=\langle f,g\rangle \cdot 1\qquad (f,g\in \mathcal{H}).$$

For $q=1$, $q=0$, and $q=-1$ this reduces to the CCR-relations, the Cuntz relations, and the CAR-relations, respectively. With the exception of the case $q=1,$ the operators $a^*(f)$ are bounded.

## q-Gaussian elements and definition of multivariate q-Gaussian distribution (q-Gaussian process) {#q-Gaussian_elements_and_definition_of_multivariate_q-Gaussian_distribution_(q-Gaussian_process)}

Operators of the form $s_q(h)={a(h)+a^*(h)}$ for $h\in\mathcal{H}$ are called *q-Gaussian*[^o8] (or *q-semicircular*[^o13]) elements.

On $\mathcal{F}_q(\mathcal{H})$ we consider the *vacuum expectation state* $\tau(T)=\langle \Omega,T\Omega \rangle$, for $T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H}))$.

The *(multivariate) q-Gaussian distribution* or *q-Gaussian process*[^o6][^o14] is defined as the non commutative distribution of a collection of q-Gaussians with respect to the vacuum expectation state. For $h_1,\dots,h_p\in\mathcal{H}$ the joint distribution of $s_q(h_1),\dots,s_q(h_p)$ with respect to $\tau$ can be described in the following way,:[^o3][^o5] for any $i\{1,\dots,k\}\rightarrow\{1,\dots,p\}$ we have

$$\tau\left(s_q(h_{i(1)})\cdots s_q(h_{i(k)})\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_2(k)} q^{cr(\pi)} \prod_{(r,s)\in\pi} \langle h_{i(r)}, h_{i(s)} \rangle,$$

where $cr(\pi)$ denotes the number of crossings of the pair-partition $\pi$. This is a q-deformed version of the Wick/Isserlis formula.

## q-Gaussian distribution in the one-dimensional case {#q-Gaussian_distribution_in_the_one-dimensional_case}

For *p* = 1, the q-Gaussian distribution is a probability measure on the interval $[-2/\sqrt{1-q}, 2/\sqrt{1-q}]$, with analytic formulas for its density.[^o16] For the special cases $q=1$, $q=0$, and $q=-1$, this reduces to the classical Gaussian distribution, the [Wigner semicircle distribution](wikipedia://en/1028321), and the symmetric [Bernoulli distribution](wikipedia://en/199189) on $\pm 1$. The determination of the density follows from old results[^o17] on corresponding [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718).

## Operator algebraic questions {#Operator_algebraic_questions}

The [von Neumann algebra](wikipedia://en/294185) generated by $s_q(h_i)$, for $h_i$ running through an orthonormal system $(h_i)_{i\in I}$ of vectors in $\mathcal{H}$, reduces for $q=0$ to the famous free group factors $L(F_{\vert I\vert})$. Understanding the structure of those von Neumann algebras for general q has been a source of many investigations.[^o19] It is now known, by work of Guionnet and Shlyakhtenko,[^o20] that at least for finite I and for small values of q, the von Neumann algebra is isomorphic to the corresponding free group factor.
