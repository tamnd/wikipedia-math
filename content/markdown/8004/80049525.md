# Mischievous responders

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mischievous responders"}

**Mischievous responders** are individuals—typically youth—who jokingly provide dishonest and extreme answers to [survey](wikipedia://en/13437523) questions. The impact of mischievous responders on data related to [disparities between heterosexual and sexual minority youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_youth_vulnerability) has been noted, with studies finding that mischievous responders [distort data](wikipedia://en/1051310) to overestimate disparities.[^o2] These distortions of data can sometimes lead to inaccuracies that "substantively affect research, policy, and public discourse regarding a variety of disparities".[^o2] A 2019 [pre-registered study](wikipedia://en/59031980) found data from mischievous responders to account for the entirety of numerous apparent disparities between heterosexuals and sexual minorities, including in [heroin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroin) use and [drunk driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drunk_driving).[^o3] Mischievous responders are more often boys than girls, and tend to falsely report being [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian), [gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_men), [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexuality) or [queer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queer).[^o3] A 2006 study found that 99% of young respondents who'd claimed in a survey to have used an [artificial limb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_limbs) were lying, as determined in follow-up interviews.[^o4][^o5] Research suggests that mischievous responders are "ubiquitous in [adolescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence) research using [self-administered questionnaires](wikipedia://en/4144627)".[^o2] Methods for identifying and accounting for data from mischievous responders include the use of boosted regressions, and some surveys include questions with impossible answers to catch dishonest respondents. Others simply ask respondents if they had been honest.[^o5][^o6] The term "mischievous responders" was coined in 2011 by Joseph P. Robinson-Cimpian.[^o5]
