# Beurling algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Beurling algebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the term **Beurling algebra** is used for various different algebras introduced by (). Usually it is an algebra of [periodic functions](wikipedia://en/76848) with [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038)

:::

$$f(x) = \sum a_n e^{inx}$$

::: {#o3 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example** We may consider the algebra of those functions *f* where the [majorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorant)

$$c_k = \sup_{|n| \ge k} |a_n|$$

of the Fourier coefficients *a*<sub>*n*</sub> are summable. In other words

$$\sum_{k \ge 0} c_k < \infty.$$

:::

::: {#o4 .example cue=inline-head name="Example"}

**Example** We may consider a weight function *w* on $\mathbb{Z}$ such that

$$w(m+n) \leq w(m)w(n),\quad w(0)=1,$$

in which case $A_w(\mathbb{T}) = \{f : f(t) = \sum_n a_n e^{int},\,\|f\|_w = \sum_n|a_n|w(n) < \infty\} \,(\sim\ell^1_w(\mathbb{Z}))$ is a [unitary](wikipedia://en/173993) [commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative) [Banach algebra](wikipedia://en/4665).

These algebras are closely related to the [Wiener algebra](wikipedia://en/27489616).

:::
