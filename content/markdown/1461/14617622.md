# Pugh's closing lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pugh's closing lemma"}

In the mathematical field of [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), **Pugh's closing lemma** is a result that establishes a close relationship between [chaotic behavior](wikipedia://en/6295) and [periodic behavior](wikipedia://en/252250). Broadly, the lemma states that any point that is "nonwandering" within a system can be turned into a periodic (or repeating) point by making a very small, carefully chosen change to the system's rules.[^o2]

:::

This has significant implications. For example, it means that if a set of conditions on a bounded, continuous dynamical system rules out periodic orbits, that system cannot behave chaotically. This principle is the basis of some [autonomous convergence theorems](wikipedia://en/14469299).

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Formal statement"}

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Let $f:M \to M$ be a $C^1$ [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564) of a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [smooth manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) $M$. Given a [nonwandering point](wikipedia://en/2870010) $x$ of $f$, there exists a diffeomorphism $g$ arbitrarily close to $f$ in the $C^1$ [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of $\operatorname{Diff}^1(M)$ such that $x$ is a [periodic point](wikipedia://en/4941621) of $g$.[^o5]

:::
