# Product-form solution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Product-form solution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **product-form solution** is a particularly efficient form of solution for determining some metric of a system with distinct sub-components, where the metric for the collection of components can be written as a [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of the metric across the different components. Using [capital Pi notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Pi_notation) a product-form solution has algebraic form

:::

$$\text{P}(x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots,x_n) = B \prod_{i=1}^n \text{P}(x_i)$$

where *B* is some constant. Solutions of this form are of interest as they are computationally inexpensive to evaluate for large values of *n*. Such solutions in queueing networks are important for finding [performance metrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_metric) in models of multiprogrammed and time-shared computer systems.

## Equilibrium distributions {#Equilibrium_distributions}

The first product-form solutions were found for [equilibrium distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrium_distribution) of [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876). Trivially, models composed of two or more [independent](wikipedia://en/27593#Independent_random_variables) sub-components exhibit a product-form solution by the definition of independence. Initially the term was used in [queueing networks](wikipedia://en/50578#Queueing_networks) where the sub-components would be individual queues. For example, [Jackson's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson's_theorem_%28queueing_theory%29) gives the joint equilibrium distribution of an open queueing network as the product of the equilibrium distributions of the individual queues.[^o3] After numerous extensions, chiefly the [BCMP network](wikipedia://en/15373909) it was thought [local balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_balance) was a requirement for a product-form solution.[^o4][^o5]

[Gelenbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erol_Gelenbe)'s [G-network](wikipedia://en/10046651) model was the first to show that this is not the case. Motivated by the need to model biological neurons which have a point-process like spiking behaviour, he introduced the precursor of G-Networks, calling it the [random neural network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_neural_network).[^o6] By introducing "negative customers" which can destroy or eliminate other customers, he generalised the family of product form networks.[^o7] Then this was further extended in several steps, first by Gelenbe's "triggers" which are customers which have the power of moving other customers from some queue to another.[^o8] Another new form of customer that also led to product form was Gelenbe's "batch removal".[^o9] This was further extended by Erol Gelenbe and Jean-Michel Fourneau with customer types called "resets" which can model the repair of failures: when a queue hits the empty state, representing (for instance) a failure, the queue length can jump back or be "reset" to its steady-state distribution by an arriving reset customer, representing a repair. All these previous types of customers in G-Networks can exist in the same network, including with multiple classes, and they all together still result in the product form solution, taking us far beyond the reversible networks that had been considered before.[^o10]

Product-form solutions are sometimes described as "stations are independent in equilibrium".[^o11] Product form solutions also exist in networks of [bulk queues](wikipedia://en/36484704).[^o12]

[J.M. Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Michael_Harrison) and [R.J. Williams](wikipedia://en/44832164) note that "virtually all of the models that have been successfully analyzed in classical queueing network theory are models having a so-called product-form stationary distribution"[^o11] More recently, product-form solutions have been published for Markov process algebras (e.g. [RCAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCAT) in [PEPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEPA)[^o13][^o14]) and [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) [petri nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petri_nets).[^o15][^o16] [Martin Feinberg](wikipedia://en/26551450)'s deficiency zero theorem gives a sufficient condition for [chemical reaction networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction_network) to exhibit a product-form stationary distribution.[^o17]

The work by Gelenbe also shows that product form G-Networks can be used to model spiking [random neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_neural_network), and furthermore that such networks can be used to approximate bounded and continuous real-valued functions.[^o18][^o19]

## Sojourn time distributions {#Sojourn_time_distributions}

The term *product form* has also been used to refer to the sojourn time distribution in a cyclic queueing system, where the time spent by jobs at *M* nodes is given as the product of time spent at each node.[^o21] In 1957 Reich showed the result for two [M/M/1 queues](wikipedia://en/14032611) in tandem,[^o22] later extending this to *n* M/M/1 queues in tandem[^o23] and it has been shown to apply to overtake–free paths in [Jackson networks](wikipedia://en/3374374).[^o24] Walrand and Varaiya suggest that non-overtaking (where customers cannot overtake other customers by taking a different route through the network) may be a necessary condition for the result to hold.[^o24] Mitrani offers exact solutions to some simple networks with overtaking, showing that none of these exhibit product-form sojourn time distributions.[^o25]

For closed networks, Chow showed a result to hold for two service nodes,[^o26] which was later generalised to a cycle of queues[^o27] and to overtake–free paths in [Gordon–Newell networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon–Newell_network).[^o28][^o29]

## Extensions {#Extensions}

- Approximate product-form solutions are computed assuming independent marginal distributions, which can give a good approximation to the stationary distribution under some conditions.[^o32][^o33]
- Semi-product-form solutions are solutions where a distribution can be written as a product where terms have a limited functional dependency on the global state space, which can be approximated.[^o34]
- Quasi-product-form solutions are either
  - solutions which are not the product of marginal densities, but the marginal densities describe the distribution in a product-type manner[^o35] or
  - approximate form for transient probability distributions which allows transient moments to be approximated.[^o36]
