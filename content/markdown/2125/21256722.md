# Gordon–Newell theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gordon–Newell theorem"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), the **Gordon–Newell theorem** is an extension of [Jackson's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson's_theorem_%28queueing_theory%29) from open queueing networks to closed queueing networks of exponential servers where customers cannot leave the network.[^o2] Jackson's theorem cannot be applied to closed networks because the queue length at a node in the closed network is limited by the population of the network. The Gordon–Newell theorem calculates the open network solution and then eliminates the infeasible states by renormalizing the probabilities. Calculation of the [normalizing constant](wikipedia://en/398189) makes the treatment more awkward as the whole state space must be enumerated. [Buzen's algorithm](wikipedia://en/4676700) or [mean value analysis](wikipedia://en/22440733) can be used to calculate the normalizing constant more efficiently.[^o3]

:::

## Definition of a Gordon–Newell network {#Definition_of_a_Gordon–Newell_network}

A network of *m* interconnected queues is known as a **Gordon–Newell network**[^o5] or **closed Jackson network**[^o6] if it meets the following conditions:

1. the network is closed (no customers can enter or leave the network),
1. all service times are exponentially distributed and the service discipline at all queues is [FCFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FCFS),
1. a customer completing service at queue *i* will move to queue *j* with probability $P_{ij}$, with the $P_{ij}$ such that $\sum_{j =1}^m P_{ij} = 1$,
1. the utilization of all of the queues is less than one.

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

In a closed Gordon–Newell network of *m* queues, with a total population of *K* individuals, write $\scriptstyle{(k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_m)}$ (where *k*<sub>*i*</sub> is the length of queue *i*) for the state of the network and *S*(*K*, *m*) for the state space

$$S(K,m) = \left\{ \mathbf{k} \in \mathbb{N}^m \text{ such that } \sum_{i=1}^m k_i = K \right\}.$$

Then the equilibrium state probability distribution exists and is given by

$$\pi (k_1,k_2,\ldots,k_m) = \frac{1}{G(K)} \prod_{i=1}^m \left( \frac{e_i}{\mu_i} \right)^{k_i}$$

where service times at queue *i* are exponentially distributed with parameter *μ<sub>i</sub>*. The normalizing constant *G*(*K*) is given by

$$G(K) = \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in S(K,m)} \prod_{i=1}^{m} \left( \frac{e_i}{\mu_i} \right)^{k_i} ,$$

and *e*<sub>*i*</sub> is the visit ratio, calculated by solving the simultaneous equations

$$e_i = \sum_{j=1}^m e_j p_{ji} \text{ for }1 \leq i \leq m.$$

:::
