# Jerrold B. Tunnell

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jerrold B. Tunnell"}

**Jerrold Bates Tunnell** (September 16, 1950 – April 1, 2022) was a mathematician known for his work in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). He was an associate professor of mathematics at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Tunnell was born on September 16, 1950, in [Dallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas), [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas).[^o17]

He graduated from [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College) in 1972.[^o4][^o18] He received his PhD in Mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1977.[^o5][^o18] His thesis, *On the Local Langlands Conjecture for GL(2)*, was advised by [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

After graduation, Tunnell taught at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) and was a member of the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1982 to 1983.[^o5][^o18] He joined the mathematics faculty at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in 1983,[^o18] eventually becoming an associate professor of mathematics.[^o8] He advised 7 PhD students.[^o18]

## Research {#Research}

In 1981, Tunnell generalized Langlands' work on the [Artin conjecture](wikipedia://en/2774171#The_Artin_conjecture), establishing a special case known as the Langlands–Tunnell theorem that later became a key component in the [proof of Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/21950759).[^o10][^o11]

He proved [Tunnell's theorem](wikipedia://en/6144888) in 1983, which gives a partial unconditional solution to the [congruent number problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congruent_number_problem) and a complete solution conditional on the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161).[^o12]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2013, Tunnell was elected in the inaugural class of [Fellows of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society).[^o14]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Starting in 2004, Tunnell made cross-country cycling trips from [Highland Park, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_Park,_New_Jersey), to [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York), in every U.S. election cycle.[^o16]

Tunnell died on April 1, 2022, in rural Texas.[^o17][^o18] He was hit by a truck while riding his bicycle from [St. Augustine, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida), to his 50th class reunion at [Harvey Mudd College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Mudd_College) in [Claremont, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claremont,_California).[^o18]
