# Kirigami

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kirigami"}

is a variation of [origami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origami), the [Japanese art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Japan) of folding paper. In ja, the paper is cut as well as being folded, resulting in a [three-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-dimensional) design that stands away from the page. ja typically does not use glue.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

In the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), the term ja was coined by [Florence Temko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Temko) from [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language) ja, cut, and ja, paper, in the title of her 1962 book, ja*, the Creative Art of Paper cutting*. The book achieved enough success that the word ja was accepted as the Western name for the art of paper cutting.[^o3]

Typically, ja starts with a folded base, which is then unfolded; cuts are then opened and flattened to make the finished design. Simple ja are usually [symmetrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetrical), such as [snowflakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_snowflake), [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544), or [orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid) blossoms. A difference between ja and the art of "full base", or 180-degree opening structures, is that ja is made out of a single piece of paper that has then been cut.

## Notable ja artists {#Notable_artists}

- Seiji Fujishiro (born 1924–), a renowned ja (paper picture) artist known for his colourful ja, which have also been published as a book.
- [Nahoko Kojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahoko_Kojima) (born 1981–), a professional contemporary Japanese ja artist, who pioneered sculptural, three-dimensional ja.
- [Giovanni Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Russo_%28artist%29) (born 1969–), a professional contemporary Italian it artist, who pioneered monumental, three-dimensional postcards it.
