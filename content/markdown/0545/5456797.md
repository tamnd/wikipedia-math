# Method of moments (probability theory)

This article is about the method of moments in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542). See [method of moments (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_moments_%28disambiguation%29) for other techniques bearing the same name.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Method of moments"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), the **method of moments** is a way of proving [convergence in distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergence_in_distribution) by proving convergence of a sequence of [moment](wikipedia://en/368684) sequences.[^o2] Suppose *X* is a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) and that all of the moments

:::

$$\operatorname{E}(X^k)\,$$

exist. Further suppose the [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of *X* is completely determined by its moments, i.e., there is no other probability distribution with the same sequence of moments (cf. the [problem of moments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_of_moments)). If

$$\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{E}(X_n^k) = \operatorname{E}(X^k)\,$$

for all values of *k*, then the sequence {*X*<sub>*n*</sub>} converges to *X* in distribution.

The method of moments was first introduced in 1891 by [Pafnuty Chebyshev](wikipedia://en/149568) for proving the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406); although his proof is considered incomplete.[^o3][^o4] Chebyshev cited earlier contributions by [Irénée-Jules Bienaymé](wikipedia://en/16087441).[^o4] More recently, the method has been applied by [Eugene Wigner](wikipedia://en/10101) to prove [Wigner's semicircle law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner's_semicircle_law), and has since found numerous applications in the [theory of random matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory).[^o5]
