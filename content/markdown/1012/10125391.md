# Mapping cone (homological algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mapping cone"}

In [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), the **mapping cone** is a construction on a map of [chain complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_complexes) inspired by the [analogous construction in topology](wikipedia://en/1707756). In the theory of [triangulated categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangulated_categories) it is a kind of combined [kernel](wikipedia://en/142616) and [cokernel](wikipedia://en/279715): if the chain complexes take their terms in an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), so that we can talk about [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060), then the cone of a map *f* being [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_complex) means that the map is a [quasi-isomorphism](wikipedia://en/3653039); if we pass to the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of complexes, this means that *f* is an isomorphism there, which recalls the familiar property of maps of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447), [modules over a ring](wikipedia://en/276410), or elements of an arbitrary abelian category that if the kernel and cokernel both vanish, then the map is an isomorphism. If we are working in a [t-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-category), then in fact the cone furnishes both the kernel and cokernel of maps between objects of its core.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The cone may be defined in the category of [cochain complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochain_complexes) over any [additive category](wikipedia://en/60854) (i.e., a category whose morphisms form abelian groups and in which we may construct a [direct sum](wikipedia://en/2084687) of any two objects). Let $A, B$ be two complexes, with differentials $d_A, d_B;$ i.e.,

$$A = \dots \to A^{n - 1} \xrightarrow{d_A^{n - 1}} A^n \xrightarrow{d_A^n} A^{n + 1} \to \cdots$$

and likewise for $B.$

For a map of complexes $f : A \to B,$ we define the cone, often denoted by $\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ or $C(f),$ to be the following complex:

$C(f) = A[1] \oplus B = \dots \to A^n \oplus B^{n - 1} \to A^{n + 1} \oplus B^n \to A^{n + 2} \oplus B^{n + 1} \to \cdots$ on terms,

with differential

$d_{C(f)} = \begin{pmatrix} d_{A[1]} & 0 \\ f[1] & d_B \end{pmatrix}$ (acting as though on [column vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_vector)).

Here $A[1]$ is the complex with $A[1]^n=A^{n + 1}$ and $d^n_{A[1]}=-d^{n + 1}_{A}$. Note that the differential on $C(f)$ is different from the natural differential on $A[1] \oplus B$, and that some authors use a different sign convention.

Thus, if for example our complexes are of abelian groups, the differential would act as

$$\begin{array}{ccl} d^n_{C(f)}(a^{n + 1}, b^n) &=& \begin{pmatrix} d^n_{A[1]} & 0 \\ f[1]^n & d^n_B \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a^{n + 1} \\ b^n \end{pmatrix} \\ &=& \begin{pmatrix} - d^{n + 1}_A & 0 \\ f^{n + 1} & d^n_B \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a^{n + 1} \\ b^n \end{pmatrix} \\ &=& \begin{pmatrix} - d^{n + 1}_A (a^{n + 1}) \\ f^{n + 1}(a^{n + 1}) + d^n_B(b^n) \end{pmatrix}\\ &=& \left(- d^{n + 1}_A (a^{n + 1}), f^{n + 1}(a^{n + 1}) + d^n_B(b^n)\right). \end{array}$$

## Properties {#Properties}

Suppose now that we are working over an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), so that the [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) of a complex is defined. The main use of the cone is to identify [quasi-isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/3653039): if the cone is [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_complex), then the map is a quasi-isomorphism. To see this, we use the existence of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/1679700#Definition)

$$A \xrightarrow{f} B \to C(f) \to A[1]$$

where the maps $B \to C(f), C(f) \to A[1]$ are given by the direct summands (see [Homotopy category of chain complexes](wikipedia://en/10043801)). Since this is a triangle, it gives rise to a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence) on [homology groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group):

$$\dots \to H_{i - 1}(C(f)) \to H_i(A) \xrightarrow{f^*} H_i(B) \to H_i(C(f)) \to \cdots$$

and if $C(f)$ is acyclic then by definition, the outer terms above are zero. Since the sequence is exact, this means that $f^*$ induces an isomorphism on all homology groups, and hence (again by definition) is a quasi-isomorphism.

This fact recalls the usual alternative characterization of isomorphisms in an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) as those maps whose kernel and cokernel both vanish. This appearance of a cone as a combined kernel and cokernel is not accidental; in fact, under certain circumstances the cone literally embodies both. Say for example that we are working over an abelian category and $A, B$ have only one nonzero term in degree 0:

$$A = \dots \to 0 \to A_0 \to 0 \to \cdots,$$

$$B = \dots \to 0 \to B_0 \to 0 \to \cdots,$$

and therefore $f \colon A \to B$ is just $f_0 \colon A_0 \to B_0$ (as a map of objects of the underlying abelian category). Then the cone is just

$$C(f) = \dots \to 0 \to \underset{[-1]}{A_0} \xrightarrow{f_0} \underset{[0]}{B_0} \to 0 \to \cdots.$$

(Underset text indicates the degree of each term.) The homology of this complex is then

$$H_{-1}(C(f)) = \operatorname{ker}(f_0),$$

$$H_0(C(f)) = \operatorname{coker}(f_0),$$

$$H_i(C(f)) = 0 \text{ for } i \neq -1, 0.\$$

This is not an accident and in fact occurs in every [t-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-category).

:::

## Mapping cylinder {#Mapping_cylinder}

A related notion is the **mapping cylinder**: let $f\colon A \to B$ be a morphism of chain complexes, let further $g \colon \operatorname{Cone}(f)[-1] \to A$ be the natural map. The mapping cylinder of *f* is by definition the mapping cone of *g*.

## Topological inspiration {#Topological_inspiration}

This complex is called the cone in analogy to the [mapping cone (topology)](wikipedia://en/1707756) of a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) $\phi : X \rightarrow Y$: the complex of [singular chains](wikipedia://en/431041) of the topological cone $cone(\phi)$ is homotopy equivalent to the cone (in the chain-complex-sense) of the induced map of singular chains of *X* to *Y*. The mapping cylinder of a map of complexes is similarly related to the [mapping cylinder](wikipedia://en/1663142) of continuous maps.
