# Rupture field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rupture field"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **rupture field** of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) $P(X)$ over a given [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$ is a [field extension](wikipedia://en/11634) of $K$ generated by a [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) $a$ of $P(X)$.[^o2]

:::

For instance, if $K=\mathbb Q$ and $P(X)=X^3-2$ then $\mathbb Q[\sqrt[3]2]$ is a rupture field for $P(X)$.

The notion is interesting mainly if $P(X)$ is [irreducible](wikipedia://en/188725) over $K$. In that case, all rupture fields of $P(X)$ over $K$ are [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic), non-canonically, to $K_P=K[X]/(P(X))$: if $L=K[a]$ where $a$ is a root of $P(X)$, then the [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) $f$ defined by $f(k)=k$ for all $k\in K$ and $f(X\bmod P)=a$ is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). Also, in this case the [degree](wikipedia://en/4382697) of the extension equals the [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) of $P$.

A rupture field of a polynomial does not necessarily contain all the roots of that polynomial: in the above example the field $\mathbb Q[\sqrt[3]2]$ does not contain the other two ([complex](wikipedia://en/5826)) roots of $P(X)$ (namely $\omega\sqrt[3]2$ and $\omega^2\sqrt[3]2$ where $\omega$ is a [primitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_root_of_unity) [cube root of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_root_of_unity)). For a field containing all the roots of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000), see [Splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440).

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

A rupture field of $X^2+1$ over $\mathbb R$ is $\mathbb C$. It is also a [splitting field](wikipedia://en/174440).

The rupture field of $X^2+1$ over $\mathbb F_3$ is $\mathbb F_9$ since there is no element of $\mathbb F_3$ which [squares](wikipedia://en/659942) to $-1$ (and all [quadratic extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_extension) of $\mathbb F_3$ are isomorphic to $\mathbb F_9$).

:::
