# Chow–Rashevsky theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chow–Rashevsky theorem"}

In [sub-Riemannian geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Riemannian_geometry), the **Chow–Rashevsky theorem** (also known as **Chow's theorem**) asserts that any two points of a connected [sub-Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/943637), endowed with a [bracket generating distribution](wikipedia://en/2976052#Bracket-generating_distributions), are connected by a horizontal path in the manifold. It is named after [Wei-Liang Chow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei-Liang_Chow) who proved it in 1939, and Pyotr Rashevsky, who proved it independently in 1938.

:::

The theorem has a number of equivalent statements, one of which is that the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) induced by the [Carnot–Carathéodory metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnot–Carathéodory_metric) is equivalent to the intrinsic (locally Euclidean) topology of the manifold. A stronger statement that implies the theorem is the [ball&ndash;box theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball&ndash;box_theorem). See, for instance, (Montgomery 2006) and (Gromov 1996).
