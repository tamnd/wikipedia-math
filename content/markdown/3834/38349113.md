# Nathan Keyfitz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nathan Keyfitz"}

**Nathan Keyfitz** [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) [FRSS](wikipedia://en/552664) (June 29, 1913 – April 6, 2010) was a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) [demographer](wikipedia://en/45642), a pioneer of mathematical demography.[^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7]

:::

## Professional career {#Professional_career}

Keyfitz studied at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), graduating with a B.S. in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1934.[^o3][^o4][^o6] He worked for the [Dominion Bureau of Statistics](wikipedia://en/186760) in Canada from 1936 to 1959, meanwhile earning a Ph.D. in [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1952.[^o4][^o6] In 1959, he took a professorship at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto); he moved from there to the University of Chicago and the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) before joining the faculty of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as Andelot Professor of Sociology in 1972. From 1978 to 1980 he served as Director of the [Harvard Center for Population and Development Studies](http://www.hsph.harvard.edu/cpds). He retired from Harvard in 1981, only to take another faculty position at [Ohio State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_University) and then to direct the Population Program at the [International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Applied_Systems_Analysis) in Austria from 1983 to 1993.[^o3][^o4][^o6] He also consulted frequently in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia) over a period of many years.[^o4]

He was president of the [Population Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Association_of_America) from 1970 to 1971.[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Keyfitz was a fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada), the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664), the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988), and the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), and a member of the [U.S. National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o3][^o4][^o6][^o11] He was given seven honorary doctorates,[^o6] won the Mindel C. Sheps Award of the Population Association of America in 1976,[^o4][^o10] and won the Common Wealth Award of Distinguished Service in 1991.[^o11]

In June 2013, a symposium on mathematical demography was held in honor of the centennial of his birth.[^o12]
