# Daniel Dugué

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Dugué"}

**Daniel Dugué** was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) specializing in [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685). He was born on 22 September 1912 in [Saint-Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal) in [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal) and died on 10 September 1987 in [Paris, France](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

After finishing high-school studies in [Bordeaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordeaux), Daniel Dugué was admitted to [ENS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure) and with a degree agrégation de mathématiques when he was 21 years old, in 1933. He defended the doctoral dissertation in mathematics when he was 25 years old under the supervision of [Georges Darmois](wikipedia://en/28719892) and defended it before [Émile Borel](wikipedia://en/356161) and [Arnaud Denjoy](wikipedia://en/1485520).[^o5] In the course of his thesis, Dugué proves several theorems in the theory of the maximum likelihood estimation combining results and tools from probability theory, such as those of [Khinchin](wikipedia://en/1029748), [Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161), and [Doob](wikipedia://en/1547599) with [Fisher's](wikipedia://en/140824) theory of the [maximum likelihood estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimator).[^o6] In 1937, [Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824) invites Dugué to work with him in London, and Dugué spends two years as a Rockefeller fellow in London.

He subsequently contributed to the development of the rigorous theory of the [maximum likelihood estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimator).[^o7] He also worked with [Yuri Linnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Linnik) on the decomposition of probability distributions.

Dugué succeeded [Georges Darmois](wikipedia://en/28719892) as a director of the [Paris Institute of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Statistics) in 1960, leading it until his retirement in 1981.[^o8] He was married to Lucie Canaud and had four children, Catherine, Élisabeth, David, and Marc. He died from illness in 1987.

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

- *Application des propriétés de la limite au sens du calcul des probabilités à l’étude de diverses questions d’estimation*, Thesis, Faculté des sciences de Paris, 1937.
- *Analycité et convexité des fonctions caractéristiques*, in: *Généralisations de la loi de probabilité de [Laplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Simon_de_Laplace)*, 56 pages, Paris, [Institut Henri-Poincaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Henri-Poincaré), 1951.
- *Arithmétique des lois de probabilités*, 50 pages, Paris, [Gauthier-Villars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauthier-Villars), 1957.
- *Fonctions connexes de [Polya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Polya)*, avec Maurice Girault, 302 pages, Paris, Institut Henri-Poincaré, 1957.
- *Statistique et psychologie*, 4 fascicules de 48, 52, 25 et 38 pages, Paris, Institut Henri-Poincaré, 1957.
- *Sur certains exemples de décomposition en arithmétique des lois de probabilité*, in: *L'ennuple projectif et l'unification de théories de l'électromagnétisme de [Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) et de [Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen)-Hoffmann*, 39 pages, Paris, Institut Henri-Poincaré, 1951.
- *Sur la convergence presque complète des moyennes de variables aléatoires*, 273 pages, Paris, [Institut de statistique de l'université de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_statistique_de_l'université_de_Paris), 1957.
- *Algèbres de [Boole](wikipedia://en/38298), avec une introduction à la théorie algébrique des graphes orientés et aux sous-ensembles flous*, par Michel Serfati, préface de Daniel Dugué, 183 pages, Paris, Centre de documentation universitaire, 1974.
- *Probabilités et statistiques en recherche scientifique*, par Alex Rosengard, préface de Daniel Dugué, 311 pages, Paris, [Dunod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunod), 1972.
