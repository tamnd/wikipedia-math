# DF-space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="DF-space"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), **DF-spaces**, also written **(*DF*)-spaces** are [locally convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_convex) [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752) having a property that is shared by locally convex [metrizable topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/64278078). They play a considerable part in the theory of topological tensor products.[^n1]

:::

DF-spaces were first defined by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) and studied in detail by him in (Grothendieck 1954). Grothendieck was led to introduce these spaces by the following property of strong duals of metrizable spaces: If $X$ is a [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) locally convex space and $V_1, V_2, \ldots$ is a sequence of convex 0-neighborhoods in $X^{\prime}_b$ such that $V := \cap_{i} V_i$ absorbs every strongly bounded set, then $V$ is a 0-neighborhood in $X^{\prime}_b$ (where $X^{\prime}_b$ is the continuous dual space of $X$ endowed with the strong dual topology).[^n2]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) (TVS) $X$ is a **DF-space**, also written **(*DF*)-space**, if[^n1]

1. $X$ is a [countably quasi-barrelled space](wikipedia://en/64267707) (i.e. every strongly bounded countable union of equicontinuous subsets of $X^{\prime}$ is equicontinuous), and
1. $X$ possesses a fundamental sequence of bounded (i.e. there exists a countable sequence of bounded subsets $B_1, B_2, \ldots$ such that every bounded subset of $X$ is contained in some $B_i$[^n3]).

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Let $X$ be a DF-space and let $V$ be a convex balanced subset of $X.$ Then $V$ is a neighborhood of the origin if and only if for every convex, balanced, bounded subset $B \subseteq X,$ $B \cap V$ is a neighborhood of the origin in $B.$[^n1] Consequently, a linear map from a DF-space into a locally convex space is continuous if its restriction to each bounded subset of the domain is continuous.[^n1] The [strong dual space](wikipedia://en/63951372) of a DF-space is a [Fréchet space](wikipedia://en/233801).[^n4] Every infinite-dimensional [Montel](wikipedia://en/1796319) DF-space is a [sequential space](wikipedia://en/4668395) but not a [Fréchet–Urysohn space](wikipedia://en/28480560). Suppose $X$ is either a DF-space or an [LM-space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LM-space). If $X$ is a [sequential space](wikipedia://en/4668395) then it is either [metrizable](wikipedia://en/64278078) or else a [Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319) DF-space. Every [quasi-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-complete) DF-space is complete.[^n5] If $X$ is a [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856) [nuclear](wikipedia://en/2702957) DF-space then $X$ is a [Montel space](wikipedia://en/1796319).[^n6]

:::

## Sufficient conditions {#Sufficient_conditions}

The [strong dual space](wikipedia://en/63951372) $X_b^{\prime}$ of a Fréchet space $X$ is a DF-space.[^o17]

The strong dual of a metrizable locally convex space is a DF-space[^n7] but the convers is in general not true[^n7] (the converse being the statement that every DF-space is the strong dual of some metrizable locally convex space). From this it follows:

Every normed space is a DF-space.[^n8]

Every Banach space is a DF-space.[^n1]

Every [infrabarreled space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrabarreled_space) possessing a fundamental sequence of bounded sets is a DF-space. Every Hausdorff quotient of a DF-space is a DF-space.[^n9] The [completion](wikipedia://en/53695856) of a DF-space is a DF-space.[^n9] The locally convex sum of a sequence of DF-spaces is a DF-space.[^n9] An inductive limit of a sequence of DF-spaces is a DF-space.[^n9] Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are DF-spaces. Then the [projective tensor product](wikipedia://en/63566617), as well as its completion, of these spaces is a DF-space.[^n6]

However,

An infinite product of non-trivial DF-spaces (i.e. all factors have non-0 dimension) is not a DF-space.[^n9] A closed vector subspace of a DF-space is not necessarily a DF-space.[^n9] There exist complete DF-spaces that are not TVS-isomorphic to the strong dual of a metrizable locally convex TVS.[^n9]

::: {#o31 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

There exist complete DF-spaces that are not TVS-isomorphic with the strong dual of a metrizable locally convex space.[^n9] There exist DF-spaces having closed vector subspaces that are not DF-spaces.[^n10]
