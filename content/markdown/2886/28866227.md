# Sumner's conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sumner's conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does every $(2n-2)$-vertex tournament contain as a subgraph every $n$-vertex oriented tree?

:::

*Image: Polytrees in a tournament.svg.* A 6-vertex tournament, and copies of every 4-vertex oriented tree within it.

**Sumner's conjecture** (also called **Sumner's universal tournament conjecture**) is a conjecture in [extremal graph theory](wikipedia://en/529568) on oriented [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) in [tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502). It states that every [orientation](wikipedia://en/36619818) of every $n$-vertex [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) is a [subgraph](wikipedia://en/325802#Subgraphs) of every $(2n-2)$-vertex tournament.[^o3] [David Sumner](wikipedia://en/47676305), a [graph theorist](wikipedia://en/12401) at the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina), [conjectured](wikipedia://en/6138) in 1971 that [tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502) are [universal graphs](wikipedia://en/1452141) for [polytrees](wikipedia://en/1731488). The conjecture was proven for all large $n$ by [Daniela Kühn](wikipedia://en/37003499), Richard Mycroft, and [Deryk Osthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deryk_Osthus).[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Let polytree $P$ be a [star](wikipedia://en/19310129) $K_{1,n-1}$, in which all edges are oriented outward from the central vertex to the leaves. Then, $P$ cannot be embedded in the tournament formed from the vertices of a regular $2n-3$-gon by directing every edge clockwise around the polygon. For, in this tournament, every vertex has indegree and outdegree equal to $n-2$, while the central vertex in $P$ has larger outdegree $n-1$.[^o7] Thus, if true, Sumner's conjecture would give the best possible size of a universal graph for polytrees.

However, in every tournament of $2n-2$ vertices, the average outdegree is $n-\frac{3}{2}$, and the maximum outdegree is an integer greater than or equal to the average. Therefore, there exists a vertex of outdegree $\left\lceil n-\frac{3}{2}\right\rceil=n-1$, which can be used as the central vertex for a copy of $P$.

:::

## Partial results {#Partial_results}

The following partial results on the conjecture have been proven.

- There is a function $f(n)$ with asymptotic growth rate $f(n)=2n+o(n)$ with the property that every $n$-vertex polytree can be embedded as a subgraph of every $f(n)$-vertex tournament. Additionally and more explicitly, $f(n)\le 3n-3$.[^o9]
- There is a function $g(k)$ such that tournaments on $n+g(k)$ vertices are universal for polytrees with $k$ leaves.[^o10]
- There is a function $h(n,\Delta)$ such that every $n$-vertex polytree with maximum degree at most $\Delta$ forms a subgraph of every tournament with $h(n,\Delta)$ vertices. When $\Delta$ is a fixed constant, the asymptotic growth rate of $h(n,\Delta)$ is $n+o(n)$.[^o11]
- Every "near-regular" tournament on $2n-2$ vertices contains every $n$-vertex polytree.[^o12]
- Every orientation of an $n$-vertex [caterpillar tree](wikipedia://en/27258886) with [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) at most four can be embedded as a subgraph of every $(2n-2)$-vertex tournament.[^o12]
- Every $(2n-2)$-vertex tournament contains as a subgraph every $n$-vertex [arborescence](wikipedia://en/13251077).[^o13]

## Related conjectures {#Related_conjectures}

(Rosenfeld 1972) conjectured that every orientation of an $n$-vertex [path graph](wikipedia://en/4587190) (with $n\ge 8$) can be embedded as a subgraph into every $n$-vertex tournament.[^o12] After partial results by (Thomason 1986), this was proven by (Havet Thomassé 2000a).

Havet and Thomassé[^o19] in turn conjectured a strengthening of Sumner's conjecture, that every tournament on $n+k-1$ vertices contains as a subgraph every polytree with at most $k$ leaves. This has been confirmed for almost every tree by Mycroft and (Naia 2018).

(Burr 1980) conjectured that, whenever a graph $G$ requires $2n-2$ or more colors in a [coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) of $G$, then every orientation of $G$ contains every orientation of an $n$-vertex tree. Because complete graphs require a different color for each vertex, Sumner's conjecture would follow immediately from Burr's conjecture.[^o22] As Burr showed, orientations of graphs whose chromatic number grows quadratically as a function of $n$ are universal for polytrees.

:::
