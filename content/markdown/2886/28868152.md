# Jacobi operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jacobi operator"}

Jacobi matrix (operator) A **Jacobi operator**, also known as **Jacobi matrix**, is a symmetric [linear operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) acting on [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) which is given by an infinite [tridiagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/519218). It is commonly used to specify systems of [orthonormal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718) over a finite, positive [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322). This operator is named after [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).

:::

The name derives from a theorem from Jacobi, dating to 1848, stating that every [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) over a [principal ideal domain](wikipedia://en/25039) is congruent to a tridiagonal matrix.

## Self-adjoint Jacobi operators {#Self-adjoint_Jacobi_operators}

The most important case is the one of self-adjoint Jacobi operators acting on the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) of square summable sequences over the [positive integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integers) $\ell^2(\mathbb{N})$. In this case it is given by

$$Jf_0 = a_0 f_1 + b_0 f_0, \quad Jf_n = a_n f_{n+1} + b_n f_n + a_{n-1} f_{n-1}, \quad n>0,$$

where the coefficients are assumed to satisfy

$$a_n >0, \quad b_n \in \mathbb{R}.$$

The operator will be bounded if and only if the coefficients are bounded.

There are close connections with the theory of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718). In fact, the solution $p_n(x)$ of the [recurrence relation](wikipedia://en/146806)

$$J\, p_n(x) = x\, p_n(x), \qquad p_0(x)=1 \text{ and } p_{-1} (x)=0,$$

is a polynomial of degree *n* and these polynomials are [orthonormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal) with respect to the [spectral measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectral_measure) corresponding to the first basis vector $\delta_{1,n}$.

This recurrence relation is also commonly written as

$$xp_n(x)=a_{n+1}p_{n+1}(x) + b_n p_n(x) + a_np_{n-1}(x)$$

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

It arises in many areas of mathematics and physics. The case *a*(*n*) = 1 is known as the discrete one-dimensional [Schrödinger operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_operator). It also arises in:

- The [Lax pair](wikipedia://en/5527029) of the [Toda lattice](wikipedia://en/3240033).
- The three-term recurrence relationship of [orthogonal polynomials](wikipedia://en/32811718), orthogonal over a positive and finite [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322).
- Algorithms devised to calculate [Gaussian quadrature rules](wikipedia://en/50579), derived from systems of orthogonal polynomials.[^o5]
- The theory of [birth-death processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth-death_process)[^o6] where the infinite transition matrix can be transformed into a self-adjoint Jacobi operator acting on the [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $\ell^2(\mathbb{C})$.

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

When one considers [Bergman space](wikipedia://en/3205927), namely the space of [square-integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square-integrable) [holomorphic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_functions) over some domain, then, under general circumstances, one can give that space a basis of orthogonal polynomials, the [Bergman polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergman_polynomial). In this case, the analog of the tridiagonal Jacobi operator is a [Hessenberg operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessenberg_operator) – an infinite-dimensional [Hessenberg matrix](wikipedia://en/519204). The system of orthogonal polynomials is given by

$$zp_n(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1} D_{kn} p_k(z)$$

and $p_0(z)=1$. Here, *D* is the Hessenberg operator that generalizes the tridiagonal Jacobi operator *J* for this situation.[^o9][^o10][^o11] Note that *D* is the right-[shift operator](wikipedia://en/467384) on the Bergman space: that is, it is given by

$$[Df](z) = zf(z)$$

The zeros of the Bergman polynomial $p_n(z)$ correspond to the [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) of the principal $n\times n$ submatrix of *D*. That is, The Bergman polynomials are the [characteristic polynomials](wikipedia://en/218268) for the principal submatrices of the shift operator.
