# David Siegmund

**David Oliver Siegmund** (born November 15, 1941)[^o3] is an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who has worked extensively on [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530).[^o4]

## Biography {#Biography}

Siegmund grew up in [Webster Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster_Groves,_Missouri), [Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri). He received his [baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) degree, in mathematics, from [Southern Methodist University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Methodist_University) in 1963, and a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in statistics from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1966. His Ph.D. advisor was [Herbert Robbins](wikipedia://en/745193). After being an assistant and then a full professor at Columbia, he went to [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) in 1976, where he is currently a professor of statistics. He has served twice as the chair of Stanford's statistics department.[^o4][^o6] He has also held visiting positions at [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge).[^o4]

## Work {#Work}

Siegmund has written with [Herbert Robbins](wikipedia://en/745193) and [Yuan-Shih Chow](wikipedia://en/15359177) on the theory of [optimal stopping](wikipedia://en/7189886). Much of his work has been on [sequential analysis](wikipedia://en/3509530), and he has also worked on the statistics of [gene mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_mapping).[^o4]
