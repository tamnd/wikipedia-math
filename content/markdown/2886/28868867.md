# Hermann Flaschka

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hermann Flaschka"}

**Hermann Flaschka** (25 March 1945 – 18 March 2021) was an Austrian-American [mathematical physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_physicist) and [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona), known for his important contributions in [completely integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_integrable_system) ([soliton](wikipedia://en/51654) equations).

:::

## Childhood {#Childhood}

Flaschka had lived in the USA since his family immigrated when he was a teenager. They lived in Atlanta, GA. His father Hermenegild Arved Flaschka (1915 - 1999)[^o4] taught Chemistry at Georgia Tech.[^o5] Hermann graduated from [Druid Hills High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druid_Hills_High_School) with the class of 1962 and received his Bachelor's degree at [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech) in 1967.[^o6] Among other achievements there he also received the "William Gilmer Perry Awards for Freshman English" in 1963, despite the fact that he's not a native speaker.[^o7]

## Career {#Career}

He received his Ph.D. from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1970. His advisor was [Gilbert Strang](wikipedia://en/2613188) and the title of his thesis *Asymptotic Expansions and Hyperbolic Equations with Multiple Characteristics*. He then worked as post-doc at the [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University) until 1972. He was a professor at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) until his retirement in 2017.

He lectured as visiting professor at several institutions, among them the [Clarkson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_University) (1978/79), the Kyoto [RIMS](wikipedia://en/1978962) (1980/81) and the [École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Polytechnique_Fédérale_de_Lausanne) (2002).

In 1995 he received the [Norbert Wiener Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_Wiener_Prize) in Applied Mathematics. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9]

## Work {#Work}

He made important contributions to the theory of [completely integrable systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_integrable_system) in particular the [Toda lattice](wikipedia://en/3240033) and the [Korteweg&ndash;de Vries equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korteweg&ndash;de_Vries_equation).

In 1980 he co-founded *[Physica D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physica_D): Nonlinear Phenomena* for which he also served as co-editor for many years.[^o11] Publisher [Elsevier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsevier) now lists him as honorary editor.[^o12]
