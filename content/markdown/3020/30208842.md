# Precomputation

*Image: Abramowitz&Stegun.page97.agr.jpg.* Part of a 20th-century precomputed [mathematical table](wikipedia://en/239112) of [common logarithms](wikipedia://en/174482).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Precomputation"}

In [algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithms), **precomputation** is the act of performing an initial [computation](wikipedia://en/5926) before [run time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_time_%28program_lifecycle_phase%29) to generate a [lookup table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lookup_table) that can be used by an algorithm to avoid repeated computation each time it is executed. Precomputation is often used in algorithms that depend on the results of expensive computations that don't depend on the input of the algorithm. A trivial example of precomputation is the use of [hardcoded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcoded) mathematical constants, such as [π](wikipedia://en/23601) and [e](wikipedia://en/9633), rather than computing their approximations to the necessary precision at run time.

:::

In [databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database), the term **materialization** is used to refer to storing the results of a precomputation,[^o2][^o3] such as in a [materialized view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materialized_view).[^o4][^o5]

## Overview {#Overview}

Precomputing a set of intermediate results at the beginning of an algorithm's execution can often increase [algorithmic efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_efficiency) substantially. This becomes advantageous when one or more inputs is constrained to a small enough range that the results can be stored in a reasonably sized block of memory. Because memory access is essentially constant in time complexity (except for [caching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU_cache) delays), any algorithm with a component which has worse than constant efficiency over a small input range can be improved by precomputing values. In some cases efficient approximation algorithms can be obtained by computing a [discrete](wikipedia://en/8492) subset of values and [interpolating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpolating) for intermediate input values, since interpolation is also a linear operation.

## History {#History}

Before the advent of computers, printed [lookup tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lookup_table) of values were used by people to speed up hand calculations of complex functions, such as in [trigonometric tables](wikipedia://en/150170), [logarithm tables](wikipedia://en/174482), and tables of [statistical density functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_density_function).[^o8] School children are often taught to memorize "[times tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_table)" to avoid calculations of the most commonly used numbers (up to 9 x 9 or 12 x 12). Even as early as 493 A.D., [Victorius of Aquitaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorius_of_Aquitaine) wrote a 98-column multiplication table which gave (in [Roman numerals](wikipedia://en/25657)) the product of every number from 2 to 50 times and the rows were "a list of numbers starting with one thousand, descending by hundreds to one hundred, then descending by tens to ten, then by ones to one, and then the fractions down to 1/144."[^o9]

## Examples {#Examples}

Even modern computer implementations of digital [trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) often use precomputed lookup tables to either provide coefficients for [interpolation](wikipedia://en/14569) algorithms or to initialise successive [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105).

Many attacks on [cryptosystems](wikipedia://en/506383) involve precomputation.

Examples of large-scale precomputation as part of modern efficient algorithms include:

- [Rainbow tables](wikipedia://en/2403284)
- [Perfect hashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_hash)
- The [cube attack](wikipedia://en/18982200)
- Precalculated [BSP trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSP_tree) for visibility calculations in 3D graphics
- [Radiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiosity_%283D_computer_graphics%29) precomputation for illumination in 3D graphics

[Compilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) use precomputation extensively as a means of increasing the run-time speed of the resulting code: this precomputation can be regarded as in effect a form of [partial evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_evaluation) of the program code itself. Examples of this sort of precomputation include [dataflow analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dataflow_analysis) and [strength reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_reduction) steps.
