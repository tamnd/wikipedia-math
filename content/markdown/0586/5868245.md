# Transparent intensional logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Transparent intensional logic"}

**Transparent intensional logic** (frequently abbreviated as **TIL**) is a [logical system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_system) created by [Pavel Tichý](wikipedia://en/4565688). Due to its rich *procedural semantics* TIL is in particular apt for the logical analysis of [natural language](wikipedia://en/21173). From the formal point of view, TIL is a hyperintensional, partial, [typed lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/366208).

:::

TIL applications cover a wide range of topics from [formal semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_semantics_%28logic%29), [philosophy of language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_language), [epistemic logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemic_logic), [philosophical](wikipedia://en/304939), and [formal logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_logic). TIL provides an overarching semantic framework for all sorts of discourse, whether colloquial, scientific, mathematical or logical. The semantic theory is a procedural one, according to which sense is an abstract, pre-linguistic procedure detailing what operations to apply to what procedural constituents to arrive at the product (if any) of the procedure.

TIL procedures, known as *constructions*, are hyperintensionally individuated. Construction is the single most important notion of transparent intensional logic, being a philosophically well-motivated and formally worked-out conception of [Frege](wikipedia://en/48416)’s notion of mode of presentation. Constructions, and the entities they construct, are organized into a ramified [type theory](wikipedia://en/40282) incorporating a simple type theory. The semantics is tailored to the hardest case, as constituted by hyperintensional contexts, and generalized from there to intensional and [extensional contexts](wikipedia://en/2625672). The underlying logic is a Frege-style function/argument one, treating [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), rather than [relations](wikipedia://en/19509) or [sets](wikipedia://en/26691), as primitive, together with a [Church](wikipedia://en/49731)-style logic, centred on the operations of functional abstraction and [application](wikipedia://en/20576545).

Key constraints informing the TIL approach to [semantic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_analysis_%28linguistics%29) are *compositionality* and *anti-contextualism*. The assignment of constructions to expressions as their meanings is context-invariant. Depending on the sort of logical context in which a construction occurs, what is context-dependent is the logical manipulation of the respective meaning itself rather than the meaning assignment.
