# John Nelder

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="John Nelder"}

**John Ashworth Nelder** (8 October 1924 – 7 August 2010) was a British statistician known for his contributions to [experimental design](wikipedia://en/9541), [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634), [computational statistics](wikipedia://en/15832717), and [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579).

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Nelder's work was influential in statistics. While leading research at [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station), Nelder developed and supervised the updating of the [statistical software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_software) packages [GLIM](wikipedia://en/14770223) and [GenStat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GenStat): Both packages are flexible [high-level programming languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-level_programming_language) that allow statisticians to formulate [linear models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_models) concisely.[^o4] GLIM influenced later environments for [statistical computing](wikipedia://en/15832717) such as [S-PLUS](wikipedia://en/3830007) and [R](wikipedia://en/376707). Both GLIM and GenStat have powerful facilities for the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) for [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_block_design) [experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), an area where Nelder made many contributions.

In [statistical theory](wikipedia://en/27579), Nelder proposed the [generalized linear model](wikipedia://en/747122) together with [Robert Wedderburn](wikipedia://en/9808316). Nelder and Wedderburn formulated generalized linear models as a way of unifying various other statistical models, including [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386), [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631) and [Poisson regression](wikipedia://en/2708102).[^o5] They proposed an [iteratively reweighted least squares](wikipedia://en/11463665) [method](wikipedia://en/15237) for [maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood) estimation of the model parameters.

In [statistical inference](wikipedia://en/27577), Nelder (along with [George Barnard](wikipedia://en/2792579) and [A. W. F. Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._W._F._Edwards)) emphasized the importance of the [likelihood](wikipedia://en/44968) in [data analysis](wikipedia://en/2720954), promoting this "likelihood approach" as an alternative to [frequentist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequentist) and [Bayesian](wikipedia://en/49571) statistics.

In [response-surface](wikipedia://en/4481194) [optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimization_%28mathematics%29), Nelder and [Roger Mead](wikipedia://en/57205947) proposed the \[\[Nelder–Mead method |Nelder–Mead simplex heuristic\]\], widely used in engineering and statistics.

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Brushford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brushford,_Somerset), near [Dulverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulverton), Somerset, Nelder was educated at [Blundell's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blundell's_School) and [Sidney Sussex College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Sussex_College,_Cambridge), where he read Mathematics.

Nelder's appointments included Head of the Statistics Section at the [National Vegetable Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulture_Research_International), [Wellesbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesbourne), from 1951 to 1968 and head of the Statistics Department at [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station) from 1968 to 1984.[^o7] During his time at Wellesbourne he spent a year (1965–1966) at the Waite Institute in Adelaide, South Australia, where he worked with Graham Wilkinson on Genstat. He held an appointment as visiting professor at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) from 1972 onwards.

He was responsible, with [Max Nicholson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Nicholson) and [James Ferguson-Lees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ferguson-Lees), for debunking the [Hastings Rarities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Rarities) – sightings of a series of rare birds, preserved by a [taxidermist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxidermy) and provided with bogus histories.[^o8]

Nelder died on 7 August 2010 in [Luton and Dunstable Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luton_and_Dunstable_Hospital), taken there after a fall at home, which was incidental to the cause of death.[^o9]

## Awards and distinctions {#Awards_and_distinctions}

Nelder was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1976[^o11] and received the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664)'s [Guy Medal](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Gold in 2005. He was also the recipient of the inaugural Karl Pearson Prize of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute), with [Peter McCullagh](wikipedia://en/11740023), "for their monograph Generalized Linear Models (1983)".[^o12]

As tribute on his eightieth birthday, a [festschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festschrift) *Methods and Models in Statistics: In Honour of Professor John Nelder, FRS* was edited by Niall Adams, Martin Crowder, David J Hand & Dave Stephens, Imperial College Press (2004).[^o13]

The first annual John Nelder memorial lecture was held at Imperial College London, on 8 March 2012, as part of the Mathematics department Colloquium series. The lecture was given by Nelder's long term co-author, [Peter McCullagh](wikipedia://en/11740023). An interview[^o14] with McCullagh, about statistical modelling, includes some reminiscences about Nelder.
