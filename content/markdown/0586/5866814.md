# Harry Pitt

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Harry Pitt"}

**Sir Harry Raymond Pitt** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) (3 June 1914 – 8 October 2005)[^o3] was a British mathematician.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Harry Raymond Pitt was born in [West Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich) in 1914, the son of Harry and Harriet Pitt. He attended [King Edward's School, Stourbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_College,_Stourbridge), before going up to [Peterhouse, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge).

From 1936 to 1939 he held a Fellowship at Peterhouse, during which period he spent about a year at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) as a Choate Memorial Fellow. He was awarded a PhD by [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1938 for research on [Tauberian theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauberian_theorem), giving him a long-term interest in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542).

In 1942 Pitt went to work in [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) at the Air Ministry and the Ministry of Aircraft Production.

In 1945 Harry Pitt was appointed Professor of Mathematics at [Queen's University of Belfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_University_of_Belfast). In 1950 he moved to the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) as Professor of Pure Mathematics. In 1962–63 he once more crossed the Atlantic to serve as a visiting professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University).

In 1964 Pitt was appointed [Vice-Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor) of the [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading), in which post he remained until 1978.[^o5]

Pitt was at Reading University during the student rebellion of 1968. In one well-publicised incident, he and the registrar were taken hostage by students and locked in a building on the campus. But he had anticipated this possibility and was able to escape using a spare set of keys.

Between 1975 and 1978 Pitt served as chairman of the Universities Central Council on Admissions, and between 1984 and 1985 he was President of the Institute of Mathematics and its Applications, the association of practising mathematicians.

Pitt was awarded honorary degrees by the universities of [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Aberdeen) (1970), [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) (1970), Reading (1978), and Belfast (1981). He was elected [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) in 1957 and was knighted in 1978.
