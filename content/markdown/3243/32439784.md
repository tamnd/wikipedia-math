# Physical mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Physical mathematics"}

The subject of **physical mathematics** is concerned with [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that is motivated by [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) and is considered by some as a subfield of [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Physically motivated mathematics existed within a tradition of mathematical analysis of nature that goes back to the ancient Greeks. A good example is Archimedes' [*Method of Mechanical Theorems*](wikipedia://en/261272), where the [principle of the balance](wikipedia://en/30400) is used to find results in pure [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446). This tradition, elaborated further by Islamic and Byzantine scholars, was reintroduced to the West in the [12th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_of_the_12th_century) and during the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). It became known as "mixed mathematics" and was a major contributor to the emergence of modern mathematical physics in the [17th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_Revolution).[^o3]

The details of [physical units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_unit) and their manipulation were addressed by [Alexander Macfarlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Macfarlane) in *Physical Arithmetic* in 1885.[^o4] The science of [kinematics](wikipedia://en/65914) created a need for mathematical representation of [motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion) and has found expression with [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), and [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422).

At the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) the [Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Tripos) tested students on their knowledge of "mixed mathematics".[^o5] "... \[N\]ew books which appeared in the mid-eighteenth century offered a systematic introduction to the fundamental operations of the fluxional calculus and showed how it could be applied to a wide range of mathematical and physical problems. ... The strongly problem-oriented presentation in the treatises ... made it much easier for university students to master the fluxional calculus and its applications \[and\] helped define a new field of mixed mathematical studies..."

An adventurous expression of physical mathematics is found in [Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696)'s *[A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Treatise_on_Electricity_and_Magnetism)*, which used [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564). The text aspired to describe phenomena in four dimensions, but the foundation for this physical world, [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), trailed by forty years.

String theorist [Greg Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Moore_%28physicist%29) said this about physical mathematics in his vision talk at Strings 2014.[^o6] "The use of the term “Physical Mathematics” in contrast to the more traditional “[Mathematical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Physics)” by myself and others is not meant to detract from the venerable subject of Mathematical Physics but rather to delineate a smaller subfield characterized by questions and goals that are often motivated, on the physics side, by [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312), [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), and [supersymmetry](wikipedia://en/224636), (and more recently by the notion of [topological phases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_phase) in [condensed matter physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter_physics)), and, on the mathematics side, often involve deep relations to [infinite-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite-dimensional) [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) (and groups), [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), and even [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), in addition to the more traditional relations of physics to algebra, [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396)."
