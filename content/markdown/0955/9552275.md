# Michael Luby

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Michael Luby"}

**Michael George Luby** is a mathematician and computer scientist, CTO of BitRipple, senior research scientist at the [International Computer Science Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Computer_Science_Institute) (ICSI), former VP Technology at [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm), co-founder and former [chief technology officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Technology_Officer) of Digital Fountain. In [coding theory](wikipedia://en/321869) he is known for leading the invention of the [Tornado codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_code) and the [LT codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LT_code). In cryptography he is known for his contributions showing that any [one-way function](wikipedia://en/363890) can be used as the basis for private cryptography, and for his analysis, in collaboration with [Charles Rackoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Rackoff), of the [Feistel cipher](wikipedia://en/352709) construction. His [distributed algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_algorithm) to find a [maximal independent set](wikipedia://en/1793590) in a computer network has also been influential.

:::

Luby received his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Sc.) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) from [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1975. In 1983 he was awarded a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) from [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). In 1996–1997, while at the ICSI, he led the team that invented Tornado codes. These were the first [LDPC codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDPC_code) based on an irregular degree design that has proved crucial to all later good LDPC code designs, which provably achieve [channel capacity](wikipedia://en/231204) for the [erasure channel](wikipedia://en/6948409), and which have linear time encoding and decoding algorithms. In 1998 Luby left ICSI to found the Digital Fountain company, and shortly thereafter in 1998 he invented the [LT codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LT_code), the first practical [fountain codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_code). Qualcomm acquired Digital Fountain in 2009.[^o4]

## Awards {#Awards}

Luby's publications have won the 2002 [IEEE Information Theory Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Information_Theory_Society) Information Theory Paper Award for leading the design and analysis of the first irregular LDPC error-correcting codes,[^o6] the 2003 [SIAM](wikipedia://en/1883376) Outstanding Paper Prize for the seminal paper showing how to construct a cryptographically unbreakable pseudo-random generator from any one-way function, and the 2009 ACM SIGCOMM Test of Time Award.[^o7]

In 2016 he was awarded the [ACM Edsger W. Dijkstra Prize in Distributed Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dijkstra_Prize); the prize is given "for outstanding papers on the principles of distributed computing, whose significance and impact on the theory and/or practice of distributed computing have been evident for at least a decade", and was awarded to Luby for his work on [parallel algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_algorithm) for [maximal independent sets](wikipedia://en/1793590).

Luby won the 2007 [IEEE Eric E. Sumner Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Eric_E._Sumner_Award) together with [Amin Shokrollahi](wikipedia://en/30220369) "for bridging mathematics, Internet design and mobile broadcasting as well as successful standardization".[^o8] He was given the 2012 [IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Richard_W._Hamming_Medal) together with Amin Shokrollahi "for the conception, development, and analysis of practical rateless codes".[^o9] In 2015, he won the ACM [Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Kanellakis_Theory_and_Practice_Award) "for groundbreaking contributions to erasure correcting codes, which are essential for improving the quality of video transmission over a variety of networks."[^o10]

Luby was elected to the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) in 2014, "for contributions to coding theory including the inception of rateless codes". In 2015 he was elected as a Fellow of the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery).[^o11] Luby was elected as a Fellow of the IEEE in 2009.
