# Martin Klazar

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Martin Klazar"}

**Martin Klazar** (born 1966) is a Czech mathematician specializing in [enumerative combinatorics](wikipedia://en/3925533) and [extremal combinatorics](wikipedia://en/2675917). He is a docent (associate professor) in the Department of Applied Mathematics at [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in Prague.[^o3] Klazar is known for his work on pattern avoidance in discrete structures (such as permutations and set partitions) and on extremal problems for sequences and matrices.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Klazar was born in [Děčín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Děčín), Czechoslovakia (now the Czech Republic) in 1966.[^o5] He studied mathematics at the [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) in [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague) from 1984 to 1989, earning the degree of RNDr. (Rerum Naturalium Doctor). He received his Ph.D. from Charles University in 1995 under the supervision of [Jaroslav Nešetřil](wikipedia://en/6948446), with a dissertation on combinatorial aspects of [Davenport–Schinzel sequences](wikipedia://en/20232529).[^o6] In 1997–98, Klazar was awarded a [Humboldt Research Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Research_Fellowship) to conduct research at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) in Germany under host [Bernhard Korte](wikipedia://en/23541073).[^o7] He later habilitated at Charles University, where he became a docent (associate professor) in the Department of Applied Mathematics.[^o6]

## Research {#Research}

Klazar's research deals with problems in enumerative combinatorics, permutation patterns, and extremal combinatorics.

In a 1992 paper, he proved a general upper bound in the extremal theory of sequences, showing that the maximum length of sequences over an *n*-letter alphabet in which any two occurrences of the same letter are separated by at least *k-1* other letters, and which avoid a fixed forbidden subsequence $u$ of length $|u|>4$ over a *k*-letter alphabet is almost linear in *n* (more precisely, it is $O(n2^{O(\alpha(n)^{|u|-4})})$, where $\alpha(n)$ denotes the inverse Ackermann function and $|u|$ is the length of $u$).[^o9]

His 1996 paper "*On $abab$-free and $abba$-free set partitions*"[^o10] is credited as initiating[^o11] the study of pattern-avoiding set partitions (analogous to pattern avoidance in permutations), generalizing Germain Kreweras's notion of [noncrossing partitions](wikipedia://en/695026).[^o12] That work found connections to [Davenport–Schinzel sequences](wikipedia://en/20232529) and provided exact formulas for the number of set partitions avoiding certain 4-element patterns. Klazar later continued his investigations of pattern-avoiding set partitions in other works.[^o13][^o14]

In 2000, Klazar showed that the long-standing [Stanley–Wilf conjecture](wikipedia://en/7892663) on permutation patterns would follow from an extremal conjecture of Zoltán Füredi and Péter Hajnal concerning forbidden submatrices.[^o15] This approach foreshadowed the subsequent 2004 proof of the Stanley–Wilf conjecture by [Adam Marcus](wikipedia://en/39832362) and [Gábor Tardos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gábor_Tardos),[^o16] for which Marcus was awarded the inaugural [Dénes König Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dénes_König_Prize) in 2008.

Klazar's joint 2002 paper with Tomáš Kaiser, "*On growth rates of closed permutation classes"* was the first research to systemically consider the set of possible [growth rates of permutation classes](wikipedia://en/7892663#Allowable_growth_rates). Their paper characterized all accumulation points of growth constants below 2, showing in particular that 2 is the least accumulation point of permutation class growth rates, and that there are no such growth rates between 1 and the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^o17]
