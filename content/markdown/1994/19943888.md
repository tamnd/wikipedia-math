# Nikolay Nekhoroshev

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolay Nekhoroshev"}

**Nikolai Nikolaevich Nekhoroshev** (Николай Николаевич Нехорошев; 2 October 1946 – 18 October 2008) was a prominent [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) mathematician specializing in [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586) and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems). His research concerned [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319), [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/102140), [celestial mechanics](wikipedia://en/102182), [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), the [quasiclassical approximation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiclassical_approximation), and [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763). He proved, in particular, a stability result in [KAM-theory](wikipedia://en/16972) stating that, under certain conditions, solutions of nearly integrable systems stay close to invariant tori for exponentially long times (Giorgilli 1989).

:::

Nekhoroshev was professor of the [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) and [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan). He was an alumnus of Moscow's boarding school no. 18 (1964).

Winner of the [Kolmogorov Prize](wikipedia://en/73812406) (1997).

Nekhoroshev obtained his PhD in 1973 from [Lomonosov Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Moscow_State_University) under the supervision of [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490).[^o3]
