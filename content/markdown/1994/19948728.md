# Covering number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Covering number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **covering number** is the number of [balls](wikipedia://en/160556) of a given size needed to completely cover a given space, with possible overlaps between the balls. The covering number quantifies the size of a set and can be applied to general [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018). Two related concepts are the *packing number*, the number of disjoint balls that fit in a space, and the *metric entropy*, the number of points that fit in a space when constrained to lie at some fixed minimum distance apart.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let (*M*, *d*) be a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), let *K* be a subset of *M*, and let *r* be a positive [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). Let *B*<sub>*r*</sub>(*x*) denote the closed [ball](wikipedia://en/160556#In_general_metric_spaces) of radius *r* centered at *x*. A subset *C* of *M* is an *r-external covering* of *K* if:

$$K \subseteq \bigcup_{x \in C} B_r(x).$$

In other words, for every $y\in K$ there exists $x\in C$ such that $d(x,y)\leq r$.

If furthermore *C* is a subset of *K*, then it is an *r-internal covering*.

The **external covering number** of *K*, denoted $N^{\text{ext}}_r(K)$, is the minimum [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) of any external covering of *K*. The **internal covering number**, denoted $N^{\text{int}}_r(K)$, is the minimum cardinality of any internal covering.

A subset *P* of *K* is a *packing* if $P \subseteq K$ and the set $\{B_r(x)\}_{x \in P}$ is [pairwise disjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pairwise_disjoint). The **packing number** of *K*, denoted $N^{\text{pack}}_r(K)$, is the maximum cardinality of any packing of *K*.

A subset *S* of *K* is *r*-*separated* if each pair of points *x* and *y* in *S* satisfies *d*(*x*, *y*) ≥ *r*. The **metric entropy** of *K*, denoted $N^{\text{met}}_r(K)$, is the maximum cardinality of any *r*-separated subset of *K*.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The metric space is the real line $\mathbb{R}$. $K\subset \mathbb{R}$ is a set of real numbers whose absolute value is at most $k$. Then, there is an external covering of $\left\lceil \frac{2k}{r} \right\rceil$ intervals of length $r$, covering the interval $[-k, k]$. Hence: $N^{\text{ext}}_r(K) \leq \frac{2 k}{r}$; The metric space is the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^m$ with the [Euclidean metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_metric). $K\subset \mathbb{R}^m$ is a set of vectors whose length (norm) is at most $k$. If $K$ lies in a *d*-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$, then: $N^{\text{ext}}_r(K) \leq \left(\frac{2 k \sqrt{d}}{r}\right)^d$.; The metric space is the space of real-valued functions, with the [ℓ-infinity](wikipedia://en/46395425) metric. The covering number $N^{\text{int}}_r(K)$ is the smallest number $k$ such that there exist $h_1,\ldots,h_k \in K$ such that, for all $h\in K$ there exists $i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ such that the supremum distance between $h$ and $h_i$ is at most $r$. The above bound is not relevant since the space is $\infty$-dimensional. However, when $K$ is a [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set), every covering of it has a finite sub-covering, so $N^{\text{int}}_r(K)$ is finite.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The internal and external covering numbers, the packing number, and the metric entropy are all closely related. The following chain of inequalities holds for any subset *K* of a metric space and any positive real number *r*.[^o10] $N^{\text{met}}_{2 r}(K) \leq N^{\text{pack}}_r(K) \leq N^{\text{ext}}_r(K) \leq N^{\text{int}}_r(K) \leq N^{\text{met}}_r(K)$; Each function except the internal covering number is non-increasing in *r* and non-decreasing in *K*. The internal covering number is monotone in *r* but not necessarily in *K*.

The following properties relate to covering numbers in the standard [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697), $\mathbb{R}^m$:[^o6] If all vectors in $K$ are translated by a constant vector $k_0\in \mathbb{R}^m$, then the covering number does not change.; If all vectors in $K$ are multiplied by a scalar $k \in \mathbb{R}$, then: for all $r$: $N^{\text{ext}}_{|k|\cdot r}(k\cdot K) = N^{\text{ext}}_{r}(K)$; If all vectors in $K$ are operated by a [Lipschitz function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) $\phi$ with [Lipschitz constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_constant) $k$, then: for all $r$: $N^{\text{ext}}_{|k|\cdot r}(\phi\circ K) \leq N^{\text{ext}}_{r}(K)$

:::

## Application to machine learning {#Application_to_machine_learning}

Let $K$ be a space of real-valued functions, with the [ℓ-infinity](wikipedia://en/46395425) metric (see example 3 above). Suppose all functions in $K$ are bounded by a real constant $M$. Then, the covering number can be used to bound the [generalization error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalization_error) of learning functions from $K$, relative to the squared loss:[^o7]

$$\operatorname{Prob}\left[ \sup_{h\in K} \big\vert\text{GeneralizationError}(h) - \text{EmpiricalError}(h)\big\vert \geq \epsilon \right] \leq N^\text{int}_r (K)\, 2\exp{-m\epsilon^2 \over 2M^4}$$

where $r = {\epsilon \over 8M}$ and $m$ is the number of samples.
