# Pauline van den Driessche

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Pauline van den Driessche"}

**Pauline van den Driessche** (born 1941)[^o7] is a British and Canadian [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969) who is a [professor emerita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emerita) in the department of mathematics and statistics at the [University of Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Victoria),[^o3] where she has also held an affiliation in the department of computer science.[^o8] Her research interests include [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), [matrix analysis](wikipedia://en/40956141), and [stability theory](wikipedia://en/5493795).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Van den Driessche earned bachelor's and master's degrees in 1961 and 1963 respectively from [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London). She completed her doctorate in 1964 from the [University College of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University); her dissertation concerned [fluid mechanics](wikipedia://en/2684988).[^o7][^o8][^o9] She stayed on for a year in Wales as an assistant lecturer;[^o7] she was hired as an assistant professor at the University of Victoria in 1965, and retired in 2006.[^o9]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

In [mathematical biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_biology), van den Driessche's contributions include important work on [delay differential equations](wikipedia://en/3941333) and on [Hopf bifurcations](wikipedia://en/3626981),[^o7][^o9] and the effects of changing population size and immigration on [epidemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemic).[^o8]

She has also done more fundamental research in [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), motivated by applications in mathematical biology.[^o8] Her work in this area includes pioneering contributions to the theory of [combinatorial matrix theory](wikipedia://en/59047031) in which she proved connections between the sign pattern of a matrix and its stability,[^o7][^o8][^o9] as well as results on [matrix decomposition](wikipedia://en/253873).[^o8]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2005, the journal *[Linear Algebra and its Applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_Algebra_and_its_Applications)* published a special issue in her honor.[^o7] She was the 2007 winner of the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) of the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055),[^o8] and in the same year became the inaugural [Olga Taussky-Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Taussky-Todd) Lecturer, an award given every four years at the [International Congress on Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13606431) by the [International Council for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/13606227) and [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o9] In 2013 she became a [fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1883376) "for contributions to linear algebra and mathematical biology".[^o10] She received the CAIMS Research Prize from the Canadian Applied and Industrial Mathematics Society in 2019,[^o11] and the [2022 Hans Schneider Prize in Linear Algebra](wikipedia://en/58527033).
