# Ionescu-Tulcea theorem

Not to be confused with: the [Ionescu-Tulcea–Marinescu ergodic theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionescu-Tulcea–Marinescu_ergodic_theorem).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ionescu-Tulcea theorem"}

In the mathematical [theory of probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_probability), the **Ionescu-Tulcea theorem**, sometimes called the **Ionesco Tulcea extension theorem**, deals with the existence of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) for probabilistic events consisting of a countably infinite number of individual probabilistic events. In particular, the individual events may be [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) or dependent with respect to each other. Thus, the statement goes beyond the mere existence of countable [product measures](wikipedia://en/2986564). The theorem was proved by [Cassius Ionescu-Tulcea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassius_Ionescu-Tulcea) in 1949.[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Suppose that $(\Omega_0, \mathcal A_0, P_0)$ is a [probability space](wikipedia://en/43325) and $(\Omega_i, \mathcal A_i)$ for $i \in \mathbb{N}$ is a sequence of [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333). For each $i \in \mathbb{N}$ let

$$\kappa_i \colon (\Omega^{i-1}, \mathcal A^{i-1}) \to (\Omega_i, \mathcal A_i)$$

be the [Markov kernel](wikipedia://en/21180014) derived from $(\Omega^{i-1}, \mathcal A^{i-1})$ and $(\Omega_i, \mathcal A_i),$, where

$$\Omega^i:=\prod_{k=0}^i\Omega_k \text{ and } \mathcal A^i:= \bigotimes_{k=0}^i \mathcal A_k.$$

Then there exists a sequence of probability measures

$P_i:=P_0 \otimes \bigotimes_{k=1}^i \kappa_k$ defined on the product space for the sequence $(\Omega^i, \mathcal A^i)$, $i \in \mathbb{N},$

and there exists a uniquely defined probability measure $P$ on $\left(\prod_{k=0}^\infty \Omega_k, \bigotimes_{k=0}^\infty \mathcal A_k\right)$, so that

$$P_i(A)=P\left( A \times \prod_{k=i+1}^\infty \Omega_k \right)$$

is satisfied for each $A \in \mathcal A^i$ and $i \in\mathbb{N}$. (The measure $P$ has [conditional probabilities](wikipedia://en/24104134) equal to the stochastic kernels.)[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The construction used in the proof of the Ionescu-Tulcea theorem is often used in the theory of [Markov decision processes](wikipedia://en/1125883), and, in particular, the theory of [Markov chains](wikipedia://en/60876).[^o6]
