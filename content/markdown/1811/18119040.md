# Apeirogonal prism

Uniform tiling stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirogonal prism"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apeirogonal prism** or **infinite prism** is the arithmetic limit of the family of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007); it can be considered an infinite [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or a [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane.[^o2]

:::

[Thorold Gosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorold_Gosset) called it a *2-dimensional semi-check*, like a single row of a [checkerboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkerboard).

If the sides are [squares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29), it is a [uniform tiling](wikipedia://en/12126635). If colored with two sets of alternating squares it is still uniform.

## Related tilings and polyhedra {#Related_tilings_and_polyhedra}

The apeirogonal tiling is the arithmetic limit of the family of [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007) t{2, *p*} or *p*.4.4, as *p* tends to [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), thereby turning the prism into a Euclidean tiling.

An [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operation can create an [apeirogonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/18119626) composed of three triangles and one [apeirogon](wikipedia://en/5658261) at each vertex.

*Image: Infinite antiprism.svg.*

Similarly to the [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) and the [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635), eight uniform tilings may be based from the regular [apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/38764584). The [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) and [cantellated](wikipedia://en/6317514) forms are duplicated, and as two times infinity is also infinity, the [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) and [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) forms are also duplicated, therefore reducing the number of unique forms to four: the apeirogonal tiling, the apeirogonal hosohedron, the apeirogonal prism, and the [apeirogonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/18119626).
