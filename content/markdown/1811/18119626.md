# Apeirogonal antiprism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Apeirogonal antiprism"}

Uniform tiling stat table In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), an **apeirogonal antiprism** or **infinite antiprism**[^o2] is the arithmetic limit of the family of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851); it can be considered an infinite [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) or a [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) of the plane.

:::

If the sides are [equilateral triangles](wikipedia://en/173285), it is a [uniform tiling](wikipedia://en/12126635). In general, it can have two sets of alternating congruent [isosceles triangles](wikipedia://en/315428), surrounded by two half-planes.

## Related tilings and polyhedra {#Related_tilings_and_polyhedra}

The apeirogonal antiprism is the arithmetic limit of the family of [antiprisms](wikipedia://en/1851) sr{2, *p*} or *p*.3.3.3, as *p* tends to [infinity](wikipedia://en/20110824), thereby turning the antiprism into a Euclidean tiling.

Similarly to the [uniform polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polyhedra) and the [uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635), eight uniform tilings may be based from the regular [apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/38764584). The [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) and [cantellated](wikipedia://en/6317514) forms are duplicated, and as two times infinity is also infinity, the [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) and [omnitruncated](wikipedia://en/7083690) forms are also duplicated, therefore reducing the number of unique forms to four: the [apeirogonal tiling](wikipedia://en/35870958), the [apeirogonal hosohedron](wikipedia://en/42115167), the [apeirogonal prism](wikipedia://en/18119040), and the apeirogonal antiprism.
