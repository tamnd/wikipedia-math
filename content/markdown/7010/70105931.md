# Cole–Davidson equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cole–Davidson equation"}

The **Cole–Davidson** **equation** is a model used to describe dielectric relaxation in glass-forming liquids. The equation for the [complex permittivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dielectric_constant#Complex_permittivity) is

:::

$$\hat{\varepsilon}(\omega) = \varepsilon_{\infty} + \frac{\Delta\varepsilon}{(1+i\omega\tau)^{\beta}},$$

where $\varepsilon_{\infty}$ is the [permittivity](wikipedia://en/53933) at the high frequency limit, $\Delta\varepsilon = \varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\infty}$ where $\varepsilon_{s}$ is the static, low frequency permittivity, and $\tau$ is the characteristic [relaxation time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxation_time) of the medium. The exponent $\beta$ represents the exponent of the decay of the high frequency wing of the imaginary part, $\varepsilon''(\omega) \sim \omega^{-\beta}$.

The equation is named after Robert Hugh Cole and D.W. Davidson, who developed it in 1950.[^o2]

The Cole–Davidson equation is a generalization of the [Debye relaxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debye_relaxation) keeping the initial increase of the low frequency wing of the imaginary part, $\varepsilon''(\omega) \sim \omega$. Because this is also a characteristic feature of the Fourier transform of the [stretched exponential function](wikipedia://en/6262575) it has been considered as an approximation of the latter,[^o3] although nowadays an approximation by the [Havriliak–Negami function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havriliak-Negami_relaxation) or exact numerical calculation may be preferred.

Because the slopes of the peak in $\varepsilon''(\omega)$ in double-logarithmic representation are different it is considered an asymmetric generalization in contrast to the [Cole–Cole equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole–Cole_equation).

The Cole–Davidson equation is the special case of the [Havriliak–Negami relaxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havriliak–Negami_relaxation) with $\alpha=1$.

The real and imaginary parts are

$$\varepsilon'(\omega) = \varepsilon_{\infty} + \Delta\varepsilon\left( 1 + (\omega\tau)^{2} \right)^{-\beta/2} \cos (\beta\arctan(\omega\tau))$$

and

$$\varepsilon''(\omega) = \Delta\varepsilon\left( 1 + (\omega\tau)^{2} \right)^{-\beta/2} \sin (\beta\arctan(\omega\tau))$$
