# Signature operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Signature operator"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **signature operator** is an [elliptic differential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_differential_operator) defined on a certain subspace of the space of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on an even-dimensional [compact](wikipedia://en/669475) [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), whose [analytic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem#The_analytical_index) is the same as the [topological signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) of the manifold if the dimension of the manifold is a multiple of four.[^o2] It is an instance of a Dirac-type operator.

:::

## Definition in the even-dimensional case {#Definition_in_the_even-dimensional_case}

Let $M$ be a compact [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) of even dimension $2l$. Let

$$d : \Omega^p(M)\rightarrow \Omega^{p+1}(M)$$

be the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) on $i$-th order [differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_forms) on $M$. The Riemannian metric on $M$ allows us to define the [Hodge star operator](wikipedia://en/310914) $\star$ and with it the inner product

$$\langle\omega,\eta\rangle=\int_M\omega\wedge\star\eta$$

on forms. Denote by

$$d^*: \Omega^{p+1}(M)\rightarrow \Omega^p(M)$$

the [adjoint operator](wikipedia://en/667175) of the exterior differential $d$. This operator can be expressed purely in terms of the Hodge star operator as follows:

$$d^*= (-1)^{2l(p+1) + 2l + 1} \star d \star= - \star d \star$$

Now consider $d + d^*$ acting on the space of all forms $\Omega(M)=\bigoplus_{p=0}^{2l}\Omega^{p}(M)$. One way to consider this as a graded operator is the following: Let $\tau$ be an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450#Linear_algebra) on the space of *all* forms defined by:

$$\tau(\omega)=i^{p(p-1)+l}\star \omega\quad,\quad\omega \in \Omega^p(M)$$

It is verified that $d + d^*$ anti-commutes with $\tau$ and, consequently, switches the $(\pm 1)$-[eigenspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenspace) $\Omega_{\pm}(M)$ of $\tau$

Consequently,

$$d + d^* = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & D \\ D^* & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

::: {#o4 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition:** The operator $d + d^*$ with the above grading respectively the above operator $D: \Omega_+(M) \rightarrow \Omega_-(M)$ is called the **signature operator** of $M$.[^o2]

:::

## Definition in the odd-dimensional case {#Definition_in_the_odd-dimensional_case}

In the odd-dimensional case one defines the signature operator to be $i(d+d^*)\tau$ acting on the even-dimensional forms of $M$.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Hirzebruch Signature Theorem"}

## Hirzebruch Signature Theorem {#Hirzebruch_Signature_Theorem}

If $l = 2k$, so that the dimension of $M$ is a multiple of four, then [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590) implies that:

$$\mathrm{index}(D) = \mathrm{sign}(M)$$

where the right hand side is the [topological signature](wikipedia://en/1152311) (*i.e.* the [signature of a quadratic form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_of_a_quadratic_form) on $H^{2k}(M)\$ defined by the [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684)).

The *Heat Equation* approach to the [Atiyah-Singer index theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiyah-Singer_index_theorem) can then be used to show that:

$$\mathrm{sign}(M) = \int_M L(p_1,\ldots,p_l)$$

where $L$ is the [Hirzebruch L-Polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirzebruch_L-polynomial#L_genus_and_the_Hirzebruch_signature_theorem),[^o8] and the $p_i\$ the [Pontrjagin forms](wikipedia://en/385944) on $M$.[^o9]

:::

## Homotopy invariance of the higher indices {#Homotopy_invariance_of_the_higher_indices}

Kaminker and Miller proved that the higher indices of the signature operator are homotopy-invariant.[^o11]
