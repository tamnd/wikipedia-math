# Curtis Greene

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Curtis Greene"}

**Curtis Greene** is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specializing in [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582). He is the J. McLain King Professor of Mathematics at [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) in [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania).[^o2]

:::

Greene did his undergraduate studies at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), and earned his Ph.D. in 1969 from the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) under the supervision of [Robert P. Dilworth](wikipedia://en/9581646).[^o2] He held positions at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) and the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) before moving to Haverford.

Greene has written highly cited research papers on [Sperner families](wikipedia://en/748730),[^o4] [Young tableaux](wikipedia://en/683368),[^o5] and [combinatorial equivalences](wikipedia://en/917006) between [hyperplane arrangements](wikipedia://en/864438), [zonotopes](wikipedia://en/669402), and [graph orientations](wikipedia://en/16678715).[^o6] With [Daniel Kleitman](wikipedia://en/5299894), he has also written a highly cited survey paper on [combinatorial proof](wikipedia://en/741875) techniques.[^o7]

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]
