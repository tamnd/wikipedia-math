# Kurt Schütte

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kurt Schütte"}

**Kurt Schütte** (14 October 1909 – 18 August 1998) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478) and [ordinal analysis](wikipedia://en/16084282). The [Feferman–Schütte ordinal](wikipedia://en/16084455), which he showed to be the precise ordinal bound for [predicativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicativity), is named after him. He was the [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) of 16 students, including [Wolfgang Bibel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Bibel), Wolfgang Maaß, Wolfram Pohlers, and [Martin Wirsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Wirsing). He made important contributions to the proof theory of systems with the [ω-rule](wikipedia://en/4066001#ω-logic), and made progress towards solving [Takeuti's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2372007).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Kurt Wilhelm Schütte was born on 14 October 1909 in [Salzwedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzwedel), Germany as the son of August Schütte and Martha Schütte (Schröder). From 1916 to 1919, he attended the preschool of the Francisceum [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) in [Zerbst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zerbst), and from 1920 to 1928 attended the [König Wilhelms-Gymnasium in Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/König_Wilhelms-Gymnasium_in_Magdeburg). Schütte studied math, physics, chemistry, and philosophy at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), and got a doctorate degree in math with [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) as his advisor, being Hilbert's last doctoral student. he worked as a [meteorologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) for the Reichswetterdienst (Reich Weather Service) from 1937 until the end of World War II. In 1937 he married Hanna Lechte, with whom he had two daughters: Sigrid (born 1939) and Gisela (born 1942).

Later in life, Schütte became blind and used a tape recording device to write mathematical work. He died of a heart attack in 1998.[^o4]
