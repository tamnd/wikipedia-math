# Line graph of a hypergraph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Line graph of a hypergraph"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), particularly in the theory of [hypergraphs](wikipedia://en/232535), the **line graph of a hypergraph** $H$, denoted $L(H)$, is the [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) whose [vertex](wikipedia://en/638899) set is the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of the hyperedges of $H$, with two vertices adjacent in $L(H)$ when their corresponding hyperedges have a nonempty [intersection](wikipedia://en/41780044) in $H$. In other words, $L(H)$ is the [intersection graph](wikipedia://en/7726759) of a family of [finite sets](wikipedia://en/11742). It is a [generalization](wikipedia://en/12746) of the [line graph](wikipedia://en/675231) of a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806).

:::

Questions about line graphs of hypergraphs are often generalizations of questions about line graphs of graphs. For instance, a hypergraph whose edges all have size $k$ is called **$k$-uniform**. (A 2-uniform hypergraph is a graph). In hypergraph theory, it is often natural to require that hypergraphs be $k$-uniform. Every graph is the line graph of some hypergraph, but, given a fixed edge size $k$, not every graph is a line graph of some $k$-uniform hypergraph. A main problem is to characterize those that are, for each $k \ge 3$.

A hypergraph is **linear** if each pair of hyperedges intersects in at most one vertex. Every graph is the line graph, not only of some hypergraph, but of some linear hypergraph.[^o2]

## Line graphs of *k*-uniform hypergraphs, *k* ≥ 3 {#Line_graphs_of_k-uniform_hypergraphs,_k_≥_3}

Beineke[^o4] characterized line graphs of graphs by a list of 9 [forbidden induced subgraphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_induced_subgraph). (See the article on [line graphs](wikipedia://en/675231).) No characterization by forbidden induced subgraphs is known of line graphs of k-uniform hypergraphs for any *k* ≥ 3, and Lovász[^o5] showed there is no such characterization by a finite list if *k* = 3.

Krausz[^o6] characterized line graphs of graphs in terms of [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) covers. (See [Line Graphs](wikipedia://en/675231#Characterization_and_recognition).) A global characterization of Krausz type for the line graphs of *k*-uniform hypergraphs for any *k* ≥ 3 was given by Berge[^o7]

## Line graphs of *k*-uniform linear hypergraphs, *k* ≥ 3 {#Line_graphs_of_k-uniform_linear_hypergraphs,_k_≥_3}

A global characterization of Krausz type for the line graphs of *k*-uniform linear hypergraphs for any *k* ≥ 3 was given by Naik, Rao, Shrikhande, and Singhi.[^o9] At the same time, they found a finite list of forbidden induced subgraphs for linear 3-uniform hypergraphs with minimum vertex degree at least 69. Metelsky|l and Tyshkevich[^o10] and Jacobson, Kézdy, and Lehel[^o11] improved this bound to 19. At last Skums, Suzdal', and Tyshkevich[^o12] reduced this bound to 16. Metelsky and Tyshkevich[^o13] also proved that, if *k* > 3, no such finite list exists for linear *k*-uniform hypergraphs, no matter what lower bound is placed on the degree.

The difficulty in finding a characterization of linear *k*-uniform hypergraphs is due to the fact that there are infinitely many forbidden induced subgraphs. To give examples, for *m* > 0, consider a chain of *m* [diamond graphs](wikipedia://en/20796238) such that the consecutive diamonds share vertices of degree two. For *k* ≥ 3, add pendant edges at every vertex of degree 2 or 4 to get one of the families of minimal forbidden subgraphs of Naik, Rao, Shrikhande, and Singhi[^o14] as shown here. This does not rule out either the existence of a polynomial recognition or the possibility of a forbidden induced subgraph characterization similar to Beineke's of line graphs of graphs.

*Image: Repeated diamond graph.svg.*

There are some interesting characterizations available for line graphs of linear *k*-uniform hypergraphs due to various authors[^o15] under constraints on the minimum degree or the minimum edge degree of G. Minimum edge degree at least *k*<sup>3</sup>-2*k*<sup>2</sup>+1 in Naik, Rao, Shrikhande, and Singhi[^o16] is reduced to 2*k*<sup>2</sup>-3*k*+1 in Jacobson, Kézdy, and Lehel[^o17] and Zverovich[^o18] to characterize line graphs of *k*-uniform linear hypergraphs for any *k* ≥ 3.

The complexity of recognizing line graphs of linear *k*-uniform hypergraphs without any constraint on minimum degree (or minimum edge-degree) is not known. For *k* = 3 and minimum degree at least 19, recognition is possible in polynomial time.[^o19] Skums, Suzdal', and Tyshkevich[^o20] reduced the minimum degree to 10.

There are many interesting open problems and conjectures in Naik et al., Jacoboson et al., Metelsky et al. and Zverovich.

## Disjointness graph {#Disjointness_graph}

The **disjointness graph** of a hypergraph *H*, denoted D(*H*), is the graph whose vertex set is the set of the hyperedges of *H*, with two vertices adjacent in D(*H*) when their corresponding hyperedges are *disjoint* in *H*.[^o22] In other words, D(*H*) is the [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) of L(*H*). A [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) in D(*H*) corresponds to an independent set in L(*H*), and vice versa.
