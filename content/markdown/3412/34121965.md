# Freudenthal spectral theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Freudenthal spectral theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Freudenthal spectral theorem** is a result in [Riesz space theory](wikipedia://en/8099018) proved by [Hans Freudenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Freudenthal) in 1936. It roughly states that any element dominated by a positive element in a [Riesz space](wikipedia://en/8099018) with the [principal projection property](wikipedia://en/8099018#Projection_properties) can in a sense be approximated uniformly by [simple functions](wikipedia://en/1890594).

:::

Numerous well-known results may be derived from the Freudenthal spectral theorem. The well-known [Radon–Nikodym theorem](wikipedia://en/338746), the validity of the [Poisson formula](wikipedia://en/2214583) and the [spectral theorem](wikipedia://en/123495) from the theory of [normal operators](wikipedia://en/190167) can all be shown to follow as special cases of the Freudenthal spectral theorem.

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Let *e* be any positive element in a Riesz space *E*. A positive element of *p* in *E* is called a component of *e* if $p\wedge(e-p)=0$. If $p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_n$ are pairwise [disjoint](wikipedia://en/8099018#Disjointness) components of *e*, any real linear combination of $p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_n$ is called an *e*-simple function.

The Freudenthal spectral theorem states: Let *E* be any Riesz space with the principal projection property and *e* any positive element in *E*. Then for any element *f* in the principal ideal generated by *e*, there exist sequences $\{s_n\}$ and $\{t_n\}$ of *e*-simple functions, such that $\{s_n\}$ is monotone increasing and converges [*e*-uniformly](wikipedia://en/8099018#Order_convergence) to *f*, and $\{t_n\}$ is monotone decreasing and converges *e*-uniformly to *f*.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Relation to the Radon–Nikodym theorem"}

## Relation to the Radon–Nikodym theorem {#Relation_to_the_Radon–Nikodym_theorem}

Let $(X,\Sigma)$ be a [measure space](wikipedia://en/45270) and $M_\sigma$ the real space of [signed $\sigma$-additive measures](wikipedia://en/2985811#The_space_of_signed_measures) on $(X,\Sigma)$. It can be shown that $M_\sigma$ is a [Dedekind complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_complete) [Banach Lattice](wikipedia://en/54391334) with the [total variation norm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_variation_norm), and hence has the [principal projection property](wikipedia://en/8099018#Projection_properties). For any positive measure $\mu$, $\mu$-simple functions (as defined above) can be shown to correspond exactly to $\mu$-measurable [simple functions](wikipedia://en/1890594) on $(X,\Sigma)$ (in the usual sense). Moreover, since by the Freudenthal spectral theorem, any measure $\nu$ in the [band generated](wikipedia://en/8099018#Bands_and_σ-Ideals) by $\mu$ can be monotonously approximated from below by $\mu$-measurable simple functions on $(X,\Sigma)$, by [Lebesgue's monotone convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/146263#Lebesgue's_monotone_convergence_theorem) $\nu$ can be shown to correspond to an $L^1(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ function and establishes an isometric lattice isomorphism between the band generated by $\mu$ and the Banach Lattice $L^1(X,\Sigma,\mu)$.

:::
