# Chris Stevens (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chris Stevens"}

**Terrie Christine Stevens**, also known as **T. Christine Stevens**, is an American mathematician whose research concerns [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315), the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471).[^o2] She is also known as the co-founder of [Project NExT](wikipedia://en/14984955), a mentorship program for recent doctorates in mathematics, which she directed from 1994 until 2009.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Stevens graduated from [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) in 1970,[^o7] and completed her doctorate in 1978 at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) under the supervision of [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871). Her dissertation was *Weakened Topologies for Lie Groups*.[^o9]

She held teaching positions at the [University of Massachusetts Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Lowell), at [Mount Holyoke College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Holyoke_College) and at [Arkansas State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_State_University) before joining [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_%28United_States%29), where for 25 years she was a professor of mathematics and computer science.[^o13][^o9]

She was also a Congressional Science Fellow assisting congressman [Theodore S. Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_S._Weiss) in 1984–1985,[^o2][^o7] and was a program officer at the [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) in 1987–1989.[^o2] After retiring from SLU, she became Associate Executive Director for Meetings and Professional Services of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o16][^o9] She also served as an [AMS](wikipedia://en/198822) Council member at large from 2011 to 2013.[^o10]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1997 Stevens received the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics).[^o12]

In 2004 she won the Gung and Hu Award for Distinguished Service to Mathematics of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) for her work on Project NExT.[^o9][^o13]

In 2010 she was awarded the Smith College Medal by her alma mater.[^o5][^o7]

She has been a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science) since 2005,[^o14] and in 2012, she became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o15]

She was the 2015 winner of the [Louise Hay Award](wikipedia://en/51270001) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698).[^o16]
