# W. Dale Brownawell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="W. Dale Brownawell"}

**Woodrow Dale Brownawell** (born April 21, 1942) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who has performed research in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). He is a Distinguished Professor emeritus at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University),[^o2] and is particularly known for his proof of explicit degree bounds that can be used to turn [Hilbert's Nullstellensatz](wikipedia://en/149215) into an effective [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775).[^o4][^o3]

:::

Brownawell was born in [Grundy County, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundy_County,_Missouri);[^o2] his father was a farmer and train inspector.[^o4] He earned a double baccalaureate in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) and mathematics (with highest distinction) in 1964 from the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas),[^o2] and after studying for a year at the [University of Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hamburg)[^o2] (at which he met Eva, the woman he later married)[^o4] he returned to the US for graduate study at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).[^o2] His graduate advisor, [Stephen Schanuel](wikipedia://en/6512886), moved to [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in 1969, and Brownawell followed him there for a year,[^o2] but earned his Ph.D. from Cornell in 1970.[^o2] That year, he joined the Penn State faculty, and he remained there until his retirement in 2013.[^o2]

Brownawell and [Michel Waldschmidt](wikipedia://en/35216482) shared the 1986 Hardy–Ramanujan Prize for their independent proofs that at least one of the two numbers $e^e$ and $e^{e^2}$ is a [transcendental number](wikipedia://en/30325); here $e$ denotes [Euler's number](wikipedia://en/9633), approximately 2.718.[^o6] In 2004, a conference at the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo) was held in honor of Brownawell's 60th birthday.[^o7] In 2012, he became one of the inaugural [fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o8]
