# Signal (software)

::: {#o4 .subject cue=lead name="Signal"}

**Signal** is an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software), [encrypted](wikipedia://en/10294) messaging service for [instant messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_messaging), [voice calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_call), and [video calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videotelephony).[^o250][^o5] The instant messaging function includes sending text, voice notes, images, videos, and other files.[^o75] Communication may be one-to-one between users or may involve group messaging.

:::

The application uses a [centralized computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralized_computing) architecture and is [cross-platform software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-platform_software). It is developed by the nonprofit [Signal Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Foundation) and its subsidiary [Signal Messenger LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Messenger_LLC). Signal's software is [free and open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software). Its mobile clients, desktop client, and [server](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_%28computing%29) are all published under the [AGPL-3.0-only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_Affero_General_Public_License) license.[^o235][^o236][^o237][^o238] The official Android app generally uses the proprietary [Google Play Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Play_Services), although it is designed to be able to work without them. Signal is also distributed for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) and desktop programs for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows), [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS), and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux). Registration for desktop use requires an iOS or Android device.[^o6][^o7]

Signal uses mobile [telephone numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_number) to register and manage user accounts, though configurable usernames were added in March 2024 to allow users to hide their phone numbers from other users.[^o8] After removing support for [SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMS) on Android in 2023,[^o106][^o9] the app now secures all communications with [end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362). The client software includes mechanisms by which users can independently verify their contacts' identities and the data channel's integrity.[^o106][^o100]

The [nonprofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization) Signal Foundation was launched in February 2018 with initial funding of \$50 million from [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867) co-founder [Brian Acton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Acton).[^o36] , the platform had approximately 70 million monthly active users. , it had been downloaded more than 220 million times.[^o10][^o11]

## History {#History}

### 2010–2013: Origins {#2010–2013:_Origins}

Signal is the successor of the RedPhone encrypted voice calling app and the [TextSecure](wikipedia://en/42057646) encrypted texting program. The [beta versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_version) of RedPhone and TextSecure were first launched in May 2010 by [Whisper Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_Systems),[^o14] a startup company co-founded by security researcher [Moxie Marlinspike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moxie_Marlinspike) and roboticist Stuart Anderson.[^o230][^o15] Whisper Systems also produced a firewall and tools for encrypting other forms of data.[^o230][^o222] All of these were [proprietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_software) enterprise mobile security software and were only available for Android.

In November 2011, Whisper Systems announced that it had been acquired by [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter). Neither company disclosed the financial terms of the deal.[^o227] The acquisition was done "primarily so that Mr. Marlinspike could help the then-startup improve its security".[^o16] Shortly after the acquisition, Whisper Systems' RedPhone service was made unavailable.[^o228] Some criticized the removal, arguing that the software was "specifically targeted \[to help\] people under repressive regimes" and that it left people like the Egyptians in "a dangerous position" during the events of the [Egyptian revolution of 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_revolution_of_2011).[^o231]

Twitter released TextSecure as [free and open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software) under the [GPLv3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNU_General_Public_License) license in December 2011.[^o230][^o232][^o17][^o229] RedPhone was also released under the same license in July 2012.[^o18] Marlinspike later left Twitter and founded [Open Whisper Systems](wikipedia://en/42081298) as a collaborative Open Source project for the continued development of TextSecure and RedPhone.[^o239][^o223]

### 2013–2018: Open Whisper Systems {#2013–2018:_Open_Whisper_Systems}

Open Whisper Systems' website was launched in January 2013.[^o223]

In February 2014, Open Whisper Systems introduced the second version of their TextSecure Protocol (now [Signal Protocol](wikipedia://en/50607084)), which added end-to-end encrypted group chat and instant messaging capabilities to TextSecure.[^o20] Toward the end of July 2014, they announced plans to merge the RedPhone and TextSecure applications as Signal.[^o241] This announcement coincided with the initial release of Signal as a RedPhone counterpart for [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS). The developers said that their next steps would be to provide TextSecure instant messaging capabilities for iOS, unify the RedPhone and TextSecure applications on Android, and launch a [web client](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_application).[^o241] Signal was the first iOS app to enable end-to-end encrypted voice calls for free.[^o239][^o240] TextSecure compatibility was added to the iOS application in March 2015.[^o221][^o224]

From its launch in May 2010[^o14] until March 2015, the Android version of Signal (then called TextSecure) included support for encrypted SMS/[MMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_Messaging_Service) messaging.[^o21] From version 2.7.0 onward, the Android application only supported sending and receiving encrypted messages via the data channel.[^o22] Reasons for this included security flaws of SMS/MMS and problems with the [key exchange](wikipedia://en/578092).[^o22] Open Whisper Systems' abandonment of SMS/MMS encryption prompted some users to create a [fork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_%28software_development%29) named Silence (initially called SMSSecure[^o23]) that is meant solely for the exchange of encrypted SMS and MMS messages.[^o24][^o25]

In November 2015, the TextSecure and RedPhone applications on Android were merged to become Signal for Android.[^o26] A month later, Open Whisper Systems announced Signal Desktop, a [Chrome app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome_Apps) that could link with a Signal mobile client.[^o27] At launch, the app could only be linked with the Android version of Signal.[^o28] On 26 September 2016, Open Whisper Systems announced that Signal Desktop could now be linked with the iOS version of Signal as well.[^o29] On 31 October 2017, Open Whisper Systems announced that the Chrome app was [deprecated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deprecation).[^o3] At the same time, they announced the release of a standalone desktop client (based on the [Electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_%28software_framework%29) [framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_framework)[^o237]) for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), [macOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS) and certain [Linux distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_distribution).[^o3][^o30]

On 4 October 2016, the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) (ACLU) and Open Whisper Systems published a series of documents revealing that OWS had received a [subpoena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subpoena) requiring them to provide information associated with two phone numbers for a federal [grand jury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_juries_in_the_United_States) investigation in the first half of 2016.[^o31][^o32][^o33] Only one of the two phone numbers was registered on Signal, and because of how the service is designed, OWS was only able to provide "the time the user's account had been created and the last time it had connected to the service".[^o32][^o31] Along with the subpoena, OWS received a [gag order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gag_order) requiring OWS not to tell anyone about the subpoena for one year.[^o31] OWS approached the ACLU, and they were able to lift part of the gag order after challenging it in court.[^o31] OWS said it was the first time they had received a subpoena, and that they were "committed to treating any future requests the same way".[^o33]

In March 2017, Open Whisper Systems transitioned Signal's calling system from RedPhone to [WebRTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebRTC), also adding the ability to make video calls with the mobile apps.[^o119][^o117][^o250]

### Since 2018: Signal Technology Foundation {#Since_2018:_Signal_Technology_Foundation}

On 21 February 2018, Moxie Marlinspike and [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867) co-founder [Brian Acton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Acton) announced the formation of the [Signal Technology Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Technology_Foundation), a [501(c)(3) nonprofit organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/501%28c%29_organization) whose mission is "to support, accelerate, and broaden Signal's mission of making private communication accessible and ubiquitous".[^o35][^o36] Acton started the foundation with \$50 million in funding and became the foundation's executive chairman after leaving WhatsApp's parent company Facebook in September 2017.[^o36] Marlinspike continued as Signal Messenger's first [CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_executive_officer).[^o35] , Signal ran entirely on donations, as a [nonprofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonprofit_organization).[^o249]

Between November 2019 and February 2020, Signal added [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad) support, view-once images and videos, [stickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticker_%28messaging%29), and reactions.[^o37] They also announced plans for a new group messaging system and an "experimental method for storing encrypted contacts in the cloud."[^o37]

Signal was reportedly popularized in the United States during the [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests). Heightened awareness of police monitoring led protesters to use the platform to communicate. [Black Lives Matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Lives_Matter) [organizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activism) had used the platform "for several years".[^o38][^o249] During the first week of June, the encrypted messaging app was downloaded over five times more than it had been during the week prior to the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd).[^o38] In June 2020, Signal Foundation announced a new feature that enables users to blur faces in photos, in response to increased federal efforts to monitor protesters.[^o249][^o39]

On 7 January 2021, Signal saw a surge in new user registrations, which temporarily overwhelmed Signal's capacity to deliver account verification messages.[^o40] [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN) and [MacRumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacRumors) linked the surge with a [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867) privacy policy change and a Signal endorsement by [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) and [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) via Twitter.[^o40][^o41] The surge was also tied to the attack on the [United States Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_United_States_Capitol_attack).[^o42] International newspapers reported similar trends in the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates).[^o43] [Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters) reported that more than 100,000 people had installed Signal between 7 and 8 January.[^o44]

Between 12 and 14 January 2021, the number of Signal installations listed on Google Play increased from over 10 million to over 50 million.[^o45][^o46][^o47][^o48] On 15 January 2021, due to the surge of new users, Signal was overwhelmed with the new traffic and was down for all users.[^o49][^o50] On the afternoon of 16 January, Signal announced via Twitter that service had been restored.[^o51]

On 10 January 2022, Moxie Marlinspike announced that he was stepping down from his role as CEO of Signal Messenger.[^o52] He continues to remain on the Signal Foundation's [board of directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors) and Brian Acton has volunteered to serve as [interim CEO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_CEO) during the search for a new CEO.[^o52]

In August 2022, Signal notified 1900 users that their data had been affected by the [Twilio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilio) breach including user phone numbers and SMS verification codes.[^o53] At least one journalist had his account re-registered to a device he did not control as a result of the attack.[^o54]

In September 2022 Signal Messaging LLC announced that AI researcher and noted critic of big tech [Meredith Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meredith_Whittaker) would fill the newly created position of president.[^o55]

In August 2024, Reuters reported on the blocking of Signal in Venezuela and Russia. In Venezuela, the blocking was a result of protests after the controversial presidential elections in which Nicolas Maduro declared his victory. In Russia, the reason was that Signal did not meet the requirements of legislation in the field of countering terrorism and extremism.[^o56][^o199]

In May 2026, Signal threatened to leave Canada because of the [Lawful Access Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawful_Access_Act),[^o57] stating that "Bill C-22 could potentially allow hackers to exploit these very vulnerabilities engineered into electronic systems, with private messaging services serving as an ideal target for foreign adversaries".[^o58]

### Usage {#Usage}

Signal's userbase started in May 2010, when its predecessor [TextSecure](wikipedia://en/42057646) was launched by [Whisper Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisper_Systems).[^o14] According to App Annie, Signal had approximately 20 million monthly active users at the end of December 2020.[^o60] In January 2022, the BBC reported that Signal was used by over 40 million people.[^o61] In February 2025, Signal had over 7 million [monthly active users](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monthly_active_user) in the USA according to [Similarweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Similarweb).[^o62]

According to [John Ratcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ratcliffe), as of 2025 Signal is installed by default on the devices of most [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) employees and its usage is covered by standard onboarding training.[^o63]

### Developers and funding {#Developers_and_funding}

In November 2023, Meredith Whittaker revealed that she expected the annual cost of running Signal to reach \$50 million in 2025, with the current cost estimated around \$40 million.[^o65] According to its 2024 Form 990 filing with the U.S. Internal Revenue Service, the Signal Technology Foundation recorded \$29,413,537 in revenue against \$38,019,696 in expenses that year.[^o66]

The development of Signal and its predecessors at [Open Whisper Systems](wikipedia://en/42081298) was funded by a combination of consulting contracts, donations and [grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_%28money%29).[^o67] The [Freedom of the Press Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_Press_Foundation) acted as Signal's [fiscal sponsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_sponsorship).[^o35][^o68][^o242] Between 2013 and 2016, the project received grants from the [Knight Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Foundation),[^o243] the [Shuttleworth Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttleworth_Foundation),[^o244] and almost \$3 million from the US government–sponsored [Open Technology Fund](wikipedia://en/49417443).[^o245] Signal is now developed by Signal Messenger LLC, a software company founded by Moxie Marlinspike and [Brian Acton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Acton) in 2018, which is wholly owned by a tax-exempt nonprofit corporation called the [Signal Technology Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Foundation), also created by them in 2018. The Foundation was funded with an initial loan of \$50 million from Acton, "to support, accelerate, and broaden Signal's mission of making private communication accessible and ubiquitous".[^o35][^o36][^o69] All of the organization's products are published as [free and open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_open-source_software).

## Features {#Features}

Signal provides one-to-one and group[^o72] voice and video[^o250] calls with up to seventy-five participants on iOS, Android, and desktop platforms.[^o73][^o74] The calls are carried via the devices' wired or wireless (carrier or [WiFi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WiFi)) data connections.[^o240] The application can send text messages, document files,[^o75] voice notes, pictures, [stickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticker_%28messaging%29), [GIFs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GIF),[^o76] and video messages. The platform also supports group messaging.

All communication sessions between Signal users are automatically [end-to-end encrypted](wikipedia://en/1065362) (the encryption [keys](wikipedia://en/53039) are generated and stored on the devices, and not on servers).[^o234] To verify that a correspondent is really the person that they claim to be, Signal users can compare key fingerprints (or scan QR codes) [out-of-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-band_data).[^o80] The platform employs a [trust-on-first-use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_on_first_use) mechanism to notify the user if a correspondent's key changes.[^o80]

Until 2023, Android users could opt into making Signal the default SMS/MMS application, allowing them to send and receive unencrypted SMS messages in addition to the standard end-to-end encrypted Signal messages.[^o20] Users could then use the same application to communicate with contacts who do not have Signal.[^o20] As of October 2022, this feature has been deprecated due to safety and security concerns, and was removed in 2023.[^o77][^o9]

TextSecure allowed the user to set a passphrase that encrypted the local message database and the user's encryption keys.[^o78] This did not encrypt the user's contact database or message timestamps.[^o78] The Signal applications on Android and iOS can be locked with the phone's pin, passphrase, or biometric authentication.[^o79] The user can define a "screen lock timeout" interval, where Signal will re-encrypt the messages after a certain amount of time, providing an additional protection mechanism in case the phone is lost or stolen.[^o80][^o79]

Signal has a feature for scheduling messages.[^o81] In addition, timers may be attached to messages[^o82] to automatically delete the messages from both the sender's and the receivers' devices.[^o82] The time period for keeping the message may be between five seconds and one week,[^o82] and begins for each recipient once they have read their copy of the message.[^o83] The developers stressed that this is meant to be "a collaborative feature for conversations where all participants want to automate minimal data hygiene, not for situations where the recipient is an adversary".[^o82][^o83]

Signal's app icon may be changed with a variety of colour themes for customization and to hide the app. The application name can also be customized.[^o84] Messages can have effects like spoilers and italics, and users can add each other via QR code.[^o85]

Signal excludes users' messages from non-encrypted cloud backups by default.[^o144]

Signal allows users to automatically blur faces of people in photos to protect identities.[^o86][^o87]

Signal includes a [cryptocurrency wallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet) functionality for storing, sending and receiving in-app payments.[^o88] Apart from certain regions and countries,[^o88] the feature was enabled globally in November 2021.[^o89] , the only supported payment method is [MobileCoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MobileCoin).[^o88]

In February 2024, Signal added a username feature to the beta version of the app. This is a privacy feature that allows users to communicate with others without having to share their telephone number.[^o90][^o91]

## Limitations {#Limitations}

Signal requires that the user provide a telephone number for verification,[^o93] eliminating the need for user names or passwords and facilitating contact discovery (see below).[^o94] The number does not have to be the same as on the device's SIM card; it can be a [VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VoIP) number[^o93] or a landline as long as the user can receive the verification code and has a separate device to set up the software. A number can be registered on only one mobile device at a time.[^o95] Account registration requires an iOS or Android device.[^o6][^o7]

This mandatory connection to a phone number (a feature Signal shares with [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867), [KakaoTalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KakaoTalk), and others) has been criticized as a "major issue" for privacy-conscious users who are not comfortable giving out their private number.[^o94] A workaround is to use a secondary phone number.[^o94] The ability to choose a public, changeable username instead of sharing one's phone number was a widely requested feature.[^o94][^o96][^o97] This feature was added to the beta version of Signal in February 2024.[^o90]

Using phone numbers as identifiers may also create security risks if an attacker takes over a phone number.[^o94] A similar vulnerability was used to attack at least one user in August 2022, though the attack was performed via the provider of Signal's SMS services, not any user's provider.[^o53] The threat of this attack can be mitigated by enabling Signal's Registration Lock feature, a form of [multi-factor authentication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-factor_authentication) that requires the user to enter a PIN to register the phone number on a new device.[^o98]

## Usability {#Usability}

In July 2016, the [Internet Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Society) published a [user study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_study) that assessed the ability of Signal users to detect and deter [man-in-the-middle attacks](wikipedia://en/144676).[^o100] The study concluded that 21 out of 28 participants failed to correctly compare [public key fingerprints](wikipedia://en/7943312) in order to verify the identity of other Signal users, and that most of these users believed they had succeeded, while they had actually failed.[^o100] Four months later, Signal's user interface was updated to make verifying the identity of other Signal users simpler.[^o101]

## Architecture {#Architecture}

### Encryption protocols {#Encryption_protocols}

Signal messages are encrypted with the Signal Protocol (formerly known as the TextSecure Protocol). The protocol combines the [Double Ratchet Algorithm](wikipedia://en/49111333), prekeys, and an Extended Triple [Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/7903) (X3DH) handshake.[^o104][^o105] It uses [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444), [AES-256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AES-256), and [HMAC-SHA256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAC-SHA256) as [primitives](wikipedia://en/4928985).[^o106] The protocol provides confidentiality, integrity, [authentication](wikipedia://en/2244296), participant consistency, destination validation, [forward secrecy](wikipedia://en/2191130), backward secrecy ( future secrecy), causality preservation, message unlinkability, [message repudiation](wikipedia://en/9246275), participation repudiation, and [asynchronicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asynchronous_communication).[^o107] It does not provide anonymity preservation, and requires servers for the relaying of messages and storing of public key material.[^o107]

The Signal Protocol also supports end-to-end encrypted group chats. The group chat protocol is a combination of a pairwise double ratchet and [multicast encryption](wikipedia://en/31679498).[^o107] In addition to the properties provided by the one-to-one protocol, the group chat protocol provides speaker consistency, out-of-order resilience, dropped message resilience, computational equality, trust equality, subgroup messaging, as well as contractible and expandable membership.[^o107] According to a 2023 technical report, Signal adopted a protocol called [Sender Keys](wikipedia://en/80630265) for group messaging, and favors the protocol where possible (rather than the double ratchet) for group messaging, even in small groups.[^o108]

In October 2014, researchers from [Ruhr University Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_University_Bochum) (RUB) published an analysis of the Signal Protocol.[^o106] Among other findings, they presented an [unknown key-share attack](wikipedia://en/22828799) on the protocol, but in general, they found that it was secure.[^o220] In October 2016, researchers from UK's [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), [Queensland University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_University_of_Technology) in Australia, and Canada's [McMaster University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMaster_University) published a formal analysis of the protocol.[^o109][^o110] They concluded that the protocol was cryptographically sound.[^o109][^o110] In July 2017, researchers from RUB found during another analysis of group messengers a purely theoretic attack against the group protocol of Signal: A user who knows the secret group ID of a group (due to having been a group member previously or stealing it from a member's device) can become a member of the group. Since the group ID cannot be guessed and such member changes are displayed to the remaining members, this attack is likely to be difficult to carry out without being detected.[^o111]

, the Signal Protocol has been implemented into [WhatsApp](wikipedia://en/32058867), [Facebook Messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Messenger), [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype),[^o112] and [Google Allo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Allo),[^o113] making it possible for the conversations of "more than a billion people worldwide" to be end-to-end encrypted.[^o114] In Google Allo, Skype and Facebook Messenger, conversations are not encrypted with the Signal Protocol by default; they only offer end-to-end encryption in an optional mode.[^o144][^o115][^o112][^o116]

Up until March 2017, Signal's voice calls were encrypted with [SRTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Real-time_Transport_Protocol) and the [ZRTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZRTP) key-agreement protocol, which was developed by [Phil Zimmermann](wikipedia://en/23486).[^o239][^o142] In March 2017, Signal transitioned to a new [WebRTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebRTC)-based calling system that introduced the ability to make video calls.[^o117] Signal's voice and video calling functionalities use the Signal Protocol channel for authentication instead of ZRTP.[^o118][^o119][^o250]

#### Authentication {#Authentication}

To verify that a correspondent is really the person that they claim to be, Signal users can compare key fingerprints (or scan QR codes) [out-of-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-band_data).[^o80] The platform employs a [trust on first use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_on_first_use) mechanism in order to notify the user if a correspondent's key changes.[^o80]

### Local storage {#Local_storage}

After receiving and decrypting messages, the application stored them locally on each device in a [SQLite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SQLite) database that is encrypted with SQLCipher.[^o123] The cryptographic key for this database is also stored locally and can be accessed if the device is unlocked.[^o123][^o122] In December 2020, [Cellebrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellebrite) published a blog post announcing that one of their products could now access this key and use it to "decrypt the Signal app".[^o123][^o124] Technology reporters later published articles about how Cellebrite had claimed to have the ability to "break into the Signal app" and "crack Signal's encryption".[^o125][^o126] This latter interpretation was rejected by several experts,[^o127] as well as representatives from Signal, who said the original post by Cellebrite had been about accessing data on "an unlocked Android phone in their physical possession" and that they "could have just opened the app to look at the messages".[^o128][^o129] Similar extraction tools also exist for iOS devices and Signal Desktop.[^o130][^o131] In April 2021, Moxie Marlinspike responded with a blog post highlighting input validation vulnerabilities in Cellebrite's [UFED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellebrite_UFED) and Physical Analyzer software. He also alleged that the company had potentially engaged in license violations by redistributing compiled software libraries from Apple without authorization.[^o132]

### Servers {#Servers}

Signal uses infrastructure from large providers like [Amazon Web Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services), [Google Compute Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Compute_Engine) and [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure) around the world.[^o134] In addition to routing Signal's messages, the servers also facilitate the discovery of contacts who are also registered Signal users and the automatic [exchange](wikipedia://en/578092) of users' [public keys](wikipedia://en/24222). By default, Signal's voice and video calls are [peer-to-peer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer-to-peer).[^o250] If the caller is not in the receiver's address book, the call is routed through a server in order to hide the users' [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address).[^o250]

#### Contact discovery {#Contact_discovery}

The servers store registered users' phone numbers, public key material and push tokens which are necessary for setting up calls and transmitting messages.[^o136] In order to determine which contacts are also Signal users, [cryptographic hashes](wikipedia://en/439526) of the user's contact numbers are periodically transmitted to the server.[^o138] The server then checks to see if those match any of the [SHA-256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA-256) hashes of registered users and tells the client if any matches are found.[^o138] The hashed numbers are thereafter discarded from the server.[^o136] In 2014, Moxie Marlinspike wrote that it is easy to calculate a map of all possible hash inputs to hash outputs and reverse the mapping because of the limited [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) space (the set of all possible hash inputs) of phone numbers, and that a "practical privacy preserving contact discovery remains an unsolved problem."[^o137][^o138] In September 2017, Signal's developers announced that they were working on a way for the Signal client applications to "efficiently and scalably determine whether the contacts in their address book are Signal users without revealing the contacts in their address book to the Signal service."[^o139][^o140]

#### Metadata {#Metadata}

All client–server communications are protected by [TLS](wikipedia://en/187813).[^o142][^o143] Signal's developers have asserted that their servers do not keep logs about who called whom and when.[^o246] In June 2016, Marlinspike told *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* that "the closest piece of information to metadata that the Signal server stores is the last time each user connected to the server, and the precision of this information is reduced to the day, rather than the hour, minute, and second".[^o144]

The group messaging mechanism is designed so that the servers do not have access to the membership list, group title, or group icon.[^o22] Instead, the creation, updating, joining, and leaving of groups is done by the clients, which deliver pairwise messages to the participants in the same way that one-to-one messages are delivered.[^o145][^o146]

#### Federation {#Federation}

Signal's server architecture was [federated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_%28information_technology%29) between December 2013 and February 2016. In December 2013, it was announced that the messaging protocol Signal uses had successfully been integrated into the Android-based open-source operating system [CyanogenMod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CyanogenMod).[^o148][^o149][^o150] Since CyanogenMod 11.0, the client logic was contained in a system app called WhisperPush. According to Signal's developers, the Cyanogen team ran their own Signal messaging server for WhisperPush clients, which federated with the main server, so that both clients could exchange messages with each other.[^o150] The WhisperPush source code was available under the GPLv3 license.[^o151] In February 2016, the CyanogenMod team discontinued WhisperPush and recommended that its users switch to Signal.[^o152] In May 2016, Moxie Marlinspike wrote that federation with the CyanogenMod servers had degraded the user experience and held back development, and that their servers will probably not federate with other servers again.[^o153]

In May 2016, Moxie Marlinspike requested that a third-party client called LibreSignal not use the Signal service or the Signal name.[^o153] As a result, on 24 May 2016 the LibreSignal project posted that the project was "abandoned".[^o154] The functionality provided by LibreSignal was subsequently incorporated into Signal by Marlinspike.[^o155]

### Licensing {#Licensing}

The complete [source code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_code) of the Signal clients for Android, iOS and desktop is available on [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub) under a [free software license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software_license).[^o235][^o236][^o237] This enables interested parties to examine the code and help the developers verify that everything is behaving as expected. It also allows advanced users to compile their own copies of the applications and compare them with the versions that are distributed by Signal Messenger. In March 2016, Moxie Marlinspike wrote that, apart from some shared libraries that are not compiled with the project build due to a lack of [Gradle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradle) [NDK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_NDK) support, Signal for Android is [reproducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_compilation).[^o157] Instructions for creating and verifying a reproducible build of the application can be found in the GitHub repository.[^o158] Signal's servers are mostly open source, but the server software's anti-spam component is proprietary and closed source due to abuse concerns.[^o238][^n1]

## Reception {#Reception}

### Security {#Security}

In October 2014, the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation) (EFF) included Signal in their updated surveillance self-defense guide.[^o233] In November 2014, Signal received a perfect score on the EFF's secure messaging scorecard;[^o234] it received points for having communications encrypted in transit, having communications encrypted with keys the provider does not have access to ([end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362)), making it possible for users to independently verify their correspondents' identities, having past communications secure if the keys are stolen ([forward secrecy](wikipedia://en/2191130)), having the code open to independent review ([free software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_software)), having the security designs well-documented, and having a recent independent security audit.[^o234] At the time, "[ChatSecure](wikipedia://en/41518933) + [Orbot](wikipedia://en/34650947)", [Pidgin](wikipedia://en/188211) (with [OTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-Record_Messaging)), [Silent Phone](wikipedia://en/38503539), and [Telegram](wikipedia://en/41501305)'s optional "secret chats" also received seven out of seven points on the scorecard.[^o234]

Former [NSA](wikipedia://en/21939) contractor [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) has endorsed Signal on multiple occasions.[^o27] In his keynote speech at [SXSW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_by_Southwest) in March 2014, he praised Signal's predecessors (TextSecure and RedPhone) for their ease of use.[^o247][^o248] In December 2014, [Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel) leaked slides from an internal NSA presentation dating to June 2012 in which the NSA deemed Signal's encrypted voice calling component (RedPhone) on its own as a "major threat" to its mission of accessing users' private data, and when used in conjunction with other privacy tools such as Cspace, [Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_%28anonymity_network%29), [Tails](wikipedia://en/31672755), and [TrueCrypt](wikipedia://en/905962) was ranked as "catastrophic" and led to a "near-total loss/lack of insight to target communications \[and\] presence".[^o226][^o225]

Following the [2016 Democratic National Committee email leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Democratic_National_Committee_email_leak), it was reported by *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_%28magazine%29)* that [Marc Elias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Elias) (the general counsel for [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton)'s presidential campaign) had instructed [DNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_National_Committee) staffers to exclusively use Signal when saying anything negative about Republican presidential nominee [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump).[^o161][^o162]

In March 2017, Signal was approved by the sergeant at arms of the [U.S. Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Senate) for use by senators and their staff.[^o163][^o164]

On 27 September 2019, Natalie Silvanovich, a security engineer working in [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google)'s vulnerability research team at [Project Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Zero), disclosed how a bug in the [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29) Signal client could let an attacker spy on a user without their knowledge.[^o165] The bug allowed an attacker to phone a target device, mute the call, and the call would complete – keeping the audio open but without the owner being aware of that (however they would still be aware of a ring and / or a vibration from the initial call).[^o166] The bug was fixed the same day that it was reported and patched in release 4.47.7 of the app for Android.[^o167]

In February 2020, the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission) recommended that its staff use Signal.[^o168] Following the [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests), which began in May 2020, Signal was downloaded 121,000 times in the U.S. between 25 May and 4 June.[^o169] In July 2020, Signal became the most downloaded app in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) on both the Apple App Store and the Google Play Store after the passage of the [Hong Kong national security law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Hong_Kong_national_security_law).[^o170]

, Signal is a contact method for securely providing tips to major news outlets such as *[The Washington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Post)*,[^o171] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*,[^o172] *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)*,[^o173] and *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal)*.[^o174]

The spyware companies [Candiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candiru_%28spyware_company%29) and [FinFisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FinFisher) claim the ability to extract messages from Signal when installed on a phone using their spyware.[^o175][^o176] Some forks of Signal attempt to combat this by encrypting [data at rest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_at_rest).[^o177]

On 9 August 2022, [Ismail Sabri Yaakob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Sabri_Yaakob), the [Prime Minister of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Malaysia), reported that his Signal account was "hacked" and infiltrated by a third party, sending out messages and impersonating the politician. No details were disclosed regarding the method used to gain access to the account.[^o178]

### In-app payments {#In-app_payments}

In April 2021, Signal announced the addition of a [cryptocurrency wallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet) feature that would allow users to send and receive payments in [MobileCoin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MobileCoin).[^o180] This received criticism from security expert [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732), who had previously praised the software. Schneier stated that this would bloat the client and attract unwanted attention from the authorities.[^o181] The wallet functionality was initially only available in certain countries, but was later enabled globally in November 2021.[^o89]

### Blocking {#Blocking}

*Image: Countries\_where\_Signal\_provides\_domain\_fronting.svg.* Countries where\#346733\#8E0000

In December 2016, [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt) blocked access to Signal.[^o183] In response, Signal's developers added [domain fronting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_fronting) to their service.[^o184] This allows Signal users in a specific country to circumvent censorship by making it look like they are connecting to a different internet-based service.[^o184][^o185] , Signal's domain fronting is enabled by default in Egypt, [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates), [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman), [Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran), [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba), [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan) and [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan).[^o186]

, Signal was blocked in Iran.[^o187][^o188] Signal's domain fronting feature relies on the [Google App Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_App_Engine) (GAE) service.[^o188][^o187] This does not work in Iran because Google has blocked Iranian access to GAE in order to comply with U.S. sanctions.[^o187][^o189]

In early 2018, [Google App Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_App_Engine) made an internal change to stop domain fronting for all countries. Due to this issue, Signal made a public change to use [Amazon CloudFront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_CloudFront) for domain fronting. However, [AWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services) also announced that they would be making changes to their service to prevent domain fronting. As a result, Signal said that they would start investigating new methods/approaches.[^o190][^o191] Signal switched from AWS back to Google in April 2019.[^o192]

In January 2021, Iran removed the app from app stores,[^o193][^o194] and blocked Signal.[^o195] Signal was later blocked by China in March 2021, followed by its removal from the App Store in China on 19 April 2024.[^o196][^o197]

On August 9, 2024, Signal was blocked in Russia. [Roskomnadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskomnadzor) claimed that this was due to "violations of the law on combating terrorism and extremism".[^o199][^o198] Around the same time, Signal was also blocked in Venezuela following the contested [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Venezuelan_presidential_election) and subsequent protests.[^o199]

### Audience {#Audience}

#### Use by activists {#Use_by_activists}

In 2020, the app was used for coordination and communication by protesters during the [George Floyd protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) as they relied on the app's end-to-end encryption to share information securely.[^o38]

In March 2021, the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations) recommended Myanmar residents use Signal and [Proton Mail](wikipedia://en/43191172) to pass and preserve evidence of human rights violations committed during the [2021 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Myanmar_coup_d'état).[^o202]

#### Controversial use {#Controversial_use}

Signal's [terms of service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_service) states that the product may not be used to violate the law.[^o204] According to a former employee, Signal's leadership at the time told him they would say something "if and when people start abusing Signal or doing things that we think are terrible".[^o204] In January 2021, the position of Signal's leadership was to take a "hands-off approach to moderation" as the company's employees are not able to read user messages and the Signal Foundation does not "want to be a media company".[^o204][^o110]

In 2016, authorities in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) arrested members of a suspected [ISIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISIS)-affiliated terrorist cell that communicated via Signal.[^o205]

[Radical right-wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_%28United_States%29) militias and white nationalists use Signal for organizing their actions, including the [Unite the Right II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unite_the_Right_II) rally in 2018.[^o206][^o207][^o208][^o209]

The claim that Signal is used to fund terrorist or criminal activities is the justification for [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey) to criminalize the app for the general population, which [Abdullah Bozkurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Bozkurt) claims is a way the "government abuses its counterterrorism laws to punish critics, opponents and dissidents."[^o210][^o211]

#### Use by Trump administration {#Use_by_Trump_administration}

In March 2025, it was revealed that senior members of the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Trump_administration)—including vice president [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance), secretary of state [Marco Rubio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Rubio), and defense secretary [Pete Hegseth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hegseth)—were using Signal to discuss sensitive information, including details of [military attack plans in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March–May_2025_United_States_attacks_in_Yemen). The existence of the chat was disclosed by *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* editor-in-chief [Jeffrey Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Goldberg), who was accidentally added to the chat. Brian Hughes of the [National Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Security_Council) later confirmed the authenticity of Goldberg's account.[^o213][^o214][^o215]

On October 4, 2025, *[The Minnesota Star Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Minnesota_Star_Tribune)* reported that [Deputy Homeland Security Advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Advisor) [Anthony Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Salisbury) had been seen in public exchanging information on Signal with other government officials. Subjects discussed included potential [troop deployments to Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_deployment_of_federal_forces_in_the_United_States#Portland,_Oregon), upcoming [Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) operations in Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Midway_Blitz), Vice President [JD Vance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JD_Vance)'s desire to prosecute two unnamed Florida [influencers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influencers), and [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Bureau_of_Investigation) director [Kash Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kash_Patel)'s firing of FBI employees.[^o216]
