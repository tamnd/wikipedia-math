# Giuseppe Melfi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giuseppe Melfi"}

**Giuseppe Melfi** (June 11, 1967) is an [Italo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy)-[Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) mathematician who works on [practical numbers](wikipedia://en/1205310) and [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000).

:::

## Career {#Career}

He gained his PhD in mathematics in 1997 at the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa). After some time spent at the [University of Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lausanne) during 1997-2000, Melfi was appointed at the [University of Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Neuchâtel), as well as at the [University of Applied Sciences Western Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Applied_Sciences_Western_Switzerland) and at the local University of Teacher Education.

## Work {#Work}

His major contributions are in the field of [practical numbers](wikipedia://en/1205310). This prime-like sequence of numbers is known for having an asymptotic behavior and other distribution properties similar to the sequence of [primes](wikipedia://en/23666). Melfi proved two conjectures both raised in 1984:[^o6] the first one states that every even number is a sum of two practical numbers, a property that is still unproven for primes. The second one is that there exist infinitely many triples of practical numbers of the form $m-2,m,m+2$. He also proved that there exist infinitely many [Fibonacci numbers](wikipedia://en/10918) that are practicals[^o7]

Another notable contribution has been in an application of the theory of [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms), where he found new [Ramanujan-type identities](wikipedia://en/1270458) for the [sum-of-divisor functions](wikipedia://en/407249). His seven new identities extended the ten other identities found by Ramanujan in 1913.[^o8] In particular he found the remarkable identity

$$\sum_{\stackrel{0< k< n}{k\equiv1\bmod3}} \sigma(k)\sigma(n-k)=\frac19\sigma_3(n) \qquad \mbox{ for }n\equiv2\bmod3$$

where $\sigma(n)$ is the sum of the divisors of $n$ and $\sigma_3(n)$ is the sum of the third powers of the divisors of $n$.

In 1996 [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) wrote him two letters[^o9][^o10] in which several problems were proposed. They met in [Eger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eger) in July [^o11] , but Erdős died in September. In the subsequent decade Melfi studied some [Erdős problems](wikipedia://en/601284#Erdős's_problems) with some notable contribution[^o12][^o13][^o14][^o15] in particular concerning [sum-free sequences](wikipedia://en/1938936).

Among other problems in elementary [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), he is the author of a theorem that allowed him to get a 5328-digit number that has been for a while the largest known primitive [weird number](wikipedia://en/321930). Since 2019, with a 14712-digits primitive weird number, he shares this record, with a team of collaborators.[^o16]

In applied mathematics his research interests include [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation).
