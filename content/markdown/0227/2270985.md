# Donald A. Martin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donald A. Martin"}

*Image: Donald Martin May 2004.jpg.* Martin in 2004

**Donald Anthony Martin** (born December 24, 1940), also known as **Tony Martin**, is an American [set theorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_theorist) and [philosopher of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher_of_mathematics) at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), where he is an emeritus professor of mathematics and [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Department_of_Philosophy).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Martin received his B.S. from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1962 and was a [Junior Fellow of the Harvard Society of Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Harvard_Junior_Fellows) in 1965–67.[^o3] In 2014, he became a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o4]

Martin was the 1992 [Tarski lecturer](wikipedia://en/54104355).

## Philosophical and mathematical work {#Philosophical_and_mathematical_work}

Among Martin's most notable works are the proofs of [analytic](wikipedia://en/634261) [determinacy](wikipedia://en/2596030)[^o6] (from the existence of a [measurable cardinal](wikipedia://en/248102)), [Borel determinacy](wikipedia://en/15483838)[^o7] (from [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) alone), the proof (with [John R. Steel](wikipedia://en/2290967)) of [projective determinacy](wikipedia://en/1236592)[^o8] (from suitable [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) axioms), and his work on [Martin's axiom](wikipedia://en/1550783).[^o9] The [Martin measure](wikipedia://en/11016148) on [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405) and the [Martin's Conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin's_Conjecture) on Turing invariant functions are also named after Martin.

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Martin's conjecture"}

### Martin's conjecture {#Martin's_conjecture}

In mathematics, more precisely in [recursion theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursion_theory), Martin's conjecture states, in essence, that the only nontrivial definable Turing invariant functions are the Turing jump and its iterates through the transfinite. Martin made this conjecture in the late 1970s; it first appeared in print as item 5 in the list titled “The Victoria Delfino problems” which was published as an appendix[^o12] to a volume of proceedings of the joint Caltech-UCLA Logic Seminar.

:::
