# Kingman's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kingman's formula"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), **Kingman's formula**, also known as the VUT equation, is an approximation for the mean waiting time in a [G/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/38677129).[^o2] The formula is the product of three terms which depend on utilization (U), variability (V) and service time (T). It was first published by [John Kingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kingman) in his 1961 paper *The single server queue in heavy traffic*.[^o3] It is known to be generally very accurate, especially for a system operating close to saturation.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="Statement of formula"}

## Statement of formula {#Statement_of_formula}

Kingman's approximation states:

$$\mathbb E(W_q) \approx \left( \frac{\rho}{1-\rho} \right) \left( \frac{c_a^2+c_s^2}{2}\right) \tau$$

where $\mathbb E(W_q)$ is the mean waiting time, *τ* is the mean service time (i.e. *μ* = 1/*τ* is the service rate), *λ* is the mean arrival rate, *ρ* = *λ*/*μ* is the utilization, *c<sub>a</sub>* is the [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) for arrivals (that is the standard deviation of arrival times divided by the mean arrival time) and *c<sub>s</sub>* is the coefficient of variation for service times.

:::
