# Taxicab number

*Image: Srinivasa Ramanujan - OPC - 2.jpg.* [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) (*picture*) was bedridden when he developed the idea of taxicab numbers, according to an anecdote from [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Taxicab number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the *n*th **taxicab number**, typically denoted Ta(*n*) or Taxicab(*n*), is defined as the smallest integer that can be expressed as a sum of two *positive* [integer cubes](wikipedia://en/451445) in *n* distinct ways.[^o2] The most famous taxicab number is [1729](wikipedia://en/512549) = Ta(2) = 1<sup>3</sup> + 12<sup>3</sup> = 9<sup>3</sup> + 10<sup>3</sup>, also known as the Hardy–Ramanujan number.[^o3][^o4]

:::

The name is derived from a conversation *ca.*1919 involving [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717). As told by Hardy:

I remember once going to see him \[Ramanujan\] when he was lying ill at [Putney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putney). I had ridden in taxi-cab No. [1729](wikipedia://en/512549), and remarked that the number seemed to be rather a dull one, and that I hoped it was not an unfavourable omen. "No," he replied, "it is a very interesting number; it is the smallest number expressible as the sum of two cubes in two different ways."[^o5][^o6]

## History and definition {#History_and_definition}

The pairs of summands of the Hardy–Ramanujan number Ta(2) = 1729 were first mentioned by [Bernard Frénicle de Bessy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Frénicle_de_Bessy), who published his observation in 1657. 1729 was made famous as the first taxicab number in the early 20th century by a story involving [Srinivasa Ramanujan](wikipedia://en/47717) in claiming it to be the smallest for his particular example of two summands. In 1938, [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [E. M. Wright](wikipedia://en/529941) proved that such numbers exist for all positive [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) *n*, and their proof is easily converted into a [computer program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_program) to generate such numbers. However, the proof makes no claims about whether the thus-generated numbers are *the smallest possible* and so it does not directly provide the actual value of Ta(*n*).

The taxicab numbers subsequent to 1729 were found with the help of computers. [John Leech](wikipedia://en/4548516) obtained Ta(3) in 1957. E. Rosenstiel, J. A. Dardis and C. R. Rosenstiel found Ta(4) in 1989.[^o8] J. A. Dardis found Ta(5) in 1994 and it was confirmed by David W. Wilson in 1999.[^o9][^n1] Ta(6) was announced by Uwe Hollerbach on the NMBRTHRY [mailing list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mailing_list) on March 9, 2008,[^o11] following a 2003 paper by Calude et al. that gave a 99% probability that the number was actually Ta(6).[^n2] Upper bounds for Ta(7) to Ta(12) were found by Christian Boyer in 2006.[^o13]

The restriction of the [summands](wikipedia://en/61338#Notation_and_terminology) to positive numbers is necessary, because allowing negative numbers allows for more (and smaller) instances of numbers that can be expressed as sums of cubes in *n* distinct ways. . The concept of a [cabtaxi number](wikipedia://en/678138) has been introduced to allow for alternative, less restrictive definitions of this nature. In a sense, the specification of two summands and [powers of three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_three) is also restrictive; a [generalized taxicab number](wikipedia://en/678194) allows for these values to be other than two and three, respectively.

## Cubefree taxicab numbers {#Cubefree_taxicab_numbers}

A more restrictive taxicab problem requires that the taxicab number be [cubefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubefree), which means that it is not divisible by any cube other than 1<sup>3</sup>. When a cubefree taxicab number $T$ is written as $T = x^{3} + y^{3}$, the numbers $x$ and $y$ must be [relatively prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_prime). Among the taxicab numbers $\operatorname{Ta}(n)$ listed above, only $\operatorname{Ta}(1)$ and $\operatorname{Ta}(2)$ are cubefree taxicab numbers. The smallest cubefree taxicab number with three representations was discovered by [Paul Vojta](wikipedia://en/1802312) (unpublished) in 1981 while he was a graduate student:

$$\begin{aligned} 15170835645 &= 517^3 + 2468^3 \\ &= 709^3 + 2456^3 \\ &= 1733^3 + 2152^3 \end{aligned}$$

The smallest cubefree taxicab number with four representations was discovered by Stuart Gascoigne and independently by Duncan Moore in 2003:

$$\begin{aligned} 1801049058342701083 &= 92227^3 + 1216500^3 \\ &= 136635^3 + 1216102^3 \\ &= 341995^3 + 1207602^3 \\ &= 600259^3 + 1165884^3 \end{aligned}$$
