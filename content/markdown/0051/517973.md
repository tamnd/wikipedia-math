# Jakob Rosanes

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jakob Rosanes"}

**Jakob Rosanes** (also **Jacob**; 16 August 1842 – 6 January 1922) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who worked on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) and [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762). He was also a [chess master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_master).[^o3]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

*Image: Grab Jakob Rosanes.jpg.* Grave of Jacob Rosanes at the Old Jewish Cemetery in Breslau

Rosanes was a grandson of Rabbi [Akiva Eiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Eiger), one of the most revered Jewish religious scholars of the [Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud) and *halachic* decisors of the 18th century. Eiger's daughter Baila was Rosanes' mother. Rosanes grew up during a period when the [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) and increasing opportunities for social, academic and economic advancement for culturally assimilated Jews influenced large numbers of Jews to reconsider their faith. He was not religiously observant, and his children converted to [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity).

Rosanes studied at [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau). He obtained his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) from Breslau ([Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław)) in 1865 and taught there for the rest of his working life. He became [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) in 1876 and [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of the university during the years 1903–1904.[^o5] In his ‘inspiring’ rectorial speech on 15 October 1903, he described the development of mathematics in the nineteenth century.[^o6]

Rosanes made significant contributions in [Cremona transformations](wikipedia://en/7252026).
