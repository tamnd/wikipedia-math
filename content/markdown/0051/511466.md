# Computational number theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Computational number theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), **computational number theory**, also known as **algorithmic number theory**, is the study of [computational methods](wikipedia://en/5926) for investigating and solving problems in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177), including algorithms for [primality testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primality_testing) and [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491), finding solutions to [diophantine equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_equations), and explicit methods in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). Computational number theory has applications to [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), including [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28cryptosystem%29), [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography) and [post-quantum cryptography](wikipedia://en/26605226), and is used to investigate [conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjectures) and [open problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_problems) in number theory, including the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125), the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161), the [ABC conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_conjecture), the [modularity conjecture](wikipedia://en/174475), the [Sato-Tate conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sato-Tate_conjecture), and explicit aspects of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905).

:::
