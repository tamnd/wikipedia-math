# Canonical form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canonical form"}

*Image: Anagram canonical svg.svg.* Algorithmic [anagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagram) test using [multisets](wikipedia://en/305303) as canonical forms: The strings "`madam curie`" and "`radium came`" are given as [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_%28programming_language%29) arrays. Each one is converted into a canonical form by sorting. Since both sorted strings literally agree, the original strings were anagrams of each other.

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science), a **canonical**, **normal**, or **standard** **form** of a [mathematical object](wikipedia://en/19453961) is a standard way of presenting that object as a [mathematical expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_expression). Often, it is one which provides the simplest representation of an object and allows it to be identified in a unique way. The distinction between "canonical" and "normal" forms varies from subfield to subfield. In most fields, a canonical form specifies a *unique* representation for every object, while a normal form simply specifies its form, without the requirement of uniqueness.[^o2]

:::

The canonical form of a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer) in [decimal representation](wikipedia://en/3293594) is a finite sequence of digits that does not begin with zero. More generally, for a class of objects on which an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) is defined, a canonical form consists in the choice of a specific object in each class. For example:

- [Jordan normal form](wikipedia://en/312877) is a canonical form for [matrix similarity](wikipedia://en/341149).
- The [row echelon form](wikipedia://en/330215) is a canonical form, when one considers as equivalent a matrix and its left product by an [invertible matrix](wikipedia://en/217122).

In computer science, and more specifically in [computer algebra](wikipedia://en/23659805), when representing mathematical objects in a computer, there are usually many different ways to represent the same object. In this context, a canonical form is a representation such that every object has a unique representation (with [canonicalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonicalization) being the process through which a representation is put into its canonical form).[^o3] Thus, the equality of two objects can easily be tested by testing the equality of their canonical forms.

Despite this advantage, canonical forms frequently depend on arbitrary choices (like ordering the variables), which introduce difficulties for testing the equality of two objects resulting in independent computations. Therefore, in computer algebra, *normal form* is a weaker notion: A normal form is a representation such that zero is uniquely represented. This allows testing for equality by putting the difference of two objects in normal form.

Canonical form can also mean a [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) that is defined in a natural (canonical) way.

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Given a set *S* of objects with an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) *R on S*, a canonical form is given by designating some objects of *S* to be "in canonical form", such that every object under consideration is equivalent to exactly one object in canonical form. In other words, the canonical forms in *S* represent the equivalence classes, once and only once. To test whether two objects are equivalent, it then suffices to test equality on their canonical forms. A canonical form thus provides a [classification theorem](wikipedia://en/4576710) and more, in that it not only classifies every class, but also gives a distinguished (canonical) [representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_%28mathematics%29) for each object in the class.

Formally, a canonicalization with respect to an equivalence relation *R* on a set *S* is a mapping *c*:*S*→*S* such that for all *s*, *s*<sub>1</sub>, *s*<sub>2</sub> ∈ *S*:

1. *c*(*s*) = *c*(*c*(*s*)) ([idempotence](wikipedia://en/14972)),
1. *s*<sub>1</sub> *R* *s*<sub>2</sub> if and only if *c*(*s*<sub>1</sub>) = *c*(*s*<sub>2</sub>) (decisiveness), and
1. *s* *R* *c*(*s*) (representativeness).

Property 3 is redundant; it follows by applying 2 to 1.

In practical terms, it is often advantageous to be able to recognize the canonical forms. There is also a practical, algorithmic question to consider: how to pass from a given object *s* in *S* to its canonical form *s*\*? Canonical forms are generally used to make operating with equivalence classes more effective. For example, in [modular arithmetic](wikipedia://en/20087), the canonical form for a residue class is usually taken as the least non-negative integer in it. Operations on classes are carried out by combining these representatives, and then reducing the result to its least non-negative residue. The uniqueness requirement is sometimes relaxed, allowing the forms to be unique up to some finer equivalence relation, such as allowing for reordering of terms (if there is no natural ordering on terms).

A canonical form may simply be a convention, or a deep theorem. For example, polynomials are conventionally written with the terms in descending powers: it is more usual to write *x*<sup>2</sup> + *x* + 30 than *x* + 30 + *x*<sup>2</sup>, although the two forms define the same polynomial. By contrast, the existence of [Jordan canonical form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_canonical_form) for a matrix is a deep theorem.

:::

## History {#History}

According to [OED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary) and [LSJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LSJ), the term *[canonical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical)* stems from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) word *kanonikós* (*κανονικός*, "regular, according to rule") from *kanṓn* (*κᾰνών*, "rod, rule"). The sense of norm, standard, or [archetype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archetypal) has been used in many disciplines. Mathematical usage is attested in a 1738 letter from [Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Logan_%28statesman%29).[^o7] The German term *kanonische Form* is attested in a 1846 paper by [Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482),[^o8] later the same year [Richelot](wikipedia://en/20346321) uses the term *Normalform* in a paper,[^o9] and in 1851 [Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861) writes:[^o10]

"I now proceed to \[...\] the mode of reducing Algebraical Functions to their simplest and most symmetrical, or as my admirable friend [M. Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) well proposes to call them, their *Canonical forms*."

In the same period, usage is attested by [Hesse](wikipedia://en/740995) ("Normalform"),[^o11] [Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) ("forme canonique"),[^o12] [Borchardt](wikipedia://en/482522) ("forme canonique"),[^o13] and [Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) ("canonical form").[^o14]

In 1865, the [Dictionary of Science, Literature and Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_Science,_Literature_and_Art) defines canonical form as:

"In Mathematics, denotes a form, usually the simplest or most symmetrical, to which, without loss of generality, all functions of the same class can be reduced."

### Geometry {#Geometry}

In [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202):

- The equation of a line: *Ax* + *By* = *C*, with *A<sup>2</sup>* + *B*<sup>2</sup> = 1 and *C* ≥ 0
- The equation of a circle: $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$

By contrast, there are alternative forms for writing equations. For example, the equation of a line may be written as a [linear equation](wikipedia://en/17570) in [point-slope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-slope) and [slope-intercept form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope-intercept_form).

[Convex polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedra) can be put into [canonical form](wikipedia://en/4709281#Canonical_polyhedron) such that:

- All faces are flat,
- All edges are tangent to the unit sphere, and
- The centroid of the polyhedron is at the origin.[^o31]

### Integrable systems {#Integrable_systems}

Every differentiable [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) has a [cotangent bundle](wikipedia://en/221600). That bundle can always be endowed with a certain [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519), called the [canonical one-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_one-form). This form gives the cotangent bundle the structure of a [symplectic manifold](wikipedia://en/28356), and allows vector fields on the manifold to be integrated by means of the [Euler-Lagrange equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler-Lagrange_equation), or by means of [Hamiltonian mechanics](wikipedia://en/198319). Such systems of integrable [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) are called [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213).

### Dynamical systems {#Dynamical_systems}

The study of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems) overlaps with that of [integrable systems](wikipedia://en/2573213); there one has the idea of a [normal form (dynamical systems)](wikipedia://en/43672852).

### Three dimensional geometry {#Three_dimensional_geometry}

In the study of manifolds in three dimensions, one has the [first fundamental form](wikipedia://en/510355), the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078) and the [third fundamental form](wikipedia://en/48361423).

### Rewriting systems {#Rewriting_systems}

The symbolic manipulation of a formula from one form to another is called a "rewriting" of that formula. One can study the abstract properties of rewriting generic formulas, by studying the collection of rules by which formulas can be validly manipulated. These are the "rewriting rules"—an integral part of an [abstract rewriting system](wikipedia://en/23947591). A common question is whether it is possible to bring some generic expression to a single, common form, the normal form. If different sequences of rewrites still result in the same form, then that form can be termed a normal form, with the rewrite being called a confluent. It is not always possible to obtain a normal form.

### Lambda calculus {#Lambda_calculus}

- A lambda term is in [beta normal form](wikipedia://en/1800460) if no beta reduction is possible; [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203) is a particular case of an abstract rewriting system. In the untyped lambda calculus, for example, the term $(\lambda x.(x x) \; \lambda x.(x x))$ does not have a normal form. In the typed lambda calculus, every well-formed term can be rewritten to its normal form.

### Graph theory {#Graph_theory}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a branch of mathematics, graph canonization is the problem of finding a canonical form of a given graph *G*. A canonical form is a [labeled graph](wikipedia://en/1609861) Canon(*G*) that is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to *G*, such that every graph that is isomorphic to *G* has the same canonical form as *G*. Thus, from a solution to the graph canonization problem, one could also solve the problem of [graph isomorphism](wikipedia://en/247577): to test whether two graphs *G* and *H* are isomorphic, compute their canonical forms Canon(*G*) and Canon(*H*), and test whether these two canonical forms are identical.

### Computing {#Computing}

In [computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing), the reduction of data to any kind of canonical form is commonly called *data normalization*.

For instance, [database normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_normalization) is the process of organizing the [fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28computer_science%29) and [tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_%28database%29) of a [relational database](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relational_database) to minimize [redundancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_redundancy) and dependency.[^o49]

In the field of [software security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_security), a common [vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_%28computing%29) is unchecked malicious input (see *[Code injection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_injection)*). The mitigation for this problem is proper [input validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input_validation). Before input validation is performed, the input is usually normalized by eliminating encoding (e.g., [HTML encoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_encodings_in_HTML)) and reducing the input data to a single common [character set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character_set).

Other forms of data, typically associated with [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324) (including [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal_processing) and [imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_processing)) or [machine learning](wikipedia://en/233488), can be normalized in order to provide a limited range of values.

In [content management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_management), the concept of a [single source of truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_source_of_truth) (SSOT) is applicable, just as it is in [database normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_normalization) generally and in [software development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development). Competent [content management systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_management_system) provide logical ways of obtaining it, such as [transclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transclusion).
