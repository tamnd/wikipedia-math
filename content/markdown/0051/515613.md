# Gorenstein ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gorenstein ring"}

In [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990), a **Gorenstein local ring** is a commutative [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) *R* with finite [injective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_dimension) as an [*R*-module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-module). There are many equivalent conditions, some of them listed below, often saying that a Gorenstein ring is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual) in some sense.

:::

Gorenstein rings were introduced by [Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) in his 1961 seminar (published in (Hartshorne 1967)). The name comes from a duality property of singular [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691) studied by (txt) (who was fond of claiming that he did not understand the definition of a Gorenstein ring [^o4]). The zero-dimensional case had been studied by (Macaulay 1934), (Serre 1961) and (Bass 1963) publicized the concept of Gorenstein rings.

[Frobenius rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_ring) are noncommutative analogs of zero-dimensional Gorenstein rings. [Gorenstein schemes](wikipedia://en/48497362) are the geometric version of Gorenstein rings.

## Definitions {#Definitions}

A **Gorenstein ring** is a commutative Noetherian ring such that each [localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_a_ring) at a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) is a Gorenstein local ring, as defined below. A Gorenstein ring is in particular [Cohen–Macaulay](wikipedia://en/497407).

One elementary characterization is: a Noetherian local ring *R* of [dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) zero (equivalently, with *R* of [finite length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_length) as an *R*-module) is Gorenstein if and only if Hom<sub>*R*</sub>(*k*, *R*) has dimension 1 as a *k*-[vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), where *k* is the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) of *R*. Equivalently, *R* has simple [socle](wikipedia://en/3571350#Socle_of_a_module) as an *R*-module.[^o10] More generally, a Noetherian local ring *R* is Gorenstein if and only if there is a [regular sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_sequence_%28algebra%29) *a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub> in the maximal ideal of *R* such that the quotient ring *R*/( *a*<sub>1</sub>,...,*a*<sub>*n*</sub>) is Gorenstein of dimension zero.

For example, if *R* is a commutative [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) over a field *k* such that *R* has finite dimension as a *k*-vector space, *R* = *k* ⊕ *R*<sub>1</sub> ⊕ ... ⊕ *R*<sub>*m*</sub>, then *R* is Gorenstein if and only if it satisfies [Poincaré duality](wikipedia://en/540732), meaning that the top graded piece *R*<sub>*m*</sub> has dimension 1 and the product *R*<sub>*a*</sub> × *R*<sub>*m*−*a*</sub> → *R*<sub>*m*</sub> is a [perfect pairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_pairing) for every *a*.[^o11]

Another interpretation of the Gorenstein property as a type of duality, for not necessarily [graded rings](wikipedia://en/103094), is: for a field *F*, a commutative *F*-algebra *R* of finite dimension as an *F*-vector space (hence of dimension zero as a ring) is Gorenstein if and only if there is an *F*-linear map *e*: *R* → *F* such that the [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) (*x*, *y*) := *e*(*xy*) on *R* (as an *F*-vector space) is [nondegenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondegenerate_bilinear_form).[^o12]

For a commutative Noetherian local ring (*R*, *m*, *k*) of [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) *n*, the following are equivalent:[^o13]

- *R* has finite [injective dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective_dimension) as an *R*-module;
- *R* has injective dimension *n* as an *R*-module;
- The [Ext group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ext_group) $\operatorname{Ext}^i_R(k,R) = 0$ for *i* ≠ *n* while $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(k,R) \cong k;$
- $\operatorname{Ext}^i_R(k,R) = 0$ for some *i* > *n*;
- $\operatorname{Ext}^i_R(k,R) = 0$ for all *i* \< *n* and $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(k,R) \cong k;$
- *R* is an *n*-dimensional Gorenstein ring.

A (not necessarily commutative) ring *R* is called Gorenstein if *R* has finite injective dimension both as a left *R*-module and as a right *R*-module. If *R* is a local ring, *R* is said to be a local Gorenstein ring.

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every local [complete intersection ring](wikipedia://en/3116851), in particular every [regular local ring](wikipedia://en/506959), is Gorenstein.
- The ring *R* = *k*\[*x*,*y*,*z*\]/(*x*<sup>2</sup>, *y*<sup>2</sup>, *xz*, *yz*, *z*<sup>2</sup>−*xy*) is a 0-dimensional Gorenstein ring that is not a complete intersection ring. In more detail: a [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) for *R* as a *k*-vector space is given by: $\{1,x,y,z,z^2\}.$ *R* is Gorenstein because the socle has dimension 1 as a *k*-vector space, [spanned](wikipedia://en/56353) by *z*<sup>2</sup>. Alternatively, one can observe that *R* satisfies Poincaré duality when it is viewed as a graded ring with *x*, *y*, *z* all of the same degree. Finally. *R* is not a complete intersection because it has 3 [generators](wikipedia://en/3171371) and a minimal set of 5 (not 3) relations.
- The ring *R* = *k*\[*x*,*y*\]/(*x*<sup>2</sup>, *y*<sup>2</sup>, *xy*) is a 0-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay ring that is not a Gorenstein ring. In more detail: a basis for *R* as a *k*-vector space is given by: $\{1,x,y\}.$ *R* is not Gorenstein because the socle has dimension 2 (not 1) as a *k*-vector space, spanned by *x* and *y*.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- A Noetherian local ring is Gorenstein if and only if its [completion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completion_%28algebra%29) is Gorenstein.[^o18]
- The [canonical module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_module) of a Gorenstein local ring *R* is isomorphic to *R*. In geometric terms, it follows that the standard [dualizing complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dualizing_complex) of a Gorenstein scheme *X* over a field is simply a [line bundle](wikipedia://en/581859) (viewed as a complex in degree −dim(*X*)); this line bundle is called the [canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893) of *X*. Using the canonical bundle, [Serre duality](wikipedia://en/359175) takes the same form for Gorenstein schemes as in the [smooth](wikipedia://en/35033819) case.

In the context of graded rings *R*, the canonical module of a Gorenstein ring *R* is isomorphic to *R* with some degree shift.[^o19]

- For a Gorenstein local ring (*R*, *m*, *k*) of dimension *n*, [Grothendieck local duality](wikipedia://en/38470542) takes the following form.[^o20] Let *E*(*k*) be the [injective hull](wikipedia://en/665821) of the residue field *k* as an *R*-module. Then, for any finitely generated *R*-module *M* and integer *i*, the [local cohomology](wikipedia://en/3212091) group $H^i_m(M)$ is dual to $\operatorname{Ext}_R^{n-i}(M,R)$ in the sense that:

$$H_m^i(M) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_R(\operatorname{Ext}_R^{n-i}(M,R), E(k)).$$

- [Stanley](wikipedia://en/1633397) showed that for a finitely generated commutative graded algebra *R* over a field *k* such that *R* is an [integral domain](wikipedia://en/15462), the Gorenstein property depends only on the Cohen–Macaulay property together with the [Hilbert series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_series)

$$f(t) = \sum\nolimits_j \dim_k(R_j)t^j.$$

Namely, a graded domain *R* is Gorenstein if and only if it is Cohen–Macaulay and the Hilbert series is symmetric in the sense that

$$f\left(\tfrac{1}{t} \right)=(-1)^n t^s f(t)$$

for some integer *s*, where *n* is the dimension of *R*.[^o21]

- Let (*R*, *m*, *k*) be a Noetherian local ring of embedding codimension *c*, meaning that *c* = dim<sub>*k*</sub>(*m*/*m*<sup>2</sup>) − dim(*R*). In geometric terms, this holds for a local ring of a subscheme of codimension *c* in a regular scheme. For *c* at most 2, [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) showed that *R* is Gorenstein if and only if it is a [complete intersection](wikipedia://en/3116851).[^o22] There is also a structure theorem for Gorenstein rings of codimension 3 in terms of the [Pfaffians](wikipedia://en/530060) of a skew-symmetric matrix, by [Buchsbaum](wikipedia://en/30781131) and [Eisenbud](wikipedia://en/3649845).[^o23] In 2011, [Miles Reid](wikipedia://en/5863755) extended this structure theorem to case of codimension 4.[^o24]
