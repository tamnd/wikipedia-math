# Argumentum ad captandum

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Argumentum ad captandum"}

In [rhetoric](wikipedia://en/25447), an ***argumentum ad captandum***, "for capturing" the [gullibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullibility) of the [naïve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naïve) among the listeners or readers, is an unsound, specious argument designed to appeal to the emotions rather than to the mind. It is used to describe "claptrap or meretricious attempts to catch popular favor or applause."[^o2]

:::

The longer form of the term is ***ad captandum vulgus*** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), "to ensnare the vulgar" or "to captivate the masses");[^o3] the shorter and longer versions of the phrase are synonymous. The word "vulgus" in Latin meant the common people, the multitude; it was also sometimes used contemptuously to imply a rabble or a mob.[^o4]

The *ad captandum* approach is commonly seen in political speech, advertising, and popular entertainment.[^o4] The classic example of something *ad captandum vulgus* was the "[bread and circuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_and_circuses)" by which the Roman emperors maintained the support of the people of Rome.[^o4]
