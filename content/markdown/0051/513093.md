# Monoclinic crystal system

*Image: Barytocalcite 1.png.* Monoclinic crystal

*Image: Ortoclasio.jpg.* An example of the monoclinic crystal [orthoclase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoclase)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Monoclinic crystal system"}

In [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), the **monoclinic crystal system** is one of the seven [crystal systems](wikipedia://en/456410). A crystal system is described by three [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29). In the monoclinic system, the [crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal) is described by vectors of unequal lengths, as in the [orthorhombic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthorhombic) system. They form a [parallelogram](wikipedia://en/133496) [prism](wikipedia://en/283007). Hence two pairs of vectors are [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) (meet at right angles), while the third pair makes an angle other than 90°.

:::

## Bravais lattices {#Bravais_lattices}

Bravais lattice

Two monoclinic Bravais lattices exist: the primitive monoclinic and the base-centered monoclinic.

::: {#o3 .table cue=table}

| Bravais lattice | Primitive monoclinic | Base-centered monoclinic |
|---|---|---|
| [Pearson symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_symbol) | mP | mS |
| [Unit cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_cell) | *Image: Monoclinic.svg.* | *Image: Base-centered monoclinic.svg.* |

:::

The body-centered and face-centered monoclinic lattices are equivalent to the base-centered monoclinic lattice with different choice of axes, while the B side-centered monoclinic lattice (with centerings on the parallelogram faces) is equivalent to the primitive monoclinic lattice with different choice of axes.[^o6]

The [primitive cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_cell) of the base-centered monoclinic lattice has the shape of an oblique rhombic prism;[^o7] it can be constructed because the two-dimensional centered rectangular base layer can also be described with primitive rhombic axes. The length $a$ of the primitive cell below equals $\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{(a^{2} + b^{2})}$ of the conventional cell above.

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="2 (crystal class)"}

Sphenoidal is also called monoclinic hemimorphic, domatic is also called monoclinic hemihedral, and prismatic is also called monoclinic normal.

The three monoclinic hemimorphic space groups are as follows:

- a prism with a [wallpaper group p2](wikipedia://en/386214#Group_p2) cross-section
- ditto with screw axes instead of axes
- ditto with screw axes as well as axes, parallel, in between; in this case an additional translation vector is one half of a translation vector in the base plane plus one half of a perpendicular vector between the base planes.

The four monoclinic hemihedral space groups include

- those with pure reflection at the base of the prism and halfway
- those with glide planes instead of pure reflection planes; the glide is one half of a translation vector in the base plane
- those with both in between each other; in this case an additional translation vector is this glide plus one half of a perpendicular vector between the base planes.

:::
