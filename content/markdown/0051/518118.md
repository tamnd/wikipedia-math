# Semi-locally simply connected

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Semi-locally simply connected"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), the **semi-locally simply connected** (or **semilocally simply connected**[^n1][^n2]) property is a certain [local connectedness](wikipedia://en/6959754) condition that arises in the theory of [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323). Roughly speaking, a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X* satisfies the property if for each point *x* in *X* any sufficiently small loop going through *x* can be [contracted](wikipedia://en/212250) within *X* to a point. This condition is necessary for most of the theory of covering spaces, including the existence of a [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) and the [Galois correspondence](wikipedia://en/156411) between covering spaces and [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004).

:::

Most “nice” spaces such as [manifolds](wikipedia://en/2073470) and [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) are semi-locally simply connected, and topological spaces that do not satisfy this condition are considered somewhat [pathological](wikipedia://en/372742). The standard example of a non-semi-locally simply connected space is the [Hawaiian earring](wikipedia://en/910263).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A space *X* is called **semi-locally simply connected** if every [point](wikipedia://en/593693) *x* in *X* has a [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_%28mathematics%29) *U* with the property that every [loop](wikipedia://en/1189560) in *U* based at *x* can be [contracted](wikipedia://en/212250) within *X* to the constant loop at *x* (i.e., every loop in *U* starting and ending at *x* is [nullhomotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullhomotopic) in *X* via a basepoint-preserving homotopy).

Note that if *U* satisfies this condition, so does any smaller neighborhood of *x*, so that *x* has arbitrarily small neighborhoods satisfying the condition. The neighborhood *U* need not be [simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_connected): though every loop in *U* based at *x* must be contractible within *X*, the contraction is not required to take place inside of *U*. For this reason, a space can be semi-locally simply connected without being [locally simply connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_simply_connected). Also, it is not required that every loop in *U* is nullhomotopic in *X*; it is only the loops in *U* based at *x* that must be nullhomotopic in *X*. In general, a semi-locally simply connected space may have points *x* with arbitrarily small neighborhoods containing loops (not going through *x*) that cannot be contracted to a point, even with homotopies in *X*.

An equivalent formulation of the definition is that every point in $x\in X$ has an open neighborhood $U$ for which the [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) $\pi_1(U,x)\to\pi_1(X,x)$ [induced](wikipedia://en/11004#Functoriality) by the [inclusion map](wikipedia://en/492516) of $U$ into $X$ is trivial.[^n1] Here, $\pi_1(U,x)$ is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of $U$ relative to the basepoint $x;$ and similarly for $\pi_1(X,x).$

Most of the main theorems about [covering spaces](wikipedia://en/250323), including the existence of a universal cover and the Galois correspondence, require a space to be [path-connected](wikipedia://en/6233), [locally path-connected](wikipedia://en/6959754), and semi-locally simply connected, a condition known as **unloopable** (*délaçable* in French).[^n3] In particular, this condition is necessary for a locally path-connected space to have a simply connected covering space.

## Examples {#Examples}

*Image: Hawaiian earrings.svg.* The [Hawaiian earring](wikipedia://en/910263) is not semi-locally simply connected.

A simple example of a space that is not semi-locally simply connected is the [Hawaiian earring](wikipedia://en/910263): the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of the [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) in the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) with centers (1/*n*, 0) and [radii](wikipedia://en/1780815) 1/*n*, for *n* a [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474). Give this space the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926). Then all [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood) of the [origin](wikipedia://en/1313432) contain [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) that are not [nullhomotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullhomotopic).

The Hawaiian earring can also be used to construct a semi-locally simply connected space that is not [locally simply connected](wikipedia://en/7541497). In particular, the [cone](wikipedia://en/782162) on the Hawaiian earring is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible) and therefore semi-locally simply connected, but it is clearly not locally simply connected.

:::

## Topology of fundamental group {#Topology_of_fundamental_group}

In terms of the natural topology on the fundamental group, a locally path-connected space is semi-locally simply connected if and only if its quasitopological fundamental group is discrete.
