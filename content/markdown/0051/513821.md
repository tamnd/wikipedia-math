# 5-cell

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="5-cell"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **5-cell** is the convex [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979) with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {3,3,3}. It is a 5-vertex [four-dimensional](wikipedia://en/1364622) object bounded by five tetrahedral cells. It is also known as a **C<sub>5</sub>**, **hypertetrahedron**, **pentachoron**,[^n1] **pentatope**, **pentahedroid**,[^n2] **tetrahedral pyramid**, or **4-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781)** (Coxeter's *α*<sub>4</sub> polytope),[^n3] the simplest possible convex 4-polytope, and is analogous to the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) in three dimensions and the [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) in two dimensions. The 5-cell is a [4-dimensional pyramid](wikipedia://en/11247993) with a tetrahedral base and four tetrahedral sides.

:::

The **regular 5-cell** is bounded by five [regular tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/5242521), and is one of the six [regular convex 4-polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_convex_4-polytope) (the four-dimensional analogues of the [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solids)). A regular 5-cell can be constructed from a regular tetrahedron by adding a fifth vertex one edge length distant from all the vertices of the tetrahedron. This cannot be done in 3-dimensional space. The regular 5-cell is a solution to the problem: *Make 10 equilateral triangles, all of the same size, using 10 matchsticks, where each side of every triangle is exactly one matchstick, and none of the triangles and matchsticks intersect one another.* No solution exists in three dimensions.

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The 5-cell is the 4-dimensional [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781), the simplest possible [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979). In other words, the 5-cell is a [polychoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychoron) analogous to a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) in high dimension.[^n4] It is formed by any five points which are not all in the same [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) (as a tetrahedron is formed by any four points which are not all in the same plane, and a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is formed by any three points which are not all in the same line). Any such five points constitute a 5-cell, though not usually a regular 5-cell. The *regular* 5-cell is not found within any of the other regular convex 4-polytopes except one: the 600-vertex [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) is a [compound](wikipedia://en/23901) of 120 regular 5-cells.

The 5-cell is [self-dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dual_polytope), meaning its dual polytope is 5-cell itself.[^n5] Its maximal intersection with 3-dimensional space is the [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584). Its dichoral angle between cells is [arccos](wikipedia://en/374220) $\tfrac{1}{4}$ ≈ 75.52°. Its [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) between faces is arccos $\tfrac{1}{3}$ ≈ 70.53°.[^n6]

It is the first in the sequence of 6 convex regular 4-polytopes, in order of volume at a given radius or number of vertexes.[^n7]

The convex hull of two 5-cells in dual configuration is the [disphenoidal 30-cell](wikipedia://en/4046891#Disphenoidal_30-cell), dual of the [bitruncated 5-cell](wikipedia://en/4046891#Bitruncated_5-cell).

### As a configuration {#As_a_configuration}

This [configuration matrix](wikipedia://en/18801624#As_configurations) represents the 5-cell. The rows and columns correspond to vertices, edges, faces, and cells. The diagonal numbers say how many of each element occur in the whole 5-cell. The nondiagonal numbers say how many of the column's element occur in or at the row's element. This self-dual polytope's matrix is identical to its 180 degree rotation.[^n8] The *k*-faces can be read as rows left of the diagonal, while the *k*-figures are read as rows after the diagonal.[^o14]

### Geodesics and rotations {#Geodesics_and_rotations}

*Image: 5-cell-orig.gif.* A 3D projection of a 5-cell performing a [double rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SO%284%29#Double_rotations).

The 5-cell has only [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) central planes through vertices. It has 10 digon central planes, where each vertex pair is an edge, not an axis, of the 5-cell. Each digon plane is orthogonal to 3 others, but completely orthogonal to none of them. The characteristic [isoclinic rotation](wikipedia://en/2307854#Double_rotations) of the 5-cell has, as pairs of invariant planes, those 10 digon planes and their completely orthogonal central planes, which are 0-gon planes which intersect no vertices of the 5-cell.

## Irregular 5-cells {#Irregular_5-cells}

In the case of [simplexes](wikipedia://en/39781) such as the 5-cell, certain irregular forms are in some sense more fundamental than the regular form. Although regular 5-cells cannot fill 4-space or the regular 4-polytopes, there are irregular 5-cells which do. These **characteristic 5-cells** are the [fundamental domains](wikipedia://en/469365) of the different [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/297004) which give rise to the various 4-polytopes.

::: {#o22 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="4-orthoscheme"}

### Orthoschemes {#Orthoschemes}

A **4-orthoscheme** is a 5-cell where all 10 faces are [right triangles](wikipedia://en/30654#By_internal_angles). (The 5 vertices form 5 tetrahedral [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29) face-bonded to each other, with a total of 10 edges and 10 triangular faces.) An [orthoscheme](wikipedia://en/20740340) is an irregular [simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) that is the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) in which all edges are mutually perpendicular. In a 4-dimensional orthoscheme, the tree consists of four perpendicular edges connecting all five vertices in a linear path that makes three right-angled turns. The elements of an orthoscheme are also orthoschemes (just as the elements of a regular simplex are also regular simplexes). Each tetrahedral cell of a 4-orthoscheme is a [3-orthoscheme](wikipedia://en/30606#Orthoschemes), and each triangular face is a 2-orthoscheme (a right triangle).

Orthoschemes are the [characteristic simplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoscheme#Characteristic_simplex_of_the_general_regular_polytope) of the regular polytopes, because each regular polytope is [generated by reflections](wikipedia://en/297004) in the bounding facets of its particular characteristic orthoscheme.[^n9] For example, the special case of the 4-orthoscheme with equal-length perpendicular edges is the characteristic orthoscheme of the [4-cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-cube) (also called the *tesseract* or *8-cell*), the 4-dimensional analogue of the 3-dimensional cube. If the three perpendicular edges of the 4-orthoscheme are of unit length, then all its edges are of length $\sqrt{1}$, $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$, or $\sqrt{4}$, precisely the [chord lengths of the unit 4-cube](wikipedia://en/31112#Radial_equilateral_symmetry) (the lengths of the 4-cube's edges and its various diagonals). Therefore this 4-orthoscheme fits within the 4-cube, and the 4-cube (like every regular convex polytope) can be [dissected into instances of its characteristic orthoscheme](wikipedia://en/56244281).

*Image: Triangulated cube.svg.* A 3-cube dissected into six [3-orthoschemes](wikipedia://en/30606#Orthoschemes). Three are left-handed and three are right handed. A left and a right meet at each square face.

A 3-orthoscheme is easily illustrated, but a 4-orthoscheme is more difficult to visualize. A 4-orthoscheme is a [tetrahedral pyramid](wikipedia://en/11247993) with a 3-orthoscheme as its base. It has four more edges than the 3-orthoscheme, joining the four vertices of the base to its apex (the fifth vertex of the 5-cell). Pick out any one of the 3-orthoschemes of the six shown in the 3-cube illustration. Notice that it touches four of the cube's eight vertices, and those four vertices are linked by a 3-edge path that makes two right-angled turns. Imagine that this 3-orthoscheme is the base of a 4-orthoscheme, so that from each of those four vertices, an unseen 4-orthoscheme edge connects to a fifth apex vertex (which is outside the 3-cube and does not appear in the illustration at all). Although the four additional edges all reach the same apex vertex, they will all be of different lengths. The first of them, at one end of the 3-edge orthogonal path, extends that path with a fourth orthogonal $\sqrt{1}$ edge by making a third 90 degree turn and reaching perpendicularly into the fourth dimension to the apex. The second of the four additional edges is a $\sqrt{2}$ diagonal of a cube face (not of the illustrated 3-cube, but of another of the tesseract's eight 3-cubes). The third additional edge is a $\sqrt{3}$ diagonal of a 3-cube (again, not the original illustrated 3-cube). The fourth additional edge (at the other end of the orthogonal path) is a [long diameter of the tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112#Radial_equilateral_symmetry) itself, of length $\sqrt{4}$. It reaches through the exact center of the tesseract to the [antipodal](wikipedia://en/367577) vertex (a vertex of the opposing 3-cube), which is the apex. Thus the **characteristic 5-cell of the 4-cube** has four $\sqrt{1}$ edges, three $\sqrt{2}$ edges, two $\sqrt{3}$ edges, and one $\sqrt{4}$ edge.

The 4-cube can be [dissected into 24 such 4-orthoschemes](wikipedia://en/20740340#Properties) eight different ways, with six 4-orthoschemes surrounding each of four orthogonal $\sqrt{4}$ tesseract long diameters. The 4-cube can also be dissected into 384 *smaller* instances of this same characteristic 4-orthoscheme, just one way, by all of its symmetry hyperplanes at once, which divide it into 384 4-orthoschemes that all meet at the center of the 4-cube.

More generally, any regular polytope can be dissected into *g* instances of its characteristic orthoscheme that all meet at the regular polytope's center.[^n10] The number *g* is the *order* of the polytope, the number of reflected instances of its characteristic orthoscheme that comprise the polytope when a *single* mirror-surfaced orthoscheme instance is reflected in its own facets. More generally still, characteristic simplexes are able to fill uniform polytopes because they possess all the requisite elements of the polytope. They also possess all the requisite angles between elements (from 90 degrees on down). The characteristic simplexes are the [genetic codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_code) of polytopes: like a [Swiss Army knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Army_knife), they contain one of everything needed to construct the polytope by replication.

Every regular polytope, including the regular 5-cell, has its characteristic orthoscheme. There is a 4-orthoscheme which is the **characteristic 5-cell of the regular 5-cell**. It is a [tetrahedral pyramid](wikipedia://en/11247993) based on the [characteristic tetrahedron of the regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606#Orthoschemes). The regular 5-cell can be dissected into 120 instances of this characteristic 4-orthoscheme just one way, by all of its symmetry hyperplanes at once, which divide it into 120 4-orthoschemes that all meet at the center of the regular 5-cell.

The characteristic 5-cell (4-orthoscheme) of the regular 5-cell has four more edges than its base characteristic tetrahedron (3-orthoscheme), which join the four vertices of the base to its apex (the fifth vertex of the 4-orthoscheme, at the center of the regular 5-cell). If the regular 5-cell has unit radius and edge length $\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{2}}$, its characteristic 5-cell's ten edges have lengths $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{10}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{30}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{15}}$ around its exterior right-triangle face (the edges opposite the *characteristic angles* 𝟀, 𝝉, 𝟁), plus $\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{20}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{20}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{60}}$ (the other three edges of the exterior 3-orthoscheme facet the characteristic tetrahedron, which are the *characteristic radii* of the regular tetrahedron), plus $\sqrt{1}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{8}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{6}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{16}}$ (edges which are the characteristic radii of the regular 5-cell). The 4-edge path along orthogonal edges of the orthoscheme is $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{30}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{15}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{60}}$, $\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{16}}$, first from a regular 5-cell vertex to a regular 5-cell edge center, then turning 90° to a regular 5-cell face center, then turning 90° to a regular 5-cell tetrahedral cell center, then turning 90° to the regular 5-cell center.

## Construction {#Construction}

### As a Boerdijk–Coxeter helix {#As_a_Boerdijk–Coxeter_helix}

*Image: 5-cell 5-ring net.png.* The 5-cell Boerdijk–Coxeter helix

A 5-cell can be constructed as a [Boerdijk–Coxeter helix](wikipedia://en/19172682) of five chained tetrahedra, folded into a 4-dimensional ring.[^n13] The 10 triangle faces can be seen in a 2D net within a [triangular tiling](wikipedia://en/2863858), with 6 triangles around every vertex, although folding into 4-dimensions causes edges to coincide. The purple edges form a [regular pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014#Regular_pentagons) which is the [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) of the 5-cell. The blue edges connect every second vertex, forming a [pentagram](wikipedia://en/197544) which is the *Clifford polygon* of the 5-cell. The pentagram's blue edges are chords of the 5-cell's *isocline*, the circular rotational path its vertices take during an [isoclinic rotation](wikipedia://en/2307854#Double_rotations), also known as a [Clifford displacement](wikipedia://en/2307854#Isoclinic_rotations).

### Net {#Net}

*Image: 5-cell net.png.* [Net](wikipedia://en/1148129) of five tetrahedra (one hidden)

When a net of five tetrahedra is folded up in 4-dimensional space such that each tetrahedron is face bonded to the other four, the resulting 5-cell has a total of 5 vertices, 10 edges, and 10 faces. Four edges meet at each vertex, and three tetrahedral cells meet at each edge. This makes the six tetrahedron as its [cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28geometry%29).[^n6]

### Coordinates {#Coordinates}

The simplest set of [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates) is $(2,0,0,0), (0,2,0,0), (0,0,2,0), (0,0,0,2), (\phi, \phi, \phi, \phi),$ with edge length $2 \sqrt{2}$, where $\phi$ is the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^n14] While these coordinates are not origin-centered, subtracting $(1,1,1,1)/\left(2-\tfrac{1}{\phi}\right)$ from each translates the 4-polytope's [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) to the origin with radius $2\left(\phi-1/\left(2-\tfrac{1}{\phi}\right)\right) =\sqrt{\tfrac{16}{5}}\approx 1.7888$, with the following coordinates:

$$\begin{aligned} \left(\tfrac{2}{\phi}-3, 1, 1, 1\right)/\left(\tfrac{1}{\phi}-2\right), &\quad \left(1,\tfrac{2}{\phi}-3,1,1 \right)/\left(\tfrac{1}{\phi}-2\right) \\ \left(1,1,\tfrac{2}{\phi}-3,1 \right)/\left(\tfrac{1}{\phi}-2\right) &\quad \left(1,1,1,\tfrac{2}{\phi}-3 \right)/\left(\tfrac{1}{\phi}-2\right) \\ \left(\tfrac{2}{\phi},\tfrac{2}{\phi},\tfrac{2}{\phi},\tfrac{2}{\phi} \right)/\left(\tfrac{1}{\phi}-2\right) \end{aligned}$$

The following set of origin-centered coordinates with the same radius and edge length as above can be seen as a hyperpyramid with a [regular tetrahedral base](wikipedia://en/30606#Coordinates_for_a_regular_tetrahedron) in 3-space:

$$\left( 1, 1, 1, \frac{-1}\sqrt{5}\right)$$

$$\left( 1,-1,-1,\frac{-1}\sqrt{5} \right)$$

$$\left(-1, 1,-1,\frac{-1}\sqrt{5} \right)$$

$$\left(-1,-1, 1,\frac{-1}\sqrt{5} \right)$$

$$\left( 0, 0, 0,\frac{4}\sqrt{5} \right)$$

Scaling these or the previous set of coordinates by $\tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}$ give ***unit-radius*** origin-centered regular 5-cells with edge lengths $\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{2}}$. The hyperpyramid has coordinates:

$$\left( \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5}, -1 \right)/4$$

$$\left( \sqrt{5},-\sqrt{5},-\sqrt{5}, -1 \right)/4$$

$$\left(-\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5},-\sqrt{5}, -1 \right)/4$$

$$\left(-\sqrt{5},-\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5}, -1 \right)/4$$

$$\left( 0, 0, 0, 1 \right)$$

Coordinates for the vertices of another origin-centered regular 5-cell with edge length 2 and radius $\sqrt{\tfrac{8}{5}}\approx 1.265$ are:

$$\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}},\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\ \pm1\right)$$

$$\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}},\ \frac{-2}{\sqrt{3}},\ 0 \right)$$

$$\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}},\ -\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}},\ 0,\ 0 \right)$$

$$\left( -2\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0 \right)$$

Scaling these by $\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{8}}$ to unit-radius and edge length $\sqrt{\tfrac{5}{2}}$ gives:

$$\left(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, \sqrt{10},\pm\sqrt{30} \right)/(4\sqrt{3})$$

$$\left(\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{5}, -\sqrt{40},0\right)/(4\sqrt{3})$$

$$\left(\sqrt{3},-\sqrt{45},0,0\right)/(4\sqrt{3})$$

$$\left(-1, 0, 0, 0 \right)$$

The vertices of a 4-simplex (with edge $\sqrt{2}$ and radius 1) can be more simply constructed on a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862) in 5-space, as (distinct) permutations of (0,0,0,0,1) *or* (0,1,1,1,1); in these positions it is a [facet](wikipedia://en/7186647) of, respectively, the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615) or the [rectified penteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectified_penteract).

## Related polytopes and honeycombs {#Related_polytopes_and_honeycombs}

The pentachoron (5-cell) is the simplest of 9 [uniform polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polychoron) constructed from the \[3,3,3\] [Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004).

It is in the {p,3,3} sequence of [regular polychora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polychora) with a [tetrahedral](wikipedia://en/30606) [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719): the [tesseract](wikipedia://en/31112) {4,3,3} and [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358) {5,3,3} of Euclidean 4-space, and the [hexagonal tiling honeycomb](wikipedia://en/40281224) {6,3,3} of hyperbolic space.

It is one of three {3,3,p} [regular 4-polytopes](wikipedia://en/18801624) with tetrahedral cells, along with the [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) {3,3,4} and [600-cell](wikipedia://en/501447) {3,3,5}. The [order-6 tetrahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/40281358) {3,3,6} of hyperbolic space also has tetrahedral cells.

It is self-dual like the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485) {3,4,3}, having a [palindromic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palindromic) {3,p,3} [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394).
