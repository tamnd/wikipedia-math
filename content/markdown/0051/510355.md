# First fundamental form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First fundamental form"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **first fundamental form** is the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) on the [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) of a [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_%28differential_geometry%29) in three-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) which is induced [canonically](wikipedia://en/515096) from the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) of $\mathbf{R}^{3}$. It permits the calculation of [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) and metric properties of a surface such as length and area in a manner consistent with the [ambient space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_space). The first fundamental form is denoted by the Roman numeral $I$,

$$\mathrm{I}(x,y)= \langle x,y \rangle.$$

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X(u, v)$ be a [parametric surface](wikipedia://en/3400953). Then the inner product of two [tangent vectors](wikipedia://en/191999) is

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{I}(aX_u+bX_v,cX_u+dX_v) \\[5pt] = {} & ac \langle X_u,X_u \rangle + (ad+bc) \langle X_u,X_v \rangle + bd \langle X_v,X_v \rangle \\[5pt] = {} & Eac + F(ad+bc) + Gbd, \end{aligned}$$

where $E$, $F$, and $G$ are the **coefficients of the first fundamental form**.

The first fundamental form may be represented as a [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474).

$$\mathrm{I}(x,y) = x^\mathsf{T} \begin{bmatrix} E & F \\ F & G \end{bmatrix}y$$

:::

## Further notation {#Further_notation}

When the first fundamental form is written with only one argument, it denotes the inner product of that vector with itself.

$$\mathrm{I}(v)= \langle v,v \rangle = |v|^2$$

The first fundamental form is often written in the modern notation of the [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795). The coefficients may then be written as $g_{ij}$:

$$\left(g_{ij}\right) = \begin{pmatrix} g_{11} & g_{12} \\ g_{21} & g_{22} \end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix} E & F \\ F & G \end{pmatrix}$$

The components of this tensor are calculated as the scalar product of tangent vectors $X_{1}$ and $X_{2}$:

$$g_{ij} = \langle X_i, X_j \rangle$$

for $i, j = 1, 2$. See example below.

## Calculating lengths and areas {#Calculating_lengths_and_areas}

The first fundamental form completely describes the metric properties of a surface. Thus, it enables one to calculate the lengths of curves on the surface and the areas of regions on the surface. The [line element](wikipedia://en/855138) $ds$ may be expressed in terms of the coefficients of the first fundamental form as

$$ds^2 = E\,du^2+2F\,du\,dv+G\,dv^2 \,.$$

The classical area element given by $dA = \lvert X_{u} \times X_{v}\rvert du dv$ can be expressed in terms of the first fundamental form with the assistance of [Lagrange's identity](wikipedia://en/2557590),

$$dA = |X_u \times X_v| \ du\, dv= \sqrt{ \langle X_u,X_u \rangle \langle X_v,X_v \rangle - \left\langle X_u,X_v \right\rangle^2 } \, du\, dv = \sqrt{EG-F^2} \, du\, dv.$$

### Example: curve on a sphere {#Example:_curve_on_a_sphere}

A [spherical curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_curve) on the [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090) in $\mathbf{R}^{3}$ may be parametrized as

$$X(u,v) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos u \sin v \\ \sin u \sin v \\ \cos v \end{bmatrix},\ (u,v) \in [0,2\pi) \times [0,\pi].$$

Differentiating $X(u,v)$ with respect to $u$ and $v$ yields

$$\begin{aligned} X_u &= \begin{bmatrix} -\sin u \sin v \\ \cos u \sin v \\ 0 \end{bmatrix},\\[5pt] X_v &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos u \cos v \\ \sin u \cos v \\ -\sin v \end{bmatrix}. \end{aligned}$$

The coefficients of the first fundamental form may be found by taking the dot product of the [partial derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_derivatives).

$$\begin{aligned} E &= X_u \cdot X_u = \sin^2 v \\ F &= X_u \cdot X_v = 0 \\ G &= X_v \cdot X_v = 1 \end{aligned}$$

so:

$$\begin{bmatrix}E & F \\F & G\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} \sin^2 v & 0 \\0 & 1\end{bmatrix}.$$

#### Length of a curve on the sphere {#Length_of_a_curve_on_the_sphere}

The [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) of the unit sphere is a parametrized curve given by

$$(u(t),v(t))=(t,\tfrac{\pi}{2})$$

with $t$ ranging from 0 to 2$\pi$. The line element may be used to calculate the length of this curve.

$$\int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{ E\left(\frac{du}{dt}\right)^2 + 2F \frac{du}{dt} \frac{dv}{dt} + G\left(\frac{dv}{dt}\right)^2 } \,dt = \int_0^{2\pi} \left|\sin v\right| \, dt = 2\pi \sin \tfrac{\pi}{2} = 2\pi$$

#### Area of a region on the sphere {#Area_of_a_region_on_the_sphere}

The area element may be used to calculate the area of the unit sphere.

$$\int_0^\pi \int_0^{2\pi} \sqrt{ EG-F^2 } \ du\, dv = \int_0^\pi \int_0^{2\pi} \sin v \, du\, dv = 2\pi \Big[ {-\cos v} \Big]_0^{\pi} = 4\pi$$

## Gaussian curvature {#Gaussian_curvature}

The [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) of a surface is given by

$$K = \frac{\det \mathrm{I\!I}_p}{\det \mathrm{I}_p} = \frac{ LN-M^2}{EG-F^2 },$$

where $L$, $M$, and $N$ are the coefficients of the [second fundamental form](wikipedia://en/652078).

[Theorema egregium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorema_egregium) of [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) states that the Gaussian curvature of a surface can be expressed solely in terms of the first fundamental form and its derivatives, so that $K$ is in fact an intrinsic invariant of the surface. An explicit expression for the Gaussian curvature in terms of the first fundamental form is provided by the [Brioschi formula](wikipedia://en/285623#Alternative_formulas).
