# Friedrich Kasiski

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrich Kasiski"}

Major **Friedrich Wilhelm Kasiski** (29 November 1805 – 22 May 1881) was a German [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) [officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_%28armed_forces%29), [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and [archeologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archeologist). Kasiski was born in Schlochau, [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) (now [Człuchów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Człuchów), Poland).

:::

## Military service {#Military_service}

Kasiski enlisted in [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia)'s 33rd Infantry [Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment) on 20 March 1823 at the age of 17. In May 1824, he was promoted to the [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_rank) of [master sergeant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_sergeant), and eight months later was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer) as a [second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) in February 1825. It took fourteen years to earn his next [promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_%28rank%29) when, in May 1839, he advanced to the rank of [first lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_lieutenant). His next advancement was quicker, promoted to [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_%28land%29) in November 1842. Kasiski finally retired from active service with the rank of major on 17 February 1852.

Between 1860 and 1868 he was the commander of a [National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_%28Prussia%29) [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion).

## Cryptography {#Cryptography}

In 1863, Kasiski published a 95-page book on [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), *Die Geheimschriften und die Dechiffrir-Kunst* (German, "Secret writing and the Art of Deciphering"). This was the first published account of a procedure for [attacking](wikipedia://en/5715) [polyalphabetic substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyalphabetic_substitution) ciphers, especially the [Vigenère cipher](wikipedia://en/69509) (it is possible [Charles Babbage](wikipedia://en/5698) was already aware of a similar method but had not published it). The method relied on the analysis of gaps between repeated fragments in the [ciphertext](wikipedia://en/175560); such analysis can give hints as to the length of the [key](wikipedia://en/53039) used. This technique is known as [Kasiski examination](wikipedia://en/801809).

The significance of Kasiski's cryptanalytic work was not widely realised at the time, and he turned his mind to [archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology) instead. The later years of his life were spent at Neustettin ([Szczecinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczecinek)); [the 11th edition of Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopædia_Britannica_Eleventh_Edition) cited a scholarly article by Kasiski in its entry on the town. Historian [David Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kahn_%28writer%29) notes, "Kasiski died on May 22, 1881, almost certainly without realizing that he had written a revolution in cryptology" (*[The Codebreakers](wikipedia://en/518398)*).
