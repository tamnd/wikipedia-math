# Zariski tangent space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zariski tangent space"}

In [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), the **Zariski tangent space** is a construction that defines a [tangent space](wikipedia://en/30807) at a point *P* on an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* (and more generally). It does not use [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416), being based directly on [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), and in the most concrete cases just the theory of a [system of linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

For example, suppose *C* is a [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691) defined by a polynomial equation

*F*(*X,Y*) *\= 0*

and take *P* to be the origin (0,0). Erasing terms of higher order than 1 would produce a 'linearised' equation reading

*L*(*X,Y*) *\= 0*

in which all terms *X<sup>a</sup>Y<sup>b</sup>* have been discarded if *a + b > 1*.

We have two cases: *L* may be 0, or it may be the equation of a line. In the first case the (Zariski) tangent space to *C* at (0,0) is the whole plane, considered as a two-dimensional [affine space](wikipedia://en/298834). In the second case, the tangent space is that line, considered as affine space. (The question of the origin comes up, when we take *P* as a general point on *C*; it is better to say 'affine space' and then note that *P* is a natural origin, rather than insist directly that it is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370).)

It is easy to see that over the [real field](wikipedia://en/20646438) we can obtain *L* in terms of the first [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of *F*. When those both are 0 at *P*, we have a [singular point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_singularity) ([double point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_point), [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758) or something more complicated). The general definition is that *singular points* of *C* are the cases when the tangent space has dimension 2.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **cotangent space** of a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) *R*, with [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) $\mathfrak{m}$ is defined to be

$$\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$$

where $\mathfrak{m}$<sup>2</sup> is given by the [product of ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_of_ideals). It is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) *k:= R/$\mathfrak{m}$*. Its [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_vector_space) (as a *k*-vector space) is called **tangent space** of *R*.[^n1]

This definition is a generalization of the above example to higher dimensions: suppose given an affine algebraic variety *V* and a point *v* of *V*. Morally, modding out *$\mathfrak{m}$<sup>2</sup>* corresponds to dropping the non-linear terms from the equations defining *V* inside some affine space, therefore giving a system of linear equations that define the tangent space.

The tangent space $T_P(X)$ and cotangent space $T_P^*(X)$ to a scheme *X* at a point *P* is the (co)tangent space of $\mathcal{O}_{X,P}$. Due to the [functoriality of Spec](wikipedia://en/37291#Functorial_perspective), the natural quotient map $f:R\rightarrow R/I$ induces a homomorphism $g:\mathcal{O}_{X,f^{-1}(P)}\rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{Y,P}$ for *X*=Spec(*R*), *P* a point in *Y*=Spec(*R/I*). This is used to embed $T_P(Y)$ in $T_{f^{-1}P}(X)$.[^o6] Since morphisms of fields are injective, the surjection of the [residue fields](wikipedia://en/10789680) induced by *g* is an isomorphism. Then a morphism *k* of the cotangent spaces is induced by *g*, given by

$$\mathfrak{m}_P/\mathfrak{m}_P^2$$

$$\cong (\mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}/I)/((\mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}^2+I)/I)$$

$$\cong \mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}/(\mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}^2+I)$$

$$\cong (\mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}/\mathfrak{m}_{f^{-1}P}^2)/\mathrm{Ker}(k).$$

Since this is a surjection, the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844#Transpose_of_a_linear_map) $k^*:T_P(Y) \rightarrow T_{f^{-1}P}(X)$ is an injection.

(One often defines the [tangent](wikipedia://en/30807) and [cotangent spaces](wikipedia://en/6620) for a manifold in the analogous manner.)

:::

## Analytic functions {#Analytic_functions}

If *V* is a subvariety of an *n*-dimensional vector space, defined by an ideal *I*, then *R = F<sub>n</sub>* / *I*, where *F<sub>n</sub>* is the ring of smooth/analytic/holomorphic functions on this vector space. The Zariski tangent space at *x* is

*m<sub>n</sub> /* (*I+m<sub>n</sub><sup>2</sup>*)*,*

where *m<sub>n</sub>* is the maximal ideal consisting of those functions in *F<sub>n</sub>* vanishing at *x*.

In the planar example above, *I* = (*F*(*X,Y*)), and *I+m<sup>2</sup> =* (*L*(*X,Y*))*\+m<sup>2</sup>.*

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

If *R* is a [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129) local ring, the dimension of the tangent space is at least the [dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) of *R*:

$$\dim{\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \geq \dim{R}}$$

*R* is called [regular](wikipedia://en/506959) if equality holds. In a more geometric parlance, when *R* is the local ring of a variety *V* at a point *v*, one also says that *v* is a regular point. Otherwise it is called a **singular point**.

The tangent space has an interpretation in terms of *K*\[*t*\]*/*(*t<sup>2</sup>*), the [dual numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_numbers) for *K*; in the parlance of [schemes](wikipedia://en/364754), [morphisms](wikipedia://en/13564) from *Spec* *K*\[*t*\]*/*(*t<sup>2</sup>*) to a scheme *X* over *K* correspond to a choice of a [rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200) *x ∈ X(k)* and an element of the tangent space at *x*.[^n2] Therefore, one also talks about **tangent vectors**. See also: [tangent space to a functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangent_space_to_a_functor).

In general, the dimension of the Zariski tangent space can be extremely large. For example, let $C^1(\mathbf{R})$ be the ring of continuously differentiable real-valued functions on $\mathbf{R}$. Define $R = C_0^1(\mathbf{R})$ to be the ring of germs of such functions at the origin. Then *R* is a local ring, and its maximal ideal *m* consists of all germs which vanish at the origin. The functions $x^\alpha$ for $\alpha \in (1, 2)$ define linearly independent vectors in the Zariski cotangent space $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, so the dimension of $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ is at least the $\mathfrak{c}$, the cardinality of the continuum. The dimension of the Zariski tangent space $(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2)^*$ is therefore at least $2^\mathfrak{c}$. On the other hand, the ring of germs of smooth functions at a point in an *n*-manifold has an *n*-dimensional Zariski cotangent space.

:::
