# Alfred Foster (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alfred Foster"}

**Alfred Leon Foster** (1904-1994) was an [American mathematician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_mathematician).[^o3] He was a professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), from 1934 until 1971.[^o4] In 1932, he was an Invited Speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in Zürich.

:::

In 1934, he accepted a regular position at [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California_Berkeley). At that time, [Griffith Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith_Evans) was Head of the Mathematics Department and was charged by President Sproul with building a first-class mathematics center, which he did. Alfred Foster and [Charles Morrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Morrey) (the first department chairman after Evans' retirement) were Evans' first two appointments. Except for subsequent sabbatical leaves, spent most notably in [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) and [Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tübingen), Foster served continuously at Berkeley until his retirement at the then-mandatory age of 67 in 1971.

Foster's Ph.D. dissertation and his first few papers were in [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). From this point, he soon focused on the related theory of [Boolean algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebras) and [Boolean rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_rings) and was thus led from logic to algebra. He extensively studied the role of [duality](wikipedia://en/609737) in Boolean theory. Subsequently, he developed a theory of n-ality for certain rings, which played for [n-valued logics](wikipedia://en/38024) the role of Boolean rings vis-a-vis Boolean algebras. The late [Benjamin Bernstein](wikipedia://en/50635919) of the Berkeley mathematics faculty was his collaborator in some of this research. This work culminated in his seminal paper "The Theory of Boolean-like Rings", appearing in 1946.[^o3]

Foster was married to Else Wagner;[^o5] their marriage produced four children and eight grandchildren.[^o3]
