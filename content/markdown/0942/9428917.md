In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **snub** is an operation applied to a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470). The term originates from [Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler)'s names of two [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847), for the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) (cubus simus) and [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) (dodecaedron simum).[^o4]

:::

In general, snubs have [chiral symmetry](wikipedia://en/32703814) with two forms: with clockwise or counterclockwise orientation. By Kepler's names, a snub can be seen as an [expansion](wikipedia://en/6341070) of a [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299): moving the faces apart, twisting them about their centers, adding new polygons centered on the original vertices, and adding pairs of triangles fitting between the original edges.

The terminology was generalized by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter), with a slightly different definition, for a wider set of [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/6842392).

## Conway snubs {#Conway_snubs}

[John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) explored generalized polyhedron operators, defining what is now called [Conway polyhedron notation](wikipedia://en/5021705), which can be applied to polyhedra and tilings. Conway calls Coxeter's operation a *semi-snub*.[^o6]

In this notation, [snub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_snub_operator) is defined by the dual and [gyro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_gyro_operator) operators, as *s* = *dg*, and it is equivalent to an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) of a truncation of an [ambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_ambo_operator) operator. Conway's notation itself avoids Coxeter's alternation (half) operation since it only applies for polyhedra with even-sided faces.

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter)'s snub terminology is slightly different, meaning an [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429), deriving the [snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249) as a *snub [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280)*, and the [snub dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/287137) as a *snub [icosidodecahedron](wikipedia://en/15023)*. This definition is used in the naming of two [Johnson solids](wikipedia://en/16034): the [snub disphenoid](wikipedia://en/1133565) and the [snub square antiprism](wikipedia://en/1133918), and of higher dimensional polytopes, such as the 4-dimensional [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756), with extended Schläfli symbol s{3,4,3}, and Coxeter diagram .

A [regular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/333299) (or tiling), with Schläfli symbol $\begin{Bmatrix} p , q \end{Bmatrix}$, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , has [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) defined as $t \begin{Bmatrix} p , q \end{Bmatrix}$, and , and has snub defined as an [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) truncation $ht \begin{Bmatrix} p , q \end{Bmatrix} = s \begin{Bmatrix} p , q \end{Bmatrix}$, and . This alternated construction requires *q* to be even.

A [quasiregular polyhedron](wikipedia://en/8810651), with Schläfli symbol $\begin{Bmatrix} p \\ q \end{Bmatrix}$ or *r*{*p*,*q*}, and Coxeter diagram or , has quasiregular [truncation](wikipedia://en/4224429) defined as $t\begin{Bmatrix} p \\ q \end{Bmatrix}$ or *tr*{*p*,*q*}, and or , and has quasiregular snub defined as an [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) truncated rectification $ht\begin{Bmatrix} p \\ q \end{Bmatrix} = s\begin{Bmatrix} p \\ q \end{Bmatrix}$ or *htr*{*p*,*q*} = *sr*{*p*,*q*}, and or .

For example, Kepler's **[snub cube](wikipedia://en/180249)** is derived from the quasiregular [cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280), with a vertical [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) $\begin{Bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , and so is more explicitly called a **snub cuboctahedron**, expressed by a vertical Schläfli symbol $s\begin{Bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and Coxeter diagram . The snub cuboctahedron is the alternation of the *truncated cuboctahedron*, $t\begin{Bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and .

Regular polyhedra with even-order vertices can also be snubbed as alternated truncations, like the *snub octahedron*, as $s\begin{Bmatrix} 3 , 4 \end{Bmatrix}$, , is the alternation of the [truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654), $t\begin{Bmatrix} 3 , 4 \end{Bmatrix}$, and . The *snub octahedron* represents the [pseudoicosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoicosahedron), a regular [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084) with [pyritohedral symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyritohedral_symmetry).

The *snub tetratetrahedron*, as $s\begin{Bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and , is the alternation of the truncated tetrahedral symmetry form, $t\begin{Bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \end{Bmatrix}$, and .

### Coxeter's higher-dimensional snubbed polytopes and honeycombs {#Coxeter's_higher-dimensional_snubbed_polytopes_and_honeycombs}
