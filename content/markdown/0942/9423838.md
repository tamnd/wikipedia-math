# Protocol analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Protocol analysis"}

**Protocol analysis** is a [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology) research method that [elicits](wikipedia://en/39717451) verbal reports from research participants. Protocol analysis is used to study thinking in [cognitive psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_psychology) (Crutcher, 1994), [cognitive science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_science) (Simon & Kaplan, 1989), and behavior analysis (Austin & Delaney, 1998). It has found further application in the design of surveys and interviews (Sudman, Bradburn & Schwarz, 1996), [usability testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usability_testing) (Henderson, Smith, Podd, & Varela-Alvarez, 1995), [educational psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_psychology) (Pressley & Afflerbach 1995; Renkl, 1997) and [design research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_research) (Gero & McNeill 1998). With the introduction of video- and audio-based surveys, the scale and scope of verbal report collection is increased dramatically compared to in-person verbal report recording (Byrd, Joseph, Gongora, & Sirota 2023).

:::
