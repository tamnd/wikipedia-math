# Order-4 pentagonal tiling

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Order-4 pentagonal tiling"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **order-4 pentagonal tiling** is a [regular](wikipedia://en/505253#Hyperbolic_tilings) tiling of the [hyperbolic plane](wikipedia://en/241291). It has [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) of {5,4}. It can also be called a **pentapentagonal tiling** in a bicolored quasiregular form.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

This tiling represents a hyperbolic [kaleidoscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaleidoscope) of 5 mirrors meeting as edges of a regular pentagon. This symmetry by [orbifold notation](wikipedia://en/2915678) is called \*22222 with 5 order-2 mirror intersections. In [Coxeter notation](wikipedia://en/32088502) can be represented as \[5<sup>\*</sup>,4\], removing two of three mirrors (passing through the pentagon center) in the \[5,4\] symmetry.

The kaleidoscopic domains can be seen as bicolored pentagons, representing mirror images of the fundamental domain. This coloring represents the uniform tiling t<sub>1</sub>{5,5} and as a [quasiregular tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasiregular_tiling) is called a *pentapentagonal tiling*.

:::

## Related polyhedra and tiling {#Related_polyhedra_and_tiling}

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra and tilings with [pentagonal](wikipedia://en/20741014) faces, starting with the [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {5,n}, and [Coxeter diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) , progressing to infinity.

This tiling is also topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra and tilings with four faces per vertex, starting with the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), with [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {n,4}, and Coxeter diagram , with n progressing to infinity.

This tiling is topologically related as a part of sequence of regular polyhedra and tilings with vertex figure (4<sup>n</sup>).
