# Alexander G. Burgess

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander G. Burgess"}

**Alexander G. Burgess** (24 December 1872 – 29 March 1932) was a Scottish mathematician. He served as president of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940). He is noted for his work on Tripolar Co-ordinates.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Wishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wishaw), [Lanarkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanarkshire) in southern Scotland in 1872.

He attended Wishaw Academy and then [George Watson's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Watson's_College) in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). In 1890 he won a bursary allowing him to study mathematics and natural philosophy at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) receiving an MA degree in 1894. He studied under [Professor George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803).[^o8]

He became a teacher at [Merchiston Castle School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchiston_Castle_School). From there he moved to [Rothesay Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothesay_Academy) and Edinburgh Ladies' College before returning to Rothesay as [Rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of Rothesay Academy and the Thomson Institute in 1917.[^o11][^o10]

He died on 29 March 1932.

## Contributions and service {#Contributions_and_service}

In 1900 he became a member of the [Edinburgh Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/635940), presenting at least 13 papers to the body over his membership. He served as its Secretary 1908–11, Vice-president 1911–12 and President 1912–13. During this period he organised the first Mathematical Colloquium in the city.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1902 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).

The University of Edinburgh awarded him an honorary Doctorate (DSc) in 1924, specifically for his thesis on Tripolar Co-ordinates.[^o9][^o6]
