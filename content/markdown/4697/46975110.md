# Lasse Rempe

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Lasse Rempe"}

**Lasse Rempe** (born 20 January 1978) is a German mathematician born in [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel).[^o11] His research interests include [holomorphic dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_dynamics) and [function theory](wikipedia://en/14110).[^o4] He currently holds the position of [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Mathematics) at the [Department of Mathematics, University of Manchester](wikipedia://en/10387568).[^o4] He previously worked at the [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool) from 2006 to 2024,[^o5] holding the "Shipowners' Chair" of Pure Mathematics from 2020 to 2024.[^o6] Rempe recorded the voiceover for a [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC) feature on the art of mathematics, where he explained how certain pictures have arisen from [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087).[^o7]

:::

## Name {#Name}

From 2012 to 2020, he used the name Lasse Rempe-Gillen.[^o9]

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Rempe earned his Master of Arts degree in mathematics from [State University of New York at Stony Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University) in 2000[^o11] and his doctorate at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel) in Germany.

## Awards {#Awards}

In June 2010, Rempe was awarded a [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) by the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) for his work in [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518), in particular his research on the [escaping set](wikipedia://en/29115268) for [entire functions](wikipedia://en/10372).[^o14]

In 2012 he was awarded a [Philip Leverhulme Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Leverhulme_Prize).[^o15]

He was elected as a member of the 2017 class of [Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) "for contributions to complex dynamics and function theory, and for communication of mathematical research to broader audiences".[^o16]
