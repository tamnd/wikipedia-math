# Covariance group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Covariance group"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **covariance group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [coordinate transformations](wikipedia://en/81931#Transformations) between [frames of reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference).[^o2] A frame of reference provides a set of coordinates for an observer moving with that frame to make measurements and define physical quantities. The [covariance principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariance_principle) states that the [laws of physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_physics) should transform from one frame to another covariantly, that is, according to a [representation](wikipedia://en/12695) of the covariance group.

:::

[Special relativity](wikipedia://en/26962) considers observers in [inertial frames](wikipedia://en/14838), and the covariance group consists of [rotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_group_SO%283%29), [velocity boosts](wikipedia://en/18404), and the [parity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_%28physics%29). It is denoted as [O(1, 3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_orthogonal_group) and is often referred to as [Lorentz group](wikipedia://en/230489).

For example, the [Maxwell equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equation#Covariant_formulation_of_Maxwell.27s_equations) with sources,

$$\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 4\pi j^\nu,$$

transforms as a [four-vector](wikipedia://en/226424), that is, under the [(1/2, 1/2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representations_of_the_Lorentz_group#Common_representations) representation of the O(1,3) group.

The [Dirac equation](wikipedia://en/39407#Covariant_form_and_relativistic_invariance),

$$(i\gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0,$$

transforms as a [Dirac spinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirac_spinor), that is, under the (1/2, 0) ⊕ (0, 1/2) representation of the O(1, 3) group.

The covariance principle, unlike the [relativity principle](wikipedia://en/74327), does not imply that the equations are [invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_%28physics%29) under transformations from the covariance group. In practice the equations for [electromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_force) and [strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction) interactions *are* invariant, while the [weak interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_interaction) is not invariant under the parity transformation. For example, the Maxwell equation *is* invariant, while the corresponding equation for the [weak field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_model_%28basic_details%29#Electroweak_sector) explicitly contains left currents and thus is not invariant under the parity transformation.

In [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) the covariance group consists of all arbitrary ([invertible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertible) and [differentiable](wikipedia://en/330206)) coordinate transformations.
