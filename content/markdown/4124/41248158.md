# David A. Cox

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David A. Cox"}

**David Archibald Cox** (born September 23, 1948) is a retired[^o4] American mathematician, working in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

Cox graduated from [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) with a bachelor's degree in 1970 and his Ph.D. in 1975 at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), under the supervision of [Eric Friedlander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Friedlander) (*Tubular Neighborhoods in the Etale Topology*).[^o5] From 1974 to 1975, he was assistant professor at [Haverford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) and at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) from 1975 to 1979. In 1979, he became assistant professor and in 1988 professor at [Amherst College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College).

He studies, among other things, [étale homotopy theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_homotopy_theory), [elliptic surfaces](wikipedia://en/3073462), computer-based algebraic geometry (such as [Gröbner basis](wikipedia://en/357328)), [Torelli sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torelli_set) and [toric varieties](wikipedia://en/737087), and [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220). He is also known for several textbooks. He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o6]

From 1987 to 1988 he was a guest professor at [Oklahoma State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_State_University). In 2012, he received the [Lester Randolph Ford Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Randolph_Ford_Award) for *Why [Eisenstein](wikipedia://en/1452482) Proved the [Eisenstein Criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_Criterion) and Why [Schönemann](wikipedia://en/31259301) Discovered It First*.[^o7]
