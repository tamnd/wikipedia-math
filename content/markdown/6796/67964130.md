# Polarization (Lie algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polarization"}

In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), **polarization** is the maximal [totally isotropic subspace](wikipedia://en/10917170) of a certain skew-symmetric [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) on a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944). The notion of polarization plays an important role in construction of [irreducible](wikipedia://en/385982) [unitary representations](wikipedia://en/292788) of some classes of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) by means of the [orbit method](wikipedia://en/4231851)[^o2] as well as in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) on Lie groups and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G$ be a Lie group, $\mathfrak{g}$ the [corresponding](wikipedia://en/43302095) Lie algebra and $\mathfrak{g}^*$ its [dual](wikipedia://en/7988). Let $\langle f,\,X\rangle$ denote the value of the [linear form](wikipedia://en/214137) (covector) $f\in\mathfrak{g}^*$ on a vector $X\in\mathfrak{g}$. The subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}$ of the algebra $\mathfrak g$ is called **subordinate** of $f\in\mathfrak{g}^*$ if the condition

$$\forall X, Y\in\mathfrak{h}\ \langle f,\,[X,\,Y]\rangle = 0,$$

or, alternatively,

$$\langle f,\,[\mathfrak{h},\,\mathfrak{h}]\rangle = 0$$

is satisfied. Further, let the group $G$ [act](wikipedia://en/12781) on the space $\mathfrak{g}^*$ via [coadjoint representation](wikipedia://en/3226011) $\mathrm{Ad}^*$. Let $\mathcal{O}_f$ be the [orbit](wikipedia://en/12781#Orbits_and_stabilizers) of such action which passes through the point $f$ and let $\mathfrak{g}^f$ be the Lie algebra of the [stabilizer](wikipedia://en/12781#Fixed_points_and_stabilizer_subgroups) $\mathrm{Stab}(f)$ of the point $f$. A subalgebra $\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}$ subordinate of $f$ is called a **polarization of the algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ with respect to $f$**, or, more concisely, **polarization of the covector $f$**, if it has maximal possible dimensionality, namely

$$\dim\mathfrak{h} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\dim\,\mathfrak{g} + \dim\,\mathfrak{g}^f\right) = \dim\,\mathfrak{g} - \frac{1}{2}\dim\,\mathcal{O}_f.$$

:::

## Pukanszky condition {#Pukanszky_condition}

The following condition was obtained by [L. Pukanszky](wikipedia://en/22378202):[^o6]

Let $\mathfrak{h}$ be the polarization of algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ with respect to covector $f$ and $\mathfrak{h}^\perp$ be its [annihilator](wikipedia://en/7988#Quotient_spaces_and_annihilators): $\mathfrak{h}^\perp := \{\lambda\in\mathfrak{g}^*|\langle\lambda,\,\mathfrak{h}\rangle = 0\}$. The polarization $\mathfrak{h}$ is said to satisfy the Pukanszky condition if

$$f + \mathfrak{h}^\perp\in\mathcal{O}_f.$$

L. Pukanszky has shown that this condition guaranties applicability of the [Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515)'s [orbit method](wikipedia://en/4231851) initially constructed for [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569) to more general case of [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012) as well.[^o7]

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Polarization is the maximal totally isotropic subspace of the bilinear form $\langle f,\,[\cdot,\,\cdot]\rangle$ on the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$.[^o10]
- For some pairs $(\mathfrak{g},\,f)$ polarization may not exist.[^o10]
- If the polarization does exist for the covector $f$, then it exists for every point of the orbit $\mathcal{O}_f$ as well, and if $\mathfrak{h}$ is the polarization for $f$, then $\mathrm{Ad}_g\mathfrak{h}$ is the polarization for $\mathrm{Ad}^*_g f$. Thus, the existence of the polarization is the property of the orbit as a whole.[^o10]
- If the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ is [completely solvable](wikipedia://en/2966308#Completely_solvable_Lie_algebras), it admits the polarization for any point $f\in\mathfrak{g}^*$.[^o11]
- If $\mathcal{O}$ is the orbit of [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) (i. e. has maximal dimensionality), for every point $f\in\mathcal{O}$ there exists solvable polarization.[^o11]
