::: {#o12 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform tilings in hyperbolic plane"}

In [hyperbolic geometry](wikipedia://en/241291), a **uniform hyperbolic tiling** (or regular, quasiregular or semiregular hyperbolic tiling) is an edge-to-edge filling of the hyperbolic plane which has [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) as [faces](wikipedia://en/149926) and is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) ([transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_group_action) on its [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417), isogonal, i.e. there is an [isometry](wikipedia://en/254777) mapping any vertex onto any other). It follows that all vertices are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330), and the [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671) has a high degree of rotational and translational [symmetry](wikipedia://en/53741).

:::

Uniform tilings can be identified by their [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745), a sequence of numbers representing the number of sides of the polygons around each vertex. For example, 7.7.7 represents the [heptagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/8834198) which has 3 [heptagons](wikipedia://en/365349) around each vertex. It is also regular since all the polygons are the same size, so it can also be given the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {7,3}.

Uniform tilings may be [regular](wikipedia://en/333299) (if also face- and edge-transitive), quasi-regular (if edge-transitive but not face-transitive) or [semi-regular](wikipedia://en/1548894) (if neither edge- nor face-transitive). For right triangles (*p* *q* 2), there are two regular tilings, represented by [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {*p*,*q*} and {*q*,*p*}.

## Wythoff construction {#Wythoff_construction}

*Image: Wythoffian construction diagram.svg.* Example of Wythoff construction with right triangles (*r* = 2) and the 7 generator points. Lines to the active mirrors are colored red, yellow, and blue with the 3 nodes opposite them as associated by the Wythoff symbol.

There are an infinite number of uniform tilings based on the [Schwarz triangles](wikipedia://en/2069325#Triangles_for_the_hyperbolic_plane) (*p q r*) where $\tfrac{1}{p}$ + $\tfrac{1}{q}$ + $\tfrac{1}{r}$ \< 1, where *p*, *q*, *r* are each orders of reflection symmetry at three points of the [fundamental domain triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_domain_triangle) – the symmetry group is a hyperbolic [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342).

Each symmetry family contains 7 uniform tilings, defined by a [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) or [Coxeter-Dynkin diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram), 7 representing combinations of 3 active mirrors. An 8th represents an [alternation](wikipedia://en/4349117) operation, deleting alternate vertices from the highest form with all mirrors active.

Families with *r* = 2 contain [regular hyperbolic tilings](wikipedia://en/505253#Hyperbolic_tilings), defined by a [Coxeter group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_Coxeter_group) such as \[7,3\], \[8,3\], \[9,3\], ... \[5,4\], \[6,4\], ....

Hyperbolic families with *r* = 3 or higher are given by (*p q r*) and include (4 3 3), (5 3 3), (6 3 3) ... (4 4 3), (5 4 3), ... (4 4 4)....

Hyperbolic triangles (*p q r*) define compact uniform hyperbolic tilings. In the limit any of *p*, *q* or *r* can be replaced by ∞ which defines a paracompact hyperbolic triangle and creates uniform tilings with either infinite faces (called [apeirogons](wikipedia://en/5658261)) that converge to a single ideal point, or infinite vertex figure with infinitely many edges diverging from the same ideal point.

More symmetry families can be constructed from fundamental domains that are not triangles.

Selected families of uniform tilings are shown below (using the [Poincaré disk model](wikipedia://en/30871836) for the hyperbolic plane). Three of them – (7 3 2), (5 4 2), and (4 3 3) – and no others, are *minimal* in the sense that if any of their defining numbers is replaced by a smaller integer the resulting pattern is either Euclidean or spherical rather than hyperbolic; conversely, any of the numbers can be increased (even to infinity) to generate other hyperbolic patterns.

Each uniform tiling generates a [dual uniform tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_tessellation), with many of them also given below.

### Regular hyperbolic tilings {#Regular_hyperbolic_tilings}

The regular tiling {*p*,*q*} has a dual tiling {*q*,*p*} across the diagonal axis of the table. Self-dual tilings {2,2}, [{3,3}](wikipedia://en/30606), [{4,4}](wikipedia://en/2863809), [{5,5}](wikipedia://en/38358615), etc. pass down the diagonal of the table.

## General triangle domains {#General_triangle_domains}

There are infinitely many general [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342) families (*p q r*). This article shows uniform tilings in 9 families: (4 3 3), (4 4 3), (4 4 4), (5 3 3), (5 4 3), (5 4 4), (6 3 3), (6 4 3), and (6 4 4).

### (4 3 3) {#(4_3_3)}

The [(4 3 3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/433_symmetry) [triangle group](wikipedia://en/2069342), [Coxeter group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_Coxeter_group) \[(4,3,3)\], [orbifold](wikipedia://en/2915678#Hyperbolic_plane) (\*433) contains these uniform tilings. Without right angles in the fundamental triangle, the [Wythoff constructions](wikipedia://en/2950777) are slightly different. For instance in the (4,3,3) [triangle family](wikipedia://en/2069342), the *snub* form has six polygons around a vertex and its dual has hexagons rather than pentagons. In general the [vertex figure](wikipedia://en/1005719) of a snub tiling in a triangle (*p*,*q*,*r*) is p. 3.q.3.r.3, being 4.3.3.3.3.3 in this case below.

## Quadrilateral domains {#Quadrilateral_domains}

*Image: Square\_kaleidoscope\_generators.png.* A quadrilateral domain has 9 generator point positions that define uniform tilings. Vertex figures are listed for general orbifold symmetry \**pqrs*, with 2-gonal faces degenerating into edges.

### (3 2 2 2) {#(3_2_2_2)}

*Image: Example 3222 hyperbolic uniform tilings kaleidoscopes.png.* Example uniform tilings of \*3222 symmetry

Quadrilateral fundamental domains also exist in the hyperbolic plane, with the [\*3222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3222_symmetry) [orbifold](wikipedia://en/2915678#Hyperbolic_plane) (\[∞,3,∞\] Coxeter notation) as the smallest family. There are 9 generation locations for uniform tiling within quadrilateral domains. The vertex figure can be extracted from a fundamental domain as 3 cases (1) Corner (2) Mid-edge, and (3) Center. When generating points are corners adjacent to order-2 corners, degenerate {2} [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728) faces at those corners exist but can be ignored. [Snub](wikipedia://en/9428917) and [alternated](wikipedia://en/4349117) uniform tilings can also be generated (not shown) if a vertex figure contains only even-sided faces.

[Coxeter diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_diagram) of quadrilateral domains are treated as a degenerate [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) graph with 2 of 6 edges labeled as infinity, or as dotted lines. A logical requirement of at least one of two parallel mirrors being active limits the uniform cases to 9, and other ringed patterns are not valid.
