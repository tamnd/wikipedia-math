# Book (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Book"}

*Image: Graph book sample.gif.* A triangular book

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **book graph** (often written $B_p$ ) may be any of several kinds of graph formed by multiple cycles sharing an edge.

:::

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Variations"}

## Variations {#Variations}

One kind, which may be called a **quadrilateral book**, consists of *p* [quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/25278) sharing a common edge (known as the "spine" or "base" of the book). That is, it is a [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of a [star](wikipedia://en/19310129) and a single edge.[^o5] The 7-page book graph of this type provides an example of a graph with no [harmonious labeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonious_labeling).[^o5]

A second type, which might be called a **triangular book**, is the complete tripartite graph *K*<sub>1,1,*p*</sub>. It is a graph consisting of $p$ [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) sharing a common edge.[^o6] A book of this type is a [split graph](wikipedia://en/9928681). This graph has also been called a $K_e(2,p)$[^o7] or a **thagomizer graph** (after [thagomizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thagomizer), the spiked tails of [stegosaurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stegosauria) dinosaurs, because of their pointy appearance in certain drawings) and their [graphic matroids](wikipedia://en/745714) have been called thagomizer matroids.[^o8] Triangular books form one of the key building blocks of [line perfect graphs](wikipedia://en/54306580).[^o9]

The term "book-graph" has been employed for other uses. Barioli[^o10] used it to mean a graph composed of a number of arbitrary subgraphs having two vertices in common. (Barioli did not write $B_p$ for his book-graph.)

:::

## Within larger graphs {#Within_larger_graphs}

Given a graph $G$, one may write $bk(G)$ for the largest book (of the kind being considered) contained within $G$.

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Theorems on books"}

## Theorems on books {#Theorems_on_books}

Denote the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) of two triangular books by $r(B_p,\ B_q).$ This is the smallest number $r$ such that for every $r$-vertex graph, either the graph itself contains $B_p$ as a subgraph, or its [complement graph](wikipedia://en/1175649) contains $B_q$ as a subgraph.

- If $1\leq q$, then $r(B_1,\ B_q)=2q+3$.[^o14]
- There exists a constant $c=o(1)$ such that $r(B_p,\ B_q)=2q+3$ whenever $q\geq cp$.
- If $p\leq q/6+o(q)$, and $q$ is large, the [Ramsey number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_number) is given by $2q+3$.
- Let $C$ be a constant, and $k = Cn$. Then every graph on $n$ vertices and $m$ edges contains a (triangular) $B_k$.[^o15]

:::
