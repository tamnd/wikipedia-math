# Felix Bernstein (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Felix Bernstein"}

**Felix Bernstein** (24 February 1878 – 3 December 1956), was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for proving in 1896 the [Schröder–Bernstein theorem](wikipedia://en/44218028), a central result in [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553),[^o15][^o14][^o4] and less well known for demonstrating in 1924 the correct [blood group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_type) inheritance pattern of multiple [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) at one [locus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_%28genetics%29) through [statistical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_analysis).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Felix Bernstein was born in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle,_Saxony-Anhalt) on 24 February 1878 to a Jewish family of academics.[^o6] His father [Julius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Bernstein) held the Chair of Physiology at the [Martin Luther University of Halle-Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_University_of_Halle-Wittenberg), and was the Director of the Physiological Institute at the University of Halle.

While still in [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28Germany%29) in Halle, Bernstein heard the university seminar of [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216), who was a friend of Bernstein's father.[^o15] From 1896 to 1900, Bernstein studied at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin), and the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen).[^o7] In the early [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), Bernstein temporarily was Göttingen vice-chairman of the local chapter of [German Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Party).[^o8][^o9][^o10] In 1933,[^o11] after [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler)'s rise to power, Bernstein was removed from his chair, per §6 of the Nazi [Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_for_the_Restoration_of_the_Professional_Civil_Service), often used against politically unpopular persons. He received the message of his dismissal during a research/lecturing journey (started on Dec. 1st, 1932) to the United States, and he stayed there.[^o7][^o8][^o10]

Bernstein was a visiting professor of mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) from 1933 to 1936 and a professor of [biometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometry) at [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University) from 1936 to 1943.[^o12] In 1942, he was elected a fellow of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science).[^o13] In 1948, Bernstein retired from teaching in the US, and returned to Europe. He mainly lived in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) and [Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg), occasionally visiting Göttingen,[^o7] where he became [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).[^o14] He died in [Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich) on 3 December 1956.[^o15][^o14]
