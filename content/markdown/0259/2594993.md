# James Pierpont (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James Pierpont"}

**James P. Pierpont** (June 16, 1866 – December 9, 1938) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) born in Connecticut.

:::

## Life {#Life}

His father [Cornelius Pierpont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius_Pierpont) was a wealthy [New Haven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Haven) businessman. He did undergraduate studies at [Worcester Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester_Polytechnic_Institute), initially in mechanical engineering, but turned to mathematics. He went to Europe after graduating in 1886. He studied in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and later in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866). He prepared his Ph.D. at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) under [Leopold Gegenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Gegenbauer) and [Gustav Ritter von Escherich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Ritter_von_Escherich). His thesis, defended in 1894, was entitled *Zur Geschichte der Gleichung fünften Grades bis zum Jahre 1858*. After his defense, he returned to New Haven and was appointed as a lecturer at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University), where he would spend most of his career. In 1898, he became professor.

## Research {#Research}

Initially, his research dealt with the [Galois theory](wikipedia://en/61316) of equations. The [Pierpont primes](wikipedia://en/2595060) are named after him, as he introduced them in 1895 in connection with a problem of constructing [regular polygons](wikipedia://en/333306) using [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673). After 1900, he worked in real and complex analysis.

In his textbooks of [real analysis](wikipedia://en/26478), he introduced a definition of the integral analogous to [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration). His definition was later criticized by [Maurice Fréchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fréchet). In the 1920s, he finally turned his interest to [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610).
