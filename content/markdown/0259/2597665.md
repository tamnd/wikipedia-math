# Bulgarian solitaire

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bulgarian solitaire"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [game theory](wikipedia://en/11924), **Bulgarian solitaire** is a [card game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitaire) that was introduced by [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435).[^o2]

:::

## Rules {#Rules}

In the game, a pack of $N$ cards is divided into several piles. Then for each pile, remove one card; collect the removed cards together to form a new pile (piles of zero size are ignored).

If $N$ is a [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) (that is, $N=1+2+\cdots+k$ for some $k$), then it is known that Bulgarian solitaire will reach a stable configuration in which the sizes of the piles are $1,2,\ldots, k$. This state is reached in $k^2-k$ moves or fewer. If $N$ is not triangular, no stable configuration exists and a [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950) is reached.

### Random Bulgarian solitaire {#Random_Bulgarian_solitaire}

In *random Bulgarian solitaire* or *[stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222) Bulgarian solitaire* a pack of $N$ cards is divided into several piles. Then for each pile, either leave it intact or, with a fixed [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) $p$, remove one card; collect the removed cards together to form a new pile (piles of zero size are ignored). This is a [finite](wikipedia://en/11742) irreducible [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876).

## History {#History}

Martin Gardner introduced the game in the August 1983 issue of [Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American).[^o2]

In 2004, Brazilian [probabilist](wikipedia://en/22934) Serguei Popov demonstrated that stochastic Bulgarian solitaire spends "most" of its time in a "roughly" [triangular distribution](wikipedia://en/663496).[^o6]
