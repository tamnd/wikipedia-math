# Psychologism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Psychologism"}

**Psychologism** (or **logical psychologism**) is a family of [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical) positions, according to which [logical laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_law), truths, or principles are grounded in, [reducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductionism) to, or explained by [psychological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological) facts, laws, or processes.

:::

The term was coined by [Johann Eduard Erdmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Eduard_Erdmann) as *Psychologismus*, being translated into English as *psychologism*.[^o2][^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The *[Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)* defines *psychologism* as: "The view or doctrine that a theory of psychology or ideas forms the basis of an account of metaphysics, epistemology, or meaning; (sometimes) spec. the explanation or derivation of mathematical or logical laws in terms of psychological facts."[^o6] Psychologism in [epistemology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology), the idea that its problems "can be solved satisfactorily by the psychological study of the development of mental processes", was argued in [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke)'s *[An Essay Concerning Human Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Essay_Concerning_Human_Understanding)* (1690).[^o7]

Other forms of psychologism are logical psychologism and mathematical psychologism. Logical psychologism is a position in [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) (or the [philosophy of logic](wikipedia://en/21368888)) according to which [logical laws](wikipedia://en/55868) and [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) laws are grounded in, derived from, explained or exhausted by psychological facts or laws. Psychologism in the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439) is the position that mathematical concepts and/or truths are grounded in, derived from or explained by psychological facts or laws.[^o8]

:::

## Viewpoints {#Viewpoints}

[John Stuart Mill](wikipedia://en/15626) was accused by [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl) of being an advocate of a type of logical psychologism, although this may not have been the case.[^o10] So were many nineteenth-century German philosophers such as [Christoph von Sigwart](wikipedia://en/885684), [Benno Erdmann](wikipedia://en/35195717), [Theodor Lipps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Lipps), [Gerardus Heymans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Heymans), [Wilhelm Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Jerusalem), and [Theodor Elsenhans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Elsenhans),[^o11] as well as a number of psychologists, past and present (e.g., [Wilhelm Wundt](wikipedia://en/34128)[^o11] and [Gustave Le Bon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Le_Bon)).[^o12]

Psychologism was notably criticized by [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) in his [anti-psychologistic](wikipedia://en/2021691) work *[The Foundations of Arithmetic](wikipedia://en/8983001)*, and many of his works and essays, including his review of Husserl's *[Philosophy of Arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6704759)*.[^o13] Husserl, in the first volume of his *[Logical Investigations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_Investigations_%28Husserl%29)*, called "The Prolegomena of Pure Logic", criticized psychologism thoroughly and sought to distance himself from it. Frege's arguments were largely ignored, while Husserl's were widely discussed.[^o2]

In "Psychologism and Behaviorism", [Ned Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Block) describes psychologism in the [philosophy of mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mind) as the view that "whether behavior is intelligent behavior depends on the character of the internal information processing that produces it." This is in contrast to a behavioral view which would state that intelligence can be ascribed to a being solely via observing its behavior. This latter type of behavioral view is strongly associated with the [Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test).[^o14][^o15]
