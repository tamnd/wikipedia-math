# Chevalley scheme

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chevalley scheme"}

A **Chevalley scheme** in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) was a precursor notion of [scheme theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_theory).

:::

Let *X* be a separated integral [noetherian scheme](wikipedia://en/2783891), *R* its [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843). If we denote by $X'$ the set of subrings $\mathcal O_x$ of *R*, where *x* runs through *X* (when $X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)$, we denote $X'$ by $L(A)$), $X'$ verifies the following three properties:

- For each $M\in X'$, *R* is the field of fractions of *M*.
- There is a finite set of noetherian subrings $A_i$ of *R* so that $X'=\cup_i L(A_i)$ and that, for each pair of indices *i,j*, the subring $A_{ij}$ of *R* generated by $A_i \cup A_j$ is an $A_i$-algebra of finite type.
- If $M\subseteq N$ in $X'$ are such that the maximal ideal of *M* is contained in that of *N*, then *M=N*.

Originally, [Chevalley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley) also supposed that R was an extension of finite type of a field K and that the $A_i$'s were algebras of finite type over a field too (this simplifies the second condition above).
