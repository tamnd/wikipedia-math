# David L Wiltshire

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David L Wiltshire"}

**David Lauri Wiltshire** is a [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) at the [University of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury), in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch), New Zealand, where he leads the Gravity and Cosmology Group.[^o3]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Wiltshire completed undergraduate studies in physics and mathematics and an MSc at the University of Canterbury, Christchurch in 1983.[^o5] In 1987, Wiltshire completed a PhD in theoretical physics at [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), supervised by [Gary Gibbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gibbons).[^o5][^o6]

Following his PhD, Wiltshire held [postdoctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) positions at the [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy); the [University of Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Newcastle_upon_Tyne), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK); and the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide), [South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Australia), where he then began lecturing. In July 2001, he returned to the University of Canterbury to take up an academic position in the Department of Physics and Astronomy.[^o5]

## Research {#Research}

Wiltshire's research interests include [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) and [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).

Wiltshire is best known for the [timescape cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhomogeneous_cosmology#Timescape_cosmology).[^o8] He reinterprets [dark energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_energy) as a misidentification of gradients in [gravitational energy](wikipedia://en/6513985) in an inhomogeneous universe, in which the present epoch [cosmic web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable_universe#Large-scale_structure) is dominated by [voids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Void_%28astronomy%29).[^o9][^o10] His predictions about average cosmic expansion will be subject to precision tests including observations by the [Euclid mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_%28spacecraft%29), the [Dark Energy Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Energy_Survey), and the [Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Energy_Spectroscopic_Instrument) (DESI).

On 19 December 2024, Wiltshire and colleagues announced [^o11] the results of a new model-independent statistical analysis of the Pantheon+ [Type Ia Supernova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supernova#Type_I) catalogue. They found that the timescape model fit the data better than the standard [ΛCDM model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ΛCDM_model) at 99% confidence level, providing evidence for a foundational change to cosmological models.[^o12]

## Awards {#Awards}

In 2023 Wiltshire was awarded the [Dan Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Walls) Medal by the New Zealand Institute of Physics.[^o14]
