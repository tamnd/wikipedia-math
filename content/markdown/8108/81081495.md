# Jayadev Athreya

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jayadev Athreya"}

**Jayadev Siddhanta Athreya** (born 1983[^o3]) is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington). He is a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822). His research interests are in [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems), and [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Athreya grew up in [Ames, Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames,_Iowa) where he obtained a Bachelor of Science at [Iowa State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_State_University) in 2000. He went on to earn a MS in 2001 and a PhD in 2006 both in mathematics at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) where he was advised by [Alex Eskin](wikipedia://en/31883666).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

After graduating Athreya was a postdoctoral fellow at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). In 2006, he was at Yale. In 2007, he took on a position at Princeton for a year before returning back to be a Gibbs instructor at Yale.[^o5][^o7]

In 2010, he started a position as assistant professor at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois) where he founded and directed the Illinois Geometry Lab, now known as the Illinois Mathematics Lab.[^o8] In 2015, he went on to be an associate professor at the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) and created the Washington Experimental Mathematics Lab.[^o9] In 2020, he was promoted to full professor at the University of Washington.[^o5]

In 2021, Athreya became Interim Director of the [Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/34978739) where in 2022 he took on the position of co-Director. Since 2021, Athreya still resides on the board of directors of the Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences.[^o7]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He was awarded a visiting fellowship by the London Mathematical Society in 2009.[^o5]

In 2025, Athreya became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o11]

## Social justice {#Social_justice}

The activity of collaborating with the police is not something we feel a mathematician should be doing.

In reaction to [recent killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_racial_unrest_%282020–2023%29) of [individuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_killed_during_the_2020–23_United_States_racial_unrest) in 2020, Athreya and other mathematicians wrote a letter pleading for their peers to cease collaborations with [police departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_departments) due to well documented disparities in how these agencies treated people of different races and ethnicities. More specifically, they criticized the practice of [predictive policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predictive_policing).[^o13]

In 2023, Athreya signed a letter in solidarity with the [Haverford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverford_College) [Faculty for Justice in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_for_Justice_in_Palestine) as well as to sharing mourning over the killings of the students Kinnan Abdalhamid, Tahseen Ahmed, and Hisham Awartani in November of the same year.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

Athreya along with [David Aulicino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Aulicino) and Patrick Hooper discovered that there exists an infinite number of straight paths on the dodecahedron that start and end at one vertex without intersecting any other vertex.[^o16][^o17]
