# Higher Topos Theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Higher Topos Theory"}

***Higher Topos Theory*** is a treatise on the theory of [∞-categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-categories) written by American mathematician [Jacob Lurie](wikipedia://en/26663863). In addition to introducing Lurie's new theory of [∞-topoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-topoi), the book is widely considered foundational to [higher category theory](wikipedia://en/5274013). Since 2018, Lurie has been transferring the contents of *Higher Topos Theory* (along with new material) to Kerodon, an "online resource for homotopy-coherent mathematics" inspired by the [Stacks Project](wikipedia://en/48211685).

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

*Higher Topos Theory* covers two related topics: ∞-categories and ∞-topoi (which are a special case of the former). The first five of the book's seven chapters comprise a rigorous development of general ∞-category theory in the language of quasicategories, a special class of [simplicial set](wikipedia://en/1027784) which acts as a model for ∞-categories. The path of this development largely parallels classical [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), with the notable exception of the ∞-categorical [Grothendieck construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_construction); this correspondence, which Lurie refers to as "straightening and unstraightening", gains considerable importance in his treatment.

The last two chapters are devoted to ∞-topoi, Lurie's own invention and the ∞-categorical analogue of [topoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topoi) in classical category theory. The material of these chapters is original, and is adapted from an earlier preprint of Lurie's. There are also appendices discussing background material on [categories](wikipedia://en/53759), [model categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_categories), and [simplicial categories](wikipedia://en/29190294).

## History {#History}

*Higher Topos Theory* followed an earlier work by Lurie, *On Infinity Topoi*, uploaded to the [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) in 2003. Algebraic topologist [Peter May](wikipedia://en/14644098) was critical of this preprint, emailing Lurie's then-advisor [Mike Hopkins](wikipedia://en/20652569) "to say that Lurie’s paper had some interesting ideas, but that it felt preliminary and needed more rigor." Lurie released a draft of *Higher Topos Theory* on the arXiv in 2006, and the book was finally published in 2009.

Lurie released a second book on higher category theory, *Higher Algebra*, as a preprint on his website in 2017. This book assumes the content of *Higher Topos Theory* and uses it to study algebra in the ∞-categorical context.
