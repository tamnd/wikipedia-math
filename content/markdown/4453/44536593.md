# Saito–Kurokawa lift

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Saito–Kurokawa lift"}

In mathematics, the **Saito–Kurokawa lift** (or **lifting**) takes [elliptic modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_modular_form) to [Siegel modular forms](wikipedia://en/7721927) of degree 2. The existence of this lifting was conjectured in 1977 independently by [Hiroshi Saito](wikipedia://en/44554025) and (txt). Its existence was almost proved by (txt), and (Andrianov 1979) and (Zagier 1981) completed the proof.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

The Saito–Kurokawa lift *σ*<sub>*k*</sub> takes level 1 modular forms *f* of weight 2*k* − 2 to level 1 Siegel modular forms of degree 2 and weight *k*. The L-functions (when *f* is a Hecke eigenforms) are related by *L*(*s*,*σ*<sub>*k*</sub>(*f*)) = ζ(*s* − *k* + 2)ζ(*s* − *k* + 1)*L*(*s*, *f*).

The Saito–Kurokawa lift can be constructed as the composition of the following three mappings:

1. The Shimura correspondence from level 1 modular forms of weight 2*k* − 2 to a space of level 4 modular forms of weight *k* − 1/2 in the Kohnen plus-space.
1. A map from the Kohnen plus-space to the space of [Jacobi forms](wikipedia://en/23991325) of index 1 and weight *k*, studied by [Eichler](wikipedia://en/4224478) and Zagier.
1. A map from the space of Jacobi forms of index 1 and weight *k* to the Siegel modular forms of degree 2, introduced by Maass.

The Saito–Kurokawa lift can be generalized to forms of higher level.

The image is the **Spezialschar** (special band), the space of Siegel modular forms whose Fourier coefficients satisfy

$$a \begin{pmatrix} n & t/2 \\ t/2 & m \end{pmatrix} =\sum_{d\mid t,m,n} d^{k-1}a \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t/2d \\ t/2d & nm/d^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

:::
