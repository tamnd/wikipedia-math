# 2-ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="2-ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **categorical ring** is, roughly, a [category](wikipedia://en/53759) equipped with addition and multiplication. In other words, a categorical ring is obtained by replacing the [underlying set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underlying_set) of a [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) by a category. For example, given a ring *R*, let *C* be a category whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are the elements of the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *R* and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are only the identity morphisms. Then *C* is a categorical ring. But the point is that one can also consider the situation in which an element of *R* comes with a "nontrivial [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160)".[^o2]

:::

This line of generalization of a ring eventually leads to the notion of an [*E*<sub>*n*</sub>-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_ring).
