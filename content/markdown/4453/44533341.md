# ∞-topos

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="∞-topos"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **∞-topos** (infinity-[topos](wikipedia://en/21391464)) is, roughly, an [∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/∞-category) such that its objects behave like [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) of spaces with some choice of [Grothendieck topology](wikipedia://en/12910); in other words, it gives an intrinsic notion of sheaves without reference to an external space. The prototypical example of an ∞-topos is the ∞-category of sheaves of spaces on some [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). But the notion is more flexible; for example, the ∞-category of [étale sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Étale_sheaves) on some [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) is not the ∞-category of sheaves on any topological space but it is still an ∞-topos.

:::

Precisely, in [Lurie](wikipedia://en/26663863)'s *[Higher Topos Theory](wikipedia://en/44533369)*, an ∞-topos is defined[^o2] as an ∞-category *X* such that there is a small ∞-category *C* and an ([accessible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessible_∞-category)) left exact [localization functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localization_of_an_∞-category) from the ∞-category of [presheaves of spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presheaf_of_spaces) on *C* to *X*. A theorem of Lurie[^o3] states that an ∞-category is an ∞-topos if and only if it satisfies an ∞-categorical version of Giraud's axioms in [ordinary topos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_topos) theory. A "[topos](wikipedia://en/21391464)" is a category behaving like the category of sheaves of sets on a topological space. In analogy, Lurie's definition and characterization theorem of an ∞-topos says that an ∞-topos is an ∞-category behaving like the category of sheaves of spaces.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Lurie characterization theorem"}

## Lurie characterization theorem {#Lurie_characterization_theorem}

It says:

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Theorem"}

**Theorem.**

Let $X$ be an ∞-category. Then the following are equivalent.

(a) $X$ is an ∞-topos.

(b) $X$ satisfies Giraud's axioms in the ∞-category setting: (1) it is a [presentable ∞-category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presentable_∞-category), (2) colimits in *X* are universal, (3) coproducts in *X* are disjoint and (4) every groupoid object in *X* is [effective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_groupoid).
