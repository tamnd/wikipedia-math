# Douglas Woodall

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Douglas Woodall"}

**Douglas Robert Woodall** (born November 1943 in [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent)) is a British mathematician and [election scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_science). He studied mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) and earned his PhD from the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) in 1969, his thesis being "Some results in combinatorial mathematics". He worked in the Department of Mathematics from 1969 until his retirement in 2007, as researcher, lecturer, associate professor and reader.[^o2] He devised the [later-no-harm criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later-no-harm_criterion), a [voting system criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_system_criterion) in the [comparison of electoral systems](wikipedia://en/53544734), and demonstrated it is compatible with the [monotonicity criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotonicity_criterion) by developing his method of descending solid coalitions as an improvement on [instant-runoff voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting). He also contributed to the problem of [fair cake-cutting](wikipedia://en/43212478), for example, by presenting an algorithm for finding a [super-proportional division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super-proportional_division).

:::
