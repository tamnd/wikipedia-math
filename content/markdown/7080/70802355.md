# Spectral dimension

the spacetime concept

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spectral dimension"}

The **spectral dimension** is a [real-valued](wikipedia://en/20646438) quantity that characterizes a [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). It characterizes a spread into space over time, e.g. an ink drop [diffusing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusing) in a water glass or the evolution of a [pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandemic) in a population. Its definition is as follow: if a phenomenon spreads as $t^n$, with $t$ the time, then the spectral dimension is $2n$. The spectral dimension depends on the topology of the space, e.g., the distribution of neighbors in a population, and the diffusion rate.

:::

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), the concept of spectral dimension is used, among other things, in [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312),[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5][^o6] [percolation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percolation_critical_exponents), [superstring theory](wikipedia://en/19595676),[^o7] or [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267).[^o8]

::: {#o10 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The diffusion of ink in an [isotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotropic) homogeneous medium like still water evolves as $t^{3/2}$, giving a spectral dimension of 3.

Ink in a 2D [Sierpiński triangle](wikipedia://en/29638) diffuses following a more complicated path and thus more slowly, as $t^{0.6826}$, giving a spectral dimension of 1.3652.[^o11]

:::
