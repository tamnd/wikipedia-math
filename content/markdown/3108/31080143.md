# S (set theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="S"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **S** is an [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) set out by [George Boolos](wikipedia://en/340621) in 1989. **S**, a [first-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_order_logic) theory, is two-sorted because its [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) includes "stages" as well as [sets](wikipedia://en/26691). Boolos designed **S** to embody his understanding of the "iterative conception of set" and the associated [iterative hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iterative_hierarchy). **S** has the important property that all axioms of [Zermelo set theory](wikipedia://en/322623), except the [axiom of extensionality](wikipedia://en/52376) and the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840), are theorems of **S** or a slight modification thereof.

:::

## Ontology {#Ontology}

Any grouping together of [mathematical](wikipedia://en/19453961), [abstract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_object), or concrete objects, however formed, is a *collection*, a synonym for what other [set theories](wikipedia://en/27553) refer to as a [class](wikipedia://en/20696). The things that make up a collection are called [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Element_%28mathematics%29) or members. A common instance of a collection is the [domain of discourse](wikipedia://en/229180) of a [first-order theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory).

All sets are collections, but there are collections that are not sets. A synonym for collections that are not sets is [proper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_class). An essential task of [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory) is to distinguish sets from proper classes, if only because mathematics is grounded in sets, with proper classes relegated to a purely descriptive role.

The [Von Neumann universe](wikipedia://en/445980) implements the "iterative conception of set" by stratifying the universe of sets into a series of "stages", with the sets at a given stage being possible members of the sets formed at all higher stages. The notion of stage goes as follows. Each stage is assigned an [ordinal number](wikipedia://en/26547932). The lowest stage, stage $0$, consists of all entities having no members. We assume that the only entity at stage $0$ is the [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566), although this stage would include any [urelements](wikipedia://en/643648) we would choose to admit. For any $n > 0$, stage $n$ consists of all possible sets formed from elements to be found in any stage whose number is less than $n$. It follows that every set formed at stage $n$ can also be formed at every stage greater than $n$.[^n1]

Hence the stages form a nested and [well-ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well-ordered) sequence, and would form a [hierarchy](wikipedia://en/3612259) if set membership were [transitive](wikipedia://en/200463). The iterative conception has gradually become more accepted, despite an imperfect understanding of its historical origins.

The iterative conception of set steers clear, in a well-motivated way, of the well-known [paradoxes](wikipedia://en/24390) of [Russell](wikipedia://en/46095), [Burali-Forti](wikipedia://en/51653), and [Cantor](wikipedia://en/2732904). These paradoxes all result from the [unrestricted use of the principle of comprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrestricted_comprehension) of [naive set theory](wikipedia://en/4944). Collections such as "the class of all sets" or "the class of all [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932)" include sets from all stages of the iterative hierarchy. Hence such collections cannot be formed at any given stage, and thus cannot be sets.

## Primitive notions {#Primitive_notions}

Let $x$ and $y$ be sets, and $r$, $s$, and $t$ be stages. There are three [primitive](wikipedia://en/23706) two-place [predicates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_%28mathematical_logic%29):

- Set–set: $x\in y$ denotes that set $x$ is a member of set $y$;
- Set–stage: $Fxr$ denotes that set $x$ is formed at stage $r$;
- Stage–stage: $r<s$ denotes that stage $r$ is earlier than stage $s$.

The axioms below include a defined two-place set-stage predicate, $Bxr$, which abbreviates

$$\exists \,s\,[s<r \land Fxs].$$

$Bxr$ is read as "set $x$ is formed before stage $r$".

[Identity](wikipedia://en/298428), denoted by infix '=', does not play the role in **S** it plays in other set theories, and Boolos does not make fully explicit whether the background [logic](wikipedia://en/10983) includes identity. **S** has no [axiom of extensionality](wikipedia://en/52376) and identity is absent from the other **S** axioms. Identity does appear in the axiom schema distinguishing **S+** from **S**,[^n2] and in the derivation in **S** of the [pairing](wikipedia://en/52385), [null set](wikipedia://en/52534), and [infinity](wikipedia://en/246449) axioms of **[Z](wikipedia://en/322623)**.[^n3]

## Axioms {#Axioms}

The symbolic axioms shown below govern how sets and stages behave and interact.[^n4] The natural language versions of the axioms are intended to aid the intuition.

The axioms come in two groups of three. The first group consists of axioms pertaining solely to stages and the stage-stage relation ‘\<’.

**Tra**: $\forall r \forall s \forall t[r<s \land s<t \rightarrow r<t] \,.$

"Earlier than" is transitive.

**Net**: $\forall s \forall t \exists r[t<r \land s<r] \,.$

A consequence of *Net* is that every stage is earlier than some stage.

**Inf**: $\exists r \exists u [u<r \land \forall t[t<r \rightarrow \exists s[t<s \land s<r]]] \,.$

The sole purpose of *Inf* is to enable deriving in **S** the [axiom of infinity](wikipedia://en/246449) of other set theories.

The second and final group of axioms involve both sets and stages, and the predicates other than '\<':

**All**: $\forall x \exists r Fxr \,.$

Every set is formed at some stage in the hierarchy.

**When**: $\forall r \forall x [Fxr \leftrightarrow [\forall y (y \in x \rightarrow Byr) \land \lnot Bxr] ] \,.$

A set is formed at some stage [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) its members are formed at earlier stages.

Let *A*(*y*) be a formula of **S** where *y* is free but *x* is not. Then the following axiom schema holds:

**Spec**: $\exists r \forall y[A(y) \rightarrow Byr] \rightarrow \exists x \forall y[y \in x \leftrightarrow A(y)] \,.$

If there exists a stage *r* such that all sets satisfying *A*(*y*) are formed at a stage earlier than *r*, then there exists a set *x* whose members are just those sets satisfying *A*(*y*). The role of *Spec* in **S** is analogous to that of the [axiom schema of specification](wikipedia://en/52386) of [Z](wikipedia://en/322623).

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

Boolos’s name for [Zermelo set theory](wikipedia://en/322623) minus extensionality was *Z-*. Boolos derived in **S** all axioms of *Z-* except the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).[^n5][^n3] The purpose of this exercise was to show how most of conventional set theory can be derived from the iterative conception of set, assumed embodied in **S**. [Extensionality](wikipedia://en/294056) does not follow from the iterative conception, and so is not a theorem of **S**. However, **S** + Extensionality is free of contradiction if **S** is free of contradiction.

Boolos then altered *Spec* to obtain a variant of **S** he called **S+**, such that the [axiom schema of replacement](wikipedia://en/52387) is derivable in **S+** + Extensionality. Hence **S+** + Extensionality has the power of [ZF](wikipedia://en/152214). Boolos also argued that the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) does not follow from the iterative conception, but did not address whether Choice could be added to **S** in some way.[^n2] Hence **S+** + Extensionality cannot prove those theorems of the conventional set theory [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) whose proofs require Choice.

**Inf** guarantees the existence of stages ω, and of ω + *n* for finite *n*, but not of stage ω + ω. Nevertheless, **S** yields enough of [Cantor's paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_paradise) to ground almost all of contemporary mathematics.[^o14]

Boolos compares **S** at some length to a variant of the system of [Frege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frege)’s *Grundgesetze*, in which [Hume's principle](wikipedia://en/314743), taken as an axiom, replaces Frege’s Basic Law V, an [unrestricted comprehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrestricted_comprehension) axiom which made Frege's system inconsistent; see [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095).
