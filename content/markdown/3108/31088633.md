# Real closed ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Real closed ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **real closed ring** (**RCR**) is a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *A* that is a [subring](wikipedia://en/24873145) of a [product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_ring) of [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093), which is closed under [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [semi-algebraic](wikipedia://en/13329722) functions defined over the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

:::

## Examples of real closed rings {#Examples_of_real_closed_rings}

Since the rigorous definition of a real closed ring is of technical nature it is convenient to see a list of prominent examples first. The following rings are all real closed rings:

- [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093). These are exactly the real closed rings that are [fields](wikipedia://en/10603).
- the ring of all [real-valued continuous functions](wikipedia://en/30554#Real-valued_continuous_functions) on a [completely regular space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completely_regular_space) *X*. Also, the ring of all [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) [real](wikipedia://en/20646438)-valued continuous functions on *X* is real closed.
- convex subrings of real closed fields. These are precisely those real closed rings which are also [valuation rings](wikipedia://en/966856) and were initially studied by Cherlin and Dickmann (they used the term "real closed ring" for what is now called a "real closed valuation ring").
- the ring *A* of all continuous [semi-algebraic functions](wikipedia://en/13329722) on a [semi-algebraic set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-algebraic_set) of a real closed field (with values in that field). Also, the subring of all bounded (in any sense) functions in *A* is real closed.
- (generalizing the previous example) the ring of all (bounded) continuous [definable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definable_function) on a [definable set](wikipedia://en/5569486) *S* of an arbitrary first-order [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_a_first-order_structure) *M* of a real closed field (with values in *M*). Also, the ring of all (bounded) definable functions $S\to M$ is real closed.
- Real closed rings are precisely the rings of [global sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_section) of affine real closed spaces (a generalization of [semialgebraic spaces](wikipedia://en/18748966)) and in this context they were invented by Niels Schwartz in the early 1980s.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A real closed ring is a reduced, commutative unital ring *A* which has the following properties:

1. The set of squares of *A* is the set of nonnegative elements of a partial order ≤ on *A* and (*A*,≤) is an [f-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-ring).
1. Convexity condition: For all *a*, *b* in *A*, if 0 ≤ *a* ≤ *b* then *b* | *a*<sup>2</sup>.
1. For every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) *p* of *A*, the [residue class ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_class_ring) *A*/*p* is [integrally closed](wikipedia://en/21420209) and its [field of fractions](wikipedia://en/25271) is a real closed field.

The link to the definition at the beginning of this article is given in the section on algebraic properties below.

:::

## The real closure of a commutative ring {#The_real_closure_of_a_commutative_ring}

Every commutative unital ring *R* has a so-called **real closure** rcl(*R*) and this is unique up to a unique [ring homomorphism](wikipedia://en/26411) over *R*. This means that rcl(*R*) is a real closed ring and there is a (not necessarily [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective)) ring homomorphism $r:R\to rcl(R)$ such that for every ring homomorphism $f:R\to A$ to some other real closed ring *A*, there is a unique ring homomorphism $g:rcl(R)\to A$ with $f=g\circ r$.

For example, the real closure of the [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065) $\mathbb{R}[T_1,...,T_n]$ is the ring of continuous semi-algebraic functions $\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}$.

An arbitrary ring *R* is semi-real (i.e. −1 is not a sum of squares in *R*) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the real closure of *R* is not the null ring.

The real closure of an [ordered field](wikipedia://en/22430) is in general **not** the real closure of the underlying field. For example, the real closure of the **ordered** subfield $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$ of $\mathbb{R}$ is the field $\mathbb{R}_{alg}$ of real [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158), whereas the real closure of the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$ is the ring $\mathbb{R}_{alg}\times \mathbb{R}_{alg}$ (corresponding to the two orders of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$). More generally the real closure of a field *F* is a certain [subdirect product](wikipedia://en/17395221) of the real closures of the ordered fields (*F*,*P*), where *P* runs through the orderings of *F*.

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

- The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) **RCR** of real closed rings which has real closed rings as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and ring homomorphisms as [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) has the following properties:

<!-- -->

1. Arbitrary [products](wikipedia://en/237868), direct limits and inverse limits (in the category of commutative unital rings) of real closed rings are again real closed. The [fibre sum](wikipedia://en/582024) of two real closed rings *B*,*C* over some real closed ring *A* exists in **RCR** and is the real closure of the [tensor product](wikipedia://en/30844) of *B* and *C* over *A*.
1. **RCR** has arbitrary [limits and colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limits_and_colimits).
1. **RCR** is a [variety](wikipedia://en/1021753) in the sense of [universal algebra](wikipedia://en/45200) (but not a subvariety of commutative rings).

<!-- -->

- For a real closed ring *A*, the natural homomorphism of *A* to the product of all its [residue fields](wikipedia://en/10789680) is an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) [onto](wikipedia://en/27873) a subring of this product that is closed under continuous [semi-algebraic](wikipedia://en/13329722) functions defined over the integers. Conversely, every subring of a product of real closed fields with this property is real closed.
- If *I* is a [radical ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_ideal) of a real closed ring *A*, then also the [residue class ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_class_ring) *A*/*I* is real closed. If *I* and *J* are radical ideals of a real closed ring then the sum *I* + *J* is again a radical ideal.
- All classical [localizations](wikipedia://en/252216) *S*<sup>−1</sup>*A* of a real closed ring *A* are real closed. The epimorphic hull and the complete ring of quotients of a real closed ring are again real closed.
- The (real) holomorphy ring *H*(*A*) of a real closed ring *A* is again real closed. By definition, *H*(*A*) consists of all elements *f* in *A* with the property *−N* ≤ *f* ≤ *N* for some [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) *N*. Applied to the examples above, this means that the rings of bounded (semi-algebraic/definable) continuous functions are all real closed.
- The support map from the [real spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_spectrum) of a real closed ring to its [Zariski spectrum](wikipedia://en/37291), which sends an ordering *P* to its support $P\cap -P$ is a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660). In particular, the Zariski spectrum of every real closed ring *A* is a [root system](wikipedia://en/277087) (in the sense of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401)) and therefore *A* is also a Gel'fand ring (i.e. every [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) of *A* is contained in a unique [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) of *A*). The comparison of the Zariski spectrum of *A* with the Zariski spectrum of *H*(*A*) leads to a homeomorphism between the maximal spectra of these rings, generalizing the Gel'fand-Kolmogorov theorem for rings of real valued continuous functions.
- The natural map *r* from an arbitrary ring *R* to its real closure rcl(*R*) as explained above, induces a homeomorphism from the real spectrum of rcl(*R*) to the real spectrum of *R*.
- Summarising and significantly strengthening the previous two properties, the following is true: The natural map *r* from an arbitrary ring *R* to its real closure rcl(*R*) induces an identification of the [affine scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_scheme) of rcl(*R*) with the affine real closed space of *R*.
- Every local real closed ring is a [Henselian ring](wikipedia://en/14623383) (but in general local real closed domains are not valuation rings).

:::

## Model theoretic properties {#Model_theoretic_properties}

The class of real closed rings is [first-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-order_theory) [axiomatizable](wikipedia://en/928) and [undecidable](wikipedia://en/913118). The class of all real closed valuation rings is [decidable](wikipedia://en/913118) (by Cherlin-Dickmann) and the class of all real closed fields is decidable (by Tarski). After naming a definable radical relation, real closed rings have a [model companion](wikipedia://en/15931153), namely [von Neumann regular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_regular) real closed rings.

## Comparison with characterizations of real closed fields {#Comparison_with_characterizations_of_real_closed_fields}

There are many different characterizations of [real closed **fields**](wikipedia://en/1066093). For example, in terms of maximality (with respect to algebraic extensions): a real closed field is a maximally orderable field; or, a real closed field (together with its unique ordering) is a maximally ordered field. Another characterization says that the [intermediate value theorem](wikipedia://en/14884) holds for all polynomials in one variable over the (ordered) field. In the case of commutative rings, all these properties can be (and are) analyzed in the literature. They all lead to different classes of rings which are unfortunately also called "real closed" (because a certain characterization of real closed fields has been extended to rings). **None** of them lead to the class of real closed rings and none of them allow a satisfactory notion of a closure operation. A central point in the definition of real closed rings is the globalisation of the notion of a real closed field to rings when these rings are represented as rings of functions on some space (typically, the real spectrum of the ring).
