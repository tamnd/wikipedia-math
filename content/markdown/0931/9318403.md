# Markov brothers' inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov brothers' inequality"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Markov brothers' inequality** is an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489), [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) in the 1890s by brothers [Andrey Markov](wikipedia://en/915) and [Vladimir Markov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Andreevich_Markov), two Russian mathematicians. This inequality [bounds](wikipedia://en/42693) the maximum of the [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) on an [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) in terms of the maximum of the polynomial.[^o2] For *k* = 1 it was proved by Andrey Markov,[^o3] and for *k* = 2, 3, ... by his brother Vladimir Markov.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="The statement"}

## The statement {#The_statement}

Let *P* be a polynomial of [degree](wikipedia://en/5930652) ≤ *n*. Then for all nonnegative [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $k$

$$\max_{-1 \leq x \leq 1} \big|P^{(k)}(x)\big| \leq \frac{n^2 (n^2 - 1^2) (n^2 - 2^2) \cdots (n^2 - (k - 1)^2)}{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdots (2k - 1)} \max_{-1 \leq x \leq 1} |P(x)|.$$

This inequality is tight, as equality is attained for [Chebyshev polynomials](wikipedia://en/184539) of the first kind.

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

Markov's inequality is used to obtain lower bounds in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) via the so-called "polynomial method".[^o10]
