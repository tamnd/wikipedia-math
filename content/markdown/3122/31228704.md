# Prime factor exponent notation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Prime factor exponent notation"}

In his 1557 work *[The Whetstone of Witte](wikipedia://en/19934039)*, [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Robert Recorde](wikipedia://en/310247) proposed an [exponent](wikipedia://en/99491) [notation](wikipedia://en/277184) by [prime factorisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factorisation), which remained in use up until the eighteenth century and acquired the name ***Arabic exponent notation***. The principle of Arabic exponents was quite similar to [Egyptian fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_fractions); large exponents were broken down into smaller prime numbers. Squares and cubes were so called; prime numbers from five onwards were called *sursolids*.

:::

Although the terms used for defining exponents differed between authors and times, the general system was the primary exponent notation until [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) devised the Cartesian exponent notation, which is still used today.
