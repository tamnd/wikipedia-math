# Ergo decedo

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ergo decedo"}

**Ergo decedo**, [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "therefore I leave" or "then I go off", a truncation of ***argumentum ergo decedo***, and colloquially denominated the **traitorous critic fallacy**,[^o2] denotes responding to the criticism of a critic by implying that the critic is motivated by undisclosed favorability or affiliation to an [out-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingroups_and_outgroups), rather than responding to the criticism itself. The fallacy implicitly alleges that the critic does not appreciate the [values and customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition) of the criticized group or is [traitorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason), and thus suggests that the critic should avoid the question or topic entirely, typically by leaving the criticized group.[^o3]

:::

*Argumentum ergo decedo* is generally categorized as a type of [informal fallacy](wikipedia://en/1655450) and more specifically as a species of the subclass of [ad hominem](wikipedia://en/2226) informal fallacies.

## In politics {#In_politics}

*Argumentum ergo decedo* is directly related to the *[tu quoque](wikipedia://en/1090534)* fallacy when responding to political criticism. As [whataboutism](wikipedia://en/35842043) is used against external criticism, *ergo decedo* is used against internal criticism.
