# Frank Anscombe

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frank Anscombe"}

**Francis John Anscombe** (13 May 1918 – 17 October 2001) was an English [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in [Hove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hove) in England, Anscombe was educated at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge). After serving in the Second World War, he joined [Rothamsted Experimental Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothamsted_Experimental_Station) for two years before returning to Cambridge as a lecturer.

In [experiments](wikipedia://en/59861), Anscombe emphasized [randomization](wikipedia://en/65462#Statistical_analysis) in both the design and analysis phases. In the [design](wikipedia://en/9541) phase, Anscombe argued that the experimenters should [randomize](wikipedia://en/2940730) the labels of [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_block_design).[^o5][^o6] In the [analysis](wikipedia://en/634#Randomization-based_analysis) phase, Anscombe argued that the randomization plan should guide the analysis of data; Anscombe's approach has influenced [John Nelder](wikipedia://en/5865261) and [R. A. Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._A._Bailey) in particular.

Anscombe moved to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1956, and in the same year he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o7] He became the founding chairman of the [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) department at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 1963.[^o8][^o10]

## Research {#Research}

*Image: Anscombe's quartet 3.svg.* Anscombe illustrated the importance of graphing data with these four data sets.

According to [David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771), his best-known work may be his 1961 account of formal properties of [residuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errors_and_residuals_in_statistics) in [linear regression](wikipedia://en/48758386).[^o10][^o11] His earlier suggestion for a [variance-stabilizing transformation](wikipedia://en/22715170) for [Poisson](wikipedia://en/23009144) data is often known as the [Anscombe transform](wikipedia://en/12382740).[^o12]

He later became interested in [statistical computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_computing), and stressed that "a computer should make both calculations *and* graphs", and illustrated the importance of graphing data with four data sets now known as [Anscombe's quartet](wikipedia://en/10019241).[^o13] He later published a textbook on statistical computing in [APL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APL_%28programming_language%29).[^o14]

In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [decision theory](wikipedia://en/446216), Anscombe is best known for a 1963 paper with [Robert Aumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Aumann) which provides the standard basis for the theory of [subjective probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjective_probability).[^o15]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1953, Anscombe visited [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he worked with fellow statistician [John Tukey](wikipedia://en/415974). In Princeton, he married Phyllis Rapp, the sister of Tukey's wife Elizabeth. Anscombe and his wife had four children.[^o10]
