# Julius Telesin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Julius Telesin"}

**Julius Zinovyevich Telesin** (Юлиус Зиновьевич Телесин, Julius Zinov'evič Telesin; born April 1, 1933, in Moscow) is a Russian-Israeli mathematician, human rights activist, chess player, poet, and translator. He was a notable participant in the Soviet *[samizdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samizdat)* culture of the 1960s. He emigrated to Israel where he lived since 1970.[^o2] He taught mathematics at [the University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev) of [Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba).[^o3]

:::

Telesin was born on April 1, 1933, in Moscow. He is the son of Jewish Yiddish-language poet Zyama Telesin (Зяма Телесин / Zinovy Lvovich Telesin; 1907, Kalinkovichi, Minsk province – 1996, Jerusalem).[^o4] His mother was the poet Rakhil (Rachel) Boymvol.[^o5]

## 1001 anekdot {#1001_anekdot}

Telesin compiled a collection of *1001 Soviet political jokes*[^o7] published in 1986 in [Tenafly, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenafly,_New_Jersey) (Hermitage Press). Bruce Friend Adams,[^o8] who cites it in his study of Soviet and Russian history through jokes, praised it as the most clever and funny presentation of such material:

There are many collections of Soviet anecdotes. The funniest, most clever presentation of a large number of these that I have seen is Iulius Telesin's *1001 Izbrannyi Sovetskii Politicheskii Anekdot*. Telesin arranged 1001 well-chosen anecdotes topically and added humorous rhyming quatrains called *chastushki* (the Russian equivalent of limericks), excerpts from Soviet dissident publications such as the *Khronika tekushchikh sobytii* (*Chronicle of Current Events*) and other snippets to make a very entertaining book.

*Image: 1938\_CPA\_594.jpg.* Soviet stamp (1938) featuring [Vasily Chapayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Chapayev), [Petka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Isaev) and a [Maxim gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_gun), referencing the film *[Chapayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapayev_%28film%29)*

The book includes jokes involving [Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), [Rabinovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_jokes#Rabinovich),[^o9] [Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), [Chapayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Chapayev), [Petka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Isaev),[^o10] [Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky), [Kosygin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Kosygin), [Mao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong), [Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev), [Gierek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Gierek), [Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin), [Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Dzerzhinsky), [Radio Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Yerevan), [Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter), [Vovochka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Johnny), [Molotov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Molotov), [Furtseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterina_Furtseva), [Chernenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Chernenko) and many others.

Example (Joke No. 43):

*Why did Lenin wear shoes, and Stalin boots? – Because under Lenin, the filth in Russia only reached the ankles.*

## Selected publications {#Selected_publications}

- *Samizdat in Brief: An Introductory Article*. 1973.
- Foreword to Peter Reddaway, *Uncensored Russia: Protest and Dissent in the Soviet Union; the Unofficial Moscow Journal, a Chronicle of Current Events*. New York: American Heritage Press, 1972.
- *Detstvo v tyurʹme: memuary Petra Yakira* (*Childhood in Prison: Recollections of [Pyotr Yakir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Yakir)*). London: Macmillan, 1972.

- *Stishki o suverennoi demokratii* (*Poems about Sovereign Democracy*). Jerusalem: J. Telesin, 2008.
