# Bernhard Minnigerode

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Bernhard Minnigerode"}

**Bernhard Minnigerode** (10 August 1837 – 15 August 1896) was a German mathematician, mineralogist and Alpine climber. His name, together with [Franz Ernst Neumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ernst_Neumann), is associated with [Curie's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie's_principle) which is also named the Neumann–Minnigerode–Curie principle.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Career {#Career}

Minnigerode was born on 10 August 1837 in [Darmstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt). Minnigerode was a student of Neumann at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and gained his PhD with a study of heat conduction in crystals in 1862. Subsequently he moved to the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald) and was appointed an associate professor of mathematics in 1874 and a full professor in 1885.[^o7] Minnigerode's work spanned mathematics, physics, mineralogy and crystallography.

In 1884 Minnigerode studied the derivation of the [elastic constants of crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_crystallography_before_X-rays#Elastic_properties), and stated the general relation between the physical characteristics and the symmetry of crystals: "Physically speaking, crystals have all the symmetrical properties of their form; some of the physical properties, however, have still higher symmetry."[^o8][^o9]

In the same three-part paper Minnigerode also derived the 32 [crystal classes](wikipedia://en/663786) (point groups) using [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890) and stated "The symmetry group of a crystal is a subgroup of the symmetry groups of all the physical phenomena which may possibly occur in that crystal."[^o10] However, in a letter to [Arthur Moritz Schoenflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moritz_Schoenflies) dated 21 October 1890, [Evgraf Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgraf_Fedorov) claimed that the credit for discovering the 32 crystal classes should go to his countryman [Axel Gadolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Gadolin) and not to Minnigerode.[^o11][^o12] Gadolin's method was based on [stereographic projection](wikipedia://en/143431) rather than group theory, and it was not until the work of [Andreas Speiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Speiser) in the 1920s that it was recognised that it is exactly the group properties that make symmetry significant for crystals.[^o13]

In 1886 Minnigerode extended his symmetry method from elasticity to [thermal conduction in crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_crystallography_before_X-rays#Thermal_conduction).[^o14][^o15] According to Shaul Katzir, a historian of science, "This was the earliest application of a rigorous argument of symmetry in physics beyond elasticity."[^o8][^o16] In 1887 Minnigerode published the first written statement of the Neumann–Minnigerode–Curie symmetry principle.[^o17] A later reviewer commented on Minnigerode's paper: "His discussion, while highly mathematical, is elegant and brief."[^o18]

In the 1880s [Arthur Moritz Schoenflies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Moritz_Schoenflies) was using group theory in his studies of crystallographic [space groups](wikipedia://en/463721). His derivation of the 230 space groups was published in 1894 and tended to obscure Minnigerode's earlier contributions.[^o19]

Minnigerode died in [Špindlerův Mlýn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Špindlerův_Mlýn) in the area of the [Giant Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Mountains) on 15 August 1896.

## Publications {#Publications}

- 1862: PhD thesis: Über Wärmeleitung in Krystallen \[On heat conduction in crystals\][^o21]
- 1873: Paper introducing the [nearest integer](wikipedia://en/169945#Rounding_to_the_nearest_integer) algorithm using [continued fractions](wikipedia://en/739199)[^o22][^o23]
- 1873: Paper on [quadratic forms](wikipedia://en/251478) with [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) coefficients[^o24]
- 1884: Derivation of the 32 [crystal classes](wikipedia://en/663786) using [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890)[^o9] [part II](http://eudml.org/doc/180034) [part III](https://eudml.org/doc/180042) [^o25]
- 1886: Paper on [heat conduction in crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_crystallography_before_X-rays#Thermal_conduction) making use of symmetry considerations[^o14]
- 1887: Paper in which the Neumann–Minnigerode–Curie principle is formally stated[^o17]

## Alpine climber {#Alpine_climber}

Minnigerode was an [Alpine climber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climbing) and a member of the Basel section of the [Swiss Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alpine_Club).[^o27] One of the couloirs on the East face of the [Ortler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortler) was named after Minnigerode following his first attempted climb in 1878. For further details of Minnigerode's climbing achievements see it:Bernhard Minnigerode.

## Honours {#Honours}

Minnigerode was elected as a corresponding member of the mathematical class of the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) in 1874.[^o29]
