# Gerald Schwarz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gerald Schwarz"}

**Gerald Walter Schwarz** (born February 15, 1946, [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon), United States) is an American mathematician and Professor Emeritus at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University). Schwarz specializes in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762), algebraic [group actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) and [invariant differential operators](wikipedia://en/6935363).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Of German descent, Schwarz's father, Ernst, was one of the 30,000 Jews seized during [Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht). He was imprisoned at the [Buchenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald) concentration camp until his wife, Elaine, managed to secure a visa to travel abroad. Upon his release from the camp, the couple fled to England where Gerald's older brother, Maurice, was born. In November 1939, Ernst, Elaine and Maurice arrived in the United States, eventually settling in Portland, Oregon.[^o4] Gerald was born seven years later. He spent his childhood in Portland, then moved to Cambridge, Massachusetts to attend school.

Schwarz earned his B.S. and M.S. degrees from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) (MIT) in 1969 and his Ph.D. in mathematics from MIT in 1972.[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Schwarz began his career at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (1972–74) as a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher), then joined the faculty at Brandeis University in [Waltham, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltham,_Massachusetts) (1974). He spent the next academic year at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey (1975–76), where he recognized that the solution of the [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250)/[isotopy](wikipedia://en/212250#Isotopy) [lifting problem](wikipedia://en/522786) requires [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924). The resulting [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) helps mathematicians classify smooth compact [lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) actions on [manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifolds). The [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) of the theorem appears in the paper *Lifting smooth homotopies of orbit spaces* [^o7] and led to a [tenured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenure) position at Brandeis in 1978. Four years later, Schwarz was promoted to full Professor.

Schwarz has written or co-authored over 60 journal articles in the field of mathematics.[^o8] In 1996, he was one of the founding editors of the journal *Transformation Groups,*[^o9] and continued as one of its Managing Editors until February 2000.[^o10] In 2012, he became a member of the inaugural class of fellows of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) which recognizes mathematicians who have made significant contributions to the field.[^o11]
