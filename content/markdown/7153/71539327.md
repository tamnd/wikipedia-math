# Hockey stick graph

*Image: Hockey pencil.jpg.* A "hockey pencil" imitating the shape of an ice hockey stick, with the eraser being the blade; its bottom edge here is a fair representation of a hockey stick graph. If the graph starts above zero and drops a little before rising, it may be called a [J curve](wikipedia://en/2839796).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hockey stick graph"}

A **hockey stick graph** or **hockey stick curve** is a graph, or curve shape, that resembles an [ice hockey stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_stick), in that it turns sharply from a nearly flat "blade" to a long "handle".

:::

In [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics),[^o2][^o3] [marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing),[^o4] and [dose–response relationships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dose–response_relationship),[^o5][^o6] a hockey stick graph is one in which the "blade" is near zero (hugging the floor) before the graph turns upward to a long nearly straight increasing section. By contrast, in [climate science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_science), the well-known [hockey stick graph (global temperature)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_stick_graph_%28global_temperature%29) describing 1000 years of global or hemispheric temperature has the "handle" horizontal and "blade" turning upward.[^o7] This difference of viewpoint is remarked on in a 2020 novel about [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change):

*Image: T comp 61-90.pdf.* The northern hemisphere hockey stick graph – smoothed curve shown in blue with its uncertainty range in light blue, overlaid with green dots showing the 30-year global average of the 2013 reconstruction. The red curve shows measured global mean temperature, according to [HadCRUT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HadCRUT)4 data from 1850 to 2013.
