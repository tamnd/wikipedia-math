# Gheorghe Călugăreanu

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Gheorghe Călugăreanu"}

**Gheorghe Călugăreanu** (16 June 1902 – 15 November 1976) was a Romanian mathematician, professor at [Babeș-Bolyai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University), and full member of the [Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Academy).

:::

He was born in [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iași), the son of physician, naturalist, and physiologist [Dimitrie Călugăreanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Călugăreanu). From 1913 to 1921 he studied at the [Gheorghe Lazăr High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Lazăr_National_College,_Bucharest) in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), after which he attended [University of Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeș-Bolyai_University), graduating in 1924. In 1926 he went to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) to pursue his studies at the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris), supported by a scholarship from the Romanian government. He obtained his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1929, with thesis *Sur les fonctions polygènes d'une variable complexe* written under the direction of [Émile Picard](wikipedia://en/42000)[^o3] and defended before a jury that also included [Édouard Goursat](wikipedia://en/1674255) and [Gaston Julia](wikipedia://en/249107).[^o5] After returning to Romania, he was appointed assistant the University of Cluj in 1930; he was promoted to lecturer in 1934 and named professor in 1942. From 1953 to 1957 he served as Dean of the Faculty of Mathematics.[^o3][^o6] His Ph.D. students include [Petru Mocanu](wikipedia://en/43247897).[^o4] He was elected a corresponding member of the Romanian Academy in 1955, and he became a full member in 1963.[^o15]

Călugăreanu studied the theory of [functions of a complex variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functions_of_a_complex_variable) ([meromorphic functions](wikipedia://en/80857), [univalent functions](wikipedia://en/1534382), [analytic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_extension) invariants), as well as [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), especially in [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008). In his best-known work,[^o7] he established in 1961 the following foundational result regarding the [writhe](wikipedia://en/968591) of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808): take a [ribbon](wikipedia://en/40355051) in [three-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/3054853), let $\operatorname{Lk}$ be the [linking number](wikipedia://en/451999) of its border components, and let $\operatorname{Tw}$ be its total [twist](wikipedia://en/40392210); then the difference $\operatorname{Wr}=\operatorname{Lk}-\operatorname{Tw}$ depends only on the core curve of the ribbon.[^o8] In a paper from 1959,[^o9] he showed how to calculate the writhe of a knot by means of a Gaussian [double integral](wikipedia://en/2757224).[^o10] Călugăreanu's formula has since been pursued by James H. White[^o11] and F. Brock Fuller,[^o12] leading to applications in [DNA topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid_structure), where writhe is used to describe the amount a piece of [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) is deformed as a result of [torsional](wikipedia://en/3039253) stress (a phenomenon known as [DNA supercoiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_supercoil)).[^o13] The topological interpretation of [helicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicity_%28particle_physics%29) in terms of the Gauss linking number and its limiting form has been called the "Călugăreanu invariant" by [Keith Moffatt](wikipedia://en/9071425) and [Renzo L. Ricca](wikipedia://en/40410682).[^o14]

He died of cancer in [Cluj-Napoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluj-Napoca) in 1976; following his wishes, he was cremated and the urn was deposited at [Bellu Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellu_Cemetery) in Bucharest.[^o15]
