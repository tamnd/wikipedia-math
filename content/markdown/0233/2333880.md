# Hilbert modular form

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hilbert modular form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Hilbert modular form** is a generalization of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) to functions of two or more variables. It is a (complex) [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) on the *m*-fold product of [upper half-planes](wikipedia://en/287826) $\mathcal{H}$ satisfying a certain kind of [functional equation](wikipedia://en/364377).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *F* be a [totally real number field](wikipedia://en/312853) of degree *m* over the rational field. Let $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_m$ be the [real embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_embedding) of *F*. Through them we have a map

$$GL_2(F) \to GL_2(\mathbb{R})^m$$

sending $\gamma$ to $(\sigma_1(\gamma),\ldots,\sigma_m(\gamma).$ Let $\mathcal O_F$ be the [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/460613) of *F*. The group $GL_2^+(\mathcal O_F)$ of matrices with totally positive determinant is called the *full Hilbert modular group*. There is a group action of $GL_2^+ (\mathcal O_F)$ on $\mathcal{H}^m$ defined by $\gamma \cdot (z_1, \ldots, z_m) = (\sigma_1(\gamma) z_1, \ldots, \sigma_m(\gamma) z_m)$.

For

$$g = \begin{pmatrix}a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} \in GL_2(\mathbb{R}),$$

define:

$$j(g, z) = \det(g)^{-1/2} (cz+d)$$

A Hilbert modular form of weight $(k_1,\ldots,k_m)$ is an analytic function on $\mathcal{H}^m$ such that for every $\gamma \in GL_2^+(\mathcal O_F)$

$$f(\gamma z) = \prod_{i=1}^m j(\sigma_i(\gamma), z_i)^{k_i} f(z).$$

If $F=\mathbb{Q}$ then one has to also add an extra boundedness condition at the cusps; however for larger fields $F$ this condition is automatically satisfied because of [Koecher's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koecher's_principle).

:::

## History {#History}

These modular forms, for [real quadratic fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_quadratic_field), were first treated in the 1901 [Göttingen University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_University) *[Habilitationsschrift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitationsschrift)* of [Otto Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Blumenthal). There he mentions that [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) had considered them initially in work from 1893-4, which remained unpublished. Blumenthal's work was published in 1903. For this reason Hilbert modular forms are now often called **Hilbert-Blumenthal modular forms**.

The theory remained dormant for some decades; [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990) appealed to it in his early work, but major interest in Hilbert modular forms awaited the development of [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) theory.
