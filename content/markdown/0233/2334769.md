# Congruent number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Congruent number"}

*Image: Rtriangle-mathsinegypt.svg.* Right triangle with the area 6, a congruent number.

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **congruent number** is a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) that is the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a [right triangle](wikipedia://en/39420) with three [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) sides.[^o3] A more general definition includes all positive rational numbers with this property.[^o4]

:::

For example, 5 is a congruent number because it is the area of a (20/3, 3/2, 41/6) triangle. Similarly, 6 is a congruent number because it is the area of a (3,4,5) triangle. 3 and 4 are not congruent numbers. The triangle sides demonstrating a number is congruent can have very large numerators and denominators, for example 263 is the area of a triangle whose two shortest sides are 16277526249841969031325182370950195/2303229894605810399672144140263708 and 4606459789211620799344288280527416/61891734790273646506939856923765.[^o6]

If $q$ is a congruent number then $s^{2}q$ is also a congruent number for any [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $s$ (just by multiplying each side of the triangle by $s$), and vice versa. This leads to the observation that whether a nonzero rational number $q$ is a congruent number depends only on its residue in the [group](wikipedia://en/19447)

$$\mathbb{Q}^{*}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2},$$

where $\mathbb{Q}^{*}$ is the set of nonzero rational numbers.

Every residue class in this group contains exactly one [square-free integer](wikipedia://en/29525), and it is common, therefore, only to consider square-free positive integers when speaking about congruent numbers.

## History {#History}

While solutions of [quadratic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_equations) and construction of [Pythagorean triples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_triples) were known in ancient times and were transmitted through [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Mathematics) and [Indian Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mathematics), congruent numbers were first tabulated in Arabic manuscripts by the 10th centuryincluding a formulation of the congruent number problem by [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) mathematician [Al-Khazin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Khazin)with 5 and 6 among the earliest known examples.[^o8][^o9] Some scholars speculate an Indian origin for the congruent number problem, mathematician [Frans Oort](wikipedia://en/58171100) finds such an origin unclear since it is not evidenced in standard literature even though related quadratic equations were studied in [medieval India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_India).[^o9] According to historians of mathematics [Norbert Schappacher](wikipedia://en/64764918) and [Roshdi Rashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshdi_Rashed), 3rd century Greek mathematician [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) didn't formulate the problem of congruent numbers.[^o8][^o10] In the 13th century, Fibonacci identified 7 as a congruent number and noted that 1 is not. The first accepted proof of the non-congruence of 1 was later given by [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966), who also proved that 2 and 3 are not congruent numbers.[^o11][^o12]

## Congruent number problem {#Congruent_number_problem}

The question of determining whether a given rational number is a congruent number is called the **congruent number problem**. , this problem has not been brought to a successful resolution. [Tunnell's theorem](wikipedia://en/6144888) provides an easily testable criterion for determining whether a number is congruent; but his result relies on the [Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture](wikipedia://en/435161), which is still unproven.

[Fermat's right triangle theorem](wikipedia://en/29559494), named after [Pierre de Fermat](wikipedia://en/7576966), states that no [square number](wikipedia://en/213919) can be a congruent number. However, in the form that every [congruum](wikipedia://en/3504161) (the difference between consecutive elements in an [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) of three squares) is non-square, it was already known (without proof) to [Fibonacci](wikipedia://en/17949).[^o15] Every congruum is a congruent number, and every congruent number is a product of a congruum and the square of a rational number.[^o16] However, determining whether a number is a congruum is much easier than determining whether it is congruent, because there is a parameterized formula for congrua for which only finitely many parameter values need to be tested.[^o17]

:::

## Solutions {#Solutions}

*n* is a congruent number [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the system

$x^2 - n y^2 = u^2$, $x^2 + n y^2 = v^2$

has a solution where $x, y, u$, and $v$ are integers.[^o19]

Given a solution, the three numbers $u^2$, $x^2$, and $v^2$ will be in an [arithmetic progression](wikipedia://en/168389) with common difference $n y^2$.

Furthermore, if there is one solution (where the right-hand sides are squares), then there are infinitely many: given any solution $(x, y)$, another solution $(x', y')$ can be computed from[^o20]

$$x' = (x u)^2 + n (y v)^2,$$

$$y' = 2 x y u v.$$

For example, with $n = 6$, the equations are:

$$x^2 - 6 y^2 = u^2,$$

$$x^2 + 6 y^2 = v^2.$$

One solution is $x = 5, y = 2$ (so that $u = 1, v = 7$). Another solution is

$$x' = (5 \cdot 1)^2 + 6 (2 \cdot 7)^2 = 1201,$$

$$y' = 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 7 = 140.$$

With this new $x'$ and $y'$, the new right-hand sides are still both squares:

$$u'^2 = 1201^2 - 6 \cdot 140^2 = 1324801 = 1151^2,$$

$$v'^2 = 1201^2 + 6 \cdot 140^2 = 1560001 = 1249^2.$$

Using $x'=1201, y'=140, u', v'$ as above gives

$$u''=1,727,438,169,601$$

$$v''=2,405,943,600,001$$

Given $x, y, u$, and $v$, one can obtain $a, b$, and $c$ such that

$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, and $\frac{ab}{2} = n$

from

$$a = \frac{v - u}{y}, \quad b = \frac{v + u}{y}, \quad c = \frac{2x}{y}.$$

Then $a, b$ and $c$ are the legs and [hypotenuse](wikipedia://en/251366) of a right triangle with area $n$.

The above values $(x, y, u, v) = (5, 2, 1, 7)$ produce $(a, b, c) = (3, 4, 5)$. The values $(1201, 140, 1151, 1249)$ give $(a, b, c) = (7/10, 120/7, 1201/70)$. Both of these right triangles have area $n = 6$.

## Relation to elliptic curves {#Relation_to_elliptic_curves}

According to [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247)'s compilation of historical texts, a tenth-century Arabic manuscript by [Mohammed Ben Alhocain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Ben_Alhocain) states that the core goal of the theory of rational right triangles is to identify a square that, when increased or decreased by a given number, results in another square. In modern terminology, this task translates to locating a rational point of infinite order on the elliptic curve $my^2 = x^3 - x$.[^o22]

The question of whether a given number is congruent turns out to be equivalent to the condition that a certain [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) has positive [rank](wikipedia://en/244541).[^o4] An alternative approach to the idea is presented below (as can essentially also be found in the introduction to Tunnell's paper).

Fix nonzero $n$. Suppose $a$, $b$, $c$ are numbers (not necessarily positive or rational) which satisfy the following two equations:

$$\begin{aligned} a^2 + b^2 &= c^2, \\ \tfrac{1}{2}ab &= n. \end{aligned}$$

Then set $x = n(a + c)/b$ and $y = 2n^{2}(a + c)/b^{2}$. A calculation shows

$$y^2 = x^3 - n^2 x$$

and $y$ is not 0 (if $y = 0$ then $a = -c$, so $b = 0$, but $(\tfrac{1}{2})ab = n$ is nonzero, a contradiction).

[Conversely](wikipedia://en/155941), if $x$ and $y$ are numbers which satisfy the above equation and $y$ is not 0, set $a = (x^{2} - n^{2})/y$, $b = 2nx/y$, and $c = (x^{2} + n^{2})/y$. A calculation shows these three numbers satisfy the two equations for $a$, $b$, and $c$ above.

These two correspondences between ($a$,$b$,$c$) and ($x$,$y$) are inverses of each other, so we have a [one-to-one correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence) between any solution of the two equations in $a$, $b$, and $c$ and any solution of the equation in $x$ and $y$ with $y$ nonzero. In particular, from the formulas in the two correspondences, for rational $n$ we see that $a$, $b$, and $c$ are rational if and only if the corresponding $x$ and $y$ are rational, and vice versa. (We also have that $a$, $b$, and $c$ are all positive if and only if $x$ and $y$ are all positive; from the equation $y^{2} = x^{3} - xn^{2} = x(x^{2} - n^{2})$ we see that if $x$ and $y$ are positive then $x^{2} - n^{2}$ must be positive, so the formula for $a$ above is positive.)

Thus a positive rational number $n$ is congruent if and only if the equation $y^{2} = x^{3} - n^{2}x$ has a [rational point](wikipedia://en/2960200) with $y$ not equal to 0. It can be shown (as an application of [Dirichlet's theorem](wikipedia://en/101453) on [primes](wikipedia://en/23666) in arithmetic progression) that the only torsion points on this elliptic curve are those with $y$ equal to 0, hence the existence of a rational point with $y$ nonzero is equivalent to saying the elliptic curve has positive rank.

Another approach to solving is to start with integer value of *n* denoted as *N* and solve

$$N^2 = ed^2 + e^2$$

where

$$\begin{aligned} c &= n^2/e + e\\ a &= 2n\\ b &= n^2/e - e \end{aligned}$$

## Current progress {#Current_progress}

For example, it is known that for a prime number $p$, the following holds:[^o24]

- if $p \equiv 3 (\bmod 8)$, then $p$ is not a congruent number, but 2$p$ is a congruent number.
- if $p \equiv 5\pmod{8}$, then $p$ is a congruent number.
- if $p \equiv 7\pmod{8}$, then $p$ and 2$p$ are congruent numbers.

It is also known that in each of the congruence classes $5, 6, 7\pmod{8}$, for any given $k$ there are infinitely many square-free congruent numbers with $k$ odd prime factors.[^o25]
