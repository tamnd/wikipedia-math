# Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique"}

***Cours d'analyse de l'École polytechnique*** is an 1882 book on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) by [Camille Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931). The three volumes (tomes) address [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416), [integral calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_calculus), and [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). The book is remembered for introducing [Jordan content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_content) of sets in a plane.

:::

Tome II was published in 1883, and tome III in 1897. A second edition was started in 1893 and completed in 1896.

### Notable developments {#Notable_developments}

Beyond the service as a [textbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textbook) for students at [École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique), Jordan's book advanced mathematical science as follows: Jordan enunciated and proved a topological property of a simple, closed, non-self-intersecting [plane curve](wikipedia://en/971691): the [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444). In the question of which real-valued [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) have an [integral](wikipedia://en/15532), researchers sought the appropriate refinement: "Historically it was the introduction of the [concept of measurability](wikipedia://en/44055) in the work of Peano and Jordan that was to suggest the manner of insuring the requisite refinement."[^o5] See [Jordan content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_content). In the study of [convergence of Fourier series](wikipedia://en/927236), Jordan extended a criterion first stated by [Johann Dirichlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Dirichlet): the [Dirichlet–Jordan test](wikipedia://en/1461290).

## Mentions {#Mentions}

"In the 1880s and 1890s the most significant and influential theory of the integral was Camille Jordan's."[^o7]

\[The *Cours*\] of Jordan occupies amongst \[Treatises on Analysis\] a pre-eminent place, for aesthetic reasons on one hand, but also because, if it constitutes an admirable setting out of the results of classical analysis, it announces in many ways modern analysis and prepares the way for it.[^o8]
