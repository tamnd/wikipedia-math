# Supersingular prime (algebraic number theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Supersingular prime"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), a **supersingular prime** for a given [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) is a [prime number](wikipedia://en/23666) with a certain relationship to that curve. If the curve $E$ is defined over the [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024), then a prime $p$ is **supersingular for *E*** if the [reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction#G) of $E$ modulo $p$ is a [supersingular elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/3028216) over the [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) $\mathbb{F}_p$.[^n1]

:::

Equivalently, for a prime $p > 3$ of [good reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_reduction) for $E$, the prime is supersingular for $E$ if and only if the [trace of the Frobenius endomorphism](wikipedia://en/1108758#Hasse–Weil_Bound) $a_p = p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is zero, that is, $\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1$.[^n1] This condition means that the reduction of $E$ modulo $p$ has the maximum possible [endomorphism ring](wikipedia://en/59623)—an order in a [quaternion algebra](wikipedia://en/2776890)—rather than an order in an [imaginary quadratic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_quadratic_field).[^n2]

## Distribution {#Distribution}

### Complex multiplication case {#Complex_multiplication_case}

When $E$ has [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) (CM) by an order in an imaginary quadratic field $K$, the distribution of supersingular primes is well understood. A classical result of [Deuring](wikipedia://en/5638258) (1941) implies that a prime $p$ of good reduction is supersingular for $E$ if and only if $p$ is inert or ramified in $K$.[^n2] By the [Chebotarev density theorem](wikipedia://en/535349), these primes constitute exactly half of all primes, so the set of supersingular primes for a CM curve has [natural density](wikipedia://en/1819983) $1/2$.[^n3]

### Non-CM case {#Non-CM_case}

When $E$ does not have complex multiplication, the situation is more subtle. In 1968, [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) proved that the set of supersingular primes has [asymptotic density](wikipedia://en/1819983) zero by applying the [Chebotarev density theorem](wikipedia://en/535349) to the number fields generated by coordinates of the [torsion points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_point) of $E$.[^n3][^n4] Serre later obtained the unconditional upper bound

$$\pi_{E,0}(x) \ll \frac{x}{\left(\log x\right)^{3/2 - \varepsilon}}$$

for any $\varepsilon > 0$, where $\pi_{E,0}(x)$ denotes the number of supersingular primes up to $x$, and showed that under the [Generalized Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_Riemann_Hypothesis) (GRH) one could achieve the bound $\pi_{E,0}(x) \ll x^{3/4}$.[^n5][^n4] The unconditional exponent was subsequently improved by Wan (1990), who showed

$$\pi_{E,0}(x) \ll \frac{x}{\left(\log x\right)^{2 - \varepsilon}}$$

by incorporating sieve-theoretic techniques.[^n6]

Despite this rarity, [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999) proved in 1987 that every elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ has infinitely many supersingular primes.[^n7] His proof uses the theory of [complex multiplication](wikipedia://en/537183) and [Deuring's lifting lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deuring's_lifting_lemma): given any finite set $S$ of primes, one can find a negative [fundamental discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_discriminant) $-D$ such that the [Hilbert class polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_class_polynomial) $P_D(X)$ evaluated at the [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) $j(E)$ has a prime factor outside $S$, and this prime is necessarily supersingular for $E$.[^n7] Elkies later extended this result to elliptic curves defined over any [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) with at least one [real embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_embedding).[^n8]

::: {#o19 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Lang–Trotter conjecture"}

### Lang–Trotter conjecture {#Lang–Trotter_conjecture}

(Lang Trotter 1976) conjectured that the number of supersingular primes less than a bound $X$ satisfies

$$\pi_{E,0}(X) \sim C_E \frac{\sqrt{X}}{\log X}$$

as $X \to \infty$, where $C_E > 0$ is an explicit constant depending on $E$.[^n9] This prediction arises from a probabilistic heuristic: by the [Hasse bound](wikipedia://en/1108758), $|a_p| \leq 2\sqrt{p}$, so the "probability" that $a_p = 0$ for a random prime $p$ is roughly $1/\sqrt{p}$, and summing this over primes up to $X$ gives an expected count on the order of $\sqrt{X}/\log X$.[^n9] , this conjecture remains open.[^n10]

:::

::: {#o25 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Consider the [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/669552) $E : y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20$, which is the modular curve $X_0(11)$ with [j-invariant](wikipedia://en/451988) $j = -2^{12}/11$. The supersingular primes for this curve begin: 2, 19, 29, 199, 569, 809, 1289, 1439, 2539, 3319, ... .[^n4] These are exactly the primes $p$ for which the coefficient of $q^p$ in $\eta(\tau)^2 \eta(11\tau)^2$ vanishes modulo $p$, where $\eta$ is the [Dedekind eta function](wikipedia://en/447020).

:::

::: {#o28 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Generalization"}

## Generalization {#Generalization}

More generally, if $K$ is any [global field](wikipedia://en/288358)—that is, a [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of $\mathbb{Q}$ or of $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$—and $A$ is an [abelian variety](wikipedia://en/247152) defined over $K$, then a **supersingular prime $\mathfrak{p}$ for *A*** is a [finite place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_place) of $K$ such that the reduction of $A$ modulo $\mathfrak{p}$ is a [supersingular abelian variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersingular_abelian_variety).[^n1] It is conjectured that every abelian variety over a number field has infinitely many supersingular primes, but this is known only in special cases.[^n8]

:::
