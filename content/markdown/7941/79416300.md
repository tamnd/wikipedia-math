# Infinite-dimensional Chern–Simons theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Infinite-dimensional Chern–Simons theory"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **infinite-dimensional Chern–Simons theory** (not to be confused with [∞-Chern–Simons theory](wikipedia://en/79416318)) is a generalization of [Chern–Simons theory](wikipedia://en/391908) to manifolds with infinite dimensions. These are not modeled with finite-dimensional [Euclidean spaces](wikipedia://en/9697), but infinite-dimensional [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752), for example [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/20598932), [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989) and [Fréchet spaces](wikipedia://en/233801), which lead to [Hilbert](wikipedia://en/2504412), [Banach](wikipedia://en/3989) and [Fréchet manifolds](wikipedia://en/3873038) respectively. [Principal bundles](wikipedia://en/310950), which in finite-dimensional Chern–Simons theory are considered with ([compact](wikipedia://en/6042)) [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) as gauge groups, are then fittingly considered with Hilbert Lie, Banach Lie and Fréchet Lie groups as gauge groups respectively, which also makes their total spaces into a Hilbert, Banach and Fréchet manifold respectively. These are called Hilbert, Banach and Fréchet principal bundles respectively. The theory is named after Shiing-Shen Chern and [James Simons](wikipedia://en/2430844), who first described [Chern–Simons forms](wikipedia://en/294383) in 1974,[^o2] although the generalization was not developed by them.

:::
