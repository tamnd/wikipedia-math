# Power, root-power, and field quantities

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power, root-power, and field quantities"}

A **power quantity** is a power or a quantity directly proportional to [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_%28physics%29), e.g., [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density), [acoustic intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_intensity), and [luminous intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminous_intensity).[^o2] Energy quantities may also be labelled as power quantities in this context.[^o3]

:::

A **root-power quantity** is a quantity such as [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage), [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current), [sound pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_pressure), [electric field strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_field_strength), [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed), or [charge density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_density), the square of which, in linear systems, is proportional to power.[^o4] The term *root-power quantity* refers to the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) that relates these quantities to power. The term was introduced in ISO 80000-1; it replaces and deprecates the term "**field quantity**".

## Implications {#Implications}

It is essential to know which category a measurement belongs to when using [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel) (dB) for comparing the [levels](wikipedia://en/39999171) of such quantities. A change of one bel in the level corresponds to a 10× change in *power*, so when comparing power quantities *x* and *y*, the difference is defined to be 10×log<sub>10</sub>(*y*/*x*) decibel. With root-power quantities, however the difference is defined as 20×log<sub>10</sub>(*y*/*x*) dB.[^o4]

In the analysis of signals and systems using sinusoids, field quantities and root-power quantities may be [complex](wikipedia://en/5826)-valued,[^o6][^o7][^o8] as in the [propagation constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propagation_constant).

## "Root-power quantity" vs. "field quantity" {#"Root-power_quantity"_vs._"field_quantity"}

In justifying the deprecation of the term "field quantity" and instead using "root-power quantity" in the context of levels, ISO 80000 draws attention to the conflicting use of the former term to mean a quantity that depends on the position,[^o10] which in physics is called a *[field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29)*. Such a field is often called a *field quantity* in the literature, but is called a *field* here for clarity. Several types of field (such as the [electromagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field)) meet the definition of a root-power quantity, whereas others (such as the [Poynting vector](wikipedia://en/49718) and [temperature](wikipedia://en/20647050)) do not. Conversely, not every root-power quantity is a field (such as the voltage on a [loudspeaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudspeaker)).
