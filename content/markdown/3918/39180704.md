# Lev M. Bregman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lev M. Bregman"}

**Lev M. Bregman** (Лев Брэгман; 1941–2023) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) mathematician, most known for the [Bregman divergence](wikipedia://en/4491248) named after him.

:::

Bregman received his M. Sc. in mathematics in 1963 at [Leningrad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leningrad_University) and his Ph.D. in mathematics in 1966 at the same institution, under the direction of his advisor Prof. [J. V. Romanovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._V._Romanovsky), for his thesis about relaxation methods for finding a common point of convex sets, which led to one of his most well-known publications.[^o3]

[Bregman's Theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bregman's_Theorem), proving a 1963 conjecture of [Henryk Minc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Minc), gives an upper bound on the permanent of a [0-1 matrix](wikipedia://en/4109196).

Bregman was employed at the [Institute for Industrial Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Industrial_Mathematics), Beer-Sheva, Israel, after having spent one year at [Ben-Gurion University of the Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev), Beer-Sheva. Formerly, from 1966 through 1991, he was senior researcher at the Leningrad University.[^o4]

Bregman is author of several textbooks and dozens of publications in international journals.
