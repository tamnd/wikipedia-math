# Keybase

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Keybase"}

**Keybase** is a [key directory](wikipedia://en/616119) that maps [social media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media) identities to encryption keys (including, but not limited to [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080) keys) in a publicly auditable manner.[^o4] Additionally it offers an [end-to-end encrypted](wikipedia://en/1065362) chat and [cloud storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_storage) system,[^o5][^o6] called Keybase Chat and the Keybase Filesystem respectively. Files placed in the public portion of the filesystem are served from a public endpoint,[^o8] as well as locally from a filesystem [union-mounted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_mount) by the Keybase client.[^o7]

:::

Keybase supports publicly connecting [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter), [GitHub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitHub), [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit), and [Hacker News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker_News) identities, including websites and domains under one's control, to encryption keys. It also supports [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265), [Zcash](wikipedia://en/51849458), [Stellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_%28payment_network%29), and QRL wallet addresses.[^o8][^o5][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] Keybase has supported [Coinbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coinbase) identities since initial public release, but ceased to do so on March 17, 2017, when Coinbase terminated public payment pages.[^o13] In general, Keybase allows any service with public identities to integrate with Keybase.[^o8][^o14]

On May 7, 2020, Keybase announced it had been acquired by [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_Video_Communications),[^o15] as part of Zoom's "plan to further strengthen the security of \[its\] video communications platform".[^o16] Since the acquisition, development has stopped, but the service has been kept functional.

::: {#o18 .proof cue=named-heading name="Identity proofs"}

## Identity proofs {#Identity_proofs}

Keybase allows users to *prove* a link between certain online identities (such as a [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter) or [Reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit) account) and their encryption keys. Instead of using a system such as [OAuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAuth), identities are proven by posting a signed statement as the account a user wishes to prove ownership of. This makes identity proofs publicly verifiable – instead of having to trust that the service is being truthful, a user can find and check the relevant proof statements themselves, and the Keybase client does this automatically.

:::

## App {#App}

In addition to the web interface, Keybase offers a client application for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows),[^o20][^o21] [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS),[^o20][^o21] [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_%28operating_system%29),[^o22][^o21] [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS),[^o21] and most desktop [Linux distributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_distribution),[^o21] written in [Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_%28programming_language%29) with an [Electron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_%28software_framework%29) front end. The app offers additional features to the website, such as the end-to-end encrypted chat, teams feature, and the ability to add files to and access private files in their personal and team Keybase Filesystem storage. Each device running the client app is authorized by a [signature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_%28cryptography%29) made either by another device or the user's [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080) key. Each device also has a device-specific key to enable the user to establish their identity using any of their devices.[^o23]

## Chat {#Chat}

Keybase Chat is an end-to-end encrypted chat built in to Keybase launched in February 2017. A distinguishing feature of Keybase Chat is that it allows Keybase users to send messages to someone using their online aliases (for example a [reddit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reddit) account), even if they haven't signed up to Keybase yet.[^o5]

If the recipient (the online alias owner) has an account on Keybase, they will seamlessly receive the message. If the recipient doesn't have a Keybase account, and later signs up and proves the link between the online account and their devices, the sender's device will [rekey](wikipedia://en/55500399) the message for the recipient based on the public proof they posted, allowing them to read the message. Since the Keybase app checks the proof, it avoids [trust on first use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_on_first_use).[^o25]

## Keybase Filesystem (KBFS) {#Keybase_Filesystem_(KBFS)}

*Image: Meekwire\_Keybase\_-\_James\_Gordon\_Meek.png.* Keybase screenshot showing a user's keys

Keybase allows users to store up to 250 GB[^o27] of files in a cloud storage called the Keybase Filesystem for free. There are no storage upgrades available, but paid plans allowing for more data are planned.[^o28] The filesystem is divided into three parts: public files, private files, and team files. On [Unix-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unix-like) machines, the filesystem is [mounted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_%28computing%29) to `/keybase`, and on [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows) systems it is usually mounted to the K drive.[^o29] Currently, mobile versions of the Keybase client can only download files from kbfs, and can not mount it. However, they do support operations such as rekeying files as necessary. In October 2017 Keybase brought out end-to-end encrypted [Git](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Git) repositories.[^o30]

### Public files {#Public_files}

Public files are stored in `/public/''username''`, and are publicly visible. All files in the public filesystem are automatically [signed](wikipedia://en/59644) by the client.[^o7] Only the user who the folder is named after can edit its contents, however, a folder may be named after a comma-separated list of users (e.g. a folder `/public/''foo,bar,three''` would be editable by the users *foo*, *bar*, and *three*).[^o7]

Public files can be accessed by any user. Single user folders are displayed at and are also accessible by opening the directory in the mounted version of the filesystem. Multi user folders (such as `/public/''foo,bar,three''`) are only accessible through the mounted version of the system.

### Private files {#Private_files}

Private files are stored in `/private/''username''`, and are only visible to *username*. Private folders, like public folders, can be named after more than one user (e.g. a folder `/private/''foo,bar,three''` would be readable and editable by the users *foo*, *bar*, and *three*). Private files can also be read only for users after "#" (e.g. a folder `/private/''writer1,writer2,#reader1,reader2''` would be readable and editable by the users *writer1* and *writer2* but only readable for *reader1* and *reader2*).[^o7] Unlike public files, all private files are both encrypted and signed before being uploaded, making them end-to-end encrypted.[^o6]

### Team files {#Team_files}

Team files are stored in `/team/''teamname''`, and are publicly visible to team members. All files in the team filesystem are automatically [encrypted and signed](wikipedia://en/59644) by the client.[^o7] Only users who are marked as writers can edit its contents, however, any readers can access the files stored there.[^o34]

## Teams {#Teams}

In September 2017, Keybase launched Keybase Teams.[^o36] A team is described as "...a *named* group of people."[^o37] Each team has a private folder in the Keybase filesystem, and a number of chat channels (similar to [Slack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slack_%28software%29)). Teams can also be divided into "subteams" by placing a [`.`](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_stop) in the team name. For example, `wikipedia.projects` would be a subteam of `wikipedia`, while `wikipedia.projects.foobar` would be a subteam of `wikipedia.projects` (and therefore, also of `wikipedia`).

### Team administration {#Team_administration}

Teams are largely administered by adding signatures to a chain. Each signature can add, remove, or change the membership of a user in a team, as well as when changes are made to subteams.

Each chain starts with a signature made by the team owner, with subsequent actions signed on by team admins or users.[^o39] This ensures that every action is made by an authorized user, and that actions can be verified by anyone in possession of the public key used.
