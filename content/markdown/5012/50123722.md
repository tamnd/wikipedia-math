# Adriaan Metius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adriaan Metius"}

**Adriaan Adriaanszoon**, called **Adriaan Metius** (9 December 1571 – 6 September 1635), was a Dutch [geometer](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), born in [Alkmaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkmaar). The name "Metius" comes from the Dutch word meten ("measuring"), and therefore means something like "measurer" or "surveyor".

:::

## Father and brother {#Father_and_brother}

Adriaan Metius was born in [Alkmaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkmaar), [North Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Holland). His father, [Adriaan Anthonisz](wikipedia://en/50124015), was a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [land-surveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying), [cartographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartographer), and [military engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineer) who from 1582 served also as [burgomaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgomaster) of Alkmaar.

Metius's brother, [Jacob Metius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Metius), worked as an instrument-maker and a specialist in grinding [lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lens_%28optics%29). Also born in Alkmaar, Jacob died between 1624 and 1631. Little is known of him besides the fact that, in October 1608, the [States General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates-General_of_the_Netherlands) discussed his [patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent) application for an [optical telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_telescope) of his own invention described as a device for "seeing faraway things as though nearby", consisting of a convex and concave lens in a tube, and the combination magnified three or four times.[^o4]

## Education {#Education}

Adriaan Metius attended a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) school in Alkmaar and studied [philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) in 1589 at the recently founded [University of Franeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Franeker). He continued his studies at the [University of Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leiden) in 1594, where he studied under [Rudolph Snellius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_Snellius). He worked for a brief time under [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe) on the island of [Hven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hven), where Brahe had built two observatories, and subsequently worked at [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) and [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena), where he gave lectures in 1595. Subsequently, he returned to Alkmaar and assisted his father for a time as a military engineer inspecting [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications), and also worked as a teacher of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Franeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franeker) in [Frisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisia), his teaching especially geared towards the training of surveyors.

At the University of Franeker, he was appointed *professor extraordinarius* in 1598, and served from 1600 to 1635 as *professor ordinarius* of mathematics, [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation), surveying, military engineering, and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy). He was permitted to teach in the vernacular instead of Latin. He served as rector of the university in 1603 and 1632.

With his father and brother he established an instrument making business which specialised in optical instruments. The family business seems to have manufactured the precision Jacob's staffs used by [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe) for his star sightings.

He died in Franeker.

## Accomplishments {#Accomplishments}

*Image: Metius De genuino usu.jpg.* Adriaen Metius: *De genuino usu utriusqve globi tractatus. Adjecta est nova Sciatericorum, & artis Navigandi ratio novis Instrumentis, & inventionibus illustrata*. Franeker, 1624.

Though he scoffed at [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology), Metius is said to have spent a lot of time pursuing [alchemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), especially the [philosophers' stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophers'_stone).

Metius published treatises on the [astrolabe](wikipedia://en/73664) and on [surveying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveying). His works include *Arithmeticæ et geometriæ practica* (1611), *Institutiones Astronomicae Geographicae*, and *Arithmeticæ libri duo: et geometriæ libri VI* (1640). Metius also manufactured astronomical instruments and developed a special form of [Jacob's staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob's_staff).

In 1585, his father had estimated the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, later called [pi](wikipedia://en/23601), to be approximately $\tfrac{355}{113}$.[^o7] Metius later published his father's results, and the value $\tfrac{355}{113}$ is traditionally referred to as [Metius's number](wikipedia://en/7315901).[^o8]

The [lunar crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_crater) [Metius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metius_%28crater%29) is named after him.

In [Vermeer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Vermeer) painting *[The Astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Astronomer_%28Vermeer%29)* (1668), the book lying on the table has been identified as a 1621 second edition of Metius's *Institutiones Astronomicae Geographicae*. It is opened to Book III, where "inspiration from God" is recommended for astronomical research along with knowledge of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and the aid of mechanical instruments.[^o9]
