# Israel Gohberg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Israel Gohberg"}

**Israel Gohberg** (ישראל גוכברג; Изра́иль Цу́дикович Го́хберг; 23 August 1928 – 12 October 2009)[^o3] was a [Bessarabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia)-born [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), most known for his work in [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), in particular [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) and [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gohberg was born in [Tarutino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabske) to parents Tsudik and Haya Gohberg. His father owned a small typography shop and his mother was a midwife. The young Gohberg studied in a Hebrew school in Taurtyne and then a Romanian school in [Orhei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orhei), where he was influenced by the tutelage of Modest Shumbarsky, a student of the renowned topologist [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk).

He studied at the Kyrgyz Pedagogical Institute in [Bishkek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishkek) and at [Moldova State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_State_University) in [Chișinău](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chișinău), completed his doctorate at [Leningrad State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) on a thesis advised by [Mark Krein](wikipedia://en/1190470) (1954), and attended [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) for his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) degree.

Gohberg joined the faculty at the Teacher's college in [Soroca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroca) and the Teachers college in [Bălți](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bălți) before returning to Chișinău where he was elected into the Academy of Sciences and also being appointed head of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) at Moldova State University (1964–73). After moving to [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel), he joined [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) (1974) and was at the [Weizmann Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute) at [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot). Since then he also had positions at [Vrije Universiteit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit) in [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam) (1983), as well as at the [University of Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calgary) and the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park).

He founded the *Integral equations and operator theory* journal (1983).

Gohberg was the visionary and driving force of the [International Workshop on Operator Theory and its Applications](wikipedia://en/47771159), or [IWOTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IWOTA), starting with its first meeting on August 1, 1981.[^o6] He became a lifetime president of the [IWOTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IWOTA) Steering Committee and a founder of the [*Springer*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media) / [*Birkhäuser Verlag*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkhäuser) book series *Operator Theory: Advances and Applications (OTAA)*.[^o7]

Gohberg was awarded the [Humboldt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Prize) in 1992. He received honorary doctorates from the [Darmstadt University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darmstadt_University_of_Technology) in 1997; from the [Vienna University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_University_of_Technology) in 2001; from [West University of Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_University_of_Timișoara) in 2002; from [Moldova State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_State_University), Chișinău, Moldova in 2002; from [Alecu Russo State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alecu_Russo_State_University_of_Bălți), Bălți, Moldova in 2002; and from [Technion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technion_–_Israel_Institute_of_Technology), June 2008. He also was awarded the M.G. Krein Prize of the [Ukrainian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences_of_Ukraine) in 2008, and was elected [SIAM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAM_Fellow) in 2009.

He died in [Ra'anana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra'anana) in 2009.

## Publications {#Publications}

Gohberg has authored near five hundred articles in his field. Books, a selection:

- \1986. *Invariant subspaces of matrices with applications*. With [Peter Lancaster](wikipedia://en/60928034), and Leiba Rodman. . Vol. 51. SIAM, 1986.
- \2003. *Basic classes of linear operators.* With [Rien Kaashoek](wikipedia://en/40502851) and Seymour Goldberg. Springer, 2003.
- \2005. *Convolution equations and projection methods for their solution. Izrail*. With Aronovich Felʹdman. Vol. 41. AMS Bookstore.
- \2009. *Matrix polynomials*. With Peter Lancaster, and Leiba Rodman. Vol. 58. SIAM, 2009.

Articles, a selection:

- Gohberg, Israel C., and Kreĭn, Mark Grigor'evič. Introduction to the theory of linear nonselfadjoint operators in Hilbert space. Vol. 18. American Mathematical Soc., 1969.
- Gohberg, I; Kaashoek, M. A. (1984). "Time varying linear systems with boundary conditions and integral operators. I. The transfer operator and its properties". *Integral Equations and Operator Theory*. 7 (3). p. 325. doi:10.1007/BF01208381
- Branges, Louis de. (1994). "Book Review: Classes of linear operators, Volume 2". *Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society*. 31 (2). p. 236–244. doi:10.1090/S0273-0979-1994-00526-9
