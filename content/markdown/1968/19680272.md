# Lie product formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lie product formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Lie product formula**, named for [Sophus Lie](wikipedia://en/54010) (1875), but also widely called the **Trotter product formula**,[^o2] named after [Hale Trotter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hale_Trotter), states that for arbitrary *m* × *m* [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) *A* and *B*,[^o3]

$$e^{A+B} = \lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} (e^{A/n}e^{B/n})^n,$$

where *e*<sup>*A*</sup> denotes the [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) of *A*. The **Lie–Trotter product formula**[^o4] and the **Trotter–Kato theorem**[^o5] extend this to certain unbounded linear operators *A* and *B*.[^o6]

:::

This formula is an analogue of the classical exponential law

$$e^{x+y} = e^x e^y$$

which holds for all real or complex numbers *x* and *y*. If *x* and *y* are replaced with matrices *A* and *B*, and the [exponential](wikipedia://en/9678) replaced with a [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731), it is usually necessary for *A* and *B* to commute for the law to still hold. However, the Lie product formula holds for all matrices *A* and *B*, even ones which do not commute.

The Lie product formula is conceptually related to the [Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula](wikipedia://en/285422), in that both are replacements, in the context of noncommuting operators, for the classical exponential law.

The formula has applications, for example, in the [path integral formulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_integral_formulation) of quantum mechanics. It allows one to separate the [Schrödinger evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schrödinger_evolution) [operator (*propagator*)](wikipedia://en/698759) into alternating increments of kinetic and potential operators (the Suzuki–Trotter decomposition, after Trotter and [Masuo Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masuo_Suzuki)). The same idea is used in the construction of [splitting methods](wikipedia://en/2063558) for the numerical solution of [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). Moreover, the Lie product theorem is sufficient to prove the [Feynman–Kac formula](wikipedia://en/630017).[^o7]

The Trotter–Kato theorem can be used for approximation of linear [C<sub>0</sub>-semigroups](wikipedia://en/1644938).[^o8]

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

By the [Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula](wikipedia://en/285422), $(e^{A/n}e^{B/n})^n = e^{A + B + \frac{1}{2n} [A,B] + \cdots} \to e^{A+B}$ as $n \to +\infty$.

:::
