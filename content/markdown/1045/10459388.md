# Paul Malliavin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Malliavin"}

**Paul Malliavin** (; September 10, 1925 – June 3, 2010) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made important contributions to [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147) and [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis). He is known for the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), an infinite-dimensional calculus for functionals on the [Wiener space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_space), and his probabilistic proof of [Hörmander’s theorem](wikipedia://en/13659915). He was [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) at the [Pierre and Marie Curie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_and_Marie_Curie_University) and a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences) from 1979 to 2010.[^o3]

:::

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Malliavin was the son of René Malliavin, also known as *Michel Dacier*, a political writer and journalist, and Madeleine Delavenne, a physician. On 27 April 1965 he married [Marie-Paule Brameret](wikipedia://en/74973605), who was also a mathematician and with whom he published several mathematical papers.[^o5][^o6] They had two children.[^o5]

## Scientific contributions {#Scientific_contributions}

Malliavin's early work was in [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), where he derived important results on the spectral synthesis problem, providing definitive answers to fundamental questions in this field, including a complete characterization of 'band-limited' functions whose Fourier transform has compact support, known as the [Beurling-Malliavin theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beurling-Malliavin_theorem).[^o8]

In [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis), Malliavin is known for his work on the stochastic calculus of variation, now known as the [Malliavin calculus](wikipedia://en/837875), a mathematical theory which has found many applications in [Monte Carlo simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Carlo_simulation) and [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680).

As stated by [Stroock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_W._Stroock) and [Yor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Yor): "Like Norbert Wiener, Paul Malliavin came to probability theory from harmonic analysis, and, like Wiener, his analytic origins were apparent in everything he did there."[^o9] Malliavin introduced a differential operator on [Wiener space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_space), now called the [Malliavin derivative](wikipedia://en/6814674), and derived an [integration by parts](wikipedia://en/147252) formula for Wiener functionals. Using this integration by parts formula, Malliavin initiated a probabilistic approach to [Hörmander's theorem](wikipedia://en/13659915) for hypo-elliptic operators and gave a condition for the existence of smooth densities for Wiener functionals in terms of their [Malliavin covariance matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malliavin_covariance_matrix).
