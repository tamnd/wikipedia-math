# Oxford University Invariant Society

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Oxford University Invariant Society"}

**The Oxford University Invariant Society**, or 'The Invariants', is a university society open to members of the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), dedicated to promotion of interest in mathematics.[^o3] The society regularly hosts talks from professional mathematicians on topics both technical and more popular, from the [mathematics of juggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_of_juggling) to the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).[^o4] Many prominent British mathematicians were members of the society during their time at Oxford.[^o5]

:::

## History {#History}

The Society was founded in 1936 by [J. H. C. Whitehead](wikipedia://en/385179) together with two of his students at [Balliol College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balliol_College), [Graham Higman](wikipedia://en/2600152) and Jack de Wet.[^o8] The name of the society was chosen at random by Higman from the titles of the books on Whitehead's shelf;[^o9] in this case, [Oswald Veblen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Veblen)'s *Invariants of Quadratic Differential Forms*. The opening lecture was given by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) in [Hilary Term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Term) 1936, with the title 'Round Numbers'.[^o10][^o11]

Though many members joined the armed forces during the war, meetings continued, including lectures by [Douglas Hartree](wikipedia://en/1252692) and [Max Newman](wikipedia://en/19867), as well as debates - 'Is Mathematics an end in itself?' - and mathematical films.[^o12]

The society has hosted hundreds of prominent mathematicians,[^o13] including lectures by [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999),[^o14] [Sir Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Roger_Penrose),[^o15] and [Simon Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Singh).[^o16]

Since 1961, the Society has published a magazine entitled *The Invariant*.[^o17]
