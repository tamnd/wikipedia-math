# List of uniform polyhedra by vertex figure

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of uniform polyhedra by vertex figure"}

There are many relations among the [uniform polyhedra](wikipedia://en/2537223).[^o2][^o3][^o4] Some are obtained by truncating the vertices of the regular or quasi-regular polyhedron. Others share the same vertices and edges as other polyhedron. The grouping below exhibit some of these relations.

:::

## The vertex figure of a polyhedron {#The_vertex_figure_of_a_polyhedron}

The relations can be made apparent by examining the [vertex figures](wikipedia://en/1005719) obtained by listing the faces adjacent to each vertex (remember that for uniform polyhedra all vertices are the same, that is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)). For example, the cube has vertex figure 4.4.4, which is to say, three adjacent square faces. The possible faces are

- 3 – equilateral triangle
- 4 – square
- 5 – regular pentagon
- 6 – regular hexagon
- 8 – regular octagon
- 10 – regular decagon
- 5/2 – pentagram
- 8/3 – octagram
- 10/3 – decagram

Some faces will appear with reverse orientation which is written here as

- −3 – a triangle with reverse orientation (often written as 3/2)

Others pass through the origin which we write as

- 6\* – hexagon passing through the origin

The [Wythoff symbol](wikipedia://en/3691548) relates the polyhedron to [spherical triangles](wikipedia://en/650405). Wythoff symbols are written p|q r, p q|r, p q r| where the spherical triangle has angles π/p,π/q,π/r, the bar indicates the position of the vertices in relation to the triangle.

*Image: Vertex figures.png.* Example vertex figures

Johnson (2000) classified uniform polyhedra according to the following:

1. Regular (regular polygonal vertex figures): p<sup>q</sup>, Wythoff symbol q|p 2
1. Quasi-regular (rectangular or ditrigonal vertex figures): p.q.p.q 2|p q, or p.q.p.q.p.q, Wythoff symbol 3|p q
1. Versi-regular (orthodiagonal vertex figures), p.q\*.−p.q\*, Wythoff symbol q q|p
1. Truncated regular (isosceles triangular vertex figures): p.p.q, Wythoff symbol q 2|p
1. Versi-quasi-regular (dipteroidal vertex figures), p.q.p.r Wythoff symbol q r|p
1. Quasi-quasi-regular (trapezoidal vertex figures): p\*.q.p\*.−r q.r|p or p.q\*.−p.q\* p q r|
1. Truncated quasi-regular (scalene triangular vertex figures), p.q.r Wythoff symbol p q r|
1. Snub quasi-regular (pentagonal, hexagonal, or octagonal vertex figures), Wythoff symbol p q r|
1. Prisms (truncated hosohedra),
1. Antiprisms and crossed antiprisms (snub dihedra)

The format of each figure follows the same basic pattern

1. image of polyhedron
1. name of polyhedron
1. alternate names (in brackets)
1. Wythoff symbol

::: {#o6 .entry cue=list-entry name="polyhedra models"}

1. Numbering systems: W – number used by Wenninger in *[polyhedra models](wikipedia://en/2915428)*, U – uniform indexing, K – Kaleido indexing, C – numbering used in Coxeter *et al.* 'Uniform Polyhedra'.

:::

1. Number of vertices V, edges E, Faces F and number of faces by type.
1. Euler characteristic χ = V − E + F

The vertex figures are on the left, followed by the [Point groups in three dimensions#The seven remaining point groups](wikipedia://en/2694525#The_seven_remaining_point_groups), either tetrahedral T<sub>d</sub>, octahedral O<sub>h</sub> or icosahedral I<sub>h</sub>.

The compound of 5-cubes shares the same set of edges and vertices.
