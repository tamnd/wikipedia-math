# Biquadratic field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Biquadratic field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **biquadratic field** is a [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) $K$ of a particular kind, which is a [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of degree 4 of the [rational number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_number_field) $\mathbb{Q}$ with [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) isomorphic to the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928).

:::

## Structure and subfields {#Structure_and_subfields}

Biquadratic fields are obtained by adjoining two [square roots](wikipedia://en/29208) of rational numbers $a,b$:

$$K = \mathbb{Q} \left( \sqrt{a}, \sqrt{b} \right).$$

Without [loss of generality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_of_generality), we may assume *a* and *b* are non-zero [square-free integers](wikipedia://en/29525).

The [Galois group](wikipedia://en/61316) has three [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) of index 2, corresponding to the three [quadratic subfields](wikipedia://en/454781)

> $$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt a),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt b),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{ab}) \subset K.$$

Biquadratic fields are the simplest examples of [abelian extensions](wikipedia://en/174448) of $\mathbb{Q}$ that are not [cyclic extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_extension). Also biquadratic fields are usually not [monogenic](wikipedia://en/18062676): although there exists a [primitive element](wikipedia://en/448933) which generates the field $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, its [ring of integers](wikipedia://en/98981) $\mathcal{O}_K$ might not possess a single generator over $\mathbb{Z}$.
