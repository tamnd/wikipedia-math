# William G. McCallum

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William G. McCallum"}

**William G. McCallum** (born 1956 in [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney), Australia)[^o3] is a retired University Distinguished Professor of Mathematics at the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona). His professional interests include [arithmetical algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetical_algebraic_geometry) and [mathematics education](wikipedia://en/326471).

:::

## Education and professional work {#Education_and_professional_work}

McCallum received a bachelor's degree from the [University of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_South_Wales) in 1977,[^o5] and his Ph.D. in Mathematics from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1984, under the supervision of [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679).

He was a postdoctoral researcher at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute) in Berkeley before joining the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) in 1987.[^o3] He became University Distinguished Professor from 2006 until his retirement in 2018, and headed the Department of Mathematics from 2009 to 2013.[^o5]

McCallum helped found the Harvard Calculus Consortium with other mathematicians including [Andrew M. Gleason](wikipedia://en/4244871) and [Deborah Hughes Hallett](wikipedia://en/48069592).[^o12] He led the development of the [Common Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Core) standards for mathematics from 2009 until the standards were first released in 2010.[^o7] In 2014 he founded a company, eventually known as Illustrative Mathematics, to develop teaching resources for the mathematics standards that he helped develop.[^o8]

## Selected honors and awards {#Selected_honors_and_awards}

In 2012, McCallum became a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] In the same year he received the inaugural [Mary P. Dolciani Award](wikipedia://en/65434570) for distinguished contributions to mathematical education, administered by the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772),[^o11] and the [AMS Distinguished Public Service Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMS_Distinguished_Public_Service_Award) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), "for his energetic and effective efforts in promoting improvements to mathematics education"[^o12]
