# Agnes Sime Baxter

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Agnes Sime Baxter"}

**Agnes Sime Baxter** (**Hill**) (18 March 1870 – 9 March 1917) was a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians)-born [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). She studied at [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University), receiving her BA in 1891, and her MA in 1892. She received her Ph.D. from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in 1895; her dissertation was "On Abelian integrals", a resume of Neumann's [Abelian integral](wikipedia://en/1138853) with comments and applications."[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Agnes Sime Baxter was born on March 18, 1870, in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Regional_Municipality), [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia). The Baxter family had emigrated to [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) from [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland). Her father, Robert Baxter, was manager of the Halifax Gas Light Company, having managed a Scottish electric light company before moving to Nova Scotia.

Baxter enrolled at [Dalhousie University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalhousie_University) in 1887. Her primary courses of study were [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). Despite the relative lack of female [scholars](wikipedia://en/772062) in these areas, Baxter received her [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) in 1891 and was the first women at the university to gain an honours degree.[^o5] She received multiple awards at graduation, including the Sir William Young Medal for highest standing in mathematics and mathematical physics.[^o5] She completed her [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at Dalhousie in 1892.

From 1892 to 1894, she held a [fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29). On the completion of her thesis, "On [Abelian integrals](wikipedia://en/1138853), a resume of [Neumann](wikipedia://en/915551)'s 'Abelsche Integrele' with comments and applications," she became the second Canadian woman and the fourth [North American](wikipedia://en/21139) woman to receive a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics.[^o6][^o7] Her supervisor [James Edward Oliver](wikipedia://en/72580196)'s mathematical notes were edited by Baxter in 1894 and later published.[^o5]

Agnes Baxter married Dr. [Albert Ross Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ross_Hill) on August 20, 1896. The marriage produced two daughters. Agnes chose not to teach at the institutions where her husband was a professor, although Albert credited her with assisting him in his work.

Agnes Ross Hill died on March 9, 1917, in [Columbia, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri), after protracted illness and was buried in the [Columbia Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Cemetery_%28Columbia,_Missouri%29).[^o6][^o7] On her death, her husband Albert Ross Hill wanted his wife's memory to be preserved and donated \$1,000 to Dalhousie University for the purchase of a collection of books at Dalhousie University. The university also created the Agnes Baxter Reading Room within the Department of Mathematics, Statistics and Computing Sciences.
