# Projective range

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Projective range"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **projective range** is a set of points in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) considered in a unified fashion. A projective range may be a [projective line](wikipedia://en/46379102) or a [conic](wikipedia://en/19008673). A projective range is the [dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_duality) of a [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_%28mathematics%29) of lines on a given point. For instance, a [correlation](wikipedia://en/7984037) interchanges the points of a projective range with the lines of a pencil. A [projectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectivity) is said to act from one range to another, though the two ranges may coincide as sets.

:::

A projective range expresses projective invariance of the relation of [projective harmonic conjugates](wikipedia://en/16094600). Indeed, three points on a projective line determine a fourth by this relation. Application of a projectivity to this quadruple results in four points likewise in the harmonic relation. Such a quadruple of points is termed a **harmonic range**. In 1940 [Julian Coolidge](wikipedia://en/4379392) described this structure and identified its originator:[^o2]

Two fundamental one-dimensional forms such as point ranges, pencils of lines, or of planes are defined as projective, when their members are in one-to-one correspondence, and a harmonic set of one ... corresponds to a harmonic set of the other. ... If two one-dimensional forms are connected by a train of projections and intersections, harmonic elements will correspond to harmonic elements, and they are projective in the sense of [Von Staudt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Staudt).

## Conic ranges {#Conic_ranges}

When a conic is chosen for a projective range, and a particular point *E* on the conic is selected as origin, then *addition of points* may be defined as follows:[^o4]

Let *A* and *B* be in the range (conic) and *AB* the line connecting them. Let *L* be the line through *E* and parallel to *AB*. The "sum of points *A* and *B*", *A* + *B*, is the intersection of *L* with the range.

The [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) and [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052) are instances of a conic and the summation of angles on either can be generated by the method of "sum of points", provided points are associated with [angles](wikipedia://en/1196) on the circle and [hyperbolic angles](wikipedia://en/1139981) on the hyperbola.
