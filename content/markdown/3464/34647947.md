# Charles Cook (academic)

*Image: Charles H. H. Cook (cropped).jpg.* Portrait of Cook

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Cook"}

**Charles Henry Herbert Cook** (30 September 1843 – 21 May 1910) was an English-born, Australian-raised, New Zealand-based mathematician. He was born in [Kentish Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentish_Town), [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), on 30 September 1843, but educated in [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia), where he got a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) and an [LLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LLB) from [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne). He then went to [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), initially to train for the [English Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Bar) but became interested in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

In 1874, a year before being due to be called to the bar, Cook was appointed founding head of mathematics at [Canterbury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Canterbury), [University of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Zealand) (now [Canterbury University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canterbury_University)).[^o2] He joined co-founders [John Macmillan Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Macmillan_Brown) and [Alexander Bickerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bickerton) in [Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christchurch), New Zealand, and initially focused on [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). He was also involved in promoting the establishment of the University's engineering school.[^o3] He is remembered primarily for his teaching; [Nobel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize)–winning physicist [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford) cited him as an influence:[^o5]

Cook was evidently a sound mathematician and an excellent teacher along orthodox lines, with no marked tendency to stray from those lines.[^o6]

He was involved in [secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education), acting as an examiner for the New Zealand [Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_%28New_Zealand%29) and holding a fellowship at the [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) [Christ's College, Christchurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_College,_Christchurch) from 1891 to 1908.[^o2]

Cook was a member of the Royal Commission on Higher Education 1878–1800[^o4][^o7] and a member of the [senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Senate) of the [University of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Zealand).[^o2]

In 1903, Cook appeared in the [vanity press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_press) *[The Cyclopedia of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cyclopedia_of_New_Zealand)*, with a photo and short article.[^o2] He died in [Marton, New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton,_New_Zealand), on 21 May 1910.[^o4]

After his death in 1910 a memorial prize was established, with [Ernest Rutherford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rutherford) among the contributors.[^o9][^o10]
