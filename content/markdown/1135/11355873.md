# Johann Christian Martin Bartels

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Christian Martin Bartels"}

**Johann Christian Martin Bartels** (12 August 1769 – 20 December 1836) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He was the tutor of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) in [Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig) and the educator of [Nikolai Lobachevsky](wikipedia://en/648527) at the [University of Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University) in Russia.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bartels was born in [Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig), in the [Duchy of Brunswick-Lüneburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brunswick-Lüneburg) (now part of [Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Saxony), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany)), the son of [pewterer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pewter) Heinrich Elias Friedrich Bartels and his wife Johanna Christine Margarethe Köhler. In his childhood he showed a great interest in mathematics. In 1783 he was employed as an assistant to the teacher Büttner in the Katherinenschule in [Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig). He became acquainted with [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) there and encouraged his talent and recommended him to the [Duke of Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Wilhelm_Ferdinand,_Duke_of_Brunswick-Luneburg) who awarded Gauss a fellowship to the Collegium Carolinum (now [Technische Universität Braunschweig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Universität_Braunschweig)). A friendship developed between Gauss and Bartels and they corresponded between 1799 and 1823.

From 23 August 1788 he was a visitor at the Collegium Carolinum in Braunsschweig.

On 23 October 1791 Bartels studied mathematics under [Johann Friedrich Pfaff](wikipedia://en/1669324) in [Helmstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmstedt) and [Abraham Gotthelf Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Gotthelf_Kästner) in [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen). In the winter semester of 1793/1794 he studied Experimental Physics, Astronomy, Meteorology and Geology under [Georg Christoph Lichtenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Christoph_Lichtenberg).

In 1800 he worked in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) as [Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of Mathematics in [Reichenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichenau,_Switzerland) ([Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Switzerland) [Graubünden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graubünden)). In 1801 he was active in the cantonal school in [Aarau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarau). He married Anna Magdalena Saluz from [Chur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chur) in 1802. The [University of Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jena) promoted him to the Faculty of Philosophy in 1803.

In 1807 he was invited to join the [University of Kazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_State_University) by the founder [Stepan Jakowlewitsch Rumowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Jakowlewitsch_Rumowski) (1734–1812), and went there in 1808 where he was appointed to the chair of Mathematics. During his twelve years tenure he lectured on the History of Mathematics, Higher Arithmetic, Differential and Integral Calculus, Analytical Geometry and Trigonometry, Spherical Trigonometry, Analytical Mechanics and Astronomy. During this time he taught [Nikolai Ivanovich Lobachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Ivanovich_Lobachevsky).

In 1821 he moved to the [University of Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tartu), now [Tartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartu), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia), where he founded the Centre for [Differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625). He remained at Dorpat until his death. He was appointed [Privy Councillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Councillor) in 1823. From 1826 he was a corresponding member of the [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) [Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Science). He was also awarded high Russian honours. He died in [Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorpat).

## Family {#Family}

His daughter, [Johanna Henriette Francisca Bartels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanna_Henriette_Francisca_Bartels) (1807–1867), married [Friedrich Georg Wilhelm von Struve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Georg_Wilhelm_von_Struve) in 1835,[^o6] after the death of Struve's first wife. She bore Struve six children, the best known of which was [Karl de Struve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_de_Struve) (1835–1907), who served successively as Russian ambassador to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan), the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States), and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands).
