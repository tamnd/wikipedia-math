# Satellite navigation solution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Satellite navigation solution"}

[Satellite navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation) solution for the receiver's position ([geopositioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopositioning)) involves an algorithm. In essence, a [GNSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNSS) receiver measures the transmitting time of GNSS signals emitted from four or more GNSS satellites (giving the [pseudorange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorange)) and these measurements are used to obtain its position (i.e., [spatial coordinates](wikipedia://en/81931)) and reception time.

:::

The following are expressed in [inertial-frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_frame) coordinates.

## Calculation steps {#Calculation_steps}

A [global-navigation-satellite-system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_navigation_satellite_system) (GNSS) receiver measures the apparent transmitting time, $\tilde{t}_i$, or "phase", of GNSS signals emitted from four or more GNSS [satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite) ($i = 1,\, 2,\, 3,\, 4,\, ..,\, n$ ), simultaneously.[^o4]

### Notes for above {#Notes_for_above}

- In the field of GNSS, $\tilde{r}_i = -c (\tilde{t}_i - \tilde{t}_{\text{rec}})$ is called [pseudorange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorange), where $\tilde{t}_{\text{rec}}$ is a provisional reception time of the receiver. $\delta t_{\text{clock,rec}} = \tilde{t}_{\text{rec}} - t_{\text{rec}}$ is called receiver's clock bias (i.e., clock advance).[^o4]
- Standard GNSS receivers output $\tilde{r}_i$ and $\tilde{t}_{\text{rec}}$ per an observation [epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoch_%28astronomy%29).
- The temporal variation in the relativistic clock bias of satellite is linear if its orbit is circular (and thus its velocity is uniform in inertial frame).
- The signal time of flight from satellite to receiver is expressed as $-(t_i - t_{\text{rec}}) = \tilde{r}_i/c + \delta t_{\text{clock},i} - \delta t_{\text{clock,rec}}$, whose right side is [round-off-error](wikipedia://en/432450) resistive during calculation.
- The geometric range is calculated as $r(\boldsymbol{r}_i,\, \boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec}}) = | \Omega_{\text{E}} (t_i - t_{\text{rec}}) \boldsymbol{r}_{i,\text{ECEF}} - \boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec,ECEF}} |$, where the [Earth-centred, Earth-fixed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECEF) (ECEF) rotating frame (e.g., [WGS84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGS84) or [ITRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Terrestrial_Reference_Frame)) is used in the right side and $\Omega_{\text{E}}$ is the Earth rotating matrix with the argument of the signal [transit time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_of_flight).[^o5] The matrix can be factorized as $\Omega_{\text{E}} (t_i - t_{\text{rec}}) = \Omega_{\text{E}} (\delta t_{\text{clock,rec}}) \Omega_{\text{E}} (-\tilde{r}_i/c - \delta t_{\text{clock},i})$.
- The line-of-sight unit vector of satellite observed at $\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec,ECEF}}$ is described as: $\boldsymbol{e}_{i, \text{rec,ECEF}} = -\frac{\partial r(\boldsymbol{r}_i,\, \boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec}})}{\partial \boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec,ECEF}}}$.
- The [satellite-navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_navigation_satellite_system) [positioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_locating_system) [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) may be expressed by using the [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109) $\boldsymbol{r}_{\text{rec,ECEF}}$ and $\delta t_{\text{clock,rec}}$.
- The [nonlinearity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinearity) of the vertical dependency of [tropospheric delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropospheric_delay) degrades the convergence efficiency in the [Gauss–Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss–Newton) iterations in step 7.
- The above notation is different from that in the Wikipedia articles, 'Position calculation introduction' and 'Position calculation advanced', of [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) (GPS).

## The GPS case {#The_GPS_case}

For [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) (GPS),[^o5] the non-closed-form equations in step 3 result in

$$\begin{cases} \Delta t_i (t_i,\, E_i) \triangleq t_i + \delta t_{\text{clock},i} (t_i,\, E_i) - \tilde{t}_i = 0, \\ \Delta M_i (t_i,\, E_i) \triangleq M_i (t_i) - (E_i - e_i \sin E_i) = 0, \end{cases}$$

in which $E_i$ is the orbital [eccentric anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccentric_anomaly) of satellite $i$, $M_i$ is the [mean anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_anomaly), $e_i$ is the [eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity), and $\delta t_{\text{clock},i} (t_i,\, E_i) = \delta t_{\text{clock,sv},i} (t_i) + \delta t_{\text{orbit-relativ},i} (E_i)$.

## The GLONASS case {#The_GLONASS_case}

- The [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS) ephemerides don't provide clock biases $\delta t_{\text{clock,sv},i} (t)$, but $\delta t_{\text{clock},i} (t)$.
