# Švarc–Milnor lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Švarc–Milnor lemma"}

In the mathematical subject of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), the **Švarc–Milnor lemma** (sometimes also called **Milnor–Švarc lemma**, with both variants also sometimes spelling Švarc as Schwarz) is a statement which says that a group $G$, equipped with a "nice" [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_action) isometric [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $X$, is [quasi-isometric](wikipedia://en/12747259) to $X$.

:::

This result goes back, in different form, before the notion of [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) was formally introduced, to the work of [Albert S. Schwarz](wikipedia://en/28995317) (1955)[^o2] and [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632) (1968).[^o3] Pierre de la Harpe called the Švarc–Milnor lemma "the *fundamental observation in geometric group theory*"[^o4] because of its importance for the subject. Occasionally the name "fundamental observation in geometric group theory" is now used for this statement, instead of calling it the Švarc–Milnor lemma; see, for example, Theorem 8.2 in the book of [Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776) and Margalit.[^o5]

::: {#o7 .statement cue=heading name="Precise statement"}

## Precise statement {#Precise_statement}

Several minor variations of the statement of the lemma exist in the literature. Here we follow the version given in the book of Bridson and Haefliger (see Proposition 8.19 on p. 140 there).[^o8]

Let $G$ be a group acting by isometries on a [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_space) [length space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Length_space) $X$ such that the action is [properly discontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Properly_discontinuous_action) and [cocompact](wikipedia://en/12746615).

Then the group $G$ is finitely generated and for every finite generating set $S$ of $G$ and every point $p\in X$ the orbit map

$$f_p:(G,d_S)\to X, \quad g\mapsto gp$$

is a [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259).

Here $d_S$ is the [word metric](wikipedia://en/1289716) on $G$ corresponding to $S$.

Sometimes a properly discontinuous cocompact isometric action of a group $G$ on a proper geodesic metric space $X$ is called a *geometric* action.[^o9]

:::

## Explanation of the terms {#Explanation_of_the_terms}

Recall that a metric space $X$ is *proper* if every closed ball in $X$ is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042).

An action of $G$ on $X$ is *properly discontinuous* if for every compact $K\subseteq X$ the set

$$\{g\in G \mid gK\cap K\ne \varnothing\}$$

is finite.

The action of $G$ on $X$ is *cocompact* if the quotient space $X/G$, equipped with the [quotient topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quotient_topology), is compact. Under the other assumptions of the Švarc–Milnor lemma, the cocompactness condition is equivalent to the existence of a closed ball $B$ in $X$ such that

$$\bigcup_{g\in G} gB=X.$$

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Examples of applications of the Švarc–Milnor lemma"}

## Examples of applications of the Švarc–Milnor lemma {#Examples_of_applications_of_the_Švarc–Milnor_lemma}

For Examples 1 through 5 below see pp. 89–90 in the book of de la Harpe.[^o4] Example 6 is the starting point of the part of the paper of [Richard Schwartz](wikipedia://en/1213887).[^o13]

1. For every $n\ge 1$ the group $\mathbb Z^n$ is quasi-isometric to the Euclidean space $\mathbb R^n$.
1. If $\Sigma$ is a closed connected oriented surface of negative [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) then the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) $\pi_1(\Sigma)$ is quasi-isometric to the hyperbolic plane $\mathbb H^2$.
1. If $(M,g)$ is a closed connected smooth manifold with a smooth [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) $g$ then $\pi_1(M)$ is quasi-isometric to $(\tilde M, d_{\tilde g})$, where $\tilde M$ is the [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) of $M$, where $\tilde g$ is the pull-back of $g$ to $\tilde M$, and where $d_{\tilde g}$ is the path metric on $\tilde M$ defined by the Riemannian metric $\tilde g$.
1. If $G$ is a connected finite-dimensional [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) equipped with a left-invariant [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) and the corresponding path metric, and if $\Gamma\le G$ is a [uniform lattice](wikipedia://en/15782871#Definition) then $\Gamma$ is quasi-isometric to $G$.
1. If $M$ is a closed hyperbolic 3-manifold, then $\pi_1(M)$ is quasi-isometric to $\mathbb H^3$.
1. If $M$ is a complete finite volume hyperbolic 3-manifold with cusps, then $\Gamma=\pi_1(M)$ is quasi-isometric to $\Omega= \mathbb H^3-\mathcal B$, where $\mathcal B$ is a certain $\Gamma$-invariant collection of [horoballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoball), and where $\Omega$ is equipped with the induced path metric.

:::

## Proof {#Proof}

For the sake of simplicity,[^o16] we assume that $X$ is a *geodesic metric space*. This proof readily carries over to the more general setting.

### Sketch of the proof {#Sketch_of_the_proof}

Fix any $x_0\in X$. Since the action is cocompact and $X$ is proper, we may find $R>0$ such that the $G$-translates of $B(x_0, R)$ cover the whole space $X$. That is,

::: {#o18 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$X=\bigcup_{g\in G}{B(gx_0, R)}. \tag{1}$$

:::

Consider the subset $S=\{g\mid gx_0\in B(x_0, 2R+1)\}$ which is finite, because the action is proper. Note that it suffices to show that such $S$ generates $G$ and that its Cayley graph, denoted $Cay(G, S)$, is quasi-isometric to $X$. Indeed, because $G$ acts properly and cocompactly and by isometries on its own Cayley graph, applying this theorem with $X= Cay(G, S^\prime)$ shows that the theorem holds for every $S^\prime$, by transitivity.

Divide the geodesic between $x_0$ and $gx_0$ into $n$ pieces, where each piece is of length $1$, except possibly the last piece, which may be less than $1$. Denote the endpoints of these pieces by

$$p_0(=x_0), p_1, \dots, p_n(=gx_0).$$

For each $p_i$, we may take $g_i\in G$ such that $p_i\in B(g_ix_0, R)$. Setting $g_0=e$ and $g_n=g$. By a triangle inequality, and since the action is by isometries, we have

::: {#o19 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$d(x_0, g_{i-1}^{-1}g_ix_0)=d(g_{i-1}x_0, g_ix_0)\le 2R+1. \tag{2}$$

:::

Thus, by definition of $S$, $g_{i-1}^{-1}g_i=:s_i\in S$. Therefore, $g=s_1s_2\cdots s_n$ and hence $\|g\|_S\le n$. Also, $g$ is arbitrary, so $S$ generates $G$.

Additionally, because each $p_i$ lies along a geodesic from $x_0$ to $gx_0$ with segment lengths of at most $1$, the following inequality holds

::: {#o20 .equation cue=numblk name="3"}

$$d(x_0, gx_0)\ge n-1\ge \|g\|_S-1. \tag{3}$$

:::

By repeatedly applying (2) between each $g_{i-1}x_0$ and $g_ix_0$, and by the triangle inequality, it's easy to see that

::: {#o21 .equation cue=numblk name="4"}

$$d(x_0, gx_0)\le (2R+1)\cdot \|g\|_S. \tag{4}$$

:::

Now, (3) and (4) imply that for any $u, v\in G$,

::: {#o22 .equation cue=numblk name="5"}

$$d_S(u, v)-1\le d(ux_0, vx_0)\le (2R+1)\cdot d_S(u, v), \tag{5}$$

:::

where we take $u^{-1}v$ as the above $g$.

From (1), the \[[coarse surjectivity](wikipedia://en/12747259#:~:text=Every_point_of,point._More_formally%3A)\] clearly holds.

### How to extend the map {#How_to_extend_the_map}

Finally, by identifying each edge of $Cay(G, S)$ as the geodesic between two vertices of length $1$, we may extend the domain from $G$ to $Cay(G, S)$. More precisely, we can map the geodesic between $u$ and $v$ to the geodesic between $ux_0$ and $vx_0$ in $X$.

Note that the value of $d_S(g, h)$ varies at most $1$ from $d_S(u, v)$, and $d(gx_0, hx_0)$ varies at most $M$ from $d(ux_0, vx_0)$. If we denote

$$M:=\max_{u^{-1}v\in S}{d(ux_0, vx_0)}=\max_{s\in S}{d(x_0, sx_0)},$$

this makes sense because $S$ is finite.

Incorporating the adjustment $(2R+1)\cdot 1+M$ into (5), we have demonstrated that this map is a quasi-isometry.

:::
