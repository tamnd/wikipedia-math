# Toniann Pitassi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Toniann Pitassi"}

**Toniann Pitassi** is a Canadian-American mathematician and [computer scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_scientist) specializing in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543). She is currently Jeffrey L. and Brenda Bleustein Professor of Engineering at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and was Bell Research Chair at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

A native of [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh), Pitassi earned bachelor's and master's degrees at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) before moving to the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) for her doctoral studies; she earned her PhD in 1992 from Toronto under the supervision of [Stephen Cook](wikipedia://en/39432). After postdoctoral studies at the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) and faculty positions at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) and [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona), she returned to Toronto in 2001, and was a professor in the [Department of Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Computer_Science_%28University_of_Toronto%29) and [Department of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mathematics_%28University_of_Toronto%29) at the University of Toronto until 2021, when she joined the faculty of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).[^o6]

She was an invited speaker at [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin in 1998.[^o8][^o9] She was the program chair for the 2012 [Symposium on Theory of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symposium_on_Theory_of_Computing).[^o10] From September through December 2017, she was a visiting professor at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).[^o11]

## Research {#Research}

Pitassi's research has largely focused on [proof complexity](wikipedia://en/2801284), a branch of [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543) that seeks [upper and lower bounds](wikipedia://en/42693) on the lengths of [mathematical proofs](wikipedia://en/82285) of [logical propositions](wikipedia://en/81094) within various formalized proof systems. The goal of this study is to use these bounds to understand both the [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) of proof-finding procedures, and the relative strengths of different proof systems.

Research contributions that she has made in this area include exponential lower bounds for [Frege proofs](wikipedia://en/27801252) of the [pigeonhole principle](wikipedia://en/54217),[^o13] exponential lower bounds for the [cutting-plane method](wikipedia://en/1597680) applied to propositions derived from the [maximum clique problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_clique_problem),[^o14] exponential lower bounds for [resolution](wikipedia://en/2724082) proofs of dense random [3-satisfiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-satisfiability) instances,[^o15] and subexponential upper bounds for the same dense random instances using the [Davis–Putnam algorithm](wikipedia://en/2732435).[^o16] With various coauthors, she has several expositions and surveys: on proof complexity in general,[^o17] on algebraic proof complexity,[^o18] and on semialgebraic proof complexity.[^o19]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Pitassi was elected as an [ACM Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_Fellow) in 2018 for "contributions to research and education in the fields of computational and proof complexity".[^o21]

Pitassi was also the recipient of the [EATCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EATCS) (European Association for Theoretical Computer Science) Award in 2021 for her "fundamental and wide-ranging contributions to computational complexity".[^o22]

She was named to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2022.[^o4][^o23]
