# Mode-coupling theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mode-coupling theory"}

In [statistical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_physics), **mode-coupling theory** (**MCT**) is a family of approximations for the dynamics of many-body systems. In MCT, a relaxation rate or transport coefficient is expressed through the nonlinear coupling of a few slow collective variables ("modes"), and the resulting equations are closed [self-consistently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-consistency). The name covers two bodies of work that are historically connected but differ in scope and technique:

:::

- the mode-coupling theory of [critical dynamics](wikipedia://en/376800), developed in the 1960s mainly by [Kyozi Kawasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyozi_Kawasaki), which explains the anomalous transport coefficients near [critical points](wikipedia://en/1951419) and was later extended to long-time tails and to the kinetic theory of dense liquids;
- the mode-coupling theory of liquids and [colloidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloid) suspensions, developed from 1984 by [Wolfgang Götze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Götze) and co-workers.

## Common framework {#Common_framework}

Both variants start from the exact equations of motion for time [correlation functions](wikipedia://en/1651916) that follow from the [projection operator formalism](wikipedia://en/60437375) of [Robert Zwanzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Zwanzig) and Hazime Mori.[^o4][^o5] For a normalized correlation function $\phi(t)$ of a conserved or otherwise slow variable, this formalism yields a generalized [Langevin equation](wikipedia://en/166890) of the form

$$\partial_t\phi(t) + \Gamma\phi(t) + \int_0^t\!\mathrm{d}t'\,M(t-t')\,\phi(t') = 0,$$

where the *memory kernel* $M(t)$ is itself a correlation function of the fluctuating forces, which evolve under a projected dynamics. Equivalently, a transport coefficient $L$ is given by a [Green–Kubo formula](wikipedia://en/1458192) as the time integral of a flux autocorrelation function. These relations are exact but not closed. The mode-coupling approximation closes them in two steps:

1. the fluctuating force (or flux) is projected onto *products* of slow variables, typically pairs $A_{\mathbf k}B_{\mathbf q-\mathbf k}$ of Fourier components;
1. the resulting four-point correlation functions are factorized into products of two-point functions, $\langle A_{\mathbf k}(t)B_{\mathbf p}(t)\, A^*_{\mathbf k}B^*_{\mathbf p}\rangle \approx \langle A_{\mathbf k}(t)A^*_{\mathbf k}\rangle \langle B_{\mathbf p}(t)B^*_{\mathbf p}\rangle.$

The memory kernel thereby becomes a quadratic functional of the very correlation functions it determines. The so obtained equation of motion of the correlation functions is genuinely non-linear, which is decisive for the explanation slow dynamics.
