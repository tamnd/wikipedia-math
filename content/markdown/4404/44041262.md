# Coefficient of colligation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coefficient of colligation"}

In statistics, **Yule's *Y***, also known as the **coefficient of colligation**, is a measure of association between two binary variables. The measure was developed by [George Udny Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Udny_Yule) in 1912,[^o2][^o3] and should not be confused with [Yule's coefficient](wikipedia://en/28212#Quantile-based_measures) for measuring [skewness](wikipedia://en/28212) based on [quartiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartiles).

:::

::: {#o5 .statement cue=named-heading name="Formula"}

## Formula {#Formula}

For a 2×2 table for [binary variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_variable) *U* and *V* with frequencies or proportions

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || *a* || *b* |- | *U* = 1 || *c* || *d* |}

Yule's *Y* is given by

$$Y = \frac{\sqrt{ad}-\sqrt{bc}}{\sqrt{ad}+\sqrt{bc}}.$$

Yule's *Y* is closely related to the [odds ratio](wikipedia://en/406880) *OR* = *ad*/(*bc*) as is seen in following formula:

$$Y = \frac{\sqrt{OR}-1}{\sqrt{OR}+1}$$

Yule's *Y* varies from −1 to +1. −1 reflects total negative [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057), +1 reflects perfect positive association while 0 reflects no association at all. These correspond to the values for the more common [Pearson correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_correlation).

Yule's *Y* is also related to the similar [Yule's *Q*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yule's_Q), which can also be expressed in terms of the odds ratio. *Q* and *Y* are related by:

$$Q = \frac{2Y}{1+Y^2}\ ,$$

$$Y = \frac{1-\sqrt{1-Q^2}}{Q}\ .$$

:::

## Interpretation {#Interpretation}

Yule's *Y* gives the fraction of perfect association in per [unum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unum_%28number_format%29) (multiplied by 100 it represents this fraction in a more familiar percentage). Indeed, the formula transforms the original 2×2 table in a crosswise symmetric table wherein *b* = *c* = 1 and *a* = *d* = $\sqrt{OR}$.

For a crosswise symmetric table with frequencies or proportions *a* = *d* and *b* = *c* it is very easy to see that it can be split up in two tables. In such tables association can be measured in a perfectly clear way by dividing (*a* – *b*) by (*a* + *b*). In transformed tables b has to be substituted by 1 and a by $\sqrt{OR}$. The transformed table has the same degree of association (the same OR) as the original not-crosswise symmetric table. Therefore, the association in asymmetric tables can be measured by Yule's *Y*, interpreting it in just the same way as with symmetric tables. Of course, Yule's *Y* and (*a* − *b*)/(*a* + *b*) give the same result in crosswise symmetric tables, presenting the association as a fraction in both cases.

Yule's *Y* measures association in a substantial, intuitively understandable way and therefore it is the measure of preference to measure association.

::: {#o8 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The following crosswise symmetric table

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || 40 || 10 |- | *U* = 1 || 10 || 40 |}

can be split up into two tables:

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || 10 || 10 |- | *U* = 1 || 10 || 10 |}

and

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || 30 || 0 |- | *U* = 1 || 0 || 30 |}

It is obvious that the degree of association equals 0.6 per unum (60%).

The following asymmetric table can be transformed in a table with an equal degree of association (the odds ratios of both tables are equal).

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || 3 || 1 |- | *U* = 1 || 3 || 9 |}

Here follows the transformed table:

{| class="wikitable"

! !! *V* = 0 !! *V* = 1 |- | *U* = 0 || 3 || 1 |- | *U* = 1 || 1 || 3 |}

The odds ratios of both tables are equal to 9. *Y* = (3 − 1)/(3 + 1) = 0.5 (50%)

:::
