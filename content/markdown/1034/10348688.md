# Adam Back

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Adam Back"}

**Adam Back** (born July 1970) is a British [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer) and [cypherpunk](wikipedia://en/37314). He is the CEO of [Blockstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockstream), which he co-founded in 2014. He invented [Hashcash](wikipedia://en/567023), which is used in the [bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265) mining process. An investigation by *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* suggested that he may be [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto), the pseudonymous inventor of bitcoin, though Back has denied this claim.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Back was born in London, England, in July 1970.[^o4] His first computer was a [Sinclair ZX81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZX81). He taught himself [BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BASIC), and spent his time reverse engineering [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game), finding [decryption keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decryption_key) in [software packages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_suite). He completed his [A levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-Levels) in advanced [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), and [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).

He has a [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in [distributed systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_systems) from the [University of Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Exeter).[^o5] During his PhD, Back worked with [compilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compiler) to make use of [parallel computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computers) in a semi automated way. He became interested in [PGP](wikipedia://en/23080) encryption, [electronic cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_cash_system) and [remailers.](wikipedia://en/54666) He spent two thirds of his time working with [encryption](wikipedia://en/10294). After graduation, Back spent his career as a [consultant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultant) in [startups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_company) and larger companies in [applied cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_cryptography), writing cryptographic libraries, [designing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design), [reviewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review) and breaking other people's [cryptographic protocols](wikipedia://en/591994).[^o6]

## Cryptography software {#Cryptography_software}

*Image: Munitions T-shirt (front).jpg.* Back's "Munitions" T-shirt featured computer code that was considered to be a weapon in the United States.

Back is a pioneer of early digital asset research similar to [Wei Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Dai), [David Chaum](wikipedia://en/239365), and [Hal Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Finney_%28computer_scientist%29).[^o8][^o9] In 1997, Back invented [Hashcash](wikipedia://en/567023).[^o10] A similar system is used in [Bitcoin](wikipedia://en/28249265).[^o11][^o12][^o13]

He also implemented *credlib*,[^o14][^o15] a library that implements the credential systems of [Stefan Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Brands) and [David Chaum](wikipedia://en/239365).

He was the first to describe the "non-interactive forward secrecy"[^o16][^o17][^o18] security property for email and to observe that any [identity-based encryption](wikipedia://en/385892) scheme can be used to provide non-interactive forward secrecy.

He is also known for promoting the use of ultra-compact code with his 2-line[^o19] and 3-line [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28cryptosystem%29) in Perl[^o20][^o21][^o22] [signature block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signature_block) and non-exportable [T-shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-shirt)[^o23][^o24] to protest [cryptography export regulations](wikipedia://en/826003).[^o25]

Back has promoted the use of satellites and mesh networks to broadcast and receive bitcoin transactions, as a backup for the traditional internet.[^o26]

### Relationship to Satoshi Nakamoto {#Relationship_to_Satoshi_Nakamoto}

Back was one of the first two people to receive an email from [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satoshi_Nakamoto), the pseudonymous inventor of bitcoin.[^o28][^o5] In 2016, the *[Financial Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Times)* cited Back as a potential Nakamoto candidate, along with [Nick Szabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Szabo) and [Hal Finney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Finney_%28computer_scientist%29).[^o29] [Craig Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Steven_Wright) had sued Back for stating that Wright was not Nakamoto, with Wright subsequently dropping the suit.[^o5] In 2020, the YouTube channel *Barely Sociable* claimed that Back is Nakamoto. Back subsequently denied this.[^o30]

The 2024 [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) documentary [Money Electric: The Bitcoin Mystery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_Electric:_The_Bitcoin_Mystery), directed by [Cullen Hoback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cullen_Hoback), examined Back as its principal Satoshi Nakamoto suspect, with Hoback stating that "the evidence pointing in Adam's direction was a lot harder to poke holes in."[^o31][^o32] The film noted that Back is the only person named in the body of the [Bitcoin white paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitcoin_white_paper), and that Back's writing style resembles Nakamoto's. Hoback also highlighted Back's move to [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta), his edits to Wikipedia's Bitcoin article that restored its Nakamoto section and listed other suspects but not himself, and his joining the Bitcoin forum on April 17, 2013, the same day the size of Nakamoto's holdings first became public.[^o31][^o33] In an on-camera interview for the documentary, after Hoback listed him among the suspects, Back replied: "I thought you might think I'm Satoshi. And I don't want that to be on the record, really."[^o31][^o34] The film argued that both Back and the developer [Peter Todd](wikipedia://en/78123626) had been involved in the creation of Bitcoin, and characterized Blockstream, the company Back co-founded in 2014, as possible "corporate Satoshi".[^o31]

In 2026, an investigation by [John Carreyrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carreyrou) in *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* asserted that Back is Nakamoto, citing his use of certain less common phrases, shared niche interests, and the timing of Nakamoto’s final known communication and Back’s first public comment on Bitcoin, among other things. Back once again denied the allegation. Carreyrou concluded the article by demonstrating that while Back denied being Satoshi, he was unable to adequately explain a substantial amount of circumstantial evidence beyond categorical denial, and refused to provide the [metadata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metadata) attached to the e-mails he exchanged with Nakamoto. During an in-person interview, Back's body language at times appeared to contradict his denials, with his face reddening as he shifted uncomfortably when confronted with specific points. In one notable exchange, when Carreyrou raised a Satoshi quote stating "I'm better with code than with words," Back interrupted and responded as though speaking for himself, a slip that Carreyrou interpreted as Back accidentally responding as Satoshi.[^o34][^o35]

## Business career {#Business_career}

On 3 October 2016, Back was appointed as CEO of [Blockstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockstream).[^o37]
