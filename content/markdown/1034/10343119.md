# Alex Wilkie

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alex Wilkie"}

**Alex James Wilkie** [FRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (born 1948 in [Northampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northampton)[^o3]) is a British mathematician known for his contributions to [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858) and [logic](wikipedia://en/19636). Previously Reader in Mathematical Logic at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), he was appointed to the [Fielden Chair of Pure Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielden_Chair_of_Pure_Mathematics) at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Mathematics,_University_of_Manchester) in 2007.[^o4]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Alex Wilkie attended [Aylesbury Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aylesbury_Grammar_School) and went on to gain his [BSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in mathematics with [first class honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_class_honours) from [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) in 1969, his [MSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (in mathematical logic) from the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) in 1970, and his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from the [Bedford College, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_College,_University_of_London) in 1973 under the supervision of [Wilfrid Hodges](wikipedia://en/1789771) with a dissertation titled *Models of Number Theory*.

## Career and research {#Career_and_research}

After his doctoral research he was appointment as a lecturer in mathematics at [Leicester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester_University) from 1972 to 1973, then a research fellow at the [Open University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_University) from 1973 until 1978. He spent two periods as a junior lecturer in mathematics at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University) (1978–80 and 1981-2) with (1980–1) as a visiting assistant professor at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University). In 1980 Wilkie solved [Tarski's high school algebra problem](wikipedia://en/21394895).

In October 1982 Wilkie was appointed as a research fellow in the department of mathematics at the [University of Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VII), then returned to England the following year to take up a three-year SERC (now [EPSRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EPSRC)) advanced research fellowship at the University of Manchester. After two years he was appointed lecturer in the Department of Mathematics. In 1986 he went on to Oxford where he was appointed to the readership in mathematical logic there which had become vacant upon the retirement of [Robin Gandy](wikipedia://en/3155895). He remained in this post until appointment to the Fielden Chair at Manchester.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Wilkie was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) (FRS) in 2001. To quote the citation

*Wilkie has combined logical techniques and differential-geometric techniques to establish fundamental Finiteness Theorems for sets definable using the exponential function, and more general [Pfaffian functions](wikipedia://en/14644287). The results, going far beyond those obtained by conventional methods, have already had striking applications to Lie groups.*[^o9]

Wilkie received the [Carol Karp Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karp_Prize) (the highest award made by the [Association for Symbolic Logic](wikipedia://en/3770438), every five years) jointly with [Ehud Hrushovski](wikipedia://en/3132981) in 1993.[^o10][^o11] He was elected to the Council of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2007, vice-president of the Association for Symbolic Logic (2006) and president of the Association for Symbolic Logic in 2009. In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12] He received the Karp Prize again in 2013, jointly with Moti Gitik, Ya'acov Peterzil, [Jonathan Pila](wikipedia://en/31960316), and Sergei Starchenko.[^o11] In 2017, Wilkie was awarded the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28LMS%29).[^o13]

He was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berkeley in 1986[^o14] and in Berlin in 1998.[^o15]

In 2015, Wilkie held the [Gödel Lecture](wikipedia://en/69216431) titled *Complex continuations of functions definable in $\mathbb{R}_{an, exp}$ with a diophantine application.*
