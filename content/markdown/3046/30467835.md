# Hexagonal tortoise problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hexagonal tortoise problem"}

*Image: hexagonal\_tortoise\_problem.svg.* Choi Seok-jeong's original magic hexagonal tortoise pattern. All the sums of six numbers of each hexagon are the same number, 93. The magic sum varies if the numbers 1 through 30 are rearranged. For example, the magic sum could be 77 through 109.

The **hexagonal tortoise problem** (지수귀문도) was invented by Korean aristocrat and mathematician [Choi Seok-jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choi_Seok-jeong) (1646–1715). It is a mathematical problem that involves a hexagonal lattice, like the hexagonal pattern on some tortoises' shells, to the (*N*) [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) of which must be assigned integers (from 1 to *N*) in such a way that the sum of all integers at the vertices of each hexagon is the same.[^n1] The problem has apparent similarities to a [magic square](wikipedia://en/52202) although it is a vertex-magic format rather than an edge-magic form or the more typical rows-of-cells form.[^n1]

:::

His book, *[Gusuryak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusuryak)*, contains many mathematical discoveries.

## Related Problems {#Related_Problems}

Analogues of the hexagonal tortoise problem based on other [Uniform tilings](wikipedia://en/12126635) have also been studied. [Yang Hui](wikipedia://en/1716898) and [Choi Seok-jeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choi_Seok-jeong) studied related magic figures based on the [truncated square tiling](wikipedia://en/2864244). Analogues based on the [square tiling](wikipedia://en/2863809), in which the sums of the 2×2 subsquares are equal, have also been studied , as have analogues based on the [trihexagonal tiling](wikipedia://en/2864397) .[^o5]
