# Arnold Scholz

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arnold Scholz"}

*Image: Arnold Scholz.jpg.* Photo showing Arnold Scholz

Arnold Scholz **Arnold Scholz** (24 December 1904 in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) – 1 February 1942 in [Flensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg)) was a German mathematician working in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) who proved [Scholz's reciprocity law](wikipedia://en/33627178) and introduced the [Scholz conjecture](wikipedia://en/578650).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

### Life {#Life}

Scholz was the son of Reinhold Scholz, an executive at the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian) [Military Research Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_War_%28Prussia%29). He attended the Kaiserin Auguste Gymnasium in [Charlottenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottenburg). From 1923 to 1928, he studied Mathematics, Philosophy and [Musicology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicology) at the [Universität Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt-Universität_zu_Berlin). In 1928, Scholz wrote his dissertation, supervised by [Issai Schur](wikipedia://en/617522) and titled *Über die Bildung algebraischer Zahlkörper mit auflösbarer Galoisscher Gruppe* ("On the creation of [algebraic number fields](wikipedia://en/28730822) with [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012) [Galois Groups](wikipedia://en/12673)").

In 1927, Scholz spent a semester in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866), where he studied under [Philipp Furtwängler](wikipedia://en/895349). After his promotion, he became a lecture assistant in Berlin in 1928, a [Privatdozent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in [Freiburg im Breisgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau) in 1930, and a lecturer at the [University of Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kiel) from 1935-1940. In 1940, he was [conscripted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription) and became a lecturer at the [Mürwik Naval School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mürwik_Naval_School) in [Flensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flensburg)-[Mürwik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mürwik).

Throughout his life, he exchanged many letters with [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118), and in the 1930s he worked with [Olga Taussky-Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Taussky-Todd).

In 1942, Scholz died of [Diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes). [^o4]

### Work {#Work}

Scholz worked in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705). He published early works on the [inverse Galois problem](wikipedia://en/739166) in [algebraic number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_number_fields), where, together with [Hans Reichardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Reichardt), he proved the solvability of the problem for [p-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) in the case where *p* is an odd prime. After the end of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), the works of Reichardt and Scholz were carried on by [Igor Shafarevich](wikipedia://en/1091815), who showed that the problem is solvable for all [solvable groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvable_groups).

In 1928, Scholz proved the existence of algebraic number fields with arbitrarily large class field towers.

In 1930, Scholz participated in the [Second Conference on the Epistemology of the Exact Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Conference_on_the_Epistemology_of_the_Exact_Sciences) contributing the paper "On the Use of the Term Holism in Axiomatics" to the discussion on the [foundation of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_of_mathematics).

[Scholz's reciprocity law](wikipedia://en/33627178) is named after him, although according to historian of mathematics [Franz Lemmermeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Lemmermeyer),[^o6] the law was already known to [Theodor Schönemann](wikipedia://en/31259301).

His *[Nachlass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachlass)* included an almost complete manuscript titled *Spezielle Zahlkörper* (Special Number Fields), intended for the newest edition of the [Encyclopedia of Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/33686790).
