# 2D rotation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="2D rotation"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **2D rotation** is a [rotation](wikipedia://en/410009) in [two dimensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_dimensions), commonly on the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410). It leaves unchanged a single [point](wikipedia://en/593693), the **centre of rotation**, often assumed at the [origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_%28geometry%29) of the [coordinate system](wikipedia://en/81931). The amount of rotation is represented by the *[angle of rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_rotation)*, expressed in [units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_of_measurement) such as degrees or radians; a [signed angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signed_angle) allows for two different senses of rotation: [clockwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockwise) or anti-clockwise. It corresponds to the 2D [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) or SO(2).

:::

## Formulation {#Formulation}

In two dimensions, to carry out a [rotation using a matrix](wikipedia://en/856005), the point $(x,\,y)$ to be rotated counterclockwise is written as a column vector, then multiplied by a [rotation matrix](wikipedia://en/856005) calculated from the angle $\theta$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \\ \sin \theta & \cos \theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}.$$

The coordinates of the point after rotation are $x',\,y'$, and the formulae for $x'$ and $y'$ are

$$\begin{aligned} x'&=x\cos\theta-y\sin\theta\\ y'&=x\sin\theta+y\cos\theta. \end{aligned}$$

The vectors $\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{bmatrix}$ have the same magnitude and are separated by an angle $\theta$ as expected.

Points on the $\mathbf{R}^{2}$ plane can be also presented as [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826): the point $(x,\,y)$ in the plane is represented by the complex number

$$z = x + iy$$

This can be rotated through an angle $\theta$ by multiplying it by $e^{i\theta}$, then expanding the product using [Euler's formula](wikipedia://en/9613) as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} e^{i \theta} z &= (\cos \theta + i \sin \theta) (x + i y) \\ &= x \cos \theta + i y \cos \theta + i x \sin \theta - y \sin \theta \\ &= (x \cos \theta - y \sin \theta) + i ( x \sin \theta + y \cos \theta) \\ &= x' + i y' , \end{aligned}$$

and equating real and imaginary parts gives the same result as a two-dimensional matrix:

$$\begin{aligned} x'&=x\cos\theta-y\sin\theta\\ y'&=x\sin\theta+y\cos\theta. \end{aligned}$$

Since complex numbers form a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), vector rotations in two dimensions are commutative, unlike in higher dimensions. They have only one [degree of freedom](wikipedia://en/2321587), as such rotations are entirely determined by the angle of rotation.[^o4]
