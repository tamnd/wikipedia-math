# Rayleigh–Faber–Krahn inequality

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rayleigh–Faber–Krahn inequality"}

In [spectral geometry](wikipedia://en/20635289), the **Rayleigh–Faber–Krahn inequality**, named after its conjecturer, [Lord Rayleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Rayleigh), and the two individuals who independently proved the conjecture, [G. Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Faber) and [Edgar Krahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Krahn), is an [inequality](wikipedia://en/89489) concerning the lowest [Dirichlet eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/22860539) of the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706) on a bounded domain in $\mathbb{R}^n$, $n \ge 2$. It states that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is no less than the corresponding Dirichlet eigenvalue of a Euclidean ball having the same volume. Furthermore, the inequality is [rigid](wikipedia://en/1458875) in the sense that if the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is equal to that of the corresponding ball, then the domain must actually be a ball. In the case of $n=2$, the inequality essentially states that among all drums of equal area, the circular drum (uniquely) has the lowest voice.

:::

More generally, the Faber–Krahn inequality holds in any [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $M$ which satisfies the same sharp [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182) as a complete simply connected space form of constant sectional curvature.[^o3] In particular, if the [Cartan–Hadamard conjecture](wikipedia://en/62358280) holds, then the Faber–Krahn inequality should also hold in all simply connected manifolds of nonpositive curvature.
