# ΔP

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ΔP"}

**ΔP** (**Delta P**) is a mathematical term symbolizing a change (Δ) in [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) (*P*).

:::

::: {#o3 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

- [Young–Laplace equation](wikipedia://en/9122581)

### Darcy–Weisbach equation {#Darcy–Weisbach_equation}

Given that the [head loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_loss) *h<sub>f</sub>* expresses the [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) loss *Δp* as the height of a column of fluid,

$$\Delta p = \rho \cdot g \cdot h_f$$

where ρ is the density of the fluid. The [Darcy–Weisbach equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy–Weisbach_equation) can also be written in terms of pressure loss:

$$\Delta p = f \cdot \frac{L}{D} \cdot \frac{\rho V^2}{2}$$

### Lung compliance {#Lung_compliance}

In general, compliance is defined by the change in volume (Δ*V*) versus the associated change in pressure (Δ*P*), or [Δ*V*/Δ*P*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_compliance#Calculation):

$$Compliance = \frac{ \Delta V}{ \Delta P}$$

During [mechanical ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_ventilation), compliance is influenced by three main physiologic factors:

1. [Lung compliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_compliance)
1. [Chest wall compliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chest_wall_compliance)
1. [Airway resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_resistance)

Lung compliance is influenced by a variety of primary abnormalities of [lung parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_parenchyma), both chronic and acute. [Airway resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway_resistance) is typically increased by [bronchospasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchospasm) and airway secretions. Chest wall compliance can be decreased by fixed abnormalities (e.g. [kyphoscoliosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyphoscoliosis), [morbid obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbid_obesity)) or more variable problems driven by patient agitation while intubated.[^o6]

Calculating compliance on [minute volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_volume) (V<sub>E</sub>: Δ*V* is always defined by [tidal volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_volume) (V<sub>T</sub>), but Δ*P* is different for the measurement of dynamic vs. static compliance.

#### Dynamic compliance (C<sub>dyn</sub>) {#Dynamic_compliance_(Cdyn)}

$$C_{dyn} = \frac{V_T}\mathrm{PIP-PEEP}$$

where PIP = [peak inspiratory pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_inspiratory_pressure) (the maximum pressure during inspiration), and PEEP = [positive end expiratory pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_end_expiratory_pressure). Alterations in airway resistance, [lung compliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung_compliance) and chest wall compliance influence C<sub>dyn</sub>.

#### Static compliance (C<sub>stat</sub>) {#Static_compliance_(Cstat)}

$$C_{stat} = \frac{{V_T}}{{P_{plat}-PEEP}}$$

where P<sub>plat</sub> = plateau pressure. P<sub>plat</sub> is measured at the end of inhalation and prior to exhalation using an inspiratory hold maneuver. During this maneuver, airflow is transiently (~0.5 sec) discontinued, which eliminates the effects of airway resistance. P<sub>plat</sub> is never > PIP and is typically \< 3-5 cmH<sub>2</sub>O lower than PIP when airway resistance is normal.
