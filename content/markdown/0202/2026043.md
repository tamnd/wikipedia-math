# Hyperbolic secant distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperbolic secant distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **hyperbolic secant distribution** is a continuous [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) whose [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) and [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401) are proportional to the [hyperbolic secant function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function). The hyperbolic secant function is equivalent to the reciprocal [hyperbolic cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_function), and thus this distribution is also called the **inverse-cosh distribution**.

:::

Generalisation of the distribution gives rise to the **Meixner distribution**, also known as the **Natural Exponential Family - Generalised Hyperbolic Secant** or **NEF-GHS distribution**.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

### Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

A [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) follows a hyperbolic secant distribution if its probability density function can be related to the following standard form of density function by a location and shift transformation:

$$f(x) = \frac12 \operatorname{sech}\frac{\pi x}{2},$$

where "sech" denotes the hyperbolic secant function.

### Cumulative distribution function {#Cumulative_distribution_function}

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) (cdf) of the standard distribution is a scaled and shifted version of the [Gudermannian function](wikipedia://en/303274),

$$\begin{aligned} F(x) &= \frac12 + {\frac{1}{\pi} \arctan} \Bigl({\operatorname{sinh}\frac{\pi x}2}\Bigr) \\[8mu] &= {\frac{2}{\pi} \arctan}\Bigl({\exp\frac{\pi x}2} \Bigr) . \end{aligned}$$

where "arctan" is the [inverse (circular) tangent function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function#Inverse_functions).

Johnson et al. (1995)[^o6]p. 147 places this distribution in the context of a class of generalized forms of the [logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666), but use a different parameterisation of the standard distribution compared to that here. Ding (2014)[^o7] shows three occurrences of the Hyperbolic secant distribution in statistical modeling and inference.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The hyperbolic secant distribution shares many properties with the standard [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462): it is symmetric with unit [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) and zero [mean](wikipedia://en/9653), [median](wikipedia://en/18837) and [mode](wikipedia://en/1432127), and its probability density function is proportional to its characteristic function. However, the hyperbolic secant distribution is [*leptokurtic*](wikipedia://en/16848#Terminology_and_examples); that is, it has a more acute peak near its mean, and heavier tails, compared with the standard normal distribution. Both the hyperbolic secant distribution and the [logistic distribution](wikipedia://en/542666) are special cases of the [Champernowne distribution](wikipedia://en/36020629), which has exponential tails.

The inverse cdf (or quantile function) for a uniform variate 0 ≤ p \< 1 is

$$F^{-1}(p) = -\frac{2}{\pi}\, \operatorname{arsinh}\!\left[\cot(\pi\,p)\right] \! ,$$

$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\, \ln\!\left[\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\,p\right)\right] \! .$$

where "arsinh" is the [inverse hyperbolic sine function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_hyperbolic_function) and "cot" is the [(circular) cotangent function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function).

:::

## Generalisations {#Generalisations}

### Convolution {#Convolution}

Considering the (scaled) sum of $r$ [independent and identically distributed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_identically_distributed_random_variables) hyperbolic secant random variables:

$$X = \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\; (X_1 + X_2 + \;...\; + X_r)$$

then in the limit $r\to\infty$ the distribution of $X$ will tend to the normal distribution $N(0,1)$, in accordance with the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406).

This allows a convenient family of distributions to be defined with properties intermediate between the hyperbolic secant and the normal distribution, controlled by the shape parameter $r$, which can be extended to non-integer values via the [characteristic function](wikipedia://en/3229401)

$$\varphi(t) = \big(\operatorname{sech}(t /\sqrt{r})\big)^r$$

Moments can be readily calculated from the characteristic function. The excess [kurtosis](wikipedia://en/16848) is found to be $2/r$.

### Location and scale {#Location_and_scale}

The distribution (and its generalisations) can also trivially be shifted and scaled in the usual way to give a corresponding [location-scale family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location-scale_family):

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2 \sigma} \operatorname{sech} \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)$$

### Skew {#Skew}

A [skewed](wikipedia://en/28212) form of the distribution can be obtained by multiplying by the exponential $e^{\theta x}, |{\theta}|<{\pi}/2$ and normalising, to give the distribution

$$f(x) = \cos \theta \; \frac{e^{\theta x}}{2 \operatorname{cosh}(\frac{\pi x}{2})}$$

where the parameter value $\theta = 0$ corresponds to the original distribution.

### Kurtosis {#Kurtosis}

The [Champernowne distribution](wikipedia://en/36020629) has an additional parameter to shape the core or wings.

### Meixner distribution {#Meixner_distribution}

Allowing all four of the adjustments above gives distribution with four parameters, controlling shape, skew, location, and scale respectively, called either the **Meixner distribution**[^o16] after [Josef Meixner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Meixner) who first investigated the family, or the **NEF-GHS distribution** ([Natural exponential family](wikipedia://en/9413032) - Generalised Hyperbolic Secant distribution).

In [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) the Meixner distribution has been used to model non-Gaussian movement of stock-prices, with applications including the pricing of [options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_%28finance%29).

### Related distribution {#Related_distribution}

Losev (1989) has studied independently the asymmetric (skewed) curve $h(x)=\frac{1}{\exp(-a x) + \exp(b x)}$, which uses just two parameters $a, b$. In it, $a$ is a measure of left skew and $b$ a measure of right skew, in case the parameters are both positive. They have to be both positive or negative, with $a = b$ being the hyperbolic secant - and therefore symmetric - and $h(x)^r$ being its further reshaped form.[^o18]

The normalising constant is as follows:

$$\frac{a+b}{\pi}\sin\left(\frac{b \pi}{a+b}\right)$$

which reduces to $\frac{2a}{\pi}$ for the symmetric version.

Furthermore, for the symmetric version, $a$ can be estimated as $a=\frac{\pi}{2\sigma}$.
