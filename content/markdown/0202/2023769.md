# Peter of Spain

*Image: Summulae logicales.jpg.* Frontispiece of work by Peter of Hispania

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter of Spain"}

**Peter of Hispania** (Petrus Hispanus; [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) and Pedro Hispano; 13th century) was the author of the *Tractatus*, later known as the *Summulae Logicales*, an important [medieval university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_university) textbook on [Aristotelian logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelian_logic). As the Latin *[Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania)* was considered to include the entire [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), he is traditionally and usually identified with the [medieval Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) [scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholar) and [ecclesiastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy_of_the_Catholic_Church) Peter Juliani, who was elected [Pope John XXI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXI) in 1276.[^o2][^o3] The identification is sometimes disputed, usually by [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people) authors, who claim the author of the *Tractatus* was a [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilian_people) [Blackfriar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order). He is also sometimes identified as [Petrus Ferrandi Hispanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_Ferrandi_Hispanus) (d. 1254 x 1259).

:::

## Life {#Life}

The author of the *Tractatus* is assumed to have studied under [John Pagus](wikipedia://en/16091363).

## Philosophical works {#Philosophical_works}

There are a large volume of manuscripts and printed editions of the *Summulae Logicales*, indicative of its great success throughout [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) universities well into the seventeenth century. The most recent edition is **Peter of Hispania** (**Petrus Hispanus Portugalensis**), *Tractatus called afterwards Summule Logicales*, edited by Lambertus Marie de Rijk, Wijsgerige Teksten en Studies 22 (Assen: Van Gorcum, 1972). A later logical work of his is, in the most recent edition, Peter of Hispania (Petrus Hispanus Portugalensis), *Syncategoreumata*, edited by Lambertus Marie de Rijk and translated by Joke Spruyt, Studien und Text zur Geistesgeschichte des Mittelalters 30 (New York: E. J. Brill, 1992).

Peter of Hispania supported an investigation of teaching at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) which resulted in the [Parisian bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris)'s [Condemnation of 1277](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condemnation_of_1277), which denounced Aristotelian propositions which conflicted with [church doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma).[^o6]

*Dialectica est ars artium, scientia scientiarum, ad omnium methodorum principia viam habens; sola enim dialectica probabiliter disputat de principiis omnium aliarum scientiarum, et ideo in acquisitione omnium aliarum scientiarum dialectica debet esse prior.* "Dialectic \[that is, logic, in Peter's terminology\] is the art of arts, science of sciences, having the way to the principles of all methods; for in fact dialectic alone credibly argues about the principles of all other sciences, and therefore in \[one's\] acquisition (learning) of all other sciences dialectic must be prior." — Peter of Hispania.[^o7]

## Medical works {#Medical_works}

*Image: Thesaurus pauperum V00179 00000004.tif.* *Thesaurus pauperum*

A Petrus Hispanus, usually identified as the same scholar,[^o9] was also credited as the author of a *Commentary on Isaac*, one of the foundational texts of [clinical pharmacology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_pharmacology). A Pedro Hispano was also credited with the *Treasury of The Poor* (*Thesaurus Pauperum*), a comprehensive medical manual of diseases and remedies.[^o10][^o11]
