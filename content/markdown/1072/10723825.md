# Waterman polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Waterman polyhedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **Waterman polyhedra** are a family of [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) discovered around 1990 by the mathematician [Steve Waterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Waterman_%28mathematician%29). A Waterman polyhedron is created by [packing spheres](wikipedia://en/368621) according to the [cubic close(st) packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_close_packing) (CCP), also known as the face-centered cubic (fcc) packing, then sweeping away the spheres that are farther from the center than a defined radius,[^o2] then creating the [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of the sphere centers.

:::

*Image: W5 sphere cluster.jpg.* Waterman/fcc sphere cluster W5

*Image: W5 polyhedron.jpg.* Waterman/fcc polyhedra interpretation of sphere cluster w5

Waterman polyhedra form a vast family of polyhedra. Some of them have a number of special properties such as multiple symmetries, or interesting and regular shapes. Others are less structured collection of faces formed from irregular [convex polygons](wikipedia://en/660019).

The most popular Waterman polyhedra are those with centers at the point (0,0,0) and built out of hundreds of polygons. Such polyhedra resemble spheres. In fact, the more faces a Waterman polyhedron has, the more it resembles its [circumscribed sphere](wikipedia://en/991784) in volume and total area.

With each point of 3D space we can associate a family of Waterman polyhedra with different values of radii of the circumscribed spheres. Therefore, from a mathematical point of view we can consider Waterman polyhedra as 4D spaces W(x, y, z, r), where x, y, z are coordinates of a point in 3D, and r is a positive number greater than 1.[^o3]

## Seven origins of cubic close(st) packing (CCP) {#Seven_origins_of_cubic_close(st)_packing_(CCP)}

There can be seven origins defined in CCP,[^o5] where n = {1, 2, 3, …}:

- Origin 1: offset 0,0,0, radius $\sqrt{2n}$
- Origin 2: offset $\tfrac{1}{2}$,$\tfrac{1}{2}$,0, radius $\tfrac12\sqrt{2+4n}$
- Origin 3: offset $\tfrac{1}{3}$,$\tfrac{1}{3}$,$\tfrac{2}{3}$, radius $\tfrac13\sqrt{6(n+1)}$
- Origin 3\*: offset $\tfrac{1}{3}$,$\tfrac{1}{3}$,$\tfrac{1}{3}$, radius $\tfrac13\sqrt{3+6n}$
- Origin 4: offset $\tfrac{1}{2}$,$\tfrac{1}{2}$,$\tfrac{1}{2}$, radius $\tfrac12\sqrt{3+8(n-1)}$
- Origin 5: offset 0,0,$\tfrac{1}{2}$, radius $\tfrac12\sqrt{1+4n}$
- Origin 6: offset 1,0,0, radius $\sqrt{1+2(n-1)}$

Depending on the origin of the sweeping, a different shape and resulting polyhedron are obtained.

## Relation to Platonic and Archimedean solids {#Relation_to_Platonic_and_Archimedean_solids}

Some Waterman polyhedra create [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905) and [Archimedean solids](wikipedia://en/1847). For this comparison of Waterman polyhedra they are normalized, e.g. W2 O1 has a different size or volume than W1 O6, but has the same form as an octahedron.

### Platonic solids {#Platonic_solids}

- Tetrahedron: W1 O3\*, W2 O3\*, W1 O3, W1 O4
- Octahedron: W2 O1, W1 O6
- Cube: W2 O6
- Icosahedron and dodecahedron have no representation as Waterman polyhedra.

### Archimedean solids {#Archimedean_solids}

- [Cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/6280): W1 O1, W4 O1
- [Truncated octahedron](wikipedia://en/279654): W10 O1
- [Truncated tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/279651): W4 O3, W2 O4
- The other Archimedean solids have no representation as Waterman polyhedra.

The W7 O1 might be mistaken for a [truncated cuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/285879), as well W3 O1 = W12 O1 mistaken for a [rhombicuboctahedron](wikipedia://en/26213), but those Waterman polyhedra have two edge lengths and therefore do not qualify as Archimedean solids.

## Generalized Waterman polyhedra {#Generalized_Waterman_polyhedra}

Generalized Waterman polyhedra are defined as the convex hull derived from the point set of any spherical extraction from a regular lattice.

Included is a detailed analysis of the following 10 lattices – bcc, cuboctahedron, diamond, fcc, hcp, truncated octahedron, [rhombic dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/607631), simple cubic, truncated tet tet, truncated tet truncated octahedron cuboctahedron.

Each of the 10 lattices were examined to isolate those particular origin points that manifested a unique polyhedron, as well as possessing some minimal symmetry requirement. From a viable origin point, within a lattice, there exists an unlimited series of polyhedra. Given its proper sweep interval, then there is a one-to-one correspondence between each [integer value](wikipedia://en/585797) and a generalized Waterman polyhedron.
