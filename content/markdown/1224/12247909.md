# Siegel disc

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Siegel disc"}

A **Siegel disc** or **Siegel disk** is a [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [component in the Fatou set](wikipedia://en/10422205) where the dynamics is analytically [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_conjugation) to an [irrational rotation](wikipedia://en/9445837).

:::

## Description {#Description}

Given a [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) $f:S\to S$ on a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181) $S$ we consider the [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) generated by the [iterates](wikipedia://en/1448702) of $f$ denoted by $f^n=f\circ\stackrel{\left(n\right)}{\cdots}\circ f$. We then call the [orbit](wikipedia://en/252250) $\mathcal{O}^+(z_0)$ of $z_0$ as the set of forward iterates of $z_0$. We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the orbits in $S$ (which will usually be $\mathbb{C}$, the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) or $\mathbb{\hat C}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}$, the [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799)), and we call $S$ the [phase plane](wikipedia://en/2110756) or *dynamical plane*.

One possible asymptotic behavior for a point $z_0$ is to be a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738), or in general a *periodic point*. In this last case $f^p(z_0)=z_0$ where $p$ is the [period](wikipedia://en/252250) and $p=1$ means $z_0$ is a fixed point. We can then define the *multiplier* of the orbit as $\rho=(f^p)'(z_0)$ and this enables us to classify periodic orbits as *attracting* if $|\rho|<1$ *superattracting* if $|\rho|=0$), *repelling* if $|\rho|>1$ and indifferent if $|\rho|=1$. Indifferent periodic orbits can be either *rationally indifferent* or *irrationally indifferent*, depending on whether $\rho^n=1$ for some $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ or $\rho^n\neq1$ for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, respectively.

**Siegel discs** are one of the possible cases of connected components in the Fatou set (the complementary set of the [Julia set](wikipedia://en/56434)), according to [Classification of Fatou components](wikipedia://en/10422205), and can occur around irrationally indifferent periodic points. The Fatou set is, roughly, the set of points where the iterates behave similarly to their neighbours (they form a [normal family](wikipedia://en/1480325)). **Siegel discs** correspond to points where the dynamics of $f$ are analytically [conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_conjugation) to an irrational rotation of the complex [unit disc](wikipedia://en/252738).

## Name {#Name}

The Siegel disc is named in honor of [Carl Ludwig Siegel](wikipedia://en/716602).

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $f\colon S\to S$ be a [holomorphic](wikipedia://en/14110) [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) where $S$ is a [Riemann surface](wikipedia://en/173181), and let U be a [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28analysis%29) of the Fatou set $\mathcal{F}(f)$. We say U is a Siegel disc of f around the point $z_0$ if there exists a biholomorphism $\phi:U\to\mathbb{D}$ where $\mathbb{D}$ is the unit disc and such that $\phi(f^n(\phi^{-1}(z)))=e^{2\pi i\alpha n}z$ for some $\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}$ and $\phi(z_0)=0$.

[Siegel's](wikipedia://en/716602) theorem proves the existence of **Siegel discs** for [irrational numbers](wikipedia://en/20647689) satisfying a *strong irrationality condition* (a [Diophantine condition](wikipedia://en/251558)), thus solving an open problem since Fatou conjectured his theorem on the [Classification of Fatou components](wikipedia://en/10422205).[^o9]

Later [Alexander D. Brjuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bruno) improved this condition on the irrationality, enlarging it to the [Brjuno numbers](wikipedia://en/31775773).[^o10]

This is part of the result from the [Classification of Fatou components](wikipedia://en/10422205).
