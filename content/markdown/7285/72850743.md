# Maria Gramegna

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maria Gramegna"}

**Maria Paola Gramegna** (1887–1915) was an Italian mathematician and a student of [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043). Her work with Peano on systems of [linear differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868) has been cited as an important early milestone in the history of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and its transition from working with concrete [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) to more abstract basis-independent formulations of [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422). After becoming a schoolteacher, she died in the [1915 Avezzano earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1915_Avezzano_earthquake).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

*Image: Maria Gramegna.jpg.* Students and teachers at the "[Maria Clotilde di Savoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Maria_Clotilde_of_Savoy)" Royal Normal and Complementary School for Girls in [Avezzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avezzano), [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo), in the early 1900s

Gramegna was born on 11 May 1887 in [Tortona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortona), the youngest of five children of a pasta factory owner. After studying mathematics in high school in [Voghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voghera) with [Giuseppe Vitali](wikipedia://en/634990), she was admitted to the [University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin) in 1906, where she became a student of Peano. She completed her mathematics degree in 1910, with the thesis *Serie di equazioni differenziali lineari ed equazioni integro-differenziali* \[*Series of linear differential equations and integro-differential equations*\], supervised by Peano. Peano also presented this work to the [Academy of Sciences of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Sciences_of_Turin).

## Later life {#Later_life}

Immediately after finishing her mathematics degree, she also earned a diploma from the mathematics section of the university's school of education, with a second thesis, *Area della zona sferica e della sfera* \[*Area of the spherical zone and of the sphere*\]. With this credential, she became a high school teacher in [Avezzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avezzano). She began there in 1910 as a substitute mathematics teacher at the [normal school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school), won a competition for a permanent position as extraordinary mathematics teacher in 1912, and directed the associated boarding school for 1912–1913. During this period she also published on the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).

She was killed by the [1915 Avezzano earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1915_Avezzano_earthquake), on 13 January 1915.

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Minor planet [37840 Gramegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37840_Gramegna), discovered in 1998 by the [San Vittore Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vittore_Observatory), was named in honor of Gramegna.
