# Russo–Vallois integral

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Russo–Vallois integral"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), the **Russo–Vallois integral** is an extension to [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) of the classical [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491)

:::

$$\int f \, dg=\int fg' \, ds$$

for suitable functions $f$ and $g$. The idea is to replace the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) $g'$ by the difference quotient

$g(s+\varepsilon)-g(s)\over\varepsilon$ and to pull the limit out of the integral. In addition one changes the type of convergence.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

::: {#o4 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition:** A sequence $H_n$ of [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) [converges](wikipedia://en/50723) uniformly on [compact sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) in probability to a process $H,$

$$H=\text{ucp-}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}H_n,$$

if, for every $\varepsilon>0$ and $T>0,$

$$\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|H_n(t)-H(t)|>\varepsilon)=0.$$

One sets:

$$I^-(\varepsilon,t,f,dg)={1\over\varepsilon}\int_0^tf(s)(g(s+\varepsilon)-g(s))\,ds$$

$$I^+(\varepsilon,t,f,dg)={1\over\varepsilon}\int_0^t f(s)(g(s)-g(s-\varepsilon)) \, ds$$

and

$$[f,g]_\varepsilon (t)={1\over \varepsilon}\int_0^t(f(s+\varepsilon)-f(s))(g(s+\varepsilon)-g(s))\,ds.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition:** The forward integral is defined as the ucp-limit of

$I^-$: $\int_0^t fd^-g=\text{ucp-}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty (0?)}I^-(\varepsilon,t,f,dg).$

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition:** The backward integral is defined as the ucp-limit of

$I^+$: $\int_0^t f \, d^+g = \text{ucp-}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty (0?)}I^+(\varepsilon,t,f,dg).$

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=inline-head name="Definition"}

**Definition:** The generalized bracket is defined as the ucp-limit of

$[f,g]_\varepsilon$: $[f,g]_\varepsilon=\text{ucp-}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow\infty}[f,g]_\varepsilon (t).$

For continuous [semimartingales](wikipedia://en/9653775) $X,Y$ and a [càdlàg](wikipedia://en/30862839) function H, the Russo–Vallois integral coincidences with the usual [Itô integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itô_integral):

$$\int_0^t H_s \, dX_s=\int_0^t H \, d^-X.$$

In this case the generalised bracket is equal to the classical covariation. In the special case, this means that the process

$$[X]:=[X,X] \,$$

is equal to the [quadratic variation process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_variation_process).

Also for the Russo-Vallois Integral an [Ito formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ito_formula) holds: If $X$ is a continuous semimartingale and

$$f\in C_2(\mathbb{R}),$$

then

$$f(X_t)=f(X_0)+\int_0^t f'(X_s) \, dX_s + {1\over 2}\int_0^t f''(X_s) \, d[X]_s.$$

By a duality result of [Triebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triebel) one can provide optimal classes of [Besov spaces](wikipedia://en/18542594), where the Russo–Vallois integral can be defined. The norm in the Besov space

$$B_{p,q}^\lambda(\mathbb{R}^N)$$

is given by

$$||f||_{p,q}^\lambda=||f||_{L_p} + \left(\int_0^\infty {1\over |h|^{1+\lambda q}}(||f(x+h)-f(x)||_{L_p})^q \, dh\right)^{1/q}$$

with the well known modification for $q=\infty$. Then the following theorem holds:

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Suppose

$$f\in B_{p,q}^\lambda,$$

$$g\in B_{p',q'}^{1-\lambda},$$

$$1/p+1/p'=1\text{ and }1/q+1/q'=1.$$

Then the Russo–Vallois integral

$$\int f \, dg$$

exists and for some constant $c$ one has

$$\left| \int f \, dg \right| \leq c ||f||_{p,q}^\alpha ||g||_{p',q'}^{1-\alpha}.$$

Notice that in this case the Russo–Vallois integral coincides with the [Riemann–Stieltjes integral](wikipedia://en/330491) and with the [Young integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_integra) for functions with [finite p-variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_p-variation).

:::
