# James D. Murray

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="James D. Murray"}

**James Dickson Murray** (born 2 January 1931) is professor [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus) of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) at [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) and [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). He is best known for his authoritative and extensive work entitled *[Mathematical Biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biology)*.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

Murray was born in [Moffat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffat,_Dumfries_and_Galloway), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), and was educated at [St. Andrews University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrews_University), where he received with honours a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1953. He took his PhD there in 1956.

## Research and career {#Research_and_career}

His first post was at the [University of Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_University), UK; later he has held positions at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), London and Oxford, becoming professor of mechanical engineering at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1965, at the age of 34.

He later became professor of mathematical biology at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), a fellow and tutor in mathematics at [Corpus Christi College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford) and founder and director of the Centre for Mathematical Biology. He left Oxford in the late 1980s for the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in Seattle, where he spent the rest of his career as professor of mathematics and adjunct professor of zoology.

His research is characterised by its great range and depth: an early example is his fundamental contributions to understanding the [biomechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomechanics) of the human body when launched from an aircraft in an [ejection seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejection_seat). He has made contributions to many other areas, ranging from understanding and preventing severe scarring; fingerprint formation; [sex determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex-determination_system), modelling of animal coat and territory formation in wolf-deer interacting populations.

## Awards and honours {#Awards_and_honours}

Murray was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh) in 1979[^o8] and a [Fellow of the Royal Society (FRS) in 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fellows_of_the_Royal_Society_elected_in_1985).[^o9]

In 2008 Murray and [Professor T. J. Pedley](wikipedia://en/23705786), FRS were jointly awarded the [Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_Gold_Medal) of the [Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/697139) in recognition of their "outstanding contributions to mathematics and its applications over a period of years".[^o10]
