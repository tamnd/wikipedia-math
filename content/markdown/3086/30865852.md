# O-minimal theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="O-minimal theory"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), and more specifically in [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), an infinite [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) $(M,<,\dots)$ that is [totally ordered](wikipedia://en/30330) by $<$ is called an **o-minimal structure** if and only if every [definable](wikipedia://en/5569486) subset $X\subseteq M$ (with parameters taken from $M$) is a finite [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of [intervals](wikipedia://en/49172) and points.

:::

O-minimality can be regarded as a weak form of [quantifier elimination](wikipedia://en/2150441). A structure $M$ is o-minimal if and only if every [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28logic%29) with one [free variable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_variable) and parameters in $M$ is equivalent to a quantifier-free formula involving only the ordering, also with parameters in $M$. This is analogous to the [minimal](wikipedia://en/14385549) structures, which are exactly the analogous property down to equality.

A [theory](wikipedia://en/3512103) $T$ is an **o-minimal theory** if every [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_%28model_theory%29) of $T$ is o-minimal. It is known that the [complete theory](wikipedia://en/10086335) $T$ of an o-minimal structure is an o-minimal theory.[^o2] This result is remarkable because, in contrast, the complete theory of a minimal structure need not be a [strongly minimal theory](wikipedia://en/14385549), that is, there may be an [elementarily equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementarily_equivalent) structure that is not minimal.

## Set-theoretic definition {#Set-theoretic_definition}

O-minimal structures can be defined without recourse to model theory. Here we define a structure on a nonempty set $M$ in a set-theoretic manner, as a sequence $(S_n)_{n=0}^\infty$ such that

1. $S_n$ is a [boolean algebra](wikipedia://en/3959) of subsets of $M^n$
1. if $D\in S_n$ then $M\times D$ and $D\times M$ are in $S_{n+1}$
1. the set $\{(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in M^n : x_1=x_n\}$ is in $S_n$
1. if $D\in S_{n+1}$ and $\pi:M^{n+1}\to M^n$ is the projection map on the first $n$ coordinates, then $\pi(D)\in S_n$.

For a subset $A$ of $M$, we consider the smallest structure $S(A)$ containing $S$ such that every finite subset of $A$ is contained in $S_1$. A subset $D$ of $M^n$ is called $A$-definable if it is contained in $S_n(A)$; in that case, $A$ is called a set of parameters for $D$. A subset is called definable if it is $A$-definable for some $A$.

If $M$ has a dense linear order without endpoints on it, say $<$, then a structure $S$ on $M$ is called o-minimal (with respect to $<$) if it satisfies the extra axioms

the set $<$ (identified with its graph $\{(x, y) \in M^2: x < y\}$) is in $S_2$ the definable subsets of $M$ are precisely the finite unions of intervals and points.

The "o" stands for "order", since any o-minimal structure requires an ordering on the underlying set.

## Model theoretic definition {#Model_theoretic_definition}

O-minimal structures originated in model theory and so have a simpler—but equivalent—definition using the language of model theory.[^o5] Namely, if $L$ is a language including a binary relation $<$, and $(M,<,\dots)$ is an $L$-structure where $<$ is interpreted to satisfy the axioms of a dense linear order,[^o6] then $(M,<,\dots)$ is called an o-minimal structure if for any definable set $X\subseteq M$ there are finitely many open intervals $I_1,\dots,I_r$ in $M\cup \{\pm\infty\}$ and a finite set $X_0$ such that

$$X=X_0\cup I_1\cup\ldots\cup I_r.$$

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of o-minimal theories are:

- The complete theory of dense linear orders in the language with just the ordering.
- RCF, the [theory](wikipedia://en/30746) of [real closed fields](wikipedia://en/1066093).[^o9]
- The complete theory of the [real field](wikipedia://en/20646438) with restricted [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478) added (i.e., analytic functions on a neighborhood of $[0,1]^n$, restricted to $[0,1]^n$; note that the unrestricted sine function has infinitely many roots, and so cannot be definable in an o-minimal structure.)
- The complete theory of the real field with a symbol for the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) by [Wilkie's theorem](wikipedia://en/22304328). More generally, the complete theory of the real numbers with [Pfaffian functions](wikipedia://en/14644287) added.
- The last two examples can be combined: given any o-minimal expansion of the real field (such as the real field with restricted analytic functions), one can define its Pfaffian closure, which is again an o-minimal structure.[^o10] (The Pfaffian closure of a structure is, in particular, closed under Pfaffian chains where arbitrary definable functions are used in place of polynomials.)

In the case of RCF, the definable sets are the [semialgebraic sets](wikipedia://en/13329722). Thus the study of o-minimal structures and theories generalises [real algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/7252198). A major line of current research is based on discovering expansions of the real ordered field that are o-minimal. Despite the generality of application, one can show a great deal about the geometry of set definable in o-minimal structures. There is a cell decomposition theorem,[^o11] [Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) and [Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893) [stratification](wikipedia://en/571280) theorems and a good notion of dimension and Euler characteristic.

Moreover, continuously differentiable definable functions in a o-minimal structure satisfy a generalization of [Łojasiewicz inequality](wikipedia://en/22483813),[^o12] a property that has been used to guarantee the convergence of some non-smooth optimization methods, such as the stochastic subgradient method (under some mild assumptions).[^o13][^o14][^o15]
