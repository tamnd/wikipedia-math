# The Strange Logic of Random Graphs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="The Strange Logic of Random Graphs"}

***The Strange Logic of Random Graphs*** is a book on [zero-one laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-one_law) for [random graphs](wikipedia://en/358477). It was written by [Joel Spencer](wikipedia://en/4244250) and published in 2001 by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag) as volume 22 of their book series [Algorithms and Combinatorics](wikipedia://en/57504451).

:::

## Topics {#Topics}

The random graphs of the book are generated from the [Erdős–Rényi–Gilbert model](wikipedia://en/10477221) $G(n,p)$ in which $n$ vertices are given and a random choice is made whether to connect each pair of vertices by an edge, independently for each pair, with probability $p$ of making a connection. A zero-one law is a theorem stating that, for certain properties of graphs, and for certain choices of $p$, the probability of generating a graph with the property tends to zero or one in the limit as $n$ goes to infinity.

A fundamental result in this area, proved independently by Glebskiĭ et al. and by [Ronald Fagin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Fagin), is that there is a zero-one law for $G(n,1/2)$ for every property that can be described in the [first-order logic of graphs](wikipedia://en/47857242). Moreover, the limiting probability is one if and only if the infinite [Rado graph](wikipedia://en/7785594) has the property. For instance, a random graph in this model contains a triangle with probability tending to one; it contains a [universal vertex](wikipedia://en/51352780) with probability tending to zero. For other choices of $p$, other outcomes can occur. For instance, the limiting probability of containing a triangle is between 0 and 1 when $p=c/n$ for a constant $c$; it tends to 0 for smaller choices of $p$ and to 1 for larger choices. The function $1/n$ is said to be a *threshold* for the property of containing a triangle, meaning that it separates the values of $p$ with limiting probability 0 from the values with limiting probability 1.

The main result of the book (proved by Spencer with [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211)) is that irrational powers of $n$ are never threshold functions. That is, whenever $a>0$ is an [irrational number](wikipedia://en/20647689), there is a zero-one law for the first-order properties of the random graphs $G(n,n^{-a})$. A key tool in the proof is the [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game](wikipedia://en/1040475).

## Audience and reception {#Audience_and_reception}

Although it is essentially the proof of a single theorem, aimed at specialists in the area, the book is written in a readable style that introduces the reader to many important topics in [finite model theory](wikipedia://en/1773596) and the theory of random graphs. Reviewer Valentin Kolchin, himself the author of another book on random graphs, writes that the book is "self-contained, easily read, and is distinguished by elegant writing", recommending it to [probability theorists](wikipedia://en/23542) and [logicians](wikipedia://en/19636). Reviewer Alessandro Berarducci calls the book "beautifully written" and its subject "fascinating".
