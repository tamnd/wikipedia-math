# Extended side

*Image: Incircle and Excircles.svg.* Each of a triangle's [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircles) (orange) is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to one of the triangle's sides and to the other two extended sides.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Extended side"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), an **extended side** or **sideline** of a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) is the [line](wikipedia://en/946975) that contains one [side](wikipedia://en/12158034) of the polygon. The extension of a finite side (a [line segment](wikipedia://en/22634860)) into an infinite supporting line arises in various contexts.

:::

## Triangle {#Triangle}

In an [obtuse triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obtuse_triangle), the [altitudes](wikipedia://en/161241) from the [acute angled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_angle) [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) intersect the corresponding *[extended base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_base)* sides but not the base sides themselves.

The [excircles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of a triangle, as well as the triangle's [inconics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inconic) that are not [inellipses](wikipedia://en/43943006), are externally [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to one side and to the other two extended sides.

[Trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790) locate a point in the plane by its relative distances from the extended sides of a reference triangle. If the point is outside the triangle, the perpendicular from the point to the sideline may meet the sideline outside the triangle—that is, not on the actual side of the triangle.

In a triangle, three intersection points, each of an [external angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_angle) [bisector](wikipedia://en/152547) with the opposite extended side, are [collinear](wikipedia://en/3189581).[^o3]

In a triangle, three intersection points, two of them between an [interior angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interior_angle) bisector and the opposite side, and the third between the other exterior angle bisector and the opposite side extended, are collinear.[^o3]

## Ex-tangential quadrilateral {#Ex-tangential_quadrilateral}

*Image: Ex-tangential quadrilateral.png.* An ex-tangential quadrilateral *ABCD* and its excircle

An [ex-tangential quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/32791638) is a [quadrilateral](wikipedia://en/25278) for which there exists a circle that is tangent to all four extended sides. The excenter (center of the tangent circle) lies at the intersection of six [angle bisectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_bisector). These are the [internal angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_angle) bisectors at two opposite vertex angles, the [external angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_angle) bisectors ([supplementary angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supplementary_angle) bisectors) at the other two vertex angles, and the external angle bisectors at the angles formed where the extensions of opposite sides intersect.

## Hexagon {#Hexagon}

*Image: THPascal.svg.* The intersections of the extended opposite sides of inscribed hexagon ABCDEF lie on the blue **Pascal line** MNP. The hexagon's extended sides are in gray and red.

[Pascal's theorem](wikipedia://en/699966) states that if six arbitrary points are chosen on a [conic section](wikipedia://en/19008673) (i.e., [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231) or [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052)) and joined by line segments in any order to form a [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), then the three pairs of opposite sides of the hexagon (extended if necessary) meet in three points which lie on a straight line, called the Pascal line of the hexagon.
