# Stereohedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stereohedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and [crystallography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallography), a **stereohedron** is a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) that [fills space isohedrally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isohedral_tiling), meaning that the [symmetries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetries) of the tiling take any copy of the stereohedron to any other copy.[^o2]

:::

Two-dimensional analogues to the stereohedra are called [planigons](wikipedia://en/53413131). Higher dimensional [polytopes](wikipedia://en/23471) can also be stereohedra, while they would more accurately be called **stereotopes**.

## Plesiohedra {#Plesiohedra}

A subset of stereohedra are called [plesiohedrons](wikipedia://en/53396858), defined as the [Voronoi cells](wikipedia://en/177668) of a symmetric [Delone set](wikipedia://en/38607512).

[Parallelohedrons](wikipedia://en/25207334) are plesiohedra which are space-filling by translation only. Edges here are colored as parallel vectors.

## Other periodic stereohedra {#Other_periodic_stereohedra}

The [catoptric tessellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catoptric_tessellation) contain stereohedra cells. [Dihedral angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_angles) are integer divisors of 180°, and are colored by their order. The first three are the fundamental domains of ${\tilde{C}}_3$, ${\tilde{B}}_3$, and ${\tilde{A}}_3$ symmetry, represented by [Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter-Dynkin_diagram): , and . ${\tilde{B}}_3$ is a half symmetry of ${\tilde{C}}_3$, and ${\tilde{A}}_3$ is a quarter symmetry.

Any space-filling stereohedra with symmetry elements can be [dissected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_%28geometry%29) into smaller identical cells which are also stereohedra. The name modifiers below, half, quarter, and eighth represent such dissections.
