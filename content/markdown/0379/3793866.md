# American Cryptogram Association

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="American Cryptogram Association"}

The **American Cryptogram Association** (**ACA**) is an American [non-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit) organization devoted to the [hobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobby) of [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432), with an emphasis on types of codes, ciphers, and [cryptograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptograms) that can be solved either with pencil and paper, or with computers, but not computer-only systems.

:::

## History {#History}

The ACA was formed on September 1, 1930. Initially the primary interest was in monoalphabetic [substitution ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_ciphers) (also known as "single alphabet" or "Aristocrat" puzzles). This has since been extended to dozens of different systems, such as [Playfair](wikipedia://en/173586), [autokey](wikipedia://en/157792), [transposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transposition_%28cryptography%29), and [Vigenère ciphers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigenère_ciphers).[^o5]

Since some of its members had belonged to the “[National Puzzlers' League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Puzzlers'_League)”, some of the NPL terminology ("nom," "Krewe," etc.) is also used in the ACA.[^o6]

## Publications and activities {#Publications_and_activities}

The association has a collection of books and articles on cryptography and related subjects in the library at [Kent State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_State_University).[^o8]

An annual convention takes place in late August or early September. Recent conventions have been held in [Bletchley Park](wikipedia://en/4037) and [Fort Lauderdale, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale,_Florida).[^o9]

There is also a regular journal called “*The Cryptogram*”, which first appeared in February, 1932, and has grown to a 28-page bimonthly periodical which includes articles and challenge ciphers.[^o5]

## Notable members {#Notable_members}

- [H. O. Yardley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Yardley), who used the nom BOZO, first Vice President in 1933.
- [Helen Fouché Gaines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Fouché_Gaines), member since 1933, who used the nom PICCOLA, editor of the 1939 book *Elementary Cryptanalysis*.
- [Rosario Candela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario_Candela), who used the nom ISKANDER, member since June 1934.
- [David Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Kahn_%28writer%29), who used the noms DAKON, ISHCABIBEL and more recently Kahn D.
- [Will Shortz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Shortz), *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times)* Puzzle Editor, who uses the nom WILLz.
- [David Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shulman), [lexicographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexicographer) and [cryptographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographer), member since 1933, who used the nom Ab Struse.
- [James Gillogly](wikipedia://en/3793338), who uses the nom SCRYER.
- [Gelett Burgess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelett_Burgess), American artist, art critic, poet, author and humorist, used the nom TWO O'CLOCK.
