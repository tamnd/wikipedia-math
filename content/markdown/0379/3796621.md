# Inner model theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Inner model theory"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), **[inner model](wikipedia://en/1118686) theory** is the study of certain [models](wikipedia://en/19858) of [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) or some fragment or strengthening thereof. Ordinarily these models are [transitive](wikipedia://en/2462396) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) or [subclasses](wikipedia://en/1828474) of the [von Neumann universe](wikipedia://en/445980) *V*, or sometimes of a [generic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generic_extension) of *V*. Inner model theory studies the relationships of these models to [determinacy](wikipedia://en/2596030), [large cardinals](wikipedia://en/383644), and [descriptive set theory](wikipedia://en/396619). Despite the name, it is considered more a branch of set theory than of [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- The [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all sets is an inner model containing all other inner models.
- The first non-trivial example of an inner model was the [constructible universe](wikipedia://en/343335) *L* developed by [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736). Every model *M* of ZF has an inner model *L*<sup>*M*</sup> satisfying the [axiom of constructibility](wikipedia://en/696472), and this will be the smallest inner model of *M* containing all the [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932) of *M*. Regardless of the properties of the original model, *L*<sup>*M*</sup> will satisfy the [generalized continuum hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_continuum_hypothesis) and combinatorial axioms such as the [diamond principle](wikipedia://en/691736) ◊.
- HOD, the class of sets that are hereditarily [ordinal definable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_definable), form an inner model, which satisfies ZFC.
- The sets that are hereditarily definable over a countable sequence of ordinals form an inner model, used in [Solovay's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solovay's_theorem).
- [L(R)](wikipedia://en/2250604), the smallest inner model containing all real numbers and all ordinals.
- L\[*U*\], the class constructed relative to a normal, non-principal, $\kappa$-complete [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/67416519) *U* over an ordinal $\kappa$ (see [zero dagger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_dagger)).

:::

## Consistency results {#Consistency_results}

One important use of inner models is the proof of consistency results. If it can be shown that every model of an axiom *A* has an inner model satisfying axiom *B*, then if *A* is [consistent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistent), *B* must also be consistent. This analysis is most useful when *A* is an axiom independent of ZFC, for example a [large cardinal axiom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cardinal_axiom); it is one of the tools used to rank axioms by [consistency strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consistency_strength).
