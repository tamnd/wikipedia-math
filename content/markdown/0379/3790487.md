# Plaintext-aware encryption

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Plaintext-aware encryption"}

**Plaintext-awareness** is a notion of security for [public-key encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public-key_encryption). A [cryptosystem](wikipedia://en/506383) is plaintext-aware if it is difficult for any efficient [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) to come up with a valid [ciphertext](wikipedia://en/175560) without being aware of the corresponding [plaintext](wikipedia://en/157935).

:::

From a lay point of view, this is a strange property. Normally, a ciphertext is computed by [encrypting](wikipedia://en/10294) a plaintext. If a ciphertext is created this way, its creator would be aware, in some sense, of the plaintext. However, many cryptosystems are *not* plaintext-aware. As an example, consider the [RSA cryptosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) without padding. In the RSA cryptosystem, plaintexts and ciphertexts are both values [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) N (the modulus). Therefore, RSA is not plaintext aware: one way of generating a ciphertext without knowing the plaintext is to simply choose a random number modulo N.

In fact, plaintext-awareness is a very strong property. Any cryptosystem that is [semantically secure](wikipedia://en/960585) and is plaintext-aware is actually secure against a [chosen-ciphertext attack](wikipedia://en/103586), since any adversary that chooses ciphertexts would already know the plaintexts associated with them.

## History {#History}

The concept of plaintext-aware encryption was developed by [Mihir Bellare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihir_Bellare) and [Phillip Rogaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Rogaway) in their paper on [optimal asymmetric encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_Asymmetric_Encryption_Padding),[^o3] as a method to prove that a cryptosystem is chosen-ciphertext secure.

## Further research {#Further_research}

Limited research on plaintext-aware encryption has been done since Bellare and Rogaway's paper. Although several papers have applied the plaintext-aware technique in proving encryption schemes are chosen-ciphertext secure, only three papers revisit the concept of plaintext-aware encryption itself, both focussed on the definition given by Bellare and Rogaway that inherently require [random oracles](wikipedia://en/451286). Plaintext-aware encryption is known to exist when a public-key infrastructure is assumed. [^o5] Also, it has been shown that weaker forms of plaintext-awareness exist under the knowledge of exponent assumption, a non-standard assumption about [Diffie-Hellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffie-Hellman) triples. [^o6] Finally a variant of the [Cramer Shoup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cramer-Shoup_system) encryption scheme was shown to be fully plaintext aware in the standard model under the knowledge of exponent assumption. [^o7]
