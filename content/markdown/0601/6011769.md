# Quantum mutual information

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum mutual information"}

In [quantum information theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_information_theory), **quantum mutual information (QMI)**, or **von Neumann mutual information**, after [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942), is a measure of correlation between subsystems of [quantum state](wikipedia://en/30876419).[^o2] It is the quantum mechanical analog of [Shannon](wikipedia://en/5693) [mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282).

:::

## Motivation {#Motivation}

For simplicity, it will be assumed that all objects in the article are finite-dimensional.

The definition of quantum mutual entropy is motivated by the classical case. For a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) of two variables *p*(*x*, *y*), the two marginal distributions are

$$p(x) = \sum_{y} p(x,y), \qquad p(y) = \sum_{x} p(x,y).$$

The classical mutual information *I*(*X*:*Y*) is defined by

$$I(X:Y) = S(p(x)) + S(p(y)) - S(p(x,y))$$

where *S*(*q*) denotes the [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_entropy) of the probability distribution *q*.

One can calculate directly

$$\begin{aligned} S(p(x)) + S(p(y)) &= - \left (\sum_x p_x \log p(x) + \sum_y p_y \log p(y) \right ) \\ &= -\left (\sum_x \left ( \sum_{y'} p(x,y') \log \sum_{y'} p(x,y') \right ) + \sum_y \left ( \sum_{x'} p(x',y) \log \sum_{x'} p(x',y) \right ) \right ) \\ &= -\left (\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \left (\log \sum_{y'} p(x,y') + \log \sum_{x'} p(x',y) \right ) \right )\\ &= -\sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log p(x) p(y) \end{aligned}$$

So the mutual information is

$$I(X:Y) = \sum_{x,y} p(x,y) \log \frac{p(x,y)}{p(x) p(y)},$$

Where the [logarithm](wikipedia://en/17860) is taken in basis 2 to obtain the mutual information in [bits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bit). But this is precisely the [relative entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_entropy) between *p*(*x*, *y*) and *p*(*x*)*p*(*y*). In other words, if we assume the two variables *x* and *y* to be uncorrelated, mutual information is the *discrepancy in uncertainty* resulting from this (possibly erroneous) assumption.

It follows from the property of relative entropy that *I*(*X*:*Y*) ≥ 0 and equality holds if and only if *p*(*x*, *y*) = *p*(*x*)*p*(*y*).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The quantum mechanical counterpart of classical probability distributions are modeled with [density matrices](wikipedia://en/62844).

Consider a quantum system that can be divided into two parts, A and B, such that independent measurements can be made on either part. The state space of the entire quantum system is then the [tensor product of the spaces for the two parts](wikipedia://en/2728973).

$$H_{AB} := H_A \otimes H_B.$$

Let *ρ*<sup>*AB*</sup> be a density matrix acting on states in *H*<sub>*AB*</sub>. The [von Neumann entropy](wikipedia://en/2515425) of a density matrix S(*ρ*), is the quantum mechanical analogy of the Shannon entropy.

$$S(\rho) = - \operatorname{Tr} \rho \log \rho.$$

For a probability distribution *p*(*x*,*y*), the marginal distributions are obtained by integrating away the variables *x* or *y*. The corresponding operation for density matrices is the [partial trace](wikipedia://en/814148). So one can assign to *ρ* a state on the subsystem *A* by

$$\rho^A = \operatorname{Tr}_B \; \rho^{AB}$$

where Tr<sub>*B*</sub> is partial trace with respect to system *B*. This is the **reduced state** of *ρ<sup>AB</sup>* on system *A*. The **reduced von Neumann entropy** of *ρ<sup>AB</sup>* with respect to system *A* is

$$\;S(\rho^A).$$

*S*(*ρ<sup>B</sup>*) is defined in the same way.

It can now be seen that the definition of quantum mutual information, corresponding to the classical definition, should be as follows.

$$\; I(A\!:\!B) := S(\rho^A) + S(\rho^B) - S(\rho^{AB}).$$

Quantum mutual information can be interpreted the same way as in the classical case: it can be shown that

$$I(A\!:\!B) = S(\rho^{AB} \| \rho^A \otimes \rho^B)$$

where $S(\cdot \| \cdot)$ denotes [quantum relative entropy](wikipedia://en/5993806). Note that there is an alternative generalization of mutual information to the quantum case. The difference between the two for a given state is called [quantum discord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_discord), a measure for the quantum correlations of the state in question.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

When the state $\rho^{AB}$ is pure (and thus $S(\rho^{AB})=0$), the mutual information is twice the [entanglement entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entanglement_entropy) of the state:

$$I(A\!:\!B) = S(\rho^A) + S(\rho^B) - S(\rho^{AB}) = S(\rho^A) + S(\rho^B) = 2S(\rho^A)$$

A positive quantum mutual information is not necessarily indicative of entanglement, however. A classical mixture of [separable states](wikipedia://en/2833933) will always have zero entanglement, but can have nonzero QMI, such as

$$\rho^{AB} = \frac{1}{2}\left(|00\rangle\langle00| + |11\rangle\langle11|\right)$$

$$\begin{aligned} I(A\!:\!B) &= S(\rho^A) + S(\rho^B) - S(\rho^{AB})\\ &= S\left(\frac{1}{2}(|0\rangle\langle0| + |1\rangle\langle1|)\right) + S\left(\frac{1}{2}(|0\rangle\langle0| + |1\rangle\langle1|)\right) - S\left(\frac{1}{2}(|00\rangle\langle00| + |11\rangle\langle11|)\right)\\ &= \log 2 +\log 2 - \log 2= \log 2 \end{aligned}$$

In this case, the state is merely a [classically correlated](wikipedia://en/157057) state.

:::
