# Karel Lambert

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karel Lambert"}

**Karel Lambert** (born 1928) is an American [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) and [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) at the [University of California, Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Irvine) and the [University of Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Salzburg). He has written extensively on the subject of [free logic](wikipedia://en/1635110), a term which he coined.[^o3][^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement cue=named-heading name="Lambert's law"}

## Lambert's law {#Lambert's_law}

**Lambert's law** is the major principle in any free [definite description theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definite_description_theory) that says: For all x, x = the y (A) if and only if (A(x/y) & for all y (if A then y = x)).

Free logic itself is an adjustment of a given standard [predicate logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predicate_logic) such as to relieve it of existential assumptions, and so make it a free logic. Taking [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)'s predicate logic in his *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)* as standard, one replaces universal instantiation, $\forall x \,\phi x \rightarrow \phi y$, with universal specification $(\forall x \,\phi x \land E!y \,\phi y) \rightarrow \phi z$. Thus universal statements, like "All men are mortal," or "Everything is a unicorn," do not presuppose that there are men or that there is anything. These would be symbolized, with the appropriate predicates, as $\forall x\,(Mx \rightarrow Lx)$ and $\forall x\, Ux$, which in *Principia Mathematica* entail $\exists x\,(Mx \land Lx)$ and $\exists x\,Ux$, but not in free logic. The truth of these last statements, when used in a free logic, depend on the [domain of quantification](wikipedia://en/229180), which may be the [null set](wikipedia://en/21520).

:::

## Published works {#Published_works}

- "Free Logic and the Concept of Existence", *[Notre Dame Journal of Formal Logic](wikipedia://en/40764984)*, VIII, numbers 1 and 2, April 1967.
- *Philosophical Applications of Free Logic*, New York: Oxford University Press, 1991, "A Theory of Definite Descriptions", pp. 17–27, details an account of Russell's Theory of Descriptions in free logic. In the process, he demonstrates how a formulation from [Hintikka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hintikka) allows for a contradiction by a correlate in logic to [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095). He introduces the predicate $(\lambda x)(\phi x \land \neg \phi x)$.
- *Free Logic. Selected Essays*, Cambridge University Press, 2003.
