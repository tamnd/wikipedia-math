# Free loop

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Free loop"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a **free loop** is a variant of the notion of a [loop](wikipedia://en/1189560). Whereas a loop has a distinguished point on it, called its basepoint, a free loop lacks such a distinguished point. Formally, let $X$ be a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). Then a free loop in $X$ is an [equivalence class](wikipedia://en/9260) of [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) from the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) $S^1$ to $X$. Two loops are equivalent if they differ by a reparameterization of the circle. That is, $f \sim g$ if there exists a [homeomorphism](wikipedia://en/13660) $\psi : S^1 \rightarrow S^1$ such that $g = f\circ\psi.$

:::

Thus, a free loop, as opposed to a based loop used in the definition of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004), is a map from the circle to the space without the basepoint-preserving restriction. Assuming the space is [path-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path-connected), free [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) classes of free loops correspond to [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) in the fundamental group.

Recently, interest in the space of all free loops $LX$ has grown with the advent of [string topology](wikipedia://en/28725806), i.e. the study of new [algebraic structures](wikipedia://en/106364) on the [homology](wikipedia://en/431041) of the free loop space.
