# Complex analytic variety

*Image: Cone intersects line.png.* A [cone](wikipedia://en/782427) is not a complex manifold, but it is a complex analytic variety.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex analytic variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), a **complex analytic variety**[^o2] or **complex analytic space** is a generalization of a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) that allows the presence of [singularities](wikipedia://en/581763). Complex analytic varieties are [locally ringed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_ringed_space) that are locally isomorphic to local model spaces, where a local model space is an open subset of the vanishing locus of a finite set of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110).

:::

Complex analytic varieties are analogous to [algebraic varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_varieties). Roughly speaking, a complex analytic variety is a zero locus of a set of a complex analytic function, while an algebraic variety is a zero locus of a set of a polynomial function.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Denote the [constant](wikipedia://en/2783581) [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) on a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with value $\mathbb{C}$ by $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$. A **$\mathbb{C}$-space** is a [locally ringed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_ringed_space) $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$, whose [structure sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_sheaf) is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) over $\underline{\mathbb{C}}$.

Choose an open subset $U$ of some [complex affine space](wikipedia://en/4850001) $\mathbb{C}^n$, and fix finitely many holomorphic functions $f_1,\dots,f_k$ in $U$. Let $X=V(f_1,\dots,f_k)$ be the common vanishing locus of these holomorphic functions, that is, $X=\{x\mid f_1(x)=\cdots=f_k(x)=0\}$. Define a sheaf of rings on $X$ by letting $\mathcal{O}_X$ be the restriction to $X$ of $\mathcal{O}_U/(f_1, \ldots, f_k)$, where $\mathcal{O}_U$ is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on $U$. Then the locally ringed $\mathbb{C}$-space $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ is a **local model space**.

A **complex analytic variety** is a locally ringed $\mathbb{C}$-space $(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ that is locally isomorphic to a local model space.

Morphisms of complex analytic varieties are defined to be morphisms of the underlying locally ringed spaces, they are also called holomorphic maps. A structure sheaf may have nilpotent elements;[^n1] if the structure sheaf is [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749), then the complex analytic space is called reduced.

An **associated complex analytic space (variety)** $X_h$ is such that:[^n1]

Let X be a [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754) of [finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheme_of_finite_type) over $\mathbb{C}$, and cover X with open affine subsets $Y_i = \operatorname{Spec} A_i$ ($X =\cup Y_i$) ([Spectrum of a ring](wikipedia://en/37291)). Then each $A_i$ is an [algebra of finite type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_of_finite_type) over $\mathbb{C}$, and $A_i \simeq \mathbb{C}[z_1, \dots, z_n]/(f_1,\dots, f_m)$, where $f_1,\dots, f_m$ are polynomials in $z_1, \dots, z_n$, which can be regarded as a holomorphic functions on $\mathbb{C}$. Therefore, their set of common zeros is the complex analytic subspace $(Y_i)_h \subseteq \mathbb{C}$. Here, the scheme X is obtained by [glueing](wikipedia://en/49016437) the data of the sets $Y_i$, and then the same data can be used for glueing the complex analytic spaces $(Y_i)_h$ into a complex analytic space $X_h$, so we call $X_h$ an associated complex analytic space with X. The complex analytic space X is reduced if and only if the associated complex analytic space $X_h$ is reduced.[^o7]
