# Continuum (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuum"}

In the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) field of [point-set topology](wikipedia://en/178649), a **continuum** (plural: "continua") is a [nonempty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonempty) [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [connected](wikipedia://en/6233) [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), or, less frequently, a compact connected [Hausdorff space](wikipedia://en/13637). **Continuum theory** is the branch of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) devoted to the study of continua.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

- A continuum that contains more than one point is called **nondegenerate**.
- A subset *A* of a continuum *X* such that *A* itself is a continuum is called a **subcontinuum** of *X*. A space homeomorphic to a subcontinuum of the [Euclidean plane](wikipedia://en/15575410) **R**<sup>2</sup> is called a **planar continuum**.
- A continuum *X* is **homogeneous** if for every two points *x* and *y* in *X*, there exists a homeomorphism *h*: *X* → *X* such that *h*(*x*) = *y*.
- A **Peano continuum** is a continuum that is [locally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_connected) at each point.
- An [indecomposable continuum](wikipedia://en/3261244) is a continuum that cannot be represented as the union of two proper subcontinua. A continuum *X* is **hereditarily indecomposable** if every subcontinuum of *X* is indecomposable.
- The **dimension** of a continuum usually means its [topological dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_dimension). A one-dimensional continuum is often called a **curve**.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- An **arc** is a space [homeomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeomorphic) to the [closed interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_interval) \[0,1\]. If *h*: \[0,1\] → *X* is a homeomorphism and *h*(0) = *p* and *h*(1) = *q* then *p* and *q* are called the **endpoints** of *X*; one also says that *X* is an arc from *p* to *q*. An arc is the simplest and most familiar type of a continuum. It is one-dimensional, [arcwise connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcwise_connected), and locally connected.
- The [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) is a subset of the plane that is the union of the graph of the function *f*(*x*) = sin(1/*x*), 0 \< *x* ≤ 1 with the segment −1 ≤ *y* ≤ 1 of the *y*-axis. It is a one-dimensional continuum that is not arcwise connected, and it is locally disconnected at the points along the *y*-axis.
- The [Warsaw circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_circle) is obtained by "closing up" the [topologist's sine curve](wikipedia://en/292224) by an arc connecting (0,−1) and (1,sin(1)). It is a one-dimensional continuum whose [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506) are all trivial, but it is not a [contractible space](wikipedia://en/1752414).

*Image: Warsaw Circle.png.* Warsaw circle

- A [component](wikipedia://en/6233#connected_components) of a particular compact Hausdorff space can be formulated in terms of continua; it is defined as a continuum that contains two arbitrary points of a compact and Hausdorff space.
- An ***n*-cell** is a space homeomorphic to the closed [ball](wikipedia://en/39782#n-ball) in the [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. It is contractible and is the simplest example of an *n*-dimensional continuum.
- An ***n*-sphere** is a space homeomorphic to the standard [n-sphere](wikipedia://en/39782) in the (*n* + 1)-dimensional Euclidean space. It is an *n*-dimensional homogeneous continuum that is not contractible, and therefore different from an *n*-cell.
- The [Hilbert cube](wikipedia://en/216389) is an infinite-dimensional continuum.
- [Solenoids](wikipedia://en/2217712) are among the simplest examples of indecomposable homogeneous continua. They are neither arcwise connected nor locally connected.
- The [Sierpinski carpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierpinski_carpet), also known as the *Sierpinski universal curve*, is a one-dimensional planar Peano continuum that contains a homeomorphic image of any one-dimensional planar continuum.
- The [pseudo-arc](wikipedia://en/3264412) is a homogeneous hereditarily indecomposable planar continuum.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

There are two fundamental techniques for constructing continua, by means of *nested intersections* and *inverse limits*.

- If {*X*<sub>*n*</sub>} is a nested family of continua, i.e. *X*<sub>*n*</sub> ⊇ *X*<sub>*n*+1</sub>, then their intersection is a continuum.
- If {(*X*<sub>*n*</sub>, *f*<sub>*n*</sub>)} is an inverse sequence of continua *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, called the **coordinate spaces**, together with [continuous maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) *f*<sub>*n*</sub>: *X*<sub>*n*+1</sub> → *X*<sub>*n*</sub>, called the **bonding maps**, then its [inverse limit](wikipedia://en/15109) is a continuum.

A finite or countable product of continua is a continuum.
