# Continuum (set theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Continuum"}

In the mathematical field of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), the **continuum** means the [real numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_numbers), or the corresponding (infinite) [cardinal number](wikipedia://en/6173), denoted by $\mathfrak{c}$.[^o2][^o3] [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) proved that the cardinality $\mathfrak{c}$ is larger than the smallest infinity, namely, $\aleph_0$. He also proved that $\mathfrak{c}$ is equal to $2^{\aleph_0}\!$, the cardinality of the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers).

:::

The *[cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901)* is the [size](wikipedia://en/6174) of the set of real numbers. The [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) is sometimes stated by saying that no [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174) lies between that of the continuum and that of the [natural numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_numbers), $\aleph_0$, or alternatively, that $\mathfrak{c} = \aleph_1$.[^o2]

## Linear continuum {#Linear_continuum}

According to [Raymond Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Wilder) (1965), there are four axioms that make a set *C* and the relation \< into a **linear continuum**:

- *C* is [simply ordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simply_ordered_set) with respect to \<.
- If \[*A,B*\] is a cut of *C*, then either *A* has a last element or *B* has a first element. (compare [Dedekind cut](wikipedia://en/45569))
- There exists a non-empty, [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable) subset *S* of *C* such that, if *x,y* ∈ *C* such that *x* \< *y*, then there exists *z* ∈ *S* such that *x* \< *z* \< *y*. ([separability axiom](wikipedia://en/27855))
- *C* has no first element and no last element. ([Unboundedness axiom](wikipedia://en/48258))

These axioms characterize the [order type](wikipedia://en/4499021) of the [real number line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_number_line).
