# Anders Björner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anders Björner"}

**Anders Björner** (born 17 December 1947)[^o3] is a Swedish professor emeritus in the Department of Mathematics at the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology), [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University) in 1979, under [Bernt Lindström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernt_Lindström). His research interests are in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), as well as the related areas of algebra, geometry, topology, and computer science.[^o4]

:::

His other positions included being director of the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452)[^o5] and [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of *[Acta Mathematica](wikipedia://en/6860708)*.[^o6]

Björner is a recognized expert in [algebraic](wikipedia://en/14835582) and [topological combinatorics](wikipedia://en/17633579).[^o7] He is a 1983 recipient of the [Pólya Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pólya_Prize_%28SIAM%29), and is a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) since 1999.

In 2025, he received the ‘Mathematics in Sweden Leader Award’.[^o8]

By mid-2025, he had 120 publications and his [D-index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-index) was 43.[^o8]
