# Benjamin Pulleyne

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Benjamin Pulleyne"}

**Benjamin Pulleyne**, *sometimes spelt* **Pullan** (30 September 1785 – 20 October 1861), was a mathematician, [Church of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England) clergyman, fellow of [Clare College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_College,_Cambridge), and [schoolmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoolmaster). For almost fifty years he was the Master of [Gresham's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham's_School), then usually known as Holt Grammar School.

:::

*Image: 20130808 Castle End from Kings Bridge.jpg.* Clare College, Cambridge

Born at [Scarborough, North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire), the son of Benjamin Pullan, a merchant, and his wife Elizabeth,[^o2][^o3] the young Pulleyne (the spelling of the name he used for himself) was educated at [Wakefield Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Grammar_School,_Wakefield) and was admitted to Clare College, Cambridge, on 2 February 1804.[^o4] He was elected a Cave Scholar on 19 January 1805 and was [Senior Optime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1808.[^o5]

He graduated [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1808, promoted by seniority to [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts_%28Oxford,_Cambridge,_and_Dublin%29) in 1811, and was a Fellow of his college from 1808 to 1809. Becoming a [deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) of the Church of England in 1808, he was ordained as a priest in 1810 by [Henry Bathurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bathurst_%28bishop%29), [Bishop of Norwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Norwich).[^o4] This was at a time when most academics at Oxford were obliged to lead celibate lives in college and had to resign their fellowships if they wished to marry.

*Image: Old School House, Holt, 1838.jpg.* Holt Grammar School in 1838

Pulleyne was [Master of Holt Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_masters_of_Gresham's_School) from 1809 to 1857,[^o4] the salary for which was £200 a year.[^o6] Having held the post for 48 years, he remains the school's longest-serving head since its foundation in 1555.[^o7] He was also Vicar of [Sheringham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheringham) from 1825 until his death, and of [Weybourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weybourne,_Norfolk) from 1845.[^o4]

John William Burgon, in *The Life and Times of Sir Thomas Gresham* (1839), says Holt school... is an ornament and a blessing to the county, and reflects much credit on the trustees and its worthy principal—the Rev. B. Pulleyne.[^o8]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

At the [census of March 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1851_United_Kingdom_census), Pulleyne was living at 38, Market Place, Holt, with his wife Rebecca, who was blind, his son Walter M. Pulleyne, aged 34, an [apothecary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothecary), his daughter in law, his seven-year-old granddaughter Anne, and two servants.[^o10] His wife, Rebecca Pulleyne, died at Holt on 13 March 1853.[^o11] On 12 July of the same year, at [Buckenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckenham), Norfolk, Pulleyne married Mary Dinah Partridge, a farmer's daughter.[^o3][^o12]

Having retired from his school, Pulleyne then lived at Sheringham, where he died in October 1861. He left a widow, Mary Dinah.[^o13]
