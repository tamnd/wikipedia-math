# Melnikov group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Melnikov group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Melnikov group** or **Melnikov subgroup** of a [profinite group](wikipedia://en/24266) $G$, denoted $M(G)$, is the intersection of all maximal open [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) of $G$.[^n1][^n2] If $G$ is nontrivial, then $M(G)$ is a proper closed subgroup invariant under every continuous automorphism of $G$ (that is, it is topologically [characteristic](wikipedia://en/7198)).[^n2] Fried and Jarden use the term "Melnikov group", while later literature also uses "Melnikov subgroup".[^n3][^n2]

:::

The construction is a normal-subgroup analogue of the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) $\Phi(G)$. Whereas the Frattini subgroup is defined using all maximal open subgroups, the Melnikov group uses only maximal open *normal* subgroups.[^n1] Equivalently, $M(G)$ is the intersection of the kernels of all continuous epimorphisms from $G$ onto finite [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600). It therefore records the part of $G$ that is invisible in every finite simple quotient.[^n1]

## Definition and simple quotients {#Definition_and_simple_quotients}

Throughout, homomorphisms of profinite groups are understood to be continuous. A proper open normal subgroup $N$ of $G$ is **maximal open normal** if there is no proper open normal subgroup of $G$ strictly between $N$ and $G$. The Melnikov group is

$M(G)=\bigcap\{N\trianglelefteq G:N\text{ is maximal open normal in }G\}.$[^n3]

If $N$ is maximal open normal, then $G/N$ is a finite simple group. Conversely, if $S$ is a finite simple group and $\varphi:G\twoheadrightarrow S$ is an epimorphism, then $\ker\varphi$ is maximal open normal. Thus

$M(G)=\bigcap\{\ker\varphi:\varphi:G\twoheadrightarrow S,\; S\text{ is a finite simple group}\}.$[^n3]

For a finite simple group $S$, define

$$M_S(G)=\bigcap\{N\trianglelefteq G:G/N\cong S\},$$

with $M_S(G)=G$ when $G$ has no quotient isomorphic to $S$. Then

$$M(G)=\bigcap_S M_S(G).$$

For each $S$, the quotient $G/M_S(G)$ is a [Cartesian power](wikipedia://en/3706015) of $S$. More precisely,

$$G/M(G)\cong\prod_S S^{r_G(S)},$$

where $S$ ranges over the isomorphism classes of finite simple groups and $r_G(S)$ is the $S$-rank of $G$, equivalently the cardinal number of copies of $S$ occurring in $G/M_S(G)$.[^n1][^n4] In particular,

$M(G)=1$ if and only if $G$ is a Cartesian product of finite simple groups.[^n3]

This also characterizes $G/M(G)$ as the largest quotient of $G$ that is a Cartesian product of finite simple groups: every homomorphism from $G$ onto such a product factors through $G/M(G)$.[^n1]

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

The Melnikov group has several useful functorial and normal-generation properties. Let $G$ and $H$ be profinite groups.[^n1]

- If $\alpha:G\twoheadrightarrow H$ is an epimorphism, then $\alpha(M(G))=M(H)$.
- If $N$ is a closed normal subgroup of $G$, then $M(N)\leq M(G)$.
- If $N$ is a closed normal subgroup of $G$ and $NM(G)=G$, then $N=G$.

The last property expresses the role of $M(G)$ in normal generation: adjoining $M(G)$ to a proper closed normal subgroup cannot make it equal to the whole group.[^n3]

:::

## Relation with the Frattini subgroup {#Relation_with_the_Frattini_subgroup}

For every profinite group $G$, the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) is contained in the Melnikov group:

$\Phi(G)\leq M(G).$[^n3]

The containment can be strict. For the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_3$, viewed as a finite profinite group, the only maximal proper normal subgroup is $A_3$, so

$$M(S_3)=A_3,$$

whereas $\Phi(S_3)=1$.[^n3]

For [pro-$p$ groups](wikipedia://en/1842075), however, the two constructions agree. Every maximal open subgroup of a pro-$p$ group is normal and has index $p$, and consequently

$$M(G)=\Phi(G)$$

for every pro-$p$ group $G$.[^n3]

## Melnikov covers {#Melnikov_covers}

An epimorphism $\alpha:G\twoheadrightarrow H$ of profinite groups is called a **Melnikov cover** if

$\ker\alpha\leq M(G).$[^n5]

This condition has a normal-generation interpretation: $\alpha$ is a Melnikov cover if and only if every closed normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ satisfying $\alpha(N)=H$ is equal to $G$. Melnikov covers are closed under composition. Moreover, a Melnikov cover induces an isomorphism

$G/M(G)\;\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\;H/M(H).$[^n5]

Thus a Melnikov cover preserves the quotient obtained after factoring out the Melnikov subgroup.

::: {#o28 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

The Melnikov subgroup occurs in constructions that separate abelian and simple quotients of profinite groups. Bary-Soroker, Fehm and Wiese define a generalized derived subgroup $D(G)$ satisfying

$$D(G)=M(G)\cap G',$$

where $G'$ is the [commutator subgroup](wikipedia://en/8847). Iterating this operation gives their *abelian-simple length*, which they use in criteria for the preservation of the [Hilbertian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbertian_field) property in algebraic extensions.[^n6]

Also, this subgroup appears in modern structure theory for profinite groups[^n7]

:::
