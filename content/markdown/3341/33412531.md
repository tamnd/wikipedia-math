# Sigma-martingale

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sigma-martingale"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **sigma-martingale** is a [semimartingale](wikipedia://en/9653775) with an integral representation. Sigma-martingales were introduced by C.S. Chou and M. Emery in 1977 and 1978.[^o4] In [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics), sigma-martingales appear in the [fundamental theorem of asset pricing](wikipedia://en/442967) as an equivalent condition to [no free lunch with vanishing risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_free_lunch_with_vanishing_risk) (a no-[arbitrage](wikipedia://en/3162) condition).[^o2]

:::

## Mathematical definition {#Mathematical_definition}

An $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) $X:\Omega\times[0,T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is a *sigma-martingale* if it is a [semimartingale](wikipedia://en/9653775) and there exists an $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued [martingale](wikipedia://en/274536) *M* and an *M*-[integrable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ito_integral) [predictable process](wikipedia://en/33414015) $\phi$ with values in $\mathbb{R}_+$ such that

$X = \int_{0}^{\cdot}\phi dM,$[^o4]

where integration is understood in the sense of [Ito calculus](wikipedia://en/1498680).
