# Cyclical monotonicity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cyclical monotonicity"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **cyclical monotonicity** is a generalization of the notion of [monotonicity](wikipedia://en/48260) to the case of [vector-valued function](wikipedia://en/3675281).[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ denote the inner product on an [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856) $X$ and let $U$ be a [nonempty](wikipedia://en/9566) subset of $X$. A [correspondence](wikipedia://en/12506030) $f: U \rightrightarrows X$ is called *cyclically monotone* if for every set of points $x_1,\dots,x_{m+1} \in U$ with $x_{m+1}=x_1$ it holds that $\sum_{k=1}^m \langle x_{k+1},f(x_{k+1})-f(x_k)\rangle\geq 0.$[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

For the case of scalar functions of one variable the definition above is equivalent to usual [monotonicity](wikipedia://en/48260). [Gradients](wikipedia://en/12461) of [convex functions](wikipedia://en/245568) are cyclically monotone. In fact, the [converse](wikipedia://en/155941) is true. Suppose $U$ is [convex](wikipedia://en/6292) and $f: U \rightrightarrows \mathbb{R}^n$ is a correspondence with nonempty values. Then if $f$ is cyclically monotone, there exists an [upper semicontinuous](wikipedia://en/57824) convex function $F:U\to \mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x)\subset \partial F(x)$ for every $x\in U$, where $\partial F(x)$ denotes the [subgradient](wikipedia://en/3462862) of $F$ at $x$.[^o9]

:::
