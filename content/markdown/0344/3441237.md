# Pseudocircle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudocircle"}

The **pseudocircle** is the [finite topological space](wikipedia://en/14682596) *X* consisting of four distinct points {*a*,*b*,*c*,*d*} with the following [non-Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) [topology](wikipedia://en/30450):

$$\{\{a,b,c,d\}, \{a,b,c\}, \{a,b,d\}, \{a,b\}, \{a\}, \{b\}, \varnothing\}.$$

:::

This topology corresponds to the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) $a<c,\ b<c,\ a<d,\ b<d$ where the [open sets](wikipedia://en/39358) are downward-closed sets. *X* is highly [pathological](wikipedia://en/372742) from the usual viewpoint of [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649), as it fails to satisfy any [separation axiom](wikipedia://en/40117302) besides [T<sub>0</sub>](wikipedia://en/68121). However, from the viewpoint of [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801), *X* has the remarkable property that it is indistinguishable from the [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) S<sup>1</sup>. More precisely, the [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function_%28topology%29) $f$ from S<sup>1</sup> to *X* (where we think of S<sup>1</sup> as the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) in $\mathbb{R}^2$) given by

$$f(x,y) = \begin{cases}a,& x<0\\ b,& x>0\\ c,& (x,y)=(0,1)\\ d,& (x,y)=(0,-1)\end{cases}$$

is a [weak homotopy equivalence](wikipedia://en/976793); that is, $f$ induces an [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828) on all [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506). It follows[^o2] that $f$ also induces an isomorphism on [singular homology and cohomology](wikipedia://en/431041), and more generally an isomorphism on all ordinary or extraordinary [homology and cohomology theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) (e.g., [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748)).

This can be [proven](wikipedia://en/82285) using the following observation. Like S<sup>1</sup>, *X* is the [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of two [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible) open sets {*a*,*b*,*c*} and {*a*,*b*,*d*} whose [intersection](wikipedia://en/23476429) {*a*,*b*} is also the union of two [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) contractible open sets {*a*} and {*b*}. So, like S<sup>1</sup>, the result follows from the [groupoid](wikipedia://en/12543) [Seifert-van Kampen theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifert-van_Kampen_theorem), as in the book *Topology and Groupoids*.[^o3]

More generally, McCord has shown that, for any finite [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105) *K*, there is a finite [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) *X*<sub>*K*</sub> which has the same weak homotopy type as the [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/723105) |*K*| of *K*. More precisely, there is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) taking *K* to *X*<sub>*K*</sub>, from the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of finite simplicial complexes and simplicial maps and a [natural](wikipedia://en/45022) weak homotopy equivalence from |*K*| to *X*<sub>*K*</sub>.[^o4]
