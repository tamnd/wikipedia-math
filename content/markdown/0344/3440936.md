# Residue-class-wise affine group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Residue-class-wise affine group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), **residue-class-wise affine** [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) are certain [permutation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_groups) [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on $\mathbb{Z}$ (the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563)), whose elements are [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942) residue-class-wise affine [mappings](wikipedia://en/516931).

:::

A mapping $f: \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}$ is called **residue-class-wise affine** if there is a nonzero integer $m$ such that the restrictions of $f$ to the [residue classes](wikipedia://en/20087#Congruence) (mod $m$) are all [affine](wikipedia://en/38449). This means that for any residue class $r(m) \in \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$ there are coefficients $a_{r(m)}, b_{r(m)}, c_{r(m)} \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that the [restriction](wikipedia://en/1589032) of the mapping $f$ to the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) $r(m) = \{r + km \mid k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is given by

$$f|_{r(m)}: r(m) \rightarrow \mathbb{Z}, \ n \mapsto \frac{a_{r(m)} \cdot n + b_{r(m)}}{c_{r(m)}}.$$

Residue-class-wise affine groups are [countable](wikipedia://en/6026), and they are accessible to [computational investigations](wikipedia://en/618584). Many of them act [multiply transitively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiply_transitive) on $\mathbb{Z}$ or on subsets thereof.

A particularly basic type of residue-class-wise affine [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) are the **class transpositions**: given [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) residue classes $r_1(m_1)$ and $r_2(m_2)$, the corresponding **class transposition** is the permutation of $\mathbb{Z}$ which interchanges $r_1+km_1$ and $r_2+km_2$ for every $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ and which [fixes](wikipedia://en/449738) everything else. Here it is assumed that $0 \leq r_1 < m_1$ and that $0 \leq r_2 < m_2$.

The set of all class transpositions of $\mathbb{Z}$ [generates](wikipedia://en/99945) a countable [simple group](wikipedia://en/47600) which has the following properties:

- It is not [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/99945#Finitely_generated_group).
- Every [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707), every [free product](wikipedia://en/360036) of finite groups and every [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of finite rank embeds into it.
- The class of its [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011) is closed under taking [direct products](wikipedia://en/3706015), under taking [wreath products](wikipedia://en/146625) with finite groups, and under taking restricted wreath products with the infinite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327).
- It has finitely generated subgroups which do not have [finite presentations](wikipedia://en/99494).
- It has finitely generated subgroups with [algorithmically unsolvable](wikipedia://en/15631055) [membership problem](wikipedia://en/8336).
- It has an [uncountable](wikipedia://en/32248) series of simple subgroups which is parametrized by the sets of odd [primes](wikipedia://en/23666).

It is straightforward to generalize the notion of a residue-class-wise affine group to groups acting on suitable [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) other than $\mathbb{Z}$, though only little work in this direction has been done so far.

See also the [Collatz conjecture](wikipedia://en/37895), which is an assertion about a [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873), but not [injective](wikipedia://en/45196) residue-class-wise affine mapping.
