# Coplanarity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coplanarity"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a set of points in space are **coplanar** if there exists a geometric [plane](wikipedia://en/19469852) that contains them all. For example, three points are always coplanar, and if the points are distinct and [non-collinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-collinear_points), the plane they determine is unique. However, a set of four or more distinct points will, in general, not lie in a single plane.

*Image: Coplanar points.svg.* An example of coplanar points

Two [lines](wikipedia://en/946975) in three-dimensional space are coplanar if there is a plane that includes them both. This occurs if the lines are [parallel](wikipedia://en/664497), or if they [intersect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line-line_intersection) each other. Two lines that are not coplanar are called [skew lines](wikipedia://en/1975821).

:::

[Distance geometry](wikipedia://en/654387) provides a solution technique for the problem of determining whether a set of points is coplanar, knowing only the distances between them.

## Properties in three dimensions {#Properties_in_three_dimensions}

In three-dimensional space, two [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) vectors with the same initial point determine a plane through that point. Their [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) is a [normal](wikipedia://en/173224) vector to that plane, and any vector [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) to this cross product through the initial point will lie in the plane.[^o3] This leads to the following coplanarity test using a [scalar triple product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_triple_product):

Four distinct points, $x_{1}, x_{2}, x_{3}, x_{4}$, are coplanar if and only if,

$$[(x_2 - x_1) \times (x_4 - x_1)] \cdot (x_3 - x_1) = 0.$$

which is also equivalent to

$$(x_2 - x_1) \cdot [(x_4 - x_1) \times (x_3 - x_1)] = 0.$$

If three vectors $\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, \mathbf{c}$ are coplanar, then if $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0$ (i.e., $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are orthogonal) then

$$(\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{\hat a})\mathbf{\hat a} + (\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{\hat b})\mathbf{\hat b} = \mathbf{c},$$

where $\mathbf \hat a$ denotes the [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) in the [direction](wikipedia://en/54516626) of $\mathbf{a}$. That is, the [vector projections](wikipedia://en/1508507) of $\mathbf{c}$ on $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ add to give the original $\mathbf{c}$.

## Coplanarity of points in *n* dimensions whose coordinates are given {#Coplanarity_of_points_in_n_dimensions_whose_coordinates_are_given}

Since three or fewer points are always coplanar, the problem of determining when a set of points is coplanar is generally of interest only when there are at least four points involved. In the case that there are exactly four points, several *ad hoc* methods can be employed, but a general method that works for any number of points uses vector methods and the property that a plane is determined by two [linearly independent vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent_vectors).

In an $n$-dimensional space where $n \ge 3$, a set of $k$ points $\{p_0,\ p_1,\ \dots,\ p_{k-1}\}$ are coplanar if and only if the matrix of their relative differences, that is, the matrix whose columns (or rows) are the vectors $\overrightarrow{p_0 p_1},\ \overrightarrow{p_0 p_2},\ \dots,\ \overrightarrow{p_0 p_{k-1}}$ is of [rank](wikipedia://en/26561) 2 or less.

For example, given four points

$$\begin{aligned} X &= (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n), \\ Y &= (y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n), \\ Z &= (z_1, z_2, \dots, z_n), \\ W &= (w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n), \end{aligned}$$

if the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859)

$$\begin{bmatrix} x_1 - w_1 & x_2 - w_2 & \dots & x_n - w_n \\ y_1 - w_1 & y_2 - w_2 & \dots & y_n - w_n \\ z_1 - w_1 & z_2 - w_2 & \dots & z_n - w_n \\ \end{bmatrix}$$

is of rank 2 or less, the four points are coplanar.

In the special case of a plane that contains the origin, the property can be simplified in the following way: A set of $k$ points and the origin are coplanar if and only if the matrix of the coordinates of the $k$ points is of rank 2 or less.

## Geometric shapes {#Geometric_shapes}

A [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) is a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621) whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/11308417) are not coplanar. Such a polygon must have at least four vertices; there are no skew triangles.

A [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) that has positive [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) has vertices that are not all coplanar.
