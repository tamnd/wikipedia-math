# Encrypted key exchange

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Encrypted key exchange"}

*Image: EKE scheme.svg.* DH-EKE scheme

**Encrypted Key Exchange** (also known as **EKE**) is a family of [password-authenticated key agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Password-authenticated_key_agreement) methods described by [Steven M. Bellovin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_M._Bellovin) and Michael Merritt.[^o2] Although several of the forms of EKE in this paper were later found to be flawed , the surviving, refined, and enhanced forms of EKE effectively make this the first method to amplify a shared [password](wikipedia://en/24304) into a shared key, where the [shared key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_key) may subsequently be used to provide a [zero-knowledge password proof](wikipedia://en/2152316) or other functions.

:::

In the most general form of EKE, at least one party encrypts an ephemeral (one-time) public key using a password, and sends it to a second party, who decrypts it and uses it to negotiate a shared key with the first party.

A second paper describes Augmented-EKE,[^o3] and introduced the concept of **augmented** [password-authenticated key agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Password-authenticated_key_agreement) for client/server scenarios. Augmented methods have the added goal of ensuring that password verification data stolen from a server cannot be used by an attacker to masquerade as the client, unless the attacker first determines the password (e.g. by performing a brute force attack on the stolen data).

A version of EKE based on [Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/7903), known as DH-EKE, has survived attack and has led to improved variations, such as the PAK family of methods in [IEEE P1363.2](wikipedia://en/1644154).

Since the [US patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_patent) on EKE expired in late 2011, an [EAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensible_Authentication_Protocol) authentication method using EKE was published as an IETF RFC.[^o4] The EAP method uses the [Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/7903) variant of EKE.

## Patents {#Patents}

5241599, owned by [Lucent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent), describes the initial EKE method. It expired in October 2011. 5440635, owned by [Lucent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent), describes the augmented EKE method. It expired in August 2013.
