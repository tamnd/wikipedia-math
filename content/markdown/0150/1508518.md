# Scalar projection

*Image: Dot Product.svg.* If 0° ≤ *θ* ≤ 90°, as in this case, the scalar projection of **a** on **b** coincides with the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of the [vector projection](wikipedia://en/1508507).

*Image: Projection and rejection.svg.* [Vector projection](wikipedia://en/1508507) of **a** on **b** (**a**<sub>1</sub>), and vector rejection of **a** from **b** (**a**<sub>2</sub>).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Scalar projection"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **scalar projection** of a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) $\mathbf{a}$ on (or onto) a vector $\mathbf{b},$ also known as the **scalar resolute** of $\mathbf{a}$ in the [direction](wikipedia://en/54516626) of $\mathbf{b},$ is given by:

:::

$$s = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|\cos\theta = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{\hat b},$$

where the operator $\cdot$ denotes a [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093), $\hat{\mathbf{b}}$ is the [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) in the direction of $\mathbf{b},$ $\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|$ is the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of $\mathbf{a},$ and $\theta$ is the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.[^o2]

The term **scalar component** refers sometimes to scalar projection, as, in [Cartesian coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_coordinates), the [components of a vector](wikipedia://en/18420) are the scalar projections in the directions of the [coordinate axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinate_axes).

The scalar projection is a [scalar](wikipedia://en/3588331), equal to the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$, with a negative sign if the projection has an opposite direction with respect to $\mathbf{b}$.

Multiplying the scalar projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ by $\mathbf{\hat b}$ converts it into the above-mentioned orthogonal projection, also called [vector projection](wikipedia://en/1508507) of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$.

## Definition based on angle *θ* {#Definition_based_on_angle_θ}

If the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) $\theta$ between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is known, the scalar projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ can be computed using

$s = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \cos \theta .$ ($s = \left\|\mathbf{a}_1\right\|$ in the figure)

The formula above can be inverted to obtain the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196#Dot_product), *θ*.

## Definition in terms of a and b {#Definition_in_terms_of_a_and_b}

When $\theta$ is not known, the [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine) of $\theta$ can be computed in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b},$ by the following property of the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$:

$$\frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|} = \cos \theta$$

By this property, the definition of the scalar projection $s$ becomes:

$$s = \left\|\mathbf{a}_1\right\| = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \cos \theta = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\| }\,$$

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The scalar projection has a negative sign if $90^\circ < \theta \le 180^\circ$. It coincides with the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of the corresponding [vector projection](wikipedia://en/1508507) if the angle is smaller than 90°. More exactly, if the vector projection is denoted $\mathbf{a}_1$ and its length $\left\|\mathbf{a}_1\right\|$:

$s = \left\|\mathbf{a}_1\right\|$ if $0^\circ \le \theta \le 90^\circ,$

$s = -\left\|\mathbf{a}_1\right\|$ if $90^\circ < \theta \le 180^\circ.$

:::
