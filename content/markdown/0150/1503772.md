# Christoph Gudermann

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Christoph Gudermann"}

**Christoph Gudermann** (25 March 1798 – 25 September 1852) was a German mathematician noted for introducing the [Gudermannian function](wikipedia://en/303274) and the concept of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652), and for being the teacher of [Karl Weierstrass](wikipedia://en/93459), who was greatly influenced by Gudermann's course on [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939) in 1839–1840, the first such course to be taught in any institute.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Gudermann was born in [Vienenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienenburg). He was the son of a school teacher and became a teacher himself after studying at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen). He began his teaching career in [Kleve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleve) and then transferred to a school in [Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Münster).

Gudermann introduced the concept of [uniform convergence](wikipedia://en/50652) in an 1838 paper on elliptic functions, but only observed it informally, neither formalizing it nor using it in his proofs.[^o4] Instead, Weierstrass elaborated and applied uniform convergence.

His researches into [spherical geometry](wikipedia://en/174026) and [special functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_function) focused on particular cases, so that he did not receive the credit given to those who published more general works. The [Gudermannian function](wikipedia://en/303274), or hyperbolic amplitude, is named after him.

Gudermann died in Münster.
