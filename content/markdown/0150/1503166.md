# Terminal yield

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Terminal yield"}

In [formal language theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_language_theory), the **terminal yield** (or **fringe**) of a [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_data_structure) is the sequence of leaves encountered in an ordered walk of the tree.[^o2]

:::

Parse trees and/or derivation trees are encountered in the study of [phrase structure grammars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrase_structure_grammar) such as [context-free grammars](wikipedia://en/6759) or [linear grammars](wikipedia://en/1798692). The leaves of a derivation tree for a [formal grammar](wikipedia://en/18020716) *G* are the [terminal symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_symbol) of that grammar, and the internal nodes the [nonterminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonterminal) or variable symbols. One can read off the corresponding terminal string by performing an ordered [tree traversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_traversal) and recording the terminal symbols in the order they are encountered. The resulting sequence of terminals is a string of the language *L*(*G*) generated by the grammar *G*.
