# Vector projection

Projection (linear algebra)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vector projection"}

The **vector projection**, also known as the **vector component** or **vector resolution**, of a [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometry%29) $\mathbf{a}$ on (or onto) a non-[zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_vector) vector $\mathbf{b}$ is the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) of $\mathbf{a}$ onto a [straight line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) parallel to $\mathbf{b}$. The projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$ is often written as $\operatorname{proj}_\mathbf{b} \mathbf{a}$ or $\mathbf{a}_{\parallel\mathbf{b}}$.

:::

The vector component or vector resolute of $\mathbf{a}$ [perpendicular](wikipedia://en/76944) to $\mathbf{b}$, sometimes also called the **vector rejection** of $\mathbf{a}$ *from* $\mathbf{b}$ (denoted $\operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}$ or $\mathbf{a}_{\perp\mathbf{b}}$),[^o2] is the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto the [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) (or, in general, [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862)) that is [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) to $\mathbf{b}$. Since both $\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}$ and $\operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}$ are vectors, and their sum is equal to $\mathbf{a}$, the rejection of $\mathbf{a}$ from $\mathbf{b}$ is given by:

$$\operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a} - \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}.$$

*Image: Projection and rejection.svg.* Projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ (**a**<sub>1</sub>), and rejection of $\mathbf{a}$ from $\mathbf{b}$ (**a**<sub>2</sub>)

*Image: Projection and rejection 2.svg.* When $90^{\circ} < \theta \le 180^{\circ}$, $\mathbf{a}_{1}$ has an opposite direction with respect to $\mathbf{b}$.

To simplify notation, this article defines $\mathbf{a}_1 := \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{a}_2 := \operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a}.$ Thus, the vector $\mathbf{a}_1$ is parallel to $\mathbf{b},$ the vector $\mathbf{a}_2$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{b},$ and $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{a}_1 + \mathbf{a}_2.$

The projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$ can be decomposed into a direction and a scalar magnitude by writing it as $\mathbf{a}_1 = a_1\mathbf{\hat b}$ where $a_1$ is a scalar, called the *[scalar projection](wikipedia://en/1508518)* of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{b\text{̂}}$ is the [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) in the direction of $\mathbf{b}$. The scalar projection is defined as[^o3]

$$a_1 = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|\cos\theta = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{\hat b}$$

where the operator **⋅** denotes a [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093), ‖**a**‖ is the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of $\mathbf{a}$, and *θ* is the [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. The scalar projection is equal in absolute value to the length of the vector projection, with a minus sign if the direction of the projection is [opposite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposite_vector) to the direction of $\mathbf{b}$, that is, if the angle between the vectors is more than 90 degrees.

The vector projection can be calculated using the dot product of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ as:

$$\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf{b}} \mathbf{a} = \left(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{\hat b}\right) \mathbf{\hat b} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\| } \frac {\mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|^2}{\mathbf{b}} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{b}} ~ .$$

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Notation"}

## Notation {#Notation}

This article uses the convention that vectors are denoted in a bold font (e.g. $\mathbf{a}_{1}$), and scalars are written in normal font (e.g. *a*<sub>1</sub>).

The dot product of vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is written as $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}$, the norm of $\mathbf{a}$ is written ‖**a**‖, and the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is denoted by *θ*.

:::

## Definitions based on angle *theta* {#Definitions_based_on_angle_theta}

### Scalar projection {#Scalar_projection}

The scalar projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is a scalar equal to

$$a_1 = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \cos \theta ,$$

where *θ* is the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$.

A scalar projection can be used as a [scale factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_factor) to compute the corresponding vector projection.

### Vector projection {#Vector_projection}

The vector projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is a vector whose magnitude is the scalar projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ with the same direction as $\mathbf{b}$. Namely, it is defined as

$$\mathbf{a}_1 = a_1 \mathbf{\hat b} = (\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \cos \theta) \mathbf{\hat b}$$

where $a_1$ is the corresponding scalar projection, as defined above, and $\mathbf{\hat b}$ is the [unit vector](wikipedia://en/167053) with the same direction as $\mathbf{b}$:

$$\mathbf{\hat b} = \frac {\mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|}$$

### Vector rejection {#Vector_rejection}

By definition, the vector rejection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is:

$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{a}_1$$

Hence,

$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \mathbf{a} - \left(\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \cos \theta\right) \mathbf{\hat b}$$

## Definitions in terms of a and b {#Definitions_in_terms_of_a_and_b}

When $\theta$ is not known, the cosine of $\theta$ can be computed in terms of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, by the following property of the [dot product](wikipedia://en/157093) $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$

$$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \left\|\mathbf{b}\right\| \cos \theta$$

### Scalar projection {#Scalar_projection}

By the above-mentioned property of the dot product, the definition of the scalar projection becomes:[^o3]

$${\displaystyle a_{1}=\left\|\mathbf {a} \right\|\cos \theta ={\frac {\mathbf {a} \cdot \mathbf {b} }{\left\|\mathbf {b} \right\|}}.}$$

In two dimensions, this becomes

$$a_1 = \frac {\mathbf{a}_x \mathbf{b}_x + \mathbf{a}_y \mathbf{b}_y} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|}.$$

### Vector projection {#Vector_projection}

Similarly, the definition of the vector projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}$ becomes:[^o3]

$$\mathbf{a}_1 = a_1 \mathbf{\hat b} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\| } \frac {\mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|},$$

which is equivalent to either

$$\mathbf{a}_1 = \left(\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{\hat b}\right) \mathbf{\hat b},$$

or[^o13]

$$\mathbf{a}_1 = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|^2}{\mathbf{b}} = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{b}} ~ .$$

### Scalar rejection {#Scalar_rejection}

In two dimensions, the scalar rejection is equivalent to the projection of $\mathbf{a}$ onto $\mathbf{b}^\perp = \begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf{b}_y & \mathbf{b}_x\end{pmatrix}$, which is $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{b}_x & \mathbf{b}_y\end{pmatrix}$ rotated 90° to the left. Hence,

$$a_2 = \left\|\mathbf{a}\right\| \sin \theta = \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}^\perp} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\|} = \frac {\mathbf{a}_y \mathbf{b}_x - \mathbf{a}_x \mathbf{b}_y} {\left\|\mathbf{b}\right\| }.$$

Such a dot product is called the "perp dot product."

### Vector rejection {#Vector_rejection}

By definition,

$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \mathbf{a} - \mathbf{a}_1$$

Hence,

$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \mathbf{a} - \frac {\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}} {\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{b}}.$$

By using the Scalar rejection using the perp dot product this gives

$$\mathbf{a}_2 = \frac{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}^\perp}{\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{b}}\mathbf{b}^\perp$$

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Dot Product.svg.* If 0° ≤ *θ* ≤ 90°, as in this case, the [scalar projection](wikipedia://en/1508518) of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ coincides with the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) of the vector projection.

### Scalar projection {#Scalar_projection}

The scalar projection $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is a scalar which has a negative sign if [90 degrees](wikipedia://en/76956) \< *θ* ≤ [180 degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_angle). It coincides with the [length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_norm) $\|\mathbf{c}\|$ of the vector projection if the angle is smaller than 90°. More exactly:

- $a_{1} = \|\mathbf{a}_{1}\|$ if $0^{\circ} \le \theta \le 90^{\circ}$,
- $a_{1} = -\|\mathbf{a}_{1}\|$ if $90^{\circ} < \theta \le 180^{\circ}$.

### Vector projection {#Vector_projection}

The vector projection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is a vector $\mathbf{a}_{1}$ which is either null or parallel to $\mathbf{b}$. More exactly:

- $\mathbf{a}_{1} = \mathbf{0}$ if $\theta = 90^{\circ}$,
- $\mathbf{a}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ have the same direction if $0^{\circ} \le \theta < 90^{\circ}$,
- $\mathbf{a}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ have opposite directions if $90^{\circ} < \theta \le 180^{\circ}$.

### Vector rejection {#Vector_rejection}

The vector rejection of $\mathbf{a}$ on $\mathbf{b}$ is a vector $\mathbf{a}_{2}$ which is either null or orthogonal to $\mathbf{b}$. More exactly:

- $\mathbf{a}_{2} = \mathbf{0}$ if $\theta = 0^{\circ}$ or $\theta = 180^{\circ}$,
- $\mathbf{a}_{2}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{b}$ if $0 < \theta < 180^{\circ}$,

## Uses {#Uses}

The vector projection is an important operation in the [Gram–Schmidt](wikipedia://en/82361) [orthonormalization](wikipedia://en/194743) of [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) [bases](wikipedia://en/18420). It is also used in the [separating axis theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separating_axis_theorem) to detect whether two convex shapes intersect.

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

Since the notions of vector [length](wikipedia://en/18542) and [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) between vectors can be generalized to any *n*-dimensional [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856), this is also true for the notions of orthogonal projection of a vector, projection of a vector onto another, and rejection of a vector from another.

::: {#o27 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Vector projection on a plane"}

### Vector projection on a plane {#Vector_projection_on_a_plane}

In some cases, the inner product coincides with the dot product. Whenever they don't coincide, the inner product is used instead of the dot product in the formal definitions of projection and rejection. For a three-dimensional [inner product space](wikipedia://en/14856), the notions of projection of a vector onto another and rejection of a vector from another can be generalized to the notions of projection of a vector onto a [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29), and rejection of a vector from a plane.[^o28] The projection of a vector on a plane is its [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) on that plane. The rejection of a vector from a plane is its orthogonal projection on a straight line which is orthogonal to that plane. Both are vectors. The first is parallel to the plane, the second is orthogonal.

For a given vector and plane, the sum of projection and rejection is equal to the original vector. Similarly, for inner product spaces with more than three dimensions, the notions of projection onto a vector and rejection from a vector can be generalized to the notions of projection onto a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862), and rejection from a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862). In [geometric algebra](wikipedia://en/12939), they can be further generalized to the notions of [projection and rejection](wikipedia://en/12939#Projection_and_rejection) of a general multivector onto/from any invertible *k*-blade.
