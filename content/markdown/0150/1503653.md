# Donato Acciaioli

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donato Acciaioli"}

*Image: Borgo degli Albizi 18-20, Palazzo valori-altoviti (dei Visacci), erme di g.b. caccini, 1604 circa, piano terra 04 donato acciaiuoli 3.jpg.*

**Donato Acciaioli** (15 March 1428[^o2] – 28 August 1478) was an Italian scholar and statesman. He was known for his learning, especially in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) and [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and for his services to his native state, the [Republic of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Florence).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence), Italy. He was educated under the patronage or guidance of [Jacopo Piccolomini-Ammannati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Piccolomini-Ammannati) (1422–1479), who subsequently was named cardinal. He also putatively gained his knowledge of the classics from Lionardo and [Carlo Marsuppini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Marsuppini) (1399–1453)[^o5] and from the refugee scholar from Byzantium, [Giovanni Argiropolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Argiropolo).[^o6]

Having previously been entrusted with several important embassies, in 1473 he became [Gonfalonier of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonfalonier_of_Florence), one of the nine citizens selected by drawing lots every two months, who formed the government. He died at [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan) in 1478, when on his way to Paris to ask the aid of [Louis XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XI) on behalf of the Florentines against [Pope Sixtus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV). His body was taken back to Florence and buried in the church of the [Carthusian order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthusian_order) at the public expense, and his daughters were endowed by his fellow-citizens, since he had little in terms of wealth.[^o3]

He wrote Latin translations of some of [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch)'s *[Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_Lives)* (Florence, 1478); Commentaries on [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308)'s *Ethics,* *[Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_%28Aristotle%29), Physics,* and *[De anima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Soul)*;[^o7] the lives of [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal), [Scipio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scipio_Africanus) and [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) as well as the biography of the [grand seneschal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_seneschal) of the [Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples), [Niccolò Acciaioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccolò_Acciaioli) by [Matteo Palmieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Palmieri). In the work on Aristotle he had the cooperation of his master [John Argyropulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Argyropulus).[^o3]
