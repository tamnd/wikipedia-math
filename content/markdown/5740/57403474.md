# Lillian Pierce

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lillian Pierce"}

*Image: Lillian Beatrix Pierce Oberwolfach 2022.jpg.* Pierce at [Oberwolfach](wikipedia://en/1368669)

**Lillian Beatrix Pierce** is a mathematician whose research connects [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) with [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). She is a professor of mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Pierce was [home-schooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling) in [Fallbrook, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallbrook,_California) and began playing the [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin) at age four. By age 11 she began performing professionally as a violinist. As a teenager, she also started taking classes at a local [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college), accumulating so many units that some of the universities she applied to refused to consider her for freshman admission. She entered [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) majoring in mathematics but intending to pursue an [MD–PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MD–PhD) program; under the influence of faculty mentor and undergraduate thesis supervisor [Elias M. Stein](wikipedia://en/418073), her interests shifted towards pure mathematics. As an undergraduate, she also became an intern at the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939). She was Princeton's 2002 [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian) and became a [Rhodes Scholar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_Scholar), repeating two accomplishments of her brother [Niles Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niles_Pierce) from nine years earlier.

She earned a master's degree at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) in 2004. Returning to Princeton for doctoral study in mathematics, she completed her Ph.D. in 2009. Her dissertation, *Discrete Analogues in Harmonic Analysis*, was supervised by Stein.

## Career {#Career}

After postdoctoral studies with [Roger Heath-Brown](wikipedia://en/2984550) at Oxford and at the [Hausdorff Center for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/23690221) in Bonn, Germany, she became an assistant professor at Duke in 2014 and is now a full professor.

## Research {#Research}

Pierce was one of the first mathematicians to prove nontrivial [upper bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) on the number of [elements of finite order](wikipedia://en/144052) in an [ideal class group](wikipedia://en/150907).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

Pierce won the 2018 [Sadosky Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadosky_Prize) for research that "spans and connects a broad spectrum of problems ranging from [character sums](wikipedia://en/2986034) in number theory to [singular integral operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singular_integral_operators) in Euclidean spaces" including in particular "a polynomial [Carleson theorem](wikipedia://en/24105681) for manifolds". She received the 2019 [Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Early_Career_Award_for_Scientists_and_Engineers). She was elected a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society) in the class of 2021 "for contributions to number theory and harmonic analysis". The [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) named her to their 2025 Class of AWM Fellows.

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Her husband, Tobias Overath, also works at Duke as a neuroscientist.
