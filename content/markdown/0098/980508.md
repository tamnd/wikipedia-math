# Cubic graph

*Image: Petersen1 tiny.svg.* The [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) is a cubic graph.

*Image: Biclique K 3 3.svg.* The [complete bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/525320) $K_{3,3}$ is an example of a bicubic graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cubic graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), a **cubic graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) in which all [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) have [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) three. In other words, a cubic graph is a 3-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821). Cubic graphs are also called **trivalent graphs**.

:::

A **bicubic graph** is a cubic [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431).

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Symmetry"}

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

In 1932, [Ronald M. Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._M._Foster) began collecting examples of cubic [symmetric graphs](wikipedia://en/1577896), forming the start of the [Foster census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_census).[^o4] Many well-known individual graphs are cubic and symmetric, including the [utility graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water,_gas,_and_electricity), the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), the [Heawood graph](wikipedia://en/1396870), the [Möbius–Kantor graph](wikipedia://en/13396591), the [Pappus graph](wikipedia://en/7250518), the [Desargues graph](wikipedia://en/1578059), the [Nauru graph](wikipedia://en/24052345), the [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570), the [Tutte–Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/1396880), the [Dyck graph](wikipedia://en/24041180), the [Foster graph](wikipedia://en/11197065) and the [Biggs–Smith graph](wikipedia://en/24030130). [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) classified the symmetric cubic graphs by the smallest integer number *s* such that each two oriented paths of length *s* can be mapped to each other by exactly one symmetry of the graph. He showed that *s* is at most 5, and provided examples of graphs with each possible value of *s* from 1 to 5.[^o5]

[Semi-symmetric](wikipedia://en/2554240) cubic graphs include the [Gray graph](wikipedia://en/2554738) (the smallest semi-symmetric cubic graph), the [Ljubljana graph](wikipedia://en/24131381), and the [Tutte 12-cage](wikipedia://en/24384368).

The [Frucht graph](wikipedia://en/23784721) is one of the five smallest cubic graphs without any symmetries:[^o6] it possesses only a single [graph automorphism](wikipedia://en/15094186), the identity automorphism.[^o7]

:::

## Coloring and independent sets {#Coloring_and_independent_sets}

According to [Brooks' theorem](wikipedia://en/21042117) every connected cubic graph other than the [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) *K*<sub>4</sub> has a [vertex coloring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_coloring) with at most three colors. Therefore, every connected cubic graph other than *K*<sub>4</sub> has an [independent set](wikipedia://en/524501) of at least *n*/3 vertices, where *n* is the number of vertices in the graph: for instance, the largest color class in a 3-coloring has at least this many vertices.

According to [Vizing's theorem](wikipedia://en/5449464) every cubic graph needs either three or four colors for an [edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647). A 3-edge-coloring is known as a Tait coloring, and forms a partition of the edges of the graph into three [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031). By [Kőnig's line coloring theorem](wikipedia://en/5465118) every bicubic graph has a Tait coloring.

The bridgeless cubic graphs that do not have a Tait coloring are known as [snarks](wikipedia://en/727811). They include the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), [Tietze's graph](wikipedia://en/20058152), the [Blanuša snarks](wikipedia://en/24054041), the [flower snark](wikipedia://en/23759337), the [double-star snark](wikipedia://en/24093107), the [Szekeres snark](wikipedia://en/4776914) and the [Watkins snark](wikipedia://en/24064939). There is an infinite number of distinct snarks.[^o9]

## Topology and geometry {#Topology_and_geometry}

Cubic graphs arise naturally in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) in several ways. For example, the cubic graphs with *2g-2* vertices describe the different ways of cutting a [surface](wikipedia://en/50328015) of genus *g ≥ 2* into [pairs of pants](wikipedia://en/2812977). If one considers a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) to be a 1-dimensional [CW complex](wikipedia://en/353022), cubic graphs are *[generic](wikipedia://en/12886758)* in that most 1-cell attaching maps are disjoint from the 0-skeleton of the graph. Cubic graphs are also formed as the graphs of [simple polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470) in three dimensions, polyhedra such as the [regular dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/7149361) with the property that three faces meet at every vertex.

*Image: Graph-encoded map.svg.* Representation of a planar embedding as a graph-encoded map

An arbitrary [graph embedding](wikipedia://en/8149170) on a two-dimensional surface may be represented as a cubic graph structure known as a [graph-encoded map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph-encoded_map). In this structure, each vertex of a cubic graph represents a [flag](wikipedia://en/9352497) of the embedding, a mutually incident triple of a vertex, edge, and face of the surface. The three neighbors of each flag are the three flags that may be obtained from it by changing one of the members of this mutually incident triple and leaving the other two members unchanged.[^o11]

## Hamiltonicity {#Hamiltonicity}

There has been much research on [Hamiltonicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) of cubic graphs. In 1880, [P.G. Tait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guthrie_Tait) conjectured that every cubic [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270) has a [Hamiltonian circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_circuit). [William Thomas Tutte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thomas_Tutte) provided a counter-example to [Tait's conjecture](wikipedia://en/52435), the 46-vertex [Tutte graph](wikipedia://en/24511700), in 1946. In 1971, Tutte conjectured that all bicubic graphs are Hamiltonian. However, Joseph Horton provided a counterexample on 96 vertices, the [Horton graph](wikipedia://en/24116567).[^o13] Later, [Mark Ellingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ellingham) constructed two more counterexamples: the [Ellingham–Horton graphs](wikipedia://en/24175436).[^o14][^o15] [Barnette's conjecture](wikipedia://en/28059825), a still-open combination of Tait's and Tutte's conjecture, states that every bicubic polyhedral graph is Hamiltonian. When a cubic graph is Hamiltonian, [LCF notation](wikipedia://en/24186720) allows it to be represented concisely.

If a cubic graph is chosen [uniformly at random](wikipedia://en/358477) among all *n*-vertex cubic graphs, then it is very likely to be Hamiltonian: the proportion of the *n*-vertex cubic graphs that are Hamiltonian tends to one in the limit as *n* goes to infinity.[^o16]

[David Eppstein](wikipedia://en/4015871) conjectured that every *n*-vertex cubic graph has at most 2<sup>*n*/3</sup> (approximately 1.260<sup>*n*</sup>) distinct Hamiltonian cycles, and provided examples of cubic graphs with that many cycles.[^o17] The best proven estimate for the number of distinct Hamiltonian cycles is $O({1.276}^n)$.[^o18]

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

::: {#o20 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the largest possible [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142) of an $n$-vertex cubic graph?

:::

The [pathwidth](wikipedia://en/5133142) of any *n*-vertex cubic graph is at most *n*/6. The best known lower bound on the pathwidth of cubic graphs is 0.082*n*. It is not known how to reduce this gap between this [lower bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) and the *n*/6 upper bound.[^o21]

It follows from the [handshaking lemma](wikipedia://en/9607933), proven by [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) in 1736 as part of the first paper on graph theory, that every cubic graph has an even number of vertices.

[Petersen's theorem](wikipedia://en/38481437) states that every cubic [bridgeless](wikipedia://en/1305071) graph has a [perfect matching](wikipedia://en/44031).[^o22] [Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) and [Plummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Plummer) conjectured that every cubic bridgeless graph has an exponential number of perfect matchings. The conjecture was recently proved, showing that every cubic bridgeless graph with *n* vertices has at least 2<sup>n/3656</sup> perfect matchings.[^o23]

## Algorithms and complexity {#Algorithms_and_complexity}

Several researchers have studied the complexity of [exponential time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_time) algorithms restricted to cubic graphs. For instance, by applying [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) to a [path decomposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_decomposition) of the graph, Fomin and Høie showed how to find their [maximum independent sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_independent_set) in time 2<sup>*n*/6 + o(*n*)</sup>.[^o21] The [travelling salesman problem](wikipedia://en/31248) in cubic graphs can be solved in time O(1.2312<sup>*n*</sup>) and polynomial space.[^o25][^o26]

Several important graph optimization problems are [APX hard](wikipedia://en/2018925), meaning that, although they have [approximation algorithms](wikipedia://en/563105) whose [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio) is bounded by a constant, they do not have [polynomial time approximation schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time_approximation_scheme) whose approximation ratio tends to 1 unless [P=NP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_vs_NP_problem). These include the problems of finding a minimum [vertex cover](wikipedia://en/562782), [maximum independent set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_independent_set), minimum [dominating set](wikipedia://en/1747972), and [maximum cut](wikipedia://en/19636775).[^o27] The [crossing number](wikipedia://en/18298594) (the minimum number of edges which cross in any [graph drawing](wikipedia://en/325813)) of a cubic graph is also [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) for cubic graphs but may be approximated.[^o28] The [Travelling Salesman Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelling_Salesman_Problem) on cubic graphs has been proven to be [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) to approximate to within any factor less than 1153/1152.[^o29]
