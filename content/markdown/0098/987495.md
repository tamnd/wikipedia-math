# Friedrich Hirzebruch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Friedrich Hirzebruch"}

**Friedrich Ernst Peter Hirzebruch** [ForMemRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society)[^o4] (17 October 1927 – 27 May 2012) was a German mathematician, working in the fields of [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [complex manifolds](wikipedia://en/509742) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), and a leading figure in his generation. He has been described as "the most important mathematician in Germany of the postwar period."[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8][^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Hirzebruch was born in [Hamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamm,_Westphalia), [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Westphalia) in 1927. His father, of the same name, was a maths teacher.

In March 1945, Hirzebruch became a soldier, and in April, in the last weeks of Hitler's rule, he was taken prisoner by the British forces then invading Germany from the west. When a British soldier found that he was studying mathematics, he drove him home, released him, and told him to continue studying.[^o16] Hirzebruch studied at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Münster) from 1945 to 1950, with one year at [ETH Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zürich).

## Career {#Career}

Hirzebruch then held a position at [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen-Nuremberg), followed by the years 1952–54 at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). After one year at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) 1955–56, he was made a professor at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), where he remained, becoming director of the *[Max-Planck-Institut für Mathematik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max-Planck-Institut_für_Mathematik)* in 1981. More than 300 people gathered in celebration of his 80th birthday in Bonn in 2007.

The [Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/2892412) (1954) for [complex manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_manifolds) was a major advance and quickly became part of the mainstream developments around the classical [Riemann–Roch theorem](wikipedia://en/247261); it was also a precursor of the [Atiyah–Singer index theorem](wikipedia://en/324752) and [Grothendieck's powerful generalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch_theorem). Hirzebruch's book *Neue topologische Methoden in der algebraischen Geometrie* (1956) was a basic text for the 'new methods' of [sheaf theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_theory), in [complex algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_algebraic_geometry). He went on to write the foundational papers on [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) with [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), and collaborated with [Armand Borel](wikipedia://en/365672) on the theory of [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530). In his later work, he provided a detailed theory of [Hilbert modular surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_surface), with [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557). He even found connections between the [Dedekind sum](wikipedia://en/1122242) in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) and [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), one of the many discoveries found between these different fields. His work influenced a generation of prominent mathematicians like [Kunihiko Kodaira](wikipedia://en/646326), [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632), Borel, Atiyah, [Raoul Bott](wikipedia://en/337457) and [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483).

Hirzebruch is famous for organizing the [Mathematische Arbeitstagung](wikipedia://en/1186588) ("working meetings" in German) in [Bonn University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn_University), beginning in 1957, and the first speakers include Atiyah, [Jacques Tits](wikipedia://en/219570), [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042), [Hans Grauert](wikipedia://en/2979031), [Nicolaas Kuiper](wikipedia://en/3344929), and Hirzebruch himself. It allowed international cooperation in the mathematical world for the last 60 years and was a major source of developments in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) as well as [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) in a few decades' time. He also established the [Max Planck Institute for Mathematics](wikipedia://en/1386960) at Bonn in 1980. The institute became the place for the Arbeitstagung and Hirzebruch was its director until 1995. The second Arbeitstagung began in 1993 and continues to this day.

From 1970 to 1971 he was the [Donegall Lecturer in Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donegall_Lecturer_in_Mathematics) at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin).

According to the [Mathematics Genealogy Project](wikipedia://en/1152126), Hirzebruch has supervised the doctoral studies of 52 mathematicians. Some of them include [Egbert Brieskorn](wikipedia://en/34748690), [Matthias Kreck](wikipedia://en/33582824), [Don Zagier](wikipedia://en/1408557), [Detlef Gromoll](wikipedia://en/18026868), Klaus Jänich, [Lothar Göttsche](wikipedia://en/22142294), Dietmar Arlt, [Winfried Scharlau](wikipedia://en/64284249), Walter Neumann, Wolfgang Meyer, Kang Zuo, Hans Scheerer, Erich Ossa, Klaus Lamotke, Eduardo Mendoza, Dimitrios Dais and [Friedhelm Waldhausen](wikipedia://en/20163961).

Hirzebruch died at the age of 84 on 27 May 2012.[^o18][^o19][^o20]

## Honours and awards {#Honours_and_awards}

Amongst many other honours, Hirzebruch was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 1988 and a [Lobachevsky Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobachevsky_Medal) in 1989.[^o22]

The government of Japan awarded him the [Order of the Sacred Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Sacred_Treasure) in 1996 and the Seki-Takakazu prize of the Mathematical Society of Japan (MSJ) in 1997.[^o23]

Hirzebruch won an [Einstein Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_Medal) of the Albert Einstein Society in Bern in 1999, and received the [Cantor medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_medal) in 2004.

Hirzebruch was a foreign member of numerous academies and societies, including the [United States National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society)[^o4] and the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences). In 1980–81 he delivered the first [Sackler Distinguished Lecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackler_Distinguished_Lectures) in Israel. He was also a member of academies of Russia, Poland, Ukraine, Israel, Finland, Hungary, Netherlands, [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), Austria, and Ireland as well as the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea) and the European Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Hirzebruch was the president of the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973) in 1962 and 1990, first after the foundation of a separate Eastern German mathematical due to the German division, and then again after the collapse of the wall which led to the unification of the East and West German Mathematical societies. He was also the first President of the [European Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/969470) from 1990 to 1994. In this way, he rebuilt the mathematical life in both Germany and Europe after the war.
