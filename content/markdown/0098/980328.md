# Cauchy space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cauchy space"}

In [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649) and [analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), a **Cauchy space** is a generalization of [metric spaces](wikipedia://en/20018) and [uniform spaces](wikipedia://en/32339) for which the notion of Cauchy convergence still makes sense. Cauchy spaces were introduced by H. H. Keller in 1968, as an axiomatic tool derived from the idea of a [Cauchy filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_filter), in order to study [completeness](wikipedia://en/7832) in [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450). The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of Cauchy spaces and *Cauchy continuous maps* is [Cartesian closed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_closed), and contains the category of [proximity spaces](wikipedia://en/1536920).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $X$ be a set, $\mathcal{P}(X)$ the [power set](wikipedia://en/23799) of $X$, and assume all [filters](wikipedia://en/12646549) are [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_filter) (that is, a filter may not contain the empty set).

A Cauchy space is a pair $(X, C)$ consisting of a set $X$ together with a [family](wikipedia://en/723043) $C \subseteq \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))$ of (proper) filters on $X$ having all of the following properties:

1. For each $x \in X,$ the discrete [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/67416519) at $x,$ denoted by $U(x),$ is in $C.$
1. If $F \in C,$ $G$ is a proper filter, and $F$ is a subset of $G,$ then $G \in C.$
1. If $F, G \in C$ and if each member of $F$ intersects each member of $G,$ then $F \cap G \in C.$

An element of $C$ is called a **Cauchy filter**, and a map $f$ between Cauchy spaces $(X, C)$ and $(Y, D)$ is **Cauchy continuous** if $\uparrow f(C) \subseteq D$; that is, the image of each Cauchy filter in $X$ is a Cauchy filter base in $Y.$

:::

## Properties and definitions {#Properties_and_definitions}

Any Cauchy space is also a [convergence space](wikipedia://en/66374616), where a filter $F$ converges to $x$ if $F \cap U(x)$ is Cauchy. In particular, a Cauchy space carries a natural [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology_%28structure%29).

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [uniform space](wikipedia://en/32339) (hence any [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), [topological vector space](wikipedia://en/45752), or [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315)) is a Cauchy space; see [Cauchy filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_filter) for definitions.
- A [lattice-ordered group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice-ordered_group) carries a natural Cauchy structure.
- Any [directed set](wikipedia://en/9165) $A$ may be made into a Cauchy space by declaring a filter $F$ to be Cauchy if, [given any](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Given_any) element $n \in A,$ [there is](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_is) an element $U \in F$ such that $U$ is either a [singleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton_%28set_theory%29) or a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the tail $\{m : m \geq n\}.$ Then given any other Cauchy space $X,$ the [Cauchy-continuous functions](wikipedia://en/15715699) from $A$ to $X$ are the same as the [Cauchy nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_net) in $X$ indexed by $A.$ If $X$ is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space), then such a function may be extended to the completion of $A,$ which may be written $A \cup \{\infty\};$ the value of the extension at $\infty$ will be the limit of the net. In the case where $A$ is the set $\{1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$ of [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) (so that a Cauchy net indexed by $A$ is the same as a [Cauchy sequence](wikipedia://en/6085)), then $A$ receives the same Cauchy structure as the metric space $\{1, 1/2, 1/3, \ldots\}.$

:::

## Category of Cauchy spaces {#Category_of_Cauchy_spaces}

The natural notion of [morphism](wikipedia://en/27716891) between Cauchy spaces is that of a [Cauchy-continuous function](wikipedia://en/15715699), a concept that had earlier been studied for uniform spaces.
