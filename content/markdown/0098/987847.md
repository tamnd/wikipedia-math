# Salomon Bochner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Salomon Bochner"}

**Salomon Bochner** (20 August 1899 – 2 May 1982) was a [Galizien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria)-born [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625).

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) family in [Podgórze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podgórze) (near [Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraków)), then Austria-Hungary, now [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland). Fearful of a Russian invasion in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_%28Central_Europe%29) at the beginning of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) in 1914, his family moved to Germany, seeking greater security. Bochner was educated at a [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) (secondary school), and then at the [Friedrich Wilhelm University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Wilhelm_University_of_Berlin). There, he was a student of [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191),[^o3] writing a dissertation involving what would later be called the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923). Shortly after this, he left the academy to help his family during the [escalating inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation#The_1920s_German_inflation). After returning to mathematical research, he lectured at the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München) from 1924 to 1933. His academic career in Germany ended after the [Nazis came to power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machtergreifung) in 1933, and he left for a position at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) from 1945 to 1948.[^o8] He was appointed as Henry Burchard Fine Professor in 1959, retiring in 1968. Although he was seventy years old when he retired from Princeton, Bochner was appointed as Edgar Odell Lovett Professor of Mathematics at [Rice University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_University) and went on to hold this chair until his death in 1982. He became Head of the Department at Rice in 1969 and held this position until 1976. He died in [Houston, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston,_Texas). He was an [Orthodox Jew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Jew).[^o9]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

In 1925, he started work in the area of [almost periodic functions](wikipedia://en/405512), simplifying the approach of [Harald Bohr](wikipedia://en/638305) by use of [compactness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compactness) and [approximate identity](wikipedia://en/949628) arguments. In 1933, he defined the [Bochner integral](wikipedia://en/3675277), as it is now called, for vector-valued functions. [Bochner's theorem](wikipedia://en/2369850) on [Fourier transforms](wikipedia://en/52247) appeared in a 1932 book. His techniques came into their own as [Pontryagin duality](wikipedia://en/366136) and then the representation theory of [locally compact groups](wikipedia://en/1651204) developed in the following years.

Subsequently, he worked on [multiple Fourier series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Fourier_series), posing the question of the [Bochner–Riesz means](wikipedia://en/20966805). This led to results on how the Fourier transform on [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) behaves under rotations.

In differential geometry, [Bochner's formula](wikipedia://en/3988777) on [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) was published in 1946. Joint work with [Kentaro Yano](wikipedia://en/34951366) (1912–1993) led to the 1953 book *Curvature and Betti Numbers*. It had consequences for the [Kodaira vanishing theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodaira_vanishing_theory), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), and [spin manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold). Bochner also worked on functions of [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variable), resulting in the [Bochner–Martinelli formula](wikipedia://en/30280089) and the book *Several Complex Variables* (with [W. T. Martin](wikipedia://en/37661503) in 1948).
