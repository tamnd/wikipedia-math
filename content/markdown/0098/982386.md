# Killing form

Algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Killing form"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Killing form**, named after [Wilhelm Killing](wikipedia://en/1531824), is a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) that plays a basic role in the theories of [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944). [Cartan's criteria](wikipedia://en/3052668) (criterion of solvability and criterion of semisimplicity) show that Killing form has a close relationship to the [semisimplicity](wikipedia://en/1337587) of the Lie algebras.[^n1]

:::

## History and name {#History_and_name}

The Killing form was essentially introduced into Lie algebra theory by (txt) in his thesis. In a historical survey of Lie theory, (Borel 2001) has described how the term *"Killing form"* first occurred in 1951 during one of his own reports for the [Séminaire Bourbaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Séminaire_Bourbaki); it arose as a [misnomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misnomer), since the form had previously been used by Lie theorists, without a name attached.[^o6] Some other authors now employ the term *"Cartan-Killing form"*. At the end of the 19th century, Killing had noted that the coefficients of the characteristic equation of a regular semisimple element of a Lie algebra are invariant under the adjoint group, from which it follows that the Killing form (i.e. the degree 2 coefficient) is invariant, but he did not make much use of the fact. A basic result that Cartan made use of was [Cartan's criterion](wikipedia://en/3052668), which states that the Killing form is non-degenerate if and only if the Lie algebra is a [direct sum of simple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/1337587).[^o6]

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Consider a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\mathfrak g$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $K$. Every element $x$ of $\mathfrak g$ defines the [adjoint endomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_endomorphism) $\operatorname{ad}(x)$ (also written as $\operatorname{ad}_{x}$) of $\mathfrak g$ with the help of the Lie bracket, as

$$\operatorname{ad}(x)(y) = [x, y].$$

Now, supposing $\mathfrak g$ is of finite dimension, the [trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_of_a_matrix) of the composition of two such endomorphisms defines a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375)

$$B(x, y) = \operatorname{trace}(\operatorname{ad}(x) \circ \operatorname{ad}(y)),$$

with values in $K$, the **Killing form** on $\mathfrak g$.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The following properties follow as theorems from the above definition.

- The Killing form $B$ is bilinear and symmetric.
- The Killing form is an invariant form, as are all other forms obtained from [Casimir operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_operator). The [derivation](wikipedia://en/897658) of Casimir operators vanishes; for the Killing form, this vanishing can be written as

$$B([x, y], z) = B(x, [y, z])$$

where \[ , \] is the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/17944#Definitions).

- If $\mathfrak g$ is a complex [simple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/59500649) then any invariant symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak g$ is a scalar multiple of the Killing form. This is no longer true if $\mathfrak g$ is simple but not complex; key concept: absolutely simple Lie algebra.
- The Killing form is also invariant under [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) $s$ of the algebra $\mathfrak g$, that is,

$$B(s(x), s(y)) = B(x, y)$$

for $s$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak g)$.

- The [Cartan criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartan_criterion) states that a Lie algebra is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) if and only if the Killing form is [non-degenerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degenerate_form).
- The Killing form of a [nilpotent Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/3358867) is identically zero.
- If $I, J$ are two [ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_of_a_Lie_algebra) in a Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with zero intersection, then $I$ and $J$ are [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) subspaces with respect to the Killing form.
- The orthogonal complement with respect to $B$ of an ideal is again an ideal.[^o11]
- If a given Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is a direct sum of its ideals $I_{1},...,I_{n}$, then the Killing form of $\mathfrak g$ is the direct sum of the Killing forms of the individual summands.

:::

## Matrix elements {#Matrix_elements}

Given a basis $e_{i}$ of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, the matrix elements of the Killing form are given by

$$B_{ij}= \mathrm{trace}(\mathrm{ad}(e_i) \circ \mathrm{ad}(e_j)).$$

Here

$$\left(\textrm{ad}(e_i) \circ \textrm{ad}(e_j)\right)(e_k)= [e_i, [e_j, e_k]] = [e_i, {c_{jk}}^{m}e_m] = {c_{im}}^{n} {c_{jk}}^{m} e_n$$

in [Einstein summation notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einstein_summation_notation), where the $c_{ij}^{k}$ are the [structure coefficients](wikipedia://en/20285142) of the Lie algebra. The index $k$ functions as column index and the index $n$ as row index in the matrix $\operatorname{ad}(e_{i})\operatorname{ad}(e_{j})$. Taking the trace amounts to putting $k = n$ and summing, and so we can write

$$B_{ij} = {c_{im}}^{n} {c_{jn}}^{m}$$

The Killing form is the simplest 2-[tensor](wikipedia://en/29965) that can be formed from the structure constants. The form itself is then $B=B_{ij} e^i \otimes e^j.$

In the above indexed definition, we are careful to distinguish upper and lower indices (*co-* and *contra-variant* indices). This is because, in many cases, the Killing form can be used as a metric tensor on a manifold, in which case the distinction becomes an important one for the transformation properties of tensors. When the Lie algebra is [semisimple](wikipedia://en/1337587) over a zero-characteristic field, its Killing form is nondegenerate, and hence can be used as a [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) to raise and lower indexes. In this case, it is always possible to choose a basis for $\mathfrak g$ such that the structure constants with all upper indices are [completely antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/696955).

The Killing form for some Lie algebras $\mathfrak g$ are (for $X, Y$ in $\mathfrak g$ viewed in their fundamental matrix representation):

::: {#o13 .table cue=table}

| $\mathfrak g$ | $B(X,Y)$ | Classification | Dual coxeter number |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathfrak{gl}(n, \mathbb{R})$ | $2n \text{tr}(XY) - 2 \text{tr}(X)\text{tr}(Y)$ | \- | \- |
| $\mathfrak{sl}(n, \mathbb{R}), n \geq 2$ | $2n \text{tr}(XY)$ | $A_{n-1}$ | $n$ |
| $\mathfrak{su}(n), n \geq 2$ | $2n \text{tr}(XY)$ | $A_{n-1}$ | $n$ |
| $\mathfrak{so}(n), n \geq 2$ | $(n-2) \text{tr}(XY)$ | $B_m, n = 2m+1$ for $n$ odd. $D_m, n = 2m$ for $n$ even. | $n-2$ |
| $\mathfrak{so}(n, \mathbb{C}), n \geq 2$ | $(n-2) \text{tr}(XY)$ | $B_m, n = 2m+1$ for $n$ odd. $D_m, n = 2m$ for $n$ even. | $n-2$ |
| $\mathfrak{sp}(2n, \mathbb{R}), n \geq 1$ | $2(n+1) \text{tr}(XY)$ | $C_n$ | $n+1$ |
| $\mathfrak{sp}(2n,\mathbb{C}), n \geq 1$ | $2(n+1) \text{tr}(XY)$ | $C_n$ | $n+1$ |

:::

The table shows that the [Dynkin index](wikipedia://en/10874374) for the adjoint representation is equal to twice the [dual Coxeter number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_Coxeter_number).

## Connection with real forms {#Connection_with_real_forms}

Suppose that $\mathfrak g$ is a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) over the field of real numbers $\mathbb R$. By [Cartan's criterion](wikipedia://en/3052668), the Killing form is nondegenerate, and can be diagonalized in a suitable basis with the diagonal entries $\pm1$. By [Sylvester's law of inertia](wikipedia://en/1127460), the number of positive entries is an invariant of the bilinear form, i.e. it does not depend on the choice of the diagonalizing basis, and is called the **index** of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. This is a number between $0$ and the dimension of $\mathfrak g$ which is an important invariant of the real Lie algebra. In particular, a real Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is called **compact** if the Killing form is [negative definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_definite) (or negative semidefinite if the Lie algebra is not semisimple). Note that this is one of two inequivalent definitions commonly used for [compactness of a Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/25262324); the other states that a Lie algebra is compact if it corresponds to a [compact Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Lie_group). The definition of compactness in terms of negative definiteness of the Killing form is more restrictive, since using this definition it can be shown that under the [Lie correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_correspondence), compact Lie algebras correspond to compact semisimple Lie groups.

If $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ is a semisimple Lie algebra over the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826), then there are several non-isomorphic real Lie algebras whose [complexification](wikipedia://en/755604) is $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$, which are called its **real forms**. It turns out that every complex semisimple Lie algebra admits a unique (up to isomorphism) compact real form $\mathfrak g$. The real forms of a given complex semisimple Lie algebra are frequently labeled by the positive index of inertia of their Killing form.

For example, the complex [special linear algebra](wikipedia://en/144823) $\mathfrak {sl}(2, \mathbb C)$ has two real forms, the real special linear algebra, denoted $\mathfrak {sl}(2, \mathbb R)$, and the [special unitary algebra](wikipedia://en/173997), denoted $\mathfrak {su}(2)$. The first one is noncompact, the so-called **split real form**, and its Killing form has signature $(2, 1)$. The second one is the compact real form and its Killing form is negative definite, i.e. has signature $(0, 3)$. The corresponding Lie groups are the noncompact group $\mathrm {SL}(2, \mathbb R)$ of $2 \times 2$ real matrices with the unit determinant and the special unitary group $\mathrm {SU}(2)$, which is compact.

## Trace forms {#Trace_forms}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over the field $K$, and $\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow \text{End}(V)$ be a Lie algebra representation. Let $\text{Tr}_{V}:\text{End}(V)\rightarrow K$ be the trace functional on $V$. Then we can define the trace form for the representation $\rho$ as

$$\text{Tr}_\rho:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow K,$$

$$\text{Tr}_\rho(X,Y) = \text{Tr}_V(\rho(X)\rho(Y)).$$

Then the Killing form is the special case that the representation is the adjoint representation, $\text{Tr}_\text{ad} = B$.

It is easy to show that this is symmetric, bilinear and invariant for any representation $\rho$.

If furthermore $\mathfrak{g}$ is simple and $\rho$ is irreducible, then it can be shown $\text{Tr}_\rho = I(\rho)B$ where $I(\rho)$ is the [index](wikipedia://en/10874374) of the representation.
