# Blaschke product

*Image: Blaschke-product.png.* Blaschke product, $B(z)$, associated to 50 randomly chosen points in the unit disk. $B(z)$ is represented as a [Matplotlib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matplotlib) plot, using a version of the [domain coloring](wikipedia://en/5280990) method.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Blaschke product"}

In [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), a **Blaschke product** is a bounded [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) in the open unit disc constructed to have zeros at a finite or infinite sequence of prescribed [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $a_0,a_1,\ldots$ inside the [unit disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_disc), with the property that the magnitude of the function is constant along the boundary of the disc.

:::

Blaschke products were introduced by [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998) in 1915.[^o2] They are related to [Hardy spaces](wikipedia://en/314780).

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A sequence of points $(a_n)$ inside the unit disk is said to satisfy the **Blaschke condition** when

$$\sum_n(1-|a_n|)<\infty.$$

Given a sequence obeying the Blaschke condition, the Blaschke product is defined as

$$B(z)=\prod_n B(a_n,z)$$

with factors

$$B(a,z)=\frac{|a|}{a}\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}$$

provided $a\neq0$. Here $\overline{a}$ is the [complex conjugate](wikipedia://en/173918) of $a$. When $a=0$, take $B(0,z)=z$.

The Blaschke product $B(z)$ defines a function analytic in the open unit disc and vanishing exactly at the points $a_n$, with [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790) counted. Furthermore, it belongs to the Hardy class $H^\infty$.[^o6]

The sequence $(a_n)$ satisfying the convergence criterion above is sometimes called a *Blaschke sequence*.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Szegő theorem"}

## Szegő theorem {#Szegő_theorem}

A theorem of [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065) states that if $f\in H^1$, the [Hardy space](wikipedia://en/314780) with integrable norm, and if $f$ is not identically zero, then the zeroes of $f$, which are at most countable, satisfy the Blaschke condition.

:::

## Finite Blaschke products {#Finite_Blaschke_products}

Finite Blaschke products can be characterized as analytic functions on the unit disc in the following way.[^o10] Assume that $f$ is analytic on the open unit disc and can be extended to a continuous function on the closed unit disc

$$\overline{\Delta}=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\leq1\}$$

that maps the unit circle to itself. Then $f$ is equal to a finite Blaschke product

$$B(z)=\zeta\prod_{i=1}^n\left(\frac{z-a_i}{1-\overline{a_i}z}\right)^{m_i},$$

where $\zeta$ lies on the unit circle, $|a_i|<1$, and $m_i$ is the multiplicity of the zero $a_i$. In particular, if $f$ satisfies the condition above and has no zeros inside the unit circle, then $f$ is constant. This fact is also a consequence of the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104) for [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762), applied to the harmonic function $\log|f(z)|$.

## Asymptotics of powers {#Asymptotics_of_powers}

For $0<\rho<1$, consider the Blaschke factor

$$b_\rho(z)=\frac{z-\rho}{1-\rho z}$$

and write

$$b_\rho(z)^n=\sum_{k\geq0}\widehat{b_\rho^n}(k)z^k.$$

Set

$$\alpha_0=\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho}.$$

When $n$ is large and $k$ is proportional to $n$, the asymptotic behavior of these coefficients changes at the two indices $\alpha_0 n$ and $\alpha_0^{-1}n$. If $k/n$ remains in a compact subinterval of $(\alpha_0,\alpha_0^{-1})$, the coefficients have oscillatory stationary-phase asymptotics of order $n^{-1/2}$. Outside this interval they decay exponentially. An Airy-type asymptotic near the transition point $\alpha_0^{-1}n$, approached from inside the interval $(\alpha_0 n,\alpha_0^{-1}n)$, is established in this setting.[^o12] More generally, near either endpoint, on the scale $|k-\alpha_0^{\pm1}n|=O(n^{1/3})$, the transition is described by the [Airy function](wikipedia://en/406896), and the largest coefficients are of order $n^{-1/3}$.[^o13]

For $1\leq p\leq\infty$, the corresponding asymptotic orders of the $\ell^p$-norms of the Taylor coefficients are

$$\left\|\widehat{b_\rho^n}\right\|_{\ell^p} \asymp \begin{cases} n^{\,1/p-1/2}, & 1\leq p<4,\\[4pt] \left(\dfrac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/4}, & p=4,\\[8pt] n^{\,1/(3p)-1/3}, & 4<p\leq\infty, \end{cases}$$

where $1/\infty=0$ and the implicit constants depend on $p$ and $\rho$.[^o12]

For general finite Blaschke products, the asymptotic size of the largest coefficient of $B^n$ is similarly determined by the maximal degeneracy of the stationary points of the boundary phase of $B$.[^o14]
