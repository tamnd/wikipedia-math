# Tjøstheim's coefficient

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tjøstheim's coefficient"}

**Tjøstheim's coefficient**[^o2] is a measure of spatial [association](wikipedia://en/157057) that attempts to quantify the degree to which two spatial data sets are related. Developed by Norwegian statistician [Dag Tjøstheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Tjøstheim). It is similar to rank correlation coefficients like [Spearman's rank correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/235623) and the [Kendall rank correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/7287830) but also explicitly considers the spatial relationship between variables.

:::

Consider two variables, $F(x,y)$ and $G(x,y)$, observed at the same set of $N$ spatial locations with co-ordinates $x_i$ and $y_i$. The Rank of $F$ at $(x_i,y_i)$ is

$$R_F(x_i,y_i) = \sum_i^N \theta(F(x_i,y_i) - F(x_j,y_j) )$$

with a similar definition for $G$. Here $\theta$ is a [step function](wikipedia://en/87299) and this formula counts how many values $F(x_j,y_j)$ are less than or equal to the value at the target point $F(x_i,y_i)$.

Now define

$$X_F(i) = \sum_j^N x_j \delta(i, R_F(x_j,y_j))$$

where $\delta$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). This is the $x$ coordinate of the $i^{\text{th}}$ ranked $F$ value. The quantities $Y_F(i), X_G(i)$ and $Y_G(i)$ can be defined similarly.

Tjøstheim's coefficient is defined by[^o3]

$$A = \frac{\sum_i^N (X_F(i) - \bar{X}_F)(X_G(i) - \bar{X}_G) + (Y_F(i) - \bar{Y}_F)(Y_G(i) - \bar{Y}_G) }{\left(\sum_i^N\left[(X_F(i) - \bar{X}_F)^2 + (Y_F(i) - \bar{Y}_F)^2\right] \sum_i^N\left[(X_G(i) - \bar{X}_G)^2 + (Y_G(i) - \bar{Y}_G)^2\right] \right)^{1/2}}$$

Under the assumptions that $F$ and $G$ are [independent and identically distributed random variables](wikipedia://en/30876902) and are [independent](wikipedia://en/27593) of each other it can be shown that $E[A] = 0$ and

$$var(A) = \frac{\left(\sum_i^N x_i^2\right)^2 + 2 \left(\sum_i^N x_i y_i\right)^2 + \left(\sum_i^N y_i^2\right)^2 }{(N-1)\left(\sum_i^N x_i^2 + \sum_i^N y_i^2\right)^2 }$$

The maximum variance of $1/(N-1)$ occurs when all points are on a straight line and the minimum variance of $1/(2(N-1))$ occurs for a symmetric cross pattern where $x_i y_i = 0$ and $\sum_i^N x_i^2 = \sum_i^N y_i^2$.[^o4]

Tjøstheim's coefficient is implemented as *cor.spatial* in the [R](wikipedia://en/376707) package SpatialPack.[^o5] Numerical simulations suggest that $A$ is an effective measure of correlation between variables but is sensitive to the degree of [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) in $F$ and $G$.[^o4]
