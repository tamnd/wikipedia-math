# Kirillov model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kirillov model"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Kirillov model**, studied by (txt), is a realization of a representation of *GL*<sub>2</sub> over a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) on a space of functions on the local field.

:::

If *G* is the [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) *GL*<sub>2</sub> and **F** is a non-Archimedean local field, and τ is a fixed nontrivial character of the additive group of **F** and π is an [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of *G*(**F**), then the Kirillov model for π is a representation π on a space of locally constant functions *f* on **F**<sup>\*</sup> with compact support in **F** such that

$$\pi\left(\begin{pmatrix}a & b \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}\right)f(x) = \tau(bx)f(ax).$$

(Jacquet Langlands 1970) showed that an irreducible representation of dimension greater than 1 has an essentially unique Kirillov model. Over a local field, the space of functions with compact support in **F**<sup>\*</sup> has codimension 0, 1, or 2 in the Kirillov model, depending on whether the irreducible representation is cuspidal, special, or principal.

The [Whittaker model](wikipedia://en/20679744) can be constructed from the Kirillov model, by defining the image *W*<sub>ξ</sub> of a vector ξ of the Kirillov model by

*W*<sub>ξ</sub>(*g*) = π(g)ξ(1)

where π(*g*) is the image of *g* in the Kirillov model.

(Bernstein 1984) defined the Kirillov model for the general linear group GL<sub>*n*</sub> using the [mirabolic subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirabolic_subgroup). More precisely, a Kirillov model for a representation of the general linear group is an embedding of it in the representation of the mirabolic group induced from a non-degenerate character of the group of upper triangular matrices.
