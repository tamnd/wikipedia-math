# Laplace limit

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laplace limit"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Laplace limit** is the maximum value of the [eccentricity](wikipedia://en/1239472) for which a solution to [Kepler's equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler's_equation), in terms of a power series in the eccentricity, converges. It is approximately

:::

\0.66274 34193 49181 58097 47420 97109 25290.

Kepler's equation *M* = *E* − ε sin *E* relates the [mean anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_anomaly) *M* with the [eccentric anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccentric_anomaly) *E* for a body moving in an [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277) with eccentricity ε. This equation cannot be solved for *E* in terms of [elementary functions](wikipedia://en/10412), but the [Lagrange reversion theorem](wikipedia://en/953148) gives the solution as a [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) in ε:

$$E = M + \sin(M) \, \varepsilon + \tfrac12 \sin(2M) \, \varepsilon^2 + \left( \tfrac38 \sin(3M) - \tfrac18 \sin(M) \right) \, \varepsilon^3 + \cdots$$

or in general[^o2][^o3]

$$E = M \;+\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\varepsilon^n}{2^{n-1}\,n!} \sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor} (-1)^k\,\binom{n}{k}\,(n-2k)^{n-1}\,\sin((n-2k)\,M)$$

[Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783) realized that this series converges for small values of the eccentricity, but diverges for any value of *M* other than a multiple of π if the eccentricity exceeds a certain value that does not depend on *M*. The Laplace limit is this value. It is the [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) of the power series.

It is also the maximum of the function $\frac{x}{\text{cosh} (x)}$ [^o4]

It is the unique real solution of the [transcendental equation](wikipedia://en/6073963)[^o5]

$$\frac{x\exp(\sqrt{1+x^2})}{1+\sqrt{1+x^2}}=1$$

A [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) in terms of [r-Lambert special function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-Lambert_special_function) and an infinite series representation were given by István Mező.[^o6]

## History {#History}

Laplace calculated the value 0.66195 in 1827. The Italian astronomer [Francesco Carlini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Carlini) found the limit 0.66 five years before Laplace. [Cauchy](wikipedia://en/1842) in 1829 gave the precise value 0.66274.[^o8]
