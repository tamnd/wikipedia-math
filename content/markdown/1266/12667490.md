# Edward Willes (bishop)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Edward Willes"}

*Image: Bp Edward Willes.jpg.* Edward Willes, Bishop of Bath and Wells

**Edward Willes** (6 March 1693 – 24 November 1773) was an [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) bishop who was [Bishop of St David's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_St_David's) and later [Bishop of Bath and Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Bath_and_Wells) and one of the most prominent English cryptanalysts of his time.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire) son of the Rev John Willes and his wife Anne (or Mary) Walker, daughter of Sir William Walker, [Mayor of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Oxford). They belonged to a junior branch of the long-established Willes family of [Newbold Comyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbold_Comyn); Sir [John Willes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Willes_%28judge%29), the long-serving [Chief Justice of the Common Pleas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Common_Pleas), was his brother. He was educated at [Oriel College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriel_College,_Oxford) and graduated with a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree in 1712. While there he learned [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432) from [William Blencowe](wikipedia://en/74636643).[^o3]

In 1716 he became a Decipherer for [George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain), and distinguished himself by deciphering messages between Swedish diplomats which were sympathetic to the [Jacobite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobitism) cause. He was rewarded by the government by being granted the living of [Barton in the Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_in_the_Clay), [Bedfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedfordshire), which he held between 1718 and 1730.[^o3]

He subsequently deciphered the correspondence between [Francis Atterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Atterbury), [Bishop of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester), and Jacobite exiles abroad between 1719 and 1722. His evidence at the trial secured the conviction and exile of Atterbury, and led to his being appointed a [Canon of Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_and_Canons_of_Westminster). He was [Dean of Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_of_Lincoln) 1730–1743.[^o3]

In 1743 he became bishop of St. Davids and in 1744 he became Bishop of Bath and Wells.[^o3] During his episcopate he undertook some repairs to the [Bishop's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop's_Palace,_Wells) in [Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells,_Somerset).

By his wife Jane, Willes had five sons and four daughters. He was a popular and respected man: one of his sons was reportedly told by the [Earl of Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_of_Chesterfield) that he should try to imitate his father in everything.

Willes died in London in 1773, and is buried in [Westminster Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Abbey).
