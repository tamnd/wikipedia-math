# Power object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Power object"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **power object** in a category is an analogue of a [powerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerset) in the category of sets.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a [finitely complete category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_complete_category). A *power object* of $A \in \mathcal{C}$ is an object $\mathcal{P}(A)$ together with a [subobject](wikipedia://en/540476) $(\in) \hookrightarrow A \times \mathcal{P}(A)$ satisfying the following [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245): for every other object $B \in \mathcal{C}$ and subobject $R \hookrightarrow B \times A$, there exists a unique morphism $\chi : B \to \mathcal{P}(A)$ such that $R \hookrightarrow B \times A$ is the pullback of $(\in) \hookrightarrow A \times \mathcal{P}(A)$ along $\chi$.[^n1]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

In the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), power objects exist: $\mathcal{P}(A)$ is the usual power set of $A$, and $(\in) \hookrightarrow A \times \mathcal{P}(A)$ is the set membership relation.

More generally, in any [elementary topos](wikipedia://en/815802), the power object of $A$ can be constructed as $\mathcal{P}(A) := \Omega^A$ (where $\Omega$ is the [subobject classifier](wikipedia://en/366909)), with $(\in) \hookrightarrow A \times \Omega^A$ being the subobject classified by the [evaluation map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaluation_map_%28category_theory%29) $A \times \Omega^A \to \Omega$.[^n2]

Conversely, every finitely complete category with power objects is an elementary topos.[^n3] Thus, power objects provide a possible simplification of the definition of an elementary topos.

:::
