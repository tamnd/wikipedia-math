# David Cox (statistician)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="David Cox"}

**Sir David Roxbee Cox** (15 July 1924 – 18 January 2022) was a British statistician and educator. His wide-ranging contributions to the field of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) included introducing [logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631), the [proportional hazards model](wikipedia://en/5352267) and the [Cox process](wikipedia://en/6196078), a [point process](wikipedia://en/2147685) named after him.

:::

Cox was a professor of statistics at [Birkbeck College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London), [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) and the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford), and served as [Warden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warden_%28college%29) of [Nuffield College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford). The first recipient of the [International Prize in Statistics](wikipedia://en/52048719), he also received the [Guy](wikipedia://en/6752269), [George Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Box_Medal) and [Copley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) medals, as well as a [knighthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_bachelor).

## Early life {#Early_life}

Cox was born in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) on 15 July 1924.[^o4][^o5] His father was a [die](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_%28manufacturing%29) sinker and part-owner of a jewellery shop, and they lived near the [Jewellery Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery_Quarter).[^o6] The aeronautical engineer [Harold Roxbee Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Roxbee_Cox) was a distant cousin.[^o7] He attended [Handsworth Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handsworth_Grammar_School), Birmingham.[^o6][^o8] He received a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) in mathematics at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge),[^o4][^o6][^o9] and obtained his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) in 1949, advised by [Henry Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Daniels_%28statistician%29) and [Bernard Welch](wikipedia://en/57043307). His dissertation was entitled *Theory of Fibre Motion*.

## Career {#Career}

Cox was employed from 1944 to 1946 at the [Royal Aircraft Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Aircraft_Establishment), from 1946 to 1950 at the [Wool Industries Research Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool_Industries_Research_Association) in Leeds,[^o12] and from 1950 to 1955 worked at the Statistical Laboratory at the University of Cambridge. From 1956 to 1966 he was Reader and then Professor of Statistics at [Birkbeck College, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkbeck,_University_of_London). In 1966, he took up the Chair position in Statistics at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) where he later became head of the mathematics department. In 1988 he became Warden of [Nuffield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford) and a member of the Department of Statistics at [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University). He formally retired from these positions in 1994, but continued to work at Oxford.[^o12][^o13]

Cox supervised, collaborated with, and encouraged many notable researchers prominent in statistics. He collaborated with [George Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Box) on a study of transformations such as the [Box–Cox transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box–Cox_transformation) and they were especially delighted to be credited as [Box and Cox](wikipedia://en/15811316).[^o14][^o15] He was the doctoral advisor of [David Hinkley](wikipedia://en/9539349), [Peter McCullagh](wikipedia://en/11740023), [Basilio de Bragança Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilio_de_Bragança_Pereira), [Wally Smith](wikipedia://en/14537040), [Gauss Moutinho Cordeiro](wikipedia://en/5642513), [Valerie Isham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Isham), [Henry Wynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wynn), [Claudio Di Veroli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Di_Veroli) and [Jane Hutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Hutton).[^o10] He served as president of the [Bernoulli Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli_Society) from 1979 to 1981,[^o16] of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664) from 1980 to 1982,[^o17] and of the [International Statistical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Statistical_Institute) from 1995 to 1997.[^o18] He was an Honorary Fellow of [Nuffield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuffield_College,_Oxford) and [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), and was a member of the Department of Statistics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford).[^o4]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

In 1947, Cox married Joyce Drummond, and they had four children.[^o20] He died at his home in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), on 18 January 2022, at the age of 97.[^o21][^o22][^o23] Cox is buried in Wolvercote cemetery.

## Research {#Research}

Cox made pioneering and important contributions to numerous areas of statistics and applied probability, of which the best known are:

- [Logistic regression](wikipedia://en/226631), which is employed when the variable to be predicted is [categorical](wikipedia://en/821165) (i.e., can take a limited number of values, e.g., gender, race (in the US census)), binary (a special case of categorical with only two values - e.g., success/failure, disease/no disease), or [ordinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_variable), where the categories can be ranked (e.g., pain intensity can be absent, mild, moderate, severe, unbearable). Cox's 1958 paper[^o25] and further publications in the 1960s addressed the case of binary logistic regression.[^o26]
- The [proportional hazards model](wikipedia://en/5352267), which is widely used in the analysis of survival data, was developed by him in 1972.[^o27][^o28] An example of the use of the proportional hazards model is in survival analysis in medical research. The model can be used in clinical trials to investigate time-based information about cohorts of patients, such as their response to exposure to certain chemical substances.[^o29]
- The [Cox process](wikipedia://en/6196078) was named after him.[^o30]

## Awards {#Awards}

Cox received numerous awards and honours for his work. He was awarded the [Guy Medals](wikipedia://en/6752269) in Silver (1961) and Gold (1973) of the [Royal Statistical Society](wikipedia://en/552664).[^o4][^o32] He was elected Fellow of the [Royal Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_London) in 1973.[^o33] The next year, he was elected to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences).[^o34] He was [knighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_bachelor) by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II_of_the_United_Kingdom) in 1985.[^o9] He was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society) in 1990.[^o36] Cox became an Honorary Fellow of the [British Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy) in 1997 and was a Fellow of the [American Statistical Association](wikipedia://en/341988).[^o37] He was a Foreign Associate of the [US National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) and a foreign member of the [Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Letters).[^o9][^o32] In 1990, he won the [Kettering Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettering_Prize) and Gold Medal for Cancer Research for "the development of the Proportional Hazard Regression Model." In 2010 he was awarded the [Copley Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copley_Medal) of the Royal Society "for his seminal contributions to the theory and applications of statistics",[^o38] the same year in which he was elected a foreign fellow of the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada).[^o39] He was also the first ever recipient of the [International Prize in Statistics](wikipedia://en/52048719). He received the award in 2016.[^o40] In 2013 Cox was elected an Honorary Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh).[^o41] In 2016, he won the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Awards) in the Basic Sciences category jointly with [Bradley Efron](wikipedia://en/7250868), for the development of "pioneering and hugely influential" statistical methods that have proved indispensable for obtaining reliable results in a vast spectrum of disciplines from medicine to astrophysics, genomics or particle physics.[^o13]

## Publications {#Publications}

Cox wrote or co-authored over 300 papers and books.[^o43] From 1966 to 1991 he was the editor of *[Biometrika](wikipedia://en/1734178)*.[^o38] His books are as follows:

- *Planning of experiments* (1958)
- *Queues* ([Methuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methuen_Publishing), 1961). With [Walter L. Smith](wikipedia://en/14537040)
- *Renewal Theory* ([Methuen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methuen_Publishing), 1962).
- *The theory of stochastic processes* (1965). With [Hilton David Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilton_David_Miller)
- *Analysis of binary data* (1969). With [Joyce Snell](wikipedia://en/61766920)
- *Theoretical statistics* (1974). With [D. V. Hinkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._V._Hinkley)
- *Problems and Solutions in Theoretical Statistics* (1978). With [D. V. Hinkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._V._Hinkley)
- *[Point Processes](wikipedia://en/66099877)* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 1980). With [Valerie Isham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Isham)
- *Applied statistics, principles and examples* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 1981). With [Joyce Snell](wikipedia://en/61766920)
- *Analysis of survival data* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 1984). With David Oakes
- *Asymptotic techniques for use in statistics*. (1989) With [Ole E. Barndorff-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_E._Barndorff-Nielsen)
- *Inference and asymptotics* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 1994). With [Ole E. Barndorff-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_E._Barndorff-Nielsen)
- *Multivariate dependencies: models, analysis and interpretation* (Chapman & Hall, 1995). With [Nanny Wermuth](wikipedia://en/37384247)
- *The theory of design of experiments*. (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2000). With [Nancy M. Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_M._Reid)
- *Complex stochastic systems* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2000). With [Ole E. Barndorff-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ole_E._Barndorff-Nielsen) and [Claudia Klüppelberg](wikipedia://en/61766304)
- *Components of variance* (Chapman & Hall/CRC, 2003). With P. J. Solomon
- *Principles of Statistical Inference* (Cambridge University Press, 2006).
- [Selected Statistical Papers of Sir David Cox 2 Volume Set](https://web.archive.org/web/20060114072317/http://www.cambridge.org/uk/catalogue/catalogue.asp?isbn=052185816X#contributors)
- [Principles of Applied Statistics](https://www.amazon.com/Principles-Applied-Statistics-D-Cox/dp/1107644453/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1310742640&sr=8-1) (CUP). With [Christl Donnelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christl_Donnelly)

- (1992). "The Collected Works of John W. Tukey: Factorial and ANOVA". VII. Taylor & Francis. isbn:978-0-412-06321-3
- (1996). "Time series models in econometrics, finance and others". Taylor & Francis. isbn:978-0-412-72930-0
- (2001). "Biometrika: One Hundred Years". Oxford University Press. isbn:978-0-19-850993-6
