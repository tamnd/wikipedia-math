# Chilton and Colburn J-factor analogy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chilton and Colburn J-factor analogy"}

**Chilton–Colburn J-factor analogy** (also known as the *modified Reynolds analogy*[^o2]) is a successful and widely used analogy between [heat](wikipedia://en/184726), [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431), and [mass transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transfer). The basic mechanisms and mathematics of heat, mass, and momentum transport are essentially the same. Among many analogies (like [Reynolds analogy](wikipedia://en/10109430), Prandtl–Taylor analogy) developed to directly relate [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) coefficients, [mass transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transfer) coefficients and [friction](wikipedia://en/11062) factors, Chilton and Colburn J-factor analogy proved to be the most accurate. The factors are named after [Thomas H. Chilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_H._Chilton) and Allan Philip Colburn (1904–1955).[^o3]

:::

It is written as follows,

$$J_M=\frac{f}{2} = J_H = \frac{h}{c_p\, G}\,{Pr}^{\frac{2}{3}}= J_D = \frac{k'_c}{\overline{v}} \cdot {Sc}^{\frac{2}{3}}$$

This equation permits the prediction of an unknown transfer coefficient when one of the other coefficients is known. The analogy is valid for fully developed [turbulent flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbulent_flow) in conduits with *[Re](wikipedia://en/23868856)* > 10000, 0.7 \< *[Pr](wikipedia://en/58283)* \< 160, and tubes where *L*/*d* > 60 (the same constraints as the [Sieder–Tate correlation](wikipedia://en/58285)). The wider range of data can be correlated by Friend–Metzner analogy.

Relationship between Heat and Mass;

$$J_M = \frac{f}{2} = \frac{Sh}{Re\, Sc^{\frac{1}{3}}} = J_H = \frac{f}{2} = \frac{Nu}{Re\, Pr^{\frac{1}{3}}}$$
