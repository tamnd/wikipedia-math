# Nicholas Varopoulos

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicholas Varopoulos"}

**Nicholas Theodore Varopoulos** (Νικόλαος Βαρόπουλος, *Nikolaos Varopoulos*, also *Nicolas Varopoulos*; born 16 June 1940) is a Greek mathematician, who works on harmonic analysis and especially analysis on Lie groups.

:::

Varopoulos is the son of the [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaloniki) mathematics professor [Theodore Varopoulos](wikipedia://en/55880521) (1894–1957). Nicholas Varopoulos received his PhD in 1965 from [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) under John Hunter Williamson. There he was in 1965 a lecturer in mathematics. In the academic year 1966–1967 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). Varopoulos became a professor at the [Université Pierre et Marie Curie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Pierre_et_Marie_Curie) (Université Paris VI).

In 1968 Varopoulos became the first recipient of the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize). In 1990 he was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) (*Analysis and geometry on groups*) and in 1970 in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) (*Groupes des fonctions continues en analyse harmoniques*). His doctoral students include [Thomas William Körner](wikipedia://en/8920453) and [Laurent Saloff-Coste](wikipedia://en/45608368).
