# Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics"}

The **Ruth Lyttle Satter Prize in Mathematics**, also called the **Satter Prize**, is one of twenty-one prizes given out by the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) (AMS).[^o3] It is presented biennially in recognition of an outstanding contribution to mathematics research by a woman in the previous six years.[^o4] The award was funded in 1990 using a donation from [Joan Birman](wikipedia://en/8625257), in memory of her sister, [Ruth Lyttle Satter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Lyttle_Satter),[^n1] who worked primarily in biological sciences, and was a proponent for equal opportunities for women in science.[^o6] First awarded in 1991, the award is intended to "honor \[Satter's\] commitment to research and to encourage women in science".[^o7] The winner is selected by the council of the AMS, based on the recommendation of a selection committee.[^o7]

:::

## Award details and history {#Award_details_and_history}

The prize is awarded at the [Joint Mathematics Meetings](wikipedia://en/14985143) during odd numbered years, and has always carried a modest cash reward. Since 2003, the prize has been [\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_dollar)5,000,[^o7][^o9] while from 1997 to 2001, the prize came with \$1,200,[^o13][^o10] and \$4,000 prior to that.[^o11] If a joint award is given, the prize money is split between the recipients.[^o13]

[Dusa McDuff](wikipedia://en/2838322) was the first recipient of the award, for her work on [symplectic geometry](wikipedia://en/294298).[^n2] A joint award was given for the first time in 2001, when [Karen E. Smith](wikipedia://en/41972321) and [Sijue Wu](wikipedia://en/48326992) shared the award.[^o13] The 2013 prize winner was [Maryam Mirzakhani](wikipedia://en/6409655), who, the following year, became the first woman to be awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859), which is considered to be the highest honor a mathematician can receive.[^o14][^o15] She won both awards for her work on "the geometry of Riemann surfaces and their moduli spaces".[^o16] The most recent winner is [Ana Caraiani](wikipedia://en/44896201), who was awarded the prize in 2025 "for contributions to arithmetic geometry and number theory: in particular, the Langlands program.".[^o17]
