# Jenny Harrison

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jenny Harrison"}

**Jenny Harrison** is an American mathematician and professor of mathematics at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). Her research interests include [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) and the intersection of [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694).

:::

## Education and early career {#Education_and_early_career}

Harrison grew up in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, and earned her undergraduate degree from the [University of Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alabama).[^o4] Awarded a [Marshall Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Scholarship), she pursued her graduate studies at the [University of Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warwick). While at Warwick, Harrison produced a counterexample to a conjecture of [Arnaud Denjoy](wikipedia://en/1485520) (a similar one was discovered independently by [Charles Fefferman](wikipedia://en/502831) and [William Thurston](wikipedia://en/39190)) which was published in the [Annals of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/2785) in 1975.[^o5] She completed her PhD that same year under the supervision of [Christopher Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415).

[Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794) served as Harrison's postdoctoral adviser at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) (1975–1976), and she was later a [Miller Research Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Miller_Research_Fellows) at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). She served on the faculty at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and was a Fellow of [Somerville College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_College,_Oxford) from 1978 to 1981, before returning to Berkeley as an assistant professor.

In 1986, after being denied tenure at Berkeley, Harrison filed a lawsuit alleging gender discrimination. The case drew national attention within the American academic community.[^o7] A 1993 settlement led to a new review of her research by an independent panel of seven mathematicians and scientists, who recommended her promotion to full professor.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Harrison specializes in [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474). She developed a theory of [differential chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_chain), a framework that unifies infinitesimal calculus with the classical theory of the smooth continuum.[^o10] Her work in this area provides methods that apply to domains including [soap films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_film), [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractals), and [Whitney stratified spaces](wikipedia://en/9322242).[^o11]

Her research includes applications to the calculus of variations and continuum mechanics. She has notably worked on [Plateau's problem](wikipedia://en/338129), providing proofs regarding the existence of solutions for boundary curves that account for various soap film structures found in nature.[^o12] She also extended the [divergence theorem](wikipedia://en/151864) (Gauss–Green theorem) to apply to fractal boundaries.[^o13] This work builds upon and generalizes previous solutions by [Jesse Douglas](wikipedia://en/387571), [Herbert Federer](wikipedia://en/12185345), and [Wendell Fleming](wikipedia://en/35262774).[^o14]
