# Arithmetic topology

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Arithmetic topology"}

**Arithmetic topology** is an area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) that is a combination of [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). It establishes an analogy between [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) and closed, orientable [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257).

:::

## Analogies {#Analogies}

The following are some of the analogies used by mathematicians between number fields and 3-manifolds:[^o3]

1. A number field corresponds to a closed, orientable 3-manifold
1. [Ideals](wikipedia://en/25977) in the ring of integers correspond to [links](wikipedia://en/1161784), and [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) correspond to knots.
1. The field **Q** of [rational numbers](wikipedia://en/19727024) corresponds to the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792).

Expanding on the last two examples, there is an analogy between [knots](wikipedia://en/366808) and [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) in which one considers "links" between primes. The triple of primes (13, 61, 937) are "linked" modulo 2 (the [Rédei symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rédei_symbol) is −1) but are "pairwise unlinked" modulo 2 (the [Legendre symbols](wikipedia://en/18567) are all 1). Therefore these primes have been called a "proper Borromean triple modulo 2"[^o4] or "mod 2 Borromean primes".[^o5]

## History {#History}

In the 1960s topological interpretations of [class field theory](wikipedia://en/242695) were given by [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933)[^o7] based on [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315), and also by [Michael Artin](wikipedia://en/1519821) and [Jean-Louis Verdier](wikipedia://en/9168893)[^o8] based on [étale cohomology](wikipedia://en/396231). Then [David Mumford](wikipedia://en/394594) (and independently [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086)) came up with an analogy between [prime ideals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_ideals) and [knots](wikipedia://en/366808)[^o9] which was further explored by [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679).[^o10][^o11] In the 1990s Reznikov[^o12] and Kapranov[^o13] began studying these analogies, coining the term **arithmetic topology** for this area of study.
