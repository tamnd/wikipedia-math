# Tony O'Farrell

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tony O'Farrell"}

**Tony O'Farrell** (born Anthony G. O'Farrell in 1947 in Dublin) is an Irish mathematician who is Professor Emeritus at [Maynooth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth_University). He has been in the Mathematics and Statistics Department there since 1975.[^o3]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in Dublin and grew up both there and in [Tipperary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipperary_%28town%29).[^o5]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

He attended [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin) (UCD) earning a BSc in mathematical science (1967).[^o7] After a year working for the [Irish Meteorological Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Meteorological_Service), he returned to UCD for his MSc (1969). He then moved to the USA, to [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) from which he earned a PhD in 1973, for a thesis on "Capacities in Uniform Approximation" done under Brian Cole. After two years at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) (UCLA), during which he published extensively,[^o9] in 1975 he returned to Ireland as Professor of Mathematics at [St. Patrick's College, Maynooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_College,_Maynooth) (later [Maynooth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynooth_University)), outside Dublin. This appointment was notable for two reasons: he was only 28, and, while Maynooth had lay lecturers and senior lecturers, he was the first layman appointed to a chair at this traditionally [pontifical institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_university).[^o10]

O'Farrell has long been active in the [Irish Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Mathematical_Society), serving as president in 1983 and 1984,[^o11] and as editor of the *Bulletin of the IMS* since 2011.

In 1981 he was elected to the Royal Irish Academy.[^o12]

From 1992-1995, he also served as head of the Computer Science Department at Maynooth.[^o3]

In 2002, O'Farrell established Logic Press which publishes mathematics books at various levels in both English and Irish.[^o13] These range from the Irish Mathematical Olympiad Manual[^o14] to undergraduate and postgraduate level texts and research monographs.[^o15]

In 2012, he formally retired from Maynooth, though he remains very active in many arenas.

## Hamilton Walk {#Hamilton_Walk}

In 1990 O’Farrell established the annual [Hamilton Walk](wikipedia://en/58841228), which commemorates the 16 October 1843 discovery of quaternions by [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817). It starts at [Dunsink Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunsink_Observatory) in [County Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Dublin), just west of the city, and follows the [Royal Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canal) east to [Broom Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broom_Bridge). Over the decades, this has grown in popularity and stature, attracting [Nobel laureates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Laureate) and [Fields Medallists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fields_Medallist). O'Farrell's younger colleague Fiacre Ó Cairbre took over the organisation of the walk at the end of the 1990s, but O'Farrell always gives a speech at Broom Bridge. In 2018, O’Farrell and Ó Cairbre received the 2018 [Maths Week Ireland](wikipedia://en/65912318) Award, for "outstanding work in raising public awareness of mathematics" resulting from the founding and nurturing the Hamilton Walk.[^o3]

## Selected papers {#Selected_papers}

- 1973 "An isolated bounded point derivation", Proceedings AMS 39 (1973) 559-562.
- 1974 "A generalized Walsh-Lebesgue theorem", Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh 73A(1974/75) 231-234.
- 1977 "Hausdorff content and rational approximation in fractional Lipschitz norms", Transactions AMS 228 (1977) 187-206.
- 1976 "Sobolev approximation by a sum of subalgebras on the circle" (with J.B. Garnett), Pacific J. Math. 65 (1976) 55-63.
- 1983 "Approximation by a sum of two algebras. The lightning bolt principle" (with D. Marshall). Journal of Functional Analysis, 52 (1983) 353-368.
- 1984 "Approximation by polynomials in two diffeomorphisms" (with K.J. Preskenis), Bulletin AMS, 10 (1984) 105-107
- 1986 "$C^\infty$ maps may increase $C^\infty$ dimension", Inventiones Math. 89 (1987) 663-668. MR 89d: 58022. A preprint appeared as IHES preprint M/86/47 (1986).
- 1992 "The tangent stars of a set, and extensions of smooth functions" (with R.O. Watson), J. für die Reine und Angew. Math., 430 (1992) 109-137. Announced in: Bulletin IMS 26 (1991) 10-1. MR 93h:58015.
- 1997 "An example on Sobolev space approximation", Bulletin L.M.S., 29 (1997) 470-4.
- 1998 "Finitely-generated algebras of smooth functions in one dimension" (with G. Allan, G. Kakiko and R. Watson), J. Functional Analysis, 158 (1998) 458-74.
- 2000 "Pervasive algebras of analytic functions" (with I. Netuka and A. Sanabria), J. Approx. Theory, 106 (2000) 262-75.
- 2002 "De Paepe's disc has nontrivial polynomial hull" (with A. Sanabria), Bull. LMS, 34 (2002) 490-4.
