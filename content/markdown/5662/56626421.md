# Frances Cope

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frances Cope"}

**Elizabeth Frances Cope** (née **Thorndike**; August 19, 1902 – May 14, 1982), was an American mathematician who published on irregular differential equations. The Thorndike nomogram, a two-dimensional diagram of the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144), is named for her.

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Frances Cope was born in New York City to Elizabeth (Moulton) Thorndike and [Edward L. Thorndike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_L._Thorndike), an educational psychologist who taught at [Teachers College, Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers_College,_Columbia_University).[^o6]

She was educated at [Horace Mann School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Mann_School) in New York and at [Drum Hill High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_Hill_High_School) in Peekskill.[^o6] She graduated from [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College) in 1922 and earned her master's degree in mathematics from Columbia University in 1925.[^o6] In a 1926 paper, she first published a two-dimensional diagram of the [Poisson distribution](wikipedia://en/23009144) that is now named the Thorndike nomogram after her.[^o5]

She worked for several years as an engineering assistant at [American Telephone and Telegraph Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Telephone_and_Telegraph_Company) (1922–24, 1925–27) before becoming an instructor of physics at Vassar (1927–28).[^o6] She spent the 1928–29 academic year as a fellow working towards a Ph.D. at [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College), where she met fellow mathematician Thomas Freeman Cope; they married in 1929.[^o6]

In 1930, Cope and her husband moved to Ohio (where he had a job at Marietta College), and two years later she completed her doctorate at Radcliffe as a student of [George David Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/30871906).[^o6] The subject of her Ph.D. thesis was formal solutions of irregular [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations), and the publications that came out of it continue to be cited in the literature of both mathematics and physics.[^o6]

Cope taught mathematics as an instructor at Vassar in 1935–36.[^o6] In 1937 the Copes moved to New York, where she was an instructor of math at Queens College in 1941 and Adelphi College from 1941 to 1943.[^o6]

She died in Montrose, New York.[^o6]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Frances and Thomas had three children, a son and two daughters.[^o6] Thomas survived Frances by two years.[^o6]
