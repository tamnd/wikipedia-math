# Toby Gee

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Toby Gee"}

**Toby Stephen Gee** (born 2 January 1980) is a British [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in number theory and arithmetic aspects of the [Langlands Program](wikipedia://en/171905). He specialises in [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705).

:::

Gee was awarded the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) in 2012,[^o3] the [Leverhulme Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverhulme_Prize) in 2012,[^o4] and was elected as a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) in 2014[^o5] and of the Royal Society in 2024.[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Gee read mathematics at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he was [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) in 2000. After completing his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) with [Kevin Buzzard](wikipedia://en/9277730) at [Imperial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) in 2004, he was a Benjamin Peirce Assistant Professor at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) until 2010. From 2010 to 2011 Gee was an assistant professor at [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University), at which point he moved to Imperial College London, where he has been a professor since 2013.[^o8]

With [Mark Kisin](wikipedia://en/30016901), he proved the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for potentially Barsotti–Tate representations,[^o9] and with [Thomas Barnet-Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Barnet-Lamb) and David Geraghty, he proved the [Sato–Tate conjecture](wikipedia://en/597837) for [Hilbert modular forms](wikipedia://en/2333880).[^o10] One of his most influential ideas has been the introduction of a general 'philosophy of weights', which has clarified some aspects of the emerging mod *p* Langlands philosophy.[^o11]
