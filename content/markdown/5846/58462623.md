# Roger Millsap

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Millsap"}

**Roger Ellis Millsap** (November 18, 1954 – May 9, 2014) was an American psychometrician known for his research on [measurement invariance](wikipedia://en/37409460).[^o3][^o4]

:::

Millsap was born on November 18, 1954, in [Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Washington), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_%28state%29). He was the only child of Max Ellis Millsap, who was a general manager for the [Olympia Oil and Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Oil_and_Wood) company, and Lillian Rogers Millsap (née Turner), an executive director of two chapters of the [Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Cross).[^o3] He received his BS degree from the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington) in 1977, followed by an MA in [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) and a PhD in [quantitative psychology](wikipedia://en/3067624). He received both of his advanced degrees in 1983 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), where he was supervised by Bill Meredith.[^o5][^o6]

After receiving his PhD, Millsap taught [industrial/organizational psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial/organizational_psychology) at [Baruch College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_College) until 1997, where he eventually became a full professor. In 1997, he joined the quantitive psychology faculty of [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University), where he taught until his death in 2014. He served as [editor-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor-in-chief) of *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)* from 1996 to 2006, and of *[Psychometrika](wikipedia://en/22991646)* from 2007 until his death. He also served as president of the [Society of Multivariate Experimental Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Multivariate_Experimental_Psychology) in 2001–2002, of Division 5 of the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association) in 2004–2005, and of the [Psychometric Society](wikipedia://en/20788500) in 2006–2007.[^o3][^o4] His awards included the Society for Multivariate Experimental Psychology's [Raymond Cattell](wikipedia://en/1145683) Award (1993) and [Herb Eber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Eber) Award (2001), as well as three [Tanaka Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_S._Tanaka#Legacy) (1995, 1999, and 2005) for work in *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)*.[^o4]

Millsap died suddenly on May 9, 2014. He was survived by his wife and four children.[^o4]
