# Paul Lorenzen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Lorenzen"}

**Paul Lorenzen** (March 24, 1915 – October 1, 1994) was a German philosopher and mathematician, founder of the Erlangen School (with Wilhelm Kamlah) and inventor of [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121) (with [Kuno Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuno_Lorenz)).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lorenzen studied at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) until he earned his PhD there in 1938 under [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) with a thesis titled *Zur Abstrakten Begründung der multiplikativen Idealtheorie*. In 1933, he joined the [SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturmabteilung) and the German Nazionalist Studenti Union (NSDStB), while, four years later, he became a member of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party).[^o4] In early 1940 he was drafted into the Army.[^o5] Through Hasse's mediation, Lorenzen worked with [Wilhelm Tranow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Tranow) from July 1940 to April 1941 on the Bavy's decoding project.[^o6]

In 1939, he became an assistant to [Wolfgang Krull](wikipedia://en/964328) at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn), where he officially remained until 1949.[^o7] His main work was on the [foundations of mathematics](wikipedia://en/169358)—[proof theory](wikipedia://en/183478). He created and modified [constructive mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_%28mathematics%29). Lorenzen taught at [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford), the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas), and [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) in the USA. He was [John Locke Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke_Lectures) in 1967/1968.

## Theory {#Theory}

Lorenzen came in 1962 to [University of Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen) (South Germany) and founded the [Erlangen School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen_School_of_constructivism) of [epistemological constructivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemological_constructivism) there.

He wrote with Wilhelm Kamlah the famous book *Logical Propaedeutic* ("Logische Propädeutik") and worked on [game semantics](wikipedia://en/617121) (*Dialogische Logik*) with [Kuno Lorenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuno_Lorenz). With [Peter Janich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Janich) he invented *protophysics* of time and space. He developed [constructive logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_logic), [constructive type theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_type_theory) and [constructive analysis](wikipedia://en/239851).

Lorenzen's work on calculus *Differential and Integral* was dedicated to [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544). Lorenzen used Weyl's technique to develop a [predicative](wikipedia://en/1930406) analysis, which can reconstruct [classical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_analysis), without the [principle of excluded middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principle_of_excluded_middle) or the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840). He worked also on [Gerhard Gentzen](wikipedia://en/46027)'s [cut elimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_elimination) to find a way to continue [Hilbert's program after the results of Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_program#Hilbert.27s_program_after_G.C3.B6del).

In the theory of geometry and physics, Lorenzen was influenced by [Hugo Dingler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Dingler). He followed Dingler in building up geometry and physics out of primitive operations. Lorenzen took an early interpretation of [Steven Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Weinberg) (Gravitation and Cosmology, 1972) for his doubts about geometrical elements of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024), believing that [Maxwell's equations](wikipedia://en/19737) are to be modified by general relativity instead.

Lorenzen was also influenced by [Wilhelm Dilthey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Dilthey)'s [hermeneutics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermeneutics), and liked to quote Dilthey's saying that knowledge cannot go behind life. Dilthey's *[Lebensphilosophie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensphilosophie)* was the description of the setting in ordinary experience in which we construct the abstractions of mathematics and physics.

As [John Locke Lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke_Lectures) he invented normative logic as a base on [ethics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics) and political argumentation.
