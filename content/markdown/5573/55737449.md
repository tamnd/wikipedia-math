# Laura Guggenbühl

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laura Guggenbühl"}

**Laura Guggenbühl** (November 18, 1901 – March 8, 1985) was an American mathematician, one of the earliest women in the U.S. to earn a Ph.D. in mathematics, known for her work in [triangle geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_geometry) and the [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Guggenbühl was born in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City), to a family of Swiss immigrants; her father, a butcher and baker, died by 1920. She graduated from [Hunter College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_College) in 1922 with a bachelor's degree in mathematics, after also taking some classes at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) and [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University). She became an instructor at Hunter College while earning a master's degree and Ph.D. from [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College) in 1924 and 1926 respectively. Her dissertation, supervised by [Anna Johnson Pell Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Johnson_Pell_Wheeler), was *An Integral Equation with an Associated Integral Condition*. She became a regular-rank faculty member at Hunter College in 1932 and retired from there as an associate professor in 1972.

She died on a round-the-world cruise, shortly after leaving [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong), after being overcome with grief at the recent death of her brother.

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Although not active in research mathematics after her doctorate, Guggenbühl represented Hunter College at many offerings of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842). She also published several works on the history of mathematics, including biographies of [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496) and [Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach](wikipedia://en/161246), on elementary geometry including [triangle geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_geometry), and on the [Rhind mathematical papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhind_mathematical_papyrus).
