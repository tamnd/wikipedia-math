# Donegall Lectureship at Trinity College Dublin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Donegall Lectureship at Trinity College Dublin"}

*Image: 3rdEarlOfDonnegall.jpg.* The 3rd Earl of Donegall.

The **Donegall Lecturership at Trinity College Dublin** is one of two endowed mathematics positions at [Trinity College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin) (TCD), the other being the [Erasmus Smith's Chair of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Smith's_Chair_of_Mathematics). The Donegall (sometimes spelt Donegal) Lectureship was endowed in 1668 by [the 3rd Earl of Donegall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Chichester,_3rd_Earl_of_Donegall).[^o2][^o3] In 1675, after the [restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Restoration), it was combined with the previous public Professor in Mathematics position that had been created in 1652 by the Commonwealth parliament.

:::

For much of its history, the Donegall Lectureship was awarded to a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) as an additional honour which came with a supplementary income.[^o4] Since 1967, the lectureship has been awarded to a leading international scientist who visits the Department of Pure and Applied Mathematics and gives talks, including a public lecture called the Donegall Lecture.
