# Giuseppe Suzzi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Giuseppe Suzzi"}

*Image: Suzzi - Solutio generalis aequationum tertii gradus, 1747 - 4723909.tif.* *Solutio generalis aequationum tertii gradus*, 1747

**Giuseppe Suzzi** (1701[^o2] - 5 January 1764) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [abbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot).[^o3][^o4][^o5]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Ragogna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragogna), [province of Udine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Udine), he studied in [Udine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udine) and later in [Murano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murano), [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), in the [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) of [Somaschi Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaschi_Fathers), where he was a relevant student of [Giovanni Francesco Crivelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Francesco_Crivelli).[^o3] In 1722 he moved to the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua), when he also took lessons from [Jacopo Riccati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Riccati) privately with Lodovico da Riva. These lessons were published in 1761 in a posthumous book by Riccati, with the solutions of many problems by Suzzi.[^o3] Suzzi, an abbey, published many books on mathematical analysis and in 1744 he became professor of [natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_history) in Padua, where he taught mainly within the tradition of [aristotelianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotelianism), but also giving space to newer philosophical ideas.[^o3] In 1750 he became a member of the Accademia dei Ricovrati in Padua (later [Accademia galileiana di scienze, lettere ed arti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_galileiana_di_scienze,_lettere_ed_arti)). He retired from University in 1762 and died in Venice 5 January 1764.[^o3]
