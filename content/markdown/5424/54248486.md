# Multivalued treatment

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multivalued treatment"}

In statistics, in particular in the [design of experiments](wikipedia://en/9541), a **multi-valued treatment** is a treatment that can take on more than two values. It is related to the [dose-response model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dose–response_relationship) in the medical literature.

:::

## Description {#Description}

Generally speaking, treatment levels may be finite or infinite as well as ordinal or cardinal, which leads to a large collection of possible treatment effects to be studied in applications.[^o3] One example is the effect of different levels of program participation (e.g. full-time and part-time) in a job training program.[^o4]

Assume there exists a finite collection of multi-valued treatment status $T=\{0,1,2,\ldots,J,\}$ with J some fixed [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). As in the potential outcomes framework, denote $Y (j) \subset R$ the collection of potential outcomes under the treatment *J*, and $Y=\textstyle \sum_{j=0}^J \displaystyle D_j Y(j)$ denotes the observed outcome and $D_{j}$ is an indicator that equals **1** when the treatment equals *j* and **0** when it does not equal ***j***, leading to a fundamental problem of [causal inference](wikipedia://en/37103476).[^o5] A general framework that analyzes ordered choice models in terms of marginal treatment effects and average treatment effects has been extensively discussed by Heckman and Vytlacil.[^o6]

Recent work in the [econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390) and statistics literature has focused on estimation and inference for multivalued treatments and ignorability conditions for identifying the treatment effects. In the context of [program evaluation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Program_evaluation), the propensity score has been generalized to allow for multi-valued treatments,[^o7] while other work has also focused on the role of the conditional mean independence assumption.[^o8] Other recent work has focused more on the large sample properties of an estimator of the marginal mean treatment effect conditional on a treatment level in the context of a difference-in-differences model,[^o9] and on the efficient estimation of multi-valued treatment effects in a semiparametric framework.[^o10]
