# Cox process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cox process"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **Cox process**, also known as a **doubly stochastic Poisson process**, is a [point process](wikipedia://en/2147685) which is a generalization of a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) where the intensity that varies across the underlying mathematical space (often space or time) is itself a stochastic process. The process is named after the [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) [David Cox](wikipedia://en/1990771), who first published the model in 1955.[^o2]

:::

Cox processes are used to generate simulations of [spike trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_train) (the sequence of action potentials generated by a [neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron)),[^o3] and also in [financial mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_mathematics) where they produce a "useful framework for modeling prices of financial instruments in which [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) is a significant factor."[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\xi$ be a [random measure](wikipedia://en/10040846).

A random measure $\eta$ is called a Cox process directed by $\xi$, if $\mathcal L(\eta \mid \xi=\mu)$ is a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process) with [intensity measure](wikipedia://en/56214154) $\mu$.

Here, $\mathcal L(\eta \mid \xi=\mu)$ is the conditional distribution of $\eta$, given $\{ \xi=\mu\}$.

:::

## Laplace transform {#Laplace_transform}

If $\eta$ is a Cox process directed by $\xi$, then $\eta$ has the [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610)

$$\mathcal L_\eta(f)=\exp \left(- \int 1-\exp(-f(x))\; \xi(\mathrm dx)\right)$$

for any positive, [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055) $f$.
