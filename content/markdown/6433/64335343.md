# Patricia Grambsch

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Patricia Grambsch"}

**Patricia Louise Meller Grambsch** is an American [biostatistician](wikipedia://en/3878) known for her work on [survival models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_model) including [proportional hazards models](wikipedia://en/5352267). With Terry M. Therneau, she developed the Grambsch–Therneau test, a statistical method used to check the assumptions of the [Cox model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_model). She is an associate professor emerita of biostatistics at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Grambsch completed her Ph.D. in 1980 at the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), with the dissertation *Conditional Likelihood Inference* supervised by [David Hinkley](wikipedia://en/9539349). Before returning to Minnesota as a faculty member, she worked in the survival analysis group at the [Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic) for five years, from 1985 to 1990.

## Research {#Research}

Grambsch's research has focused on [survival analysis](wikipedia://en/419259) and diagnostic methods for statistical models. Her most notable work is on proportional hazards models used in modern survival analysis.

### Grambsch–Therneau test {#Grambsch–Therneau_test}

In 1994, Grambsch and her colleague Terry M. Therneau published the paper "Proportional hazards tests and diagnostics based on weighted residuals" in *[Biometrika](wikipedia://en/1734178)*.[^o5] The paper introduced a formal statistical test, known as the Grambsch–Therneau test, to assess the validity of the proportional hazards assumption in the [Cox proportional hazards model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_proportional_hazards_model). The test is based on the correlation between scaled Schoenfeld residuals and a function of time.[^o6] A non-zero correlation suggests that the assumption is violated.[^o7] This method is a standard diagnostic tool implemented in many statistical software packages, including [R](wikipedia://en/376707), [SAS](wikipedia://en/990677), and [Stata](wikipedia://en/1809002).[^o8]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Grambsch was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association) in 1996.
