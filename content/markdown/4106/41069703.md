# Eugenius Nulty

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Eugenius Nulty"}

**Eugenius Nulty** (1790 – July 3, 1871) was an Irish born American mathematician of the 19th century.[^o3] He served on the faculty of [Dickinson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson_College) from 1814 to 1816, and later taught and tutored prominent Philadelphians, including the brothers [Mathew Carey Lea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_Carey_Lea) and [Henry Charles Lea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Charles_Lea).

:::

## Career {#Career}

After arriving in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) from his native [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland), Nulty quickly became ensconced as a member of the new nation's small intelligentsia. Contemporaries described him as “brilliant”.[^o5][^o6]

In 1814, Nulty became a professor of mathematics at [Dickinson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson_College), where he remained for two years.[^o7][^o8] In 1816 he moved to [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania) at the invitation of The Philadelphia Life Insurance Company and the Pennsylvania Company, who each recruited Nulty as one of the first U.S. actuarial scientists.[^o9] His new countrymen also called Nulty to assist with mathematics for the Survey of the Coast[^o10] (which became the United States Coast Survey in 1836 and the [United States Coast and Geodetic Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_and_Geodetic_Survey) in 1878).

In 1817, Nulty was elected a member of the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o11] In 1823, the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) awarded Nulty an honorary A.M.[^o12] He was elected an Associate Fellow of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) 1832.[^o13] Nulty was also a correspondent of mathematician, chemist and natural philosopher [Robert M. Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Maskell_Patterson).[^o14]

Nulty contributed to the defunct *Mathematical Diary*, one of the 3 earliest learned mathematical journals published in the U.S. His *Elements of Geometry, theoretical and practical* Philadelphia: J. Wetham (1836) was one of the first two or three original geometries published in the United States[^o15] and is still over 150 years later available from multiple publishers in historical reprints.[^o16]

In 1840, P.J. Walker, director of the National Institute for the Promotion of Science, called Nulty "unsurpassed at home or abroad" in pure mathematics.[^o17][^o18]
