# Anita Burdman Feferman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anita Burdman Feferman"}

**Anita Burdman Feferman** (July 27, 1927 – April 9, 2015) was an American [historian of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220) and biographer, known for her biographies of [Jean van Heijenoort](wikipedia://en/2137251) and (with her husband, logician [Solomon Feferman](wikipedia://en/355401)) of [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Feferman was born on July 27, 1927. She was originally from [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles), and attended [Hollywood High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_High_School) and the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) before earning a bachelor's degree in 1948 from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). She became a schoolteacher in the [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California) school system, and earned another degree in teaching from UC Berkeley. In 1956 her husband [Solomon Feferman](wikipedia://en/355401) took a position at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and she moved with him and their two daughters from the [East Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bay) to the [San Francisco Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Peninsula). She died on April 9, 2015.

## Books {#Books}

At Stanford, Feferman became a member of a biography seminar led by [Barbara A. Babcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_A._Babcock) and [Diane Middlebrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Middlebrook). Her first biography, of [Jean van Heijenoort](wikipedia://en/2137251), was *Politics, Logic, and Love: The Life of Jean van Heijenoort* (Jones and Bartlett, 1993), also published as *From Trotsky to Gödel: The Life of Jean van Heijenoort* (CRC Press, 2001). With Solomon Feferman, she was the co-author of a biography of [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920), *Alfred Tarski: Life and Logic* (Cambridge University Press, 2004).
