# CrypTool

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="CrypTool"}

**CrypTool** is an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) project[^o3] that is a free [e-learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-learning) software for illustrating [cryptographic and cryptanalytic concepts](wikipedia://en/18934432).

:::

## History {#History}

The development of CrypTool started in 1998. Originally developed by German companies and universities, it is an [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) project since 2001.[^o5]

Currently 4 versions of CrypTool are maintained and developed: CrypTool 1 (CT1), CrypTool 2 (CT2), JCrypTool (JCT), and CrypTool-Online (CTO). All are available in English and German.[^o6]

The goal of the CrypTool project is to make users aware of how cryptography can help against [network security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_security) threats and to explain the underlying concepts of cryptology.[^o7]

The development of CT1 started in 1998. It is written in [C++](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C++) and designed for the [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows) operating system.

In 2007, development began on two additional projects, both based on a pure-plugin architecture, to serve as successors to the original CT1 program:

- **CrypTool 2**[^o8][^o9][^o10] (built with C#/.NET/WPF) (abbreviated CT2)

uses the concept of [visual programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_programming) to clarify cryptographic processes. Currently, CT2 contains more than 150 crypto functions.

*Image: Keysearcher.png.* Brute-force attack on a reduced version of AES in CrypTool 2

- **JCrypTool 1.0**[^o11][^o12] (built with Java/Eclipse/RCP/SWT) (abbreviated JCT)

runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux, and offers both a document-centric and a function-centric perspective. Currently, JCT contains more than 100 crypto functions. One of its focal points are modern digital signatures (like Merkle trees and [SPHINCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPHINCS)).

The browser software CrypTool-Online (CTO) has been in development since 2010 and is now the one most widely used.

Since 2023, the project has been managed by the CODE research institute at the [University of the Bundeswehr Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Bundeswehr_Munich).[^o13]

CrypTool is used in schools, universities,[^o14] companies and agencies for education and awareness training.[^o15][^o16][^o17]
