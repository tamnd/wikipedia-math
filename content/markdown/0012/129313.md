# Fort Meade

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fort Meade"}

**Fort George G. Meade** is a [United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army) installation located in [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland), that includes the [Defense Information School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Information_School), the [Defense Media Activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Media_Activity), the [United States Army Field Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_bands#Army_Field_Band), and the headquarters of [United States Cyber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cyber_Command), the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939), the [Defense Courier Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Courier_Service), [Defense Information Systems Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Information_Systems_Agency) headquarters, and the [U.S. Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Navy)'s Cryptologic Warfare Group Six.[^o4] It is named for [George G. Meade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_G._Meade), a Union general from the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), who served as commander of the [Army of the Potomac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Potomac). The fort's smaller [census-designated place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade,_Maryland) includes support facilities such as schools, housing, and the offices of the [Military Intelligence Civilian Excepted Career Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Intelligence_Civilian_Excepted_Career_Program) (MICECP).

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Prisoner of War (POW) camp at Fort Meade in 1942.jpg.* A [prisoner of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war) (POW) camp at Fort Meade in 1942 during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II)

### 20th century {#20th_century}

Initially called Camp [Annapolis Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapolis_Junction,_Maryland), the post was opened as "Camp Admiral" in 1917 on \29.7 sqmi acquired for a training camp. The post was called Camp Meade Cantonment by 1918,[^o8] During the First World War, the garrison included the 154th Depot Brigade, which was commanded for part of the conflict by Brigadier General [Tyree R. Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyree_R._Rivers).[^o9]

Camp Franklin Signal Corps school was located there and in 1919, the [Camp Benning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Benning) tank school—formed from the World War I [Camp Colt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Colt,_Pennsylvania) and [Tobyhanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobyhanna_Army_Depot) schools—was transferred to the fort before the [Tank Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Tank_Corps) was disbanded.[^o10]

Renamed to Fort Leonard Wood (February 1928[^o11] – March 5, 1929),[^o12] the fort's [Experimental Motorized Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_Motorized_Force) in the summer and fall of 1928 tested vehicles and tactics in expedition convoys (Camp Meade observers had joined the in-progress [1919 Motor Transport Corps convoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Motor_Transport_Corps_convoy)). In 1929, the fort's [1st Tank Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/66th_Armor_Regiment_%28United_States%29) [encamped on the Gettysburg Battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Battlefield_camps_after_the_American_Civil_War).[^o13]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Fort Meade was used as a [recruit training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training) post and [prisoner of war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war_camp), in addition to a holding center for approximately 384 [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_German_Americans), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Italian_Americans) immigrant residents of the U.S. arrested as potential [fifth columnists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_column). The [Second U.S. Army Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_United_States_Army) transferred to the post on June 15, 1947;[^o12] and in 1957,[^o14] the post became headquarters of the [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939).

#### Cold War air defense {#Cold_War_air_defense}

From the 1950s until the 1970s, the [Fort Meade radar station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Meade_radar_station) had various [radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar) equipment and control systems for air defense, such as the 1st [Martin AN/FSG-I Antiaircraft Defense System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_AN/FSG-I_Antiaircraft_Defense_System).[^o16] Fort Meade also had the first [Nike Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Missile#Nike_Ajax) [surface-to-air missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-air_missile) in December 1953 (operational May 1954)[^o17] and an accidental firing occurred [in 1955 with Battery C, 36th AAA Missile Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accidents_and_incidents_involving_military_aircraft_%281955–59%29#1955). In 1962, the Army's Headquarters and Headquarters Battery, 13th Air Defense Artillery Group, transferred from Meade to [Homestead AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Air_Reserve_Base#Air_Defense_Command) for initial deployment of [MIM-23 Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-23_Hawk) missiles, and during the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis), the 6th Battalion (HAWK), 65th Artillery at Fort Meade (a [United States Strike Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Strike_Command) unit) was deployed to the Miami/Key West area[^o18] (the 8th Battalion (Hawk) was at the fort in late 1964).[^o19] Fort Meade bomb disposal experts were dispatched to secure nuclear bombs in the [1964 Savage Mountain B-52 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Savage_Mountain_B-52_crash).

In 1977, a merger organized the fort's U.S. Army Intelligence Agency as part of the [United States Army Intelligence and Security Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Intelligence_and_Security_Command). On October 1, 1991, a [wing of the Air Force Intelligence Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/694th_Intelligence,_Surveillance_and_Reconnaissance_Group) transferred to Fort Meade, and the organization was replaced by the [70th Operations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/707th_Intelligence,_Surveillance_and_Reconnaissance_Group) on May 1, 2005.[^o20] In the early 1990s, \12.7 sqmi was transferred from the post to the [Patuxent Research Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patuxent_Research_Refuge).[^o24] A planned closure of the post in the 1990s was not implemented, and the Defense Information School moved to the fort in 1995.[^o21] The [311th Signal Command headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/311th_Signal_Command_%28United_States%29) was at Fort Meade from 1996 to September 2006.

### 21st century {#21st_century}

The [70th Intelligence Wing headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70th_Intelligence,_Surveillance_and_Reconnaissance_Wing) was established at Fort Meade on July 17, 2000, and the [Base Realignment and Closure, 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_Realignment_and_Closure,_2005), designated Fort Meade to gain 5,700 positions. Fort Meade currently has more than 54,000 employees (service members and civilians), and is the largest employer in the state of Maryland and second largest installation by employee population in the Army.[^o23]

After an August 27, 2007, [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Environmental_Protection_Agency) order to assess the contamination at 14 [hazardous waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazardous_waste) sites on Fort Meade, such as an ordnance disposal area, 1940s waste dump, closed sanitary landfill,[^o24] a September 2007 environmental impact report identified adding two golf courses would be a "significant threat to the biological and territorial integrity of the Patuxent Research Refuge". The US Army responded that it is "taking steps to limit the environmental damage."[^o25]

### Defense Information Systems Agency {#Defense_Information_Systems_Agency}

After [United States Cyber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cyber_Command) was established at the post in 2009; on April 15, 2011, the [Defense Information Systems Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Information_Systems_Agency) ribbon-cutting for the move from [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), was at the agency's Fort Meade complex of 95 acre.[^o27]

### Defense Information School {#Defense_Information_School}

The consolidation of the [Defense Information School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Information_School) and the Defense Visual Information School in fiscal 1996 and further consolidation with the Defense Photography School in fiscal 1998 created a single focal point in the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense) for these specialties fields. Advancements in information technology and recent base realignment and closure initiatives have contributed to the evolution of the school. The result is a single school proud of its historical roots and dedicated to serving the diverse requirements for public affairs, broadcasting and visual information.[^o27]

### Security incidents {#Security_incidents}

Alleged gunman Hong Young was arrested in connection with shootings at five public places in Maryland, including an NSA building, theaters and occupied vehicles in late February 2015. No motive has been established but his estranged wife attributed his behavior to mental issues, and he told police he heard voices telling him to shoot at a random driver.[^o31]

On March 30, 2015, National Security Agency police officers shot and killed a person who attempted to drive an SUV through a restricted entrance to the NSA campus in Fort Meade, Maryland. A passenger in the SUV was injured, as was an officer, and both were treated at a hospital. President Obama was briefed but the FBI determined "we do not believe it is related to terrorism."[^o32]

On February 14, 2018, National Security Agency police officers shot and wounded an individual who rammed an SUV into a barricade near an entry gate outside of the facility. In the immediate aftermath of the event, the NSA announced that there was "no ongoing security or safety threat."[^o33]

## Geography {#Geography}

Fort Meade is bordered by the [Baltimore–Washington Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore–Washington_Parkway) on the west and is about 5 mi east of [Interstate 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95). It is located between [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), and [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland). It is located in proximity to [Odenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odenton,_Maryland), [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Maryland), [Jessup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessup,_Maryland), [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover,_Maryland), [Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel,_Maryland), and [Severn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severn,_Maryland).[^o35]

## Library of Congress {#Library_of_Congress}

Fort Meade is used as a storage facility for the United States [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress).

In 1994, a 100 acre site located in the U.S. Army Base at Fort Meade, MD was transferred to the U.S. Congress to provide additional storage capacity for the Library of Congress and other legislative bodies. The current master plan includes the land to construct up to 13 Phased Storage Modules for collections, if this number is needed.

In subsequent years, Congress provided construction funds in the Architect of the Capitol budget for Module 1, completed in 2002, for Module 2, completed in 2005 and Modules 3 and 4 and four cold storage rooms, completed in 2009. A full-scale three-year transfer program of the special format collections to Modules 3 and 4 and the four cold storage rooms began in Spring 2010 and was completed in September 2012. Module 5 has been fully funded with occupancy scheduled for September 2017.

The state-of-the art storage modules are being built to store, preserve and protect the library's collections. Collections include books and bound periodicals as well as special format collections, such as maps, manuscripts, prints, photographs, sheet music, and microfilm masters. If needed and constructed, the 13 collections storage modules will provide a total of 180,600 gross sq ft of archival storage space for the library's collections.[^o49]

## Museums {#Museums}

The Fort George G. Meade Museum exhibited the post's historical artifacts, including uniforms, insignia, and equipment.[^o52] The museum also had a small collection of vehicles, including a [Renault FT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_FT), a [MK VIII Liberty Tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_Mark_VIII), an [M3A1 Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3A1_Stuart), an [M4A3E8 Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_Sherman), an [M41 Walker Bulldog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M41_Walker_Bulldog), an [M47 Patton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M47_Patton), armored personnel carriers such as an [M113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113), [M114](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M114_armored_fighting_vehicle), [M84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M59_armored_personnel_carrier), a [Nike Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Ajax) missile, and a [UH-1H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-1H) helicopter. The Fort George G. Meade Community Council noted in July 2018 that the museum would close, with artifacts relocated to the [National Museum of the United States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_United_States_Army) under construction in [Fort Belvoir, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Belvoir,_Virginia).[^o53]

## Transportation {#Transportation}

Since 2005, the NSA operates a shuttle service from the [Odenton station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odenton_station) of [MARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARC_Train) to its Visitor Control Center at Fort Meade. In 2009, the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army) established a similar shuttle service from the Odenton station to the Army section of Fort Meade; the NSA operates this service, allowing garrison employees, persons with Fort Meade visitor passes, and [U.S. Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Defense) IDs to board.[^o55]

## Education {#Education}

Dependent children living on post are zoned to schools operated by the [Anne Arundel County Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Arundel_County_Public_Schools). The school district operates an early childhood center, three elementary schools, two middle schools, and one high school on-post.[^o57]

On-post schools include:[^o58]

## Housing concerns {#Housing_concerns}

In February 2019, [Secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army) [Mark Esper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Esper), Chief of Staff [Mark Milley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Milley), and Sgt. Maj. of the Army [Daniel A. Dailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_A._Dailey) met with the commander of [IMCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMCOM), the Fort Meade garrison commander, and Army families over safety concerns with housing units on the base in which residents were [exposed to lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_poisoning) and [asbestos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_impact_of_asbestos).[^o60] After speaking with the CEO for the company which manages the house maintenance of the installation, the senior leaders of the Army will determine necessary actions.[^o60] "We are deeply troubled by the recent reports highlighting the deficient conditions in some of our family housing. It is unacceptable for our families who sacrifice so much to have to endure these hardships in their own homes."—Secretary of the Army, Mark T. Esper and Chief of Staff of the Army, Gen. Mark A. Milley[^o61]
