# Walter Gröbli

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Walter Gröbli"}

**Walter Gröbli** (23 September 1852 – 26 June 1903) was a Swiss [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

His father, [Issak Gröbli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issak_Gröbli), was an industrial who was invented a [shuttle embroidery machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Gallen_embroidery#Further_developments) in 1863, and his old brother is credited to have introduced the invention in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^n1] Walter Gröbli was more interested in mathematics than in embroidery[^n2] and he studied from 1871 to 1875 at the [Polytechnicum of Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) under [Hermann Schwarz](wikipedia://en/3165834) and [Heinrich Martin Weber](wikipedia://en/2643615).[^n3] Then Gröbli studied at [university of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) and he was awarded a doctorate in the [university of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) (1877).

The following six years, Gröbli was assistant of [Frobenius](wikipedia://en/341048) in Polytechnicum of Zürich. In 1883 he was elected mathematics professor in the *Gymnasium* of [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich).[^n4] Despite his mathematical talent he did not follow a research career, he was happy to be a schoolmaster.[^n5]

His other main passion was mountaineering. He died with other three colleagues on a mountain accident climbing the [Piz Blas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piz_Blas).[^n6]

The only work known by Gröbli was his doctoral thesis dissertation. It deals about three vortex motion, four vortex motion having an axis of symmetry and $2n$ vortex motion having $n$ symmetry axes.[^n7] This work is a classical in [vortex dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_fluid_mechanics#Developments_in_vortex_dynamics) literature.[^n8]
