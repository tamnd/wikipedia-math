# Karl Bopp

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Karl Bopp"}

**Karl Bopp** (28 March 1877 – 5 December 1934) was a [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans) [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian) of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bopp studied at the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) and the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg) under [Moritz Cantor](wikipedia://en/307975). In 1906 he [habilitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) with a work about the [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673) of [Grégoire de Saint-Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grégoire_de_Saint-Vincent), and in 1915 he became [professor extraordinarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor#Germany) in Heidelberg. As successor of Moritz Cantor he taught [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), political arithmetic, and [Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance). In 1933 he became ill and died in 1934.

Bopp's special field of interest were researches about [Johann Heinrich Lambert](wikipedia://en/93085). He edited Lambert's *Monatsbuch*, his letter exchanges with [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) and [Abraham Gotthelf Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Gotthelf_Kästner), and his [philosophical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) writings. Bopp wrote many historical papers, including two studies on the history of [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939), and the re-publication of a paper by [Nicolas Fatio de Duillier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Fatio_de_Duillier) on the cause of [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation). Under his supervision many [dissertations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissertation) were written by his students.[^o4]
