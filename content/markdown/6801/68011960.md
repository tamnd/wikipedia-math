# Condensed mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Condensed mathematics"}

**Condensed mathematics** is a theory developed by [Dustin Clausen](wikipedia://en/76038574) and [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) which replaces a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) by a certain [sheaf of sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheaf_of_sets), in order to solve some technical problems of doing [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) on [topological groups](wikipedia://en/42315).

:::

Essentially the same notion was also introduced by [Barwick](wikipedia://en/57157400) and Haine under the name *[pyknotic set](wikipedia://en/77327179)*.

The theory aims to unify various [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) subfields, including [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In particular, [Kiran Kedlaya](wikipedia://en/31688876) described condensed mathematics as "technology for doing commutative algebra over topological rings."[^o2]

## Idea {#Idea}

The fundamental idea in the development of the theory is given by replacing [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) by *condensed sets*, defined below. The [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of condensed sets, as well as related categories such as that of condensed [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974), are much better behaved than the [category of topological spaces](wikipedia://en/435051). In particular, unlike the category of [topological abelian groups](wikipedia://en/42315), the category of condensed abelian groups is an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063), which allows for the use of tools from [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410) in the study of those structures.

The framework of condensed mathematics turns out to be general enough that, by considering various "spaces" with [sheaves](wikipedia://en/245466) valued in condensed algebras, one might expect to be able to incorporate [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [*p*-adic analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/5828941) and [complex analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/18748850).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Liquid vector space"}

### Liquid vector space {#Liquid_vector_space}

In condensed mathematics, **liquid vector spaces** are alternatives to complete [topological vector spaces](wikipedia://en/45752),[^o7][^o8] the category of which has better abstract properties than that of complete topological vector spaces. This allows for more abstract approaches using tools such as [abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories).

:::

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A *condensed set* is a [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of sets on the [site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_%28mathematics%29) of [profinite sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profinite_set), with the [Grothendieck topology](wikipedia://en/12910) given by finite, jointly surjective collections of maps. Similarly, a *condensed group*, *condensed ring*, etc. is defined as a sheaf of groups, rings etc. on this site.

To any topological space $X$ one can associate a condensed set, customarily denoted $\underline X$, which to any profinite set $S$ associates the set of continuous maps $S\to X$. If $X$ is a topological group or ring, then $\underline X$ is a condensed group or ring.

:::

## History {#History}

In 2013, [Bhargav Bhatt](wikipedia://en/67165881) and [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306) introduced a general notion of *pro-[étale](wikipedia://en/5707590) site* associated to an arbitrary [scheme](wikipedia://en/364754). In 2018, Dustin Clausen and Scholze arrived at the conclusion that the pro-étale site of a single point, which is isomorphic to the site of profinite sets introduced above, already has rich enough structure to [realize](wikipedia://en/52119) large classes of topological spaces as sheaves on it. Further developments have led to a theory of condensed sets and *solid abelian groups*, through which one is able to incorporate [non-Archimedean geometry](wikipedia://en/35093912) into the theory.[^o12]

In 2020 Scholze completed a proof of their results which would enable the incorporation of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) as well as complex geometry into the condensed mathematics framework, using the notion of *[liquid vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_vector_space)*. The argument has turned out to be quite subtle, and to get rid of any doubts about the validity of the result, he asked other mathematicians to provide a [formalized and verified proof](wikipedia://en/1197184).[^o13][^o15] Over a 6-month period, a group led by Johan Commelin verified the central part of the proof using the [proof assistant](wikipedia://en/1258607) [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984).[^o14][^o15] As of 14 July 2022, the proof has been completed.[^o16]

Coincidentally, in 2019 Barwick and Haine introduced a similar theory of *[pyknotic objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyknotic_object)*. This theory is very closely related to that of condensed sets, with the main differences being set-theoretic in nature: pyknotic theory depends on a choice of [Grothendieck universes](wikipedia://en/1072144), whereas condensed mathematics can be developed strictly within [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC).[^o17]
