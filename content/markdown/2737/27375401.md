# Sanad ibn Ali

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sanad ibn Ali"}

**Abu al-Tayyib Sanad ibn Ali**, also known as **Sind ibn Ali** (died c. 864 C.E.),[^o3] was a ninth-century [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer), [translator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Translator), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer) during [Islamic Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Golden_Age) who was employed at the court of the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) caliph [Al-Ma'mun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ma'mun). A later convert to [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam), Sanad's father was a learned astronomer who lived and worked in [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad).[^o4]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Sanad ibn Ali was either an [Iraqi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Jewish)[^o6] or a Sindhi from [Mansura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansura,_Sindh), capital of [Arab Sind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Sind).[^o7] He is known to have translated and modified the [Zij al-Sindhind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zij_al-Sindhind). The Zij al-Sindhind was the first astronomical table ever introduced in the [Muslim World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_World). As a mathematician Sanad ibn ʿAlī was a colleague of [al-Khwarizmi](wikipedia://en/649861) and worked closely with [Yaqūb ibn Tāriq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqūb_ibn_Tāriq) together they calculated the [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007) of the [Earth](wikipedia://en/9228) and other astronomical bodies. He also wrote a commentary on *[Kitāb al-ğabr wa-l-muqābala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Compendious_Book_on_Calculation_by_Completion_and_Balancing)* and helped prove the works of al-Khwarizmi. The [decimal point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_point) notation to the [Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) was introduced by Sanad ibn Ali.

According to [Ibn Abi Usaibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Abi_Usaibia): the [Banū Mūsā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banū_Mūsā) brothers out of sheer professional jealousy kept him away from [Abbasid Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliph) [al-Mutawakkil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Mutawakkil) at his new capital [Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarra) and had caused Sanad ibn ʿAlī to be sent away to [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad). Both [Ja'far Muhammad ibn Mūsā ibn Shākir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja'far_Muhammad_ibn_Mūsā_ibn_Shākir) and [Ahmad ibn Mūsā ibn Shākir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Mūsā_ibn_Shākir) delegated the work of digging a great [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) instead to [Al-Farghani](wikipedia://en/607963) and thus ignoring Sanad ibn ʿAlī, the better engineer. Al-Farghani committed a great error, making the beginning of the canal deeper than the rest and water never reached the new garrison of *Al-Ja'fariya*. News of this greatly angered al-Mutawakkil and the two Banū Mūsā brothers were saved from severe punishment only by the gracious willingness of Sanad ibn ʿAlī, to vouch the corrections of Al-Farghani's calculations thus risking his own welfare and possibly his life.
