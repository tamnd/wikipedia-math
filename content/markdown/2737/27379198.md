# Brusselator

*Image: Bruesselator.svg.* Top: The Brusselator in the unstable regime (A=1, B=3): The system approaches a [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950) Bottom: The Brusselator in a stable regime with A=1 and B=1.7: For B\<1+A<sup>2</sup> the system is stable and approaches a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738).

*Image: Brusselator space.gif.* Simulation of the Brusselator as reaction diffusion system in two spatial dimensions

*Image: Brusselator Oscillations HQ Render.gif.* Simulation[^o1] of the reaction-diffusion system of the Brusselator with reflective border conditions

The **Brusselator** is a theoretical model for a type of [autocatalytic reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocatalytic_reaction). The Brusselator model was proposed by [Ilya Prigogine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Prigogine) and his collaborators at the [Université Libre de Bruxelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Université_Libre_de_Bruxelles).[^o3][^o4]

:::

It is a portmanteau of [Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels) and [oscillator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillator).

It is characterized by the reactions

$$A \rightarrow X$$

$$2X + Y \rightarrow 3X$$

$$B + X \rightarrow Y + D$$

$$X \rightarrow E$$

Under conditions where A and B are in vast excess and can thus be modeled at constant concentration, the rate equations become

$${d \over dt}\left[ X \right] = \left[A \right] + \left[ X \right]^2 \left[Y \right] - \left[B \right] \left[X \right] - \left[X \right] \,$$

$${d \over dt}\left[ Y \right] = \left[B \right] \left[X \right] - \left[ X \right]^2 \left[Y \right] \,$$

where, for convenience, the rate constants have been set to 1.

The Brusselator has a fixed point at

$$\left[ X \right] = \left[ A \right] \,$$

$$\left[ Y \right] = {\left[ B \right] \over \left[ A \right]} \,.$$

The fixed point becomes unstable when

$$\left[ B \right]>1+\left[ A \right]^2 \,$$

leading to an oscillation of the system. Unlike the [Lotka–Volterra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotka–Volterra) equation, the oscillations of the Brusselator do not depend on the amount of reactant present initially. Instead, after sufficient time, the oscillations approach a [limit cycle](wikipedia://en/452950).[^o5]

The best-known example is a [chemical clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_clock), such as the [Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction](wikipedia://en/186262) (BZ reaction). It can be created with a mixture of potassium bromate (KBrO<sub>3</sub>), malonic acid (CH<sub>2</sub>(COOH)<sub>2</sub>), and manganese sulfate (MnSO<sub>4</sub>) prepared in a heated solution of [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid) (H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>).[^o6]
