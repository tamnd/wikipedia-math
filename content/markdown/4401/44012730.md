# Kosnita's theorem

*Image: Kosnita points.svg.* Kosnita point of an arbitrary triangle. solid black 2px solid black 1px solid #ff99ff 2px solid blue 2px

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kosnita's theorem"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), **Kosnita's theorem** is a property of certain [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) associated with an arbitrary [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654).

:::

Let $\triangle ABC$ be an arbitrary triangle, $O$ its [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) and $O_{a}$, $O_{b}$, $O_{c}$ are the circumcenters of three triangles $\triangle OBC$, $\triangle OCA$, and $\triangle OAB$ respectively. The theorem asserts that the three [straight lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_line) $AO_{a}$, $BO_{b}$, and $CO_{c}$ are concurrent.[^o7] This result was established by the Romanian mathematician [Cezar Coşniţă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cezar_Coşniţă) (1910–1962).[^o3]

Their point of concurrence is known as the triangle's **Kosnita point** (named by Rigby in 1997). It is the [isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of the [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570).[^o5][^o4] It is [triangle center](wikipedia://en/12401488) $X(54)$ in [Clark Kimberling's list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberling_center).[^o6]
