# Vecten points

*Image: Vectenpoints.svg.* black solid solid #0373fc solid black 1px solid lime solid red 1px solid red

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vecten points"}

In [Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/9417), the **Vecten points** are two [triangle centers](wikipedia://en/12401488), points associated with any [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). They may be found by constructing three [squares](wikipedia://en/659939) on the sides of the triangle, connecting each square centre by a line to the opposite triangle point, and finding the point where these three lines meet. The outer and inner Vecten points differ according to whether the squares are extended outward from the triangle sides, or inward.

:::

The Vecten points are named after an early 19th-century French mathematician named Vecten, who taught mathematics with [Gergonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Diaz_Gergonne) in [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nîmes) and published a study of the figure of three squares on the sides of a triangle in 1817.[^o2]

## Outer Vecten point {#Outer_Vecten_point}

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any given [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). On the sides $BC, CA, AB$ of the triangle, construct outwardly drawn three squares with centres $O_{a}, O_{b}, O_{c}$ respectively. Then the lines $AO_{a}, BO_{b}, CO_{c}$ are concurrent. The point of concurrence is the outer Vecten point of $\triangle ABC$.

In [Clark Kimberling](wikipedia://en/25536421)'s [Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593), the outer Vecten point is denoted by *X*(485).[^o4]

[Isogonal conjugate](wikipedia://en/2083100) of outer Vecten point is Kenmotu point.[^o4]

## Inner Vecten point {#Inner_Vecten_point}

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any given [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654). On the sides $BC, CA, AB$ of the triangle, construct inwardly drawn three squares respectively with centres $I_{a}, I_{b}, I_{c}$ respectively. Then the lines $AI_{a}, BI_{b}, CI_{c}$ are concurrent. The point of concurrence is the inner Vecten point of $\triangle ABC$.

In the Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers, the inner Vecten point is denoted by *X*(486).[^o4]

The line $X_{485}X_{486}$ meets the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) at the [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570) of $\triangle ABC$. The Vecten points lie on the [Kiepert hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_hyperbola).
