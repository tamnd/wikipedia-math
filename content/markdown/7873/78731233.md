# Taniyama's problems

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Taniyama's problems"}

**Taniyama's problems** are a set of 36 [mathematical problems](wikipedia://en/183091) posed by [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Yutaka Taniyama](wikipedia://en/1077843) in 1955. The problems primarily focused on [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and the connections between [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) and [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225). Taniyama's twelfth and thirteenth problems were the precursor to the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture), also known as the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475), which would be used in [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)' [proof](wikipedia://en/21950759) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in 1995.

:::

## History {#History}

*Image: Jean-Pierre Serre 2003.jpg.* French mathematician [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), a participant in the 1955 international symposium, brought attention to Taniyama's problems in the early 1970s.

In the 1950s [post-World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-World_War_II) period of mathematics, there was renewed interest in the theory of [modular curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_curves) due to the work of Taniyama and [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724).[^o3] During the 1955 international symposium on [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) at [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo) and [Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkō), Taniyama compiled his 36 problems in a document titled *"Problems of Number Theory"* and distributed [mimeographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimeograph) of his collection to the symposium's participants.[^o4][^o5] Serre later brought attention to these problems in the early 1970s.[^o3]

The most famous of Taniyama's problems are his twelfth and thirteenth problems.[^o3][^o4] These problems led to the formulation of the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) (now known as the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475)), which states that every elliptic curve over the rational numbers is [modular](wikipedia://en/4559850). This conjecture played a major role in [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027)' [proof](wikipedia://en/21950759) of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in 1995.[^o4][^o5]

Taniyama's problems influenced the development of the [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905), the theory of [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), and the study of [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o4]

## The problems {#The_problems}

Taniyama's tenth problem addressed [Dedekind zeta functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_zeta_functions) and [Hecke L-series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_L-series), and while distributed in English at the 1955 [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo)-[Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkō) conference attended by both [Serre](wikipedia://en/205483) and [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), it was only formally published in Japanese in Taniyama's collected works.[^o3]

::: {#o7 .proof cue=math-proof name="Taniyama's tenth problem (translated)"}

**Taniyama's tenth problem (translated).**

Let $k$ be a totally real [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), and $F(\tau)$ be a [Hilbert modular form](wikipedia://en/2333880) to the field $k$. Then, choosing $F(\tau)$ in a suitable manner, we can obtain a system of [Erich Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990)'s *L*-series with [Größencharakter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Größencharakter) $\lambda$, which [corresponds one-to-one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-to-one_correspondence) to this $F(\tau)$ by the process of [Mellin transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellin_transformation). This can be proved by a generalization of the theory of operator $T$ of [Hecke](wikipedia://en/521837) to [Hilbert modular functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_modular_functions) (cf. [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544)).[^o3]

:::

According to [Serge Lang](wikipedia://en/872991), Taniyama's eleventh problem deals with [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) with complex multiplication, but is unrelated to Taniyama's twelfth and thirteenth problems.[^o3]

::: {#o8 .proof cue=math-proof name="Taniyama's twelfth problem (translated)"}

**Taniyama's twelfth problem (translated).**

Let $C$ be an [elliptic curve](wikipedia://en/10225) defined over an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) $k$, and $L_C(s)$ the [*L*-function](wikipedia://en/245283) of $C$ over $k$ in the sense that $\zeta_C(s) = \zeta_k(s) \zeta_k(s-1) /L_C(s)$ is the zeta function of $C$ over $k$. If the [Hasse–Weil conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasse–Weil_conjecture) is true for $\zeta_C(s)$, then the [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) obtained from $L_C(s)$ by the inverse [Mellin transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellin_transformation) must be an [automorphic form](wikipedia://en/782146) of dimension −2 of a special type (see [Hecke](wikipedia://en/1233990)). If so, it is very plausible that this form is an elliptic [differential](wikipedia://en/677191) of the field of associated automorphic functions. Now, going through these observations backward, is it possible to prove the Hasse–Weil conjecture by finding a suitable automorphic form from which $L_C(s)$ can be obtained?[^o9][^o3]

:::

Taniyama's twelfth problem's significance lies in its suggestion of a deep connection between [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves) and [modular forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_forms). While Taniyama's original formulation was somewhat imprecise, it captured a profound insight that would later be refined into the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475).[^o10][^o4] The problem specifically proposed that the *L*-functions of elliptic curves could be identified with those of certain modular forms, a connection that seemed surprising at the time.

Fellow Japanese mathematician [Goro Shimura](wikipedia://en/174724) noted that Taniyama's formulation in his twelfth problem was unclear: the proposed [Mellin transform](wikipedia://en/387297) method would only work for elliptic curves over [rational numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_numbers).[^o10] For curves over [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field), the situation is substantially more complex and remains unclear even at a conjectural level today.[^o4]

::: {#o11 .proof cue=math-proof name="Taniyama's thirteenth problem (translated)"}

**Taniyama's thirteenth problem (translated).**

To characterize the field of elliptic modular functions of [level](wikipedia://en/4559850#Modularity_theorem) $N$, and especially to decompose the [Jacobian variety](wikipedia://en/489734) $J$ of this [function field](wikipedia://en/2783843) into simple factors up to [isogeny](wikipedia://en/1085176). Also it is well known that if $N = q$, a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666), and $q \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, then $J$ contains elliptic curves with complex multiplication. What can one say for general $N$?[^o10]

:::
