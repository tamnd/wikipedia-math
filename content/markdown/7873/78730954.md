# List of common misconceptions about science, technology, and mathematics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="List of common misconceptions about science, technology, and mathematics"}

Each entry on this list of common misconceptions is worded as a correction; the misconceptions themselves are implied rather than stated. These entries are concise summaries; the main subject articles can be consulted for more detail.

:::

## Astronomy and spaceflight {#Astronomy_and_spaceflight}

::: {#o3 .entry cue=list-entry name="astrology"}

- There is no scientific evidence that the motion of stars, planets, and other celestial bodies influences the fates of humans, and [astrology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrology_and_science) has repeatedly been shown to have no explanatory power in predicting future events.

:::

::: {#o7 .entry cue=list-entry name="weightless"}

- Astronauts in orbit have the sensation of being [weightless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness) because they are in [free fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_fall) around the Earth, not because they are so far away from the Earth that its gravitational pull is negligible. For example, on the [International Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station) the Earth's gravity is nearly 90% as strong as at the surface. Objects orbiting in space would not remain in orbit if not for the gravitational force, and gravitational fields extend infinitely, though the strength of the gravitational force is attenuated with distance. There is no limit to the distance across which two objects exert gravitational forces upon one another.

:::

::: {#o12 .entry cue=list-entry name="dark side of the Moon"}

- *Image: Back side of the Moon AS16-3021.jpg.* The [dark side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon) directly illuminated by the Sun The [dark side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon) receives about the same amount of light from the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) as the [near side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_side_of_the_Moon). Since the same side of the Moon always faces the Earth due to [tidal locking](wikipedia://en/60162), the far side had not been seen until [Luna 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_3) photographed it in 1959. Hence, "dark" refers to the far side being unseen from Earth, not to any lack of illumination by the Sun. [^o13]

:::

::: {#o14 .entry cue=list-entry name="Black hole"}

- [Black holes](wikipedia://en/4650) have the same gravitational effects as any other equal mass in their place. They will draw objects towards them according to distance and mass, just as any other celestial body does, except at very close distances to the black hole, comparable to its [Schwarzschild radius](wikipedia://en/206115). If, for example, the Sun were replaced by a black hole of equal mass, the orbits of the planets would be essentially unaffected.

:::

::: {#o17 .entry cue=list-entry name="Equator"}

- *Image: Earth seasons seen as terminator motion.gif.* Earth's [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator) does not line up with the [ecliptic](wikipedia://en/9264) ([plane](wikipedia://en/3074135) of [Earth's orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_orbit)), so for half of the year, the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) is tilted more toward the [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun), and for the other half, the Northern Hemisphere is tilted more away, causing [seasonal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_lag). Seasons are not caused by Earth being [closer to the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_eccentricity) in the summer than in the winter, but by [the effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_of_Sun_angle_on_climate) of Earth's 23.4-degree axial tilt. Each hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun in its respective summer, resulting in longer days and more perpendicular sunlight, with the opposite being true in the winter. Earth reaches [the point in its orbit closest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perihelion) to the Sun in January, and it reaches [the point farthest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) from the Sun in July, so the [slight contribution of orbital eccentricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season#Elliptical_Earth_orbit) opposes the temperature trends of the seasons in the Northern Hemisphere.

:::

::: {#o21 .entry cue=list-entry name="enters the atmosphere"}

- When a meteor or spacecraft [enters the atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry), the [heat of entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_entry#Real_%28equilibrium%29_gas_model) is not primarily caused by friction, but by [adiabatic compression of air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabatic_heating) in front of the object.

:::

::: {#o25 .entry cue=list-entry name="Egg balancing"}

- [Egg balancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_balancing) is possible on every day of the year, not just the [vernal equinox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_equinox).

:::

::: {#o28 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fisher Space Pen"}

- The [Fisher Space Pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_Space_Pen) was not commissioned by NASA at a cost of millions of dollars, while the Soviets used pencils. Pencils posed a major risk to astronauts due to the release of substances such as shavings and pencil lead being a flight hazard.[^o29] The pen was independently developed by Paul C. Fisher, founder of the Fisher Pen Company, with \$1 million of his own funds (equivalent to \$US million in US).[^o30] NASA tested and approved the pen for space use, then purchased 400 pens at \$6 per pen (US).[^o31] The Soviet Union subsequently also purchased the Space Pen for its [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_%28spacecraft%29) spaceflights.

:::

::: {#o33 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tang"}

- [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_%28drink%29), [Velcro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velcro), and [Teflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teflon) were not [spun off from technology originally developed by NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_spinoff_technologies) for spaceflight, though many other products (such as [memory foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_foam) and [space blankets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_blanket)) were.

:::

::: {#o36 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sun"}

- The [Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun) is not yellow; rather, it emits light across the full [spectrum of visible colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_spectrum), and this combined light appears white when outside of Earth's atmosphere. Earth's atmosphere [scatters](wikipedia://en/26176) shorter wavelengths of light, particularly blues and violets, more than longer wavelengths like reds and yellows, and this scattering is why the Sun appears yellow during the day or orange or red during sunrise and sunset. The scattered blue/violet light, appearing to come from all directions, is what makes the rest of the sky look blue.

:::

::: {#o39 .entry cue=list-entry name="Great Wall of China"}

- *Image: Great Wall of China, Satellite image.jpeg.* A satellite image of a section of the [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China), running diagonally from lower left to upper right (not to be confused with the much more prominent river running from upper left to lower right) The [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China) is not the only [human-made object visible from space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_structures_visible_from_space) or from the Moon. None of the [Apollo astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Apollo_astronauts) reported seeing *any* specific human-made object from the Moon, and even Earth-orbiting astronauts can see it only with magnification. City lights, however, are easily visible on the night side of Earth from orbit.

:::

::: {#o42 .entry cue=list-entry name="Big Bang"}

- The [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116) model does not fully explain the [origin of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_universe). It does not describe how energy, time, and space were caused, but rather it describes the emergence of the present universe from an ultra-dense and high-temperature initial state.[^o43]

:::

## Biology {#Biology}

### Mammals {#Mammals}

::: {#o46 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bats are not blind"}

- [Bats are not blind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat#Vision). While about 70% of bat species, mainly in the [microbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbat) family, use [echolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_echolocation) to navigate, all bat species have eyes and are capable of sight. In addition, almost all bats in the [megabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabat) or fruit bat family cannot echolocate and have excellent night vision.[^o47]

:::

::: {#o48 .entry cue=list-entry name="professional bullfighters"}

- *Image: 10 - Cristina Sánchez.jpg.* The color of a red cape does not enrage a bull. Bulls are not enraged by the color red, used in capes by [professional bullfighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullfighter). Cattle are [dichromats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dichromacy), so red does not stand out as a bright color. It is not the color of the cape, but the perceived threat by the bullfighter that incites it to charge, along with the spearing of the bull, mental agitation and abuse it endures beforehand.[^o49]

:::

::: {#o50 .entry cue=list-entry name="Camels"}

- [Camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel#Ecological_and_behavioral_adaptations) do not store water in their humps, but rather fatty tissue which can be used as a reserve source of calories. While they can go long periods without water, the water is stored in the animal's bloodstream, not their humps.[^o51]

:::

::: {#o52 .entry cue=list-entry name="Domestic cats'"}

- [Domestic cats'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat) behavioral and personality traits cannot be predicted from their coat color. Rather, these traits depend on a complex interplay between genetic and environmental factors.[^o53]

:::

::: {#o54 .entry cue=list-entry name="Catnip"}

- Not all cats are attracted and intoxicated by [catnip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catnip), which affects only about two thirds of them. Alternatives exist, such as [valerian root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerian_root) and leaves.[^o55][^o56]

:::

::: {#o57 .entry cue=list-entry name="salivating"}

- Dogs do not sweat by [salivating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salivation).[^o58] Dogs actually do have [sweat glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweat_gland), but not on their tongues; they sweat mainly through their footpads. However, dogs do primarily regulate their body temperature through [panting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoregulation). (See also: Dog anatomy)

:::

::: {#o60 .entry cue=list-entry name="Aging in dogs"}

- Dogs do not consistently age seven times as quickly as humans. [Aging in dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_in_dogs) varies widely depending on the breed; certain breeds, such as [giant dog breeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_dog_breeds) and [English Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Bulldog), have much shorter lifespans than average.[^o61] Most dogs reach adolescence by one year old; smaller and medium-sized breeds begin to age more slowly in adulthood.[^o62]

:::

::: {#o63 .entry cue=list-entry name="Elephants' graveyard"}

- Old elephants near death do not leave their herd to go to an "[elephants' graveyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephants'_graveyard)" to die.[^o64]

:::

::: {#o65 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hipposudoric acid"}

- The hippopotamus does not produce pink milk, nor does it sweat blood. The skin secretions of the hippopotamus are red due to the presence of [hipposudoric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipposudoric_acid), a red pigment which acts as a natural sunscreen, and is neither sweat nor blood. It does not affect the color of their milk, which is white or beige.

:::

::: {#o67 .entry cue=list-entry name="do not engage in mass suicide"}

- Lemmings [do not engage in mass suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemming#Misconceptions) by diving off cliffs. The idea was popularized by the 1958 [Disney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney) documentary film *[White Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Wilderness_%28film%29)*, in which the lemmings were repeatedly shoved off a cliff by the filmmakers to create the illusion of a mass suicide. The misconception itself is much older, dating back to at least the late 19th century, though its exact origins are uncertain.[^o68]

:::

::: {#o71 .entry cue=list-entry name="Mice do not have a special appetite for cheese"}

- [Mice do not have a special appetite for cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouse#Diet), and will eat it only for lack of better options; they actually favor sweet sugary foods. The myth may have come from the fact that before the advent of refrigeration, cheese was usually stored outside and was therefore a food easy for mice to reach.

:::

::: {#o73 .entry cue=list-entry name="Porcupines do not shoot their quills"}

- [Porcupines do not shoot their quills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupine#Quills). They can detach, and porcupines will deliberately back into attackers to impale them, but their quills do not project.

:::

::: {#o77 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rabbits are not especially partial to carrots"}

- [Rabbits are not especially partial to carrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit#Modern_times). Their diet in the wild primarily consists of dark green vegetables such as grasses and clovers, and excessive carrot consumption is unhealthy for them due to containing high levels of sugar.

:::

::: {#o79 .entry cue=list-entry name="Skunk"}

- Tomato juice and sauce are ineffective at neutralizing the odor of a [skunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skunk). Rather, due to [olfactory fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_fatigue), a person sprayed by a skunk loses sensitivity to the smell over time.[^o80] [Effective treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skunk#Mitigation) for skunk odor involve artificial compounds rather than household remedies.

:::

::: {#o82 .entry cue=list-entry name="alpha"}

- There is no such thing as an "[alpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_%28ethology%29)" in a wolf pack. An early study that coined the term "alpha wolf" had observed only unrelated adult wolves living in captivity. In the wild, wolf packs operate like families: parents are in charge until the young grow up and start their own families, and younger wolves do not overthrow an "alpha" to become the new leader.

:::

::: {#o85 .entry cue=list-entry name="phases of the Moon"}

- The [phases of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_phase) have no effect on the [vocalizations of wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_communication), and wolves do not howl at the Moon.[^o86] Wolves howl to assemble the pack usually before and after hunts, to pass on an alarm particularly at a den site, to locate each other during a storm, while crossing unfamiliar territory, and to communicate across great distances.[^o87]

:::

### Birds {#Birds}

- A human touching or handling eggs or baby birds will not cause the adult birds to abandon them. The same is generally true for other animals having their young touched by humans as well, with the possible exception of rabbits (as rabbits will sometimes abandon their nest after an event they perceive as traumatizing).

::: {#o91 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alka-Seltzer"}

- Eating rice, yeast, or [Alka-Seltzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alka-Seltzer) does not cause birds to explode and is rarely fatal. Birds can [flatulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatulate) and [regurgitate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regurgitation_%28digestion%29) to expel gas, and some birds even include wild rice as part of their diet.[^o93] The misconception has often led to weddings using [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [confetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confetti), or other materials to shower the newlyweds as they leave the ceremony, instead of the [throwing of rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_customs_by_country) that is traditional in some places.[^o93]

:::

::: {#o96 .entry cue=list-entry name="Birds are not stupid"}

- [Birds are not stupid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_intelligence).[^o97][^o98][^o99] A wide variety of cognitive abilities have been observed in birds, particularly [corvids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvidae) and [parrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parrot).[^o100][^o101] [Tool use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_use_by_non-human_animals) is also present in [woodpecker finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodpecker_finch).[^o102]

:::

::: {#o103 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bald eagle"}

- The bold, powerful cry commonly associated with the [bald eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle) in popular culture is actually that of a [red-tailed hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-tailed_hawk). [Bald eagle vocalizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_eagle#Popular_culture) are much softer and chirpier, and bear far more resemblance to the calls of [gulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulls).[^o104]

:::

::: {#o106 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dodo"}

- *Image: Dodo head Dinkel.jpg.* The dodo was intelligent and inedible, despite popular belief. Despite the saying "dumb as a dodo", the [dodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodo)'s intelligence was above average for an avian, as it was a member of the family [Columbidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbidae). The perceived stupidity of the dodo is due to naivety and passivity from living in isolation without significant predators.[^o107]

:::

::: {#o108 .entry cue=list-entry name="Invasive species"}

- Many believe that the dodo was hunted to extinction by European settlers due to its high culinary value. However, the dodo's meat was stated to be inedible by historical accounts, as one of its early names given by the Dutch was Walghvoghel (repulsive bird).[^o109] The dodo's decline was caused more by predation of their eggs from [invasive species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_species) as opposed to direct predation from humans.[^o110]

:::

::: {#o111 .entry cue=list-entry name="echo"}

- A duck's quack actually does [echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck's_quack),[^o112] although the echo may be difficult to hear for humans under some circumstances. Despite this, a British panel show compiling interesting facts was named *[Duck Quacks Don't Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duck_Quacks_Don't_Echo)*.

:::

::: {#o114 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ostriches do not stick their heads in the sand"}

- [Ostriches do not stick their heads in the sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ostrich#"Head_in_the_sand"_misconception) to hide from enemies or to sleep. This misconception's origins are uncertain but it was probably popularized by [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) (23–79 CE), who wrote that ostriches "imagine, when they have thrust their head and neck into a bush, that the whole of their body is concealed".

:::

::: {#o117 .entry cue=list-entry name="Common starling"}

- Sixty [common starlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_starling) were released in 1890 into New York's [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) by [Eugene Schieffelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Schieffelin), but there is no evidence that he was trying to introduce every bird species mentioned in the works of [William Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shakespeare) into North America. This claim has been traced to an essay in 1948 by naturalist [Edwin Way Teale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Way_Teale), whose notes appear to indicate that it was speculation.

:::

### Other vertebrates {#Other_vertebrates}

::: {#o121 .entry cue=list-entry name="candiru"}

- There is no credible evidence that the [candiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candiru_%28fish%29), a South American parasitic catfish, can swim up a human [urethra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urethra) if one urinates in the water in which it lives. The sole documented case of such an incident, written in 1997, has been heavily criticized upon peer review, and this phenomenon is now largely considered a myth.

:::

::: {#o123 .entry cue=list-entry name="The skin of a chameleon is not adapted solely for camouflage purposes"}

- [The skin of a chameleon is not adapted solely for camouflage purposes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chameleon#Change_of_colour), nor can a chameleon change its skin color to match any background.[^o125] Chameleons usually change color for social signaling, based on their mood, and for heat regulation.[^o125][^o126] The use in social signaling may be to display bright colors for only brief periods of time to avoid increased visibility to predators.[^o124]

:::

::: {#o127 .entry cue=list-entry name="Boiling frog"}

- Contrary to the allegorical story about the [boiling frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_frog), frogs die immediately when cast into boiling water, rather than leaping out; furthermore, frogs will attempt to escape cold water that is slowly heated before reaching their [critical thermal maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_thermal_maximum).

:::

::: {#o129 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pacific tree frog"}

- The [Pacific tree frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_tree_frog) and the [Baja California chorus frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_chorus_frog) are some of the only frog species that make a "ribbit" sound. The misconception that all frogs, or at least all those found in North America, make this sound comes from its extensive use in Hollywood films.

:::

- The memory span of goldfish is much longer than just a few seconds. It is up to a few months long.[^o133]

::: {#o134 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pacu"}

- [Pacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacu), South American fish related to [piranhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranha), do not attack or feed on human testicles. This myth originated from a misinterpreted joke in a 2013 report of a pacu being found in [Øresund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Øresund), the strait between Sweden and Denmark, which claimed that the fish ate "nuts".

:::

::: {#o137 .entry cue=list-entry name="Piranha"}

- [Piranhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piranha) do not eat only meat but are omnivorous, and they swim in schools only to defend themselves from predators and not to attack. They very rarely attack humans, only when under stress and feeling threatened, and even then, bites typically only occur on hands and feet.

:::

::: {#o139 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sharks can get cancer"}

- [Sharks can get cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark#In_culture). The misconception that sharks do not get cancer was spread by the 1992 book *[Sharks Don't Get Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharks_Don't_Get_Cancer)*, which was used to sell extracts of shark [cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage) as cancer prevention treatments. Reports of [carcinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoma) in sharks exist, and current data does not support any conclusions about the incidence of tumors in sharks.

:::

::: {#o141 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pinniped"}

- Great white sharks do not mistake human divers for seals or other [pinnipeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped). When attacking pinnipeds, the shark surfaces quickly and attacks violently. In contrast, attacks on humans are slower and less violent: the shark charges at a normal pace, bites, and swims off. Great white sharks have efficient eyesight and [color vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_vision); the bite is not predatory, but rather for identification of an unfamiliar object.

:::

::: {#o143 .entry cue=list-entry name="Snake jaws cannot unhinge"}

- [Snake jaws cannot unhinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake#Feeding_and_diet). The posterior end of the lower jaw bones contains a quadrate bone, allowing jaw extension. The anterior tips of the lower jaw bones are joined by a flexible ligament allowing them to bow outwards, increasing the mouth gape.

:::

::: {#o146 .entry cue=list-entry name="Snakes are not deaf"}

- [Snakes are not deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake) or only able to sense ground vibrations.[^o147] They can hear airborne sounds, though they cannot hear high-frequency sounds as well as humans can.

:::

### Invertebrates {#Invertebrates}

*Image: AD2009Aug08 Bombus pratorum.jpg.* Aerodynamic theory does not predict that [bumblebees should be incapable of flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumblebee#Misconception_about_flight).

::: {#o149 .entry cue=list-entry name="bumblebees should not be able to fly"}

- Aerodynamic theory does not predict that [bumblebees should not be able to fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumblebee#Misconception_about_flight); the physics of insect flight is quite well understood. The misconception appears to come from a calculation based on a fixed-wing aircraft mentioned in a 1934 book, and was further popularized in the 2007 film *[Bee Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Movie)*.[^o150]

:::

::: {#o153 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pollination"}

- While certainly critical to the [pollination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollination) of many plant species, [European honey bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_honey_bee#Claims_of_human_dependency) are not essential to human food production, despite claims that [without their pollination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_collapse_disorder), humanity would starve or die out "within four years". In fact, the most essential staple food crops on the planet, like wheat, maize, rice, soybeans and sorghum,[^o157] are wind-pollinated or self-pollinating, and only slightly over 10% of the total human diet of plant crops is dependent upon insect pollination.[^o156]

:::

::: {#o158 .entry cue=list-entry name="honey bee"}

- Most bees do not die if they use their sting. This happens for only a small minority of species, including the [honey bee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_sting#Honey_bee_stings), when they sting mammals. They survive when they sting other insects.

:::

::: {#o162 .entry cue=list-entry name="organisms capable of surviving"}

- Cockroaches would not be the only [organisms capable of surviving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Exclusion_Zone#Radioactive_contamination) in [an environment contaminated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fallout_effects_on_an_ecosystem) with nuclear fallout. While cockroaches have a much higher [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation) resistance than vertebrates, they are not immune to radiation poisoning, nor are they exceptionally radiation-resistant compared to other insects or smaller living beings. For comparison, [parasitic wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitic_wasp), [tardigrades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tardigrade), and bacteria such as *[Deinococcus radiodurans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deinococcus_radiodurans)* have much higher radiation resistance compared to cockroaches.

:::

::: {#o165 .entry cue=list-entry name="Earwigs"}

- [Earwigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earwigs) are not known to purposely climb into external ear canals, though there have been anecdotal reports of earwigs being found in the ear. The name may be a reference to the appearance of their hindwings, which are unique and distinctive among insects, and resemble a human ear when unfolded.

:::

::: {#o169 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dolania americana"}

- Houseflies have an average lifespan of 20 to 30 *days*, not 24 *hours*. However, females of the [*Dolania americana*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolania) species of [mayfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfly), which do not belong to the group of [true flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_flies), have an adult lifespan of as little as 5 minutes.

::: {#o178 .entry cue=list-entry name="People do not swallow large numbers of spiders during sleep"}

- [People do not swallow large numbers of spiders during sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_spiders#Modern_myths_and_urban_legends). ### Evolution and ecology {#Evolution_and_ecology}

::: {#o208 .entry cue=list-entry name="Germ theory"}

- The word *theory* in "the theory of evolution" does not imply scientific doubt regarding its validity; the concepts of *theory* and *hypothesis* have specific meanings in a scientific context. While *theory* in colloquial usage may denote a hunch or conjecture, a *scientific theory* is a set of principles that explains an *observable phenomenon* in natural terms. Scientific facts and theories are not mutually exclusive, and evolution is a theory in the same sense as [germ theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_theory) or the theory of [gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitation).

:::

::: {#o213 .entry cue=list-entry name="Abiogenesis"}

- The theory of evolution does not attempt to explain the origin of life or the origin and development of the universe. The theory of evolution deals primarily with changes in successive generations over time after life has already originated. The scientific model concerned with the origin of the first organisms from organic or inorganic molecules is known as [abiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abiogenesis), and the prevailing theory for explaining the early development of the universe is the [Big Bang](wikipedia://en/4116) model.

:::

::: {#o216 .entry cue=list-entry name="progression from inferior to superior organisms"}

- Evolution is not a [progression from inferior to superior organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_chain_of_being), and it also does not necessarily result in an [increase in complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_complexity). Evolution through [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection) only causes successive generations of a population of organisms to become more [fit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_%28biology%29) for their environment than previous generations. A population can evolve to become simpler or to have a smaller [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome), and [atavistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atavism) ancestral genetic traits can reappear after having been lost through evolutionary change in previous generations.[^o219] Biological [devolution or de-evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_%28biology%29) is a [misnomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misnomer), not only because it implies that organisms can only evolve backward or forward, but also because it implies that evolution may cause organisms to evolve in the "wrong" direction.[^o221]

:::

::: {#o222 .entry cue=list-entry name="Survival of the fittest"}

- The phrase "[survival of the fittest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_of_the_fittest)" refers to [biological fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_%28biology%29), not [physical fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_fitness). Biological fitness is the [quantitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_data) measure of individual [reproductive success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_success), e.g. the tendency of lineages containing individuals that produce more offspring [in a particular environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem) to persist and thrive in that environment. Further, while the related concepts of "survival of the fittest" and "[natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection)" are often used interchangeably, they are not the same: natural selection is not the only form of selection that determines biological fitness (see [sexual selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_selection), [fecundity selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecundity_selection), [viability selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection#By_life_cycle_stage), and [artificial selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Selection)).[^o223][^o224]

:::

::: {#o226 .entry cue=list-entry name="Evolution does not \"plan\" to improve an organism's fitness"}

- [Evolution does not "plan" to improve an organism's fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogenesis) to survive. This misconception is encouraged as it is common shorthand for [biologists to speak of a purpose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleology_in_biology) as a concise form of expression (sometimes called the "metaphor of purpose"); it is less cumbersome to say "Dinosaurs may have evolved feathers for courtship" than "Feathers may have been selected for when they arose as they gave dinosaurs a selective advantage during courtship over their non-feathered rivals". However, this can result in many students explaining evolution as an intentional and purposeful process.

:::

::: {#o232 .entry cue=list-entry name="DNA repair"}

- Mutations are not all equally likely, nor do they occur at the same frequency everywhere in the genome. Certain regions of an organism's genome will be more or less likely to undergo mutation depending on the presence of [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair) mechanisms and other [mutation biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_bias). For instance, in a study on *[Arabidopsis thaliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabidopsis_thaliana)*, biologically important regions of the plant's genome were found to be protected from mutations, and beneficial mutations were found to be more likely, i.e. mutation was "biased in a way that benefits the plant".

:::

::: {#o236 .entry cue=list-entry name="Reptiliomorph"}

- Mammals did not evolve from any modern group of reptiles; rather, mammals descend from a [Reptiliomorph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptiliomorpha), or reptile-like, ancestor. After the first [fully terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniote#Adaptation_for_terrestrial_living) [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod) evolved, one of their lineages split into the [synapsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapsids) (the line leading to mammals) and the [diapsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diapsids) (the line leading to reptiles, including birds). The synapsids and the diapsids diverged about 320 million years ago, in the mid-[Carboniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous) period. The mammals themselves are the only survivors of the synapsid line.[^o242]

:::

::: {#o243 .entry cue=list-entry name="Old World monkeys"}

- Humans and other apes are [Old World monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catarrhini).[^o244][^o246] There is [a concerted social and religious effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejection_of_evolution_by_religious_groups) to deny evidence which connects humans to their ancestors and fellow extant simians, but there is no way to [naturally define](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophyly) the monkeys while excluding humans and other apes. *Image: Aegyptopithecus NT.jpg.* *[Aegyptopithecus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegyptopithecus)*, a prehistoric monkey predating the split between [apes and other Old World monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catarrhini) and postdating the division of Old and [New World monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_monkey), making it more closely related to humans than to New World monkeys[^o245]

:::

::: {#o248 .entry cue=list-entry name="chimpanzees"}

- Humans did not evolve from either of the living species of [chimpanzees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_%28genus%29) or any other living species of apes. Humans and chimpanzees did, however, evolve from a [common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimpanzee–human_last_common_ancestor).[^o251] This [most recent common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_recent_common_ancestor) of living humans and chimpanzees would have lived between 5 and 8 million years ago.

:::

- Humans are animals, despite the fact that the word *animal* is colloquially used as an antonym for *human*.

::: {#o255 .entry cue=list-entry name="Negative feedback"}

- Ecosystems do not naturally move back towards an equilibrium using [negative feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_feedback).[^o256] The concept of an inherent "[balance of nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_nature)" has been superseded by [chaos theory](wikipedia://en/6295).[^o257]

:::

### Paleontology {#Paleontology}

::: {#o259 .entry cue=list-entry name="the word dinosaur can be used pejoratively"}

- Although [the word *dinosaur* can be used pejoratively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_dinosaurs#Public_perception_of_dinosaurs) to describe something that is becoming obsolete due to failing to adapt to changing conditions, non-avian dinosaurs did not become extinct due to being generally maladapted or unable to cope with normal climatic change, a [view found in many older textbooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event_research).[^o260][^o261] Moreover, not all dinosaurs are extinct (see below).[^o260]

:::

::: {#o265 .entry cue=list-entry name="Theropod dinosaur"}

- Dinosaurs are not extinct, as birds are classified within the [theropod dinosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theropod_dinosaur) lineage. The word *dinosaur* is commonly used to refer only to non-avian dinosaurs, reflecting an outdated conception of the [ancestry](wikipedia://en/149326) of avian dinosaurs, the birds. [The evolutionary origin of birds was an open question in paleontology for over a century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_birds), but the modern scientific consensus is that [birds evolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_birds) from small [feathered theropods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_avian_flight) in the [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic). Not all dinosaur lineages were cut short at the end of the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) during the [Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event), and over 11,000 species of avian theropods survive as part of the modern fauna.

:::

::: {#o269 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and ichthyosaurs were not cold-blooded"}

- [Dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and ichthyosaurs were not cold-blooded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology_of_dinosaurs).[^o270] Dinosaur thermoregulation is a topic of ongoing study. However, their high growth rates and the presence of dinosaur fossils in cold polar climates[^o271] make cold-bloodedness implausible. Pterosaurs are inferred to be warm-blooded because they had [a hair-like body covering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterosaur#Pycnofibers), a trait absent in modern cold-blooded animals.[^o272] Estimates of ichthyosaur body temperature are similar to marine mammals in every climate.[^o273]

:::

::: {#o274 .entry cue=list-entry name="cultural depictions"}

- Despite their [cultural depictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_dinosaurs) as "swimming dinosaurs", [mosasaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosasaurs), [ichthyosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyosauria), [plesiosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesiosaurs), and [other](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_reptile#Extinct_groups) aquatic [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) [diapsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diapsids) were not dinosaurs. Mosasaurs were actually lizards, and ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs were [even more distantly related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauropterygia) to dinosaurs. Though some dinosaurs were or are [semiaquatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiaquatic) (*[Hesperornis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesperornis)*, *[Spinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurus)*, [auks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auks), [penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguins)), none are known to have been fully marine.[^o263] *Image: Ichthyosaur and Plesiosaur 1863.jpg.* An [ichthyosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyosauria) and [plesiosaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesiosaur) by [Édouard Riou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Édouard_Riou), 1863. This old representation of a plesiosaur lifting its head is not accurate.

:::

::: {#o276 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pterosaurs"}

- [Pterosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterosaurs) (informally called *pterodactyls*) are often called "flying dinosaurs" by popular media and the general public, but while pterosaurs were [closely related to dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avemetatarsalia), dinosaurs are defined as the descendants of the [last common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_common_ancestor) of the [Saurischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurischia) and the [Ornithischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithischia), which excludes the pterosaurs.

:::

::: {#o278 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dimetrodon"}

- *[Dimetrodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimetrodon)* is often mistakenly called a dinosaur or considered to be a contemporary of dinosaurs in popular culture, but it became extinct some 40 million years before the first appearance of dinosaurs. Being a [synapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapsid), *Dimetrodon* is actually more closely related to [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammals) than to dinosaurs, lizards, or other [diapsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diapsids). *Image: Dimetrodon8DB.jpg.* *[Dimetrodon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimetrodon)*, the iconic [sail-backed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_spine_sail) [synapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synapsid), was not a dinosaur, nor did it live at the same time as the dinosaurs.

:::

::: {#o281 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dinosaurs"}

- Humans and non-avian [dinosaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinosaurs) did not coexist at any point, although humans and [avian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird) dinosaurs currently [coexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-dinosaur_coexistence). The last of the non-avian dinosaurs died Paleogene million years ago in the [Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous–Paleogene_extinction_event) while the earliest members of the genus *[Homo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo)* (humans) evolved between 2.3 and 2.4 million years ago.

:::

::: {#o284 .entry cue=list-entry name="anoxic conditions"}

- Fossil fuels do not originate from dinosaur fossils. Petroleum is formed when algae and zooplankton die and sink in [anoxic conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_zone_%28ecology%29) to be buried on the ocean floor without being decomposed by [aerobic bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_organism), and only a tiny amount of the world's deposits of coal contain [dinosaur fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iguanodon#Bernissart_mine_discoveries_and_Dollo's_new_reconstruction); the vast majority of coal is [fossilized plant matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal#Formation).

:::

## Chemistry and materials science {#Chemistry_and_materials_science}

::: {#o288 .entry cue=list-entry name="Himalayan salt"}

::: {#o290 .entry cue=list-entry name="Glass does not flow"}

- [Glass does not flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass#Reputed_flow) at room temperature as a high-viscosity liquid.[^o291] Although [glass shares some molecular properties with liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorphous_solid), it is a solid at room temperature and begins to flow only at [hundreds of degrees above room temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_transition). Old glass which is thicker at the bottom than at the top comes from the production process, not from slow flow;[^o292][^o293] no such distortion is observed in other glass objects of similar or even greater age.[^o292][^o293]

:::

::: {#o295 .entry cue=list-entry name="Diamonds"}

- [Diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond#Geology) are not generally formed from compressed coal. Most natural gem diamonds formed in the conditions of extreme heat and pressure, commonly about 150 to below the surface, and were later brought upward by [kimberlite eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_pipe). Coal, by contrast, is a sedimentary deposit formed from buried plant remains, and is unlikely to migrate below \3.2 km through geological processes.[^o296] Because many diamonds are billions of years old, whereas the [first land plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolutionary_history_of_plants#Colonization_of_land) appeared much later, coal was not the source material for most natural diamonds.[^o297][^o298][^o299]

:::

- Neither "tin" foil nor "tin" cans still use tin as a primary material. Aluminum foil has replaced tin foil in almost all uses since the 20th century; tin cans now primarily use steel or aluminum as their main metal.[^o302]

::: {#o303 .entry cue=list-entry name="special compound"}

- There is no [special compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine-indicator_dye) added to the water in swimming pools that will reveal the presence of urine and catch those who urinate in the pool.

:::

::: {#o305 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pencil"}

- Although the core of a wooden [pencil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil) is commonly referred to as "lead", wooden pencils do not contain the chemical element [lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead), nor have they ever contained it; "black lead" was formerly a name of [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), which is commonly used for pencil leads.[^o306]

## Computing and the Internet {#Computing_and_the_Internet}

However, the vast majority of viruses are developed for [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows) due to its larger market share.[^o310]

This information is primarily found in a small portion of the deep web known as the "[dark web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_web)". Much of the deep web consists of academic libraries, databases, and anything that is not indexed by normal search engines, including most private email accounts and direct messages.[^o312]

Such entities can still use information such as [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address) and [user accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_accounts) to uniquely identify users. Private browsing also does not provide additional protection against viruses or malware. It is usually only a feature to not record browsing and searching history on the browser.

Even if submerging them in a desiccant were more effective than leaving them to dry in open air, common desiccants such as silica gel or cat litter are better than rice.

## Earth and environmental sciences {#Earth_and_environmental_sciences}

*Image: Common Era Temperature.svg.* [Global surface temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_surface_temperature) reconstruction over the last 2000 years using proxy data from tree rings, corals, and ice cores in blue. Directly observed data is in red.

::: {#o330 .entry cue=list-entry name="Global warming"}

- Contemporary [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) is [driven by human activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment), despite claims that it is [not occurring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial), [lacks strong scientific consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_conspiracy_theory), or that warming is [mostly caused by non-human factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming_controversy).[^o335] No scientific body of national or international standing disagrees with the decades-old, near-complete [scientific consensus on climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_consensus_on_climate_change).[^o336] Global warming is primarily a result of the increase in atmospheric [greenhouse gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas) concentrations (like [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) and [methane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methane)) via the [burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) as well as other [human activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropogenic_hazard) such as [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_and_climate_change), with secondary [climate change feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_feedback) mechanisms (such as the [melting of the polar ice increasing the Earth's absorption of sunlight](wikipedia://en/21435575)) assisting to perpetuate the change.

:::

::: {#o338 .entry cue=list-entry name="Ozone depletion"}

- *Image: Area of the ozone hole.jpg.* [Ozone depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion) is not a cause of [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming). Global warming is not caused by the [hole in the ozone layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_depletion). Ozone depletion is a separate problem caused by [chlorofluorocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorofluorocarbon) (CFCs)[^o339] which have been released into the atmosphere. CFCs are strong greenhouse gases; however, the hole in the ozone layer is shrinking and in 2019 was the smallest it had been since 1982, while global warming continues.

:::

::: {#o345 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cooling tower"}

- *Image: Cows and cooling towers, Cottam - geograph.org.uk - 450176.jpg.* Cooling towers from a coal-fired power plant. The white clouds are harmless water vapor from the cooling process. [Cooling towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooling_tower) in [power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_station) and other facilities do not emit smoke, harmful fumes, or radiation; they emit water vapor and do not contribute to climate change.

:::

::: {#o348 .entry cue=list-entry name="Nuclear power"}

- *Image: 5-Bar-chart-–-What-is-the-safest-form-of-energy.png.* Death rates from air pollution and accidents related to energy production, measured in deaths per terawatt hours (TWh) (left). Carbon emissions measured in tons per gigawatt hour (GWh) (right). [Nuclear power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power) is [one of the safest sources of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power#Safety), resulting in orders of magnitude fewer deaths than conventional power sources per unit of energy produced. Extremely few people are killed or injured due to nuclear power on a yearly basis. (See also: [Radiophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiophobia))

:::

::: {#o353 .entry cue=list-entry name="Earthquake"}

- [Earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake) strength (or magnitude) is not commonly measured using the [Richter scale](wikipedia://en/19467352). Although the Richter scale was used *historically* to measure earthquake magnitude (not [earthquake damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modified_Mercalli_intensity_scale)), it was found in the 1970s that it does not reliably represent the magnitude of large earthquakes. It has therefore been largely replaced by the [moment magnitude scale](wikipedia://en/631494), although very small earthquakes are still sometimes measured using the Richter scale. Nevertheless, earthquake magnitude is still widely misattributed to the Richter scale.

:::

::: {#o359 .entry cue=list-entry name="Lightning"}

- [Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning) can, and often does, strike the same place twice. Lightning in a thunderstorm is more likely to strike objects and spots that are more prominent or conductive. For instance, lightning strikes the [Empire State Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Building) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on average 23 times per year.[^o360]

:::

::: {#o361 .entry cue=list-entry name="Heat lightning"}

- [Heat lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_lightning) does not exist as a distinct phenomenon. What is mistaken for "heat lightning" is usually ordinary lightning from storms too distant to hear the associated [thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder).

:::

::: {#o363 .entry cue=list-entry name="Yellowstone Caldera"}

- The [Yellowstone Caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone_Caldera) is not overdue for a [supervolcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervolcano) eruption. There is also no evidence that it will erupt in the near future. In fact, data indicates there will not be an eruption in the coming centuries. The most likely eruption would be [hydrothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_explosion) rather than volcanic. A [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera)-forming volcanic eruption (and subsequent impacts on global weather patterns and agricultural production) is the least likely scenario and has an extremely low likelihood.

:::

::: {#o368 .entry cue=list-entry name="Earth's interior"}

- The [Earth's interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_Earth) is not molten rock. This misconception may originate from a misunderstanding based on the fact that the [Earth's mantle convects](wikipedia://en/7604906), and the incorrect assumption that only liquids and gases can convect. In fact, a solid with a large [Rayleigh number](wikipedia://en/59656) can also convect, given enough time, which is what occurs in the solid mantle due to the very large thermal gradient across it. There are small pockets of [molten rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) in the upper mantle, but these make up a tiny fraction of the mantle's volume. The Earth's [outer core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_outer_core) *is* liquid, but it is liquid metal, not rock.

:::

::: {#o373 .entry cue=list-entry name="Amazon rainforest"}

- The [Amazon rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rainforest) does not provide 20% of Earth's oxygen. This is a misinterpretation of a 2010 study which found that approximately 34% of photosynthesis by terrestrial plants occurs in tropical rainforests (so the Amazon rainforest would account for approximately half of this). Due to respiration by the resident organisms, all ecosystems (including the Amazon rainforest) have a net output of oxygen of approximately zero. The oxygen currently present in the atmosphere was accumulated over billions of years.

:::

::: {#o375 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bird"}

- [Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird) deaths due to [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) are extremely rare compared to those caused by cats, windows, vehicles, poison, and overhead power lines.

:::

::: {#o378 .entry cue=list-entry name="Contrails"}

- *Image: Contrails.jpg.* Multiple concurrent contrails. How long they last depends upon the weather, especially the temperature, humidity, and wind speed. The white streaks seen in the sky after airplanes pass overhead are [contrails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrails) consisting of condensed water vapor and engine exhaust. They are not [chemtrails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemtrail_conspiracy_theory), nor are they part of a program to control the weather or some other nefarious purpose.[^o379]

:::

## Economics {#Economics}

*Image: Total population living in extreme poverty, by world region (PovcalNet, World Bank), OWID.svg.* Total population living in extreme poverty, by world region 1987 to 2015

::: {#o383 .entry cue=list-entry name="Extreme poverty"}

- The total number of people living in [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty) globally decreased by two-thirds from 1990 to 2025 (measured using the international poverty line), with other poverty measurements showing similar trends. However, most people surveyed in several countries incorrectly think it has increased or stayed the same.[^o384]

:::

::: {#o385 .entry cue=list-entry name="human population growth"}

- Although the human population of Earth is increasing, the rate of [human population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) is decreasing and the world population is expected to peak and then begin falling during the 21st century. Improvements in agricultural productivity and technology are expected to be able to meet anticipated increased demand for resources, making a global [human overpopulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_overpopulation) scenario unlikely.

:::

::: {#o389 .entry cue=list-entry name="Production set"}

- For any given [production set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_set), there is not a set amount of labor input (a "[lump of labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lump_of_labour_fallacy)") to produce that output. This fallacy is commonly seen in [Luddite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luddite) and later, related movements as an argument either that automation causes permanent, structural unemployment, or that labor-limiting regulation can decrease unemployment. In fact, changes in capital allocation, efficiency, and [economies of learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_economy) can change the amount of labor input for a given set of production.[^o390]

:::

::: {#o391 .entry cue=list-entry name="Credit score in the United States"}

- Income is not a direct factor in determining [credit score in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_score_in_the_United_States). Rather, credit score is affected by the amount of unused available credit, which is in turn affected by income. Income is also considered when evaluating creditworthiness more generally.

:::

::: {#o393 .entry cue=list-entry name="foreign aid"}

- The US public vastly overestimates the amount spent on [foreign aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_foreign_aid#Public_opinion).

:::

::: {#o395 .entry cue=list-entry name="Gross income"}

- In the US, an increase in [gross income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_income) will never reduce a taxpayer's post-tax earnings ([net income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_income)) by putting them in a higher [tax bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_bracket). Tax brackets specify [*marginal* tax rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_tax_rate): only income earned in the higher tax bracket is taxed at the higher rate. An increase in gross income can reduce net income in a [welfare trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_trap), however, when benefits are withdrawn when passing a certain income threshold. Prevalence of the misconception varies by political party affiliation.

:::

::: {#o399 .entry cue=list-entry name="Real estate economics"}

- Constructing new housing decreases the cost of rent and the price of homes in both the immediate neighborhood and in the city as a whole. In [real estate economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_economics), "supply skepticism" leads many Americans to misunderstand the effect of increasing the supply of housing on housing costs. The misconception is unique to the housing market.[^o400]

:::

::: {#o402 .entry cue=list-entry name="collecting charitable donations from their customers"}

- Businesses do not get a tax benefit by [collecting charitable donations from their customers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkout_charity). Corporation taxes are based on [profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profit_%28accounting%29); the customer's donation would not change the amount of profit and therefore the tax payable. A business would need to donate *its own* money to receive a tax break.[^o403][^o404][^o405]

:::

::: {#o406 .entry cue=list-entry name="Import tariffs"}

- [Import tariffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff) are taxes paid to the government by importers, not by exporting countries or manufacturers as is claimed by some, including [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump).[^o407][^o408][^o409] There is a near-unanimous consensus among economists that tariffs have a net-negative effect on economic growth and welfare, and harm consumers through higher prices by more than they benefit domestic producers and governments.[^o410]

:::

## Geography {#Geography}

*Image: CapeHopeDetail.png.* The [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) and [Cape Agulhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Agulhas), the southernmost point of Africa

::: {#o412 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cape of Good Hope"}

- The [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) is not the southern tip of Africa; that distinction belongs to [Cape Agulhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Agulhas), located about 150 km to the east-southeast.

:::

::: {#o414 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sahara"}

- The majority of the [Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara) consists of [rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamada), rather than [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_%28landform%29).[^o161]

:::

::: {#o416 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rivers flow downhill"}

- Rivers do not predominantly flow from north to south. [Rivers flow downhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River#The_flow_of_rivers) in all compass directions, often changing direction along their course. Many major rivers flow northward, including the [Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile), the [Yenisey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenisey), the [Ob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ob_%28river%29), the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), the [Lena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lena_%28river%29), and the [Orinoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco).[^o418]

:::

## Human body and health {#Human_body_and_health}

*Image: Korean fans closeup.jpg.* [Leaving electric fans on while asleep is not dangerous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_death).

::: {#o421 .entry cue=list-entry name="Heavy metals"}

- It is not just [heavy metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metals) which can be toxic; other metals (for example [beryllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryllium) and [lithium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium)) can be toxic too.[^o422]

:::

::: {#o423 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fan death"}

- Sleeping in a closed room with an electric fan running does not result in "[fan death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_death)", as is widely believed in South Korea among older people. As of 2019, this belief was in decline.

:::

::: {#o427 .entry cue=list-entry name="Nocturia"}

- [Nocturia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturia) (waking up at night to urinate) is equally prevalent in women and men,[^o428] although it is more common among those over 50.[^o429]

:::

::: {#o430 .entry cue=list-entry name="Waking up a sleepwalker does not harm them"}

- [Waking up a sleepwalker does not harm them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepwalking#Treatment). Sleepwalkers may be confused or disoriented for a short time after awakening, but the health risks associated with sleepwalking are from injury or insomnia, not from being awakened.[^o431]

:::

::: {#o432 .entry cue=list-entry name="Seizure"}

- [Seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizure) cannot cause a person to swallow their own tongue,[^o433] and it is dangerous to attempt to place a foreign object into a convulsing person's mouth. Instead it is recommended to gently lay a convulsing person on their side to minimize the risk of asphyxiation.

:::

::: {#o436 .entry cue=list-entry name="Drowning"}

- [Drowning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drowning) is often inconspicuous to onlookers. In most cases, the [instinctive drowning response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instinctive_drowning_response) prevents the victim from waving or yelling,[^o437] which are therefore not dependable signs of trouble. Most drowning victims do not show prior evidence of distress.

:::

::: {#o439 .entry cue=list-entry name="Herbal medicine"}

- [Herbal medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine) are not necessarily safe and side-effect free; such medicines can have [adverse effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_herbs_with_known_adverse_effects).[^o440][^o441]

:::

::: {#o442 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vein"}

- Human blood in [veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vein) is not actually blue. Blood is red due to the presence of [hemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin); deoxygenated blood (in veins) has a deep red color, and oxygenated blood (in [arteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arteries)) has a light cherry-red color. Veins below the skin can appear blue or green due to [subsurface scattering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsurface_scattering) of light through the skin, and aspects of human color perception. Many medical diagrams also use blue to show veins, and red to show arteries, which contributes to this misconception.[^o443]

:::

::: {#o444 .entry cue=list-entry name="Asystole"}

- [Asystole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asystole), also known as a "flatline", is not a shockable rhythm and is neither treated nor cured via [defibrillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defibrillation). Medical interventions for asystole include high-quality [CPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_resuscitation) and (in a medically professional environment) [epinephrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenaline) administration. The misconception may stem from outdated medical practices and Hollywood glamorization of [resuscitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resuscitation) efforts.[^o445]

:::

::: {#o446 .entry cue=list-entry name="Uncontrolled decompression"}

- Exposure to a vacuum, or experiencing all but the most extreme [uncontrolled decompression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontrolled_decompression), does not cause the body to explode or internal fluids to boil (although the fluids in the mouth and lungs will indeed boil at altitudes above the [Armstrong limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_limit)); rather, it will lead to a loss of [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness) once the body has depleted the supply of oxygen in the blood, followed by death from [hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_%28medical%29) within minutes.[^o447]

:::

::: {#o448 .entry cue=list-entry name="Delayed onset muscle soreness"}

- Exercise-induced [delayed onset muscle soreness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delayed_onset_muscle_soreness) is not caused by [lactic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lactic_acid) build-up. Muscular lactic acid levels return to normal levels within an hour after exercise; delayed onset muscle soreness is thought to be due to [microtrauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtrauma) from unaccustomed or strenuous exercise.[^o449]

:::

::: {#o450 .entry cue=list-entry name="Delayed onset muscle soreness"}

- Stretching before or after exercise does not reduce [delayed onset muscle soreness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delayed_onset_muscle_soreness).

:::

::: {#o452 .entry cue=list-entry name="Urine is not sterile"}

- [Urine is not sterile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine#Bacteria_and_pathogens), not even in the bladder. This misconception may derive from urine bacterial screening tests, which return "negative" when bacteria levels are low, but nonzero.[^o453]

:::

::: {#o454 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hypothermia"}

- Sudden immersion into freezing water does not typically cause death by [hypothermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothermia), but rather from the [cold shock response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_shock_response), which can cause [cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest), [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack), or [hyperventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperventilation) leading to [drowning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drowning).

:::

::: {#o456 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cremated remains"}

- [Cremated remains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremation) are not ashes in the usual sense. After the incineration is completed, the dry bone fragments are swept out of the retort and pulverized by a machine called a *cremulator* (essentially a high-capacity, high-speed blender) to process them into "ashes" or "cremated remains".

:::

::: {#o458 .entry cue=list-entry name="alveoli"}

- *Image: Alveolus diagram.svg.* The alveoli The lung's [alveoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_alveolus) are not tiny balloons that expand and contract under positive pressure following the [Young–Laplace equation](wikipedia://en/9122581), as is taught in some physiology and medical textbooks. The tissue structure is more like a sponge with polygonal spaces that unfold and fold under negative pressure from the chest wall.

:::

::: {#o460 .entry cue=list-entry name="Body heat"}

- Half of [body heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_heat) is not lost through the head, and covering the head is no more effective at preventing [heat loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothermia) than covering any other portion of the body. Heat is lost from the body in proportion to the amount of exposed skin. The head accounts for around 7–9% of the body's surface, and studies have shown that having one's head submerged in cold water causes a person to lose only 10% more heat overall.[^o463][^o464]

:::

::: {#o465 .entry cue=list-entry name="Adrenochrome"}

- [Adrenochrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenochrome) is not harvested from living people and has no use as a recreational drug. [Hunter S. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_S._Thompson) conceived a fictional drug of the same name in his book *[Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fear_and_Loathing_in_Las_Vegas)*, apparently unaware that a real substance by that name existed; it is Thompson's fictional adrenochrome, and not the real chemical compound, that is the source of numerous conspiracy theories revolving around human trafficking to harvest the fictional drug.[^o467]

:::

::: {#o468 .entry cue=list-entry name="biblical creation story"}

- Men and women have the same number of ribs: 24, or 12 pairs. The erroneous idea that women have one more rib than men may stem from the [biblical creation story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_creation_narrative) of [Adam and Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_and_Eve).

:::

::: {#o470 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cotton swab"}

- The use of [cotton swabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_swab) (a.k.a. cotton buds or Q-Tips) in the ear canal has no associated medical benefits and poses definite medical risks.[^o471]

:::

::: {#o472 .entry cue=list-entry name="Five stages of grief"}

- The idea that a precise number of stages of grief exists is not supported in peer-reviewed research or objective clinical observation, let alone the [five stages of grief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_stages_of_grief) model.[^o473]

:::

::: {#o474 .entry cue=list-entry name="temperature of the human body"}

- 37 C is not the normal or average [temperature of the human body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body_temperature). That figure comes from an 1860 study,[^o475] but modern research shows that the average internal temperature is \36.4 C, with small fluctuations.

:::

::: {#o479 .entry cue=list-entry name="cells in the human body"}

- The [cells in the human body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_microbiome) are not outnumbered 10 to 1 by [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism). The 10 to 1 ratio was an estimate made in 1972; current estimates put the ratio at either 3 to 1 or 1.3 to 1.[^o480]

:::

- The total length of capillaries in the human body is not 100,000 km. That figure comes from a 1929 book by August Krogh, who used an unrealistically large model person and an inaccurately high density of capillaries. The true number is believed to be between 9,000 and 19,000 km.

::: {#o482 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wood-burning stoves"}

- Wood smoke, for example from [wood-burning stoves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood-burning_stoves), is not a benign form of pollution because it is "natural", but is as harmful as other common forms of air pollution such as [diesel fumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_fumes).[^o483]

:::

### Disease and preventive healthcare {#Disease_and_preventive_healthcare}

::: {#o485 .entry cue=list-entry name="COVID-19 vaccine"}

- It is not true that more people have died from the [COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine#Adverse_events) than from COVID-19 itself.[^o486] Severe adverse reactions from the vaccine are rare,[^o487][^o488][^o489] and an "exceedingly small" number of deaths have been caused by the vaccine.[^o490] Meanwhile, the death toll from the disease itself is in the millions.[^o491]

:::

::: {#o492 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tuberculosis"}

- [Tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis) is not purely a disease of the lungs that has symptoms of coughing. It may instead infect a wide range of other organs in the body.[^o493]

:::

::: {#o494 .entry cue=list-entry name="Healthy diet"}

- Cancer cannot be treated by restricting food intake and so supposedly "starving" tumors. Rather, the health of people with cancer is best served by maintaining a [healthy diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthy_diet).[^o495][^o496]

:::

::: {#o497 .entry cue=list-entry name="common cold"}

- The [common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold#Weather) and the [common flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_flu) are caused by [viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus), not exposure to cold temperatures. However, low temperatures may somewhat weaken the immune system, and someone already infected with a cold or influenza virus but showing no symptoms can become symptomatic after they are exposed to low temperatures. Viruses are more likely to spread during the winter for a variety of reasons such as dry air, less air circulation in homes, people spending more time indoors, and lower [vitamin D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D) levels in humans.[^o500]

:::

::: {#o501 .entry cue=list-entry name="Antibiotics"}

- [Antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotics) will not cure a cold; they treat bacterial diseases and are ineffectual against viruses. However, they are sometimes prescribed to prevent or treat [secondary infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunistic_infection).

:::

::: {#o505 .entry cue=list-entry name="Essential oil"}

- There is little to no evidence that any illnesses are curable through [essential oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_oil) or [aromatherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatherapy), and [fish oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_oil_%28medical_use%29) has not been shown to cure [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia).

:::

::: {#o508 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sputum"}

- In those with the common cold, the color of the [sputum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputum) or nasal secretion may vary from colorless to yellow to green and does not indicate the class of agent causing the infection.[^o509] The color of the sputum is determined by [immune cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell) fighting an infection in the nasal area.

:::

::: {#o511 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vitamin C"}

- [Vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C) [does not prevent or treat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C_and_the_common_cold) the common cold, although it may have a protective effect during intense cold-weather exercise. If taken daily, it may slightly reduce the duration and severity of colds, but it has no effect if taken after the cold starts.[^o512]

:::

::: {#o513 .entry cue=list-entry name="Echinacea"}

- Supplements of the plant [echinacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echinacea) do not prevent or reduce the severity of colds as widely believed. A 2014 review of 24 different randomized controlled trials found echinacea supplements do not prevent colds, with most showing no benefit over placebo.[^o514][^o515][^o516][^o517][^o518]

:::

::: {#o519 .entry cue=list-entry name="Wart"}

- *Image: Bombina bombina 1 (Marek Szczepanek).jpg.* The bumps on a toad are not [warts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wart) and cannot cause warts on humans. Humans cannot catch warts from toads or other animals; the bumps on a toad are not warts.[^o520] [Warts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wart) on human skin are caused by [human papillomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus), which is known to affect only humans.

:::

::: {#o522 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cracking one's knuckles"}

- [Cracking one's knuckles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracking_joints) does not cause [osteoarthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteoarthritis).[^o524]

:::

::: {#o525 .entry cue=list-entry name="Eczema"}

- In people with [eczema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eczema), bathing does not dry the skin as long as a moisturizer is applied soon after. If moisturizer is not applied after bathing, then the evaporation of water from the skin can result in dryness.

:::

::: {#o527 .entry cue=list-entry name="dialysis"}

- There have never been any programs in the US that provide access to [dialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_dialysis) machines in exchange for pull tabs on beverage cans. This rumor has existed since at least the 1970s, and usually cites the [National Kidney Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Kidney_Foundation) as the organization offering the program. The Foundation itself has denied the rumor, noting that dialysis machines are primarily funded by Medicare.

:::

::: {#o530 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rhinoceros horn"}

- [Rhinoceros horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros_horn) in powdered form is not used as an [aphrodisiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisiac) in [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) as *Cornu Rhinoceri Asiatici* (犀角, *xījiǎo*, "rhinoceros horn"). It is prescribed for fevers and convulsions,[^o531] a treatment not supported by [evidence-based medicine](wikipedia://en/10013).

:::

::: {#o532 .entry cue=list-entry name="Leprosy"}

- [Leprosy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leprosy) is not auto-degenerative as commonly supposed, meaning that it will not (on its own) cause body parts to be damaged or fall off.[^o533] Leprosy causes rashes to form and may degrade cartilage and, if untreated, [inflame tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation). In addition, leprosy is only mildly contagious, partly because 95% of those infected with the mycobacteria that cause leprosy do not develop the disease.[^o533] [Tzaraath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzaraath), a Biblical disease that disfigures the skin, is often identified as leprosy, and may be the source of many myths about the disease.[^o535]

:::

::: {#o536 .entry cue=list-entry name="Rust"}

- [Rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust) itself does not cause [tetanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetanus) infection. The [bacterium that causes tetanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clostridium_tetani) thrives in [low oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_%28environmental%29) environments, including rust where the oxygen has combined with iron, so many people associate rust with tetanus. However, any puncture wound can introduce spores that cause a tetanus infection, not just rusty nails.[^o537][^o538][^o539]

:::

::: {#o540 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quarantine"}

- [Quarantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarantine) has never been a standard procedure for those with [severe combined immunodeficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_combined_immunodeficiency), despite the condition's popular nickname ("bubble boy syndrome") and its portrayal in films. A [bone marrow transplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow_transplant) in the earliest months of life is the standard course of treatment. The exceptional case of [David Vetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Vetter), who lived much of his life encased in a sterile environment because he would not receive a transplant until age 12, was an inspiration for the "bubble boy" trope.

:::

::: {#o542 .entry cue=list-entry name="Post-exposure prophylaxis"}

- [Post-exposure prophylaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-exposure_prophylaxis) for [rabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabies) does not require a painful series of injections into the abdomen with a long needle. Prior to the 1980s, the rabies vaccine did indeed require injection into the abdomen, but modern rabies vaccines are given as [intramuscular injections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular_injections) into the [deltoid muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltoid_muscle) of the upper arm.[^o543][^o544]

:::

::: {#o545 .entry cue=list-entry name="Medication package insert"}

- Statements in [medication package inserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medication_package_insert) listing the frequency of [side effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_effect) describe how often the effect occurs after taking a drug, but are not making any assertion that there is a causal connection between taking the drug and the occurrence of the side effect. In other words, what is being reported on is correlation, not necessarily causation.

:::

::: {#o547 .entry cue=list-entry name="Crystal healing"}

- There is no peer-reviewed scientific evidence that [crystal healing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_healing) has any effect beyond acting as a placebo.[^o549][^o550]

:::

::: {#o551 .entry cue=list-entry name="genetically modified crops"}

- There is a scientific consensus[^o552] that currently available food derived from [genetically modified crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_food) poses no greater risk to human health than conventional food.[^o555]

:::

::: {#o556 .entry cue=list-entry name="Eye strain"}

- Reading in dim light causes [eye strain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_strain) rather than permanent damage to the eye.[^o322]

:::

::: {#o557 .entry cue=list-entry name="Color blindness"}

- [Color blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_blindness) cannot be significantly alleviated by [glasses or lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_blind_glasses). While there are lenses marketed towards the colorblind, their efficacy is doubted by professionals, and they do not enable wearers to see new colors.[^o558]

:::

::: {#o559 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fever"}

- A [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever) from infection does not cause brain damage by itself.[^o560][^o561][^o562] The myth has been linked to the association between fevers and typically non-serious [febrile seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Febrile_seizures).[^o563]

:::

::: {#o564 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tourette's syndrome"}

- [Tourette's syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourette's_syndrome) is not predominantly characterised by the compulsive or frequent use of profanity or taboo words and phrases ([coprolalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprolalia)), as it is commonly misunderstood to be. Only approximately 10% of people with Tourette's exhibit coprolalia at all, and most Tourette's tics (which can be physical or verbal) often go unnoticed by casual observers.[^o565]

:::

::: {#o566 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hippocratic Oath"}

- The [Hippocratic Oath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocratic_Oath) does not begin, "First do no harm" ([Primum non nocere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primum_non_nocere)), nor is the word "First" present in the original text. Physicians taking the Hippocratic Oath vow, however, to "abstain from all intentional wrong-doing and harm".[^o567]

:::

::: {#o568 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vaccines and SIDS"}

- There is no link between [vaccines and SIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccines_and_SIDS) (Sudden Infant Death Syndrome). The misconception might be because 2 to 4 months is the peak age for SIDS and also a period when children receive several vaccines. [^o569][^o570]

:::

### Nutrition, food, and drink {#Nutrition,_food,_and_drink}

::: {#o572 .entry cue=list-entry name="Diet"}

- [Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_%28nutrition%29) has little influence on the body's [detoxification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detoxification), and there is no evidence that [detoxification diets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detoxification_%28alternative_medicine%29) rid the body of toxins.[^o573][^o574] Toxins are metabolized and removed from the bloodstream by the liver and kidneys, and they are primarily removed from the body in urine and bile (excreted with the feces).[^o573]

:::

::: {#o575 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dairy products"}

- Drinking milk or consuming other [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_products) does not increase [mucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucus) production.[^o576] As a result, they do not need to be avoided by those with the flu or cold [congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_congestion). However, milk and saliva in one's mouth mix to create a thick liquid that can briefly coat the mouth and throat. The sensation that lingers may be mistaken for increased [phlegm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlegm).

:::

::: {#o578 .entry cue=list-entry name="Drinking eight glasses (2–3 liters) of water a day"}

- [Drinking eight glasses (2–3 liters) of water a day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_consumption_of_drinking_water) is not needed to maintain health.[^o579] The amount of water needed varies by person, weight, diet, activity level, clothing, and the ambient temperature and humidity. Water requirements can be met from liquids such as juices, tea, milk, soups, etc., and from foods including fruits and vegetables.[^o579]

:::

- Drinking coffee and other caffeinated beverages does not cause dehydration for regular drinkers, although it can for occasional drinkers.[^o581][^o580]

::: {#o582 .entry cue=list-entry name="Eating disorder"}

- [Eating disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eating_disorder) do not exclusively affect women; women are merely more likely than men to suffer from eating disorders.[^o583]

:::

::: {#o584 .entry cue=list-entry name="Peptic ulcers"}

- Neither spicy food nor coffee has a significant effect on the development of [peptic ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptic_ulcers).

:::

::: {#o586 .entry cue=list-entry name="clinical hyperactivity"}

- Sugar does not cause [clinical hyperactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_and_hyperactivity) in children.[^o587] Double-blind trials have shown no difference in behavior between children given sugar-full or sugar-free diets, even in studies specifically looking at children with [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder) or those considered sensitive to sugar. A 2019 meta-analysis found no positive effect of sugar consumption on [mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_%28psychology%29) but did find an association with lower alertness and increased fatigue within an hour of consumption, known as a [sugar crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_crash). Sugar can, however, lead to a jump in blood sugar levels, and may make a child more active even if it does not cause clinical hyperactivity.[^o590]

:::

::: {#o591 .entry cue=list-entry name="Diverticulitis"}

- Eating nuts, popcorn, or seeds does not increase the risk of [diverticulitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diverticulitis). These foods may actually have a protective effect.

:::

- Eating less than an hour before swimming does not significantly increase the risk of experiencing muscle cramps, and does not increase the risk of drowning. One study shows a correlation between alcohol consumption and drowning, but not between eating and stomach cramps.[^o594]

::: {#o595 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vegan"}

- [Vegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegan_nutrition) and [vegetarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarian_nutrition) diets can provide enough protein for adequate nutrition.[^o596] In fact, typical protein intakes of [ovo-lacto vegetarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovo-lacto_vegetarianism) meet or exceed requirements. The American Dietetic Association maintains that appropriately planned vegetarian diets are healthful. However, a vegan diet does require [dietary supplements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_supplements).

:::

::: {#o603 .entry cue=list-entry name="Swallowed chewing gum"}

- [Swallowed chewing gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swallowed_chewing_gum) does not take seven years to digest. Chewing gum is mostly indigestible, and passes through the digestive system at the same rate as other matter.[^o604]

:::

::: {#o605 .entry cue=list-entry name="beta carotene"}

- The [beta carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-Carotene) in carrots does not enhance [night vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_vision) beyond normal levels for people receiving an adequate amount, only in those with a [deficiency of vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A_deficiency).

:::

::: {#o607 .entry cue=list-entry name="Spinach"}

- [Spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinach#Nutrients) is not a particularly good source of [dietary iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_iron_metabolism). While it does contain more iron than many vegetables such as asparagus, Swiss chard, kale, or arugula, it contains only about one-third to one-fifth of the iron in lima beans, chickpeas, apricots, or wheat germ. Additionally, the [non-heme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-heme_iron_protein) iron found in spinach and other vegetables is not as readily absorbed as the [heme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heme) iron found in meats and fish.

:::

::: {#o611 .entry cue=list-entry name="Obesity"}

- Most cases of [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) are not related to slower [resting metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_metabolic_rate). Resting metabolic rate does not vary much between people. Overweight people tend to underestimate the amount of food they eat, and underweight people tend to overestimate. In fact, overweight people tend to have faster metabolic rates due to the increased energy required by the larger body.[^o612]

:::

::: {#o613 .entry cue=list-entry name="hormonal"}

- Eating normal amounts of soy does not cause [hormonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) [imbalance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_disease).[^o614]

:::

::: {#o615 .entry cue=list-entry name="Low-carbohydrate diet"}

- There is no good evidence that [low-carbohydrate diets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbohydrate_diet) have any health benefits besides [weight loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_loss), for which they are about as effective as other diets. Weight loss is primarily a result of [caloric restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloric_restriction), and is not significantly influenced by the balance between fat and carbohydrate in one's diet.[^o616][^o617]

:::

::: {#o618 .entry cue=list-entry name="Monosodium glutamate"}

- [Monosodium glutamate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monosodium_glutamate) has not been proven to cause any negative health effects. [^o619]

:::

#### Alcohol {#Alcohol}

::: {#o621 .entry cue=list-entry name="Alcoholic beverage"}

- [Alcoholic beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) do not make the entire body warmer.[^o622] Alcoholic drinks create the sensation of warmth because they cause blood vessels to dilate and stimulate nerve endings near the surface of the skin with an influx of warm blood. This can actually result in making the core body temperature lower, as it allows for easier heat exchange with a cold external environment.

:::

::: {#o623 .entry cue=list-entry name="Excitotoxicity"}

- Alcohol does not necessarily kill brain cells. Alcohol can, however, lead *indirectly* to the death of brain cells in two ways. First, in chronic, heavy alcohol users whose brains have adapted to the effects of alcohol, abrupt ceasing following heavy use can cause [excitotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excitotoxicity) leading to cellular death in multiple areas of the brain. Second, in alcoholics who get most of their daily calories from alcohol, a deficiency of [thiamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiamine) can produce [Korsakoff's syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korsakoff's_syndrome), which is associated with serious brain damage.

:::

::: {#o627 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hangover"}

- The order in which different types of alcoholic beverages are consumed ("Grape or grain but never the twain" and "Beer before liquor never sicker; liquor before beer in the clear") does not affect [hangover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangover) severity.

:::

::: {#o629 .entry cue=list-entry name="Absinthe"}

- Authentic [absinthe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absinthe) has no [hallucinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogen) properties, and is no more dangerous than any other alcoholic beverage of equivalent proof. This misconception stems from late-19th- and early-20th-century distillers who produced cheap knockoff versions of absinthe, which used [copper salts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_copper_salts) to recreate the distinct green color of true absinthe, and some also reportedly adulterated cheap absinthe with poisonous [antimony trichloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimony_trichloride), reputed to enhance the [louche effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouzo_effect).

:::

### Sexuality and reproduction {#Sexuality_and_reproduction}

::: {#o633 .entry cue=list-entry name="Older adults"}

- [Older adults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexuality_in_older_age) are not necessarily sexually inactive nor have they lost interest in sex; although the frequency of sexual activity tends to decline with age. One survey in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) of people aged 60–69 recorded 86% of men and 60% of women as sexually active.[^o634]

:::

::: {#o635 .entry cue=list-entry name="Swimming pool"}

- It is not possible to get pregnant from semen released in a commercial [swimming pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool) without [penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetrative_sex). The sperm cells would be quickly killed by the [chlorinated water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_pool_sanitation) and would not survive long enough to reach the vagina.[^o636]

:::

::: {#o637 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hymen"}

- An examination of the [hymen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymen) is not an accurate or reliable indicator one has had penetrative [sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_intercourse); the tearing of the hymen may have been the result of some other event, and some are born without one. [Virginity tests](wikipedia://en/13759906), such as the ["two-finger" test](wikipedia://en/13759906#Two-finger_test), are unscientific.[^o643]

:::

::: {#o644 .entry cue=list-entry name="Human penis size"}

- Hand size and foot size do not correlate with [human penis size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_penis_size), but [finger length ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digit_ratio) may.

:::

::: {#o648 .entry cue=list-entry name="first cousins"}

- While pregnancies from sex between [first cousins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cousin_marriage) do carry a slightly elevated risk of [birth defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defect), this risk is often exaggerated. The risk is 5–6% (similar to that of a woman in her early 40s giving birth),[^o649] compared with a baseline risk of 3–4%.[^o650] The effects of [inbreeding depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inbreeding_depression), while still relatively small compared to other factors (and thus difficult to control for in a scientific experiment), become more noticeable if isolated and maintained for several generations.[^o651]

:::

::: {#o652 .entry cue=list-entry name="Testosterone"}

- Having sex before a sporting event or contest is not physiologically detrimental to performance. In fact some studies suggest that sex prior to sports activity can elevate male [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone) levels (which could potentially enhance performance for male athletes), while long periods of abstinence can reduce those levels.[^o654]

:::

::: {#o655 .entry cue=list-entry name="G-spot"}

- The heightened sensitivity some experience at the vaginal [G-spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-spot) is not due to it being a distinct anatomical structure, but rather because pressure in that area may stimulate other internal structures, notably the [Skene's gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skene's_gland). Many [sexologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexologist) take issue with the term, concerned that women who "fail to find their G-spot" may feel abnormal.[^o656]

:::

::: {#o657 .entry cue=list-entry name="Menstrual cycle"}

- The [menstrual cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle) of those who live together do not [tend to synchronize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_synchrony). A 1971 study made this claim, but subsequent research has not supported it.

:::

::: {#o660 .entry cue=list-entry name="Abortion"}

- Having an [abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion) does not increase someone's [risk of developing breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion–breast_cancer_hypothesis). Some smaller, less reliable early studies suggested that abortion could be linked to breast cancer, but the scientific community has concluded that abortion does not cause breast cancer.[^o661][^o662][^o663]

:::

::: {#o664 .entry cue=list-entry name="Christian missionaries"}

- There is no evidence that English-speaking [Christian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_%28Christianity%29) encouraged [converts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) to use the [missionary position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary_position) in the [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_colonialism). This notion probably originated from [Alfred Kinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kinsey)'s *[Sexual Behavior in the Human Male](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsey_Reports)* (1948) through misunderstandings and misinterpretations of historical documents.[^o665][^o666][^o667]

### Skin and hair {#Skin_and_hair}

::: {#o669 .entry cue=list-entry name="Water-induced wrinkles"}

- [Water-induced wrinkles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prune_skin) are not caused by the skin absorbing water and swelling. They are caused by the [autonomic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_nervous_system), which triggers localized [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction) in response to wet skin, yielding a wrinkled appearance.[^o671]

:::

::: {#o672 .entry cue=list-entry name="grow after death"}

- A person's hair and fingernails do not continue to [grow after death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_hair_growth#Growth_cycle). Rather, the skin dries and shrinks away from the bases of hairs and nails, giving the appearance of growth.

:::

::: {#o674 .entry cue=list-entry name="Terminal hair"}

- Shaving does not cause [terminal hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_hair) to grow back thicker or darker. This belief is thought to be due to the fact that hair that has never been cut has a tapered end, so after cutting, the base of the hair is blunt and appears thicker and feels coarser. The fact that short hairs are less flexible than longer hairs contributes to this effect.[^o675]

:::

::: {#o676 .entry cue=list-entry name="MC1R"}

- [MC1R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MC1R), the gene mostly responsible for red hair, is not [becoming extinct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_hair#Extinction_hoax), nor will the [gene for blond hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearing_blonde_gene) do so, although both [alleles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) are [recessive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recessive_gene). Redheads and blonds may become rarer but will not die out unless everyone who carries those alleles dies without passing their hair color genes on to their children.

:::

::: {#o678 .entry cue=list-entry name="Acne"}

- [Acne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acne#Diet) is not caused by a lack of hygiene or eating fatty foods, though certain medications or a [carbohydrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate)-rich diet may worsen it.

:::

::: {#o680 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dandruff"}

- [Dandruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dandruff) is not caused by poor hygiene, though infrequent hair-washing can make it more obvious. The exact causes of dandruff are uncertain, but they are believed to be mostly genetic and environmental factors.[^o681]

:::

## Inventions {#Inventions}

::: {#o683 .entry cue=list-entry name="James Watt"}

- [James Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watt) did not invent the steam engine,[^o684] nor were his ideas on steam engine power inspired by a kettle lid pressured open by steam. Watt improved upon the already commercially successful [Newcomen atmospheric engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomen_atmospheric_engine) (invented in 1712 by [Thomas Newcomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Newcomen)) in the 1760s and 1770s, making certain improvements critical to its future usage; his new steam engine later gained huge fame as a result.

:::

::: {#o687 .entry cue=list-entry name="Joseph-Ignace Guillotin"}

- Although the guillotine was named after the French physician [Joseph-Ignace Guillotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph-Ignace_Guillotin), he neither invented nor was executed with this device. He died peacefully in his own bed in 1814. Rather, it was Guillotin's speech favoring beheadings over other forms of execution that led to the device being referred to as "La machine Guillotine" and later simply guillotine.

:::

::: {#o689 .entry cue=list-entry name="Thomas Crapper"}

- [Thomas Crapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Crapper) did not invent the flush toilet. A forerunner of the modern toilet was invented by the Elizabethan courtier [Sir John Harington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harington_%28writer%29#Invention_of_the_modern_toilet) in the 16th century, and in 1775 the Scottish mechanic [Alexander Cumming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Cumming) developed and patented a design for a toilet with an [S-trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trap_%28plumbing%29) and flushing mechanism. Crapper, however, did much to increase the popularity of the flush toilet and introduced several innovations in the late 19th century, holding nine patents, including one for the floating [ballcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballcock).

:::

::: {#o694 .entry cue=list-entry name="Thomas Edison"}

- *Image: Gluehlampe 01 KMJ.png.* A 230-volt incandescent light bulb [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) did not invent the light bulb. The team of inventors Edison employed at his laboratories in Menlo Park, New Jersey did, however, develop the first *practical* light bulb in 1880 (employing a carbonized [bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo) filament), shortly prior to [Joseph Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Swan), who invented an even more efficient bulb in 1881 (which used a cellulose filament).

:::

::: {#o696 .entry cue=list-entry name="Henry Ford"}

- [Henry Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford) did not invent either the automobile or the assembly line. He did improve the assembly line process substantially, sometimes through his own engineering but more often through sponsoring the work of his employees, and he was the main person behind the introduction of the [Model T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_T), regarded as the first *affordable* automobile.[^o697] [Karl Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Benz) (co-founder of [Mercedes-Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz)) is credited with the invention of the first modern automobile, and the assembly line has existed [throughout history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_assembly_lines).

:::

::: {#o699 .entry cue=list-entry name="Al Gore"}

- [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) never said that he had "invented" the Internet. What Gore actually said was, "During my service in the United States Congress, I took the initiative in creating the Internet", in reference to his political work towards developing the Internet for widespread public use. Gore was the original drafter of the [High Performance Computing and Communication Act of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Performance_Computing_and_Communication_Act_of_1991), which provided significant funding for supercomputing centers,[^o702] and this in turn led to upgrades of a major part of the already-existing early 1990s Internet backbone, the [NSFNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSFNet),[^o701] and development of [NCSA Mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCSA_Mosaic), the [browser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_browser) that popularized the [World Wide Web](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web). (See also: [Al Gore and information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore_and_information_technology))

:::

::: {#o703 .entry cue=list-entry name="Kodak"}

- [Kodak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodak) did not refuse to invest in digital cameras, but was rather a pioneer in the field,[^o704][^o705] and at one point was the market leader in digital camera sales in the United States.[^o706]

:::

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

*Image: Kapitolinischer Pythagoras adjusted.jpg.* Classical historians dispute whether [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275) made any mathematical discoveries.[^o708][^n1]

::: {#o710 .entry cue=list-entry name="Hindu-Arabic numerals"}

- [Hindu-Arabic numerals](wikipedia://en/1786) were not originally designed to indicate their numeric value through the number of angles they contain. There are no historical records of this, and the myth is difficult to reconcile with digits past 3 or 4.[^o711]

:::

::: {#o712 .entry cue=list-entry name="Pythagoras"}

- The Greek philosopher [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275) was not the first to discover what is now called the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034), as it was known and used by the [Babylonians](wikipedia://en/3861353) and [Indians](wikipedia://en/1848052) centuries before him.[^n2][^o714][^n3][^o716][^o717] Pythagoras may have been [the first to introduce it to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_discovery),[^n4][^n3] but the first record of it being [mathematically proven](wikipedia://en/82285) as a [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) is in [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331)'s [*Elements*](wikipedia://en/244107) which was published some 200 years after Pythagoras.

:::

::: {#o720 .entry cue=list-entry name="Parthenon"}

- There is no evidence that the ancient Greeks deliberately designed the [Parthenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthenon) to match the [golden ratio](wikipedia://en/12386).[^o721] The Parthenon was completed in 438 BCE, more than a century before the first recorded mention of the ratio by [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331). Similarly, [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci)'s *[Vitruvian Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvian_Man)* makes no mention of the golden ratio in its text, although it describes many other proportions.[^o722]

:::

::: {#o723 .entry cue=list-entry name="0.999..."}

- The repeating decimal written as [0.999...](wikipedia://en/1849799) represents exactly the same quantity as [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_%28number%29).

:::

::: {#o727 .entry cue=list-entry name="p-value"}

- The [*p*-value](wikipedia://en/554994) is not the probability that the [null hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_hypothesis) is true, or the probability that the [alternative hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_hypothesis) is false; it is the probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as the results actually observed under the assumption that the null hypothesis was correct, which can indicate the incompatibility of results with the specific statistical model assumed in the null hypothesis. This misconception, and similar ones like it, contributes to the common [misuse of *p*-values](wikipedia://en/49498411) in education and research.[^o728]

:::

::: {#o730 .entry cue=list-entry name="Fair coin"}

- If one were to flip a [fair coin](wikipedia://en/9597100) five times and get heads each time, it would not be any more likely for a sixth flip to come up tails. Phrased another way, after a long and/or unlikely streak of [independently](wikipedia://en/27593) [random](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random) events, the probability of the next event is not influenced by the preceding events. [Humans often feel that the underrepresented outcome is more likely](wikipedia://en/12970), as if it is due to happen. Such thinking may be attributed to the mistaken belief that gambling, or even chance itself, is a fair process that can correct itself in the event of streaks.[^o731]

:::

## Physics {#Physics}

*Image: Equal transit-time NASA wrong1.gif.* An illustration of the *incorrect* equal transit-time explanation of airfoil lift

::: {#o733 .entry cue=list-entry name="lift force"}

- The [lift force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_%28force%29) is not generated by the air taking the same time to travel above and below an aircraft's wing.[^o734] This misconception, sometimes called the [equal transit-time fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_transit-time_fallacy), is widespread among textbooks and non-technical reference books, and even appears in pilot training materials. In fact, the air moving over the top of an aerofoil generating lift is always moving much faster than the equal transit theory would imply,[^o734] as described in the [incorrect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_transit-time_fallacy) and [correct explanations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_%28force%29#Simplified_physical_explanations_of_lift_on_an_airfoil) of lift force.

:::

*Image: Blowing over and under a piece of paper.jpg.* Blowing over a piece of paper causes it to rise. Blowing under a piece of paper also causes it to rise. Neither is a demonstration of Bernoulli's Principle.[^o735]

::: {#o736 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bernoulli's principle"}

- Blowing over a curved piece of paper does not demonstrate [Bernoulli's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernoulli's_principle#Misconceptions_and_misapplications). Although a common classroom experiment is often explained this way,[^o737] Bernoulli's principle applies only within a flow field, and the air above and below the paper are in different flow fields.[^o738] The paper rises because the air follows the curve of the paper and a curved [streamline](wikipedia://en/166086) will develop pressure differences perpendicular to the airflow.[^o739][^o740]

:::

::: {#o741 .entry cue=list-entry name="Coriolis effect"}

- The [Coriolis effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriolis_effect) does not cause water to consistently drain from basins in a clockwise/counter-clockwise direction depending on the hemisphere. The common myth often refers to the draining action of flush toilets and bathtubs. In fact, rotation is determined by whatever minor rotation is initially present at the time the water starts to drain, as the magnitude of the Coriolis acceleration is [negligibly small](wikipedia://en/459426) compared to the inertial acceleration of flow within a typical basin.[^o742]

:::

::: {#o743 .entry cue=list-entry name="General relativity"}

- [General relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) does not imply that mass increases as an object approaches the [speed of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_light); it is an object's [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431), a quantity dependent upon both mass and velocity, that increases [asymptotically](wikipedia://en/3107) as it approaches the speed of light. The mass-energy equivalence equation is thus more accurately expressed as E=γmc², where γ is a variable dependent upon velocity. For an object at rest, γ=1 resulting in the familiar equation E=mc².[^o744][^o745]

:::

::: {#o746 .entry cue=list-entry name="gyroscopic forces"}

- Neither [gyroscopic forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_and_motorcycle_dynamics#Gyroscopic_effects) nor [geometric trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_%28steering%29) are required for a rider to [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_and_motorcycle_dynamics#Other_hypotheses) a bicycle or for it to demonstrate [self-stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_and_motorcycle_dynamics#Self-stability).[^o748] Although gyroscopic forces and trail can be contributing factors, it [has been demonstrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-mass-skate_bicycle) that those factors are neither required nor sufficient by themselves.[^o747]

:::

::: {#o749 .entry cue=list-entry name="Empire State Building"}

- A penny dropped from the [Empire State Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_Building) would not kill a person or crack the sidewalk. A penny is too light and has too much air resistance to acquire enough speed to do much damage since it reaches [terminal velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_velocity) after falling about 50 ft. Heavier or more aerodynamic objects could cause significant damage if dropped from that height.

:::

::: {#o752 .entry cue=list-entry name="Programmable thermostat"}

- Using a [programmable thermostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmable_thermostat)'s setback feature to limit heating or cooling in a temporarily unoccupied building does not waste as much energy as leaving the temperature constant. Using setback saves energy (5–15%) because [heat transfer](wikipedia://en/184726) across the surface of the building is roughly proportional to the temperature difference between its inside and the outside.

:::

- It is not possible for a person to completely submerge in quicksand, as commonly depicted in fiction, although sand entrapment in the nearshore of a body of water can be a drowning hazard as the tide rises.

::: {#o757 .entry cue=list-entry name="Quantum nonlocality"}

- [Quantum nonlocality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_nonlocality) caused by [quantum entanglement](wikipedia://en/25336) does not allow [faster-than-light communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faster-than-light_communication) or imply instant [action at a distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_at_a_distance), despite its common characterization as "spooky action at a distance". Rather, it means that [certain experiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_test) cannot be explained by [local realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_realism).

:::

::: {#o760 .entry cue=list-entry name="The slipperiness of ice"}

- [The slipperiness of ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice#Friction_properties) is not due to [pressure melting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_melting_point). While it is true that increased pressure, such as that exerted by someone standing on a sheet of ice, will lower the melting point of ice, experiments show that the effect is too weak to account for the lowered friction. [Materials scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_science) still debate whether [premelting or the heat of friction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premelting#Ice_skating) is the dominant cause of ice's slipperiness.[^o761]

:::

## Psychology and neuroscience {#Psychology_and_neuroscience}

::: {#o764 .entry cue=list-entry name="Cannabis use in pregnancy"}

- [Cannabis use in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_pregnancy) is not low risk. The tetrahydrocannabinol ([THC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THC)) within cannabis crosses the placenta, directly exposing the developing fetus to this chemical. Cannabis use in pregnancy is linked to increased risk of preterm delivery and lower birth weight, along with a higher likelihood of infants needing placement in the neonatal intensive care unit.[^o765] Additionally, infants exposed to cannabis in pregnancy may have increased rates of behavioral conditions such as hyperactivity and impulsivity, attention deficits, sleep disorders, emotional disturbances, and a higher chance of substance use.[^o766] According to a 2015 study, 70% of pregnant American women that had used marijuana in the past year thought that consumption of cannabis once or twice per week carried low to no risk of general harm.[^o767]

:::

::: {#o768 .entry cue=list-entry name="Photographic memory"}

- True [photographic memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photographic_memory) (the ability to remember endless images, particularly pages or numbers, with such a high degree of precision that the image mimics a photo) has never been demonstrated to exist in any individual, although a small number of young children have [eidetic memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidetic_memory), where they can recall an object with high precision for a few minutes after it is no longer present. Many people have claimed to have a photographic memory, but those people have been shown to have high precision memories as a result of [mnemonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnemonic) devices rather than a natural capacity for detailed memory encoding. There are rare cases of individuals with [exceptional memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exceptional_memory), but none of them have a memory that mimics that of a camera.

:::

::: {#o772 .entry cue=list-entry name="lunar cycle"}

- The phase of the Moon does not influence fertility, cause a fluctuation in crime, or affect the stock market. There is no correlation between the [lunar cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_effect) and human biology or behavior. However, the increased amount of illumination during the full moon may account for increased epileptic episodes, motorcycle accidents, or sleep disorders.[^o773]

:::

::: {#o774 .entry cue=list-entry name="Repressed memories"}

- [Repressed memories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repressed_memory) (the latent, oftentimes traumatic memories of early childhood that are recalled later in life) do not exist. This idea may have been popularized by [Marcel Proust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Proust)'s *[In Search of Lost Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Search_of_Lost_Time).* However, a person cannot retain memories from infancy. The localized amnesia of childhood trauma is impossible, although false memories can be induced.[^o775][^o776]

:::

### Mental disorders {#Mental_disorders}

::: {#o778 .entry cue=list-entry name="Vaccines do not cause autism"}

- [Vaccines do not cause autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccines_and_autism). There have been no successful attempts to reproduce [fraudulent research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMR_vaccine_controversy) by British ex-doctor [Andrew Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wakefield), where the misconception likely originates. Wakefield's research was ultimately shown to have been manipulated.

:::

::: {#o780 .entry cue=list-entry name="Dyslexia"}

- [Dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia) is not defined or diagnosed as [mirror writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirror_writing) or reading letters or words backwards. Mirror writing and reading letters or words backwards are behaviors seen in many children (dyslexic or not) as they learn to read and write.[^o781][^o782] [Dyslexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyslexia) is a neurodevelopmental disorder of people who have at least average intelligence and who have difficulty in reading and writing that is not otherwise explained by low intelligence.

:::

::: {#o784 .entry cue=list-entry name="Self-harm"}

- [Self-harm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-harm) is not generally an [attention-seeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention-seeking) behavior. People who engage in self-harm are typically very self-conscious of their wounds and scars and feel guilty about their behavior, leading them to go to great lengths to conceal it from others. They may offer alternative explanations for their injuries, or conceal their scars with clothing.

:::

- There is no evidence that a chemical imbalance or neurotransmitter deficiency is the sole factor in depression and other mental disorders, but rather a combination of biological, psychological, and social factors.

::: {#o790 .entry cue=list-entry name="Schizophrenia"}

- [Schizophrenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schizophrenia) does not involve split or multiple personalities. It is characterized by continuous or relapsing episodes of psychosis. Major symptoms include hallucinations (typically hearing voices), delusions, paranoia, and disorganized thinking. Other symptoms include social withdrawal, decreased emotional expression, and apathy.[^o791] The term was coined from the Greek roots *schizein* and *phrēn*, "to split" and "mind", in reference to a "splitting of mental functions" seen in schizophrenia, not a splitting of the personality. A split or multiple personality is [dissociative identity disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissociative_identity_disorder).[^o793]

:::

### Brain {#Brain}

::: {#o795 .entry cue=list-entry name="lateralized"}

- Broad generalizations are often made in popular psychology about certain brain functions being [lateralized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateralization_of_brain_function), or more predominant in one hemisphere than the other. [These claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateralization_of_brain_function#Misconceptions) are often inaccurate or overstated.

:::

::: {#o797 .entry cue=list-entry name="Prefrontal cortex"}

- The human brain, particularly the [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex), does not reach ["full maturity" or "full development"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroplasticity) at [any particular age (e.g. 16, 18, 21, 25, 30).](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_"brain_matures_at_25"_myth) Changes in structure and [myelination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myelination) of [gray matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_matter) are recorded to continue with relative consistency all throughout life including until death. Different mental abilities peak earlier or later in life.[^o798]

:::

::: {#o799 .entry cue=list-entry name="Neuron"}

- *Image: Gyrus Dentatus 40x.jpg.* Some neurons can reform in the human brain. Humans do not generate all of the brain cells they will ever have by the age of two years. Although this belief was held by medical experts until 1998, it is now understood that new [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) can be created [after infancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postnatal) in some parts of the brain into late adulthood.

:::

::: {#o801 .entry cue=list-entry name="only 10% of their brains"}

- People do not use [only 10% of their brains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_percent_of_the_brain_myth). While it is true that a small minority of [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) in the brain are actively firing at any one time, a healthy human will normally use most of their brain over the course of a day, and the inactive neurons are important as well. The idea that activating 100% of the brain would allow someone to achieve their maximum potential and/or gain various psychic abilities is common in [folklore and fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_percent_of_the_brain_myth#In_popular_culture),[^o803][^o807] but doing so in real life would likely result in a fatal [seizure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizure). This misconception was attributed to late 19th century leading thinker [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James), who apparently used the expression only metaphorically.

:::

::: {#o808 .entry cue=list-entry name="Phineas Gage"}

- Although [Phineas Gage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phineas_Gage)'s brain injuries, caused by a several-foot-long tamping rod driven completely through his skull, caused him to become temporarily disabled, descriptions of his aberrant behavior in later life are disputed, contradicting other claims that he recovered fully.

:::

### Senses {#Senses}

*Image: Taste buds.svg.* An incorrect [map of the tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue_map) showing taste zones. In fact, all zones can sense all tastes.

::: {#o811 .entry cue=list-entry name="Aristotle"}

- Humans have more than the commonly cited five senses. The number of senses in various categorizations ranges from 5 to more than 20. In addition to sight, smell, taste, touch, and hearing, which were the senses identified by [Aristotle](wikipedia://en/308), humans can sense [balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_%28ability%29) and acceleration ([equilibrioception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equilibrioception)), pain ([nociception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nociception)), body and limb position ([proprioception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprioception) or kinesthetic sense), and relative temperature ([thermoception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoception)). Other senses sometimes identified are the sense of time, [echolocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_echolocation), itching, pressure, hunger, thirst, fullness of the stomach, need to urinate, need to defecate, blood carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) levels, and electric field sensation.

:::

::: {#o815 .entry cue=list-entry name="Tongue map"}

- All different tastes can be detected on all parts of the tongue by taste buds, with slightly increased sensitivities in different locations depending on the person; the [tongue map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue_map) showing the contrary is fallacious.

:::

::: {#o818 .entry cue=list-entry name="Primary tastes"}

- There are not four [primary tastes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_tastes), but five: in addition to bitter, sour, salty, and sweet, humans have taste receptors for [umami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umami), which is a "savory" or "meaty" taste.[^o819] Fat does interact with specific [receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_%28biochemistry%29) in [taste bud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taste_bud) cells, but whether it is a sixth primary taste remains inconclusive.

:::

::: {#o821 .entry cue=list-entry name="Sense of smell"}

- The human [sense of smell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sense_of_smell) is not weak or underdeveloped. Humans have similar senses of smell to other mammals, and are more sensitive to some odors than rodents and dogs.

:::

## Toxicology {#Toxicology}

::: {#o824 .entry cue=list-entry name="Smokeless tobacco"}

- [Smokeless tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smokeless_tobacco) is not a "safe" alternative to conventional tobacco; smokeless tobacco products contain [nicotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotine) and are therefore highly addictive. They also can cause various harmful effects such as dental disease, [oral cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_cancer), [oesophagus cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer), and [pancreas cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatic_cancer), [coronary heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease), as well as negative reproductive effects including [stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth), [premature birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premature_birth) and [low birth weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birth_weight).[^o825]

:::

- Swallowing gasoline does not generally require special emergency treatment, as long as it goes into the stomach and not the lungs. This does not mean gasoline is safe to drink; it is very dangerous when consumed.[^o827] Inducing vomiting can make it worse.[^o828]

::: {#o829 .entry cue=list-entry name="Chloroform"}

- A [chloroform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroform)-soaked rag cannot instantly incapacitate a person. It takes at least five minutes of inhaling an item soaked in chloroform to render a person unconscious. Most criminal cases involving chloroform also involve another drug being co-administered, such as alcohol or [diazepam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diazepam), or the victim being found to have been complicit in its administration. The misconception that chloroform can be used as an [incapacitating agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incapacitating_agent) has been popularized by crime fiction authors.

:::

::: {#o832 .entry cue=list-entry name="Radioactive isotope"}

- Although bananas contain naturally occurring [radioactive isotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_isotope), particularly [potassium-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium-40) (<sup>40</sup>K), which emit [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation) when undergoing [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay), the levels of such radiation are far too low to induce [radiation poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_poisoning), and bananas are not a radiation hazard. It would not be physically possible to eat enough bananas to cause radiation poisoning, as [the radiation dose from bananas is non-cumulative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_dose).[^o835] (See also: [Banana equivalent dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_equivalent_dose))

:::

::: {#o836 .entry cue=list-entry name="Visine"}

- Ingesting [Visine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visine), a brand of eye drops, does not cause diarrhea. It is [neurotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxicity), with consumption causing several serious side effects. Pranks spiking people with Visine rose after the misconception was popularized by the film *[Wedding Crashers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wedding_Crashers)*.[^o838]

:::

## Transportation {#Transportation}

*Image: Bermuda Triangle.png.* One version of the Bermuda Triangle area

::: {#o840 .entry cue=list-entry name="Bermuda Triangle"}

- The [Bermuda Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Triangle) does not have any more shipwrecks or mysterious disappearances than most other waterways.[^o841]

:::

::: {#o842 .entry cue=list-entry name="toilet waste vehicles"}

- Toilet waste is never intentionally jettisoned from a commercial aircraft. All waste is collected in tanks and emptied into [toilet waste vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_support_equipment#Lavatory_service_vehicles). [Blue ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_ice_%28aviation%29) is caused by accidental leakage from the waste tank. [Passenger train toilets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger_train_toilet), on the other hand, have indeed historically flushed onto the tracks, and the practice still exists in some countries. Modern trains in most developed countries usually have retention tanks on board and therefore do not dispose of waste in such a manner.

:::

::: {#o845 .entry cue=list-entry name="Automotive batteries"}

- [Automotive batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_battery) stored on a concrete floor do not discharge any faster than they would on other surfaces, in spite of a worry that concrete harms batteries. Early batteries with porous, leaky cases may have been susceptible to moisture from floors, but for many years [lead–acid car batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead–acid_battery) have had impermeable [polypropylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polypropylene) cases. While most modern automotive batteries are [sealed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VRLA_battery), and do not leak battery acid when properly stored and maintained,[^o848] the sulfuric acid in them can leak out and stain, etch, or corrode concrete floors if their cases crack or tip over or their vent-holes are breached by floods.[^o849]

:::

## Weapons {#Weapons}

::: {#o851 .entry cue=list-entry name="fly backwards"}

- Someone hit by a shotgun will not [fly backwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stopping_power#Knockback) like it is often portrayed in movies, and the same is true for other handheld weapons. The [momentum](wikipedia://en/20431) of the bullet is no higher than the [recoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoil) transmitted to the shooter, which is too little to cause any significant motion.[^o852][^o853]

:::

::: {#o854 .entry cue=list-entry name="Land mine"}

- [Land mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_mine) explode immediately when stepped on. They are not armed when stepped on and then triggered by stepping off, as depicted in fictional portrayals where disarming the mine is a source of narrative.[^o855][^o856][^o857]

:::
