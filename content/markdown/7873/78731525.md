# Brokard's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Brokard's theorem"}

**Brokard's theorem** (also known as **Brocard's theorem**[^o2]) is a theorem on [poles and polars](wikipedia://en/19451201) in [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) commonly used in [Olympiad mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiad_mathematics).[^o2][^o3][^o4] It is named after French mathematician [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496).

:::

*Image: Brokard's theorem.svg.*

::: {#o6 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

> **Brokard's theorem**. The points *A*, *B*, *C*, and *D* lie in this order on a circle $\omega$ with center *O*. Lines *AC* and *BD* intersect at *P*, *AB* and *DC* intersect at *Q*, and *AD* and *BC* intersect at *R*. Then *O* is the orthocenter of $\triangle PQR$. Furthermore, *QR* is the [polar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_%28geometry%29) of *P*, *PQ* is the polar of *R*, and *PR* is the polar of *Q* with respect to $\omega$.[^o2][^o7][^o8]

:::
