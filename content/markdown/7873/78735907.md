# Gauss congruence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gauss congruence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Gauss congruence** is a property held by certain [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), including the [Lucas numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_numbers) and the [divisor sum](wikipedia://en/407249) sequence. Sequences satisfying this property are also known as Dold sequences, Fermat sequences, Newton sequences, and realizable sequences.[^o2] The property is named after [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) (1777–1855), although Gauss never defined the property explicitly.[^o3]

:::

Sequences satisfying Gauss congruence naturally occur in the study of [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713), [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705) and [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170).[^o4]

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A sequence of integers $(a_1,a_2,\dots)$ satisfies Gauss congruence if

$$\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)a_{n/d}\equiv 0\pmod{n}$$

for every $n\geq 1$, where $\mu$ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961). By [Möbius inversion](wikipedia://en/20963), this condition is equivalent to the existence of a sequence of integers $(b_1,b_2,\dots)$ such that

$$a_n=\sum_{d\mid n}b_dd$$

for every $n\geq 1$. Furthermore, this is equivalent to the existence of a sequence of integers $(c_1,c_2,\dots)$ such that

$$a_n=c_1a_{n-1}+c_2a_{n-2}+\cdots+c_{n-1}a_1+nc_n$$

for every $n\geq 1$.[^o7] If the values $c_n$ are eventually zero, then the sequence $(a_1,a_2,\dots)$ satisfies a [linear recurrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_recurrence).

A direct relationship between the sequences $(b_1,b_2,\dots)$ and $(c_1,c_2,\dots)$ is given by the equality of [generating functions](wikipedia://en/160993)

$$\sum_{n\geq 1}c_nx^n=1-\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-x^n)^{b_n}.$$

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Below are examples of sequences $(a_n)_{n\geq 1}$ known to satisfy Gauss congruence.

- $(a^n)$ for any integer $a$, with $c_1=a$ and $c_n=0$ for $n>1$.
- ${\rm{tr}}(A^n)$ for any [square matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859#Square_matrix) $A$ with integer entries.[^o4]
- The divisor-sum sequence $(1,3,4,7,6,12,\dots)$, with $b_n=1$ for every $n\geq 1$.
- The Lucas numbers $(1,3,4,7,11,18\dots)$, with $c_1=c_2=1$ and $c_n=0$ for every $n>2$.

:::

## In dynamical systems {#In_dynamical_systems}

Consider a discrete-time [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087), consisting of a set $X$ and a map $T:X\to X$. We write $T^n$ for the $n$<sup>th</sup> iteration of the map, and say an element $x$ in $X$ has *period* $n$ if $T^nx=x$.

Suppose the number of points in $X$ with period $n$ is finite for every $n\geq 1$. If $a_n$ denotes the number of such points, then the sequence $(a_n)_{n\geq 1}$ satisfies Gauss congruence, and the associated sequence $(b_n)_{n\geq 1}$ counts orbits of size $n$.[^o2]

For example, fix a positive integer $\alpha$. If $X$ is the set of [aperiodic necklaces](wikipedia://en/6226587#Aperiodic_necklaces) with beads of $\alpha$ colors and $T$ acts by rotating each necklace clockwise by a bead, then $a_n=\alpha^n$ and $b_n$ counts [Lyndon words](wikipedia://en/3919355) of length $n$ in an alphabet of $\alpha$ letters.

## In algebraic number theory {#In_algebraic_number_theory}

Gauss congruence can be extended to sequences of [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational) numbers, where such a sequence $(a_n)_{n\geq 1}$ satisfies Gauss congruence *at a prime* $p$ if

$$\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)a_{n/d}\equiv 0\pmod{n}$$

for every $n=p^r$ with $r\geq 1$, or equivalently, if $a_{p^r}\equiv a_{p^{r-1}}\text{ mod }p^r$ for every $r\geq 1$.

A sequence of rational numbers $(a_n)_{n\geq 1}$ defined by a linear recurrence satisfies Gauss congruence at all but finitely many primes if and only if

$$a_n=\sum_{i=1}^r q_i{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\mathbb{K}\mid\mathbb{Q}}(\theta_i^n),$$

where $\mathbb{K}$ is an [algebraic number field](wikipedia://en/28730822) with $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\in\mathbb{K}$, and $q_1,\dots,q_r\in\mathbb{Q}$.[^o12]
