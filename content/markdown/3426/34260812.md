# Superpartient ratio

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Superpartient ratio"}

In mathematics, a **superpartient ratio**, also called **superpartient number** or **epimeric ratio**, is a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) that is greater than one and is not [superparticular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superparticular_number). The term has fallen out of use in modern pure mathematics, but continues to be used in [music theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_theory) and in the [historical study of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220).

:::

Superpartient ratios were written about by [Nicomachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicomachus) in his treatise *[Introduction to Arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_Arithmetic)*.

## Overview {#Overview}

Mathematically, a **superpartient number** is a [ratio](wikipedia://en/87837) of the form

$$\frac{n + a}{n}\, ,$$

where *a* is greater than 1 (*a* > 1) and is also [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to *n*. Ratios of the form $\tfrac{n+1}{n}$ are also greater than one and fully reduced, but are called [superparticular ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superparticular_number) and are not superpartient.

*Examples*

::: {#o3 .table cue=table name="Examples"}

| Ratio | $\frac{n + a}{n}$ | Related musical interval | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5:3 | $\frac{3+2}{3}$ | [Major sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_sixth) | Just major sixth on C.mid |
| 7:4 | $\frac{4+3}{4}$ | [Harmonic seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_seventh) | Harmonic seventh on C.mid |
| 8:5 | $\frac{5+3}{5}$ | [Minor sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_sixth) | Just minor sixth on C.mid |

:::

## Etymology {#Etymology}

*Superpartient* is a [calque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calque) from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἐπιμερής *epimeres* "containing a whole and a fraction".
