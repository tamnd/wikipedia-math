# Xor–encrypt–xor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Xor–encrypt–xor"}

*Image: Xor Encrypt Xor.svg.* XEX technique: Key1 and Key2 extend the original (short) Key

The **xor–encrypt–xor** (**XEX**) is a (tweakable) [mode of operation of a block cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_cipher_modes_of_operation). In tweaked-codebook mode with [ciphertext stealing](wikipedia://en/1161746) ([XTS mode](wikipedia://en/3395256#XTS)), it is one of the more popular modes of operation for [whole-disk encryption](wikipedia://en/3395256#Xor–encrypt–xor_%28XEX%29). XEX is also a common form of [key whitening](wikipedia://en/2990178), and part of some [smart card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_card) proposals.[^o2][^o3]

:::

## History {#History}

In 1984, to protect DES against exhaustive search attacks, [Ron Rivest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rivest) proposed [DESX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DESX): XOR a pre-[whitening](wikipedia://en/2990178) key to the plaintext, encrypt the result with DES using a secret key, and then XOR a postwhitening key to the encrypted result to produce the final ciphertext.[^o5]

In 1991, motivated by Rivest's DESX construction, [Even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Even) and [Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishay_Mansour) proposed a much simpler scheme (the "**two-key Even–Mansour scheme**"), which they suggested was perhaps the simplest possible block cipher: XOR the plaintext with a prewhitening key, apply a publicly known unkeyed permutation (in practice, a [pseudorandom permutation](wikipedia://en/4681949)) to the result, and then XOR a postwhitening key to the permuted result to produce the final ciphertext.[^o5][^o6][^o7][^o8]

Studying simple Even–Mansour style block ciphers gives insight into the security of [Feistel ciphers](wikipedia://en/352709) (DES-like ciphers) and helps understand [block cipher](wikipedia://en/4594) design in general.[^o9]

[Orr Dunkelman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orr_Dunkelman), Nathan Keller, and [Adi Shamir](wikipedia://en/57314) later proved it was possible to simplify the Even–Mansour scheme even further and still retain the same provable security, producing the "**single-key Even–Mansour scheme**": XOR the plaintext with the key, apply a publicly known unkeyed permutation to the result, and then XOR the same key to the permuted result to produce the final ciphertext.[^o5][^o10]

In 2004, Rogaway presented the XEX scheme with key and location-dependent "tweaks":[^o11]

*Image: Xor Encrypt Xor (XEX) mode encryption.svg.*

Rogaway used XEX to allow efficient processing of consecutive blocks (with respect to the cipher used) within one data unit (e.g., a disk sector) for [whole-disk encryption](wikipedia://en/3395256#Xor–encrypt–xor_%28XEX%29).[^o11]

Many whole-disk encryption systems – [BestCrypt](wikipedia://en/7748321), [dm-crypt](wikipedia://en/10782256), [FreeOTFE](wikipedia://en/4954583), [TrueCrypt](wikipedia://en/905962), [DiskCryptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DiskCryptor), FreeBSD's [geli](wikipedia://en/22725313), OpenBSD softraid disk encryption software, and Mac OS X Lion's [FileVault](wikipedia://en/466854) 2 – support XEX-based tweaked-codebook mode with ciphertext stealing ([XTS mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XTS_mode)).
