# Orthogonal polyhedron

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal polyhedron"}

An **orthogonal polyhedron** is a [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470) in which all edges are parallel to the axes of a [Cartesian coordinate system](wikipedia://en/7706), resulting in the [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) faces and implying the [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) between faces are [right angles](wikipedia://en/76956).

:::

Though the angles between [Jessen's icosahedron](wikipedia://en/20347079)'s faces are right angles, the edges are not axis-parallel, thus Jessen's icosahedron is not an orthogonal polyhedron.

[Polycubes](wikipedia://en/710426) are a special case of orthogonal polyhedra that can be decomposed into identical cubes and are three-dimensional analogs of planar [polyominoes](wikipedia://en/72634). Orthogonal polyhedra can be either [convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) (such as [rectangular cuboids](wikipedia://en/38848329)) or non-convex.

Orthogonal polyhedra were used by (Sydler 1965), who showed that any polyhedron is equivalent to a cube: it can be decomposed into pieces that later can be used to construct a cube. This showed the requirements for the polyhedral equivalence conditions in terms of the [Dehn invariant](wikipedia://en/338051). Orthogonal polyhedra may also be used in [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927), where their constrained structure has enabled advances in problems unsolved for arbitrary polyhedra, for example, unfolding the surface of a polyhedron to a [polygonal net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygonal_net).

The **simple orthogonal polyhedra**, as defined by (Eppstein Mumford 2014), are the three-dimensional polyhedra such that three mutually perpendicular edges meet at each vertex and that have the topology of a sphere. By using [Steinitz's theorem](wikipedia://en/19762817), there are three different classes: the arbitrary orthogonal polyhedron, the skeleton of its polyhedron drawn with a hidden vertex by the [isometric projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isometric_projection), and the polyhedron wherein each axis-parallel line through a vertex contains other vertices. All of these are [polyhedral graphs](wikipedia://en/24656270) that are [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) and [bipartite](wikipedia://en/244431).
