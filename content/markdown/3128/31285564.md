# Théophile Pépin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Théophile Pépin"}

**Jean François Théophile Pépin** (14 May 1826 – 3 April 1904) was a French mathematician.

:::

Born in [Cluses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluses), [Haute-Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Savoie), he became a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) in 1846, and from 1850 to 1856 and from 1862 to 1871 he was Professor of Mathematics at various Jesuit colleges. He was appointed Professor of [Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Law) in 1873, moving to [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in 1880. He died in [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon) at the age of 77.

His work centred on [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). In 1876 he found a new proof of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) for *n* = 7,[^o2] and in 1880 he published the first general solution[^o3] to [Frénicle de Bessy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frénicle_de_Bessy)'s problem

*x*<sup>2</sup> + *y*<sup>2</sup> = *z*<sup>2</sup>, *x*<sup>2</sup> = *u*<sup>2</sup> + *v*<sup>2</sup>, *x* − *y* = *u* − *v*.

He also gave his name to [Pépin's test](wikipedia://en/3266846), a test of primality for [Fermat numbers](wikipedia://en/91127).
