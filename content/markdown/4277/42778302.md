# Multiplicity theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Multiplicity theory"}

In abstract algebra, **multiplicity theory** concerns the **multiplicity of a module** *M* at an [ideal](wikipedia://en/25977) *I* (often a maximal ideal)

:::

$$\mathbf{e}_I(M).$$

The notion of the multiplicity of a module is a generalization of the [degree of a projective variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_of_a_projective_variety). By Serre's intersection formula, it is linked to an [intersection multiplicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_multiplicity) in the [intersection theory](wikipedia://en/3148602).

The main focus of the theory is to detect and measure a [singular point of an algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/981915) (cf. [resolution of singularities](wikipedia://en/9914115)). Because of this aspect, [valuation theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_theory), [Rees algebras](wikipedia://en/25362831) and [integral closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_closure) are intimately connected to multiplicity theory.

## Multiplicity of a module {#Multiplicity_of_a_module}

Let *R* be a positively graded ring such that *R* is finitely generated as an *R*<sub>0</sub>-algebra and *R*<sub>0</sub> is [Artinian](wikipedia://en/489273). Note that *R* has finite [Krull dimension](wikipedia://en/56079) *d*. Let *M* be a finitely generated *R*-module and *F*<sub>*M*</sub>(*t*) its [Hilbert–Poincaré series](wikipedia://en/17006296). This series is a rational function of the form

$$\frac{P(t)}{(1-t)^d},$$

where $P(t)$ is a polynomial. By definition, the multiplicity of *M* is

$$\mathbf{e}(M) = P(1).$$

The series may be rewritten

$$F(t) = \sum_1^d {a_{d-i} \over (1 - t)^d} + r(t).$$

where *r*(*t*) is a polynomial. Note that $a_{d-i}$ are the coefficients of the Hilbert polynomial of *M* expanded in binomial coefficients. We have

$$\mathbf{e}(M) = a_0.$$

As Hilbert–Poincaré series are additive on exact sequences, the multiplicity is additive on exact sequences of modules of the same dimension.

The following theorem, due to Christer Lech, gives a priori bounds for multiplicity.[^o3][^o4]

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Lech"}

**Lech.**

Suppose *R* is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. If an *I* is $\mathfrak{m}$-primary ideal, then

$$e(I) \le d! \deg(R) \lambda(R/\overline{I}).$$

:::
