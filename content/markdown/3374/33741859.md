# Cryptocat

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Cryptocat"}

**Cryptocat** is a discontinued [open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software) [desktop application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_software) intended to allow encrypted [online chatting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_chat) available for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows), [OS X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OS_X), and [Linux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux).[^o3] It uses [end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362) to secure all communications to other Cryptocat users. Users are given the option of independently verifying their buddies' device lists and are notified when a buddy's device list is modified and all updates are verified through the built-in update downloader.[^o24]

:::

Cryptocat was created by [Nadim Kobeissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadim_Kobeissi) and further developed along with a community of open source contributors and is published under the terms of the [GPLv3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPLv3) license, although it has since been discontinued.

## History {#History}

Cryptocat was first launched on 19 May 2011 as a [web application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_application).

In June 2012, Kobeissi said he was detained at the U.S. border by the [DHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Homeland_Security) and questioned about Cryptocat's censorship resistance. He tweeted about the incident afterwards, resulting in media coverage and a spike in the popularity of the software.[^o5]

In June 2013, security researcher Steve Thomas pointed out a security bug that could be used to decrypt any group chat message that had taken place using Cryptocat between September 2012 and 19 April 2013.[^o6][^o7] Private messages were not affected, and the bug had been resolved a month before. In response, Cryptocat issued a security advisory, requested that all users ensure that they had upgraded, and informed users that past group conversations may have been compromised.[^o7]

In February 2014, an audit by iSec Partners criticized Cryptocat's authentication model as insufficient.[^o8] In response, Cryptocat made improvements to user authentication, making it easier for users to authenticate and detect [man-in-the-middle attacks](wikipedia://en/144676).[^o9]

In February 2016, citing dissatisfaction with the project's current state after 19 months of non-maintenance, Kobeissi announced that he would be taking Cryptocat temporarily offline and discontinuing the development of its mobile application, pending a complete rewrite and relaunch of the software.[^o10] In March 2016 Kobeissi announced the re-release of Cryptocat, rewritten completely as desktop software instead of the original web application software, as a public beta and the resumption of the service.[^o11] The new desktop-centric approach allowed Cryptocat to benefit from stronger desktop integration, in a style similar to [Pidgin](wikipedia://en/188211).

In February 2019, it was announced that Cryptocat would be discontinued.[^o12] As of December 2019, the cryptocat domain is for sale and links to the site for the [Wire messenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_%28software%29).[^o13]

## Features {#Features}

Cryptocat allows its users to set up [end-to-end encrypted](wikipedia://en/1065362) chat conversations. Users can exchange one-to-one messages, encrypted files, photos as well as create and share audio/video recordings. All devices linked to Cryptocat accounts will receive [forward secure](wikipedia://en/2191130) messages, even when offline.

All messages, files and audio/video recordings sent over Cryptocat are end-to-end encrypted. Cryptocat users link their devices to their Cryptocat account upon connection, and can identify each other's devices via the client's device manager in order to prevent [man-in-the-middle attacks](wikipedia://en/144676). Cryptocat also employs a [Trust on first use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_on_first_use) mechanism in order to help detect device identity key changes.

Cryptocat also includes a built-in auto-update mechanism that automatically performs a signature check on downloaded updates in order to verify authenticity, and employs TLS [certificate pinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certificate_pinning) in order to prevent network impersonation attacks.

Originally in 2013, Cryptocat offered the ability to connect to Facebook Messenger to initiate encrypted chatting with other Cryptocat users.[^o15] According to the developers, the feature was meant to help offer an alternative to the regular Cryptocat chat model which did not offer long-term contact lists.[^o16] This feature was disconnected in November 2015.

## Reception and usage {#Reception_and_usage}

In June 2013, Cryptocat was used by journalist [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald) while in [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong) to meet [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) whistleblower [Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) for the first time, after other encryption software failed to work.[^o18]

In November 2013, Cryptocat was banned in [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran), shortly after the election of Iran's new president [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani) who had promised more open Internet laws.[^o19]

Cryptocat was listed on the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation)'s "Secure Messaging Scorecard" from 4 November 2014 until 13 March 2016. During that time, Cryptocat had a score of 7 out of 7 points on the scorecard. It had received points for having communications encrypted in transit, having communications encrypted with keys the provider did not have access to ([end-to-end encryption](wikipedia://en/1065362)), making it possible for users to independently verify their correspondent's identities, having past communications secure if the keys were stolen ([forward secrecy](wikipedia://en/2191130)), having its code open to independent review ([open-source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software)), having its security designs well-documented, and having completed an independent security audit.[^o20]

## Architecture {#Architecture}

### Encryption {#Encryption}

Cryptocat uses a [Double Ratchet Algorithm](wikipedia://en/49111333) in order to obtain [forward](wikipedia://en/2191130) and future secrecy across messages, after a session is established using a four-way [Elliptic-curve Diffie–Hellman](wikipedia://en/3357958) handshake. The handshake mixes in long-term identity keys, an intermediate-term signed pre-key, and a one-time use pre-key.[^o23] The approach is similar to the encryption protocol adopted for encrypted messaging by the [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_%28messaging_app%29) mobile application. Cryptocat's goal is for its messages to obtain confidentiality, integrity, source authenticity, forward and future secrecy and indistinguishability even over a network controlled by an active attacker.[^o24] The forward secrecy features of the protocol that Cryptocat uses are similar to those first introduced by [Off-the-Record Messaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-the-Record_Messaging).

Cryptocat uses the [Advanced Encryption Standard](wikipedia://en/1260) in [Galois/Counter Mode](wikipedia://en/5897139) for authenticated encryption, [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444) for Elliptic curve Diffie-Hellman shared secret agreement, [HMAC-SHA256](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMAC-SHA256) for key derivation and [Ed25519](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed25519) for signing.[^o25][^o26] In order to limit the effect of a long-term identity key compromise, long-term keys are used exclusively once for the initial [Authenticated Key Exchange](wikipedia://en/36103897), and once for signing a newly generated intermediate-term signed pre-key.

For the transport layer, Cryptocat adopts the [OMEMO Multi-End Message and Object Encryption](wikipedia://en/48005845) standard, which also gives Cryptocat multi-device support and allows for offline messaging.[^o24]

### Network {#Network}

Cryptocat's network relies on a [XMPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XMPP) configuration served over [WebSockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebSockets). According to the project's mission statement, Cryptocat's network only relays encrypted messages and does not store any data.[^o28] In addition to the Cryptocat client's end-to-end encryption protocol, client-server communication is protected by [TLS](wikipedia://en/187813).

#### Distribution {#Distribution}

From March 2011 until March 2016, Cryptocat was officially distributed through the [Google Chrome Web Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Chrome_Web_Store), the [Apple App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_App_Store) and other official channels controlled by targeted platforms. After Cryptocat's re-write into desktop software in March 2016, the software became distributed exclusively through Cryptocat's own servers, which also handle signed update delivery.[^o30]
