# James Colliander

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="James Colliander"}

**James Ellis Colliander** (born 22 June 1967) is an American-[Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He is currently Professor of Mathematics at [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) and served as Director of the [Pacific Institute for the Mathematical Sciences](wikipedia://en/34978739) (PIMS) during 2016-2021. He was born in [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas), and lived there until age 8 and then moved to [Hastings, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings,_Minnesota). He graduated from [Macalester College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macalester_College) in 1989. He worked for two years at the [United States Naval Research Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Research_Laboratory) on [fiber optic sensors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber_optic_sensor) and then went to graduate school to study mathematics. He received his PhD from the [University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Urbana–Champaign) in 1997 and was advised by [Jean Bourgain](wikipedia://en/1024131). Colliander was a postdoctoral fellow at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and spent semesters at the University of Chicago, the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) and the [Mathematical Sciences Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).

:::

## Research {#Research}

Colliander's research mostly addresses dynamical aspects of solutions of Hamiltonian [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), especially [non-linear Schrödinger equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_Schrödinger_equation).[^o4]

Colliander is a collaborator with Markus Keel, [Gigliola Staffilani](wikipedia://en/44916025), Hideo Takaoka, and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498), forming a group known as the "I-team".[^o5][^o6] The name of this group has been said to come from a mollification [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) used in the team's *method of almost conserved quantities*,[^o7] or as an abbreviation for "interaction", referring both to the teamwork of the group and to the interactions of light waves with each other.[^o8] The group's work was featured in the 2006 [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) citations for group member Tao.[^o6][^o8]

## Organization creation {#Organization_creation}

Colliander is co-founder of the education technology company called [Crowdmark](https://crowdmark.com).

Colliander, with colleagues from PIMS, created [Syzygy](https://syzygy.ca/), a project that provides interactive computing for students and teachers at universities across Canada. Syzygy operates on infrastructure provided by Compute Canada.

Colliander, with colleagues from PIMS and [Cybera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cybera), created [Callysto](https://callysto.ca), a project designed to improve computational thinking for students and teachers in grades 5-12.

Colliander is co-founder of the [International Interactive Computing Collaboration (2i2c)](https://2i2c.org).
