# R. H. Jude

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="R. H. Jude"}

**Richard Henry Jude** (1853 – 1 June 1924) was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), physicist and [animal rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_rights) campaigner.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Jude was born in [Camberwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camberwell) and was educated at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London) where he won a mathematics scholarship.[^o4] In 1872 he received honours from [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) and qualified B.Sc with first-class honours in mathematics.[^o4] In 1878, he obtained a [Doctor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science) degree in organic chemistry from the University of London and in 1880 proceeded to a [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o4][^o5]

He was appointed lecturer in mathematics and science at [Rutherford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria_University) in 1882 where he remained until his retirement in 1917.[^o4] He was Head of the Physical and Mathematical Departments.[^o6] As a teacher he was credited with playing a prominent role in the higher education of young [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer).[^o7] He authored four physics textbooks which were translated into Russian and invented new physics apparatus which were patented.[^o4][^o8]

## Animal rights {#Animal_rights}

Jude was an advocate of animal rights and served on the provisional committee of the [Humanitarian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_League) in 1891.[^o10] In 1891, Jude authored a paper for the National Cat Club on "Feline Intelligence".[^o11] In 1892, he authored a pamphlet for the Humanitarian League criticizing the cruelty of [rabbit coursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit_coursing).[^o12][^o13] Jude wrote that the rabbits "often half-starved, and with their legs broken, are huddled together in a sack, with no more consideration than if they were so many bundles of wood" and how they were turned loose to be chased by greyhounds and other dogs to be bitten to death or [disembowelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disembowelment).[^o14]

Jude was an honorary correspondent of the [Victoria Street Society for the Protection of Animals from Vivisection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Street_Society_for_the_Protection_of_Animals_from_Vivisection).[^o15] He was a speaker at the 1892 annual meeting of the [London Anti-Vivisection Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Anti-Vivisection_Society).[^o16] In 1897, he contributed an article to [Henry S. Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_S._Salt)'s *Cruelties of Civilization: A Programme of Humane Reform*.[^o17]

## Death {#Death}

Jude towards the end of his life had [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes) and was deaf and blind.[^o7] He died at [Eltham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eltham) on 1 June 1924, aged 71.[^o7][^o19] He was buried at Borough Green Baptist Cemetery.[^o20] In spite of his ill health in his last years, an obituary in the *[Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_%28journal%29)* journal noted that he "retained to the end a brave fortitude in his outlook, an active and a clear mentality, and his letters rarely lacked in that spark of dry humour so characteristic of him in earlier years".[^o7]
