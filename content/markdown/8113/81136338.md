# Herman Rubin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Herman Rubin"}

**Herman Rubin** (1926–2018) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), professor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University). He died in 2018 at the age of 91.[^o3][^o4]

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Born in 1926 in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), Rubin received a high school diploma in at the age of 16 in 1943, and earned his undergraduate and graduate degrees from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), completing his doctorate in mathematics at the age of 21 in 1948; his official advisor was [Paul Halmos](wikipedia://en/318598). He served in the U.S. Army during the Second World War.[^o6] Prior to joining Purdue University in 1967, Rubin held faculty positions at several other prestigious institutions (Stanford University,[^o7] the University of Oregon, and Michigan State University).

## Contributions to statistics {#Contributions_to_statistics}

Rubin was considered a [polymath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath) with a unique ability to solve complex problems. He was a prolific researcher, publishing over 130 papers that have become standard texts in various fields. His key contributions include:

- [Multivariate Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_Analysis): He co-authored seminal papers with [T.W. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.W._Anderson) on the distribution theory of [maximum likelihood estimators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood_estimator) in [factor analysis](wikipedia://en/253492) and [structural equation models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_equation_model).
- [Monotone likelihood ratio](wikipedia://en/7615996): He is credited with the widely used and fundamental concept of monotone likelihood ratio families.
- [Bayesian Inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_Inference): Rubin was a lifelong [Bayesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian) statistician who took the theory and axioms of [Leonard Jimmie Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Jimmie_Savage) literally, and he contributed significantly to the theory of [Bayes risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayes_risk).
- [Econometrics](wikipedia://en/10390): He worked on problems related to the [simultaneous equations model](wikipedia://en/250436) in economics, including the development of the [Limited information maximum likelihood](wikipedia://en/250436#Limited_information_maximum_likelihood_%28LIML%29) (LIML) estimator.
- [Set Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Set_Theory) and Probability: His work also included contributions to set theory, the [theory of moderate deviations](wikipedia://en/3772909#Moderate_deviations_for_sums_of_independent_random_variables), and the characterization of probability distributions.
- [Hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing): in collaboration with [Samuel Karlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Karlin), the [Karlin-Rubin theorem](wikipedia://en/15433591#The_Karlin%E2%80%93Rubin_theorem) provides a method for finding uniformly most powerful tests for one-sided hypotheses, a significant contribution to the theory of hypothesis testing.

He was an inaugural Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) and a Fellow of the [Institute of Mathematical Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Mathematical_Statistics).

## Family {#Family}

He was married to [Jean E. Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_E._Rubin) (1926–2002), a professor of mathematics at Purdue University whose primary research focus was on the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).[^o10] His son [Arthur Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Rubin) (born 1956) is a mathematician and aerospace engineer.

## Legacy {#Legacy}

A memorial lecture series, the Herman Rubin Memorial Lecture, was established at Purdue University in his honor.[^o12]
