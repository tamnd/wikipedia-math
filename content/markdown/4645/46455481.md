# John Lewis Tiarks

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="John Lewis Tiarks"}

**John Lewis Tiarks**, FRS (born Johann Ludwig Tiarks, 10 May 1789 – 1 May 1837), **Johann Ludwig Tiarks**, was a German mathematician who became the British astronomer to the [North American Boundary Line Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada–United_States_border).[^o4][^o2]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

He was born in Waddewarden, [Wangerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wangerland), [Friesland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friesland_%28district%29), [Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Saxony) on 10 May 1789.[^o4] His father was Reverend Johann Gerhard Tiarks and his mother was Christine Dorothea Ehrentraut.[^o4]

He entered the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) to study theology but transferred to the mathematics course. He took a position as a private [tutor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutor).[^o4] He received a doctorate in mathematics in 1808.[^o4] Having gained his degree he initially moved to [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg). He fled to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England) in 1810 to avoid [conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscription) by [Napoleon](wikipedia://en/69880).[^o4] He became assistant librarian and factotum to [Joseph Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Banks) at the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society).[^o4]

## First visit to North America {#First_visit_to_North_America}

In 1817, on Banks’ recommendation, he was appointed an astronomer to one of the commissions established under the [Treaty of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ghent).[^o4] He acted for [Joseph Bouchette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bouchette) who fell ill that year.[^o4] He remained with the commission after Bouchette was replaced with [William Franklin Odell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Franklin_Odell).[^o4] In September 1817 he arrived in [Akwesasne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwesasne) to make astronomical observations.[^o4] He befriended many of the local [Mohawk people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohawk_people) and their priest, Father Joseph Marcoux.[^o4]

At the end of July 1818 Tiarks and his group left Akwesasne and with the American surveying team headed to [Lake Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Champlain).[^o4] [Ferdinand Rudolph Hassler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Rudolph_Hassler) headed the American team.[^o4] At the lake both teams independently discovered that [John Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Collins_%28merchant%29) and others had incorrectly located the [45th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_parallel_north), placing the fort at [Rouses Point, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouses_Point,_New_York) in British territory.[^o4] The American members of the boundary commission refused to accept the findings.[^o4] Hassler had been replaced as astronomer by [Andrew Ellicott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Ellicott), who was far less cooperative.[^o4] Ellicott was reported as saying to Tiarks "we should not do our work so exactingly and forget about completing most of it".[^o4] Tiarks refused to go along with the suggestion, as he felt it put his reputation on the line.[^o4] He was strongly supported in this by British Commissioner [Thomas Henry Barclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Henry_Barclay).[^o4]

In 1819 he was working in the upper [Connecticut River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_River) basin where part of the boundary was to run.[^o4] During summer 1820 he was working in what became northwestern [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick) and nearby areas of [Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec), returning to the upper Connecticut that fall.[^o4] Disputes between British and American commissioners brought his work to a halt and he returned to Europe in 1821.[^o4]

## Europe {#Europe}

Due to delays in proceeding with the border project, the [British Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty) asked him in the meantime to determine the longitude of various European locations, such as Madeira, with the help of chronometers, for which the *[SS Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Comet_%281822%29)* had been specially outfitted. He was joined from 30 June to 17 August 1824 by [Humphry Davy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphry_Davy) who used the voyage to [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) to test his [zinc protectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathodic_protection) for ships' bottoms.[^o7] In 1825 Tiarks was elected a [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society) as "a person well versed in mathematics and nautical astronomy".[^o8]

## Second visit to North America {#Second_visit_to_North_America}

He returned to North America in 1825 to determine the northwestermost point of the [Lake of the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Woods), from which the boundary was supposed to run due west to the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River).[^o4]

From 1826 to 1830 he worked on matters of concern to the boundary commission.[^o4] He was called to [The Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hague) to explain certain points to [William II of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II_of_the_Netherlands), who had been asked to arbitrate the boundary question.[^o4] Tiarks returned to Jeven.[^o4]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

In 1822 he married Auguste Antoinette Sophie Toel of [Jever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jever).[^o4] They had several children, but only one daughter survived to adulthood.[^o4]

## Final years and death {#Final_years_and_death}

He long wished to be a professor in a German university, but no position ever became available. In March 1837 he suffered from a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke) from which he never recovered.[^o4]

The [Webster–Ashburton Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster–Ashburton_Treaty), which settled the boundary disputes in question, was signed in 1842.[^o4]
