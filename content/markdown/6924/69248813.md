# Danish Sugar Beet Auction

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Danish Sugar Beet Auction"}

The **[Danish Sugar Beet Auction](https://www.partisia.com/docs/worlds-first-commercial-use-of-multi-party-computation-mpc/)** was the first large-scale and practical application of [secure multi-party computation](wikipedia://en/646233), which took place in January 2008.[^o2][^o3][^o4] An electronic [double auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_auction) was successfully run by a multiparty computation involving representatives of [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark)'s only [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet) processor ([Danisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danisco)), the Danish sugar beet growers' association, and a research group responsible for implementing and running the computation.[^o3]

:::

Due to [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union) sugar market policy reforms which reduced sugar [subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidy) and lowered prices,[^o5] as well as the recent closure of one of Danisco's sugar processing plants, the Danish [sugar beet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet) industry needed to reallocate production contracts to farmers nation-wide in an attempt to retain market efficiency. The decision made was to hold an electronic [double auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_auction) to find the new [market clearing price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_clearing) of sugar beets, where the role of the "auctioneer" was played by a computer program implementing a [secure multi-party computation](wikipedia://en/646233) (SMPC) between the farmers and Danisco.

The use of SMPC not only reduced expenses of the [auction process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction_process) (when compared to hiring an external consultancy to run the auction), but also allowed farmers' bids to remain private from [Danisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danisco), the only sugar beets processor on the Danish market and the seller of production contracts.[^o2] This was important as farmers' bids can reveal their individual economic positions and productivities, which Danisco could have hypothetically used to their advantage when selling contracts. In a survey of the auction participants, 80% of respondents indicated that it was important to them that the bids were kept confidential.[^o2]

Aside from organizing the logistics of such a novel auction, the actual computation of the market clearing price and each bidder's positions only took about 30 minutes to complete.[^o2] Ultimately, the auction resulted in the transfer of 25 thousand tons of production rights between farmers.[^o2]
