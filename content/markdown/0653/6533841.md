# Non-associative algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Non-associative algebra"}

A **non-associative algebra**[^n1] (or **distributive algebra**) is an [algebra over a field](wikipedia://en/191788) where the [binary multiplication operation](wikipedia://en/3948) is not assumed to be [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_operation). That is, an [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) *A* is a non-associative algebra over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* if it is a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over *K* and is equipped with a *K*-[bilinear](wikipedia://en/4365) binary multiplication operation *A* × *A* → *A* which may or may not be associative. Examples include [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371), the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436), and three-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with the [cross product](wikipedia://en/157092) operation. Since it is not assumed that the multiplication is associative, using parentheses to indicate the order of multiplications is necessary. For example, the expressions (*ab*)(*cd*), (*a*(*bc*))*d* and *a*(*b*(*cd*)) may all yield different answers.

:::

While this use of *non-associative* means that associativity is not assumed, it does not mean that associativity is disallowed. In other words, "non-associative" means "not necessarily associative", just as "noncommutative" means "not necessarily commutative" for [noncommutative rings](wikipedia://en/24062516).

An algebra is *[unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra)* or *unitary* if it has an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) *e* with *ex* = *x* = *xe* for all *x* in the algebra. For example, the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) are unital, but [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) never are.

The nonassociative algebra structure of *A* may be studied by associating it with other associative algebras which are subalgebras of the full algebra of *K*-[endomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endomorphisms) of *A* as a *K*-vector space. Two such are the **[derivation algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_algebra)** and the **(associative) enveloping algebra**, the latter being in a sense "the smallest associative algebra containing *A*".

More generally, some authors consider the concept of a non-associative algebra over a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) *R*: An [*R*-module](wikipedia://en/276410) equipped with an *R*-bilinear binary multiplication operation.[^n2] If a structure obeys all of the ring axioms apart from associativity (for example, any *R*-algebra), then it is naturally a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra, so some authors refer to non-associative $\mathbb{Z}$-algebras as **non-associative rings**.

## Algebras satisfying identities {#Algebras_satisfying_identities}

Ring-like structures with two binary operations and no other restrictions are a broad class, one which is too general to study. For this reason, the best-known kinds of non-associative algebras satisfy [identities](wikipedia://en/298428), or properties, which simplify multiplication somewhat. These include the following ones.

::: {#o6 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Power commutativity"}

### Usual properties {#Usual_properties}

Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ denote arbitrary elements of the algebra $A$ over the field $K$. Let powers to positive (non-zero) integer be recursively defined by $x^{1} \text{≝} x$ and either $x^{n+1} \text{≝} x^{n}x$[^n3] (right powers) or $x^{n+1} \text{≝} xx^{n}$[^n4][^n5] (left powers) depending on authors.

- [Unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra): there exist an element $e$ so that $ex = x = xe$; in that case we can define $x^{0} \text{≝} e$.
- [Associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associativity): $(xy)z = x(yz)$.
- [Commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutativity): $xy = yx$.
- [Anticommutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticommutative):[^n6] $xy = -yx$.
- [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370):[^n6][^n7] $(xy)z + (yz)x + (zx)y = 0$ or $x(yz) + y(zx) + z(xy) = 0$ depending on authors, but these are equivalent for anticommutative algebras, and [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebras) are anticommutative algebras obeying the Jacobi identity.
- [Jordan identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_identity):[^n8][^n9] $(x^{2}y)x = x^{2}(yx)$ or $(xy)x^{2} = x(yx^{2})$ depending on authors, but these are equivalent for commutative algebras, and [Jordan algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_algebras) are commutative algebras obeying the Jordan identity.
- [Alternative](wikipedia://en/3160):[^n10][^n11][^n12] $(xx)y = x(xy)$ (left alternative) and $(yx)x = y(xx)$ (right alternative).
- [Flexible](wikipedia://en/36035264):[^n13][^n14] $(xy)x = x(yx)$.
- $n$th power associative with $n \ge 2$: $x^{n-k}x^{k} = x^{n}$ for all integers $k$ so that $0 < k < n$.
  - Third power associative: $x^{2}x = xx^{2}$.
  - Fourth power associative: $x^{3}x = x^{2}x^{2} = xx^{3}$ (compare with *fourth power commutative* below).
- [Power associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_associative):[^n4][^n5][^n15][^n16][^n3] the subalgebra generated by any element is associative, i.e., *$n$th power associative* for all $n \ge 2$.
- $n$th power commutative with $n \ge 2$: $x^{n-k}x^{k} = x^{k}x^{n-k}$ for all integers $k$ so that $0 < k < n$.
  - Third power commutative: $x^{2}x = xx^{2}$.
  - Fourth power commutative: $x^{3}x = xx^{3}$ (compare with *fourth power associative* above).
- Power commutative: the subalgebra generated by any element is commutative, i.e., *$n$th power commutative* for all $n \ge 2$.
- [Nilpotent](wikipedia://en/42046875) of index $n \ge 2$: the product of any $n$ elements, in any association, vanishes, but not for some $n-1$ elements: $x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{n} = 0$ and there exist $n-1$ elements so that $y_{1}y_{2}\ldots y_{n-1} \ne 0$ for a specific association.
- [Nil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_algebra) of index $n \ge 2$: *power associative* and $x^{n} = 0$ and there exist an element $y$ so that $y^{n-1} \ne 0$.

:::

### Relations between properties {#Relations_between_properties}

For $K$ of any [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621):

- *Associative* implies *alternative*.
- Any two out of the three properties *left alternative*, *right alternative*, and *flexible*, imply the third one.
  - Thus, *alternative* implies *flexible*.
- *Alternative* implies *Jordan identity*.[^n17]
- *Commutative* implies *flexible*.
- *Anticommutative* implies *flexible*.
- *Alternative* implies *power associative*.
- *Flexible* implies *third power associative*.
- *Second power associative* and *second power commutative* are always true.
- *Third power associative* and *third power commutative* are equivalent.
- *$n$th power associative* implies *$n$th power commutative*.
- *Nil of index 2* implies *anticommutative*.
- *Nil of index 2* implies *Jordan identity*.
- *Nilpotent of index 3* implies *Jacobi identity*.
- *Nilpotent of index $n$* implies *nil of index $N$* with $2 \le N \le n$.
- *Unital* and *nil of index $n$* are incompatible.

If $K \ne \operatorname{GF}(2)$ or $\dim(A) \le 3$:

- *Jordan identity* and *commutative* together imply *power associative*.[^n18][^n19][^n20]

If $\operatorname{char}(K) \ne 2$:

- *Right alternative* implies *power associative*.[^n21][^n22][^n23][^n24]
  - Similarly, *left alternative* implies *power associative*.
- *Unital* and *Jordan identity* together imply *flexible*.[^n25]
- *Jordan identity* and *flexible* together imply *power associative*.[^n26]
- *Commutative* and *anticommutative* together imply *nilpotent of index 2*.
- *Anticommutative* implies *nil of index 2*.
- *Unital* and *anticommutative* are incompatible.

If $\operatorname{char}(K) \ne 3$:

- *Unital* and *Jacobi identity* are incompatible.

If $\operatorname{char}(K) \notin \{2,3,5$}:

- *Commutative* and $x^{4} = x^{2}x^{2}$ (one of the two identities defining *fourth power associative*) together imply *power associative*.[^n27]

If $\operatorname{char}(K) = 0$:

- *Third power associative* and $x^{4} = x^{2}x^{2}$ (one of the two identities defining *fourth power associative*) together imply *power associative*.[^n27]

If $\operatorname{char}(K) = 2$:

- *Commutative* and *anticommutative* are equivalent.

### Associator {#Associator}

The **associator** on *A* is the *K*-[multilinear map](wikipedia://en/221525) $[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot] : A \times A \times A \to A$ given by

$$[x,y,z] = (xy)z - x(yz).$$

It measures the degree of nonassociativity of $A$, and can be used to conveniently express some possible identities satisfied by *A*.

Let $x$, $y$ and $z$ denote arbitrary elements of the algebra.

- Associative: $[x,y,z] = 0$.
- Alternative: $[x,x,y] = 0$ (left alternative) and $[y,x,x] = 0$ (right alternative).
  - It implies that permuting any two terms changes the sign: $[x,y,z] = -[x,z,y] = -[z,y,x] = -[y,x,z]$; the converse holds only if $\operatorname{char}(K) \ne 2$.
- Flexible: $[x,y,x] = 0$.
  - It implies that permuting the extremal terms changes the sign: $[x,y,z] = -[z,y,x]$; the converse holds only if $\operatorname{char}(K) \ne 2$.
- Jordan identity:[^n28] $[x^{2},y,x] = 0$ or $[x,y,x^{2}] = 0$ depending on authors.
- Third power associative: $[x,x,x] = 0$.

The **nucleus** is the set of elements that associate with all others:[^n29] that is, the $n$ in *A* such that

$$[n,A,A] = [A,n,A] = [A,A,n] = \{0.$$

The nucleus is an associative subring of *A*.

### Center {#Center}

The **center** of *A* is the set of elements that commute and associate with everything in *A*, that is the intersection of

$$C(A) = \{ n \in A \ | \ nr=rn \, \forall r \in A \, \}$$

with the nucleus. It turns out that for elements of *C(A)* it is enough that two of the sets $([n,A,A], [A,n,A] , [A,A,n])$ are $\{0\}$ for the third to also be the zero set.

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>3</sup> with multiplication given by the [vector cross product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_cross_product) is an example of an algebra which is anticommutative and not associative. The cross product also satisfies the Jacobi identity.
- [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944) are algebras satisfying anticommutativity and the Jacobi identity.
- Algebras of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) on a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219) (if *K* is **R** or the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) **C**) or an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) (for general *K*);
- [Jordan algebras](wikipedia://en/936371) are algebras which satisfy the commutative law and the Jordan identity.[^n9]
- Every associative algebra gives rise to a Lie algebra by using the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) as Lie bracket. In fact every Lie algebra can either be constructed this way, or is a subalgebra of a Lie algebra so constructed.
- Every associative algebra over a field of [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) other than 2 gives rise to a Jordan algebra by defining a new multiplication *x\*y* = (*xy*+*yx*)/2. In contrast to the Lie algebra case, not every Jordan algebra can be constructed this way. Those that can are called *special*.
- [Alternative algebras](wikipedia://en/3160) are algebras satisfying the alternative property. The most important examples of alternative algebras are the [octonions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octonions) (an algebra over the reals), and generalizations of the octonions over other fields, called [octonion algebras](wikipedia://en/10277277), such as the [bioctonions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioctonions). All associative algebras are alternative. Up to isomorphism, the only finite-dimensional real alternative, division algebras (see below) are the reals, complexes, quaternions and octonions.
- [Power-associative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-associative_algebra), are those algebras satisfying the power-associative identity. Examples include all associative algebras, all alternative algebras, Jordan algebras over a field other than [GF(2)](wikipedia://en/11137263) (see previous section), and the [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434).

More classes of algebras:

- [Graded algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra). These include most of the algebras of interest to [multilinear algebra](wikipedia://en/243946), such as the [tensor algebra](wikipedia://en/363628), [symmetric algebra](wikipedia://en/654098), and [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) over a given [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370). Graded algebras can be generalized to [filtered algebras](wikipedia://en/2954244).
- [Division algebras](wikipedia://en/26549625), in which multiplicative inverses exist. The finite-dimensional alternative division algebras over the field of real numbers have been classified. They are the [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) (dimension 1), the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) (dimension 2), the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) (dimension 4), and the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) (dimension 8). The quaternions and octonions are not commutative. Of these algebras, all are associative except for the octonions.
- [Quadratic algebras](wikipedia://en/23565253), which require that *xx* = *re* + *sx*, for some elements *r* and *s* in the [ground field](wikipedia://en/27161748), and *e* a unit for the algebra. Examples include all finite-dimensional alternative algebras, and the algebra of real 2-by-2 matrices. Up to isomorphism the only alternative, quadratic real algebras without divisors of zero are the reals, complexes, quaternions, and octonions.
- The [Cayley–Dickson algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley–Dickson_algebra) (where *K* is **R**), which begin with:
  - the [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) **C** (a commutative and associative algebra);
  - the [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) **H** (an associative algebra);
  - the [octonions](wikipedia://en/51436) **O** (an [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160));
  - the [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434) **S**;
  - the [trigintaduonions](wikipedia://en/25474499) **T** and the infinite sequence of Cayley-Dickson algebras ([power-associative algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-associative_algebra)).
- [Hypercomplex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercomplex_algebra) are all finite-dimensional unital **R**-algebras, they thus include Cayley-Dickson algebras and many more.
- The [Poisson algebras](wikipedia://en/345704) are considered in [geometric quantization](wikipedia://en/1146267). They carry two multiplications, turning them into commutative algebras and Lie algebras in different ways.
- [Genetic algebras](wikipedia://en/30181557) are non-associative algebras used in mathematical genetics.
- [Triple systems](wikipedia://en/19287276)

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

There are several properties that may be familiar from ring theory, or from associative algebras, which are not always true for non-associative algebras. Unlike the associative case, elements with a (two-sided) multiplicative inverse might also be a [zero divisor](wikipedia://en/51441). For example, all non-zero elements of the [sedenions](wikipedia://en/51434) have a two-sided inverse, but some of them are also zero divisors.

:::

::: {#o48 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Free non-associative algebra"}

## Free non-associative algebra {#Free_non-associative_algebra}

The **free non-associative algebra** on a set *X* over a field *K* is defined as the algebra with basis consisting of all non-associative monomials, finite formal products of elements of *X* retaining parentheses. The product of monomials *u*, *v* is just (*u*)(*v*). The algebra is unital if one takes the [empty product](wikipedia://en/181289) as a monomial.[^n30]

Kurosh [proved](wikipedia://en/82285) that every subalgebra of a free non-associative algebra is free.[^n31]

:::

## Associated algebras {#Associated_algebras}

An algebra *A* over a field *K* is in particular a *K*-vector space and so one can consider the associative algebra End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*) of *K*-linear vector space endomorphism of *A*. We can associate to the algebra structure on *A* two subalgebras of End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*), the **derivation algebra** and the **(associative) enveloping algebra**.

### Derivation algebra {#Derivation_algebra}

A *[derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29)* on *A* is a map *D* with the property

$$D(x \cdot y) = D(x) \cdot y + x \cdot D(y) \ .$$

The derivations on *A* form a subspace Der<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*) in End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*). The [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193#Ring_theory) of two derivations is again a derivation, so that the [Lie bracket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_bracket) gives Der<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*) a structure of [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944).[^n32]

::: {#o55 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Associative enveloping algebra"}

### Enveloping algebra {#Enveloping_algebra}

There are linear maps *L* and *R* attached to each element *a* of an algebra *A*:[^n33]

$$L(a) : x \mapsto ax ; \ \ R(a) : x \mapsto xa \ .$$

Here each element $L(a),R(a)$ is regarded as an element of End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*). The *associative enveloping algebra* or *multiplication algebra* of *A* is the sub-associative algebra of End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*) generated by the left and right linear maps $L(a),R(a)$.[^n28][^n34] The *centroid* of *A* is the centraliser of the enveloping algebra in the endomorphism algebra End<sub>*K*</sub>(*A*). An algebra is *central* if its centroid consists of the *K*-scalar multiples of the identity.[^n16]

Some of the possible identities satisfied by non-associative algebras may be conveniently expressed in terms of the linear maps:[^n35]

- Commutative: each *L*(*a*) is equal to the corresponding *R*(*a*);
- Associative: any *L* commutes with any *R*;
- Flexible: every *L*(*a*) commutes with the corresponding *R*(*a*);
- Jordan: every *L*(*a*) commutes with *R*(*a*<sup>2</sup>);
- Alternative: every *L*(*a*)<sup>2</sup> = *L*(*a*<sup>2</sup>) and similarly for the right.

The *quadratic representation* *Q* is defined by[^n36]

$$Q(a) : x \mapsto 2a \cdot (a \cdot x) - (a \cdot a) \cdot x \,$$

or equivalently,

$$Q(a) = 2 L^2(a) - L(a^2) \ .$$

The article on [universal enveloping algebras](wikipedia://en/363551) describes the canonical construction of enveloping algebras, as well as the PBW-type theorems for them. For Lie algebras, such enveloping algebras have a universal property, which does not hold, in general, for non-associative algebras. The best-known example is, perhaps the [Albert algebra](wikipedia://en/9153180), an exceptional [Jordan algebra](wikipedia://en/936371) that is not enveloped by the canonical construction of the enveloping algebra for Jordan algebras.

:::
