# Frattini's argument

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frattini's argument"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Frattini's argument** is an important [lemma](wikipedia://en/18634) in the structure theory of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707). It is named after [Giovanni Frattini](wikipedia://en/8606878), who used it in a paper from 1885 when defining the [Frattini subgroup](wikipedia://en/540707) of a group. The argument was taken by Frattini, as he himself admits, from a paper of [Alfredo Capelli](wikipedia://en/29028258) dated 1884.[^o2]

:::

## Frattini's argument {#Frattini's_argument}

::: {#o5 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

If $G$ is a finite group with normal subgroup $H$, and if $P$ is a [Sylow *p*-subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylow_p-subgroup) of $H$, then

$$G = N_G(P)H,$$

where $N_G(P)$ denotes the [normalizer](wikipedia://en/145661) of $P$ in $G$, and $N_G(P)H$ means the [product of group subsets](wikipedia://en/24734).

:::

::: {#o7 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The group $P$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$, so every Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$ is an $H$-conjugate of $P$, that is, it is of the form $h^{-1}Ph$ for some $h \in H$ (see [Sylow theorems](wikipedia://en/53993)). Let $g$ be any element of $G$. Since $H$ is normal in $G$, the subgroup $g^{-1}Pg$ is contained in $H$. This means that $g^{-1}Pg$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $H$. Then, by the above, it must be $H$-conjugate to $P$: that is, for some $h \in H$

$$g^{-1}Pg = h^{-1}Ph,$$

and so

$$hg^{-1}Pgh^{-1} = P.$$

Thus

$$gh^{-1} \in N_G(P),$$

and therefore $g \in N_G(P)H$. But $g \in G$ was arbitrary, and so $G = HN_G(P) = N_G(P)H.\ \square$

:::

::: {#o9 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- Frattini's argument can be used as part of a proof that any finite [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569) is a [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of its Sylow subgroups.
- By applying Frattini's argument to $N_G(N_G(P))$, it can be shown that $N_G(N_G(P)) = N_G(P)$ whenever $G$ is a finite group and $P$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.
- More generally, if a subgroup $M \leq G$ contains $N_G(P)$ for some Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $G$, then $M$ is self-normalizing, i.e. $M = N_G(M)$.

:::
