# Quasi-Hopf algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-Hopf algebra"}

A **quasi-Hopf algebra** is a generalization of a [Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/325714), which was defined by the [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) mathematician [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) in 1989.

:::

A *quasi-Hopf algebra* is a [quasi-bialgebra](wikipedia://en/4899414) $\mathcal{B_A} = (\mathcal{A}, \Delta, \varepsilon, \Phi)$ for which there exist $\alpha, \beta \in \mathcal{A}$ and a [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942) [antihomomorphism](wikipedia://en/706374) *S* ([antipode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipode_%28algebra%29)) of $\mathcal{A}$ such that

$$\sum_i S(b_i) \alpha c_i = \varepsilon(a) \alpha$$

$$\sum_i b_i \beta S(c_i) = \varepsilon(a) \beta$$

for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$ and where

$$\Delta(a) = \sum_i b_i \otimes c_i$$

and

$$\sum_i X_i \beta S(Y_i) \alpha Z_i = \mathbb{I},$$

$$\sum_j S(P_j) \alpha Q_j \beta S(R_j) = \mathbb{I}.$$

where the expansions for the quantities $\Phi$and $\Phi^{-1}$ are given by

$$\Phi = \sum_i X_i \otimes Y_i \otimes Z_i$$

and

$$\Phi^{-1}= \sum_j P_j \otimes Q_j \otimes R_j.$$

As for a [quasi-bialgebra](wikipedia://en/4899414), the property of being quasi-Hopf is preserved under [twisting](wikipedia://en/4899414#Twisting).

## Usage {#Usage}

Quasi-Hopf algebras form the basis of the study of [Drinfeld twists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinfeld_twist) and the representations in terms of [F-matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-matrix) associated with finite-dimensional irreducible [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [quantum affine algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_affine_algebras). F-matrices can be used to factorize the corresponding [R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996). This leads to applications in [Statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), as quantum affine algebras, and their representations give rise to solutions of the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810), a solvability condition for various statistical models, allowing characteristics of the model to be deduced from its corresponding quantum affine algebra. The study of F-matrices has been applied to models such as the [Heisenberg XXZ model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_XXZ_model) in the framework of the algebraic [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884). It provides a framework for solving two-dimensional [integrable models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_model) by using the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752).
