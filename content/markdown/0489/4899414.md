# Quasi-bialgebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-bialgebra"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **quasi-bialgebras** are a generalization of [bialgebras](wikipedia://en/310887): they were first defined by the [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine) mathematician [Vladimir Drinfeld](wikipedia://en/1053747) in 1990. A quasi-bialgebra differs from a [bialgebra](wikipedia://en/310887) by having [coassociativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coassociativity) replaced by an invertible element $\Phi$ which controls the non-[coassociativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coassociativity). One of their key properties is that the corresponding [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) forms a [tensor category](wikipedia://en/330604).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A quasi-bialgebra $\mathcal{B_A} = (\mathcal{A}, \Delta, \varepsilon, \Phi,l,r)$ is an [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) $\mathcal{A}$ over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $\mathbb{F}$ equipped with morphisms of algebras

$$\Delta : \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \mathcal{A \otimes A}$$

$$\varepsilon : \mathcal{A} \rightarrow \mathbb{F}$$

along with invertible elements $\Phi \in \mathcal{A \otimes A \otimes A}$, and $r,l \in A$ such that the following identities hold:

$$(id \otimes \Delta) \circ \Delta(a) = \Phi \lbrack (\Delta \otimes id) \circ \Delta (a) \rbrack \Phi^{-1}, \quad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}$$

$$\lbrack (id \otimes id \otimes \Delta)(\Phi) \rbrack \ \lbrack (\Delta \otimes id \otimes id)(\Phi) \rbrack = (1 \otimes \Phi) \ \lbrack (id \otimes \Delta \otimes id)(\Phi) \rbrack \ (\Phi \otimes 1)$$

$$(\varepsilon \otimes id)(\Delta a) = l^{-1} a l, \qquad (id \otimes \varepsilon) \circ \Delta = r^{-1} a r, \quad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}$$

$$(id \otimes \varepsilon \otimes id)(\Phi) = r \otimes l^{-1}.$$

Where $\Delta$ and $\epsilon$ are called the comultiplication and counit, $r$ and $l$ are called the right and left unit constraints (resp.), and $\Phi$ is sometimes called the *Drinfeld associator*.[^o4] This definition is constructed so that the category $\mathcal{A}-Mod$ is a [tensor category](wikipedia://en/330604) under the usual [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) tensor product, and in fact this can be taken as the definition instead of the list of above identities.[^o4] Since many of the quasi-bialgebras that appear "in nature" have trivial unit constraints, ie. $l=r=1$ the definition may sometimes be given with this assumed.[^o4] Note that a [bialgebra](wikipedia://en/310887) is just a quasi-bialgebra with trivial unit and associativity constraints: $l=r=1$ and $\Phi=1 \otimes 1 \otimes 1$.

:::

## Braided quasi-bialgebras {#Braided_quasi-bialgebras}

A *braided quasi-bialgebra* (also called a *quasi-triangular quasi-bialgebra*) is a quasi-bialgebra whose corresponding tensor category $\mathcal{A}-Mod$ is [braided](wikipedia://en/1470466). Equivalently, by analogy with [braided bialgebras](wikipedia://en/3727020), we can construct a notion of a *universal R-matrix* which controls the non-[cocommutativity](wikipedia://en/310886) of a quasi-bialgebra. The definition is the same as in the [braided bialgebra](wikipedia://en/3727020) case except for additional complications in the formulas caused by adding in the associator.

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="Proposition"}

**Proposition:** A quasi-bialgebra $(\mathcal{A},\Delta,\epsilon,\Phi,l,r)$ is braided if it has a *universal R-matrix*, ie an invertible element $R \in \mathcal{A \otimes A}$ such that the following 3 identities hold:

$$(\Delta^{op})(a)=R \Delta(a) R^{-1}$$

$$(id \otimes \Delta)(R)=(\Phi_{231})^{-1} R_{13} \Phi_{213} R_{12} (\Phi_{213})^{-1}$$

$$(\Delta \otimes id)(R)=(\Phi_{321}) R_{13} (\Phi_{213})^{-1} R_{23} \Phi_{123}$$

Where, for every $a_1 \otimes ... \otimes a_k \in \mathcal{A}^{\otimes k}$, $a_{i_1 i_2 ... i_n}$ is the monomial with $a_j$ in the $i_j$th spot, where any omitted numbers correspond to the identity in that spot. Finally we extend this by linearity to all of $\mathcal{A}^{\otimes k}$.[^o4]

Again, similar to the [braided bialgebra](wikipedia://en/3727020) case, this universal R-matrix satisfies (a non-associative version of) the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810):

$R_{12}\Phi_{321}R_{13}(\Phi_{132})^{-1}R_{23}\Phi_{123}=\Phi_{321}R_{23}(\Phi_{231})^{-1}R_{13}\Phi_{213}R_{12}$[^o4]

:::

## Twisting {#Twisting}

Given a quasi-bialgebra, further quasi-bialgebras can be generated by twisting (from now on we will assume $r=l=1$) .

If $\mathcal{B_A}$ is a quasi-bialgebra and $F \in \mathcal{A \otimes A}$ is an invertible element such that $(\varepsilon \otimes id) F = (id \otimes \varepsilon) F = 1$, set

$$\Delta ' (a) = F \Delta (a) F^{-1}, \quad \forall a \in \mathcal{A}$$

$$\Phi ' = (1 \otimes F) \ ((id \otimes \Delta) F) \ \Phi \ ((\Delta \otimes id)F^{-1}) \ (F^{-1} \otimes 1).$$

Then, the set $(\mathcal{A}, \Delta ' , \varepsilon, \Phi ')$ is also a quasi-bialgebra obtained by twisting $\mathcal{B_A}$ by *F*, which is called a *twist* or *gauge transformation*.[^o4] If $(\mathcal{A},\Delta,\varepsilon, \Phi)$ was a braided quasi-bialgebra with universal R-matrix $R$ , then so is $(\mathcal{A},\Delta',\varepsilon, \Phi ')$ with universal R-matrix $F_{21}RF^{-1}$ (using the notation from the above section).[^o4] However, the twist of a bialgebra is only in general a quasi-bialgebra. Twistings fulfill many expected properties. For example, twisting by $F_1$ and then $F_2$ is equivalent to twisting by $F_2F_1$, and twisting by $F$ then $F^{-1}$ recovers the original quasi-bialgebra.

Twistings have the important property that they induce categorical equivalences on the tensor category of modules:

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=inline-head name="Theorem"}

**Theorem:** Let $\mathcal{B_A}$, $\mathcal{B_{A'}}$ be quasi-bialgebras, let $\mathcal{B'_{A'}}$ be the twisting of $\mathcal{B_{A'}}$ by $F$, and let there exist an isomorphism: $\alpha:\mathcal{B_A} \to \mathcal{B'_{A'}}$. Then the induced tensor functor $(\alpha^*,id,\phi_2^F)$ is a tensor category equivalence between $\mathcal{A'}-mod$ and $\mathcal{A}-mod$. Where $\phi_2^F(v \otimes w)=F^{-1}(v \otimes w)$. Moreover, if $\alpha$ is an isomorphism of braided quasi-bialgebras, then the above induced functor is a braided tensor category equivalence.[^o4]

:::

## Usage {#Usage}

Quasi-bialgebras form the basis of the study of [quasi-Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/4899676) and further to the study of [Drinfeld twists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinfeld_twist) and the representations in terms of [F-matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-matrix) associated with finite-dimensional irreducible [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [quantum affine algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_affine_algebras). F-matrices can be used to factorize the corresponding [R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996). This leads to applications in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481), as quantum affine algebras, and their representations give rise to solutions of the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810), a solvability condition for various statistical models, allowing characteristics of the model to be deduced from its corresponding quantum affine algebra. The study of F-matrices has been applied to models such as the [XXZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_model_%28quantum%29) in the framework of the Algebraic [Bethe ansatz](wikipedia://en/4327884).
