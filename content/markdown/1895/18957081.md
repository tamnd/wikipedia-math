# Joachim Jungius

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joachim Jungius"}

*Image: Joachim jungius 1587-1657 closeup.png.* Joachim Jungius (1587–1657)

**Joachim Jungius** (born **Joachim Junge**; 22 October 1587 – 23 September 1657) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician) and [philosopher of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher_of_science).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Jungius was a native of [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lübeck). He studied [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) at the Universities of [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rostock) and [Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Giessen), where in 1608 he earned his degree.

Beginning in 1609, he was a professor of mathematics at the University of [Giessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giessen), and in 1614–15, with [Wolfgang Ratke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Ratke) (1571–1635) and [Christoph Helvig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Helvig) (1581–1617), he took part in studies of [educational reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_reform). In 1616, he returned to [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) in order to study medicine, later obtaining his medical doctorate from the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) with [Santorio Santorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorio_Santorio) in 1619. From 1619 to 1623, he practiced medicine in Lübeck. In 1622 at Rostock, he founded an early scientific society known as *Societas Ereunetica sive Zetetica*.

From 1624 to 1628, Jungius worked as a professor of mathematics at Rostock, his service here being briefly interrupted in 1625, when he spent time as professor of medicine at the [University of Helmstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helmstedt). From 1629 until 1657, he was professor of [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences) at the *Akademisches Gymnasium*, a secondary school in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg). Jungius believed that science was based on mathematics, and in Hamburg stressed the importance of [critical thinking](wikipedia://en/276872) to his students. He also felt that mathematics and [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065) served as a remedy to [metaphysical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and mystical speculation.

He died in Hamburg.

## Works {#Works}

Jungius was an important figure of 17th century [atomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomism), and was an advocate of a "[corpuscular chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpuscularianism)" that assumed the [conservation of mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_of_mass). He also demonstrated that a [catenary](wikipedia://en/7163) was not a [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231).

In 1638 he published the textbook *Logica Hamburgensis*, which presented late [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) theories and techniques of [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065). Here he demonstrated oblique cases of arguments that did not adhere to simpler forms of [inference](wikipedia://en/317465); An example being: "The square of an even number is even; 6 is even; therefore, the square of 6 is even". His double position as connected to scholastic logic, but also to innovations, earns him the classification semi-[Ramist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramist).[^o4]

[Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz](wikipedia://en/12281) wrote,

"While Jungius of Lübeck is a man little known even in Germany itself, he was clearly of such judiciousness and such capacity of mind that I know of no other mortal, including even [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descartes) himself, from whom we could better have expected a great restoration of the sciences, had Jungius been either known or assisted."[^o5]

Jung was a man of great intellect, he defined a plant as: "A plant is a living non-sentient body, attached to a particular place or habitat, where it is able to feed, to grow in size, and finally to propagate itself."[^o6]
