# Alan Baker (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alan Baker"}

**Alan Baker** FRS[^o3] (19 August 1939 – 4 February 2018)[^o4] was an English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his work on effective methods in number theory, in particular those arising from [transcendental number theory](wikipedia://en/1157698).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Alan Baker was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) family in London on 19 August 1939.[^o6] He attended [Stratford Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratford_School), East London, and his academic career started as a student of [Harold Davenport](wikipedia://en/991111), at [University College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_London) and later at [Trinity College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), where he received his PhD. He was a visiting scholar at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in 1970 when he was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the age of 31.[^o8] In 1974 he was appointed Professor of Pure Mathematics at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University), a position he held until 2006 when he became an [Emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emeritus). He was a fellow of Trinity College from 1964 until his death.[^o7]

His interests were in number theory, [transcendence](wikipedia://en/1157698), [linear forms in logarithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_forms_in_logarithms), [effective methods](wikipedia://en/250237), [Diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205) and [Diophantine analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diophantine_analysis).

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o9] He has also been made a foreign fellow of [the National Academy of Sciences, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_Academy_of_Sciences,_India).[^o10]

## Research {#Research}

Baker generalised the [Gelfond–Schneider theorem](wikipedia://en/609487), which itself is a solution to [Hilbert's seventh problem](wikipedia://en/392579).[^o12] Specifically, Baker showed that if $\alpha_1,...,\alpha_n$ are [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) (besides 0 or 1), and if $\beta_1,..,\beta_n$ are [irrational](wikipedia://en/20647689) algebraic numbers such that the set $\{1,\beta_1,...,\beta_n\}$ is [linearly independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_independent) over the rational numbers, then the number $\alpha_1^{\beta_1}\alpha_2^{\beta_2}\cdots\alpha_n^{\beta_n}$ is [transcendental](wikipedia://en/30325).

Baker made significant contributions to several areas in number theory, such as the Gauss [class number problem](wikipedia://en/596282),[^o13] diophantine approximation, and to Diophantine equations such as the [Mordell curve](wikipedia://en/3132697).[^o14][^o15]
