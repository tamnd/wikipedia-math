# Demographics of Hamburg

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Hamburg"}

950 The [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany) city of [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg) is [the most populous city in the European Union which is not a national capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_of_the_European_Union_by_population_within_city_limits). The city contains an approximate 1.88 million people.

:::

The figures since 1970 are published by the Statistical Office for Hamburg and Schleswig Holstein, based on the information of several state authorities.[^o4]

## Population {#Population}

On December 31, 2006 there were 1,754,182 registered people living in Hamburg (up from 1,652,363 in 1990). The population density was 2322 PD/sqkm.[^o4]

There were 856,132 males and 898,050 females in Hamburg. For every 1,000 males there were 1,049 females. In 2006 there were 16,089 births in Hamburg, of which 33.1% were given by unmarried women, 6,921 marriages and 4,583 divorces. In 2006, 198 [registered partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registered_partnership) took place at the civil registration office (*[Standesamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standesamt)*). 40 partnerships were dissolved by court order since 2001.[^o5] The age distribution was 15.7% under the age of 18, and 18.8% were 65 or older.[^o4] In 2006, there were 257,060 foreign residents were living in Hamburg (14.8% of the population). The largest group being Turkish nationals at 58,154 (22.6% of foreign residents ), followed by 20,743 Polish nationals. 4,046 people were from the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom) and 4,369 were from the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o4] According to [GTZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Gesellschaft_für_Technische_Zusammenarbeit), 22,000 immigrants living in Hamburg are from [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan), thus forming the largest Afghan community in Germany and Europe.[^o6]

## Households {#Households}

On 31 December 2016, there were 1,860,759 people registered as living in Hamburg in an area of \755.3 km2. The population density was 2464 PD/sqkm.[^o10] The metropolitan area of the Hamburg region ([Hamburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Metropolitan_Region)) is home to 5,107,429 living in an area of 26000 km2 at a density of 196 PD/sqkm.[^o11]

There were 915,319 women and 945,440 men in Hamburg. For every 1,000 males, there were 1,033 females. In 2015, there were 19,768 births in Hamburg (of which 38.3% were to unmarried women); 6422 marriages and 3190 divorces, and 17,565 deaths. The age distribution was 16.1% under the age of 18, and 18.3% were 65 or older. 356 People in Hamburg were over the age of 100.[^o12]

According to the Statistical Office of the State of Hamburg, the number of people with a migrant background is at 34% (631,246).[^o13] Immigrants come from 180 countries. 5891 people have acquired German citizenship in 2016.[^o14]

In 2016, there were 1,021,666 households, of which 17.8% had children under the age of 18; 54.4% of households were made up of singles. 25.6% of households with children were single parent households. The average household size was 1.8.[^o15]

## Ethnic groups {#Ethnic_groups}

In 2008 Hamburg had the highest [Afghan diasporic population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Germany) of any city in the continent, with 7,000 German citizens of Afghan origin and 14,000 other residents of Afghan origin. The city has therefore been nicknamed by some as *Little [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul)*.[^o17] Immigration began with the start of the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet–Afghan_War) in 1979 and additional immigration came after its end. Due to the differing origins and political affiliations of the emigrés, Jochen-Martin Dutsch et al. wrote in *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* that "Hamburg's Afghan community was relatively loose-knit and was rarely perceived as an ethnic group, partly because these immigrants had been so deeply divided at home that there was little left to unite them as a community abroad."[^o18] Therefore the residents focused internally on their own families and keeping them together.[^o18] [Afghan Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Museum) was in Hamburg.

In 1963 there were 800 Japanese people in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), including 50 children.[^o19] In 1985 the city had a Japanese community, though it was not the largest in Germany as by then [Düsseldorf had the largest one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_community_of_Düsseldorf).[^o20] [Japanese School in Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_School_in_Hamburg) is in nearby [Halstenbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halstenbek).
