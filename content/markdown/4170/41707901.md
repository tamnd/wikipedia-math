# Peter Keevash

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Peter Keevash"}

**Peter Keevash** (born 30 November 1978) is a British mathematician, working in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170). He is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) and a Fellow of [Mansfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_College,_Oxford).

:::

## Early years {#Early_years}

Keevash was born in [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton), England, but mostly grew up in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds). He competed in the [International Mathematical Olympiad](wikipedia://en/82208) in 1995. He entered [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge), [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), in 1995 and completed his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in mathematics in 1998. He earned his doctorate from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with [Benny Sudakov](wikipedia://en/35862803) as advisor.[^o7] He took a postdoctoral position at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) before moving to [Queen Mary, University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary,_University_of_London) as a lecturer, and subsequently professor, before his move to Oxford in September 2013.

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

Keevash has published many results in [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), particularly in extremal graph and hypergraph theory and [Ramsey Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_Theory). In joint work with Tom Bohman[^o9] he established the best-known lower bound for the off-diagonal [Ramsey Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_Number) $R(3,k)$, namely $R(3,k) \geq \left(\frac{1}{4} - o(1)\right)\frac{k^2}{\log k}.$ (This result was obtained independently at the same time by Fiz Pontiveros, Griffiths and Morris.[^o10])

On 15 January 2014, he released a preprint[^o11] establishing the existence of [block designs](wikipedia://en/1188184) with arbitrary parameters, provided only that the underlying set is sufficiently large and satisfies certain obviously necessary divisibility conditions. In particular, his work provides the first examples of [Steiner systems](wikipedia://en/28016) with parameter *t* ≥ 6 (and in fact provides such systems for all *t*).

In 2018, he was an [invited speaker](wikipedia://en/47738065#2018,_Rio_de_Janeiro) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro).
