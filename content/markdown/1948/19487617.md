# Obstacle problem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Obstacle problem"}

The **obstacle problem** is a classic motivating example in the [mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical) study of [variational inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_inequalities) and [free boundary problems](wikipedia://en/32070938). The problem is to find the [equilibrium](wikipedia://en/92290) position of an [elastic membrane](wikipedia://en/458866) whose boundary is held fixed, and which is constrained to lie above a given obstacle. It is deeply related to the study of [minimal surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surfaces) and the [capacity of a set](wikipedia://en/14368398) in [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) as well. Applications include the study of fluid filtration in porous media, constrained heating, elasto-plasticity, [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565), and financial mathematics.[^o2]

:::

The mathematical formulation of the problem is to seek minimizers of the [Dirichlet energy](wikipedia://en/14377820) functional,

in some domains $D$ where the functions $u$ represent the vertical displacement of the membrane. In addition to satisfying [Dirichlet boundary conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_boundary_conditions) corresponding to the fixed boundary of the membrane, the functions $u$ are in addition constrained to be greater than some given *obstacle* function $\phi(x)$. The solution breaks down into a region where the solution is equal to the obstacle function, known as the *contact set,* and a region where the solution is above the obstacle. The interface between the two regions is the *free boundary.*

In general, the solution is continuous and possesses [Lipschitz continuous](wikipedia://en/51731) first derivatives, but that the solution is generally discontinuous in the second derivatives across the free boundary. The free boundary is characterized as a [Hölder continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hölder_continuity) surface except at certain singular points, which reside on a smooth manifold.

### Optimal stopping {#Optimal_stopping}

The obstacle problem also arises in [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039), specifically the question of finding the optimal stopping time for a [stochastic process](wikipedia://en/47895) with payoff function $\phi(x)$.

In the simple case wherein the process is [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436), and the process is forced to stop upon exiting the domain, the solution $u(x)$ of the obstacle problem can be characterized as the expected value of the payoff, starting the process at $x$, if the optimal stopping strategy is followed. The stopping criterion is simply that one should stop upon reaching the *contact set*.[^o10]

## Formal statement {#Formal_statement}

Suppose the following data is given:

1. an [open](wikipedia://en/39358) [bounded](wikipedia://en/48258) [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643#Real_and_complex_analysis) $D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ with [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824)
1. a [smooth function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) $f$ on $\partial D$ (the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of $D$)
1. a smooth function $\varphi$ defined on all of $D$ such that $\varphi|_{\partial D} < f$, i.e., the restriction of $\varphi$ to the boundary of $D$ (its [trace](wikipedia://en/11199199)) is less than $f$.

Then consider the set

A solution to the obstacle problem is a function $u\in K$ which minimizes the energy [integral](wikipedia://en/15532)

$$J(u)=\operatorname{min}_{v\in K} J(v)\text{ or }u\in\operatorname{Argmin}_K J.$$

### Least superharmonic function {#Least_superharmonic_function}

A variational argument shows that, away from the contact set, the solution to the obstacle problem is harmonic. A similar argument which restricts itself to variations that are positive shows that the solution is superharmonic on the contact set. Together, the two arguments imply that the solution is a superharmonic function.[^o2]

In fact, an application of the [maximum principle](wikipedia://en/1436104) then shows that the solution to the obstacle problem is the least superharmonic function in the set of admissible functions.[^o17]

## Regularity properties {#Regularity_properties}

### Optimal regularity {#Optimal_regularity}

The solution to the obstacle problem has $C^{1,1}$ regularity, or [bounded](wikipedia://en/311509) [second derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921#Higher_derivatives), when the obstacle itself has these properties.[^o21] More precisely, the solution's [modulus of continuity](wikipedia://en/964161) and the modulus of continuity for its [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) are related to those of the obstacle.

1. If the obstacle $\phi(x)$ has modulus of continuity $\sigma(r)$, that is to say that $|\phi(x) - \phi(y)|\leq \sigma(|x-y|)$, then the solution $u(x)$ has modulus of continuity given by $C\sigma(2r)$, where the constant depends only on the domain and not the obstacle.
1. If the obstacle's first derivative has modulus of continuity $\sigma(r)$, then the solution's first derivative has modulus of continuity given by $C r \sigma(2r)$, where the constant again depends only on the domain.[^o22]

### Level surfaces and the free boundary {#Level_surfaces_and_the_free_boundary}

Subject to a degeneracy condition, level sets of the difference between the solution and the obstacle, $\{x: u(x) -\phi(x) = t\}$ for $t > 0$ are $C^{1,\alpha}$ surfaces. The free boundary, which is the boundary of the set where the solution meets the obstacle, is also $C^{1,\alpha}$ except on a set of *singular points,* which are themselves either isolated or locally contained on a $C^1$ manifold.[^o24]

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The theory of the obstacle problem is extended to other divergence form uniformly [elliptic operators](wikipedia://en/550137),[^o17] and their associated energy functionals. It can be generalized to degenerate elliptic operators as well.

The double obstacle problem, where the function is constrained to lie above one obstacle function and below another, is also of interest.

The [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510) is a variant of the obstacle problem, where the energy functional is minimized subject to a constraint which only lives on a surface of one lesser dimension, which includes the *boundary obstacle problem*, where the constraint operates on the boundary of the domain.

The [parabolic](wikipedia://en/7466971), time-dependent cases of the obstacle problem and its variants are also objects of study.
