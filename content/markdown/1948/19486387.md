# Jordan's totient function

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jordan's totient function"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), **Jordan's totient function**, denoted as $J_k(n)$, where $k$ is a positive integer, is a function of a [positive integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_integer), $n$, that equals the number of $k$-[tuples](wikipedia://en/132729) of positive integers that are less than or equal to $n$ and that together with $n$ form a [coprime set](wikipedia://en/6556#Coprimality_in_sets) of $k+1$ integers.

:::

Jordan's totient function is a generalization of [Euler's totient function](wikipedia://en/53452), which is the same as $J_1(n)$. The function is named after [Camille Jordan](wikipedia://en/355931).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For each positive integer $k$, Jordan's totient function $J_k$ is [multiplicative](wikipedia://en/20059) and may be evaluated as

$J_k(n)=n^k \prod_{p|n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^k}\right) \,$, where $p$ ranges through the prime divisors of $n$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- $$\sum_{d | n } J_k(d) = n^k. \,$$

which may be written in the language of [Dirichlet convolutions](wikipedia://en/179017) as[^o6]

$$J_k(n) \star 1 = n^k\,$$

and via [Möbius inversion](wikipedia://en/20963) as

$$J_k(n) = \mu(n) \star n^k.$$

Since the [Dirichlet generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet_generating_function) of $\mu$ is $1/\zeta(s)$ and the Dirichlet generating function of $n^k$ is $\zeta(s-k)$, the series for $J_k$ becomes

$$\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{J_k(n)}{n^s} = \frac{\zeta(s-k)}{\zeta(s)}.$$

- An [average order](wikipedia://en/18760872) of $J_k(n)$ is

$$J_k(n) \sim \frac{n^k}{\zeta(k+1)}.$$

- The [Dedekind psi function](wikipedia://en/5234384) is

$$\psi(n) = \frac{J_2(n)}{J_1(n)},$$

and by inspection of the definition (recognizing that each factor in the product over the primes is a cyclotomic polynomial of $p^{-k}$), the arithmetic functions defined by $\frac{J_k(n)}{J_1(n)}$ or $\frac{J_{2k}(n)}{J_k(n)}$ can also be shown to be integer-valued multiplicative functions.

- $\sum_{\delta\mid n}\delta^sJ_r(\delta)J_s\left(\frac{n}{\delta}\right) = J_{r+s}(n)$.[^o7]

:::

## Order of matrix groups {#Order_of_matrix_groups}

- The [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) of matrices of order $m$ over $\mathbf{Z}/n$ has order[^o9]

$$|\operatorname{GL}(m,\mathbf{Z}/n)|=n^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\prod_{k=1}^m J_k(n).$$

- The [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) of matrices of order $m$ over $\mathbf{Z}/n$ has order

$$|\operatorname{SL}(m,\mathbf{Z}/n)|=n^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\prod_{k=2}^m J_k(n).$$

- The [symplectic group](wikipedia://en/173977) of matrices of order $m$ over $\mathbf{Z}/n$ has order

$$|\operatorname{Sp}(2m,\mathbf{Z}/n)|=n^{m^2}\prod_{k=1}^m J_{2k}(n).$$

The first two formulas were discovered by Jordan.
