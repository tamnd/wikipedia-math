# Ringing (signal)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ringing"}

*Image: High accuracy settling time measurements figure 1.png.* An illustration of [overshoot](wikipedia://en/22376472), followed by ringing and [settle time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settle_time).

In [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), and video, **ringing** is [oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation) of a signal, particularly in the [step response](wikipedia://en/545863) (the response to a sudden change in input). Often ringing is undesirable, but not always, as in the case of [resonant inductive coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonant_inductive_coupling). It is also known as **hunting**.[^o2]

:::

It is also known as [ripple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripple_%28electrical%29), particularly in electricity or in [frequency domain](wikipedia://en/370346) response.

## Electrical circuits {#Electrical_circuits}

In [electrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity) circuits, **ringing** is an [oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation) of a [voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage) or [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Current_%28electricity%29). Ringing can be undesirable because it causes extra current to flow, thereby wasting energy and causing extra heating of the components; it can cause unwanted [electromagnetic radiation](wikipedia://en/9426) to be emitted; it can increase [settling time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settling_time) for the desired final state; and it may cause unwanted triggering of bistable elements in [digital circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_circuit). Ringy communications circuits may suffer [falsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsing).

Two electrical sources of this ringing are:

1. A [resonant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resonant) [transient response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transient_response) due to undesired [parasitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitic_element_%28electrical_networks%29) [capacitances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor) and [inductances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductor) in the circuit creating a resonant [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779).[^o4] This is often the [damping](wikipedia://en/1744868) response following [overshoot](wikipedia://en/22376472) or undershoot, and thus these related concepts are at times conflated. This ringing can be reduced by a slower [slew rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slew_rate) and possibly eliminated by critically dampening the resonance.
1. [Signal reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_reflection), which may be minimized by [impedance matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impedance_matching).

While either issue can be addressed with a series [termination resistor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termination_resistor), a resistor cannot simultaneously critically damp the response and perfectly match the impedance without losing some power on the series resistor, so a [bypass capacitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_capacitor) may be preferred.[^o5]

## Analog video {#Analog_video}

In a [cathode-ray tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube) (CRT) [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video) circuit, electrical ringing causes closely spaced [repeated ghosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghosting_%28television%29) of a vertical or diagonal edge where dark changes to light or vice versa, going from left to right, whereby the electron beam's intensity overshoots and undershoots the desired intensity there a few times instead of settling quickly. This bouncing could occur anywhere in the electronics or cabling and is often caused by or accentuated by a too high setting of the sharpness control.

## Analog audio {#Analog_audio}

Ringing can affect audio equipment in a number of ways. Audio amplifiers can produce ringing depending on their design, although the transients that can produce such ringing rarely occur in audio signals.

Transducers (i.e., [microphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphones) and [loudspeakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudspeakers)) can also ring. Mechanical ringing is more of a problem with loudspeakers as the moving masses are larger and less easily damped, but unless extreme they are difficult to audibly identify.

## Signal processing {#Signal_processing}

In [signal processing](wikipedia://en/29324), "ringing" may refer to [ringing artifacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringing_artifacts): spurious signals near sharp transitions. These have a number of causes, and occur for instance in [JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG) compression and as [pre-echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-echo) in some [digital audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_audio) [compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_artifact).

Signals constructed as only a partial (not infinite) [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038) of a function containing [discontinuities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuity_%28mathematics%29) (e.g. when applying a [brickwall lowpass filter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brickwall_filter) to a [square wave](wikipedia://en/314652)) create a ringing error called the [Gibbs phenomenon](wikipedia://en/373986) *before* and *after* each discontinuity.
