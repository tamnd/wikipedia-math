# Justin T. Moore

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Justin T. Moore"}

**Justin Tatch Moore** (born 1974)[^o2] is a set theorist and logician. He is a full professor in mathematics at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Moore received his PhD in 2000 from the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) under the supervision of [Stevo Todorcevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevo_Todorcevic).[^o2][^o4] He was an assistant professor in mathematics at [Boise State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_State_University). In the fall of 2007, he joined the faculty at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University).

## Research {#Research}

His primary research area is [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175) of infinite sets. He is known for solutions to the basis problem for uncountable linear orders and to the L space problem from [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649)[^o6] and for his work in determining the consequences of relating the continuum to certain values of the [aleph function](http://www.mathreference.com/set-card,aleph.html).[^o7] Moore, together with his PhD student Yash Lodha, produced the first torsion-free and finitely presented counterexample to the [von Neumann-Day problem](wikipedia://en/662256), originally described by mathematician [John von Neumann](wikipedia://en/15942) in 1929. Lodha presented this solution at the London Mathematical Society's Geometric and Cohomological Group Theory symposium in August 2013.[^o8]

## Awards, distinctions, and recognitions {#Awards,_distinctions,_and_recognitions}

Moore won the "Young Scholar's Competition" award in 2006, in Vienna, Austria. The Competition was a part of the "Horizons of Truth" celebrating the Gödel Centenary 2006.[^o10] He was an invited speaker at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842), [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad) 2010, Logic session, where he presented his solution to the problem of constructing an L-space. The L-space was constructed without assuming additional axioms and by combining Todorcevic's rho functions with number theory.[^o11][^o12]

Moore is an editor for the Archive of Mathematical Logic where he handles papers in set theory.[^o7] He was one of the organizers of the fall 2012 Thematic Program in Forcing and its Applications (Forcing Axioms and their Applications) at the [Fields Institute](wikipedia://en/2495768).[^o13] In 2012, he was elected as a Fellow (Inaugural Class of Fellows) of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o14]
