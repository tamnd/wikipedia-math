# Zadoff–Chu sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Zadoff–Chu sequence"}

A **Zadoff–Chu (ZC) sequence** [^o2] is a [complex-valued](wikipedia://en/5826) mathematical [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) which, when applied to a [signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal), gives rise to a new signal of constant [amplitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplitude). When [cyclically shifted](wikipedia://en/1917276) versions of a Zadoff–Chu sequence are imposed upon a signal the resulting set of signals detected at the receiver are [uncorrelated](wikipedia://en/2724) with one another.

:::

## Description {#Description}

Zadoff–Chu sequences exhibit the useful property that cyclically shifted versions of themselves are [orthogonal](wikipedia://en/102221) to one another.

A generated Zadoff–Chu sequence that has not been shifted is known as a *root sequence*.

*Image: Zadoff Chu 7 353.png.* Plot of a Zadoff-Chu sequence for u=7, N=353

The complex value at each position *n* of each root Zadoff–Chu sequence parameterized by *u* is given by

$$x_u(n)=\text{exp}\left(-j\frac{\pi un(n+c_\text{f}+2q)}{N_\text{ZC}}\right), \,$$

where

$$0 \le n < N_\text{ZC},$$

$0 < u < N_\text{ZC}$ and $\text{gcd}(N_\text{ZC},u)=1$,

$$c_\text{f} = N_\text{ZC} \mod 2,$$

$$q \in \mathbb{Z},$$

$$N_\text{ZC} = \text{length of sequence}.$$

Zadoff–Chu sequences are CAZAC sequences ([constant-amplitude zero-autocorrelation waveform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constant-amplitude_zero-autocorrelation_waveform)).

Note that the special case $q = 0$ results in a Chu sequence,.[^o2] Setting $q \neq 0$ produces a sequence that is equal to the cyclically shifted version of the Chu sequence by $q$, **and** **multiplied** by a complex, modulus 1 number, where by multiplied we mean that each element is multiplied by the same number.

## Properties of Zadoff-Chu sequences {#Properties_of_Zadoff-Chu_sequences}

\1. They are [periodic](wikipedia://en/76848) with period $N_\text{ZC}$.

$$x_u ( n + N_{\text{ZC}} )= x_u(n)$$

\2. If $N_\text{ZC}$ is prime, the [discrete Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/8811) of a Zadoff–Chu sequence is another Zadoff–Chu sequence conjugated, scaled and time scaled.

$X_{u}[k]= x_{u}^{*}(\tilde{u}k) X_{u}[0]$ where $\tilde{u}$ is the multiplicative inverse of u modulo $N_\text{ZC}$.

\3. The auto correlation of a Zadoff–Chu sequence with a cyclically shifted version of itself is zero, i.e., it is non-zero only at one instant which corresponds to the cyclic shift.

\4. The [cross-correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_correlation) between two prime length Zadoff–Chu sequences, i.e. different values of $u, u=u_1, u=u_2$, is constant $1/{\sqrt{N_\text{ZC}}}$, provided that $u_1 - u_2$ is relatively prime to $N_\text{ZC}$.[^o5]

## Usages {#Usages}

Zadoff–Chu sequences are used in the [3GPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3GPP) [Long Term Evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Term_Evolution) (LTE) [air interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-UTRA) in the Primary Synchronization Signal (PSS), random access preamble (PRACH), uplink control channel (PUCCH), uplink traffic channel (PUSCH) and sounding reference signals (SRS).

By assigning [orthogonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal) Zadoff–Chu sequences to each LTE [eNodeB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENodeB) and multiplying their transmissions by their respective codes, the [cross-correlation](wikipedia://en/714163) of simultaneous eNodeB transmissions is reduced, thus reducing inter-cell interference and uniquely identifying eNodeB transmissions.

Zadoff–Chu sequences are an improvement over the [Walsh–Hadamard codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsh–Hadamard_code) used in [UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMTS) because they result in a constant-amplitude output signal, reducing the cost and complexity of the [radio's power amplifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_radio_head).[^o7]
