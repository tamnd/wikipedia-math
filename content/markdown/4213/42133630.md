# Marguerite Frank

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marguerite Frank"}

**Marguerite Josephine Straus Frank** (September 8, 1927 – December 11, 2024) was a French-American mathematician who was a pioneer in [convex optimization](wikipedia://en/1674411) theory and [mathematical programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_programming).

:::

## Education {#Education}

After attending secondary schooling in Paris and Toronto,[^o5] Frank contributed largely to the fields of [transportation theory](wikipedia://en/6631661) and [Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/17944), which later became the topic of her PhD thesis, *New Simple Lie Algebras*.[^o6] She was one of the first female PhD students in mathematics at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University),[^o7] completing her dissertation in 1956, with [Abraham Adrian Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Adrian_Albert) as her advisor.[^o6]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Together with [Philip Wolfe](wikipedia://en/41154952) in 1956 at [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), she invented the [Frank–Wolfe algorithm](wikipedia://en/3152055),[^o9] an iterative optimization method for general constrained [non-linear problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-linear_programming).

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Marguerite Frank was born in France and migrated to U.S. during the war in 1939.[^o5] She was married to [Joseph Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Frank_%28writer%29) from 1953 until his death in 2013. He was a professor of literature at Stanford and an author of widely acclaimed critical biography of [Dostoevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dostoevsky).[^o11] Frank died on December 11, 2024, at the age of 97.[^o12]
