# Moore matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Moore matrix"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a **Moore matrix**, introduced by (txt), is a [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) defined over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615). When it is a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022), its [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) is called a **Moore determinant** (this is unrelated to the [Moore determinant of a quaternionic Hermitian matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moore_determinant_of_a_quaternionic_Hermitian_matrix)). The Moore matrix has successive powers of the [Frobenius automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_automorphism) applied to its columns (beginning with the zeroth power of the Frobenius automorphism in the first column), so it is an *m* × *n* matrix

$$M=\begin{bmatrix} \alpha_1 & \alpha_1^q & \dots & \alpha_1^{q^{n-1}}\\ \alpha_2 & \alpha_2^q & \dots & \alpha_2^{q^{n-1}}\\ \alpha_3 & \alpha_3^q & \dots & \alpha_3^{q^{n-1}}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots &\vdots \\ \alpha_m & \alpha_m^q & \dots & \alpha_m^{q^{n-1}}\\ \end{bmatrix}$$

or

$$M_{i,j} = \alpha_i^{q^{j-1}}$$

for all indices *i* and *j*. (Some authors use the [transpose](wikipedia://en/173844) of the above matrix.)

:::

The Moore determinant of a square Moore matrix (so *m* = *n*) can be expressed as:

$$\det(M) = \prod_{\mathbf{c}} \left( c_1\alpha_1 + \cdots + c_n\alpha_n \right),$$

where **c** runs over a complete set of direction vectors, made specific by having the last non-zero entry equal to 1, i.e.,

$$\det(M) = \prod_{1 \le i \le n} \prod_{c_1, \dots, c_{i-1}} \left( c_1\alpha_1 + \cdots + c_{i-1}\alpha_{i-1} + \alpha_i \right).$$

In particular the Moore determinant vanishes [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the elements in the left hand column are [linearly dependent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linearly_dependent) over the finite field of order *q*. So it is analogous to the [Wronskian](wikipedia://en/302846) of several functions.

Dickson used the Moore determinant in finding the [modular invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_invariant_of_a_group) of the [general linear group](wikipedia://en/113564) over a finite field.
