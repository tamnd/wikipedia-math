# Dynkin's formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dynkin's formula"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) — specifically, in [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis) — **Dynkin's formula** is a theorem giving the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) of any suitably [smooth function](wikipedia://en/1408000) applied to a [Feller process](wikipedia://en/6891551) at a [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193). It may be seen as a stochastic generalization of the (second) [fundamental theorem of calculus](wikipedia://en/19468696). It is named after the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Eugene Dynkin](wikipedia://en/1351540).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $X$ be a Feller process with [infinitesimal generator](wikipedia://en/12193437) $A$. For a point $x$ in the state-space of $X$, let $\mathbf P^x$ denote the law of $X$ given initial datum $X_0=x$, and let $\mathbf E^x$ denote expectation with respect to $\mathbf P^x$. Then for any function $f$ in the domain of $A$, and any [stopping time](wikipedia://en/1202193) $\tau$ with $\mathbf E[\tau]<+\infty$, **Dynkin's formula** holds:[^o4]

$$\mathbf{E}^{x} [f(X_{\tau})] = f(x) + \mathbf{E}^{x} \left[ \int_{0}^{\tau} A f (X_{s}) \, \mathrm{d} s \right].$$

:::

## Example: Itô diffusions {#Example:_Itô_diffusions}

Let $X$ be the $\mathbf R^n$-valued [Itô diffusion](wikipedia://en/12139922) solving the [stochastic differential equation](wikipedia://en/1361454)

$$\mathrm{d} X_{t} = b(X_{t}) \, \mathrm{d} t + \sigma (X_{t}) \, \mathrm{d} B_{t}.$$

The infinitesimal generator $A$ of $X$ is defined by its action on [compactly-supported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_support) $C^2$ (twice differentiable with continuous [second derivative](wikipedia://en/3224219)) functions $f:\mathbf R^n \to \mathbf R$ as[^o6]

$$A f (x) = \lim_{t \downarrow 0} \frac{\mathbf{E}^{x} [f(X_{t})] - f(x)}{t}$$

or, equivalently,[^o7]

$$A f (x) = \sum_{i} b_{i} (x) \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}} (x) + \frac1{2} \sum_{i, j} \big( \sigma \sigma^{\top} \big)_{i, j} (x) \frac{\partial^{2} f}{\partial x_{i}\, \partial x_{j}} (x).$$

Since this $X$ is a Feller process, Dynkin's formula holds.[^o8] In fact, if $\tau$ is the first exit time of a [bounded set](wikipedia://en/48258) $B\subset\mathbf R^n$ with $\mathbf E[\tau]<+\infty$, then Dynkin's formula holds for all $C^2$ functions $f$, without the assumption of compact support.

## Application: Brownian motion exiting the ball {#Application:_Brownian_motion_exiting_the_ball}

Dynkin's formula can be used to find the expected first exit time $\tau_K$ of a [Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/4436) $B$ from the [closed ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_ball) $K= \{ x \in \mathbf{R}^{n} : \, | x | \leq R \},$ which, when $B$ starts at a point $a$ in the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of $K$, is given by

$$\mathbf{E}^{a} [\tau_{K}] = \frac1{n} \big( R^{2} - | a |^{2} \big).$$

This is shown as follows.[^o10] Fix an [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *j*. The strategy is to apply Dynkin's formula with $X=B$, $\tau=\sigma_j=\min\{j,\tau_K\}$, and a compactly-supported $f\in C^2$ with $f(x)=|x|^2$ on $K$. The generator of Brownian motion is $\Delta/2$, where $\Delta$ denotes the [Laplacian operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplacian_operator). Therefore, by Dynkin's formula,

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}^{a} \left[ f \big( B_{\sigma_{j}} \big) \right] &= f(a) + \mathbf{E}^{a} \left[ \int_{0}^{\sigma_{j}} \frac1{2} \Delta f (B_{s}) \, \mathrm{d} s \right] \\ &= | a |^{2} + \mathbf{E}^{a} \left[ \int_{0}^{\sigma_{j}} n \, \mathrm{d} s \right] = | a |^{2} + n \mathbf{E}^{a} [\sigma_{j}]. \end{aligned}$$

Hence, for any $j$,

$$\mathbf{E}^{a} [\sigma_{j}] \leq \frac1{n} \big( R^{2} - | a |^{2} \big).$$

Now let $j\to+\infty$ to conclude that $\tau_K=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\sigma_j<+\infty$ [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908), and so $\mathbf{E}^{a} [\tau_{K}] =( R^{2} - | a |^{2})/n$ as claimed.
