# Joanna Isabel Mayer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Joanna Isabel Mayer"}

**Joanna Isabel Mayer** (March 6, 1904 – February 28, 1991) was an American mathematician and educator. She was Marquette University's first doctoral student in mathematics and one of the few American women to earn a PhD in mathematics before World War II.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Mayer was born in [Pettis County, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pettis_County,_Missouri), the third of five children of a parochial school teacher, Anna Poinsignon, and a farmer, Edward John Mayer. The first school she attended was Sacred Heart School in [Sedalia, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedalia,_Missouri) but then the family moved many times including to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles), California, [Phoenix, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona), [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon), [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), [Nashville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville,_Tennessee), [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), Utah, and back to [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California) where she graduated from [Notre Dame High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_High_School_%28San_Jose,_California%29). Throughout her life, she attended and taught only in Catholic schools.[^o3]

Mayer received her B.A. degree from [Dominican College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_University_of_California) in [San Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Rafael,_California) in 1927 with a major in mathematics and a minor in philosophy. While there she was influenced by mathematician [Anna Marie Whelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Marie_Whelan). Mayer immediately enrolled in graduate school at [Marquette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquette_University) in [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), Wisconsin, and in 1928 she received her MA degree with the thesis titled: *A geometric interpretation and classification of the invariants of the binary and ternary conics and cubics,* directed by [Harvey Pierson Pettit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Pierson_Pettit).[^o3] In 1931, she was the person to earn a doctorate in mathematics at Marquette University, studying again under Pettit's direction with a thesis entitled: *Projective Description of Plane Quartic Curves*.[^o3][^o4][^o5]

### Educator {#Educator}

From 1929 to 1930, while she was finishing her doctoral studies, she served as department head at [Marymount College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marymount_College_%28Kansas%29) in [Salina, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salina,_Kansas). In 1930 the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) announced her membership.[^o3]

In 1932 she taught in San Jose, California, and from 1937 to 1938 she was an instructor at Seton Hill College (now [Seton Hill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seton_Hill_University)), which was a Catholic women's college at the time, located in [Greensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensburg,_Pennsylvania), Pennsylvania. In 1939 she taught at [Xavier University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavier_University_of_Louisiana) in [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans), described as "the only Catholic institution among the historically black colleges and universities" at that time.[^o3] According to Green, "Mayer wrote in a letter to the Marquette graduate school dean in 1956 that she left teaching in 1942 and had supported herself since by buying and selling stocks."[^o3]

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), she worked in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), and in 1950 for the Guided Missiles Committee of the Department of Defense, Pentagon, Washington, D.C. She worked for more than 13 years at the Military Personnel Records Center in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis), Missouri.[^o3]

### Personal life {#Personal_life}

Mayer died February 28, 1991, in San Jose, California and was buried at the [Santa Clara Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Santa_Clara_de_Asís) Cemetery.[^o3]

## Memberships {#Memberships}

According to [Judy Green](wikipedia://en/55756466), Mayer belonged to two professional societies.[^o3]

- [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772)
- [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)
