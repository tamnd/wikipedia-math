# Fenchel's Law

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fenchel's Law"}

**Fenchel's Law** is a regularity in [population ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_ecology) regarding how exponential population growth is related to the body size of the organism. It was first described by the [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) [marine ecologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_biology) [Tom Fenchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Fenchel). It contends that species with larger body sizes tend to have lower rates of population growth. More exactly, it states that the maximum rate of reproduction decreases with body size at a power of a quarter of the body mass[^o2]

:::

Fenchel's law may be expressed as an allometric equation:

$$r = {aW^{-0.25}},$$

where **r** is the intrinsic rate of natural population growth, **a** is a constant that has 3 different values (one for [unicellular organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular_organism), one for [poikilotherms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poikilotherm) and one for [homeotherms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeotherm)), and **W** is the average body mass of the organism. This means that if a species *A* has a body mass 10 times that of species B, then the maximum population growth rate of *A* will be one-half that of species B.[^o3]
