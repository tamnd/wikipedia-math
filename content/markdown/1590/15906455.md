# Half-normal distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Half-normal distribution"}

In probability theory and statistics, the **half-normal distribution** is a special case of the [folded normal distribution](wikipedia://en/6260959).

:::

Let $X$ follow an ordinary [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462), $N(0,\sigma^2)$. Then, $Y=|X|$ follows a half-normal distribution. Thus, the half-normal distribution is a fold at the mean of an ordinary normal distribution with mean zero.

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Using the $\sigma$ parametrization of the normal distribution, the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (PDF) of the half-normal is given by

$$f_Y(y; \sigma) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}\exp \left( -\frac{y^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) \quad y \geq 0,$$

where $E[Y] = \mu = \frac{\sigma\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}$.

Alternatively using a scaled precision (inverse of the variance) parametrization (to avoid issues if $\sigma$ is near zero), obtained by setting $\theta=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sigma\sqrt{2}}$, the [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) is given by

$$f_Y(y; \theta) = \frac{2\theta}{\pi}\exp \left( -\frac{y^2\theta^2}{\pi} \right) \quad y \geq 0,$$

where $E[Y] = \mu = \frac{1}{\theta}$.

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) (CDF) is given by

$$F_Y(y; \sigma) = \int_0^y \frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} \, \exp \left( -\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)\, dx$$

Using the change-of-variables $z = x/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)$, the CDF can be written as

$$F_Y(y; \sigma) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \,\int_0^{y/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)}\exp \left(-z^2\right)dz = \operatorname{erf}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2}\sigma}\right),$$

where erf is the [error function](wikipedia://en/177111), a standard function in many mathematical software packages.

The quantile function (or inverse CDF) is written:

$$Q(F;\sigma)=\sigma\sqrt{2} \operatorname{erf}^{-1}(F)$$

where $0\le F \le 1$ and $\operatorname{erf}^{-1}$ is the [inverse error function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_error_function#Inverse_functions)

The expectation is then given by

$$E[Y] = \sigma \sqrt{2/\pi},$$

The variance is given by

$$\operatorname{var}(Y) = \sigma^2\left(1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\right).$$

Since this is proportional to the variance σ<sup>2</sup> of *X*, *σ* can be seen as a [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503) of the new distribution.

The differential entropy of the half-normal distribution is exactly one bit less the differential entropy of a zero-mean normal distribution with the same second moment about 0. This can be understood intuitively since the magnitude operator reduces information by one bit (if the probability distribution at its input is even). Alternatively, since a half-normal distribution is always positive, the one bit it would take to record whether a standard normal random variable were positive (say, a 1) or negative (say, a 0) is no longer necessary. Thus,

$$h(Y) = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 \left( \frac{\pi e \sigma^2}{2} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \log_2 \left( 2\pi e \sigma^2 \right) -1.$$

:::

## Applications {#Applications}

The half-normal distribution is commonly utilized as a [prior probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_probability_distribution) for [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) parameters in [Bayesian inference](wikipedia://en/49571) applications.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Parameter estimation {#Parameter_estimation}

Given numbers $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^n$ drawn from a half-normal distribution, the unknown parameter $\sigma$ of that distribution can be estimated by the method of [maximum likelihood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_likelihood), giving

$$\hat \sigma = \sqrt{\frac 1 n \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2}$$

The bias is equal to

$$b \equiv \operatorname{E}\bigg[\;(\hat\sigma_\mathrm{mle} - \sigma)\;\bigg] = - \frac{\sigma}{4n}$$

which yields the [bias-corrected maximum likelihood estimator](wikipedia://en/140806#Higher-order_properties)

$$\hat{\sigma\,}^*_\text{mle} = \hat{\sigma\,}_\text{mle} - \hat{b\,}.$$

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

- The distribution is a special case of the [folded normal distribution](wikipedia://en/6260959) with *μ* = 0.
- It also coincides with a zero-mean normal distribution truncated from below at zero (see [truncated normal distribution](wikipedia://en/11753597))
- If *Y* has a half-normal distribution, then (*Y*/*σ*)<sup>2</sup> has a [chi square distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_square_distribution) with 1 degree of freedom, i.e. *Y*/*σ* has a [chi distribution](wikipedia://en/1901933) with 1 degree of freedom.
- The half-normal distribution is a special case of the [generalized gamma distribution](wikipedia://en/24553589) with *d* = 1, *p* = 2, *a* = $\sqrt{2}\sigma$.
- If *Y* has a half-normal distribution, *Y*<sup>\-2</sup> has a [Lévy distribution](wikipedia://en/1346871)
- The [Rayleigh distribution](wikipedia://en/262296) is a moment-tilted and scaled generalization of the half-normal distribution.
