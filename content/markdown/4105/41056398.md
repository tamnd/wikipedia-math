# Privacy-preserving computational geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Privacy-preserving computational geometry"}

**Privacy-preserving computational geometry** is the research area on the intersection of the domains of [secure multi-party computation](wikipedia://en/646233) (SMC) and [computational geometry](wikipedia://en/176927). Classical problems of computational geometry reconsidered from the point of view of SMC include shape intersection, private point inclusion problem, [range searching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_searching), [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634),[^o2] and more.[^o3]

:::

A pioneering work in this area was a 2001 paper by Atallah and Du,[^o4] in which the secure [point in polygon](wikipedia://en/504484) inclusion and polygonal intersection problems were considered.

Other problems are computation of the distance between two private points[^o5] and secure two-party point-circle inclusion problem.[^o6]

## Problem statements {#Problem_statements}

The problems use the conventional "[Alice and Bob](wikipedia://en/679079)" terminology. In all problems the required solution is a protocol of information exchange during which no additional information is revealed beyond what may be inferred from the answer to the required question.

- **Point-in-polygon:** Alice has a point *a*, and Bob has a polygon *B*. They need to determine whether *a* is inside *B*.[^o4]
- **Polygon pair intersection:** Alice has a polygon *A*, and Bob has a polygon *B*. They need to determine whether A intersects B.[^o4]
