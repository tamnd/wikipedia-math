# Nodal decomposition

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nodal decomposition"}

*Image: Definition-of-a-nodal-decomposition.jpg.* Nodal decomposition.

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), an abstract mathematical discipline, a **nodal decomposition**[^n1] of a morphism $\varphi:X\to Y$ is a representation of $\varphi$ as a product $\varphi=\sigma\circ\beta\circ\pi$, where $\pi$ is a [strong epimorphism](wikipedia://en/59539#Related_concepts),[^o3][^n2][^n3] $\beta$ a [bimorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimorphism), and $\sigma$ a [strong monomorphism](wikipedia://en/59538#Related_concepts).[^o6][^n2][^n3]

:::

## Uniqueness and notations {#Uniqueness_and_notations}

*Image: Uniqueness-of-nodal-decomposition-2.jpg.* Uniqueness of the nodal decomposition.

If it exists, the nodal decomposition is unique up to an isomorphism in the following sense: for any two nodal decompositions $\varphi=\sigma\circ\beta\circ\pi$ and $\varphi=\sigma'\circ\beta'\circ\pi'$ there exist [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891#Isomorphisms) $\eta$ and $\theta$ such that

$$\pi'=\eta\circ\pi,$$

$$\beta=\theta\circ\beta'\circ\eta,$$

$$\sigma'=\sigma\circ\theta.$$

*Image: Nodal-decomposition-notations.jpg.* Notations.

This property justifies some special notations for the elements of the nodal decomposition:

$$\begin{aligned} & \pi=\operatorname{coim}_\infty \varphi, && P=\operatorname{Coim}_\infty \varphi,\\ & \beta=\operatorname{red}_\infty \varphi, && \\ & \sigma=\operatorname{im}_\infty \varphi, && Q=\operatorname{Im}_\infty \varphi, \end{aligned}$$

– here $\operatorname{coim}_\infty \varphi$ and $\operatorname{Coim}_\infty \varphi$ are called the *nodal coimage of $\varphi$*, $\operatorname{im}_\infty \varphi$ and $\operatorname{Im}_\infty \varphi$ the *nodal image of $\varphi$*, and $\operatorname{red}_\infty \varphi$ the *nodal reduced part of $\varphi$*.

In these notations the nodal decomposition takes the form

$$\varphi=\operatorname{im}_\infty \varphi\circ\operatorname{red}_\infty \varphi \circ \operatorname{coim}_\infty \varphi.$$

## Connection with the basic decomposition in pre-abelian categories {#Connection_with_the_basic_decomposition_in_pre-abelian_categories}

In a [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775) ${\mathcal K}$ each morphism $\varphi$ has a standard decomposition

$$\varphi=\operatorname{im} \varphi\circ\operatorname{red} \varphi\circ\operatorname{coim} \varphi,$$

called the *basic decomposition* (here $\operatorname{im} \varphi=\ker(\operatorname{coker} \varphi)$, $\operatorname{coim} \varphi=\operatorname{coker}(\ker\varphi)$, and $\operatorname{red} \varphi$ are respectively the image, the coimage and the reduced part of the morphism $\varphi$).

*Image: Nodal-and-basic-decomposition-1.jpg.* Nodal and basic decompositions.

If a morphism $\varphi$ in a [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775) ${\mathcal K}$ has a nodal decomposition, then there exist morphisms $\eta$ and $\theta$ which (being not necessarily isomorphisms) connect the nodal decomposition with the basic decomposition by the following identities:

$$\operatorname{coim}_\infty \varphi=\eta\circ\operatorname{coim} \varphi,$$

$$\operatorname{red} \varphi=\theta\circ\operatorname{red}_\infty \varphi\circ\eta,$$

$$\operatorname{im}_\infty \varphi=\operatorname{im} \varphi\circ\theta.$$

## Categories with nodal decomposition {#Categories_with_nodal_decomposition}

A category ${\mathcal K}$ is called a *category with nodal decomposition*[^n1] if each morphism $\varphi$ has a nodal decomposition in ${\mathcal K}$. This property plays an important role in constructing [envelopes](wikipedia://en/55698833) and [refinements](wikipedia://en/56509649) in ${\mathcal K}$.

In an [abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) ${\mathcal K}$ the basic decomposition

$$\varphi=\operatorname{im} \varphi\circ\operatorname{red} \varphi\circ\operatorname{coim} \varphi$$

is always nodal. As a corollary, *all abelian categories have nodal decomposition*.

*If a [pre-abelian category](wikipedia://en/62775) ${\mathcal K}$ is linearly complete,[^o13] well-powered in strong monomorphisms[^o14] and co-well-powered in strong epimorphisms,[^o15] then ${\mathcal K}$ has nodal decomposition.[^n4]*

More generally, *suppose a category ${\mathcal K}$ is linearly complete,[^o13] well-powered in strong monomorphisms,[^o14] co-well-powered in strong epimorphisms,[^o15] and in addition strong epimorphisms discern monomorphisms[^o17] in ${\mathcal K}$, and, dually, strong monomorphisms discern epimorphisms[^o18] in ${\mathcal K}$, then ${\mathcal K}$ has nodal decomposition.[^n5]*

The category **Ste** of [stereotype spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_space) (being non-abelian) has nodal decomposition,[^n6] as well as the (non-[additive](wikipedia://en/60854)) category **SteAlg** of [stereotype algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_algebra) .[^n7]
