# Mabel Gweneth Humphreys

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mabel Gweneth Humphreys"}

**Mabel Gweneth Humphreys** (1911-2006) was a [Canadian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian-American) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Professor of Mathematics at [Randolph-Macon Woman's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph-Macon_Women's_College).[^o3] The [M. Gweneth Humphreys Award](wikipedia://en/51370132) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) was established in her honor.[^o11]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Humphreys attended North Vancouver High School from 1925 to 1928. She received her Bachelor of Arts with honors in mathematics from the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) in 1932, where she held scholarships for all four years. She studied at [Smith College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College) under [Susan Miller Rambo](wikipedia://en/44191494), among others. She earned a master's degree in mathematics in 1933.[^o3] She received her Ph.D. in mathematics from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) in 1935. Her dissertation was entitled *On the Waring Problem with Polynomial Summands* and her advisor was [Leonard Eugene Dickson](wikipedia://en/1790247). [^o3] [^o5]

## Career {#Career}

From 1935 to 1936, Humphreys was an instructor of mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at [Mount St. Scholastica College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Scholastica_College). She began teaching at [H. Sophie Newcomb Memorial College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Sophie_Newcomb_Memorial_College) in 1936 and was promoted to [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) in 1941. She was also an assistant professor at [Barnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnard_College) in the summer of 1944, and an assistant professor at [Tulane University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulane_University) in the summer of 1946.[^o3]

In 1949, Humphreys became an [associate professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_professor) of mathematics at Randolph-Macon Woman's College in [Lynchburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia).[^o7] After one year at Randolph-Macon, she was named [Gillie A. Larew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gillie_Larew) Professor and head of the mathematics department.[^o8] She was head of the department until 1979. For the 1955-1956 academic year, Humphreys went on sabbatical leave to the University of British Columbia (UBC). During this time, she visited undergraduate mathematics programs at several colleges and universities to examine their methods. From 1962 to 1963, she was a [visiting professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visiting_professor) at UBC as a [National Science Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation) (NSF) faculty fellow.[^o3][^o9]

In the summers, Humphreys taught high school teachers at NSF summer institutes.[^o9] From 1965 to 1969, Humphreys worked for the [Educational Testing Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Testing_Service). She was also a consultant in 1975 for the [American Council on Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Council_on_Education) regarding mathematics course credit given by nonacademic organizations. Humphreys was an active member of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) at both the sectional and national levels.[^o3]

## Awards and legacy {#Awards_and_legacy}

Humphreys earned the [Governor General's Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General's_Academic_Medal#Categories) in 1932, which was awarded to the college student with the highest grade point average in Canada.[^o3]

The [M. Gweneth Humphreys Award](wikipedia://en/51370132) of the [Association for Women in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/9858698) is named in her honor. Each year, this award is presented to a mathematics educator who has encouraged women undergraduates to pursue mathematical careers.[^o11]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Humphreys was born on October 22, 1911 in [South Vancouver, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vancouver,_British_Columbia). Her mother, Mabel Jane Thomas (1885-1963), was born in [London, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_England), and worked as a dressmaker and a florist. Her father was Richard Humphreys (1880-1969), a [machinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinist) who was born in [Pwllheli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pwllheli), Wales. Her parents were married in 1910, and Humphreys was their only child.[^o3] In 1941, Humphreys became a naturalized [U.S. citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._citizen).[^o3]

Humphreys' hobbies included gardening and reading. She was an active member of the [Natural Bridge Appalachian Trail Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Trail_Conservancy#Affiliated_trail_maintenance_clubs). She lived in [Lynchburg, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchburg,_Virginia), through her retirement in 1980 until her death on October 6, 2006.[^o3]
