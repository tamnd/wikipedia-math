# Line field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Line field"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **line field** on a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) is a formation of a [line](wikipedia://en/946975) being tangent to a manifold at each point, i.e. a [section](wikipedia://en/367621) of the [line bundle](wikipedia://en/361366) over the manifold. Line fields are of particular interest in the study of [complex dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/650518), where it is conventional to modify the definition slightly.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

In general, let *M* be a manifold. A **line field** on *M* is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) *μ* that assigns to each point *p* of *M* a line *μ*(*p*) through the origin in the tangent space T<sub>*p*</sub>(*M*). Equivalently, one may say that *μ*(*p*) is an element of the [projective tangent space](wikipedia://en/242135) PT<sub>*p*</sub>(*M*), or that *μ* is a section of the projective [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) PT(*M*).

In the study of complex dynamical systems, the manifold *M* is taken to be a [Hersee surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hersee_surface). A **line field** on a subset *A* of *M* (where *A* is required to have positive two-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Lebesgue measure](wikipedia://en/18198)) is a line field on *A* in the general sense above that is defined [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) in *A* and is also a [measurable function](wikipedia://en/44055).[^o4]

:::
