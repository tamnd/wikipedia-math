# William Gasarch

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="William Gasarch"}

**William Ian Gasarch** ( ;[^o3] born 1959[^o4]) is an American computer scientist known for his work in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), [computability theory](wikipedia://en/155414), [computational learning theory](wikipedia://en/387537), and [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175). He is currently a professor at the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) Department of Computer Science with an affiliate appointment in Mathematics.

:::

Gasarch is a frequent mentor of high school student research projects; one of these, with [Jacob Lurie](wikipedia://en/26663863), won the 1996 [Westinghouse Science Talent Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Science_Talent_Search) for Lurie.[^o5] He has co-blogged on computational complexity with [Lance Fortnow](wikipedia://en/25303350) since 2007. He was book review editor for [ACM SIGACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_SIGACT) NEWS from 1997 to 2015.

## Education {#Education}

Gasarch received his doctorate in computer science from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1985, advised by [Harry R. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_R._Lewis). His thesis was titled *Recursion-Theoretic Techniques in Complexity Theory and Combinatorics*. He was hired into a tenure track professorial job at the University of Maryland in the Fall of 1985. He was promoted to associate professor with tenure in 1991, and to full professor in 1998.[^o8]

## Work {#Work}

Gasarch co-founded (with Richard Beigel) the field of Bounded Queries in Recursion Theory[^o10] and has written many papers in the area capped off by a book on the topic co-authored with Georgia Martin, titled *Bounded Queries in Recursion Theory*.[^o11] He has published books such as *Problems with a Point*,[^o12] a book with a broad view on mathematics and theoretical computer science which he co-authored with [Clyde Kruskal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Kruskal) and includes works by other professors such as [David Eppstein](wikipedia://en/4015871).[^o13] He also co-founded the subfield of recursion-theoretic inductive inference named *Learning via Queries*[^o14] with [Carl Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Herbert_Smith). More recently he has been more involved with combinatorics, notably Ramsey Theory.[^o15][^o16][^o17] He has written three surveys of what theorists think of the [P vs NP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P_vs_NP) problem: in 2002, 2012, and 2019.[^o18][^o19][^o20] In 2020 he wrote *Mathematical Muffin Morsels: Nobody Wants a Small Piece* with Erik Metz, Jacob Prinz, and Daniel Smolyak. [^o21]

## Blog {#Blog}

Lance Fortnow began writing a blog on theoretical computer science with an emphasis on complexity theory in 2002.[^o23] Gasarch was a frequent guest blogger until 2007 when he became an official co-blogger.
