# Connection (algebraic framework)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Connection"}

Geometry of [quantum systems](wikipedia://en/25202) (e.g., [noncommutative geometry](wikipedia://en/295917) and [supergeometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergeometry)) is mainly phrased in algebraic terms of [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) and [algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebras). **Connections** on modules are generalization of a linear [connection](wikipedia://en/391816) on a smooth [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) $E\to X$ written as a [Koszul connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koszul_connection) on the $C^\infty(X)$-module of sections of $E\to X$.[^o2]

:::

## Commutative algebra {#Commutative_algebra}

Let $A$ be a commutative [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) and $M$ an *A*-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). There are different equivalent definitions of a connection on $M$.[^o4]

### First definition {#First_definition}

If $k \to A$ is a ring homomorphism, a $k$-linear connection is a $k$-linear morphism

$$\nabla: M \to \Omega^1_{A/k} \otimes_A M$$

which satisfies the identity

$$\nabla(am) = da \otimes m + a \nabla m$$

A connection extends, for all $p \geq 0$ to a unique map

$$\nabla: \Omega^p_{A/k} \otimes_A M \to \Omega^{p+1}_{A/k} \otimes_A M$$

satisfying $\nabla(\omega \otimes f) = d\omega \otimes f + (-1)^p \omega \wedge \nabla f$. A connection is said to be integrable if $\nabla \circ \nabla = 0$, or equivalently, if the curvature $\nabla^2: M \to \Omega_{A/k}^2 \otimes M$ vanishes.

### Second definition {#Second_definition}

Let $D(A)$ be the module of [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) of a ring $A$. A connection on an *A*-module $M$ is defined as an *A*-module morphism

$$\nabla:D(A) \to \mathrm{Diff}_1(M,M); u \mapsto \nabla_u$$

such that the first order [differential operators](wikipedia://en/7147956) $\nabla_u$ on $M$ obey the Leibniz rule

$$\nabla_u(ap)=u(a)p+a\nabla_u(p), \quad a\in A, \quad p\in M.$$

Connections on a module over a commutative ring always exist.

The curvature of the connection $\nabla$ is defined as the zero-order differential operator

$$R(u,u')=[\nabla_u,\nabla_{u'}]-\nabla_{[u,u']} \,$$

on the module $M$ for all $u,u'\in D(A)$.

If $E\to X$ is a vector bundle, there is one-to-one correspondence between [linear connections](wikipedia://en/391816) $\Gamma$ on $E\to X$ and the connections $\nabla$ on the $C^\infty(X)$-module of sections of $E\to X$. Strictly speaking, $\nabla$ corresponds to the [covariant differential](wikipedia://en/431848) of a connection on $E\to X$.

## Graded commutative algebra {#Graded_commutative_algebra}

The notion of a connection on modules over commutative rings is straightforwardly extended to modules over a [graded commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/469950).[^o8] This is the case of [superconnections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergeometry) in [supergeometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergeometry) of [graded manifolds](wikipedia://en/24519420) and [supervector bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergeometry). Superconnections always exist.

## Noncommutative algebra {#Noncommutative_algebra}

If $A$ is a noncommutative ring, connections on left and right *A*-modules are defined similarly to those on modules over commutative rings.[^o10] However these connections need not exist.

In contrast with connections on left and right modules, there is a problem how to define a connection on an *R*-*S*-[bimodule](wikipedia://en/492612) over noncommutative rings *R* and *S*. There are different definitions of such a connection.[^o11] Let us mention one of them. A connection on an *R*-*S*-bimodule $P$ is defined as a bimodule morphism

$$\nabla:D(A)\ni u\to \nabla_u\in \mathrm{Diff}_1(P,P)$$

which obeys the Leibniz rule

$$\nabla_u(apb)=u(a)pb+a\nabla_u(p)b +apu(b), \quad a\in R, \quad b\in S, \quad p\in P.$$
