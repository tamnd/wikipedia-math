# Lev Tumarkin

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lev Tumarkin"}

**Lev Abramovich Tumarkin** (Лев Абра́мович Тума́ркин; 14 January 1904 – 1 August 1974) was a Soviet [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made significant contributions to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), particularly in [dimension theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_theory). He served as dean of the [Faculty of Mechanics and Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSU_Faculty_of_Mechanics_and_Mathematics) at [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) from 1935 to 1939.[^o3]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Tumarkin was born in [Hadiach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadiach) (then part of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire)'s [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate)). He graduated from Moscow State University in 1925 and completed his postgraduate studies there in 1929 under the supervision of [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245). He spent his entire academic career at Moscow State University, where he became a professor in 1932 and earned his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in [physical](wikipedia://en/22939) and [mathematical sciences](wikipedia://en/18831) in 1936.[^o5][^o6]

## Mathematical contributions {#Mathematical_contributions}

Tumarkin began his research career early, making notable contributions to topology while still an undergraduate.[^o8] His main work focused on [dimension theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_theory).

- Between 1925 and 1928, Tumarkin proved that for topological spaces with countable base, the large and small [inductive dimensions](wikipedia://en/3173612) are equal: $\mathrm{Ind}\,X = \mathrm{ind}\,X$[^o9]
- He showed that any n-dimensional space with countable base can be represented as a union of $n+1$ pairwise disjoint zero-dimensional sets[^o9]
- **Hurewicz–Tumarkin theorem** (1927): Every n-dimensional [compact space](wikipedia://en/6042) contains an n-dimensional Cantor manifold (proved independently by [Witold Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385))[^o9]
- **Tumarkin's theorem** (1928): For any subset $M$ of a space $X$ with countable base, there exists a set $M'$ that is a [union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_%28mathematics%29) of [countably many](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_many) [closed sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_sets) in $X$ such that $M = M'$ and $\dim M' = \dim M$[^o10]
- In 1951, he proved that the [weight](wikipedia://en/30562#Weight) of any one-dimensional compact space equals either two or three.[^o11]
- In 1957, he demonstrated that every infinite-dimensional compact space either contains an infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold or contains compact sets of every finite dimension.[^o12]

### Tumarkin's problem {#Tumarkin's_problem}

In 1925, Tumarkin posed the following problem:

> **Tumarkin's problem:** *Does there exist an [infinite-dimensional](wikipedia://en/27908089) [compact set](wikipedia://en/6042) where every non-empty [closed subset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_subset) has dimension either [zero](wikipedia://en/517682) or infinity?*

The question remained open for over 40 years until [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) mathematician [David W. Henderson](wikipedia://en/49367356) provided a positive answer in 1967, showing that such "Tumarkin compacts" form a [dense set](wikipedia://en/23523594) in the space of all infinite-dimensional compact sets.[^o14][^o15]

[Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) mathematicians [Pavel Alexandrov](wikipedia://en/576245) and [Andrey Kolmogorov](wikipedia://en/91161) described his teaching as "the fruit of many years of creative work and finished with filigree thoroughness."[^o16][^o17] Mathematician [Vladimir Arnold](wikipedia://en/32490), one of Tumarkin's [calculus](wikipedia://en/5176) students, praised his teaching on Arnold's website.[^o18]
