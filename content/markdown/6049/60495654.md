# First International Topological Conference

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="First International Topological Conference"}

*Image: ETH-BIB-First International Topology Conference in Moscow-Portr 07561.tiff.* Attendees of the First International Topological Conference, Moscow, 4–10 September, 1935

The **first International Topological Conference** was held in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow), 4–10 September, 1935. With presentations by topologists from 10 different countries it constituted the first genuinely international meeting devoted to [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) in the world history of the mathematical community.[^o2] Although a previous mathematical conference had been held in [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv), and attended by [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675), this turned out to be the only truly international conference organised under the [Stalin regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_%281927–1953%29).[^o3] [Pavel Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Aleksandrov) played a key role in organising the conference.[^o2] The foreign delegates were accommodated in major hotels across Moscow, although according to [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019), the principal form of sustenance was Caviar Canapes served in the conference hall, as no food was available in the hotel restaurants.[^o3]

:::

## Presentations {#Presentations}

Documentation of the conference varies, but this summary was drawn from various sources.[^o2]

### Homology theory {#Homology_theory}

- Karol Borsuk: ‘‘On spheroidal spaces’’
- Eduard Čech: "Accessibility and Homology'"
- Israel Isaakovich Gordon: ‘‘On the intersection invariants of a complex and its residual space.’
- Solomon Lefschetz: ‘‘On locally connected sets.’’
