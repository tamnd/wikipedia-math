# Magnetic space group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Magnetic space group"}

In [solid state physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_state_physics), the **magnetic space groups**, or **[Shubnikov](wikipedia://en/70215429) groups**, are the [symmetry groups](wikipedia://en/28186) which classify the symmetries of a crystal both in space, and in a two-valued property such as [electron spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_spin). To represent such a property, each lattice point is colored black or white,[^o2] and in addition to the usual three-dimensional [symmetry operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_operation), there is a so-called "antisymmetry" operation which turns all black lattice points white and all white lattice points black. Thus, the magnetic space groups serve as an extension to the [crystallographic space groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallographic_space_group) which describe spatial symmetry alone.

:::

The application of magnetic space groups to crystal structures is motivated by [Curie's Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie's_Principle). Compatibility with a material's symmetries, as described by the magnetic space group, is a necessary condition for a variety of material properties, including [ferromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferromagnetism), [ferroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroelectricity), [topological insulation](wikipedia://en/23649871).

## History {#History}

A major step was the work of [Heinrich Heesch](wikipedia://en/1156248), who first rigorously established the concept of antisymmetry as part of a series of papers in 1929 and 1930.[^o4][^o5][^o6][^o7] Applying this antisymmetry operation to the 32 [crystallographic point groups](wikipedia://en/663786) gives a total of 122 magnetic point groups.[^o8][^o9] However, although Heesch correctly laid out each of the magnetic point groups, his work remained obscure, and the point groups were later re-derived by Tavger and Zaitsev.[^o10] The concept was more fully explored by Shubnikov in terms of [color symmetry](wikipedia://en/73498718).[^o11] When applied to space groups, the number increases from the usual 230 three dimensional space groups to 1651 magnetic space groups,[^o12] as found in the 1953 thesis of [Alexandr Zamorzaev](wikipedia://en/75549316).[^o13][^o14][^o15] While the magnetic space groups were originally found using geometry, it was later shown the same magnetic space groups can be found using [generating sets](wikipedia://en/99945).[^o16]

## Description {#Description}

### Magnetic space groups {#Magnetic_space_groups}

The magnetic space groups can be placed into three categories. First, the 230 colorless groups contain only spatial symmetry, and correspond to the crystallographic space groups. Then there are 230 grey groups, which are invariant under antisymmetry. Finally are the 1191 black-white groups, which contain the more complex symmetries. There are two common conventions for giving names to the magnetic space groups. They are Opechowski-Guccione (named after [Wladyslaw Opechowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wladyslaw_Opechowski) and Rosalia Guccione)[^o19] and Belov-Neronova-Smirnova (named after Nikolay Vasilyevich Belov, Nina Nikolaevna Neronova and Tamara Serafimovna Smirnova, then Kuntsevich).[^o20][^o12] For colorless and grey groups, the conventions use the same names, but they treat the black-white groups differently. A full list of the magnetic space groups (in both conventions) can be found both in the original papers, and in several places online.[^o21][^o22][^o23]

*Types of magnetic space groups[^o25]*

::: {#o24 .table cue=table name="Types of magnetic space groups"}

| Type | Name | Number of groups | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type I | Colorless groups | 230 | Ordinary [crystallographic space groups](wikipedia://en/463721), without any additional symmetry. |
| Type II | Grey groups | 230 | Space groups, with an additional anti-symmetry version of every [symmetry operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetry_operation). |
| Type III | Black-White groups (ordinary [Bravais lattices](wikipedia://en/661808)) | 674 | Space groups, with additional anti-symmetry versions of half of the symmetry operations. |
| Type IV | Black-White groups (black-white Bravais Lattices) | 517 | Space groups, with additional combined spatial translation-time reversal symmetry. |

:::

The types can be distinguished by their different construction.[^o25] Type I magnetic space groups, $\mathcal{M}_I$ are identical to the ordinary space groups,$G$.

$$\mathcal{M}_I=G$$

Type II magnetic space groups, $\mathcal{M}_{II}$, are made up of all the symmetry operations of the crystallographic space group, $G$, plus the product of those operations with time reversal operation, $\mathcal{T}$. Equivalently, this can be seen as the [direct product](wikipedia://en/3706015) of an ordinary space group with the point group $1'$.

$$\mathcal{M}_{II}=G + \mathcal{T}G$$

$$\mathcal{M}_{II}=G \times 1'$$

Type III magnetic space groups, $\mathcal{M}_{III}$, are constructed using a group $H$, which is a subgroup of $G$ with [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2.

$$\mathcal{M}_{III}=H + \mathcal{T} (G-H)$$

Type IV magnetic space groups, $\mathcal{M}_{IV}$, are constructed with the use of a pure [translation](wikipedia://en/204682), $\{E|t_0\}$, which is Seitz notation[^o26] for null rotation and a translation, $t_0$. Here the $t_0$ is a vector (usually given in [fractional coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_coordinates)) pointing from a black colored point to a white colored point, or vice versa.

$$\mathcal{M}_{IV}=G + \mathcal{T}\{E|t_0\}G$$

### Magnetic superspace groups {#Magnetic_superspace_groups}

When the periodicity of the magnetic order coincides with the periodicity of crystallographic order, the magnetic phase is said to be *commensurate*, and can be well-described by a magnetic space group. However, when this is not the case, the order does not correspond to any magnetic space group. These phases can instead be described by *magnetic superspace groups*, which describe *incommensurate* order.[^o48] This is the same formalism often used to describe the ordering of some [quasicrystals](wikipedia://en/25350).

## Phase transitions {#Phase_transitions}

The [Landau theory](wikipedia://en/995169) of second-order [phase transitions](wikipedia://en/54423) has been applied to magnetic phase transitions. The magnetic space group of disordered structure, $G_0$, transitions to the magnetic space group of the ordered phase, $G_1$. $G_1$ is a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of $G_0$, and keeps only the symmetries which have not been broken during the phase transition. This can be tracked numerically by evolution of the [order parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_parameter), which belongs to a single [irreducible representation](wikipedia://en/385982) of $G_0$.[^o50]

Important magnetic phase transitions include the paramagnetic to ferromagnetic transition at the [Curie temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curie_temperature) and the paramagnetic to [antiferromagnetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiferromagnetism) transition at the [Néel temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Néel_temperature). Differences in the magnetic phase transitions explain why [Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron%28III%29_oxide), [MnCO<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese%28II%29_carbonate), and [CoCO<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt%28II%29_carbonate) are weakly ferromagnetic, whereas the structurally similar [Cr<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromium%28III%29_oxide) and [FeCO<sub>3</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron%28II%29_carbonate) are purely antiferromagnetic.[^o51] This theory developed into what is now known as [antisymmetric exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisymmetric_exchange).

A related scheme is the classification of *Aizu species* which consist of a prototypical non-ferroic magnetic point group, the letter "F" for [ferroic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroics), and a ferromagnetic or ferroelectric point group which is a subgroup of the prototypical group which can be reached by continuous motion of the atoms in the [crystal structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_structure).[^o52][^o53]

## Applications and extensions {#Applications_and_extensions}

The main application of these space groups is to magnetic structure, where the black/white lattice points correspond to spin up/spin down configuration of [electron spin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_spin). More abstractly, the magnetic space groups are often thought of as representing [time reversal symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_reversal_symmetry).[^o55] This is in contrast to [time crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_crystal), which instead have [time translation symmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_translation_symmetry). In the most general form, magnetic space groups can represent symmetries of any two valued lattice point property, such as positive/negative electrical charge or the alignment of electric dipole moments. The magnetic space groups place restrictions on the [electronic band structure](wikipedia://en/706247) of materials. Specifically, they place restrictions on the connectivity of the different electron bands, which in turn defines whether material has [symmetry-protected topological order](wikipedia://en/36464530). Thus, the magnetic space groups can be used to identify topological materials, such as [topological insulators](wikipedia://en/23649871).[^o56][^o57][^o58]

Experimentally, the main source of information about magnetic space groups is [neutron diffraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_diffraction) experiments. The resulting experimental profile can be matched to theoretical structures by [Rietveld refinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rietveld_refinement)[^o59] or [simulated annealing](wikipedia://en/172244).[^o60]

Adding the two-valued symmetry is also a useful concept for [frieze groups](wikipedia://en/375503) which are often used to classify artistic patterns. In that case, the 7 frieze groups with the addition of color reversal become 24 color-reversing frieze groups.[^o61] Beyond the simple two-valued property, the idea has been extended further to two different types of antisymmetry ([multiple antisymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_antisymmetry)),[^o62] to three colors in three dimensions,[^o63] and to even higher dimensions and [more colors](wikipedia://en/73619197).[^o64]
