In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **rectified 120-cell** is a [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) formed as the [rectification](wikipedia://en/2192366) of the regular [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358).

:::

[E. L. Elte](wikipedia://en/27806942) identified it in 1912 as a semiregular polytope, labeling it as tC<sub>120</sub>.

There are four rectifications of the 120-cell, including the zeroth, the 120-cell itself. The birectified 120-cell is more easily seen as a rectified 600-cell, and the trirectified 120-cell is the same as the dual 600-cell.

*Image: Rectified hecatonicosachoron net.png.* [Net](wikipedia://en/1148129)

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **[rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) [120-cell](wikipedia://en/717358)** or **rectified hecatonicosachoron** is a convex [uniform 4-polytope](wikipedia://en/1645899) composed of 600 regular [tetrahedra](wikipedia://en/30606) and 120 [icosidodecahedra](wikipedia://en/15023) [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_%28mathematics%29). Its vertex figure is a [triangular prism](wikipedia://en/2859584), with three icosidodecahedra and two tetrahedra meeting at each vertex.

Alternative names:

- Rectified 120-cell ([Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527))
- Rectified hecatonicosichoron / rectified dodecacontachoron / rectified polydodecahedron
- Icosidodecahedral hexacosihecatonicosachoron
- Rahi (Jonathan Bowers: for rectified hecatonicosachoron)
- Ambohecatonicosachoron ([Neil Sloane & John Horton Conway](wikipedia://en/15807))
