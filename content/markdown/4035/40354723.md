# Structural complexity (applied mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Structural complexity"}

**Structural complexity** is a science of [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) that aims to relate fundamental physical or biological aspects of a [complex system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_system) with the mathematical description of the morphological complexity that the system exhibits, by establishing rigorous relations between mathematical and physical properties of such system.[^n1]

:::

Structural complexity emerges from all systems that display morphological organization.[^n2] Filamentary structures, for instance, are an example of [coherent structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_coherent_structures) that emerge, interact and evolve in many physical and biological systems, such as mass distribution in the [Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_of_the_universe), [vortex filaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vortex) in turbulent flows, [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_networks) in our brain and genetic material (such as [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA)) in a cell. In general information on the degree of morphological [disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_and_disorder_%28physics%29) present in the system tells us something important about fundamental physical or biological processes.

Structural complexity methods are based on applications of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) (and in particular [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008)) to interpret physical properties of [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems).[^n3][^n4] such as relations between [kinetic energy](wikipedia://en/17327) and tangles of vortex filaments in a turbulent flow or [magnetic energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_energy) and braiding of magnetic fields in the solar corona, including aspects of [topological fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_fluid_dynamics).
