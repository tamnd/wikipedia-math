# Ribbon (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ribbon"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **ribbon** (or **strip**) is the combination of a [smooth](wikipedia://en/1408000) [space curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_curve) and its corresponding [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector). More formally, a ribbon denoted by $(X,U)$ includes a curve $X$ given by a three-dimensional [vector](wikipedia://en/23218956) $X(s)$, depending continuously on the curve [arc-length](wikipedia://en/1898401) $s$ ($a\leq s \leq b$), and a unit vector $U(s)$ perpendicular to ${\partial X \over \partial s}(s)$ at each point.[^o2] Ribbons have seen particular application as regards [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA).[^o3]

:::

## Properties and implications {#Properties_and_implications}

The ribbon $(X,U)$ is called *simple* if $X$ is a [simple curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_curve) (i.e. without self-intersections) and *closed* and if $U$ and all its derivatives agree at $a$ and $b$. For any simple closed ribbon the curves $X+\varepsilon U$ given parametrically by $X(s)+\varepsilon U(s)$ are, for all sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, simple closed curves disjoint from $X$.

The ribbon concept plays an important role in the **Călugăreanu formula**,[^o5][^o6] that states that

$$Lk = Wr + Tw ,$$

where $Lk$ is the asymptotic (Gauss) *[linking number](wikipedia://en/451999)*, the integer number of turns of the ribbon around its axis; $Wr$ denotes the total *writhing number* (or simply *[writhe](wikipedia://en/968591)*), a measure of non-planarity of the ribbon's axis curve; and $Tw$ is the total *twist number* (or simply *[twist](wikipedia://en/40392210)*), the rate of rotation of the ribbon around its axis.

Ribbon theory investigates geometric and topological aspects of a mathematical reference ribbon associated with physical and biological properties, such as those arising in [topological fluid dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_fluid_dynamics), [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) modeling and in [material science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_science).
