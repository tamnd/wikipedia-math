# Artin–Hasse exponential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Artin–Hasse exponential"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically in [$p$-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327), the **Artin–Hasse exponential**, introduced by [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) and [Helmut Hasse](wikipedia://en/727118) in 1928,[^o2] is the [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) given by

:::

$$E_p(x) = \exp\left(x + \frac{x^p}{p} + \frac{x^{p^2}}{p^2} + \frac{x^{p^3}}{p^3} +\cdots\right).$$

## Motivation {#Motivation}

One motivation for considering this series to be analogous to the exponential function comes from infinite products. In the ring of formal power series $\mathbb{Q}[[x]]$ we have the identity

$$e^x = \prod_{n \geq 1}(1-x^n)^{-\mu(n)/n},$$

where $\mu$ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961). This identity can be verified by showing that the logarithmic derivatives of both sides are equal and that both sides have the same constant term. Similarly, one can verify a product expansion for the Artin–Hasse exponential:

$$E_p(x) = \prod_{(p,n)=1}(1-x^n)^{-\mu(n)/n}.$$

So passing from a product over all $n$ to a product over only $n$ [coprime](wikipedia://en/6556) to $p$, which is a typical operation in $p$-adic analysis, leads from $e^x$ to $E_p(x)$.

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The coefficients of $E_p(x)$ are rational. We can use either formula for $E_p(x)$ to prove that, unlike $e^x$, all of its coefficients are $p$-integral; in other words, the denominators of the coefficients of $E_p(x)$ are not divisible by $p$.

A first proof uses the definition of $E_p(x)$ and **Dwork's lemma**, which says that a power series $f(x)$ with rational coefficients has $p$-integral coefficients if and only if

$$\frac{f(x^p)}{f(x)^p}\equiv 1\mod p\mathbb{Z}_p[[x]].$$

When $f(x)=E_p(x)$, we have

$$\frac{f(x^p)}{f(x)^p} = e^{-px},$$

where the constant term is 1 and all higher coefficients are in $p\mathbb{Z}_p$.

A second proof comes from the [infinite product](wikipedia://en/26382006) for $E_p(x)$: each exponent $-\mu(n)/n$ for $n$ not divisible by $p$ is a $p$-integral, and when a [rational number](wikipedia://en/19727024) $a$ is $p$-integral, all coefficients in the binomial expansion of $(1-x^n)^a$ are $p$-integral by $p$-adic continuity of the [binomial coefficient](wikipedia://en/4668) polynomials

$$\frac{t!}{k!(t-k)!}$$

in $t$ together with their obvious integrality when $t$ is a nonnegative integer ($a$ is a $p$-adic limit of nonnegative integers). Thus, each factor in the product of $E_p(x)$ has $p$-integral coefficients, so $E_p(x)$ itself has $p$-integral coefficients.

The $p$-integral series expansion has [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) 1.

:::

## Combinatorial interpretation {#Combinatorial_interpretation}

The Artin–Hasse exponential is the [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for the probability that a uniformly randomly selected element of the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) $S_n$ has $p$-power order (the number of which is denoted by $t_{p,n}$):

$$E_p(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0} \frac{t_{p,n}}{n!}x^n.$$

This gives a third proof that the coefficients of $E_p(x)$ are $p$-integral, using the [theorem of Frobenius](wikipedia://en/48454377) that in a [finite group](wikipedia://en/323707) of order divisible by $d$ the number of elements of order dividing $d$ is also divisible by $d$. Apply this theorem to the $n$th symmetric group with $d$ equal to the highest power of $p$ dividing $n!$.

More generally, for any topologically finitely generated profinite group $G$ there is an identity

$$\exp\bigg(\sum_{H \subset G} x^{[G:H]}/[G:H]\bigg)=\sum_{n\ge 0} \frac{a_{G,n}}{n!}x^n,$$

where $H$ runs over open subgroups of $G$ of finite index (there are finitely many of each index since $G$ is topologically finitely generated) and $a_{G,n}$ is the number of continuous homomorphisms from $G$ to $S_n$.

Two special cases are worth noting. Firstly, if $G$ is the $p$-adic integers, it has exactly one open subgroup of each $p$-power index and a continuous homomorphism from $G$ to $S_n$ is essentially the same thing as choosing an element of $p$-power order in $S_n$, so we have recovered the above combinatorial interpretation of the Taylor coefficients in the Artin–Hasse exponential series.

Secondly, if $G$ is a finite group, then the sum in the exponential is a finite sum running over all subgroups of $G$, and continuous homomorphisms from $G$ to $S_n$ are simply homomorphisms from $G$ to $S_n$. The result in this case is due to Wohlfahrt.[^o7]

The special case when $G$ is a finite [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) is due to Chowla, Herstein, and Scott (1952), and takes the form

$$\exp\bigg(\sum_{d|m} x^d/d\bigg)=\sum_{n\ge 0} \frac{a_{m,n}}{n!}x^n,$$

where $a_{m,n}$ is the number of solutions to $g^m=1$ in *S<sub>n</sub>*.

David Roberts provided a natural combinatorial link between the Artin–Hasse exponential and the regular exponential in the spirit of the ergodic perspective (linking the $p$-adic and regular norms over the rationals) by showing that the Artin–Hasse exponential is also the generating function for the probability that an element of the symmetric group is [unipotent](wikipedia://en/1724478) in [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) $p$, whereas the regular exponential is the probability that an element of the same group is unipotent in characteristic zero.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=heading name="Conjectures"}

## Conjectures {#Conjectures}

At the 2002 [PROMYS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROMYS) program, Keith Conrad conjectured that the coefficients of $E_p(x)$ are uniformly distributed in the [$p$-adic](wikipedia://en/51423) integers with respect to the normalized [Haar measure](wikipedia://en/40645), with supporting computational evidence. The problem is still open.

Dinesh Thakur has also posed the problem of whether the Artin–Hasse exponential reduced mod $p$ is transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p(x)$.
