# Radical polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Radical polynomial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), in the realm of [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **radical polynomial** is a multivariate [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000)[^o2] over a field that can be expressed as a polynomial in the sum of squares of the variables. That is, if

:::

$$k[x_1, x_2,\ldots, x_n]$$

is a [polynomial ring](wikipedia://en/373065), the ring of radical polynomials is the subring generated by the polynomial[^o3]

$$\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2.$$

Radical polynomials are characterized as precisely those polynomials that are [invariant](wikipedia://en/1126638) under the action of the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954).

The ring of radical polynomials is a [graded subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) of the ring of all polynomials.

The standard [separation of variables theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_variables_theorem) asserts that every polynomial can be expressed as a finite sum of terms, each term being a product of a radical polynomial and a [harmonic polynomial](wikipedia://en/5755288). This is equivalent to the statement that the ring of all polynomials is a [free module](wikipedia://en/326454) over the ring of radical polynomials.
