# Canonical correspondence analysis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canonical correspondence analysis"}

In multivariate analysis, **canonical correspondence analysis** (**CCA**) is an [ordination](wikipedia://en/2139778) technique that determines axes from the response data as a unimodal combination of measured predictors. CCA is commonly used in [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) in order to extract gradients that drive the composition of ecological communities. CCA extends correspondence analysis (CA) with regression, in order to incorporate predictor variables.

:::

## History {#History}

CCA was developed in 1986 by Cajo ter Braak [^o3] and implemented in the program CANOCO, an extension of DECORANA.[^o4] To date, CCA is one of the most popular multivariate methods in ecology, despite the availability of contemporary alternatives.[^o5] CCA was originally derived and implemented using an algorithm of weighted averaging, though Legendre & Legendre (1998) derived an alternative algorithm.[^o6]

## Assumptions {#Assumptions}

The requirements of a CCA are that the samples are random and independent. Also, the data are [categorical](wikipedia://en/821165) and that the [independent variables](wikipedia://en/437701) are consistent within the sample site and error-free.[^o8] The original publication states the need for equal species tolerances, equal species maxima, and equispaced or uniformly distributed species optima and site scores.[^o3]
