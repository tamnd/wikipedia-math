# David R. Morrison (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="David R. Morrison"}

**David Robert Morrison** (born July 29, 1955, in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California)) is an American mathematician and theoretical physicist. He works on [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), especially its relations to theoretical physics.

:::

Morrison studied at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) with bachelor's degree in 1976 and at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) with master's degree in 1977 and PhD under [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414) in 1980 with thesis *Semistable Degenerations of Enriques' and Hyperelliptic Surfaces*. From 1980 he was an instructor and from 1982 an assistant professor at Princeton University and in the academic year 1984–1985 a visiting scientist at the [University of Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kyoto) (as Fellow der Japan Society for the Promotion of Science). In 1986 he became an associate professor and in 1992 a professor of mathematics at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) and then in 1997 "James B. Duke Professor of Mathematics and Physics". While at Duke, Morrison advised multiple PhD students, including [Antonella Grassi](wikipedia://en/55728075) and [Carina Curto](wikipedia://en/58042119).[^o2][^o3] Since 2006 he has been a professor at the [University of California, Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Santa_Barbara).

Although Morrison began his career as a mathematician in classical algebraic geometry, in his later career he has also been a string theorist. He works on the interfaces and mutual fertilization of algebraic geometry and string theory, especially [mirror symmetry](wikipedia://en/644671).

In 1992–1993, 1996–1997 and 2000 he was at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). In 1995 he was a visiting professor at [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University), in 2005 at the [Kavli Institute for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavli_Institute_for_Theoretical_Physics), and in 2006 a research professor at [MSRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Sciences_Research_Institute).

In 2015 he became a member of the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), in 2013 a Fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), in 2005 a Senior Scholar at the [Clay Mathematics Institute](wikipedia://en/7655) and in 2005–2006 a [Guggenheim Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellow).

He is a co-editor of selected works of his thesis supervisor [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414).

Morrison was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in Zürich in 1994 (*Mirror Symmetry and Moduli Spaces of Superconformal Field Theories*).
