# J. Malcolm Bird

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="J. Malcolm Bird"}

**James Malcolm Bird** (September 2, 1886 – October 30, 1964) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [parapsychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapsychologist).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Bird was born in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) to James Gedney Bird and Eliza (Baltz) Bird on September 2, 1886.[^o4] He trained in mathematics and taught as a Professor at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), he later became an associate editor for the *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)*, upon quitting in 1925 he became the research officer of the [American Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_for_Psychical_Research) (ASPR) from 1925–1931.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

Bird investigated spiritualist mediums such as [Mina Crandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Crandon), [John C. Sloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sloan_%28medium%29), [Gladys Osborne Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladys_Osborne_Leonard), [William Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hope_%28paranormal_investigator%29) and [Maria Vollhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Vollhardt).[^o8] His experiences are mentioned in his book *My Psychic Adventures* (1924). Bird has drawn criticism from magician [Harry Houdini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini) and the psychical researcher [Walter Franklin Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Franklin_Prince) for his conduct in the investigation of Mina Crandon. Houdini and Prince strongly suspected that Crandon was fraudulent, but Bird had endorsed some of her phenomena as genuine.[^o9][^o10][^o11][^o12] In December, 1930 Bird was compelled to resign from the ASPR after he admitted he had known of fraudulent activity regarding Crandon from the start.[^o13]

Walter Franklin Prince considered Bird "totally unreliable".[^o14] The [Society for Psychical Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_Psychical_Research)'s Honorary Research Officer V. J. Woolley noted that Bird was an inaccurate reporter, he had made factual errors about a séance sitting in 1923.[^o15] Historian [Ruth Brandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Brandon) has described Bird as a biased and unreliable witness.[^o16] More recently, authors William Kalush and [Larry Sloman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Sloman) have suggested that Bird had conspired with Crandon in "stage managing the séances and achieving a positive vote from the majority of the committee."[^o17] Bird died October 30, 1964.[^o18]
