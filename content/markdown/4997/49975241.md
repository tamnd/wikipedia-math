# Lorenzo Ramero

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lorenzo Ramero"}

**Lorenzo Ramero** is an Italian mathematician living in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), specialized in algebraic and [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177). He is currently a professor of mathematics at the [University of Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lille).

:::

Ramero obtained his Laurea in Matematica from the [University of Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pisa) and his Diploma from the [Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Normale_Superiore_di_Pisa) in 1989. He completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1994 under the supervision of [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613), with a thesis titled *An $\ell$-adic Fourier transform over local fields*.

Together with [Ofer Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofer_Gabber), Ramero developed the [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) based on [almost rings](wikipedia://en/39286332) extending earlier ideas of [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) on "almost mathematics". This theory extends already classical algebraic geometry formalism of [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042)'s school in order to treat new phenomena in [p-adic Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/26086853). This work is systematized in their monograph *Almost ring theory*.[^o3]

More foundational material was developed after the first book, and especially an extended theory of [perfectoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfectoid) rings and [perfectoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfectoid) spaces which generalizes the recent work of [Peter Scholze](wikipedia://en/39409306). These aspects were recapitulated in the book "Foundations for Almost Ring Theory".[^o4]

Ramero has authored a textbook on commutative algebra, *Grimoire d'Algèbre Commutative* (in French), emphasizing the numerous connections and interactions between commutative algebra and other areas and branches of mathematics.[^o5][^o6]
