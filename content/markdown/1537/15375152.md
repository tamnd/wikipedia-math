# Brian Conrey

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Brian Conrey"}

**John Brian Conrey**[^o3] (June 23, 1955) is an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans) mathematician and the executive director of the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005). His research interests are in [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), specifically analysis of [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) and the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

:::

## Education {#Education}

Conrey received his B.S. from [Santa Clara University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_University) in 1976 and received his Ph.D. at the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1980 under the supervision of [Hugh Lowell Montgomery](wikipedia://en/2984701).[^o5]

## Career {#Career}

Conrey is the founding executive director of the [American Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/3248005), a position he has held since 1997.[^o8][^o9] Since 2005, he has been part-time professor at the [University of Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bristol), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England).[^o10]

He is on the editorial board of the [Journal of Number Theory](wikipedia://en/8422003).

## Research {#Research}

With Bui and Young, Conrey proved in 2011 that more than 41 percent of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are on the critical line.[^o12]

With [Jonathan Keating](wikipedia://en/46796537), [Nina Snaith](wikipedia://en/9277265), and others, Conrey researched correlations of eigenvalues of random unitary matrices and Riemann zeta zeros.[^o13]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

The [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822) jointly awarded him the eighth annual [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) for [expository writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expository_writing) in 2008 for *[The Riemann Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_Hypothesis)*.[^o10][^o15] In 2015 he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Mathematical_Society).[^o16]
