# BCMP network

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="BCMP network"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), a **BCMP network** is a class of [queueing network](wikipedia://en/50578) for which a [product-form equilibrium distribution](wikipedia://en/21254527) exists. It is named after the authors of the paper where the network was first described: [Baskett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Baskett), [Chandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Mani_Chandy), Muntz, and Palacios. The theorem is a significant extension to a [Jackson network](wikipedia://en/3374374) allowing virtually arbitrary customer routing and service time distributions, subject to particular service disciplines.[^o2]

:::

The paper is well known, and the theorem was described in 1990 as "one of the seminal achievements in queueing theory in the last 20 years" by [J. Michael Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Michael_Harrison) and [Ruth J. Williams](wikipedia://en/44832164).[^o3]

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A network of *m* interconnected queues is known as a **BCMP network** if each of the queues is of one of the following four types:

1. [FCFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-come,_first-served) discipline where all customers have the same [negative exponential](wikipedia://en/45906) service time distribution. The service rate can be state dependent, so write $\scriptstyle{\mu_j}$ for the service rate when the queue length is *j*.
1. Processor sharing queues
1. Infinite-server queues
1. [LCFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LIFO_%28computing%29) with pre-emptive resume (work is not lost)

In the final three cases, service time distributions must have [rational](wikipedia://en/361210) [Laplace transforms](wikipedia://en/18610). This means the Laplace transform must be of the form[^o6]

$$L(s) = \frac{N(s)}{D(s)}.$$

Also, the following conditions must be met.

1. external arrivals to node *i* (if any) form a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process),
1. a customer completing service at queue *i* will either move to some new queue *j* with (fixed) probability $P_{ij}$ or leave the system with probability $1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}P_{ij}$, which is non-zero for some subset of the queues.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

## Theorem {#Theorem}

For a BCMP network of *m* queues which is open, closed or mixed in which each queue is of type 1, 2, 3 or 4, the equilibrium state probabilities are given by

$$\pi(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_m) = C \pi_1(x_1) \pi_2(x_2) \cdots \pi_m(x_m),$$

where *C* is a normalizing constant chosen to make the equilibrium state probabilities sum to 1 and $\scriptstyle{\pi_i(\cdot)}$ represents the equilibrium distribution for queue *i*.

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

The original proof of the theorem was given by checking the [independent balance equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_balance_equation) were satisfied.

[Peter G. Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_G._Harrison) offered an alternative proof[^o11] by considering reversed processes.[^o12]
