# Asymptotic computational complexity

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Asymptotic computational complexity"}

In [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543), **asymptotic computational complexity** is the use of [asymptotic analysis](wikipedia://en/641995) for the estimation of the [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/6511) of [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and [computational problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_problem), commonly associated with the use of the [big *O* notation](wikipedia://en/44578).

:::

## Scope {#Scope}

With respect to [computational resources](wikipedia://en/4628222), **asymptotic [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944)** and **asymptotic [space complexity](wikipedia://en/338946)** of computational algorithms and programs are commonly estimated. Other asymptotically estimated behavior include [circuit complexity](wikipedia://en/7641969) and various measures of [parallel computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_computation), such as the number of (parallel) processors.

Since the ground-breaking 1965 paper by [Juris Hartmanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Hartmanis) and [Richard E. Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_E._Stearns)[^o3] and the 1979 book by [Michael Garey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Garey) and [David S. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_S._Johnson) on [NP-completeness](wikipedia://en/23385892),[^o4] the term "[computational complexity](wikipedia://en/2230)" (of algorithms) has become commonly used to refer to asymptotic computational complexity.

Further, unless specified otherwise, the term "computational complexity" usually refers to an [upper bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound) for the asymptotic computational complexity of an algorithm or a problem, which is usually written in terms of the big *O* notation, e.g. $O(n^3).$ Other types of (asymptotic) computational complexity estimates are [lower bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) ("[big omega](wikipedia://en/44578)" notation; e.g., Ω(*n*)) and asymptotically tight estimates, when the asymptotic upper and lower bounds coincide (written using the "[big theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_theta)"; e.g., Θ(*n* log *n*)).

A further [tacit assumption](wikipedia://en/3240770) is that the [worst-case complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worst-case_complexity) is in question unless stated otherwise. An alternative approach is [probabilistic analysis of algorithms](wikipedia://en/15383889).

## Types of algorithms considered {#Types_of_algorithms_considered}

In most practical cases [deterministic algorithms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deterministic_algorithm) or [randomized algorithms](wikipedia://en/495383) are discussed, although [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392) also considers [nondeterministic algorithms](wikipedia://en/665957) and other advanced [models of computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_computation).
