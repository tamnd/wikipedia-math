# Rules of passage

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rules of passage"}

In [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636), the **rules of passage** govern how [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260) distribute over the basic [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983). The rules of passage govern the "passage" (translation) from any [formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_%28mathematical_logic%29) of first-order logic to the [equivalent](wikipedia://en/169324) formula in [prenex normal form](wikipedia://en/421068), and vice versa.

:::

## The rules {#The_rules}

See Quine (1982: 119, chpt. 23). Let *Q* and *Q**denote ∀ and ∃ or vice versa. β denotes a closed formula in which*****x*does not appear. The rules of passage then include the following sentences, whose main connective is the [biconditional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biconditional):***

- $$Qx[\lnot\alpha (x)] \leftrightarrow \lnot Q'x[\alpha (x)].$$
- $$\ Qx[\beta \lor \alpha (x)] \leftrightarrow (\beta \lor Qx \alpha (x)).$$
- $$\exists x[\alpha (x) \lor \gamma (x)] \leftrightarrow (\exists x \alpha (x) \lor \exists x \gamma (x)).$$
- $$\ Qx[\beta \land \alpha (x)] \leftrightarrow (\beta \land Qx \alpha (x)).$$
- $$\forall x \, [\alpha(x) \land \gamma(x)] \leftrightarrow (\forall x \, \alpha(x) \land \forall x \, \gamma(x) ).$$

The following conditional sentences can also be taken as rules of passage:

- $$\exists x[\alpha (x) \land \gamma (x)] \rightarrow (\exists x \alpha (x) \land \exists x \gamma (x)).$$
- $$(\forall x \, \alpha(x) \lor \forall x \, \gamma(x)) \rightarrow \forall x \, [\alpha(x) \lor \gamma(x)].$$
- $$(\exists x \, \alpha(x) \land \forall x \, \gamma(x)) \rightarrow \exists x \, [\alpha(x) \land \gamma(x)].$$

"Rules of passage" first appeared in French, in the writings of [Jacques Herbrand](wikipedia://en/159901). [Quine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Quine) employed the English translation of the phrase in each edition of his *Methods of Logic*, starting in 1950.
