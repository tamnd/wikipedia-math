# Constantin Rădulescu-Motru

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Constantin Rădulescu-Motru"}

*Image: RadulescuMotru.PNG.* Constantin Rădulescu-Motru

**Constantin Rădulescu-Motru** (; born **Constantin Rădulescu**, he added the surname *[Motru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motru)* in 1892; February 15, 1868 – March 6, 1957) was a [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania) philosopher, psychologist, sociologist, [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), academic, dramatist, as well as [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_politics)-[nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) politician. A [member of the Romanian Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) after 1923, he was its vice president in 1935–1938, 1941–1944, and its president between 1938 and 1941.

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

*Image: Butoiesti.casa Radutesti.jpg.* The Rădulescu-Motru memorial house in [Butoiești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butoiești)

Born in [Butoiești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butoiești), [Mehedinți County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehedinți_County), he was the son of Radu Poppescu, whose natural father was [Eufrosin Poteca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eufrosin_Poteca),[^o3] and Judita Butoi. His mother died during childbirth, and Radu Poppescu married Ecaterina Cernăianu, who gave birth to Constantin's nine half-siblings.

During his childhood, Constantin fell ill with [malaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaria). He also fractured a leg, resulting in a permanent physical impediment.

Radu Poppescu, who worked as a secretary for Poteca for part of his life, inherited a certain sum after the death of his employer and father; this was to take the form of a [scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarship) for Constantin Rădulescu. He ultimately refused to make use of it, indicating that he would use instead revenue from his [estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_%28land%29) in Butoiești; the scholarship was eventually awarded to [Gheorghe Țițeica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gheorghe_Țițeica), the renowned mathematician.

In 1885, he graduated from [Carol I High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_I_National_College) in [Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova), and subsequently entered the [University of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bucharest), applying to both its Faculty of Law and Faculty of Letters. He was taught by [Titu Maiorescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titu_Maiorescu), who was to become his mentor, and attended lectures by [Constantin Dimitrescu-Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Dimitrescu-Iași), [V. A. Urechia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._A._Urechia), [Grigore Tocilescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigore_Tocilescu), and [Bogdan Petriceicu Hasdeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Petriceicu_Hasdeu). He was part of the last generation of [intellectuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) to participate in the activities of the *[Junimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junimea)* [literary society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_society) (where Maiorescu had endured as the major influence).[^o4]

Rădulescu-Motru was awarded a law degree *[magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude)* in 1888, and passed his philosophy exam the following year.

## Studies abroad {#Studies_abroad}

In the summer of 1889, he accompanied Maiorescu on a trip to [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), and [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland), notably visiting the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna) and the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität_München), establishing contacts with German academics. This brought his inclusion into the last stage of a program initiated by Maiorescu as the [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania)'s Minister of Education: alongside other important cultural and scientific figures (such as [Alexandru A. Philippide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_A._Philippide), [Ștefan Zeletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ștefan_Zeletin), [Ion Rădulescu-Pogoneanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Rădulescu-Pogoneanu), [Iorgu Iordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iorgu_Iordan), [Simion Mehedinți](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simion_Mehedinți), [Nicolae Bănescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Bănescu), [P. P. Negulescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._P._Negulescu), [Teohari Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teohari_Antonescu), and [Constantin Litzica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Litzica)), he was given official assistance in order complete his education abroad (in order to provide Romania with a new generation of academics).[^o6] Initially, he directed his interest towards studies in [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France), attending [Henry Beaunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Beaunis)' lectures in psychology at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) during the fall of 1899.[^o7]

Between 1890 and 1893, Rădulescu settled in Germany – he lived in [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich) and studied at the university for one semester (as a student of [Carl Stumpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Stumpf)), and then moved to [Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig), where he began working in the laboratory of [Wilhelm Wundt](wikipedia://en/34128) at the [local university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig). While completing his training with Wundt, he attended classes in physics, [physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [psychiatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatry), and mathematics, as well as [Gustav Weigand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Weigand)'s lectures in [Romanian philology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language). He married a German woman, who later refused to accompany him back to Romania; they eventually divorced. He took his [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1893, with a thesis on [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant)'s philosophy (*Zur Entwickelung von Kant's Theorie der Naturkausalität*), one notably quoted by [Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson) in his *[Introduction à la Métaphysique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduction_to_Metaphysics_%28Bergson%29)*.

## Academic and political career {#Academic_and_political_career}

After 1897, he served on the editorial board of [Spiru Haret](wikipedia://en/1194762)'s *Albina* [popular science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_science) magazine.[^o9] On January 1, 1900, he also founded and edited *[Noua Revistă Română](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noua_Revistă_Română)* (which published articles by, among others, [Nicolae Iorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Iorga), [Ion Luca Caragiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Luca_Caragiale), [George Coşbuc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Coşbuc), [Lazăr Şăineanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazăr_Şăineanu), [Ioan Nădejde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioan_Nădejde), [Ovid Densusianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid_Densusianu), [H. Sanielevici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Sanielevici), and [Garabet Ibrăileanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garabet_Ibrăileanu)).[^o10] Appointed to the chair of Philosophy at the University of Bucharest in 1906 (after three years of employment at the cultural foundation created by [King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Romania) [Carol I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_I_of_Romania)),[^o11] he was also the founder of the review *Studii filosofice* (later renamed *Revista de filosofie*), and, in 1918, became head of the [National Theatre Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_Bucharest).

In 1923, Rădulescu-Motru joined [Virgil Madgearu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_Madgearu), [Constantin Costa-Foru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Costa-Foru), [Victor Eftimiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Eftimiu), [Grigore Iunian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigore_Iunian), [Radu D. Rosetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radu_D._Rosetti), [Dem I. Dobrescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dem_I._Dobrescu), and the [socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) [Constantin Titel Petrescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Titel_Petrescu), [Nicolae L. Lupu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_L._Lupu), and [Constantin Mille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Mille), in creating *Liga Drepturilor Omului* (the League for [Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights)), protesting against measures taken by the [National Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_%28Romania,_1875%29) cabinet of [Ion I. C. Brătianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_I._C._Brătianu) in dealing with left-wing opposition forces.[^o12] In 1925, Rădulescu-Motru, [Nicolae Basilescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Basilescu), and [Traian Bratu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_Bratu) were part of a government-appointed committee investigating the roles of [A. C. Cuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._C._Cuza) and [Corneliu Șumuleanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corneliu_Șumuleanu) in the [anti-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism) violence having occurred at the [University of Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University) in 1923–1925.[^o13]

Initially a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism),[^o14] he became active inside the newly created [National Peasants' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Peasants'_Party) towards the end of the 1920s,[^o15] and adapted the group's advocacy of a peasant state that would favour small-scale agricultural property (an echo of [Poporanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poporanism)), while taking a more [centrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrism) stand than his friend Madgearu.[^o16] A member of the party's Study Circle, he took part in drafting a new party program (a 1935 initiative taken by [Ion Mihalache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Mihalache) and [Mihai Ralea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Ralea), it also involved the left-wingers [Ernest Ene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Ene), [Mihail Ghelmegeanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihail_Ghelmegeanu), and [Petre Andrei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petre_Andrei)).[^o14]

It was at this time that his ideas on [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity) (*Romanianism*) came to be debated by various figures on the [Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-wing_politics), and were the subject of virulent criticism from [intellectuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) sympathetic to the [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) [Iron Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Guard), who notably rejected his commitment to [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) and Maiorescu's *[Junimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junimea)* tradition ([Mircea Vulcănescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Vulcănescu) spoke against "his hostile attitude, shared by his *Junimist* colleagues, against the penetration of a new, religious spirit, inside the University \[of Bucharest\]"),[^o17] as well as from the nationalist [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) [Lucian Blaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian_Blaga).[^o18] According to a later assessment of his work by Vulcănescu, who had since become influenced by the centrist National Peasants' Party member [Dimitrie Gusti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Gusti), the latter's outlook on [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology) was also in disagreement with Rădulescu-Motru's adherence to *Junimist* guidelines.[^o19]

Toward the end of the 1930s, Rădulescu-Motru was involved in a dispute with the [far right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_right) philosopher [Nae Ionescu](wikipedia://en/3818149), who, although appointed his assistant at the Philosophy department, had begun to criticize his views in the pro-Iron Guard journal *[Cuvântul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvântul)*; writing to [Mircea Eliade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Eliade) in 1938, he accused Ionescu of various unacademic practices, including using lectures on Logic to promote "a sort of dilettante [mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism)".[^o20]

The President of the academy at the moment when [Carol II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania) assumed dictatorial powers, he chose to support the new [National Renaissance Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renaissance_Front) (FRN) regime, and moved away from party politics.[^o21] He remained in office after Carol's fall from power and the establishment of the Iron Guard's [National Legionary State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Legionary_State) government; in the autumn of 1940, as Madgearu and [Nicolae Iorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Iorga), who had been assassinated by the Guard's armed groups, were being buried, he led the delegation of academy members who defied the policies of [Horia Sima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horia_Sima) by attending the funeral.[^o22] During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he was supportive of the dictatorship of [Ion Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Antonescu) and Romania's alliance with [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). As late as 1944 he abhorred the attempts of the Romanian opposition to negotiate the country's switch to the camp of the [United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II). Even after the war, in his personal journals he continued to adopt a position sympathetic to the Romanian and Nazi [war criminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_criminal), dismissing the [Nuremberg Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Trials) and the [People's Tribunals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_People's_Tribunals).[^o23]

## Communist persecution {#Communist_persecution}

Critical of Romania's exit from the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) (*see [Romania during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_during_World_War_II)*),[^o23] he was alarmed by the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) advances and the [eventual occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Romania); in his private notes, he deplored the fact that Romania failed to adopt the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan).[^o25] In June 1948, six months after the establishment of a [communist regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Romania), Rădulescu-Motru was among [the members of the academy purged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_purged_members_of_the_Romanian_Academy) by the new authorities (he was reinstated post-mortem in 1990).[^o26] Despite his protests, his entire work was dismissed by official [Stalinist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism) rhetoric as "[idealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism)".[^o27]

He was denied employment in his field of expertise, until two years before his death, when he was admitted to the minor post of researcher at the Psychology Institute; according to his biographer N. Bagdasar, his final years were marked by extreme poverty.[^o27] Granted assistance following the interventions of [Miron Constantinescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miron_Constantinescu) and [Constantin Ion Parhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Ion_Parhon), Rădulescu-Motru was hospitalized in a [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest) clinic for much of his final years.[^o27] He died while in there, and was buried in [Bellu cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellu_cemetery).

## Philosophy and politics {#Philosophy_and_politics}

### Outlook {#Outlook}

Influenced by [Wilhelm Wundt](wikipedia://en/34128)'s theories on [introspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introspection), Rădulescu-Motru moved away from [Kantian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantianism) philosophy and its tenet regarding the impossibility of transcending reality as perceived through the senses. He considered [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) to be open to [objective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistemology) scrutiny, and placed their knowledge at the summit of philosophical approaches.[^o30]

Stating that there was, in effect, a unity between [person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person) and material nature, Rădulescu-Motru developed his own version of [Personalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personalism), which thought of the human being and its [personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_psychology) as the goal of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) in nature – a theory he called *Energetic Personalism*.[^o31] He recognized the influence of [Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel](wikipedia://en/12598)'s views on the relation between [being](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being) and the [whole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holism), arguing that his [idealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idealism) concept of [externalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externalization_%28psychology%29) ("the belief that the world is being led by our ideal",[^o32] which he deemed "transitory Personalism")[^o33] had been the driving force behind all modern ideologies, from [Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) and [Anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism) to [Liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) (*see [Right Hegelians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Hegelians) and [Left Hegelians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Hegelians)*).[^o34]

Owing to Wundt's *[Völkerpsychologie](wikipedia://en/41358018)*, Rădulescu-Motru dedicated much of his work to assessing and defining [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) in Romanian social context. Concentrating his analysis on the impact of [modernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernization) and [Westernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization), he argued for a need to adapt forms to the [Romanian ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) (which he defined through [heredity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity)),[^o35] and represented as the true social fundament (the "community of spirit").[^o36] He supported the existence of [human races and differences among them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), as well as [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics), even after the defeat of Nazi Germany led to the abandonment of such theories in the mainstream scientific world.[^o23]

### Views on modernization {#Views_on_modernization}

In his *Cultura română şi politicianismul* ("Romanian culture and petty politics"), he devised a [hierarchy](wikipedia://en/13998) of cultures, placing [Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world) at the top of the scale, and the [Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East) at its bottom;[^o38] he later confessed to [Mircea Eliade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Eliade) his reticence in dealing with [Hindu philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_philosophy) (conversations between the two centered instead on [Indian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_nationalism) in general and *[Satyagraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyagraha)* in particular).[^o39] The system placed Romania in the margin of European progress, still subject to adopting cultural forms from Western societies:

> "Nowhere have [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois) institutions stemmed out of natural spiritual needs of the peoples, but rather out of the needs of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism); the complete harmonizing of these institutions with the popular [psyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyche_%28psychology%29) was a gradual, difficult enterprise, the more so in our country, where the dazzling development of capitalism has left the spiritual evolution, always more laborious, far behind."[^o40]

Elsewhere, he argued that, despite a traditional pattern of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism), Romanians lacked "[initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiative_%28enterprise%29) in economic and social life, the two characteristics traits of individualism as experienced by the cultured [Western peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_world) and constituting bourgeois spirit";[^o41] according to him, the common folk relied on collective work, which had ensured the survival of village communities during "cursed centuries"[^o41] (maintaining the mobility of villages "from plain to mountains",[^o41] but preventing their actual breakup during the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_in_the_Middle_Ages)).

In his chapters on the [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), the [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), and the [Romanian Constitution of 1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Constitution_of_Romania), Rădulescu-Motru expanded on this fundamentally [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism) thesis, arguing that such reforms had come too soon to be properly integrated.[^o42] The main danger he saw in the process was the appearance of "petty politics" (*politicianism*), which, he argued, had a potential of destroying natural developments inside the nation.[^o42]

Taking in view the characteristics of this evolution towards petty politics, he vehemently rejected [Mihai Eminescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Eminescu)'s theory on the almost exclusively foreign origin of the bourgeoisie inside the post-[Phanariote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phanariotes) [Old Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Old_Kingdom).[^o43] Instead, Rădulescu-Motru expanded on [Titu Maiorescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titu_Maiorescu)'s criticism of "forms without fundament" (in reference to the discrepancy between the Westernized facade and the underdeveloped economic and social setting), viewing the class of low-ranking [boyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars), increasingly attracted to [Liberal currents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_radicalism_in_Romania) during the 19th century, as the main agents of incoherent change – this attracted him the criticism of the [Poporanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poporanism) [Garabet Ibrăileanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garabet_Ibrăileanu), who argued that *[Junimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junimea)* was an [exclusivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusivism) school of criticism that "has never said \[of foreign models\] what, how much, and when should be imported".[^o44] In this context, Ibrăileanu emphasized the gestures of boyars before and during the *[Organic Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulamentul_Organic)* government, as indicative of a skeptical nationalist mood (rather than of a [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism) ideology).[^o45]

### *Romanianism* and secularism {#Romanianism_and_secularism}

With *Învăţământul filosofic în România* ("Philosophical education in Romania"), his 1931 essay first published in *[Convorbiri Literare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convorbiri_Literare)*, Rădulescu-Motru introduced a polemic that was to mark numerous other writings of his during the following period: reacting to the growth in appeal of [far right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_right) magazines that claimed to follow a [Romanian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Orthodox_Church) philosophy – *[Cuvântul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvântul)* and *[Gândirea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gândirea)* –, he made a difference between a "[belletristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belles-lettres)" trend in higher learning and a "scientific" one, arguing in favour of the latter, and presenting the former as the objective source of [anti-intellectualist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-intellectualism) attitudes he observed inside the new political phenomenon[^o47] (which emphasized the "human need for mystery").[^o48] In essence, the [secularist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) Rădulescu-Motru followed the *Junimea* tradition of rejecting [mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism), viewing it as the unwanted characteristic of a [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) mentality.[^o35]

He questioned the [subjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subjectivism) approaches of [Lucian Blaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucian_Blaga), [Nichifor Crainic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nichifor_Crainic), and [Nae Ionescu](wikipedia://en/3818149): developing his *Romanianism*, Rădulescu-Motru stated his support for cultural and national dialogue ("and not the isolation of each people in its own ethnicity"),[^o49] and for the ultimate integration of Romanian culture in the highest section of European culture.[^o50] He even argued that principles supported by the Right in defining Romanian specificity were in fact being shared with other cultures (answering Blaga's emphasis on [Romanian folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_folklore), he pointed out that its themes were commonplace in neighbouring [Balkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkans) cultures;[^o51] replying to Ionescu's views on allegedly particular tendencies toward [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) and [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) inside national culture, he stated his belief that "the prestige of the [metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphor), the attraction towards mystery and the [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) of the [ethnos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) \[...\] only show themselves from the second quarter of the 20th century onwards, \[and are under the influence of\] foreign university circles \[...\]";[^o49] he also rejected Crainic's views on Orthodoxy as the source for specificity, arguing for [Christian universalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_world_religions) in detriment of "nationalist spirituality"[^o52] – an idea nevertheless interpreted by Crainic as evidence of "militant philosophical [atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism)").[^o53]

### Peasant doctrine {#Peasant_doctrine}

After joining the [National Peasants' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Peasants'_Party), Rădulescu-Motru maintained a particular approach towards the group's doctrines and policies after 1935: adapting his criticism of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism) (a trait he associated with the [National Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberal_Party_%28Romania,_1875%29)) to the [Poporanist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poporanism) doctrine of the "peasant state", he defined the latter as necessarily "[totalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarianism)":

> "\[It\] differs from other totalitarian states in that it sets among its norms, first and foremost, the preeminence of the permanent interests of the peasant population. Unlike the [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_%281861–1946%29), the [National-Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany), and the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) states, it believes that it can serve the totality of the population it is supposed to govern, not on the basis of a glorious [imperialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism) tradition, or through the cultivation of a [race relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryan_race), or through industrial structuring on the basis of [a dictatorial plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_five-year_plan_%28Soviet_Union%29), but through the creation of a healthy, moral, and labouring peasantry, ready to defend the country's borders as the geographical and historical conditions demand it."[^o55]

A pro-[authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) critic soon reproached that such an ideal, despite its aim to compete with purely nationalist trends, was in fact [social class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class)-based, and its "numerical, that is to say [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy)" definition (around the argument that peasants formed the majority in Romania) was leading to "peasant [anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomie)".[^o56]

Rădulescu-Motru came to support [Carol II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania)'s [National Renaissance Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renaissance_Front) (FRN) and the [one-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_state) in 1938, speaking out in favour of the kings' initiative to introduce [uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform) for members of the academy (clashing over the matter with his fellow academic [Nicolae Iorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Iorga), in February 1939).[^o21]
