# Okubo algebra

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Okubo algebra"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), an **Okubo algebra** or **pseudo-octonion algebra** is an 8-dimensional [non-associative algebra](wikipedia://en/191788) similar to the one studied by [Susumu Okubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susumu_Okubo).[^o2] Okubo algebras are [composition algebras](wikipedia://en/3861635), [flexible algebras](wikipedia://en/36035264) $A(BA) = (AB)A$, [Lie admissible algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_admissible_algebra), and [power associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_associative), but are not associative, not [alternative algebras](wikipedia://en/3160), and do not have an identity element.

:::

Okubo's example was the algebra of 3-by-3 complex matrices with zero [trace](wikipedia://en/43270), with the product of $X$ and $Y$ given by $aXY + bYX - \tfrac{\operatorname{Tr}(XY)I}{3}$, where $I$ is the identity matrix and $a$ and $b$ satisfy $a + b = 3ab = 1$. The [Hermitian elements](wikipedia://en/189682) form an 8-dimensional real [non-associative](wikipedia://en/6533841) [division algebra](wikipedia://en/26549625). A similar construction works for any cubic alternative [separable algebra](wikipedia://en/13205367) over a field containing a primitive cube [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950). An Okubo algebra is an algebra constructed in this way from the trace-zero elements of a degree-3 [central simple algebra](wikipedia://en/404082) over a field.[^o3]

## Construction of Para-Hurwitz algebra {#Construction_of_Para-Hurwitz_algebra}

[Unital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unital_algebra) composition algebras are called **Hurwitz algebras**.[^o5] If the ground field $K$ is the field of [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) and $N$ is [positive-definite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-definite_quadratic_form), then $A$ is called a [Euclidean Hurwitz algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_Hurwitz_algebra).

### Scalar product {#Scalar_product}

If $K$ has characteristic not equal to 2, then a [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) $(a,\,b) = \tfrac{1}{2}[N(a + b) - N(a) - N(b)]$ is associated with the quadratic form $N$.

### Involution in Hurwitz algebras {#Involution_in_Hurwitz_algebras}

Assuming $A$ has a multiplicative unity, define involution and [right and left multiplication](wikipedia://en/37520883) operators by

$$\overline{a} = -a + 2(a,1)1, L(a)b = ab, R(a)b = ba$$

Evidently $\overline{}$ is an [involution](wikipedia://en/293450) and preserves the quadratic form. The overline notation stresses the fact that complex and quaternion [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_conjugation) are partial cases of it. These operators have the following properties:

- The involution is an antiautomorphism, i.e. $\overline{a b} = \overline{b}\,\overline{a}$
- $$a\,\overline{a} = N(a)\,1 = \overline{a}\,a$$
- $L(\overline{a}) = L(a)*$, $R(\overline{a}) = R(a)*$, where $*$ denotes the [adjoint operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_operator) with respect to the form $(\,,\,)$
- $\operatorname{Re}(a\,b) = \operatorname{Re}(b\,a)$ where $\operatorname{Re}\,x = (x + \overline{x})/2 = (x,\,1)$
- $$\operatorname{Re}((a\,b)\,c) = \operatorname{Re}(a\,(b\,c))$$
- $L(a^{2}) = L(a)^{2}$, $R(a^{2}) = R(a)^{2}$, so that $A$ is an [alternative algebra](wikipedia://en/3160)

These properties are proved starting from polarized version of the identity $(a\,b,\,a\,b) = (a,\,a)(b,\,b)$:

$$2(a, b)(c, d) = (ac, bd) + (ad, bc).$$

- Setting $b = 1$ or $d = 1$ yields $L(\overline{a}) = L(a)*$ and $R(\overline{c}) = R(c)*$.
- Hence $\operatorname{Re}(a\,b) = (a\,b,\,1) = (a,\,\overline{b}) = (b\,a,\,1) = \operatorname{Re}(b\,a)$.
- Similarly $(\overline{a\,b},\,c) = (a\,b,\,\overline{c}) = (b,\,\overline{a}\,\overline{c}) = (1,\,\overline{b}\,(\overline{a}\,\overline{c})) = (1,\,(\overline{b}\,\overline{a})\,\overline{c}) = (\overline{b}\,\overline{a},\,c)$.
- Hence $\operatorname{Re}(a b)c = ((a b)c,\,1) = (a\,b,\,\overline{c}) = (a,\,\overline{c}\,\overline{b}) = (a(b\,c),\,1) = \operatorname{Re}(a(b\,c))$.
- By the polarized identity $N(a)\,(c,\,d) = (ac,\,ad) = (\overline{a}\,a\,c,\,d)$ so $L(\overline{a})\,L(a) = N(a)$.
- Applied to 1 this gives $\overline{a}\,a = N(a)$.
- Replacing $a$ by $\overline{a}$ gives the other identity.
- Substituting the formula for $\overline{a}$ in $L(\overline{a})\,L(a) = L(\overline{a}\,a)$ gives $L(a)^{2} = L(a^{2})$.

### Para-Hurwitz algebra {#Para-Hurwitz_algebra}

Another operation $\ast$ may be defined in a Hurwitz algebra as

$$x\,\ast\,y = \overline{x}\,\overline{y}$$

The algebra $(A,\,\ast)$ is a composition algebra not generally unital, known as a **para-Hurwitz algebra**.[^o3] In dimensions 4 and 8 these are **para-quaternion**[^o9] and **para-octonion** algebras.[^o5]

A para-Hurwitz algebra satisfies[^o5]

$$(x * y) * x = x * (y * x) = \langle x | x\rangle y.$$

Conversely, an algebra with a non-degenerate [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) satisfying this equation is either a para-Hurwitz algebra or an eight-dimensional [pseudo-octonion algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-octonion_algebra).[^o5] Similarly, a [flexible algebra](wikipedia://en/36035264) satisfying

$$\langle xy | xy\rangle = \langle x | x\rangle \langle y | y\rangle$$

is either a Hurwitz algebra, a para-Hurwitz algebra or an eight-dimensional pseudo-octonion algebra.[^o5]
