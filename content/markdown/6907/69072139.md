# Dorian Abbot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dorian Abbot"}

**Dorian Schuyler Abbot** is an American [geophysicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysicist). He is a professor at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o3]

:::

## Education {#Education}

Abbot was educated at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he earned a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 2004 in physics, then received a [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science) (2004) and [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) (2008) in applied mathematics from the [Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_John_A._Paulson_School_of_Engineering_and_Applied_Sciences). His dissertation was titled *A high-latitude convective cloud feedback*.[^o5] Abbot's doctoral advisor was Eli Tziperman. From 2008 to 2009, he was a [postdoctoral researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postdoctoral_researcher) in the Department of Earth and Planetary Sciences at Harvard University. Abbot conducted post doctoral research in geophysical sciences from 2009 to 2011 at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

Abbot became an assistant professor of geophysical sciences at University of Chicago in 2011. He became an associate professor in 2015 and a full professor in 2024. Abbot uses low-order mathematical models and [numerical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_models) to research climates, [paleoclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoclimate), [planetary habitability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_habitability), and [exoplanets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoplanet).[^o6][^o8]

### MIT lecture cancellation {#MIT_lecture_cancellation}

In 2020, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology invited Abbot to discuss his work on extraterrestrial life in the public Carlson Lecture. The [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) postponed the lecture to the following year. In September 2021, the host department cancelled Abbot's public lecture and offered Abbot a future private lecture to its faculty and students.[^o10][^o11] According to the department, the cancellation responded to objections from faculty and students to a *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)* editorial co-written by Abbot in the prior month.[^o10][^o12][^o11][^o13]

In the editorial, Abbot and Stanford professor Ivan Marinovic argued for renewed focus on individual talent in university admissions and hiring.[^o10] They called for abolishing "group membership" preferences, including [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action_in_the_United_States) and [legacy preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_preference) in university admissions, and criticized aspects of university [diversity policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversity,_equity,_and_inclusion). They recalled how the racial ideology of [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany) "drove many of the best scholars out" of the universities, and called this "a warning of the consequences of viewing group membership as more important than merit."[^o12][^o14]

Defending the lecture cancellation, senior MIT officials stated that controversy over Abbott's views on diversity would "overshadow" the scientific content of his public lecture.[^o10][^o15] Abbot ultimately delivered his lecture on planetary science to a larger audience hosted by the [James Madison Program in American Ideals and Institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison_Program_in_American_Ideals_and_Institutions) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) on the same day as the cancelled event.[^o11][^o16] Abbot was a guest on [*Tucker Carlson Tonight*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson_Today)[^o17] and [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN)[^o18] to discuss the cancellation. He also received a Hero of Intellectual Freedom Award from the [American Council of Trustees and Alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Council_of_Trustees_and_Alumni).[^o16]

Following public outrage and rebukes from MIT faculty about free expression, the university held forums at which attending faculty were polled on free speech questions.[^o11][^o10] 60% of attendees felt that their voices were "constrained" at MIT; a new MIT Free Speech Alliance cited these findings as a call to action.[^o19] In December 2022, responding to the Abbot controversy, MIT's faculty voted by a roughly 2-to-1 margin to adopt a formal university statement on freedom of expression.[^o20][^o21] Abbot said of the faculty statement: "The challenge is always enforcement, but it looks promising."[^o20]
