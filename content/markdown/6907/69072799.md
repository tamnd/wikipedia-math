# Mixtilinear incircles of a triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mixtilinear incircles of a triangle"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), a **mixtilinear incircle** of a [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) which is [tangent](wikipedia://en/31482) to two of its sides and internally tangent to its [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/161262). The mixtilinear incircle of a triangle tangent to the two sides containing [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) $A$ is called the *$A$-mixtilinear incircle.* Every triangle has three unique mixtilinear incircles, one corresponding to each vertex.

*Image: Define of Mixtilinear cirlce.jpg.* $A$-Mixtilinear incircle of triangle $ABC$

:::

::: {#o3 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of existence and uniqueness"}

## Proof of existence and uniqueness {#Proof_of_existence_and_uniqueness}

The $A$-[excircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) of triangle $ABC$ is unique. Let $\Phi$ be a transformation defined by the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of an [inversion](wikipedia://en/295844) centered at $A$ with radius $\sqrt{AB \cdot AC}$ and a [reflection](wikipedia://en/1390873) with respect to the angle bisector on $A$. Since inversion and reflection are [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942) and preserve touching points, then $\Phi$ does as well. Then, the image of the $A$-[excircle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excircle) under $\Phi$ is a circle internally tangent to sides $AB, AC$ and the circumcircle of $ABC$, that is, the $A$-mixtilinear incircle. Therefore, the $A$-mixtilinear incircle exists and is unique, and a similar argument can prove the same for the mixtilinear incircles corresponding to $B$ and $C$.[^o11]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Mixtilinear circle.jpg.* The hexagon $XCABYT_A$ and the intersections $D, I, E$ of its 3 pairs of opposite sides.

The $A$-mixtilinear incircle can be constructed with the following sequence of steps.[^o6]

1. Draw the [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) $I$ by intersecting angle bisectors.
1. Draw a line through $I$ perpendicular to the line $AI$, touching lines $AB$ and $AC$ at points $D$ and $E$ respectively. These are the tangent points of the mixtilinear circle.
1. Draw perpendiculars to $AB$ and $AC$ through points $D$ and $E$ respectively and intersect them in $O_A$. $O_A$ is the center of the circle, so a circle with center $O_A$ and radius $O_AE$ is the mixtilinear incircle

This construction is possible because of the following fact:

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Verrier's lemma"}

### Verrier's lemma {#Verrier's_lemma}

The incenter is the midpoint of the touching points of the mixtilinear incircle with the two sides.

:::

::: {#o10 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

### Proof {#Proof}

Let $\Gamma$ be the circumcircle of triangle $ABC$ and $T_A$ be the tangency point of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle $\Omega_A$ and $\Gamma$. Let $X \neq T_A$ be the intersection of line $T_AD$ with $\Gamma$ and $Y \neq T_A$ be the intersection of line $T_AE$ with $\Gamma$. [Homothety](wikipedia://en/386138) with center on $T_A$ between $\Omega_A$ and $\Gamma$ implies that $X, Y$ are the midpoints of $\Gamma$ arcs $AB$ and $AC$ respectively. The [inscribed angle](wikipedia://en/462730) theorem implies that $X, I, C$ and $Y, I, B$ are triples of collinear points. [Pascal's theorem](wikipedia://en/699966) on hexagon $XCABYT_A$ inscribed in $\Gamma$ implies that $D, I, E$ are collinear. Since the angles $\angle{DAI}$ and $\angle{IAE}$ are equal, it follows that $I$ is the midpoint of segment $DE$.[^o11]

:::

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

### Radius {#Radius}

The following formula relates the radius $r$ of the incircle and the radius $\rho_A$ of the $A$-mixtilinear incircle of a triangle $ABC$:

$$r = \rho_A \cdot \cos^2{\frac{\alpha}{2}}$$

where $\alpha$ is the magnitude of the angle at $A$.[^o14]

### Relationship with points on the circumcircle {#Relationship_with_points_on_the_circumcircle}

- The midpoint of the [arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_%28geometric%29) $BC$ that contains point $A$ is on the line $T_AI$.[^o16][^o17]
- The quadrilateral $T_AXAY$ is [harmonic](wikipedia://en/16094600), which means that $T_AA$ is a [symmedian](wikipedia://en/320997) on triangle $XT_AY$.[^o11]

### Circles related to the tangency point with the circumcircle {#Circles_related_to_the_tangency_point_with_the_circumcircle}

$T_ABDI$ and $T_ACEI$ are [cyclic quadrilaterals](wikipedia://en/194131).[^o16]

### Spiral similarities {#Spiral_similarities}

$T_A$ is the center of a spiral similarity that maps $B, I$ to $I, C$ respectively.[^o11]

## Relationship between the three mixtilinear incircles {#Relationship_between_the_three_mixtilinear_incircles}

### Lines joining vertices and mixtilinear tangency points {#Lines_joining_vertices_and_mixtilinear_tangency_points}

The three lines joining a vertex to the point of contact of the circumcircle with the corresponding mixtilinear incircle meet at the external center of [similitude](wikipedia://en/386138) of the incircle and circumcircle.[^o14] The [Online Encyclopedia of Triangle Centers](wikipedia://en/15003593) lists this point as X(56).[^o22] It is defined by [trilinear coordinates](wikipedia://en/3837790):

$$\frac{a}{b+c-a} : \frac{b}{c+a-b} : \frac{c}{a+b-c},$$

and [barycentric coordinates](wikipedia://en/762954):

$$\frac{a^2}{b+c-a} : \frac{b^2}{c+a-b} : \frac{c^2}{a+b-c}.$$

### Radical center {#Radical_center}

The radical center of the three mixtilinear incircles is the point $J$ which divides $OI$ in the ratio:

$$OJ:JI=2R:-r$$

where $I, r, O, R$ are the incenter, inradius, circumcenter and circumradius respectively.[^o17]
