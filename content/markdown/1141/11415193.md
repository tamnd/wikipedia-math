# Swiss cheese (mathematics)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Swiss cheese"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Swiss cheese** is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) obtained by removing from a [closed disc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_%28mathematics%29) some countably infinite [union](wikipedia://en/47949) of [open discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disc_%28mathematics%29), usually with some restriction on the centres and radii of the removed discs. Traditionally the deleted discs should have pairwise [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) [closures](wikipedia://en/42309) which are subsets of the [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) of the starting disc, the sum of the radii of the deleted discs should be finite, and the Swiss cheese should have [empty](wikipedia://en/9566) interior. This is the type of Swiss cheese originally introduced by the Swiss mathematician [Alice Roth](wikipedia://en/46513994).

:::

More generally, a Swiss cheese may be all or part of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) **R**<sup>*n*</sup> – or of an even more complicated [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) – with "holes" in it.
