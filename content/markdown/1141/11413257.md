# Moving sofa problem

*Image: Movingsofaproblem.webp.* Diagram of the moving sofa problem

::: {#o1 .problem cue=unsolved}

What is the largest area of a shape that can be maneuvered through a unit-width L-shaped corridor?

:::

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Moving sofa problem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **moving sofa problem** or **sofa problem** is a two-dimensional idealization of real-life [furniture-moving problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning) and asks for the rigid two-dimensional shape of the largest [area](wikipedia://en/1209) that can be maneuvered through an L-shaped planar region with legs of unit width.[^o3] The area thus obtained is referred to as the **sofa constant**. The exact value of the sofa constant is an [open problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsolved_problems_in_mathematics).

:::

The leading solution, by Joseph L. Gerver, has a value of approximately 2.2195. In November 2024, Jineon Baek posted a 119-page [arXiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArXiv) preprint claiming that Gerver's value is optimal, which if true would solve the moving sofa problem.[^o4][^o5]

## History {#History}

The first formal publication was by the Austrian-Canadian mathematician [Leo Moser](wikipedia://en/745180) in 1966,[^o7] although there had been many informal mentions before that date.[^o3]

## Bounds {#Bounds}

Work has been done to prove that the sofa constant $A$ cannot be below or above specific values ([lower bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_bound) and [upper bounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_bound)).

### Lower {#Lower}

*Image: Hammersley sofa animated.gif.* The Hammersley sofa has an area of 2.2074, but is not the largest solution

A lower bound on the sofa constant can be proven by finding a specific shape with a high area and a path for moving it through the corner. $A \geq \pi/2 \approx 1.57$ is an obvious lower bound. This comes from a sofa that is a half-[disk](wikipedia://en/329542) of unit radius, which can slide up one passage into the corner, rotate within the corner around the center of the disk, and then slide out the other passage.

In 1968, [John Hammersley](wikipedia://en/656531) stated a lower bound of $A \geq \pi/2 + 2/\pi \approx 2.2074$.[^o10] This can be achieved using a shape resembling an old-fashioned [telephone handset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handset), consisting of two quarter-disks of radius 1 on either side of a 1 by $4/\pi$ rectangle from which a half-disk of radius $2/\pi$ has been removed.[^o11][^o12]

In 1992, Joseph L. Gerver of [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) described a sofa with 18 curve sections, each taking a smooth analytic form. This further increased the lower bound for the sofa constant to approximately 2.2195 .[^o13]

*Image: Gerver’s and Hammersley’s sofa overlap.svg.* Overlap of Hammersley’s sofa (red) and Gerver’s sofa (blue).

### Upper {#Upper}

Hammersley stated an upper bound on the sofa constant of at most $2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284$.[^o10][^o3][^o16] Yoav Kallus and [Dan Romik](wikipedia://en/74869799) published a new upper bound in 2018, capping the sofa constant at $2.37$. Their approach involves rotating the corridor (rather than the sofa) through a finite sequence of distinct angles (rather than continuously) and using a computer search to find translations for each rotated copy so that the intersection of all of the copies has a connected component with as large an area as possible. As they show, this provides a valid upper bound for the optimal sofa, which can be made more accurate using more rotation angles. Five carefully chosen rotation angles lead to the stated upper bound.[^o17]

## Ambidextrous sofa {#Ambidextrous_sofa}

*Image: Romik.svg.* Romik's ambidextrous sofa

A variant of the sofa problem asks the shape of the largest area that can go around both left and right 90-degree corners in a corridor of unit width (where the left and right corners are spaced sufficiently far apart that one is fully negotiated before the other is encountered). A lower bound of area approximately 1.64495521 has been described by [Dan Romik](wikipedia://en/74869799). 18 curve sections also describe his sofa.[^o19][^o20]
