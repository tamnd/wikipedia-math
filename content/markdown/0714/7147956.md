# Differential calculus over commutative algebras

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential calculus over commutative algebras"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **differential calculus over commutative algebras** is a part of [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) based on the observation that most concepts known from classical [differential calculus](wikipedia://en/50416) can be formulated in purely algebraic terms. Instances of this are:

:::

1. The whole topological information of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) $M$ is encoded in the algebraic properties of its $\mathbb{R}$-[algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebra_%28ring_theory%29) of smooth functions $A = C^\infty (M),$ as in the [Banach–Stone theorem](wikipedia://en/12772382).
1. [Vector bundles](wikipedia://en/276524) over $M$ correspond to projective finitely generated [modules](wikipedia://en/276410) over $A,$ via the [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $\Gamma$ which associates to a vector bundle its module of sections.
1. [Vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) on $M$ are naturally identified with [derivations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivation_%28abstract_algebra%29) of the algebra $A$.
1. More generally, a [linear differential operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_differential_operator) of order k, sending sections of a vector bundle $E\rightarrow M$ to sections of another bundle $F \rightarrow M$ is seen to be an $\mathbb{R}$-linear map $\Delta : \Gamma (E) \to \Gamma (F)$ between the associated modules, such that for any $k + 1$ elements $f_0, \ldots, f_k \in A$:

$$\left[f_k \left[f_{k-1} \left[\cdots\left[f_0, \Delta\right] \cdots \right]\right]\right] = 0$$

where the bracket $[f, \Delta] : \Gamma(E)\to \Gamma(F)$ is defined as the commutator

$$[f,\Delta](s) = \Delta(f \cdot s) - f \cdot \Delta(s).$$

Denoting the set of $k$<sup>th</sup> order linear differential operators from an $A$-module $P$ to an $A$-module $Q$ with $\mathrm{Diff}_k(P, Q)$ we obtain a bi-functor with values in the [category](wikipedia://en/53759) of $A$-modules. Other natural concepts of calculus such as [jet spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_space), [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) are then obtained as [representing objects](wikipedia://en/844461) of the functors $\mathrm{Diff}_k$ and related functors.

Seen from this point of view calculus may in fact be understood as the theory of these functors and their representing objects.

Replacing the real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ with any [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346), and the algebra $C^\infty(M)$ with any commutative algebra the above said remains meaningful, hence differential calculus can be developed for arbitrary commutative algebras. Many of these concepts are widely used in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and [secondary calculus](wikipedia://en/6899545). Moreover, the theory generalizes naturally to the setting of [graded commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/859686), allowing for a natural foundation of calculus on [supermanifolds](wikipedia://en/891255), [graded manifolds](wikipedia://en/24519420) and associated concepts like the [Berezin integral](wikipedia://en/20041308).
