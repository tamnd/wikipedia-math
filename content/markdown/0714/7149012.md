# Factorization system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Factorization system"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), it can be shown that every [function](wikipedia://en/185427) can be written as the composite of a [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) function followed by an [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) function. **Factorization systems** are a generalization of this situation in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A **factorization system** (*E*, *M*) for a [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) **C** consists of two classes of [morphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphisms) *E* and *M* of **C** such that:

1. *E* and *M* both contain all [isomorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphisms) of **C** and are closed under composition.
1. Every morphism *f* of **C** can be factored as $f=m\circ e$ for some morphisms $e\in E$ and $m\in M$.
1. The factorization is *functorial*: if $u$ and $v$ are two morphisms such that $vme=m'e'u$ for some morphisms $e, e'\in E$ and $m, m'\in M$, then there exists a unique morphism $w$ making the following diagram [commute](wikipedia://en/138484):

*Remark:* $(u,v)$ is a morphism from $me$ to $m'e'$ in the [arrow category](wikipedia://en/840950#Arrow_category).

## Orthogonality {#Orthogonality}

Two morphisms $e$ and $m$ are said to be *orthogonal*, denoted $e\downarrow m$, if for every pair of morphisms $u$ and $v$ such that $ve=mu$ there is a unique morphism $w$ such that the diagram

*Image: Factorization\_system\_orthogonality.png.*

commutes. This notion can be extended to define the orthogonals of sets of morphisms by

$H^\uparrow=\{e\quad|\quad\forall h\in H, e\downarrow h\}$ and $H^\downarrow=\{m\quad|\quad\forall h\in H, h\downarrow m\}.$

Since in a factorization system $E\cap M$ contains all the isomorphisms, the condition (3) of the definition is equivalent to

(3') $E\subseteq M^\uparrow$ and $M\subseteq E^\downarrow.$

*Proof:* In the previous diagram (3), take $m:= id ,\ e' := id$ (identity on the appropriate object) and $m' := m$.

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Equivalent definition"}

## Equivalent definition {#Equivalent_definition}

The pair $(E,M)$ of classes of morphisms of **C** is a factorization system if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

1. Every morphism *f* of **C** can be factored as $f=m\circ e$ with $e\in E$ and $m\in M.$
1. $E=M^\uparrow$ and $M=E^\downarrow.$

:::

## Weak factorization systems {#Weak_factorization_systems}

Suppose *e* and *m* are two morphisms in a category **C**. Then *e* has the *[left lifting property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_lifting_property)* with respect to *m* (respectively *m* has the *[right lifting property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_lifting_property)* with respect to *e*) when for every pair of morphisms *u* and *v* such that *ve* = *mu* there is a morphism *w* such that the following diagram commutes. The difference with orthogonality is that *w* is not necessarily unique.

*Image: Factorization\_system\_orthogonality.png.*

A **weak factorization system** (*E*, *M*) for a category **C** consists of two classes of morphisms *E* and *M* of **C** such that:[^o9]

1. The class *E* is exactly the class of morphisms having the left lifting property with respect to each morphism in *M*.
1. The class *M* is exactly the class of morphisms having the right lifting property with respect to each morphism in *E*.
1. Every morphism *f* of **C** can be factored as $f=m\circ e$ for some morphisms $e\in E$ and $m\in M$.

This notion leads to a succinct definition of [model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304): a model category is a pair consisting of a category **C** and classes of (so-called) [weak equivalences](wikipedia://en/34615176) *W*, fibrations *F* and cofibrations *C* so that

- **C** has all [limits](wikipedia://en/39248) and colimits,
- $(C \cap W, F)$ is a weak factorization system,
- $(C, F \cap W)$ is a weak factorization system, and
- $W$ satisfies the two-out-of-three property: if $f$ and $g$ are composable morphisms and two of $f,g,g\circ f$ are in $W$, then so is the third.[^o10]

A model category is a complete and cocomplete category equipped with a model structure. A map is called a trivial fibration if it belongs to $F\cap W,$ and it is called a trivial [cofibration](wikipedia://en/1697216) if it belongs to $C\cap W.$ An object $X$ is called fibrant if the morphism $X\rightarrow 1$ to the [terminal object](wikipedia://en/59405) is a fibration, and it is called cofibrant if the morphism $0\rightarrow X$ from the initial object is a cofibration.[^o11]
