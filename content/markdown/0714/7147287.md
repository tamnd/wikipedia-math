# Aspherical space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Aspherical space"}

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **aspherical space** is a [path connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path_connected) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) with all [homotopy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_groups) $\pi_n(X)$ equal to 0 when $n\not = 1$.

:::

If one works with [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022), one can reformulate this condition: an aspherical CW complex is a CW complex whose [universal cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_cover) is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible). Indeed, contractibility of a universal cover is the same, by [Whitehead's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitehead's_theorem), as asphericality of it. And it is an application of the [exact sequence of a fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_sequence_of_a_fibration) that higher homotopy groups of a space and its universal cover are same. (By the same argument, if *E* is a [path-connected space](wikipedia://en/6233) and $p\colon E \to B$ is any [covering map](wikipedia://en/250323), then *E* is aspherical if and only if *B* is aspherical.)

Each aspherical space *X* is, by definition, an [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) of type $K(G,1)$, where $G = \pi_1(X)$ is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of *X*. Also directly from the definition, an aspherical space is a [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556) for its fundamental group (considered to be a [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) when endowed with the [discrete topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_topology)).

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Using the second of above definitions we easily see that all orientable compact [surfaces](wikipedia://en/27865) of genus greater than 0 are aspherical (as they have either the Euclidean plane or the hyperbolic plane as a universal cover).
- It follows that all non-orientable surfaces, except the real [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350), are aspherical as well, as they can be covered by an orientable surface of genus 1 or higher.
- Similarly, a [product](wikipedia://en/24910) of any number of [circles](wikipedia://en/6220) is aspherical. As is any complete, Riemannian flat manifold.
- Any [hyperbolic 3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1237700) is, by definition, covered by the hyperbolic 3-space **H**<sup>3</sup>, hence aspherical. As is any *n*-manifold whose universal covering space is hyperbolic *n*-space **H**<sup>*n*</sup>.
- Let *X* = *G*/*K* be a [Riemannian symmetric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_symmetric_space) of negative type, and **Γ** be a [lattice](wikipedia://en/15782871) in *G* that acts freely on *X*. Then the [locally symmetric space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_symmetric_space) $\Gamma\backslash G/K$ is aspherical.
- The [Bruhat–Tits building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat–Tits_building) of a simple [algebraic group](wikipedia://en/247924) over a field with a [discrete valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_valuation) is aspherical.
- The complement of a [knot](wikipedia://en/366808) in **S**<sup>3</sup> is aspherical, by the [sphere theorem](wikipedia://en/4375347).
- Complete metric spaces with nonpositive curvature in the sense of [Aleksandr D. Aleksandrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Aleksandrov_%28mathematician%29) (locally [CAT(0) spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAT%280%29_space)) are aspherical. In the case of [Riemannian manifolds](wikipedia://en/144652), this follows from the [Cartan–Hadamard theorem](wikipedia://en/11939373), which has been generalized to [geodesic metric spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesic_metric_space) by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29) and [Hans Werner Ballmann](wikipedia://en/24812368). This class of aspherical spaces subsumes all the previously given examples.
- Any [nilmanifold](wikipedia://en/3668693) is aspherical.

:::

## Symplectically aspherical manifolds {#Symplectically_aspherical_manifolds}

In the context of [symplectic manifolds](wikipedia://en/28356), the meaning of "aspherical" is a little bit different. Specifically, we say that a symplectic manifold (M,ω) is symplectically aspherical if and only if

$$\int_{S^2}f^*\omega=\langle c_1(TM),f_*[S^2]\rangle=0$$

for every continuous mapping

$$f\colon S^2 \to M,$$

where $c_1(TM)$ denotes the first [Chern class](wikipedia://en/294349) of an [almost complex structure](wikipedia://en/514258) which is compatible with ω.

By [Stokes' theorem](wikipedia://en/25441497), we see that symplectic manifolds which are aspherical are also symplectically aspherical manifolds. However, there do exist symplectically aspherical manifolds which are not aspherical spaces.[^o5]

Some references[^o6] drop the requirement on *c*<sub>1</sub> in their definition of "symplectically aspherical." However, it is more common for symplectic manifolds satisfying only this weaker condition to be called "weakly exact."
