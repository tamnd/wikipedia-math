# Chris Rogers (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chris Rogers"}

**Leonard Christopher Gordon Rogers** (born 29 April 1954) is a mathematician working in [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_finance).[^o4] He is Emeritus [Professor of Statistical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship_of_Statistical_Science,_University_of_Cambridge) in the [Statistical Laboratory, University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_Laboratory,_University_of_Cambridge).

:::

Rogers' specialist fields include [stochastic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_analysis) and applications to [quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_finance).[^o5] With [David Williams](wikipedia://en/15398399) he has written two influential textbooks on [diffusion processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion_processes).

He was awarded the [Mayhew Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayhew_Prize) of [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) in 1976, and the [Rollo Davidson Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Davidson_Prize) in 1984. He was elected an Honorary Fellow of the [Institute of Actuaries](wikipedia://en/852871) in 2003.[^o6]

Rogers was an undergraduate at [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), where he graduated in 1975 and completed his PhD in 1980. He has held positions at several UK universities, including [Warwick University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_University) (1980–1983), [University College of Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_of_Swansea) (1983–1985), [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University) (1985–1991), [Queen Mary and Westfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary,_University_of_London) (1991–1994), and the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) (1994–2002). He was elected to the Cambridge [Professorship of Statistical Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professorship_of_Statistical_Science,_University_of_Cambridge) in 2002.[^o4]
