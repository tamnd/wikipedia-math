# Heiko Harborth

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Heiko Harborth"}

**Heiko Harborth** (born 11 February 1938, in [Celle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celle), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany))[^o3] is Professor of [Mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Braunschweig University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig_University_of_Technology), 1975–present, and author of more than 188 mathematical publications.[^o4] His work is mostly in the areas of [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), including [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Harborth has been an instructor or professor at [Braunschweig University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweig_University_of_Technology) since studying there and receiving his PhD in 1965 under [Hans-Joachim Kanold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Joachim_Kanold). Harborth is a member of the [New York Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Academy_of_Sciences), [Braunschweigische Wissenschaftliche Gesellschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braunschweigische_Wissenschaftliche_Gesellschaft), the [Institute of Combinatorics and its Applications](wikipedia://en/17545919), and many other mathematical societies. Harborth currently sits on the editorial boards of [Fibonacci Quarterly](wikipedia://en/12227896), [Geombinatorics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geombinatorics), [Integers: Electronic Journal of Combinatorial Number Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integers:_Electronic_Journal_of_Combinatorial_Number_Theory). He served as an editor of [Mathematische Semesterberichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematische_Semesterberichte) from 1988 to 2001. Harborth was a joint recipient (with [Stephen Milne](wikipedia://en/46816916)) of the 2007 [Euler Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_Medal).

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

*Image: Harborth graph vector.svg.* The Harborth Graph.

Harborth's research ranges across the subject areas of [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), [discrete geometry](wikipedia://en/386468), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527). In 1974, Harborth solved the [unit coin graph](wikipedia://en/53242630) problem,[^o8] determining the maximum number of edges possible in a unit coin graph on n vertices. In 1986, Harborth presented the graph that would bear his name, the [Harborth graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harborth_graph). It is the smallest known example of a 4-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [matchstick graph](wikipedia://en/25730128). It has 104 edges and 52 vertices.[^o9]

In connection with the [happy ending problem](wikipedia://en/2576185), Harborth showed that, for every finite set of ten or more points in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) in the plane, some five of them form a convex pentagon that does not contain any of the other points.[^o10]

[Harborth's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44342842)[^o11] posits that every planar graph admits a straight-line embedding in the plane where every edge has integer length. This open question () is a stronger version of [Fáry's theorem](wikipedia://en/6805386). It is known to be true for [cubic graphs](wikipedia://en/980508).[^o12]

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the [Stolarsky&ndash;Harborth constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolarsky&ndash;Harborth_constant) is named for Harborth, along with [Kenneth Stolarsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Stolarsky).

## Private life {#Private_life}

Harborth married Karin Reisener in 1961, and they had two children. He was widowed in 1980. In 1985 he married Bärbel Peter and with her has three stepchildren.[^o3]
