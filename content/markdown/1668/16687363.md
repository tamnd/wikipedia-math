# Alexander Dinghas

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Dinghas"}

**Alexander Dinghas** (February 9, 1908 – April 19, 1974) was a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece) mathematician.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Dinghas was born on February 9, 1908, in [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna) (now [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/İzmir)), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey). He did his schooling in Smyrna. He and his family moved to [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens) in 1922.

Dinghas completed his secondary school and entered the [National Technical University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Technical_University_of_Athens) in 1925. He graduated with a diploma in electrical and mechanical engineering in 1930.

In 1931 Dinghas began his studies at the [University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Berlin) in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany). He completed his doctorate in mathematics in 1936. He studied under [Erhard Schmidt](wikipedia://en/672191).

*Image: Grabstätte Alexander Dinghas.jpg.* Gravestone of Alexander Dinghas

Dinghas was not a German and his career during the Nazi years was very difficult. However, after the end of World War II, his luck changed. He became professor of mathematics at the [Humboldt University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_University_of_Berlin) in 1947. From 1949 until his death he was a professor of mathematics at the [Free University of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_University_of_Berlin) and director of the Mathematical Institute there.

Dinghas died on April 19, 1974, in Berlin, Germany.

## Work {#Work}

Dinghas is known for his work in different areas of mathematics including differential equations, functions of a complex variable, functions of several complex variables, measure theory and differential geometry. His most important contribution was his work in function theory, in particular [Nevanlinna theory](wikipedia://en/393135) and the growth of [subharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/3237359).
