# Huisken's monotonicity formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Huisken's monotonicity formula"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), **Huisken's monotonicity formula** states that, if an $n$-dimensional surface in $(n + 1)$-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) undergoes the [mean curvature flow](wikipedia://en/13110176), then its [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) with an appropriately scaled and time-reversed [heat kernel](wikipedia://en/3904336) is non-increasing.[^o2][^o3] The result is named after [Gerhard Huisken](wikipedia://en/39508202), who published it in 1990.[^o4]

:::

Specifically, the $(n + 1)$-dimensional time-reversed heat kernel converging to a point $x_{0}$ at time $t_{0}$ may be given by the formula[^o2]

$$u(x,t) = \frac{1}{(4\pi (t_0-t))^{n/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-x_0|^2}{4(t_0-t)}\right).$$

Then Huisken's monotonicity formula gives an explicit expression for the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of

$$\int u(x,t) d\mu,$$

where $\mu$ is the area element of the evolving surface at time $t$. The expression involves the negation of another integral, whose integrand is non-negative, so the derivative is non-positive.

Typically, $x_{0}$ and $t_{0}$ are chosen as the time and position of a singularity of the evolving surface, and the monotonicity formula can be used to analyze the behavior of the surface as it evolves towards this singularity. In particular, the only surfaces for which the convolution with the heat kernel remains constant rather than decreasing are ones that stay self-similar as they evolve, and the monotonicity formula can be used to classify these surfaces.

[Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) derived analogous formulas for the [Ricci flow](wikipedia://en/288291).[^o5][^o6]
