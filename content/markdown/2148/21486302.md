# Cycle double cover

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cycle double cover"}

*Image: Petersen double cover.svg.* A cycle double cover of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) (shown in gray), formed of six cycles (shown in color). These six cycles correspond to the six pentagonal faces of a [hemi-dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/10393833).

In [graph-theoretic](wikipedia://en/12401) [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **cycle double cover** is a collection of [cycles](wikipedia://en/168609) in an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) that together include each edge of the graph exactly twice. For instance, for any [polyhedral graph](wikipedia://en/24656270), the faces of a [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) that represents the graph provide a cycle double cover of the graph: the edges of each face form a cycle and each edge belongs to exactly two faces and therefore to two of these cycles.

:::

It is a problem posed in the 1970s by [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021),[^o2] Itai and Rodeh,[^o3] [George Szekeres](wikipedia://en/2563194)[^o4] and [Paul Seymour](wikipedia://en/1575708)[^o5] and known as the **cycle double cover conjecture**, whether every [bridgeless graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeless_graph) has a cycle double cover. The [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) can equivalently be formulated in terms of [graph embeddings](wikipedia://en/8149170), and in that context is also known as the **circular embedding conjecture**.

In July 2026, [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI) released a preprint claiming a positive resolution of the conjecture,[^o6][^o7] the proof of which they claim was generated by [GPT-5.6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-5.6), its [large language model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model).

::: {#o9 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

A *cycle* is a connected [subgraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgraph_%28graph_theory%29) all of whose vertices have [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 2. In particular, a single edge, traversed back and forth, does not qualify as a cycle. A *bridge* in a graph is an edge whose removal increases the number of [connected components](wikipedia://en/246223).

The usual formulation of the cycle double cover conjecture asks whether every finite [bridgeless](wikipedia://en/1305071) undirected graph admits a collection of cycles such that each edge of the graph is contained in exactly two of the cycles.

The requirement that the graph be bridgeless is an obvious necessary condition for such a double cover to exist, because a bridge cannot belong to any cycle. Some graphs such as [cycle graphs](wikipedia://en/878962) and bridgeless [cactus graphs](wikipedia://en/9944209) only have double covers that use the same cycle twice, so this sort of duplication is allowed in a cycle double cover. This means that we are actually looking for a [multiset](wikipedia://en/305303) of cycles so that every edge of the graph belongs to precisely two elements of the multiset.

:::

## Proposed solution {#Proposed_solution}

On July 10, 2026, OpenAI announced on Twitter that their GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra large language model had found a proof of the conjecture "in just under one hour".[^o11] They released the proposed three-page proof of the conjecture[^o6] along with the two-page [prompt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prompt_engineering) used to generate this proof.[^o12] The prompt instructed GPT-5.6 to employ "up to 64 concurrent agents", keep those agents independent by having them work on different approaches and not informing them about the currently favored approach, "use adversarial agents throughout" to check candidate proofs, and "spend at least 8 hours on this".[^o12] The OpenAI team also released a formalization of the proof in [Lean](wikipedia://en/62889984).[^o13]

The proof uses a reduction by [François Jaeger](wikipedia://en/77122761) mentioned below,[^o16] namely that it is sufficient to prove the conjecture for loopless multigraphs that are *cubic* (i.e. whose every vertex has degree 3). Further, the proof uses the fact that every bridgeless graph admits a nowhere-zero $\mathbb{F}_2^3$-flow, which was independently established by Jaeger (1976) and Kilpatrick (1975).

The mathematician [Thomas Bloom](wikipedia://en/71404791) pointed out that the idea of using nowhere-zero $\mathbb{F}_2^3$-flows on cubic graphs to study cycle double covers goes back at least to a 1983 paper of Bermond, Jackson, and Jaeger.[^o14]

## Reduction to snarks {#Reduction_to_snarks}

A [snark](wikipedia://en/727811) is a special case of a bridgeless graph, having the additional properties that every vertex has exactly three incident edges (that is, the graph is [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508)) and that it is not possible to partition the edges of the graph into three [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031) (that is, the graph has no 3-[edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647), and by [Vizing's theorem](wikipedia://en/5449464) has [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 4). It turns out that snarks form the only difficult case of the cycle double cover conjecture: if the conjecture is true for snarks, it is true for any graph.[^o16]

(Jaeger 1985) observes that, in any potential minimal [counterexample](wikipedia://en/143151) to the cycle double cover conjecture, all vertices must have three or more incident edges. For, a vertex with only one edge incident forms a bridge, while if two edges are incident on a vertex, one can contract them to form a smaller graph such that any double cover of the smaller graph extends to one of the original graph. On the other hand, if a vertex *v* has four or more incident edges, one may “split off” two of those edges by removing them from the graph and replacing them by a single edge connecting their two other endpoints, while preserving the bridgelessness of the resulting graph. Again, a double cover of the resulting graph may be extended in a straightforward way to a double cover of the original graph: every cycle of the split off graph corresponds either to a cycle of the original graph, or to a pair of cycles meeting at *v*. Thus, every minimal counterexample must be cubic. But if a cubic graph can have its edges 3-colored (say with the colors red, blue, and green), then the subgraph of red and blue edges, the subgraph of blue and green edges, and the subgraph of red and green edges each form a collection of disjoint cycles that together cover all edges of the graph twice. Therefore, every minimal counterexample must be a non-3-edge-colorable bridgeless cubic graph, that is, a snark.[^o16]

## Reducible configurations {#Reducible_configurations}

One possible attack on the cycle double cover problem would be to show that there cannot exist a minimum counterexample, by proving that any graph contains a *reducible configuration*, a subgraph that can be replaced by a smaller subgraph in a way that would preserve the existence or nonexistence of a cycle double cover. For instance, if a cubic graph contains a triangle, a [Δ-Y transform](wikipedia://en/268145) will replace the triangle by a single vertex; any cycle double cover of the smaller graph can be extended back to a cycle double cover of the original cubic graph. Therefore, a minimal counterexample to the cycle double cover conjecture must be a [triangle-free graph](wikipedia://en/7667996), ruling out some snarks such as [Tietze's graph](wikipedia://en/20058152) which contain triangles. Through computer searches, it is known that every cycle of length 11 or less in a cubic graph forms a reducible configuration, and therefore that any minimal counterexample to the cycle double cover conjecture must have [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) at least 12.[^o19]

Unfortunately, it is not possible to prove the cycle double cover conjecture using a finite set of reducible configurations. Every reducible configuration contains a cycle, so for every finite set *S* of reducible configurations there is a number γ such that all configurations in the set contain a cycle of length at most γ. However, there exist snarks with arbitrarily high girth, that is, with arbitrarily high bounds on the length of their shortest cycle.[^o20] A snark *G* with girth greater than γ cannot contain any of the configurations in the set *S*, so the reductions in *S* are not strong enough to rule out the possibility that *G* might be a minimal counterexample.

::: {#o22 .statement kind=statement/conjecture cue=named-heading name="Circular embedding conjecture"}

## Circular embedding conjecture {#Circular_embedding_conjecture}

If a graph has a cycle double cover, the cycles of the cover can be used to form the 2-cells of a [graph embedding](wikipedia://en/8149170) onto a two-dimensional [cell complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_complex). In the case of a cubic graph, this complex always forms a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470). The graph is said to be *circularly embedded* onto the manifold, in that every face of the embedding is a simple cycle in the graph. However, a cycle double cover of a graph with degree greater than three may not correspond to an embedding on a manifold: the cell complex formed by the cycles of the cover may have non-manifold topology at its vertices. The **circular embedding conjecture** or **strong embedding conjecture**[^o16] states that every [2-vertex-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) has a circular embedding onto a manifold. If so, the graph also has a cycle double cover, formed by the faces of the embedding.

For cubic graphs, 2-vertex-connectedness and bridgelessness are equivalent. Therefore, the circular embedding conjecture is clearly at least as strong as the cycle double cover conjecture.[^o16]

If a circular embedding exists, it might not be on a surface of minimal genus: Nguyen Huy Xuong described a 2-vertex-connected [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057) none of whose circular embeddings lie on a torus.[^o16]

:::

## Stronger conjectures and related problems {#Stronger_conjectures_and_related_problems}

A stronger version of the circular embedding conjecture that has also been considered is the conjecture that every 2-vertex-connected graph has a circular embedding on an [orientable manifold](wikipedia://en/187446). In terms of the cycle double cover conjecture, this is equivalent to the conjecture that there exists a cycle double cover, and an orientation for each of the cycles in the cover, such that for every edge *e* the two cycles that cover *e* are oriented in opposite directions through *e*.[^o16]

Alternatively, strengthenings of the conjecture that involve [colorings](wikipedia://en/426743) of the cycles in the cover have also been considered. The strongest of these is a conjecture that every bridgeless graph has a circular embedding on an orientable manifold in which the faces can be 5-colored. If true, this would imply a conjecture of [W. T. Tutte](wikipedia://en/52021) that every bridgeless graph has a [nowhere-zero 5-flow](wikipedia://en/9909979).[^o16]

A stronger type of embedding than a circular embedding is a *polyhedral embedding*, an embedding of a graph on a surface in such a way that every face is a simple cycle and every two faces that intersect do so in either a single vertex or a single edge. (In the case of a cubic graph, this can be simplified to a requirement that every two faces that intersect do so in a single edge.) Thus, in view of the reduction of the cycle double cover conjecture to snarks, it is of interest to investigate polyhedral embeddings of snarks. Unable to find such embeddings, [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) conjectured that they do not exist, but (txt) disproved Grünbaum's conjecture by finding a snark with a polyhedral embedding.

:::
