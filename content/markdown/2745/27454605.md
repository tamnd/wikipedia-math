# Chaim L. Pekeris

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Chaim L. Pekeris"}

*Image: Weizac Plaque.jpg.*

**Chaim Leib Pekeris** (חיים לייב פקריס; June 15, 1908 – February 24, 1993)[^o3] was an Israeli-American physicist and [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902). He made notable contributions to [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics) and the spectral theory of many-electron atoms, in particular the [helium atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium_atom). He was also one of the designers of the first computer in Israel, [WEIZAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WEIZAC).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Pekeris was born in [Alytus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alytus), [Vilna Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilna_Governorate) on June 15, 1908. With the assistance of his uncle, Pekeris and his two brothers emigrated to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) around 1925.[^o5] He entered the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) in 1925 graduating in 1929 with a B.Sc. in meteorology. Pekeris also took his graduate studies at MIT, studying under [Carl-Gustav Rossby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl-Gustav_Rossby). In January 1933 he married Lea Kaplan, who was also born in Lithuania. He graduated with his [Sc.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science#United_States) [doctoral degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_degree) in 1934.[^o5][^o6][^o7]

In 1934 Pekeris joined the faculty at M.I.T. as an instructor in [geophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysics) in the department of geology. He became a US citizen in 1938.[^o8] Pekeris remained at M.I.T until 1941 when he moved to the Hudson Laboratories of [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) to conduct military research. In 1946 he joined the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179).

Pekeris and his wife moved to Israel in 1948,[^o5] where he joined the [Weizmann Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weizmann_Institute) as head of its department of applied mathematics in 1949.[^o9] During the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab–Israeli_War) he was involved in a clandestine program in [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State) developing munitions for the newborn State of Israel.[^o10]

In July 1947, an advisory committee for the Applied Mathematics Department of the Weizmann Institute discussed the plan to build the computer. Among the committee's members were [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736), who did not find the idea reasonable, and John von Neumann, who supported it. In one conversation, von Neumann was asked: "What will that tiny country do with an electric computer?" He responded: "Don’t worry about that problem. If nobody else uses the computer, Pekeris will use it full time!"[^o11]

The WEIZAC project was initiated by Pekeris, who wanted it as means to solve [Laplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace)’s [tidal equations](wikipedia://en/10610469) for the Earth's oceans, and also for the benefit of the entire scientific community of Israel, including the Defense Ministry. WEIZAC's first computation was done in October 1955.[^o12][^o13][^o14]

Pekeris received the Gold Medal from the Royal Astronomical Society in 1980, and the Israel Prize from the State of Israel in 1981.[^o6] [Teddy Kollek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Kollek), the mayor of Jerusalem from 1965 to 1993, said in 1990: "I have told you a lot about Chaim Pekeris tonight and there is much more that I could tell, but you will understand that there are reasons that I can’t. Let me simply say that Chaim Pekeris played a most significant role in the establishment of the State of Israel."[^o6] He died in [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot), Israel on February 24, 1993.[^o5][^o6]
