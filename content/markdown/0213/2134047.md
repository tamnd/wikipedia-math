# Principles of Mathematical Logic

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Principles of Mathematical Logic"}

***Principles of Mathematical Logic*** is the 1950[^o3] American translation of the 1938 second edition[^o4] of [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s and [Wilhelm Ackermann](wikipedia://en/62727)'s classic text *Grundzüge der theoretischen Logik*,[^o5] on elementary [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636). The 1928 first edition thereof is considered the first elementary text clearly grounded in the formalism now known as [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) (FOL). Hilbert and Ackermann also formalized FOL in a way that subsequently achieved canonical status. FOL is now a core formalism of mathematical logic, and is presupposed by contemporary treatments of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) and nearly all treatments of [axiomatic set theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_set_theory).

:::

The 1928 edition included a clear statement of the [Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672) ([decision problem](wikipedia://en/8336)) for FOL, and also asked whether that logic was [complete](wikipedia://en/12450) (i.e., whether all semantic truths of FOL were theorems derivable from the FOL axioms and rules). The former problem was answered in the negative first by [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731) and independently by [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) in 1936. The latter was answered affirmatively by [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) in 1929.

In its description of [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), mention is made of [Russell's paradox](wikipedia://en/46095) and the [Liar paradox](wikipedia://en/17967) (page 145). Contemporary notation for logic owes more to this text than it does to the notation of *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)*, long popular in the English speaking world.
