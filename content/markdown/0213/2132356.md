# Fixed-point index

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fixed-point index"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **fixed-point index** is a concept in [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) [fixed-point](wikipedia://en/449738) theory, and in particular [Nielsen theory](wikipedia://en/3603035). The fixed-point index can be thought of as a [multiplicity](wikipedia://en/19211790) measurement of fixed points.

:::

The index can be easily defined in the setting of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759): Let *f*(*z*) be a [holomorphic mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_map) on the complex plane, and let *z*<sub>0</sub> be a fixed point of *f*. Then the function *f*(*z*) − *z* is holomorphic, and has an isolated zero at *z*<sub>0</sub>. We define the fixed-point index of *f* at *z*<sub>0</sub>, denoted *i*(*f*, *z*<sub>0</sub>), to be the multiplicity of the zero of the function *f*(*z*) − *z* at the point *z*<sub>0</sub>.

In real Euclidean space, the fixed-point index is defined as follows: If *x*<sub>0</sub> is an isolated fixed point of *f*, then let *g* be the function defined by

$$g(x) = \frac{x - f(x)}{|| x - f(x) ||}.$$

Then *g* has an isolated singularity at *x*<sub>0</sub>, and maps the boundary of some deleted neighborhood of *x*<sub>0</sub> to the unit sphere. We define *i*(*f*, *x*<sub>0</sub>) to be the [Brouwer](wikipedia://en/172896) [degree](wikipedia://en/3094450) of the mapping induced by *g* on some suitably chosen small sphere around *x*<sub>0</sub>.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="The Lefschetz–Hopf theorem"}

## The Lefschetz–Hopf theorem {#The_Lefschetz–Hopf_theorem}

The importance of the fixed-point index is largely due to its role in the [Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384)–[Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082) theorem, which states:

$$\sum_{x \in \mathrm{Fix}(f)} \mathrm{ind}(f,x) = \Lambda_f,$$

where Fix(*f*) is the set of fixed points of *f*, and *Λ*<sub>*f*</sub> is the [Lefschetz number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefschetz_number) of *f*.

Since the quantity on the left-hand side of the above is clearly zero when *f* has no fixed points, the Lefschetz–Hopf theorem trivially implies the [Lefschetz fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/582530).

:::
