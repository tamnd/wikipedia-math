# Carl Pomerance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Carl Pomerance"}

**Carl Bernard Pomerance** (born 1944) is an American [number theorist](wikipedia://en/21527). He attended college at [Brown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) and later received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1972 with a dissertation proving that any odd [perfect number](wikipedia://en/23670) has at least seven distinct [prime factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor). He joined the faculty at the [University of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Georgia), becoming full professor in 1982. He subsequently worked at [Lucent Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucent_Technologies) for a number of years, and then became a [distinguished professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_professor) at [Dartmouth College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_College).

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

He has over 120 publications, including co-authorship with [Richard Crandall](wikipedia://en/9210048) of *Prime numbers: a computational perspective* ([Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media), first edition 2001, second edition 2005[^o4]), and with [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284).[^o5] He is the inventor of one of the [integer factorization](wikipedia://en/15491) methods, the [quadratic sieve](wikipedia://en/582340) algorithm, which was used in 1994 for the factorization of [RSA-129](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA-129). He is also one of the discoverers of the [Adleman–Pomerance–Rumely primality test](wikipedia://en/8198761).

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

He has won many teaching and research awards, including the [Chauvenet Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauvenet_Prize) in 1985,[^o7] the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics) in 1997,[^o8] and the [Levi L. Conant Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_L._Conant_Prize) in 2001 for "A Tale of Two Sieves".[^o9]

In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o10] He also became the [John G. Kemeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Kemeny) Parents Professor of Mathematics in the same year.[^o11][^o12]
