# Perfect fluid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Perfect fluid"}

In [physics](wikipedia://en/22939), a **perfect fluid** or **ideal fluid** is a [fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid) that can be completely characterized by its rest frame [mass density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_density) $\rho_m$ and *isotropic* [pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure) $p$.[^o2] Real fluids are viscous ("sticky") and contain (and conduct) heat. Perfect fluids are idealized models in which these possibilities are ignored. Specifically, perfect fluids have no [shear stresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_stress), [viscosity](wikipedia://en/18963754), or [heat conduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_conduction).[^o2] A [quark–gluon plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark–gluon_plasma)[^o3] and [graphene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphene) are examples of nearly perfect fluids that could be studied in a laboratory.[^o4]

:::

## Non-relativistic fluid mechanics {#Non-relativistic_fluid_mechanics}

In [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586), ideal fluids are described by [Euler equations](wikipedia://en/396022). Ideal fluids produce no [drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_%28physics%29) according to [d'Alembert's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Alembert's_paradox). If a fluid produced drag, then work would be needed to move an object through the fluid and that work would produce heat or fluid motion. However, a perfect fluid can not dissipate energy and it can't transmit energy infinitely far from the object.[^o6]

A flock of birds in the medium of air is an example of a perfect fluid; an [electron gas](wikipedia://en/181983) is also modeled as a perfect fluid.[^o2]

### Superfluidity {#Superfluidity}

[Superfluids](wikipedia://en/35607283) are fluids with zero viscosity, however in practice superfluids cannot be accurately described as a perfect fluid.[^o8][^o9] In the [two-fluid model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-fluid_model), superfluids are macroscopically considered as having two-coexisting phases, a mixture between a normal fluid and a perfect fluid.[^o9]

## Cosmology and astrophysics {#Cosmology_and_astrophysics}

*Image: StressEnergyTensor contravariant.svg.* The [stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) of a perfect fluid contains only the diagonal components.

Perfect fluids are a [fluid solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_solution) used in [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024) to model idealized distributions of [matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter), such as the interior of a star or an isotropic universe. In the latter case, the symmetry of the cosmological principle and the [equation of state](wikipedia://en/1540704) of the perfect fluid lead to [Friedmann equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedmann_equation) for the [expansion of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_the_universe).[^o11]

### Formulation {#Formulation}

In space-positive [metric signature](wikipedia://en/463225) tensor notation, the [stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) of a perfect fluid can be written in the form

$$T^{\mu\nu} = \left( \rho_m + \frac{p}{c^2} \right) \, U^\mu U^\nu + p \, \eta^{\mu\nu} ,$$

where *U* is the [4-velocity](wikipedia://en/226424#Four-velocity) [vector field](wikipedia://en/62641) of the fluid and where $\eta_{\mu \nu} = \operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ is the metric tensor of [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488).

The case where p=0 describes a [dust solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_solution). When $p=\rho_m c^2/3$, it describes a [photon gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon_gas) (radiation).

In time-positive [metric signature](wikipedia://en/463225) tensor notation, the [stress–energy tensor](wikipedia://en/70671) of a perfect fluid can be written in the form

$$T^{\mu\nu} = \left( \rho_\text{m} + \frac{p}{c^2} \right) \, U^\mu U^\nu - p \, \eta^{\mu\nu} ,$$

where $U$ is the 4-velocity of the fluid and where $\eta_{\mu \nu} = \operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ is the metric tensor of [Minkowski spacetime](wikipedia://en/230488).

This takes on a particularly simple form in the rest frame

$$\left[ \begin{matrix} \rho_e &0&0&0\\0&p&0&0\\0&0&p&0\\0&0&0&p\end{matrix} \right]$$

where $\rho_\text{e} = \rho_\text{m} c^2$ is the *[energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density)* and $p$ is the *pressure* of the fluid.

Perfect fluids admit a [Lagrangian formulation](wikipedia://en/40752010), which allows the techniques used in [field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29), in particular, [quantization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantization_%28physics%29), to be applied to fluids.

[Relativistic Euler equations](wikipedia://en/973828) read

$$\partial_\nu T^{\mu\nu}=0$$

in the non relativistic limit, these equations reduce to the usual Euler equations.[^o14]
