# A Course of Modern Analysis

*Image: A Course of Modern Analysis - 3rd edition - 1920.jpg.* Title page for the third edition of the book.

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="A Course of Modern Analysis"}

***A Course of Modern Analysis****: an introduction to the general theory of infinite processes and of analytic functions; with an account of the principal [transcendental functions](wikipedia://en/297021)* (colloquially known as **Whittaker and Watson**) is a landmark textbook on [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) written by [Edmund T. Whittaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_T._Whittaker) and [George N. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_N._Watson), first published by [Cambridge University Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University_Press) in 1915.[^o22] The first edition was Whittaker's alone, but later editions were co-authored with Watson.

:::

## History {#History}

Its first, second, third, and the fourth edition were published in 1902,[^o10] 1915,[^o11] 1920,[^o12] and 1927,[^o13] respectively. Since then, it has continuously been reprinted and is still in print today.[^o13][^o14] A revised, expanded and [digitally reset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digitally_reset_%28typesetting%29) fifth edition, edited by [Victor H. Moll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_H._Moll), was published in 2021.[^o15]

The book is notable for being the standard reference and textbook for a generation of Cambridge mathematicians including [Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) and [Godfrey H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_H._Hardy). [Mary L. Cartwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_L._Cartwright) studied it as preparation for her final honours on the advice of fellow student [Vernon C. Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon_Morton), later Professor of Mathematics at [Aberystwyth University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth_University).[^o16] But its reach was much further than just the Cambridge school; [André Weil](wikipedia://en/2019) in his obituary of the French mathematician [Jean Delsarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Delsarte) noted that Delsarte always had a copy on his desk.[^o17] In 1941, the book was included among a "selected list" of mathematical analysis books for use in universities in an article for that purpose published by [American Mathematical Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Mathematical_Monthly).[^o18]

## Notable features {#Notable_features}

Some idiosyncratic but interesting problems from an older era of the [Cambridge Mathematical Tripos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Mathematical_Tripos) are in the exercises.

The book was one of the earliest to use [decimal numbering for its sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_book_section_numbering), an innovation the authors attribute to [Giuseppe Peano](wikipedia://en/13043).[^o19]

## Contents {#Contents}

Below are the contents of the fourth edition:

**Part I. The Process of Analysis**

Complex Numbers; The Theory of Convergence; Continuous Functions and Uniform Convergence; The Theory of Riemann Integration; The fundamental properties of Analytic Functions; Taylor's, Laurent's, and Liouville's Theorems; The Theory of Residues; application to the evaluation of Definite Integrals; The expansion of functions in Infinite Series; Asymptotic Expansions and Summable Series; Fourier Series and Trigonometrical Series; Linear Differential Equations; Integral Equations

**Part II. The Transcendental Functions**

The Gamma Function; The Zeta Function of Riemann; The Hypergeometric Function; Legendre Functions; The Confluent Hypergeometric Function; Bessel Functions; The Equations of Mathematical Physics; Mathieu Functions; Elliptic Functions. General theorems and the Weierstrassian Functions; The Theta Functions; The Jacobian Elliptic Functions; Ellipsoidal Harmonics and Lamé's Equation

## Reception {#Reception}

### Reviews of the first edition {#Reviews_of_the_first_edition}

[George B. Mathews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ballard_Mathews), in a 1903 review article published in *[The Mathematical Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mathematical_Gazette)* opens by saying the book is "sure of a favorable reception" because of its "attractive account of some of the most valuable and interesting results of recent analysis".[^o21] He notes that Part I deals mainly with [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series), focusing on [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) and [Fourier expansions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_expansion) while including the "elements of" [complex integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_integration) and the [theory of residues](wikipedia://en/73102). Part II, in contrast, has chapters on the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), [Legendre functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendre_functions), the [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series), [Bessel functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessel_functions), [elliptic functions](wikipedia://en/69939), and [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416).

[Arthur S. Hathaway](wikipedia://en/43658996), in another 1903 review published in the *[Journal of the American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Chemical_Society)*, notes that the book centers around [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759), but that topics such as [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) are "considered in all their phases" along with "all those important series and functions" developed by mathematicians such as [Joseph Fourier](wikipedia://en/25767401), [Friedrich Bessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Bessel), [Joseph-Louis Lagrange](wikipedia://en/87793), [Adrien-Marie Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695), [Pierre-Simon Laplace](wikipedia://en/344783), [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), [Niels Henrik Abel](wikipedia://en/21573), and others in their respective studies of "practice problems".[^o20] He goes on to say it "is a useful book for those who wish to make use of the most advanced developments of mathematical analysis in theoretical investigations of physical and chemical questions."[^o20]

In a third review of the first edition, [Maxime Bôcher](wikipedia://en/3180285), in a 1904 review published in the *[Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/2668304)* notes that while the book falls short of the "rigor" of French, German, and Italian writers, it is a "gratifying sign of progress to find in an English book such an attempt at rigorous treatment as is here made".[^o22] He notes that important parts of the book were otherwise non-existent in the English language.
