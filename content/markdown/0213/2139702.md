# Product term

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Product term"}

In [Boolean logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_logic), a **product term** is a conjunction of literals, where each literal is either a variable or its negation.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of product terms include:

$$A \wedge B$$

$$A \wedge (\neg B) \wedge (\neg C)$$

$$\neg A$$

:::

## Origin {#Origin}

The terminology comes from the similarity of AND to multiplication as in the ring structure of [Boolean rings](wikipedia://en/54356).

## Minterms {#Minterms}

For a [boolean function](wikipedia://en/753349) of $n$ variables ${x_1,\dots,x_n}$, a product term in which each of the $n$ variables appears **once** (in either its complemented or uncomplemented form) is called a *minterm*. Thus, a *minterm* is a logical expression of *n* variables that employs only the *complement* operator and the *conjunction* operator.
