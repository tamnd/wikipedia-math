# ShapeShift

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="ShapeShift"}

**ShapeShift AG** was a [cryptocurrency exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_exchange) which operated from 2014 to 2021[^o3] and was headquartered in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland).

:::

## History {#History}

ShapeShift AG was founded July 1, 2014 in [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) by [Erik Voorhees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Voorhees).

In March 2015, ShapeShift received a 525,000 [seed-stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_money) investment by [Roger Ver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ver) and Barry Silbert.[^o5] Additional funding totaling 1.6 million were raised by September 2015, from investors in a second funding round including [Digital Currency Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Currency_Group), [Bitfinex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitfinex), Bitcoin Capital and Mardal Investments.[^o6][^o7]

ShapeShift released initially on the [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) platform in June 2015, allowing users to swap 25 [digital currencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_currency) and value tokens.[^o8]

On 11 June 2015, ShapeShift "cut off service to [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29) in response to the state’s new regulatory policy for digital currency businesses, ... [BitLicense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BitLicense)," which was released in June with the final regulations approved in August.[^o9][^o10][^o11]

In March 2017, ShapeShift raised \$10.4 million in series A funding from both US and international venture capital firms. Berlin-based Earlybird Venture Capital was the lead investor, with additional funding from Lakestar, Access Venture Partners, Pantera Capital and [Blockchain Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain_Capital).[^o12][^o13]

ShapeShift, unlike many exchanges, did not require user identification, allowing for anonymous transactions. This lack of oversight enabled criminals, including North Korean hackers and Ponzi scheme operators, to [launder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_laundering) nearly \$90 million in criminal proceeds, with ShapeShift processing the largest portion of these funds among exchanges with U.S. presence. ShapeShift's policy facilitated the conversion of traceable cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin into untraceable ones like [Monero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monero), effectively obscuring the money trail.[^o14][^o15] A 2018 investigation by the *[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal)* alleged that ShapeShift had facilitated [money laundering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_laundering) of \$90 million in funds from criminal activities over a two-year period.[^o16]

Shortly after, the company began requiring personal identification information from its customers on October 1, 2018.[^o16]
