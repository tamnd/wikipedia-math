# Eberlein–Šmulian theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eberlein–Šmulian theorem"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), the **Eberlein–Šmulian theorem** (named after [William Frederick Eberlein](wikipedia://en/36543793) and [Witold Lwowitsch Schmulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Lwowitsch_Schmulian)) is a result that relates three different kinds of [weak](wikipedia://en/33662) [compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) in a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

**Eberlein–Šmulian theorem**: [^n1] If *X* is a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) and *A* is a subset of *X*, then the following statements are equivalent:

1. each sequence of elements of *A* has a subsequence that is weakly convergent in *X*
1. each sequence of elements of *A* has a weak [cluster point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_point) in *X*
1. the weak closure of *A* is weakly compact.

A set *A* (in any topological space) can be compact in three different ways:

- [Sequential compactness](wikipedia://en/8293820): Every sequence from *A* has a convergent subsequence whose limit is in *A*.
- [Limit point compactness](wikipedia://en/9861462): Every infinite subset of *A* has a [limit point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point) in *A*.
- [Compactness](wikipedia://en/6042) (or [Heine](wikipedia://en/608289)-[Borel](wikipedia://en/356161) compactness): Every open cover of *A* admits a finite subcover.

The Eberlein–Šmulian theorem states that the three are equivalent on a weak topology of a Banach space. While this equivalence is true in general for a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), the weak topology is not metrizable in infinite dimensional vector spaces, and so the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem is needed.

:::

::: {#o6 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The Eberlein–Šmulian theorem is important in the theory of [PDEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_Differential_Equations), and particularly in [Sobolev spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobolev_spaces). Many Sobolev spaces are [reflexive Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/210091) and therefore bounded subsets are weakly precompact by [Alaoglu's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaoglu's_theorem). Thus the theorem implies that bounded subsets are weakly sequentially precompact, and therefore from every bounded sequence of elements of that space it is possible to extract a subsequence which is weakly converging in the space. Since many PDEs only have solutions in the weak sense, this theorem is an important step in deciding which spaces of weak solutions to use in solving a PDE.
