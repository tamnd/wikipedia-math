# Johann Caspar Horner

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Caspar Horner"}

*Image: Hornerkaspar.jpg.* Johann Kaspar Horner

**Johann Caspar Horner** ([Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich), 12 March 1774 – Zürich, 3 November 1834) was a [Swiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_people) [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist), [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer).

:::

## Life {#Life}

At the beginning, he wanted to be a priest, but later he went to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen), where he learnt astronomy. Then he traveled throughout the world for three years on behalf of the Russians. After the journey he took two years in [Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) with the cataloging the items he had found. He discovered a method for approaching the roots of equation with unknown factor in a higher power. His findings were published under the titles *Über die Curven zweiten Grades* and *Die fünf regelmässigen Körper*. He wrote some other works also on the field of astronomy.

In 1805, Johann Caspar Horner visited Japan with the [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) [Georg Heinrich von Langsdorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Heinrich_von_Langsdorff), as a scientist to the [Krusenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Johann_von_Krusenstern) mission that also brought the Russian ambassador [Nikolai Rezanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Rezanov) to Japan.

*Image: JapanHotAirBalloon.jpg.* Johann Caspar Horner making the first demonstration of a Western [hot air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon), in March 1805 in [Umegasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umegasaki).[^o3]

Horner made a hot air balloon out of Japanese paper ([washi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washi)), and made a demonstration in front of about 30 Japanese delegates.[^o4][^o5] This followed the first flight of a [hot air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_air_balloon) by the brothers [Montgolfier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgolfier) in France in 1783.
