# Exposed point

*Image: Extremenotexposed.png.* The two distinguished points are examples of [extreme points](wikipedia://en/454968) of a convex set that are not exposed

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Exposed point"}

In mathematics, an **exposed point** of a [convex set](wikipedia://en/6292) $C$ is a point $x\in C$ at which some [continuous linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) attains its strict [maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum) over $C$.[^o2] Such a functional is then said to *expose* $x$. There can be many exposing functionals for $x$. The set of exposed points of $C$ is usually denoted $\exp(C)$.

:::

A stronger notion is that of *strongly exposed point* of $C$ which is an exposed point $x \in C$ such that some exposing functional $f$ of $x$ attains its strong maximum over $C$ at $x$, i.e. for each sequence $(x_n) \subset C$ we have the following implication: $f(x_n) \to \max f(C) \Longrightarrow \|x_n -x\| \to 0$. The set of all strongly exposed points of $C$ is usually denoted $\operatorname{str}\exp(C)$.

There are two weaker notions, that of [extreme point](wikipedia://en/454968) and that of [support point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Support_point) of $C$.
