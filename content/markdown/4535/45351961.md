# Mei-Chi Shaw

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Mei-Chi Shaw"}

**Mei-Chi Shaw** (; born 1955) is a [Taiwanese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese-American) mathematician. She is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame).[^o3] Her research concerns [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and complex analysis.

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Shaw was born in [Taipei, Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei,_Taiwan), in 1955.[^o5] She graduated from [National Taiwan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Taiwan_University) with a bachelor's degree in mathematics in 1977. She then completed doctoral studies in the United States, earning her [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1981 under [Joseph Kohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Kohn).

After receiving her doctorate, took a postdoctoral position at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University)[^o5] During this time, she married her husband, Hsueh-Chia Chang. In 1983, Shaw took a tenure-track position at [Texas A&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A&M_University), moving to [University of Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Houston) in 1986 and finally relocating to the [University of Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) in 1987, first as an associate professor and then as full professor.

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

In 2012, Shaw became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o7] For 2019 she received the [Stefan Bergman Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Bergman_Prize).[^o8]
