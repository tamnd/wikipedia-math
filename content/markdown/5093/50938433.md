# Robert Harley (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Harley"}

**Robert Harley** (23 January 1828 – 26 July 1910) was an English Congregational minister and mathematician.

:::

## Life {#Life}

Born in [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool) on 23 January 1828, he was third son of Robert Harley by his wife Mary, daughter of William Stevenson, and niece of General Stevenson of [Ayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayr).. The father, after a career as a merchant, became a minister of the [Wesleyan Methodist Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_Methodist_Association). Harley's mathematical aptitude developed at school in [Blackburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn) under William Hoole, and age 16 he was appointed to a mathematical mastership at [Seacombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seacombe), near Liverpool, returning later to teach at Blackburn.[^o3][^o4]

He was elected to corresponding membership of the [Manchester Literary and Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Literary_and_Philosophical_Society) on 30 April 1850.[^o5]

In 1854 Harley entered the Congregational ministry, and was at [Brighouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighouse), Yorkshire, until 1868, after a time also filling the chair of mathematics and logic at [Airedale College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airedale_College) from 1864 to 1868.[^o6] In 1863 he was admitted [Fellow of the Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society). He acted as secretary of the A section of the British Association at meetings at Norwich (1868) and Edinburgh (1871); and was a vice-president of the meetings at Bradford (1873), Bath (1888), and Cardiff (1891).[^o3]

From 1868 to 1872 he was pastor of the oldest Congregational church in [Leicester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicester), and from 1872 to 1881 was vice-principal of [Mill Hill School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_Hill_School), where he officiated in the chapel. At Mill Hill he was instrumental in erecting a public lecture hall for [total abstinence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_abstinence) talks as well as popular entertainment and instruction. From 1882 to 1885 he was principal of [Huddersfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_College), and from 1886 to 1890 minister of the Congregational church in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), where he was made hon. M.A. in 1886.[^o3]

Having taken a ministerial appointment in Australia, Harley was pastor of Heath Church, [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire), from 1892 until 1895, when he retired and settled at [Forest Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Hill,_London), near London. He continued to preach in London and the provinces, and as a [temperance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperance_%28virtue%29) advocate.[^o3]

Harley died at Rosslyn, Westbourne Road, Forest Hill, on 26 July 1910, and was buried in [Ladywell cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladywell_cemetery).[^o3]

## Works {#Works}

Throughout his career mathematics remained a major interest for Harley. He spent time on the [theory of equations](wikipedia://en/583600), especially the theory of the [general equation of the fifth degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_equation_of_the_fifth_degree). His conclusions, which were published in *Memoirs of the Manchester Lit. and Phil. Soc* 1860, xv. 172–219, were independently reached at the same time by [Sir James Cockle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_James_Cockle). Harley's two further papers on the *Theory of Quintics*[^o8] and an exposition of Cockle's method of symmetric products in *[Phil. Trans.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil._Trans.)* (1860) attracted the attention of [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034), who carried the research further.[^o3]

Harley failed to complete the treatise on [quintics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quintic_function) which he had begun, but continued to publish. A sketch of the life and work of [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) appeared in the *British Quarterly Review* (July 1866), and a memoir of his friend, Sir James Cockle, is in the *Proceedings* of the Royal Society, vol. lix.[^o3]

## Family {#Family}

In 1854 Harley married Sara, daughter of James Stroyan of Wigan; she died in 1905.[^o3] Upon his death he was survived by two sons and a daughter.[^o6]

Their son Harold Harley (1860–1937), using the pen name [Mark Ambient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ambient), was a dramatist and librettist of musical comedies.[^o10][^o11]
