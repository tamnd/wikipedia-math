# Clive W. Kilmister

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Clive W. Kilmister"}

**Clive William Kilmister** (3 January 1924 – 2 May 2010) was a British mathematician who specialised in the mathematical foundations of physics, especially quantum mechanics and relativity.

:::

Kilmister attended [Queen Mary College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_College) [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London) for both his under - and postgraduate degrees. Whilst an undergraduate he was friends with [Frank W. J. Olver](wikipedia://en/27325583). In a 1988 Gresham Coillege lecture, he recounted how the two of them would shout requests to formulas to one another in order to prepare for their final exams. Kilmister regarded Olver as a better mathematician than himself, but suggests that had Olver not caught a bout of flu, then Olver would surely of one the schjolarship which launched Kilmister's subsequent career.[^o3] His 1950 PhD on *The Use of Quaternions in Wave-Tensor Calculus* related to [Arthur Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Eddington)'s work, and was supervised by cosmologist [George C. McVittie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_C._McVittie), who was one of Eddington's students.[^o4] His own students included Brian Tupper (1959, [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London), now professor emeritus of general relativity and cosmology at [University of New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Brunswick) Fredericton <https://web.archive.org/web/20110811074000/http://www.unb.ca/fredericton/science/math/Graduate_Program.html>), [Samuel Edgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Edgar) (1977, [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London)), and Tony Crilly (reader in mathematical sciences at Middlesex University).[^o5]

Kilmister was elected as a member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) during his doctoral studies (17 March 1949). Upon graduation, he began his career as an Assistant Lecturer in the Mathematics Department of King's College in 1950. The entirety of his academic career was spent at King's. In 1954, Kilmister founded the King's Gravitational Theory Group, in concert with [Hermann Bondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Bondi) and [Felix Pirani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Pirani), which focused on Einstein's theory of general relativity. At retirement, Kilmister was both a Professor of Mathematics and Head of the King's College Mathematics Department.[^o6]

## Honors, positions, and titles {#Honors,_positions,_and_titles}

- Member, London Mathematical Society, 1949–2010
- President, [British Society for the History of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7795793), 1974–76
- President, [British Society for the Philosophy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Society_for_the_Philosophy_of_Science), 1981–82
- President, [Mathematical Association](wikipedia://en/2742403), 1979–1980
- [Gresham Professor of Geometry](wikipedia://en/4470052), 1972–1988
- Committee Member, [International Society on General Relativity and Gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_on_General_Relativity_and_Gravitation), 1971–1974[^o6]
- Founding Member, [Alternative Natural Philosophy Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Natural_Philosophy_Association)[^o8]
