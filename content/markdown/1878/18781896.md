# Elżbieta Pleszczyńska

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Elżbieta Pleszczyńska"}

**Elżbieta Pleszczyńska** (born 20 March 1933) is a Polish full [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) of [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685)[^o3] and an activist of [disability rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability_rights_movement).[^o22]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

She gained an M.Sc. in mathematics at [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw), Faculty of Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry in 1956.[^o5] She held position at Institute of Mathematics [PAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Sciences) until 1972.[^o3] She received her Ph.D. in 1965 in the area of [discriminant analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant_analysis) ("Power of Test, and Separability of Hypothesis in Statistical Design of Experiments").[^o6] Her [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) thesis, titled "Trend Estimation Problems in Time Series Analysis", was accepted in 1973.[^o6]

From 1967 to 1968, she was a visiting researcher in [University of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wales) (Great Britain), and from 1971 to 1972, in [University of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montreal).[^o3] In 1973, she moved to the Institute of Computer Science, PAS. In 1977, 1979 and 1989, she was awarded by the Polish Academy of Sciences.[^o3] In 1981, she visited [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) invited by [CNR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consiglio_Nazionale_delle_Ricerche).[^o3] In the 1990s, she with her team started the so called [grade data analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_data_analysis), a science of applying [copula](wikipedia://en/1793003) and rank methods to problems of [correspondence](wikipedia://en/11594341) and [cluster analysis](wikipedia://en/669675) together with [outlier](wikipedia://en/160951) detection.[^o7] The adjective *grade* here honors statisticians of the first half of the 20th century, who called [cumulative distribution functions](wikipedia://en/5793) in this way.[^o8] In 1993, the [President of the Republic of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_Poland) [Lech Wałęsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Wałęsa) awarded Elżbieta Pleszczyńska with the Full Professor title in the area of mathematics.[^o3] In 2000, she was an invited consultant of the [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o3]

In the Institute of Computer Science PAS, she had been leader of the Statistical Data Analysis division for many years. According to the Polish law, professors in PAS must retire at the age of 70.[^o9] Her retirement in 2003, although a bit confusing, did not stop her scientific and social activity.[^o10][^o11][^o12][^o13]

### Scientific views {#Scientific_views}

Prof. Pleszczyńska is known for her criticism of the classic statistical approach. Classic [parametric methods](wikipedia://en/223366), like [Pearson correlation coefficient](wikipedia://en/221708), or [least squares method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Least_squares_method) produce comparable results only for comparable distribution types (in practice [multivariate normal distribution](wikipedia://en/50347) is being assumed). Parametric [statistical tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_test) are derived from distribution assumptions. Classic methods fail if the input data contain strong [outliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outliers), and interpretation of their results should be different for different distribution types. In practice, the underlying assumptions are often not checked, moreover they are always violated – there is no normal distribution in the real world, because every real variable is limited (for example people cannot be –170 cm or +2 km tall), and the normal distribution implies positive [probability density](wikipedia://en/43487) for every [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438). In most cases the real distribution is [skewed](wikipedia://en/28212) or [discrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_distribution), which does not prevent people from using normal distribution methods. The extent of this violation can be measured, but its maximum accepted level is just a convention, not mathematics. The parametric methods always work out of their conditions of use. However, their results are often considered valid, which leads to "scientific" validation of false hypothesis. For the reasons mentioned above, Elżbieta Pleszczyńska is a strong advocate of [explorative data analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explorative_data_analysis)[^o15] and [non-parametric statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-parametric_statistics), like [Spearman's rho](wikipedia://en/235623), [Kendall's tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendall_tau_rank_correlation_coefficient), or grade data analysis.

## Social work {#Social_work}

Elżbieta Pleszczyńska has co-founded Foundation Supporting Physically Disabled Mathematicians and Computer Specialists in [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw) (September 1990).[^o22][^o23] She has been the Foundation chairman for multiple years.[^o24][^o25][^o26][^o27]

The Foundation under her leadership was mainly interested in the professional advancement of physically disabled people via technology and [remote work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_work).[^o22][^o28]

Pleszczyńska has been continuously focusing attention of the public opinion on rights of disable people, in her periodical reports.[^o29]

## Private life {#Private_life}

She had a son, Krzysztof Leski, who was a journalist. Pieszczyńska also had two grandchildren. She lived in Warsaw [Wola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wola) district. Since July 2019, she was in elderly care.[^o31]
