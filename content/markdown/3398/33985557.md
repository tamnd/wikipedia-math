# Nearly completely decomposable Markov chain

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nearly completely decomposable Markov chain"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), a **nearly completely decomposable (NCD) Markov chain** is a [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) where the state space can be partitioned in such a way that movement within a partition occurs much more frequently than movement between partitions.[^o2] Particularly efficient algorithms exist to compute the [stationary distribution](wikipedia://en/2524727) of Markov chains with this property.[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

[Ando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Ando) and [Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_M._Fisher) define a completely decomposable matrix as one where "an identical rearrangement of rows and columns leaves a set of square [submatrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submatrices) on the [principal diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_diagonal) and zeros everywhere else." A nearly completely decomposable matrix is one where an identical rearrangement of rows and columns leaves a set of square submatrices on the principal diagonal and *small nonzeros* everywhere else.[^o6][^o7]

:::

::: {#o9 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

A [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) with [transition matrix](wikipedia://en/217313)

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2} \\ \end{pmatrix} + \epsilon \begin{pmatrix} -\frac{1}{2} & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & -\frac{1}{2} & 0 & \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2} & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \\ \end{pmatrix}$$

is nearly completely decomposable if *ε* is small (say 0.1).[^o10]

:::

## Stationary distribution algorithms {#Stationary_distribution_algorithms}

Special-purpose iterative algorithms have been designed for NCD Markov chains[^o3] though the multi–level algorithm, a general purpose algorithm,[^o12] has been shown experimentally to be competitive and in some cases significantly faster.[^o13]
