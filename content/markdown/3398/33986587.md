# M/G/k queue

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="M/G/k queue"}

In [queueing theory](wikipedia://en/50578), a discipline within the mathematical [theory of probability](wikipedia://en/23542), an **M/G/k queue** is a queue model where arrivals are [Markovian](wikipedia://en/306422) (modulated by a [Poisson process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poisson_process)), service times have a [general distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) and there are *k* servers. The model name is written in [Kendall's notation](wikipedia://en/7345405), and is an extension of the [M/M/c queue](wikipedia://en/23152853), where service times must be [exponentially distributed](wikipedia://en/45906) and of the [M/G/1 queue](wikipedia://en/33378064) with a single server. Most performance metrics for this queueing system are not known and remain an [open problem](wikipedia://en/1333335).[^o2]

:::

## Model definition {#Model_definition}

A queue represented by a M/G/*k* queue is a stochastic process whose [state space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_space) is the set {0,1,2,3...}, where the value corresponds to the number of customers in the queue, including any being served. Transitions from state *i* to *i* + 1 represent the arrival of a new customer: the times between such arrivals have an [exponential distribution](wikipedia://en/45906) with parameter λ. Transitions from state *i* to *i* − 1 represent the departure of a customer who has just finished being served: the length of time required for serving an individual customer has a general distribution function. The lengths of times between arrivals and of service periods are [random variables](wikipedia://en/25685) which are assumed to be [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent).

## Steady state distribution {#Steady_state_distribution}

Tijms *et al.* believe it is "not likely that computationally tractable methods can be developed to compute the exact numerical values of the steady-state probability in the M/G/*k* queue."[^o5]

Various approximations for the average queue size,[^o6] stationary distribution[^o7][^o8] and approximation by a [reflected Brownian motion](wikipedia://en/35046943)[^o9][^o10] have been offered by different authors. Recently a new approximate approach based on [Laplace transform](wikipedia://en/18610) for steady state probabilities has been proposed by Hamzeh Khazaei *et al.*.[^o11][^o12] This new approach is yet accurate enough in cases of large number of servers and when the distribution of service time has a [Coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) more than one.

## Average delay/waiting time {#Average_delay/waiting_time}

There are numerous approximations for the average delay a job experiences.[^o8][^o10][^o14][^o15][^o16][^o17] The first such was given in 1959 using a factor to adjust the mean waiting time in an [M/M/c queue](wikipedia://en/23152853)[^o21][^o18] This result is sometimes known as Kingman's law of congestion.[^o19]

$$E[W^{\text{M/G/}k}] = \frac{C^2+1}{2} \mathbb E [ W^{\text{M/M/}k}]$$

where *C* is the [coefficient of variation](wikipedia://en/1012687) of the service time distribution. [Ward Whitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_Whitt) described this approximation as “usually an excellent approximation, even given extra information about the service-time distribution."[^o20]

However, it is known that no approximation using only the first two moments can be accurate in all cases.[^o21]

A [Markov–Krein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov–Krein) characterization has been shown to produce tight bounds on the mean waiting time.[^o22]

## Inter-departure times {#Inter-departure_times}

It is conjectured that the times between departures, given a departure leaves *n* customers in a queue, has a mean which as *n* tends to infinity is different from the intuitive 1/*μ* result.[^o24]

## Two servers {#Two_servers}

For an M/G/2 queue (the model with two servers) the problem of determining marginal probabilities can be reduced to solving a pair of [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234)[^o26] or the Laplace transform of the distribution when the service time distribution is a mixture of exponential distributions.[^o27] The Laplace transform of queue length[^o28] and waiting time distributions[^o29] can be computed when the waiting time distribution has a rational Laplace transform.
