# Alexander Weinstein

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Alexander Weinstein"}

**Alexander Weinstein** (21 January 1897 – 6 November 1979) was a mathematician who worked on [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) in [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034).

:::

## Early life, family and personal life {#Early_life,_family_and_personal_life}

Weinstein was born to Judel Lejb Weinstein and Praskovya Levkovich, his family was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews), and his father was a [doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine).[^o4][^o5] His family moved to [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan), but later decided to emigrate to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), there Weinstein completed his schooling, having studied first in [Würzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Würzburg), then at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) during 1913/14.[^o6]

After his graduation, he left for [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and soon undertook research for [Hermann Weyl](wikipedia://en/187544) and [Rudolf Fueter](wikipedia://en/42010211) and was awarded a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in 1921 for his thesis on the [tensor calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_calculus) and linear groups of matrices.[^o6][^o7] [Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl) suggested Weinstein to several prominent mathematicians, including [Paul Sophus Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sophus_Epstein), who was then worked at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology).[^o4] In the end, Weinstein worked as an assistant of [Leon Lichtenstein](wikipedia://en/31433458) at the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) in 1922.[^o4] In 1924, Weinstein returned to [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) and continued his research into [hydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamics), he published two works on the matter.[^o8][^o9][^o10]

Weinstein was a target of [xenophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophobia), so he struggled to find a "scientific position adequate to his abilities in Switzerland", as such [Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl) recommended Weinstein for a [Rockfeller Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_fellowship), with him being awarded it, and spent two years (1926/27) in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), where he worked with [Tulio Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180).[^o4][^o6] With [Levi-Civita](wikipedia://en/499180), Weinstein published three more works before he returned to [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich) as a [privatdocent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) in [Weyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyl)'s chair, then in 1928 he was appointed to the [Hamburg Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_University_of_Technology), he also joined the [German Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/30864973).[^o5][^o7][^o10] He married Marianne Olga Louise Ganz on 13 March 1928 in [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg), they did not have any children.[^o5]

By 1933, he was sought by [Albert Einstein](wikipedia://en/736) as a collaborator in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), however after the electoral success of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party), Weinstein, being of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews) background, instead went to [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) and the [Collège de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collège_de_France) in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989), where he worked with [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675).[^o6][^o7] He was awarded the degree of Docteur ès Sciences Mathématiques in 1937, and spent a few semesters in [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), and the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), before returning to [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989).[^o4][^o6][^o5][^o7] In May 1940, after the [Nazi invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_invasion_of_France), Weinstein and his wife fled to [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal) with the hope they could seek [refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee) to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o4]

They arrived in [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) on 26 October 1940, and lived at 22 West 75th Street, for the next eight years Weinstein taught at a number of different places, and [became a citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization) in 1946.[^o4] Together with Monroe Martin, an expert on [classical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), he founded the Institute of Fluid Dynamics and Applied Mathematics (later renamed the [Institute of Physical Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park)) at [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland) in 1949.[^o4][^o7][^o6]

Weinstein's research covered numerous topics, he is famous for having solved [Helmholtz's problem for jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_resonance), giving the first [uniqueness and existence theorem](wikipedia://en/251177) for free jets in his papers from 1923 to 1929, and he examined many [boundary problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_problem_%28spatial_analysis%29), whilst giving hydrodynamic and electromagnetic applications.[^o5][^o7] Weinstein's method was later developed to give accurate bounds of [eigenvalues](wikipedia://en/2161429) of plates and membranes, and he introduced a new branch of [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) through his examination of singular [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564).[^o5][^o7]

Weinstein retired in 1967, yet continued research at the [American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University) in [Washington D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.), he worked from 1968 to 1972 at [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University).[^o4][^o6] In 1972, Weinstein published alongside [William Stenger](https://www.mathgenealogy.org/id.php?id=156429), the book *Methods of Intermediate problems for eigenvalues*, and then in 1978, when Weinstein was eighty years old, Joe D Diaz made a collection of Weinstein's writings.[^o6][^o5] He died on 6 November 1979, following a [surgical operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgery).[^o4][^o6]
