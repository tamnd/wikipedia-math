# Richard Bellman

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Richard Bellman"}

**Richard Ernest Bellman**[^o4] (August 26, 1920 – March 19, 1984) was an American [applied mathematician](wikipedia://en/24295969), who introduced [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) in 1953, and made important contributions in other fields of mathematics, such as biomathematics. He founded the leading biomathematical journal [Mathematical Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biosciences), as well as the [Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications](wikipedia://en/54943204).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Bellman was born in 1920 in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City) to non-practising[^o6] Jewish parents of Polish and Russian descent, Pearl (née Saffian) and John James Bellman,[^o9] who ran a small grocery store on [Bergen Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_Street_Line) near [Prospect Park, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Park_%28Brooklyn%29).[^o7] He was an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist).[^o8] He attended [Abraham Lincoln High School, Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln_High_School_%28Brooklyn,_New_York%29) in 1937,[^o9] and studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) at [Brooklyn College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_College) where he earned a [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in 1941. He later earned an [MA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) from the [University of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Madison). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), he worked for a [Theoretical Physics](wikipedia://en/19594028) Division group in [Los Alamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory). In 1946, he received his Ph.D. at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) under the supervision of [Solomon Lefschetz](wikipedia://en/384384).[^o10] Beginning in 1949, Bellman worked for many years at [RAND corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAND_corporation), and it was during this time that he developed [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297).[^o11]

Later in life, Richard Bellman's interests began to emphasize biology and medicine, which he identified as "the frontiers of contemporary science". In 1967, he became founding editor of the journal *[Mathematical Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_Biosciences)*, which rapidly became (and remains) one of the most important journals in the field of Mathematical Biology. In 1985, the [Bellman Prize in Mathematical Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellman_Prize_in_Mathematical_Biosciences) was created in his honor, being awarded biannually to the journal's best research paper.

Bellman was diagnosed with a brain tumor in 1973, which was removed but resulted in complications that left him severely disabled. He was a professor at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California), a Fellow in the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) (1975),[^o12] a member of the [National Academy of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Engineering) (1977),[^o13] and a member of the National Academy of Sciences (1983).

He was awarded the [IEEE Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Medal_of_Honor) in 1979, "for contributions to decision processes and control system theory, particularly the creation and application of dynamic programming".[^o14] His key work is the [Bellman equation](wikipedia://en/1236458).

## Work {#Work}

### Bellman equation {#Bellman_equation}

A [Bellman equation](wikipedia://en/1236458), also known as the *dynamic programming equation*, is a necessary condition for optimality associated with the mathematical optimization method known as [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297). Almost any problem which can be solved using [optimal control theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_control_theory) can also be solved by analyzing the appropriate Bellman equation. The Bellman equation was first applied to engineering [control theory](wikipedia://en/7039) and to other topics in applied mathematics, and subsequently became an important tool in [economic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_theory).[^o17]

### Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation {#Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman_equation}

The [Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation](wikipedia://en/730585) (HJB) is a [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) which is central to [optimal control](wikipedia://en/362565) theory. The solution of the HJB equation is the 'value function', which gives the optimal cost-to-go for a given [dynamical system](wikipedia://en/9087) with an associated cost function. Classical variational problems, for example, the [brachistochrone problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachistochrone_problem) can be solved using this method as well. The equation is a result of the theory of [dynamic programming](wikipedia://en/125297) which was pioneered in the 1950s by Richard Bellman and coworkers. The corresponding discrete-time equation is usually referred to as the [Bellman equation](wikipedia://en/1236458). In continuous time, the result can be seen as an extension of earlier work in [classical physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_physics) on the [Hamilton–Jacobi equation](wikipedia://en/897539) by [William Rowan Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) and [Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340).[^o19]

### Curse of dimensionality {#Curse_of_dimensionality}

The *curse of dimensionality* is an expression coined by Bellman to describe the problem caused by the exponential increase in [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) associated with adding extra dimensions to a (mathematical) space. One implication of the curse of dimensionality is that some methods for numerical solution of the Bellman equation require vastly more computer time when there are more state variables in the value function. For example, 100 evenly spaced sample points suffice to sample a [unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) with no more than 0.01 distance between points; an equivalent sampling of a 10-dimensional [unit hypercube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_hypercube) with a lattice with a spacing of 0.01 between adjacent points would require 10<sup>20</sup> sample points: thus, in some sense, the 10-dimensional hypercube can be said to be a factor of 10<sup>18</sup> "larger" than the unit interval. (Adapted from an example by R. E. Bellman, see below.)[^o21]

### Bellman–Ford algorithm {#Bellman–Ford_algorithm}

Though discovering the algorithm after Ford, he is referred to in the [Bellman–Ford algorithm](wikipedia://en/221244), also sometimes referred to as the Label Correcting Algorithm, which computes single-source shortest paths in a [weighted digraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighted_digraph) where some of the [edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) weights may be negative. [Dijkstra's algorithm](wikipedia://en/45809) accomplishes the same problem with a lower running time, but requires edge weights to be non-negative.

## Publications {#Publications}

Over the course of his career he published 619 papers and 39 books. During the last 11 years of his life he published over 100 papers despite suffering from crippling complications of brain surgery (Dreyfus, 2003). A selection:[^o9]

- \1957. *Dynamic Programming*
- \1959. *Asymptotic Behavior of Solutions of Differential Equations*
- \1961. *An Introduction to Inequalities*
- \1961. *Adaptive Control Processes: A Guided Tour*
- \1962. *Applied Dynamic Programming*
- \1967. *Introduction to the Mathematical Theory of Control Processes*
- \1970. *Algorithms, Graphs and Computers*
- \1972. *Dynamic Programming and Partial Differential Equations*
- \1982. *Mathematical Aspects of Scheduling and Applications*
- \1983. *Mathematical Methods in Medicine*
- \1984. *Partial Differential Equations*
- \1984. *Eye of the Hurricane: An Autobiography,* World Scientific Publishing.
- \1985. *Artificial Intelligence*
- \1995. *Modern Elementary Differential Equations*
- \1997. *Introduction to Matrix Analysis*
- \2003. *Dynamic Programming*
- \2003. *Perturbation Techniques in Mathematics, Engineering and Physics*
- \2003. *Stability Theory of Differential Equations* (originally publ. 1953)[^o24]
