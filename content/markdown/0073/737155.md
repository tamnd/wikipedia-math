# Complex projective plane

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Complex projective plane"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **complex projective plane**, usually denoted $\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{C})$ or $\mathbb{CP}^2,$ is the two-dimensional [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). It is a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) of complex dimension 2, described by three complex coordinates

:::

$$(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3) \in \mathbb{C}^3, \qquad (Z_1,Z_2,Z_3)\neq (0,0,0)$$

where, however, the triples differing by an overall rescaling are identified:

$$(Z_1,Z_2,Z_3) \equiv (\lambda Z_1,\lambda Z_2, \lambda Z_3); \quad \lambda \in \mathbb{C}, \qquad \lambda \neq 0.$$

That is, these are [homogeneous coordinates](wikipedia://en/243316) in the traditional sense of [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849).

## Topology {#Topology}

The [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) of the complex projective plane are

1, 0, 1, 0, 1, 0, 0, .....

The middle dimension 2 is accounted for by the homology class of the complex projective line, or [Riemann sphere](wikipedia://en/30876799), lying in the plane. The nontrivial homotopy groups of the complex projective plane are $\pi_2=\pi_5=\mathbb{Z}$. The fundamental group is trivial and all other higher homotopy groups are those of the 5-sphere, i.e. torsion.

## Algebraic geometry {#Algebraic_geometry}

In [birational geometry](wikipedia://en/382733), a complex [rational surface](wikipedia://en/2380148) is any [algebraic surface](wikipedia://en/648311) birationally equivalent to the complex projective plane. It is known that any non-singular rational variety is obtained from the plane by a sequence of [blowing up](wikipedia://en/1817790) transformations and their inverses ('blowing down') of curves, which must be of a very particular type. As a special case, a non-singular complex [quadric](wikipedia://en/145570) in $\mathbb P^3$ is obtained from the plane by blowing up two points to curves, and then blowing down the line through these two points; the inverse of this transformation can be seen by taking a point $P$ on the quadric $Q$, blowing it up, and projecting onto a general plane in $\mathbb P^3$ by drawing lines through $P$.

The group of birational automorphisms of the complex projective plane is the [Cremona group](wikipedia://en/7252026).

## Differential geometry {#Differential_geometry}

As a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652), the complex projective plane is a 4-dimensional manifold whose sectional curvature is quarter-pinched, but not strictly so. That is, it attains *both* bounds and thus evades being a sphere, as the [sphere theorem](wikipedia://en/4685719) would otherwise require. The rival normalisations are for the curvature to be pinched between 1/4 and 1; alternatively, between 1 and 4. With respect to the former normalisation, the imbedded surface defined by the complex projective line has [Gaussian curvature](wikipedia://en/285623) 1. With respect to the latter normalisation, the imbedded real projective plane has Gaussian curvature 1.

An explicit demonstration of the Riemann and Ricci tensors is given in the *n*=2 subsection of the article on the [Fubini-Study metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fubini-Study_metric).
