# Large diffeomorphism

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Large diffeomorphism"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), a **large diffeomorphism** is an equivalence class of [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564) under the equivalence relation where diffeomorphisms that can be continuously connected to each other are in the same equivalence class.

:::

For example, a two-dimensional real [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) has a [SL(2,Z)](wikipedia://en/144823) group of large diffeomorphisms by which the 1-cycles $a,b$ of the torus are transformed into their integer linear combinations. This group of large diffeomorphisms is called the [modular group](wikipedia://en/325019).

More generally, for a [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) *S*, the structure of [self-homeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/13660) up to [homotopy](wikipedia://en/212250) is known as the [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295). It is known (for [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), [orientable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientable) *S*) that this is isomorphic with the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of *S*. This is consistent with the [genus](wikipedia://en/61891) 1 case, stated above, if one takes into account that then the fundamental group is *Z*<sup>2</sup>, on which the modular group acts as automorphisms (as a subgroup of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2 in all automorphisms, since the orientation may also be reverse, by a transformation with determinant −1).
