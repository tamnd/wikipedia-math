# Secundum quid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Secundum quid"}

***Secundum quid*** (comes from the Latin "a dicto simpliciter ad dictum secundum quid", meaning "\[what is true\] in a certain respect and \[what is true\] absolutely" or "from the statement unqualified to the statement qualified).[^o2] It is a type of informal fallacy that occurs when the arguer omits the fact that a statement applies with qualifications or exceptions, or fails to recognize the difference between [rules of thumb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rules_of_thumb) (*soft* generalizations, [heuristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heuristics) that hold true *as a general rule* but leave room for exceptions) and [categorical propositions](wikipedia://en/2418429), rules that hold true universally.

:::

Since it ignores the limits, or qualifications, of rules of thumb, this fallacy is also named **ignoring qualifications** or **sweeping generalizations**. The expression **misuse of a principle** can be used as well.[^o11]

::: {#o4 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Let me tell you: all great composers die young. Take Mendelssohn: he was 38. Or Mozart, just 35. And Schubert! Hundreds of songs, and he was only 31. The arguer cites only the cases that support his point, conveniently omitting Bach, Beethoven, Brahms etc

:::

## In popular culture {#In_popular_culture}

The following quatrain can be attributed to C. H. Talbot:

I talked in terms whose sense was hid, *[Dividendo](wikipedia://en/522936), [componendo](wikipedia://en/523043) et secundum quid*; Now *secundum quid* is a wise remark And it earned my reputation as a learned clerk.

## 2 Types {#2_Types}

Instances of *secundum quid* are of two kinds:

- [Accident](wikipedia://en/735233) — *a dicto simpliciter ad dictum secundum quid* (where an acceptable exception is ignored) \[from general to qualified\]. This is taking the usual case and inappropriately applying it to special cases. This is when we can't or don't want to list the exceptions to a reasoning, and then use it as a generalization as if there was no exception.[^o7]
- [Converse accident](wikipedia://en/735324) — *a dicto secundum quid ad dictum simpliciter* (where an acceptable exception is eliminated or simplified) \[from qualified to general\]. This is taking one unusual case and inappropriately applying it to all cases. This is when we focus on one exception and use it to draw a generalization. It is close to [hasty generalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasty_generalization). [^o8]
