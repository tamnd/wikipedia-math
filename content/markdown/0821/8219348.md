# Wilks's lambda distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilks's lambda distribution"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Wilks' lambda distribution** (named for [Samuel S. Wilks](wikipedia://en/8136395)), is a [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) used in [multivariate](wikipedia://en/19384) [hypothesis testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis_testing), especially with regard to the [likelihood-ratio test](wikipedia://en/45035) and [multivariate analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/682847) (MANOVA).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Wilks' lambda distribution is defined from two [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) [Wishart distributed](wikipedia://en/364996) variables as the [ratio distribution](wikipedia://en/9519371) of their [determinants](wikipedia://en/8468),[^o4]

given

$$\mathbf{A} \sim W_p(\Sigma, m) \qquad \mathbf{B} \sim W_p(\Sigma, n)$$

independent and with $m \ge p$

$$\lambda = \frac{\det(\mathbf{A})}{\det(\mathbf{A+B})} = \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{B})} \sim \Lambda(p,m,n)$$

where *p* is the number of dimensions. In the context of [likelihood-ratio tests](wikipedia://en/45035) *m* is typically the error degrees of freedom, and *n* is the hypothesis degrees of freedom, so that $n+m$ is the total degrees of freedom.[^o4]

:::

::: {#o6 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

There is a symmetry among the parameters of the Wilks distribution,[^o4]

$$\Lambda(p, m, n) \sim \Lambda(n, m + n - p, p)$$

### Approximations {#Approximations}

Computations or tables of the Wilks' distribution for higher dimensions are not readily available and one usually resorts to approximations. One approximation is attributed to [M. S. Bartlett](wikipedia://en/865429) and works for large *m*[^o8] allows Wilks' lambda to be approximated with a [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424)

$\left(\frac{p-n+1}{2}-m\right)\log \Lambda(p,m,n) \sim \chi^2_{np}.$[^o4]

Another approximation is attributed to [C. R. Rao](wikipedia://en/1418009).[^o4][^o9]

:::

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

The distribution can be related to a product of [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_independence) [beta-distributed](wikipedia://en/207074) random variables

$$u_i \sim B\left(\frac{m+i-p}{2},\frac{p}{2}\right)$$

$$\prod_{i=1}^n u_i \sim \Lambda(p,m,n).$$

As such it can be regarded as a multivariate generalization of the beta distribution.

It follows directly that for a one-dimension problem, when the Wishart distributions are one-dimensional with $p=1$ (i.e., chi-squared-distributed), then the Wilks' distribution equals the beta-distribution with a certain parameter set,

$$\Lambda(1,m,n) \sim B\left(\frac{m}{2},\frac{n}{2}\right).$$

From the relations between a beta and an [F-distribution](wikipedia://en/332116), Wilks' lambda can be related to the F-distribution when one of the parameters of the Wilks lambda distribution is either 1 or 2, e.g.,[^o4]

$$\frac{1 - \Lambda(p, m, 1)}{\Lambda(p, m, 1)} \sim \frac{p}{m - p + 1} F_{p, m-p+1},$$

and

$$\frac{1 - \sqrt{\Lambda(p, m, 2)}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(p, m, 2)}} \sim \frac{p}{m - p + 1} F_{2p, 2(m-p+1)}.$$
