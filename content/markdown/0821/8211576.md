# Geoboard

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Geoboard"}

*Image: Geoboard.JPG.* A wooden 3 × 3 geoboard

*Image: 25 peg geoboard.JPG.* A plastic 5 × 5 geoboard

A **geoboard** is a [mathematical manipulative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_manipulatives) used to explore basic concepts in [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry) such as [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636), [area](wikipedia://en/1209) and the characteristics of [triangles](wikipedia://en/30654) and other [polygons](wikipedia://en/23621). It consists of a physical board with a certain number of nails half driven in, around which are wrapped geo bands that are made of rubber. Normal rubber bands can also be used.

:::

Geoboards were invented and popularized in the 1950s by Egyptian mathematician [Caleb Gattegno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Gattegno) (1911-1988).[^o2]

## Structure and use {#Structure_and_use}

Geoboard is a board. A variety of boards are used. Originally made out of plywood and brass nails or pegs, geoboards are now usually made out of plastic. They may have an upright [square lattice](wikipedia://en/2874853) of 9, 16 or 25 nails or more, or a circle of nails around a central nail. Students are asked to place rubber bands around the nails to explore geometric concepts or to solve mathematical puzzles.

Geoboards may be used to learn about:[^o4]

- plane shapes;
- translation;
- rotation;
- reflection;
- similarity;
- co-ordination;
- counting;
- right angles;
- pattern;
- classification;
- scaling;
- position;
- congruence;
- area;
- perimeter.

Two-dimensional representations of the geoboard may be applied to ordinary paper using [rubber stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_stamp) or special "geoboard paper" with diagrams of geoboards may be used to help capture a student's explanations of the concept they have discovered or illustrated on the geoboard. There are also a number of online virtual geoboards.
