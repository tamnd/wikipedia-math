# Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture"}

The **Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture** is a claim in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). It was formulated by [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501) and [Tudor Ganea](wikipedia://en/8185502) in 1957, in a short but influential paper. It states that if a group *G* has [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) 2, then it has a 2-dimensional [Eilenberg–MacLane space](wikipedia://en/1073230) $K(G,1)$. For *n* different from 2, a group *G* of [cohomological dimension](wikipedia://en/9880197) *n* has an *n*-dimensional Eilenberg–MacLane space. It is also known that a group of cohomological dimension 2 has a 3-dimensional Eilenberg−MacLane space.

:::

In 1997, [Mladen Bestvina](wikipedia://en/26130054) and Noel Brady constructed a group *G* so that either *G* is a counterexample to the Eilenberg–Ganea conjecture, or there must be a counterexample to the [Whitehead conjecture](wikipedia://en/8213034); in other words, it is not possible for both conjectures to be true.
