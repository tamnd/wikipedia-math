# Graph entropy

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graph entropy"}

In [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773), the **graph entropy** is a measure of the information rate achievable by communicating symbols over a channel in which certain pairs of values may be confused.[^o2] This measure, first introduced by Körner in the 1970s,[^o3][^o4] has since also proven itself useful in other settings, including combinatorics.[^o5]

:::

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph). The graph entropy of $G$, denoted $H(G)$ is defined as

$$H(G) = \min_{X,Y} I(X ; Y)$$

where $X$ is chosen [uniformly](wikipedia://en/1699214) from $V$, $Y$ ranges over [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) of G, the joint distribution of $X$ and $Y$ is such that $X\in Y$ with probability one, and $I(X ; Y)$ is the [mutual information](wikipedia://en/427282) of $X$ and $Y$.[^o8]

That is, if we let $\mathcal{I}$ denote the independent vertex sets in $G$, we wish to find the joint distribution $X,Y$ on $V \times \mathcal{I}$ with the lowest mutual information such that (i) the [marginal distribution](wikipedia://en/506077) of the first term is uniform and (ii) in samples from the distribution, the second term contains the first term almost surely. The mutual information of $X$ and $Y$ is then called the entropy of $G$.

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- Monotonicity. If $G_1$ is a subgraph of $G_2$ on the same vertex set, then $H(G_1) \leq H(G_2)$.
- Subadditivity. Given two graphs $G_1 = (V, E_1)$ and $G_2 = (V, E_2)$ on the same set of vertices, the [graph union](wikipedia://en/9938244#Binary_operations) $G_1 \cup G_2 = (V, E_1 \cup E_2)$ satisfies $H(G_1 \cup G_2) \leq H(G_1) + H(G_2)$.
- Arithmetic mean of disjoint unions. Let $G_1, G_2, \cdots, G_k$ be a sequence of graphs on [disjoint sets](wikipedia://en/39359) of vertices, with $n_1, n_2, \cdots, n_k$ vertices, respectively. Then $H(G_1 \cup G_2 \cup \cdots G_k) = \tfrac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_i}\sum_{i=1}^{k}{n_i H(G_i)}$.

Additionally, simple formulas exist for certain families classes of graphs.

- Complete balanced [k-partite graphs](wikipedia://en/39469099) have entropy $\log_2 k$. In particular,
  - Edge-less graphs have entropy $0$.
  - [Complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) on $n$ vertices have entropy $\log_2 n$.
  - Complete balanced [bipartite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartite_graphs) have entropy $1$.
- Complete [bipartite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipartite_graphs) with $n$ vertices in one partition and $m$ in the other have entropy $H\left(\frac{n}{m+n}\right)$, where $H$ is the [binary entropy function](wikipedia://en/5275277).

:::

::: {#o12 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

Here, we use properties of graph entropy to provide a simple proof that a complete graph $G$ on $n$ vertices cannot be expressed as the union of fewer than $\log_2 n$ bipartite graphs.

*Proof* By monotonicity, no bipartite graph can have graph entropy greater than that of a complete bipartite graph, which is bounded by $1$. Thus, by sub-additivity, the union of $k$ bipartite graphs cannot have entropy greater than $k$. Now let $G = (V, E)$ be a complete graph on $n$ vertices. By the properties listed above, $H(G) = \log_2 n$. Therefore, the union of fewer than $\log_2 n$ bipartite graphs cannot have the same entropy as $G$, so $G$ cannot be expressed as such a union. $\blacksquare$

:::
