# Asymptotic dimension

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Asymptotic dimension"}

In [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), **asymptotic dimension** of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) is a large-scale analog of [Lebesgue covering dimension](wikipedia://en/398886). The notion of asymptotic dimension was introduced by [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Leonidovich_Gromov) in his 1993 monograph *Asymptotic invariants of infinite groups*[^o2] in the context of [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164), as a [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) invariant of finitely generated groups. As shown by [Guoliang Yu](wikipedia://en/47741388), finitely generated groups of finite homotopy type with finite asymptotic dimension satisfy the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194).[^o13] Asymptotic dimension has important applications in [geometric analysis](wikipedia://en/4325474) and [index theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_theory).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $X$ be a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) space and $n\ge 0$ be an integer. We say that $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le n$ if for every $R\ge 1$ there exists a uniformly bounded cover $\mathcal U$ of $X$ such that every closed $R$-ball in $X$ intersects at most $n+1$ subsets from $\mathcal U$. Here 'uniformly bounded' means that $\sup_{U\in \mathcal U} \operatorname{diam}(U) <\infty$.

We then define the *asymptotic dimension* $\operatorname{asdim}(X)$ as the smallest integer $n\ge 0$ such that $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le n$, if at least one such $n$ exists, and define $\operatorname{asdim}(X):=\infty$ otherwise.

Also, one says that a family $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ of metric spaces satisfies $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le n$ *uniformly* if for every $R\ge 1$ and every $i\in I$ there exists a cover $\mathcal U_i$ of $X_i$ by sets of diameter at most $D(R)<\infty$ (independent of $i$) such that every closed $R$-ball in $X_i$ intersects at most $n+1$ subsets from $\mathcal U_i$.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- If $X$ is a metric space of bounded diameter then $\operatorname{asdim}(X)=0$.
- $$\operatorname{asdim}(\mathbb R)=\operatorname{asdim}(\mathbb Z)=1.$$
- $$\operatorname{asdim}(\mathbb R^n)=n.$$
- $$\operatorname{asdim}(\mathbb H^n)=n.$$

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $Y\subseteq X$ is a subspace of a metric space $X$, then $\operatorname{asdim}(Y)\le \operatorname{asdim}(X)$.
- For any metric spaces $X$ and $Y$ one has $\operatorname{asdim}(X\times Y)\le \operatorname{asdim}(X)+\operatorname{asdim}(Y)$.
- If $A,B\subseteq X$ then $\operatorname{asdim}(A\cup B)\le \max\{\operatorname{asdim}(A), \operatorname{asdim}(B)\}$.
- If $f:Y\to X$ is a coarse embedding (e.g. a quasi-isometric embedding), then $\operatorname{asdim}(Y)\le \operatorname{asdim}(X)$.
- If $X$ and $Y$ are coarsely equivalent metric spaces (e.g. quasi-isometric metric spaces), then $\operatorname{asdim}(X)= \operatorname{asdim}(Y)$.
- If $X$ is a [real tree](wikipedia://en/371914) then $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le 1$.
- Let $f : X\to Y$ be a Lipschitz map from a geodesic metric space $X$ to a metric space $Y$ . Suppose that for every $r > 0$ the set family $\{f^{-1}(B_r(y))\}_{y\in Y}$ satisfies the inequality $\operatorname{asdim} \le n$ uniformly. Then $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le \operatorname{asdim}(Y) +n.$ See[^o9]
- If $X$ is a metric space with $\operatorname{asdim}(X)<\infty$ then $X$ admits a coarse (uniform) embedding into a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932).[^o10]
- If $X$ is a metric space of bounded geometry with $\operatorname{asdim}(X)\le n$ then $X$ admits a coarse embedding into a product of $n+1$ locally finite simplicial trees.[^o11]

:::

## Asymptotic dimension in geometric group theory {#Asymptotic_dimension_in_geometric_group_theory}

Asymptotic dimension achieved particular prominence in [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164) after a 1998 paper of [Guoliang Yu](wikipedia://en/47741388)[^o13] , which proved that if $G$ is a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) of finite homotopy type (that is with a classifying space of the homotopy type of a finite CW-complex) such that $\operatorname{asdim}(G)<\infty$, then $G$ satisfies the [Novikov conjecture](wikipedia://en/3035194). As was subsequently shown,[^o14] finitely generated groups with finite asymptotic dimension are *topologically amenable*, i.e. satisfy [Guoliang Yu](wikipedia://en/47741388)'s *Property A* introduced in[^o15] and equivalent to the exactness of the reduced [C\*-algebra](wikipedia://en/7184) of the group.

- If $G$ is a [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) then $\operatorname{asdim}(G)<\infty$.[^o16]
- If $G$ is [relatively hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_hyperbolic) with respect to subgroups $H_1,\dots, H_k$ each of which has finite asymptotic dimension then $\operatorname{asdim}(G)<\infty$.[^o17]
- $$\operatorname{asdim}(\mathbb Z^n)=n.$$
- If $H\le G$, where $H,G$ are finitely generated, then $\operatorname{asdim}(H)\le \operatorname{asdim}(G)$.
- For [Thompson's group F](wikipedia://en/612245) we have $\operatorname{asdim}(F)=\infty$ since $F$ contains subgroups isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^n$ for arbitrarily large $n$.
- If $G$ is the fundamental group of a finite [graph of groups](wikipedia://en/12281773) $\mathbb A$ with underlying graph $A$ and finitely generated vertex groups, then[^o18]

$$\operatorname{asdim}(G)\le 1+ \max_{v\in VY} \operatorname{asdim} (A_v).$$

- [Mapping class groups](wikipedia://en/793295) of orientable finite type surfaces have finite asymptotic dimension.[^o19]
- Let $G$ be a connected [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) and let $\Gamma\le G$ be a finitely generated discrete subgroup. Then $\operatorname{asdim}(\Gamma)<\infty$.[^o20]
- The fundamental group of a compact [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) has asymptotic dimension at most 3.[^o21]
- It is not known if [$Out(F_n)$](wikipedia://en/12747184) has finite asymptotic dimension for $n>2$.[^o22]
