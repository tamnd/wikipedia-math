# Hoffman–Singleton graph

*Image: Hoffman singleton graph circle2.gif.* The Hoffman–Singleton graph. The subgraph of blue edges is a sum of ten disjoint pentagons.

::: {#o6 .subject cue=lead name="Hoffman–Singleton graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Hoffman–Singleton graph** is a 7-[regular](wikipedia://en/85821) [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) with 50 vertices and 175 edges. It is the unique [strongly regular graph](wikipedia://en/1399856) with parameters (50,7,0,1).[^o20] It was constructed by [Alan Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hoffman_%28mathematician%29) and Robert Singleton while trying to classify all [Moore graphs](wikipedia://en/4112719), and is the highest-order Moore graph known to exist.[^o7] Since it is a [Moore graph](wikipedia://en/4112719) where each vertex has [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448) 7, and the [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) is 5, it is a (7,5)-[cage](wikipedia://en/7735022).

:::

## Construction {#Construction}

Here are some constructions of the Hoffman–Singleton graph.[^o10]

### Construction from pentagons and pentagrams {#Construction_from_pentagons_and_pentagrams}

Take five [pentagons](wikipedia://en/20741014) *P<sub>h</sub>* and five [pentagrams](wikipedia://en/197544) *Q<sub>i</sub>* . Join vertex *j* of *P<sub>h</sub>* to vertex *h* · *i* + *j* of *Q<sub>i</sub>* (all indices are [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 5.)[^o10]

### Construction from [PG(3,2)](wikipedia://en/59836267) {#Construction_from_PG(3,2)}

Take a [Fano plane](wikipedia://en/390404) on seven elements, such as {*abc, ade, afg, bef, bdg, cdf, ceg*} and apply all 2520 [even](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even_permutation) [permutations](wikipedia://en/44027) on the 7-set *abcdefg*. Canonicalize each such Fano plane (e.g. by reducing to [lexicographic order](wikipedia://en/567667)) and discard duplicates. Exactly 15 Fano planes remain. Each 3-set (triplet) of the set *abcdefg* is present in exactly 3 Fano planes. The incidence between the 35 triplets and 15 Fano planes induces [PG(3,2)](wikipedia://en/59836267), with 15 points and 35 lines. To make the Hoffman-Singleton graph, create a graph vertex for each of the 15 Fano planes and 35 triplets. Connect each Fano plane to its 7 triplets, like a [Levi graph](wikipedia://en/1396888), and also connect [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) triplets to each other like the [odd graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_graph) O(4).

A very similar construction from PG(3,2) is used to build the [Higman–Sims graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higman–Sims_graph), which has the Hoffman-Singleton graph as a subgraph.

### Construction on a groupoid/magma {#Construction_on_a_groupoid/magma}

Let $G$ be the set $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_5\times\mathbb{Z}_5$. Define a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) $\circ$ on $G$ such that for each $(a,b,c)$ and $(x,y,z)$ in $G$, $(a,b,c)\circ(x,y,z)=(a+x,b-bx+y,c+(-1)^a by+2^az)$. Then the Hoffman-Singleton graph has vertices $g \in G$ and that there exists an edge between $g \in G$ and $g'\in G$ whenever $g'=g\circ s$ for some $s \in \{(0,0,1),(0,0,4),(1,0,0),(1,1,0),(1,2,0),(1,3,0),(1,4,0)\}$. [^o14] (Although the authors use the word "groupoid", it is in the sense of a binary function or magma, not in the category-theoretic sense. Also note there is a typo in the formula in the paper: the paper has $(-1)^x by$ in the last term, but that does not produce the Hoffman-Singleton graph. It should instead be $(-1)^a by$ as written here.[^o15])

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the Hoffman–Singleton graph is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) of [order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_a_group) 252000 [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to PΣU(3,5<sup>2</sup>), the [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of the [projective special unitary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_unitary_group) PSU(3,5<sup>2</sup>) with the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 2 generated by the [Frobenius automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frobenius_automorphism). It [acts](wikipedia://en/12781) transitively on the vertices, on the edges and on the arcs of the graph. Therefore, the Hoffman–Singleton graph is a [symmetric graph](wikipedia://en/1577896). As a [permutation group](wikipedia://en/24634) on 50 symbols, it can be [generated](wikipedia://en/99945) by the following two permutations applied recursively[^o18]

$$(1\ 44\ 22\ 49\ 17\ 43\ 9\ 46\ 40\ 45) (2\ 23\ 24\ 14\ 18\ 10\ 12\ 42\ 38\ 6) (3\ 41\ 19\ 4\ 15\ 20\ 7\ 13\ 37\ 8) (5\ 28\ 21\ 29\ 16\ 25\ 11\ 26\ 39\ 30) (27\ 47) (31\ 36\ 34\ 32\ 35) (33\ 50)$$

and

$$(1\ 7\ 48\ 47\ 41\ 46\ 17) (2\ 39\ 11\ 4\ 15\ 14\ 42) (3\ 32\ 28\ 9\ 23\ 6\ 43) (5\ 22\ 38\ 18\ 44\ 36\ 29) (8\ 37\ 40\ 34\ 26\ 49\ 24) (10\ 16\ 31\ 27\ 13\ 21\ 45) (19\ 33\ 25\ 35\ 50\ 30\ 20)$$

The [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) of a vertex of the graph is isomorphic to the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) S<sub>7</sub> on 7 letters. The setwise stabilizer of an edge is isomorphic to Aut(A<sub>6</sub>) = A<sub>6</sub>.2<sup>2</sup>, where A<sub>6</sub> is the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) on 6 letters. Both of the two types of stabilizers are [maximal subgroups](wikipedia://en/1472649) of the whole automorphism group of the Hoffman–Singleton graph.

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Hoffman–Singleton graph is equal to $(x-7)(x-2)^{28}(x+3)^{21}$. Therefore, the Hoffman–Singleton graph is an [integral graph](wikipedia://en/24845923): its [spectrum](wikipedia://en/534914) consists entirely of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563).

The Hoffman-Singleton graph has exactly 100 [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) of size 15 each. Each independent set is disjoint from exactly 7 other independent sets. The 100-vertex graph that connects disjoint independent sets can be partitioned into two 50-vertex subgraphs, each of which is isomorphic to the Hoffman-Singleton graph, in an unusual case of self-replicating + multiplying behavior.

:::

## Subgraphs and supergraphs {#Subgraphs_and_supergraphs}

There are 1260 5-[cycles](wikipedia://en/168609) and 5250 6-cycles in the Hoffman–Singleton graph. There are 525 copies of the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926), with each 6-cycle belonging to exactly one Petersen each. Removing any one Petersen leaves a copy of the unique (6,5)-cage. The Hoffman Singleton graph also contains the odd graph O(4), the [Coxeter graph](wikipedia://en/5358570), and the [Tutte-Coxeter graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte-Coxeter_graph) as subgraphs.[^o20]

Take any edge of the Hoffman-Singleton graph, and discard those two vertices as well as the 12 vertices directly connected to either of them. The graph that remains is the [Sylvester graph](wikipedia://en/47090707) on 36 vertices. Because each such edge can be mapped to a distinct Sylvester graph, there are 175 copies of the Sylvester graph in the Hoffman Singleton graph.[^o20]

The Hoffman Singleton graph is contained in the Higman–Sims graph which is therefore a supergraph.[^o20]
