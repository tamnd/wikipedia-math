# Q-difference polynomial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Q-difference polynomial"}

In [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the ***q*-difference polynomials** or ***q*-harmonic polynomials** are a [polynomial sequence](wikipedia://en/195990) defined in terms of the [*q*-derivative](wikipedia://en/3921324). They are a generalized type of [Brenke polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenke_polynomial), and generalize the [Appell polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appell_polynomial). See also [Sheffer sequence](wikipedia://en/218596).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The q-difference polynomials satisfy the relation

$$\left(\frac {d}{dz}\right)_q p_n(z) = \frac{p_n(qz)-p_n(z)} {qz-z} = \frac{q^n-1} {q-1} p_{n-1}(z)=[n]_qp_{n-1}(z)$$

where the derivative symbol on the left is the q-derivative. In the limit of $q\to 1$, this becomes the definition of the Appell polynomials:

$$\frac{d}{dz}p_n(z) = np_{n-1}(z).$$

:::

## Generating function {#Generating_function}

The generalized [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for these polynomials is of the type of generating function for Brenke polynomials, namely

$$A(w)e_q(zw) = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{p_n(z)}{[n]_q!} w^n$$

where $e_q(t)$ is the [q-exponential](wikipedia://en/4120642):

$$e_q(t)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{t^n}{[n]_q!}= \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{t^n (1-q)^n}{(q;q)_n}.$$

Here, $[n]_q!$ is the [q-factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-factorial) and

$$(q;q)_n=(1-q^n)(1-q^{n-1})\cdots (1-q)$$

is the [q-Pochhammer symbol](wikipedia://en/11149717). The function $A(w)$ is arbitrary but assumed to have an expansion

$$A(w)=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n w^n \mbox{ with } a_0 \ne 0.$$

Any such $A(w)$ gives a sequence of q-difference polynomials.
