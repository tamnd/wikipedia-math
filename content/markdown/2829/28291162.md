# Whitney topologies

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Whitney topologies"}

In mathematics, and especially [differential topology](wikipedia://en/8562), [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and [singularity theory](wikipedia://en/581763#Arnold.27s_view), the **Whitney topologies** are a [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) family of [topologies](wikipedia://en/178649#Definition) defined on the set of [smooth mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_mapping) between two [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold). They are named after the American mathematician [Hassler Whitney](wikipedia://en/391794).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Let *M* and *N* be two real, smooth manifolds. Furthermore, let [C<sup>∞</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_mapping#Multivariate_differentiability_classes)(*M*,*N*) denote the space of smooth mappings between *M* and *N*. The notation C<sup>∞</sup> means that the mappings are infinitely differentiable, i.e. [partial derivatives](wikipedia://en/52565) of all orders exist and are [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122).[^o4]

### Whitney C<sup>*k*</sup>-topology {#Whitney_Ck-topology}

For some [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *k* ≥ 0, let J<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*,*N*) denote the [*k*-jet space](wikipedia://en/1521924) of mappings between *M* and *N*. The jet space can be endowed with a [smooth structure](wikipedia://en/1542238) (i.e. a structure as a C<sup>∞</sup> manifold) which make it into a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450). This topology is used to define a topology on C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*).

For a fixed [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *k* ≥ 0 consider an open subset *U* ⊂ J<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*,*N*), and denote by *S<sup>k</sup>*(*U*) the following:

$$S^k(U) = \{ f \in C^{\infty}(M,N) : (J^kf)(M) \subseteq U \} .$$

The sets *S<sup>k</sup>*(*U*) form a [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) for the **Whitney C<sup>*k*</sup>-topology** on C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*).[^o6]

### Whitney C<sup>∞</sup>-topology {#Whitney_C∞-topology}

For each choice of *k* ≥ 0, the Whitney C<sup>*k*</sup>-topology gives a topology for C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*); in other words the Whitney C<sup>*k*</sup>-topology tells us which subsets of C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*) are open sets. Let us denote by W<sup>*k*</sup> the set of open subsets of C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*) with respect to the Whitney C<sup>*k*</sup>-topology. Then the **Whitney C<sup>∞</sup>-topology** is defined to be the topology whose [basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_%28topology%29) is given by *W*, where:[^o6]

$$W = \bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty} W^k .$$

:::

## Dimensionality {#Dimensionality}

Notice that C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*) has infinite dimension, whereas J<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*,*N*) has finite dimension. In fact, J<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*,*N*) is a real, finite-dimensional manifold. To see this, let ℝ<sup>*k*</sup>\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*m*</sub>\] denote the space of [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000), with real coefficients, in *m* variables of order at most *k* and with zero as the [constant term](wikipedia://en/79861). This is a real [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) with dimension

$$\dim\left\{\mathbb{R}^k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\right\} = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{(m+i-1)!}{(m-1)! \cdot i!} = \left( \frac{(m+k)!}{m!\cdot k!} - 1 \right) .$$

Writing *a* = dim{ℝ<sup>*k*</sup>\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*m*</sub>\]} then, by the standard theory of vector spaces ℝ<sup>*k*</sup>\[*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*m*</sub>\] ≅ ℝ<sup>*a*</sup>, and so is a real, finite-dimensional manifold. Next, define:

$$B_{m,n}^k = \bigoplus_{i=1}^n \mathbb{R}^k[x_1,\ldots,x_m], \implies \dim\left\{B_{m,n}^k\right\} = n \dim \left\{ A_m^k \right\} = n \left( \frac{(m+k)!}{m!\cdot k!} - 1 \right) .$$

Using *b* to denote the dimension *B*<sup>*k*</sup><sub>*m*,*n*</sub>, we see that *B*<sup>*k*</sup><sub>*m*,*n*</sub> ≅ ℝ<sup>*b*</sup>, and so is a real, finite-dimensional manifold.

In fact, if *M* and *N* have dimension *m* and *n* respectively then:[^o9]

$$\dim\!\left\{J^k(M,N)\right\} = m + n + \dim \!\left\{B_{n,m}^k\right\} = m + n\left( \frac{(m+k)!}{m!\cdot k!}\right).$$

## Topology {#Topology}

Given the Whitney C<sup>∞</sup>-topology, the space C<sup>∞</sup>(*M*,*N*) is a [Baire space](wikipedia://en/200550), i.e. every [residual set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual_set) is [dense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_%28topology%29).[^o11]
