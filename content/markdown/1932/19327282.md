# Hyperinteger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperinteger"}

In [nonstandard analysis](wikipedia://en/21708), a **hyperinteger** *n* is a [hyperreal number](wikipedia://en/51429) that is equal to its own [integer part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer_part). A hyperinteger may be either finite or infinite. A finite hyperinteger is an ordinary [integer](wikipedia://en/14563). An example of an infinite hyperinteger is given by the class of the [sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_sequence) (1, 2, 3, ...) in the [ultrapower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrapower) construction of the hyperreals.

:::

## Discussion {#Discussion}

The standard integer part [function](wikipedia://en/185427):

$$\lfloor x \rfloor$$

is defined for all [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) *x* and equals the greatest integer not exceeding *x*. By the [transfer principle](wikipedia://en/637138) of nonstandard analysis, there exists a natural extension:

$${}^*\! \lfloor \,\cdot\, \rfloor$$

defined for all hyperreal *x*, and we say that *x* is a hyperinteger if $x = {}^*\! \lfloor x \rfloor.$ Thus, the hyperintegers are the [image](wikipedia://en/579311) of the integer part function on the hyperreals.

## Internal sets {#Internal_sets}

The set $^*\mathbb{Z}$ of all hyperintegers is an [internal subset](wikipedia://en/2190765) of the hyperreal line $^*\mathbb{R}$. The set of all finite hyperintegers (i.e. $\mathbb{Z}$ itself) is not an internal subset. Elements of the complement $^*\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{Z}$ are called, depending on the author, *nonstandard*, *unlimited*, or *infinite* hyperintegers. The reciprocal of an infinite hyperinteger is always an [infinitesimal](wikipedia://en/160990).

Nonnegative hyperintegers are sometimes called *hypernatural* numbers. Similar remarks apply to the sets $\mathbb{N}$ and $^*\mathbb{N}$. Note that the latter gives a [non-standard model of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6251420) in the sense of [Skolem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skolem).
