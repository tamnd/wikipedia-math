# Fisher consistency

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fisher consistency"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), **Fisher consistency**, named after [Ronald Fisher](wikipedia://en/140824), is a desirable property of an [estimator](wikipedia://en/1565926) asserting that if the estimator were calculated using the entire [population](wikipedia://en/27585) rather than a [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29), the true value of the estimated parameter would be obtained.[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Suppose we have a [statistical sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) *X*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *X*<sub>*n*</sub> where each *X*<sub>*i*</sub> follows a [cumulative distribution](wikipedia://en/5793) *F*<sub>*θ*</sub> which depends on an unknown [parameter](wikipedia://en/25065) *θ*. If an estimator of *θ* based on the sample can be represented as a [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) of the [empirical distribution function](wikipedia://en/2690455) *F̂<sub>n</sub>*:

$$\hat{ \theta } =T(\hat F_n) \,,$$

the estimator is said to be *Fisher consistent* if:

$T(F_\theta) = \theta \, .$[^o5]

As long as the *X*<sub>*i*</sub> are [exchangeable](wikipedia://en/6753623), an estimator *T* defined in terms of the *X*<sub>*i*</sub> can be converted into an estimator *T′* that can be defined in terms of *F̂<sub>n</sub>* by averaging *T* over all permutations of the data. The resulting estimator will have the same expected value as *T* and its variance will be no larger than that of *T*.

If the [strong law of large numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_law_of_large_numbers) can be applied, the empirical distribution functions *F̂<sub>n</sub>* converge pointwise to *F<sub>θ</sub>*, allowing us to express Fisher consistency as a limit — the estimator is *Fisher consistent* if

$$T \left( \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty} \hat F_n \right) = \theta. \,$$

:::

## Finite population example {#Finite_population_example}

Suppose our sample is obtained from a finite population *Z*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *Z*<sub>m</sub>. We can represent our sample of size *n* in terms of the proportion of the sample *n*<sub>i</sub> / *n* taking on each value in the population. Writing our estimator of θ as *T*(*n*<sub>1</sub> / *n*, ..., *n*<sub>m</sub> / *n*), the population analogue of the estimator is *T*(*p*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *p*<sub>m</sub>), where p<sub>i</sub> = *P*(*X* = *Z*<sub>i</sub>). Thus we have *Fisher consistency* if *T*(*p*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *p*<sub>m</sub>) = θ.

Suppose the parameter of interest is the [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) μ and the estimator is the [sample mean](wikipedia://en/612), which can be written

$$n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^mI(X_i=Z_j)Z_j,$$

where *I* is the [indicator function](wikipedia://en/240790). The population analogue of this expression is

$$n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^mp_jZ_j = n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mu = \mu,$$

so we have Fisher consistency.

## Role in maximum likelihood estimation {#Role_in_maximum_likelihood_estimation}

Maximising the likelihood function *L* gives an estimate that is Fisher consistent for a parameter *b* if

$$E\left[ \frac{d\ln L}{db} \right] = 0\text{ at }b=b_0, \,$$

where *b*<sub>0</sub> represents the true value of *b*.[^o8][^o9]

## Relationship to asymptotic consistency and unbiasedness {#Relationship_to_asymptotic_consistency_and_unbiasedness}

The term *consistency* in statistics usually refers to an estimator that is [asymptotically consistent](wikipedia://en/1497569). Fisher consistency and asymptotic consistency are distinct concepts, although both aim to define a desirable property of an estimator. While many estimators are consistent in both senses, neither definition encompasses the other. For example, suppose we take an estimator *T*<sub>n</sub> that is both Fisher consistent and asymptotically consistent, and then form *T*<sub>n</sub> + *E*<sub>n</sub>, where *E*<sub>n</sub> is a deterministic sequence of nonzero numbers converging to zero. This estimator is asymptotically consistent, but not Fisher consistent for any *n*.

The sample mean is a Fisher consistent and [unbiased](wikipedia://en/8450479) estimate of the population mean, but not all Fisher consistent estimates are unbiased. Suppose we observe a sample from a [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) on (0,θ) and we wish to estimate θ. The sample maximum is Fisher consistent, but downwardly biased. Conversely, the [sample variance](wikipedia://en/32344) is an unbiased estimate of the population variance, but is not Fisher consistent.

## Role in decision theory {#Role_in_decision_theory}

A loss function is Fisher consistent if the population minimizer of the risk leads to the Bayes [optimal decision](wikipedia://en/16414027) rule.[^o12]
