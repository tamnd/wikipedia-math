# Sasakian manifold

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sasakian manifold"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), a **Sasakian manifold** (named after [Shigeo Sasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Sasaki)) is a [contact manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_manifold) $(M,\theta)$ equipped with a special kind of [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) $g$, called a *Sasakian* metric. They are studied as a natural odd-dimensional counterpart of [Kähler manifolds](wikipedia://en/390538) (which are necessarily even-dimensional).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

A Sasakian metric is defined using the construction of the *Riemannian cone*. Given a [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) $(M,g)$, its Riemannian cone is the product

$$(M\times {\mathbb{R}}^{>0})\,$$

of $M$ with a half-line ${\mathbb{R}}^{>0}$, equipped with the *cone metric*

$$t^2 g + dt^2,\,$$

where $t$ is the parameter in ${\mathbb{R}}^{>0}$.

A manifold $M$ equipped with a 1-form $\theta$ is contact if and only if the 2-form

$$d(t^2\theta)=t^2\,d\theta + 2t\, dt \wedge \theta\,$$

on its cone is symplectic (this is one of the possible definitions of a contact structure). A contact Riemannian manifold is Sasakian, if its Riemannian cone with the cone metric is a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) with Kähler form

$$t^2\,d\theta + 2t\,dt \wedge \theta.$$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

As an example consider

$$S^{2n-1}\hookrightarrow {\mathbb{R}}^{2n}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}$$

where the right hand side is a natural Kähler manifold and read as the cone over the sphere (endowed with embedded metric). The contact 1-form on $S^{2n-1}$ is the form associated to the tangent vector $i\vec{N}$, constructed from the unit-normal vector $\vec{N}$ to the sphere ($i$ being the complex structure on ${\mathbb{C}}^n$).

Another non-compact example is ${{\mathbb{R}}^{2n+1}}$ with coordinates $(\vec{x},\vec{y},z)$ endowed with contact-form

$$\theta=\frac12 dz+\sum_i y_i\,dx_i$$

and the Riemannian metric

$$g=\sum_i (dx_i)^2+(dy_i)^2+\theta^2.$$

As a third example consider:

$${\mathbb P}^{2n-1}{\mathbb R}\hookrightarrow {\mathbb{C}}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_2$$

where the right hand side has a natural Kähler structure, and the group ${\mathbb{Z}}_2$ acts by reflection at the origin.

:::

## History {#History}

Sasakian manifolds were introduced in 1960 by the Japanese geometer [Shigeo Sasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Sasaki).[^o7] There was not much activity in this field after the mid-1970s, until the advent of [String theory](wikipedia://en/28305). Since then Sasakian manifolds have gained prominence in physics and algebraic geometry, mostly due to a string of papers by [Charles P. Boyer](wikipedia://en/61906086) and Krzysztof Galicki and their co-authors.

## The Reeb vector field {#The_Reeb_vector_field}

The [homothetic vector field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_vector_field) on the cone over a Sasakian manifold is defined to be

$$t\partial/\partial t.$$

As the cone is by definition Kähler, there exists a complex structure *J*. The *[Reeb vector field](wikipedia://en/10761147)* on the Sasaskian manifold is defined to be

$$\xi =-J(t\partial/\partial t).$$

It is nowhere vanishing. It commutes with all holomorphic [Killing vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector) on the cone and in particular with all [isometries](wikipedia://en/254777) of the Sasakian manifold. If the orbits of the vector field close then the space of orbits is a Kähler orbifold. The Reeb vector field at the Sasakian manifold at unit radius is a unit vector field and tangential to the embedding.

## Sasaki–Einstein manifolds {#Sasaki–Einstein_manifolds}

A Sasakian manifold $M$ is a manifold whose Riemannian cone is Kähler. If, in addition, this cone is [Ricci-flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricci-flat), $M$ is called ***Sasaki–Einstein***; if it is [hyperkähler](wikipedia://en/646120), $M$ is called **3-Sasakian**. Any 3-Sasakian manifold is both an [Einstein manifold](wikipedia://en/644814) and a [spin manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_manifold).

If *M* is positive-scalar-curvature Kähler–Einstein manifold, then, by an observation of [Shoshichi Kobayashi](wikipedia://en/20695112), the circle bundle *S* in its canonical line bundle admits a Sasaki–Einstein metric, in a manner that makes the projection from *S* to *M* into a Riemannian submersion. (For example, it follows that there exist Sasaki–Einstein metrics on suitable [circle bundles](wikipedia://en/2794417) over the 3rd through 8th [del Pezzo surfaces](wikipedia://en/732323).) While this Riemannian submersion construction provides a correct local picture of any Sasaki–Einstein manifold, the global structure of such manifolds can be more complicated. For example, one can more generally construct Sasaki–Einstein manifolds by starting from a Kähler–Einstein **orbifold** *M.* Using this observation, Boyer, Galicki, and [János Kollár](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/János_Kollár) constructed infinitely many homeotypes of Sasaki-Einstein 5-manifolds. The same construction shows that the moduli space of Einstein metrics on the 5-sphere has at least several hundred connected components.
