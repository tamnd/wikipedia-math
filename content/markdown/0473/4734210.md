# Puppe sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Puppe sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Puppe sequence** is a construction of [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831), so named after [Dieter Puppe](wikipedia://en/63815664). It comes in two forms: a [long exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_exact_sequence), built from the [mapping fibre](wikipedia://en/19207725) (a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826)), and a long coexact sequence, built from the [mapping cone](wikipedia://en/1707756) (which is a [cofibration](wikipedia://en/1697216)).[^o2] Intuitively, the Puppe sequence allows us to think of [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory) as a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) that takes spaces to long-exact sequences of groups. It is also useful as a tool to build long exact sequences of [relative homotopy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_homotopy_group).

:::

## Exact Puppe sequence {#Exact_Puppe_sequence}

A sequence of pointed spaces and pointed maps

$$\dots \to X_{n+1} \to X_n \to X_{n-1} \to \dots$$

is called exact if the induced sequence

$$\dots \to [Z, X_{n+1}] \to [Z, X_n] \to [Z, X_{n-1}] \to \dots$$

is exact as a sequence of pointed sets (taking the kernel of a map to be those elements mapped to the basepoint) for every pointed space $Z$.

Let $f\colon (X,x_0)\to(Y,y_0)$ be a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between [pointed spaces](wikipedia://en/1236666) and let $Mf$ denote the [mapping fibre](wikipedia://en/19207725) (the [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) dual to the [mapping cone](wikipedia://en/1707756)). One then obtains an exact sequence:

$$Mf\to X \to Y$$

where the mapping fibre is defined as:[^o2]

$$Mf = \{(x,\omega) \in X\times Y^I : \omega(0)=y_0 \mbox{ and } \omega(1)=f(x) \}$$

Observe that the [loop space](wikipedia://en/2174332) $\Omega Y$ injects into the mapping fibre: $\Omega Y \to Mf$, as it consists of those maps that both start and end at the basepoint $y_0$. One may then show that the above sequence extends to the longer sequence

$$\Omega X \to \Omega Y \to Mf\to X \to Y$$

The construction can then be iterated to obtain the exact Puppe sequence

$$\cdots \to \Omega^2(Mf) \to \Omega^2 X \to \Omega^2 Y \to \Omega(Mf) \to \Omega X \to \Omega Y \to Mf\to X \to Y$$

The exact sequence is often more convenient than the coexact sequence in practical applications, as [Joseph J. Rotman](wikipedia://en/51256431) explains:[^o2]

*(the) various constructions (of the coexact sequence) involve quotient spaces instead of subspaces, and so all maps and homotopies require more scrutiny to ensure that they are well-defined and continuous.*

## Examples {#Examples}

### Example: Relative homotopy {#Example:_Relative_homotopy}

As a special case,[^o2] one may take *X* to be a subspace *A* of *Y* that contains the basepoint *y*<sub>0</sub>, and *f* to be the inclusion $i:A\hookrightarrow Y$ of *A* into *Y*. One then obtains an exact sequence in the [category of pointed spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_pointed_spaces):

$$\begin{aligned} \cdots &\to \pi_{n+1}(A) \to \pi_{n+1}(Y) \to \left [S^0,\Omega^n(Mi) \right ]\to \pi_n(A) \to \pi_n(Y)\to\cdots \\ \cdots &\to \pi_1(A) \to \pi_1(Y) \to \left [S^0,Mi \right ]\to \pi_0(A) \to \pi_0(Y) \end{aligned}$$

where the $\pi_n$ are the [homotopy groups](wikipedia://en/297506), $S^0$ is the zero-sphere (i.e. two points) and $[U,W]$ denotes the [homotopy equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalence) of maps from *U* to *W*. Note that $\pi_{n+1}(X)=\pi_1(\Omega^n X)$. One may then show that

$$\left [S^0,\Omega^n(Mi) \right ]= \left [S^n,Mi \right ]=\pi_n(Mi)$$

is in [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) to the relative homotopy group $\pi_{n+1}(Y,A)$, thus giving rise to the **relative homotopy sequence of pairs**

$$\begin{aligned} \cdots &\to \pi_{n+1}(A) \to \pi_{n+1}(Y) \to \pi_{n+1}(Y,A) \to \pi_n(A) \to \pi_n(Y)\to\cdots \\ \cdots &\to \pi_1(A) \to \pi_1(Y) \to \pi_1(Y,A)\to \pi_0(A) \to \pi_0(Y) \end{aligned}$$

The object $\pi_n(Y,A)$ is a group for $n\ge 2$ and is abelian for $n\ge 3$.

### Example: Fibration {#Example:_Fibration}

As a special case,[^o2] one may take *f* to be a [fibration](wikipedia://en/316826) $p:E\to B$. Then the [mapping fiber](wikipedia://en/19207725) *Mp* has the [homotopy lifting property](wikipedia://en/522786) and it follows that *Mp* and the fiber $F=p^{-1}(b_0)$ have the same [homotopy type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_type). It follows trivially that maps of the sphere into *Mp* are homotopic to maps of the sphere to *F*, that is,

$$\pi_n(Mp) = \left [S^n,Mp \right ] \simeq \left [S^n, F \right ] = \pi_n(F).$$

From this, the Puppe sequence gives the **homotopy sequence of a fibration**:

$$\begin{aligned} \cdots &\to \pi_{n+1}(E) \to \pi_{n+1}(B) \to \pi_n(F) \to \pi_n(E) \to \pi_n(B)\to\cdots \\ \cdots &\to \pi_1(E) \to \pi_1(B) \to \pi_0(F)\to \pi_0(E) \to \pi_0(B) \end{aligned}$$

### Example: Weak fibration {#Example:_Weak_fibration}

[Weak fibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_fibration) are strictly weaker than fibrations, however, the main result above still holds, although the proof must be altered. The key observation, due to [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), is that, given a weak fibration $p\colon E\to B$, and the fiber at the basepoint given by $F=p^{-1}(b_0)$, that there is a bijection

$$p_*\colon \pi_n(E,F)\to\pi_n(B,b_0).$$

This bijection can be used in the relative homotopy sequence above, to obtain the **homotopy sequence of a weak fibration**, having the same form as the fibration sequence, although with a different connecting map.

## Coexact Puppe sequence {#Coexact_Puppe_sequence}

Let $f\colon A \to B$ be a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_map) between [CW complexes](wikipedia://en/353022) and let $C(f)$ denote a [mapping cone](wikipedia://en/1707756) of *f*, (i.e., the cofiber of the map *f*), so that we have a (cofiber) sequence:

$$A\to B\to C(f).$$

Now we can form $\Sigma A$ and $\Sigma B,$ [suspensions](wikipedia://en/1251702) of *A* and *B* respectively, and also $\Sigma f \colon \Sigma A \to \Sigma B$ (this is because [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702) might be seen as a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987)), obtaining a sequence:

$$\Sigma A \to \Sigma B \to C(\Sigma f).$$

Note that suspension preserves cofiber sequences.

Due to this powerful fact we know that $C(\Sigma f)$ is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to $\Sigma C(f).$ By collapsing $B\subset C(f)$ to a point, one has a natural map $C(f) \to \Sigma A.$ Thus we have a sequence:

$$A\to B\to C(f) \to \Sigma A \to \Sigma B \to \Sigma C(f).$$

Iterating this construction, we obtain the Puppe sequence associated to $A\to B$:

$$A\to B\to C(f) \to \Sigma A \to \Sigma B \to \Sigma C(f) \to \Sigma^2 A \to \Sigma^2 B \to \Sigma^2 C(f) \to \Sigma^3 A \to \Sigma^3 B \to \Sigma^3 C(f) \to \cdots$$

## Some properties and consequences {#Some_properties_and_consequences}

It is a simple exercise in topology to see that every three elements of a Puppe sequence are, up to a homotopy, of the form:

$$X\to Y\to C(f).$$

By "up to a homotopy", we mean here that every 3 elements in a Puppe sequence are of the above form if regarded as objects and morphisms in the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756).

If one is now given a [topological half-exact functor](wikipedia://en/4749620), the above property implies that, after acting with the functor in question on the Puppe sequence associated to $A\to B$, one obtains a long [exact sequence](wikipedia://en/138215).

A result, due to [John Milnor](wikipedia://en/61632),[^o11] is that if one takes the [Eilenberg–Steenrod axioms](wikipedia://en/3122600) for [homology theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_theory), and replaces excision by the exact sequence of a [weak fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serre_fibration) of pairs, then one gets the homotopy analogy of the [Eilenberg–Steenrod theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilenberg–Steenrod_theorem): there exists a unique sequence of functors $\pi_n\colon P\to\bf{Sets}$ with *P* the category of all pointed pairs of topological spaces.

## Remarks {#Remarks}

As there are two "kinds" of [suspension](wikipedia://en/1251702), [unreduced and reduced](wikipedia://en/1251702#Reduced_suspension), one can also consider unreduced and reduced Puppe sequences (at least if dealing with [pointed spaces](wikipedia://en/1236666), when it's possible to form reduced suspension).
