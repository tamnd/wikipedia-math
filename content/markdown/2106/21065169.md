# Cartan–Eilenberg resolution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cartan–Eilenberg resolution"}

In [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), the **Cartan–Eilenberg resolution** is in a sense, a [resolution](wikipedia://en/19745659) of a [chain complex](wikipedia://en/138404). It can be used to construct hyper-derived functors. It is named in honor of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) and [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an [Abelian category](wikipedia://en/45063) with [enough projectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_projectives), and let $A_{*}$ be a chain complex with objects in $\mathcal{A}$. Then a **Cartan–Eilenberg resolution** of $A_{*}$ is an upper half-plane [double complex](wikipedia://en/11797854) $P_{*,*}$ (i.e., $P_{p,q} = 0$ for $q < 0$) consisting of projective objects of $\mathcal{A}$ and an "augmentation" chain map $\varepsilon \colon P_{p,*} \to A_p$ such that

- If $A_{p} = 0$ then the *p*-th column is zero, i.e. $P_{p, q} = 0$ for all *q*.
- For any fixed column $P_{p, *}$,
  - The complex of boundaries $B_p(P, d^h) := d^h(P_{p+1. *})$ obtained by applying the horizontal differential to $P_{p+1, *}$ (the $p+1$st column of $P_{*,*}$) forms a projective resolution $B_p(\varepsilon): B_p(P, d^h) \to B_p(A)$ of the boundaries of $A_p$.
  - The complex $H_p(P, d^h)$ obtained by taking the homology of each row with respect to the horizontal differential forms a projective resolution $H_p(\varepsilon): H_p(P, d^h) \to H_p(A)$ of degree *p* homology of $A$.

It can be shown that for each *p*, the column $P_{p, *}$ is a projective resolution of $A_{p}$.

There is an analogous definition using injective resolutions and cochain complexes.

The existence of Cartan–Eilenberg resolutions can be proved via the [horseshoe lemma](wikipedia://en/4603066).

### Hyper-derived functors {#Hyper-derived_functors}

Given a right [exact functor](wikipedia://en/453945) $F \colon \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}$, one can define the left hyper-derived functors of $F$ on a chain complex $A_{*}$ by

- Constructing a Cartan–Eilenberg resolution $\varepsilon: P_{*, *} \to A_{*}$,
- Applying the functor $F$ to $P_{*, *}$, and
- Taking the homology of the resulting total complex.

Similarly, one can also define right hyper-derived functors for left exact functors.
