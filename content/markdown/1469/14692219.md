# Short division

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Short division"}

In [arithmetic](wikipedia://en/3118), **short division** is a [division algorithm](wikipedia://en/3336479) which breaks down a [division](wikipedia://en/53696) problem into a series of easier steps. It is an abbreviated form of [long division](wikipedia://en/313384) — whereby the products are omitted and the partial remainders are notated as [superscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscript_and_superscript).

:::

As a result, a short division tableau is shorter than its long division counterpart — though sometimes at the expense of relying on [mental arithmetic](wikipedia://en/567292), which could limit the size of the [divisor](wikipedia://en/49492).

For most people, small integer divisors up to 12 are handled using memorised [multiplication tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplication_tables), although the procedure could also be adapted to the larger divisors as well.

As in all division problems, a number called the *[dividend](wikipedia://en/53696)* is divided by another, called the *[divisor](wikipedia://en/49492)*. The answer to the problem would be the *[quotient](wikipedia://en/332640)*, and in the case of [Euclidean division](wikipedia://en/679987), the [remainder](wikipedia://en/502897) would be included as well.

Using short division, arbitrarily large dividends can be handled.[^o2]

## Tableau {#Tableau}

Short division does not use the [slash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_%28punctuation%29) (/) or [division sign](wikipedia://en/276918) (÷) symbols. Instead, it displays the dividend, divisor, and [quotient](wikipedia://en/332640) (when it is found) in a [tableau](wikipedia://en/313384#Method). An example is shown below, representing the division of 500 by 4. The quotient is 125.

$$\begin{array}{r} 125\\ 4\overline{)500}\\ \end{array}$$

Alternatively, the bar may be placed below the number, which means the sum proceeds down the page. This is in distinction to [long division](wikipedia://en/313384), where the space under the dividend is required for workings:

$$\begin{array}{r} 4\underline{)500}\\ 125\\ \end{array}$$

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

The procedure involves several steps. As an example, consider 950 divided by 4:

Using the alternative layout the final workings would be:

$$\begin{array}{r} 4\underline{)9^{1}5^{3}0.^{2}0}\\ 2^{\color{White}1}3^{\color{White}3}7.^{\color{White}2}5\\ \end{array}$$

As usual, similar steps can also be used to handle the cases with a decimal dividend, or the cases where the divisor involves multiple digits.[^o6]

:::

## Prime factorization {#Prime_factorization}

*Image: Factorizing.JPG.* An example of manual factorizing.

A common requirement is to reduce a number to its prime factors. This is used particularly in working with [vulgar fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraction_%28mathematics%29). The dividend is successively divided by prime numbers, repeating where possible:

$$\begin{array}{r} 2\underline{)950}\\ 5\underline{)475}\\ 5\underline{){\color{White}0}95}\\ \ \ \ 19\\ \end{array}$$

This results in the factorization $950 = 2 \times 5^2 \times 19$.

## Modulo division {#Modulo_division}

When one is interested only in the [remainder](wikipedia://en/502897) of the division, this procedure (a variation of short division) ignores the quotient and tallies only the remainders. It can be used for manual [modulo calculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulo_operation) or as a [test for even divisibility](wikipedia://en/991210). The quotient digits are not written down.

The following shows the solution (using short division) of 16762109 divided by seven.

$$\begin{matrix} 7)16^27^66^32^41^60^49^0 \end{matrix}$$

The remainder is zero, so 16762109 is exactly divisible by 7.

### As an automaton {#As_an_automaton}

Given a divisor $k$, this procedure can be written as a [deterministic finite automaton](wikipedia://en/653404) with $k$ states, each corresponding to a possible remainder.[^o10] This implies that the set of numbers divisible by $k$ is a [regular language](wikipedia://en/25723).
