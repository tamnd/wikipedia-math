# Nikolaos Ch. Nikolaidis

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nikolaos Ch. Nikolaidis"}

**Nicolaos Ch. Nikolaidis** (Νικόλαος Χ. Νικολαΐδης, in French **Nicolas Nicolaïdès**; (1826 – 11 July 1889) was an author, mechanical engineer, mathematician, soldier, and professor. He was a pioneer in 19th-century Greek mathematics introducing modern European mathematics to the Greek world. He went to school at [Evelpidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Military_Academy) and studied in France at the *[École nationale des ponts et chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_nationale_des_ponts_et_chaussées)* and *[École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique)*. His professors in France included [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196) and [Adhémar Jean Claude Barré de Saint-Venant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhémar_Jean_Claude_Barré_de_Saint-Venant). While in France he was exposed to the complex mathematics prevalent at the time. He wrote papers on geometry and kinematics. He was exposed to the works of [Jacques Charles François Sturm](wikipedia://en/1181554), [Giovanni Fagnano](wikipedia://en/42789700), [Colin Maclaurin](wikipedia://en/7824), [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196), and [Jacques Antoine Charles Bresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Antoine_Charles_Bresse). He integrated some of their works into his mathematical literature. He participated in the Cretan revolt in 1866 and was the Commander of the 174th Battalion of Verville during the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) in 1870. He became a professor at [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens) in 1871. His memoirs, a work of over four hundred pages entitled *Analectes, ou Mémoires et Notes sur les Diverses Parties des Mathématiques* published in 1874, feature most of his mathematical papers. By 1881, he retired from the [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens) due to an illness. He died on 11 July 1889, at about 63 years old. He influenced countless French and Greek mathematicians, including [Cyparissos Stephanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyparissos_Stephanos), [John Hazzidakis](wikipedia://en/55894972) and [Vassilios Lakon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassilios_Lakon).[^o3][^n1][^o5][^n2]

## Biography {#Biography}

Nikolaidis was born in 1826 in [Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli,_Greece). His father, Christodoulos, was a member of an old Greek aristocratic family. Christodoulos migrated to [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland) from [Philippopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippopolis_%28Thrace%29). The family eventually settled in the Peloponnese region of Greece around the time of the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence). From a young age, Nikolaidis exhibited a high level of intelligence. He attended Greece's elite military school known as [Evelpidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Military_Academy), graduating with honors. The Greek government sent him to Paris on a scholarship to study with the most brilliant minds of the time. He studied at the *[École nationale des ponts et chaussées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_nationale_des_ponts_et_chaussées)* and the *[École polytechnique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_polytechnique)*.[^n3] His professor at the *École polytechnique* was [Joseph Bertrand](wikipedia://en/488196). His classmates at the time were [Henri Brocard](wikipedia://en/14897496) and [Émile Lemoine](wikipedia://en/15956333). His professor at the *École nationale des ponts et chaussées* was [mechanician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanician) and mathematician [Adhémar Jean Claude Barré de Saint-Venant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhémar_Jean_Claude_Barré_de_Saint-Venant). Nikolaidis taught civil engineering and mathematics in France. He was also affiliated with [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422), [Jacques Antoine Charles Bresse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Antoine_Charles_Bresse), and [Léon Foucault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Léon_Foucault). He had an academic disagreement with Bresse and Foucault, publishing his responses in the French magazine *Cosmos*. He abandoned his teaching position at the *École nationale des ponts et chaussées* because of his disagreement with Bresse. In 1863, Nikolaidis published *Théorie du Mouvement d'une Figure Plane dans son Plan Application aux Organes des Machines* and in 1864 he published his dissertation, *Mémoire Sur la Théorie Générale des Surfaces*. By 1865, he had two Phds. That same year he returned to Greece and became an instructor at [Evelpidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Military_Academy). Around the same period at 40 years of age, he fought on the side of Crete during the [Cretan revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_revolt_%281866–1869%29) in 1866, and subsequently fought on the side of France in the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War) in 1870, commanding the 174th Battalion of Verville. When he returned to Greece he became a professor at the [University of Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_and_Kapodistrian_University_of_Athens) in 1871. Three years later he published an accumulation of his complex mathematical work in French entitled *Analectes, ou Mémoires et Notes sur les Diverses Parties des Mathématiques*. Around this period he taught [Cyparissos Stephanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyparissos_Stephanos), inspiring the young mathematician. By 1881, he retired from teaching at the University of Athens due to an illness. He was about fifty-five years old. He died eight years later at the age of sixty-three.[^n4] He was a member of several organizations including an organization for the encouragement of Greek studies in France.[^n5]
