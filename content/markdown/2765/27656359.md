# Nicod's axiom

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nicod's axiom"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), **Nicod's axiom** (named after the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France) [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) and [philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy) [Jean Nicod](wikipedia://en/2078853)) is a formula that can be used as the sole [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) of a [semantically complete](wikipedia://en/31271451) system of [propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propositional_calculus). The only [connective](wikipedia://en/18153) used in the formulation of Nicod's axiom is the [Sheffer's stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffer's_stroke).

:::

The [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) has the following form:$((\varphi\ |\ (\chi\ |\ \psi))\ |\ ((\tau\ |\ (\tau\ |\ \tau))\ |\ ((\theta\ |\ \chi)\ |\ ((\varphi\ |\ \theta)\ |\ (\varphi\ |\ \theta)))))$[^o2]

[Nicod](wikipedia://en/2078853) showed that the whole [propositional logic](wikipedia://en/18154) of *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)* could be derived from this [axiom](wikipedia://en/928) alone by using one [rule of inference](wikipedia://en/252311), called "Nicod's modus ponens":

$\begin{array}{l}\varphi \\ (\varphi\ |\ (\chi\ |\ \psi)) \\ \hline \psi \end{array}$[^o3]

In 1931, the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) [logician](wikipedia://en/46426065) [Mordechaj Wajsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordechaj_Wajsberg) discovered an equally powerful and easier-to-work-with alternative:

$((\varphi\ |\ (\psi\ |\ \chi))\ |\ (((\tau\ |\ \chi)\ |\ ((\varphi\ |\ \tau)\ |\ (\varphi\ |\ \tau)))\ |\ (\varphi\ |\ (\varphi\ |\ \psi))))$[^o4]
