# H-object

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="H-object"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [homotopical algebra](wikipedia://en/1321050), an H-object[^o2] is a [categorical generalization](wikipedia://en/1596969) of an [H-space](wikipedia://en/600570), which can be defined in any [category](wikipedia://en/5869) $\mathcal{C}$ with a [product](wikipedia://en/237868) $\times$ and an [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) $*$. These are useful constructions because they help export some of the ideas from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831) into other domains, such as in [commutative algebra](wikipedia://en/245990) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

In a category $\mathcal{C}$ with a product $\times$ and initial object $*$, an **H-object** is an object $X \in \text{Ob}(\mathcal{C})$ together with an operation called multiplication together with a two sided identity. If we denote $u_X: X \to *$, the structure of an H-object implies there are maps

> $$\begin{aligned} \varepsilon&: * \to X \\ \mu&: X\times X \to X \end{aligned}$$

which have the commutation relations

> $$\mu(\varepsilon\circ u_X, id_X) = \mu(id_X,\varepsilon\circ u_X) = id_X$$

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

### Magmas {#Magmas}

All [magmas](wikipedia://en/141916) with [units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_%28mathematics%29) are H-objects in the category $\textbf{Set}$.

### H-spaces {#H-spaces}

Another example of H-objects are H-spaces in the [homotopy category](wikipedia://en/2040756) of [topological spaces](wikipedia://en/30450) $\text{Ho}(\textbf{Top})$.

### H-objects in homotopical algebra {#H-objects_in_homotopical_algebra}

In homotopical algebra, one class of H-objects considered were by Quillen[^o2] while constructing [André–Quillen cohomology](wikipedia://en/23974317) for commutative rings. For this section, let all algebras be commutative, associative, and unital. If we let $A$ be a commutative ring, and let $A\backslash R$ be the [undercategory](wikipedia://en/65601334) of such algebras over $A$ (meaning $A$-algebras), and set $(A\backslash R)/B$ be the associatived overcategory of objects in $A\backslash R$, then an H-object in this category $(A\backslash R)/B$ is an algebra of the form $B\oplus M$ where $M$ is a $B$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410). These algebras have the addition and multiplication operations

> $$\begin{aligned} (b\oplus m)+(b'\oplus m') &= (b + b')\oplus (m+m') \\ (b\oplus m)\cdot(b'\oplus m') &= (bb')\oplus(bm' + b'm) \end{aligned}$$

Note that the multiplication map given above gives the H-object structure $\mu$. Notice that in addition we have the other two structure maps given by

> $$\begin{aligned} u_{B\oplus M }(b\oplus m) &= b\\ \varepsilon (b) &= b\oplus 0 \end{aligned}$$

giving the full H-object structure. Interestingly, these objects have the following property:

> $$\text{Hom}_{(A\backslash R)/B}(Y,B\oplus M) \cong \text{Der}_A(Y, M)$$

giving an isomorphism between the $A$-derivations of $Y$ to $M$ and morphisms from $Y$ to the H-object $B\oplus M$. In fact, this implies $B\oplus M$ is an abelian group object in the category $(A\backslash R)/B$ since it gives a contravariant functor with values in Abelian groups.
