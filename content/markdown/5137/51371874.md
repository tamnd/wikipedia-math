# Charles-Benjamin de Lubières

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles-Benjamin de Lubières"}

**Charles-Benjamin de Langes de Montmirail**, baron **de Lubières**, 1714, Berlin[^o3] – 1 June 1790, was a Genevan mathematician.[^o4]

:::

Charles-de Lubières Benjamin was the son of François de Lange de Montmirail de Lubières (1664–1720) and Marie Calandrini (1677–1762) from Geneva.[^o5] In 1703, the father left the [Principality of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Orange). He first fled to Geneva then to Berlin.

In 1732, he became a citizen of Geneva, and later gouverneur de Neuchâtel and in 1752, a member of the [Council of Two Hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Two_Hundred) of the [Republic of Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Geneva). 22 October 1764, he married Genève Olympe Camp (1709–1785) in Geneva.

Lubières is the author of *Éloge du mathématicien [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331)*, *Relation de voyage en Italie*, extracts from *Essai analytique sur les facultés de l'âme, by [Charles Bonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bonnet)* and *Considérations sur les corps organisés*.

Lubières was a member of the Société des Gens de Lettres de Genève together with the mathematician and philosopher [Gabriel Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331), [Jean-Louis Calandrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Calandrini) (1703–1758) and the attorney general [Jean-Robert Tronchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Robert_Tronchin) (1710–1793).

He wrote the articles *Probabilité*, *Idée*, *Induction* for the *[Encyclopédie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopédie)* by [Diderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Diderot).
