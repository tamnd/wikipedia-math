# Yamada–Watanabe theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yamada–Watanabe theorem"}

The **Yamada–Watanabe theorem** is a result from [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) saying that for a large class of [stochastic differential equations](wikipedia://en/1361454) a *weak solution* with *pathwise uniqueness* implies a *strong solution* and *uniqueness in distribution*. In its original form, the theorem was stated for $n$-dimensional *Itô equations* and was proven by [Toshio Yamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshio_Yamada_%28mathematician%29) and [Shinzo Watanabe](wikipedia://en/48084343) in 1971.[^o2] Since then, many generalizations appeared particularly one for general [semimartingales](wikipedia://en/9653775) by [Jean Jacod](wikipedia://en/58079626) from 1980.[^o3]

:::

## Yamada–Watanabe theorem {#Yamada–Watanabe_theorem}

### History, generalizations and related results {#History,_generalizations_and_related_results}

[Jean Jacod](wikipedia://en/58079626) generalized the result to SDEs of the form

$$dX_t=u(X,Z)dZ_t,$$

where $(Z_t)_{t\geq 0}$ is a [semimartingale](wikipedia://en/9653775) and the coefficient $u$ can depend on the path of $Z$.[^o3]

Further generalisations were done by Hans-Jürgen Engelbert (1991[^o7]) and [Thomas G. Kurtz](wikipedia://en/51178615) (2007[^o8]). For SDEs in [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) there is a result from Martin Ondrejat (2004[^o9]), one by [Michael Röckner](wikipedia://en/31709769), Byron Schmuland and Xicheng Zhang (2008[^o10]) and one by Stefan Tappe (2013[^o11]).

The converse of the theorem is also true and called the *dual Yamada–Watanabe theorem*. The first version of this theorem was proven by Engelbert (1991[^o7]) and a more general version by Alexander Cherny (2002[^o12]).

### Setting {#Setting}

Let $n,r\in\mathbb{N}$ and $C(\mathbb{R}_+,\mathbb{R}^n)$ be the space of continuous functions. Consider the $n$-dimensional Itô equation

$$dX_t=b(t,X)dt+\sigma(t,X)dW_t,\quad X_0=x_0$$

where

- $b\colon \mathbb{R}_+\times C(\mathbb{R}_+,\mathbb{R}^n)\to\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\sigma \colon \mathbb{R}_+\times C(\mathbb{R}_+,\mathbb{R}^n)\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}$ are [predictable processes](wikipedia://en/33414015),
- $(W_t)_{t\geq 0}=\left((W^{(1)}_t,\dots,W^{(r)}_t)\right)_{t\geq 0}$ is an $r$-dimensional [Brownian Motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_Motion),
- $x_0\in \mathbb{R}^n$ is deterministic.

### Basic terminology {#Basic_terminology}

We say *uniqueness in distribution* (or *weak uniqueness*), if for two arbitrary solutions $(X^{(1)},W^{(1)})$ and $(X^{(2)},W^{(2)})$ defined on (possibly different) [filtered](wikipedia://en/57854817) probability spaces $(\Omega_1,\mathcal{F}_1,\mathbf{F}_1,P_1)$ and $(\Omega_2,\mathcal{F}_2,\mathbf{F}_2,P_2)$, we have for their distributions $P_{X^{(1)}}=P_{X^{(2)}}$, where $P_{X^{(1)}}:=\operatorname{Law}(X_t^{1},t\geq 0)$.

We say *pathwise uniqueness* (or *strong uniqueness*) if any two solutions $(X^{(1)},W)$ and $(X^{(2)},W)$, defined on the same filtered probability spaces $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{F},P)$ with the same $\mathbf{F}$-Brownian motion, are [indistinguishable](wikipedia://en/47895#Indistinguishable) processes, i.e. we have $P$-almost surely that $\{X_t^{(1)}=X_t^{(2)},t\geq 0\}$

::: {#o16 .statement cue=heading name="Theorem"}

### Theorem {#Theorem}

Assume the described setting above is valid, then the theorem is:

If there is *pathwise uniqueness*, then there is also *uniqueness in distribution*. And if for every initial distribution, there exists a weak solution, then for every initial distribution, also a pathwise unique strong solution exists.[^o7][^o12]

Jacod's result improved the statement with the additional statement that

If a weak solutions exists and pathwise uniqueness holds, then this solution is also a strong solution.[^o3]
