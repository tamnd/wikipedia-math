# Conductor (class field theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Conductor"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **conductor** of a [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [abelian extension](wikipedia://en/174448) of [local](wikipedia://en/145375) or [global fields](wikipedia://en/288358) provides a quantitative measure of the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) in the extension. The definition of the conductor is related to the [Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_map).

:::

## Local conductor {#Local_conductor}

Let $L/K$ be a finite abelian extension of [non-Archimedean local fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Archimedean_local_field). The **conductor** of $L/K$, denoted $\mathfrak{f}(L/K)$, is the smallest non-negative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ such that the [higher unit group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_unit_group)

$$U^{(n)} = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_K^n = \left\{u\in\mathcal{O}^\times: u \equiv 1\, \left(\operatorname{mod} \mathfrak{m}_K^n\right)\right\}$$

is contained in $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$, where $N_{L/K}$ is [field norm](wikipedia://en/450555) map and $\mathfrak{m}_K$ is the [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/145375) of $K$.[^n1] Equivalently, $n$ is the smallest integer such that the [local Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Artin_map) is trivial on $U_K^{(n)}$. Sometimes, the conductor is defined as $\mathfrak{m}_K^n$ where $n$ is as above.[^n2]

The conductor of an extension measures the ramification. Qualitatively, the extension is [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified) if and only if the conductor is zero,[^n3] and it is [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified) if and only if the conductor is 1.[^n4] More precisely, the conductor computes the non-triviality of [higher ramification groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_ramification_group): if $s$ is the largest integer for which the "[lower numbering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_numbering)" higher ramification group *G<sub>s</sub>* is non-trivial, then $\mathfrak{f}(L/K) = \eta_{L/K}(s) + 1$, where $\eta_{L/K}$ is the function that translates from "lower numbering" to "[upper numbering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_numbering)" of higher ramification groups.[^n5]

The conductor of $L/K$ is also related to the [Artin conductors](wikipedia://en/34170016) of characters of the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. Specifically,[^n6]

$$\mathfrak{m}_K^{\mathfrak{f}(L/K)} = \operatorname{lcm}\limits_\chi \mathfrak{m}_K^{\mathfrak{f}_\chi}$$

where $\chi$ varies over all [multiplicative complex characters](wikipedia://en/19414433) of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, $\mathfrak{f}_\chi$ is the Artin conductor of $\chi$, and lcm is the [least common multiple](wikipedia://en/17961).

### More general fields {#More_general_fields}

The conductor can be defined in the same way for $L/K$ a not necessarily abelian finite Galois extension of local fields.[^n1] However, it only depends on $L^\mathrm{ab}/K$, the maximal abelian extension of $K$ in $L$, because of the "norm limitation theorem", which states that, in this situation,[^n7][^n8]

$$N_{L/K}\left(L^\times\right) = N_{L^{\text{ab}}/K} \Big(\left(L^{\text{ab}}\right)^\times \Big).$$

Additionally, the conductor can be defined when $L$ and $K$ are allowed to be slightly more general than local, namely if they are [complete valued fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_valued_field) with [quasi-finite](wikipedia://en/4644135) residue field.[^o13]

### Archimedean fields {#Archimedean_fields}

Mostly for the sake of global conductors, the conductor of the trivial extension $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{R}$ is defined to be 0, and the conductor of the extension $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$ is defined to be 1.[^n9]

## Global conductor {#Global_conductor}

### Algebraic number fields {#Algebraic_number_fields}

The **conductor** of an abelian extension $L/K$ of number fields can be defined, similarly to the local case, using the Artin map. Specifically, let $\theta: l^m\to\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ be the [global Artin map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Artin_map) where the [modulus](wikipedia://en/18510677) **m** is a [defining modulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defining_modulus) for *L*/*K*; we say that [Artin reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_reciprocity_law) holds for **m** if θ factors through the [ray class group modulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_class_group) **m**. We define the conductor of $L/K$, denoted $\mathfrak{f}(L/K)$, to be the highest common factor of all moduli for which reciprocity holds; in fact reciprocity holds for $\mathfrak{f}(L/K)$, so it is the smallest such modulus.[^n10][^n11][^o20]

::: {#o22 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

#### Example {#Example}

- Taking as base the field of rational numbers, the [Kronecker–Weber theorem](wikipedia://en/1082550) states that an algebraic number field $K$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if it is a subfield of a [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$, where $\zeta_n$ denotes a primitive $n$th root of unity.[^n12] If $n$ is the smallest integer for which this holds, the conductor of $K$ is then $n$ if $K$ is fixed by complex conjugation and $n \infty$ otherwise.
- Let $L/K$ be $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})/\mathbb{Q}$ where $d$ is a [squarefree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree) integer. Then,[^n13]

$$\mathfrak{f}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})/\mathbb{Q}) = \begin{cases} |\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}| & \text{for }d > 0 \\ \infty|\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}| & \text{for }d < 0 \end{cases}$$

where $\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})}$ is the [discriminant](wikipedia://en/3022963) of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})/\mathbb{Q}$.

:::

#### Relation to local conductors and ramification {#Relation_to_local_conductors_and_ramification}

The global conductor is the product of local conductors:[^o26]

$$\mathfrak{f}(L/K) = \prod_\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{p}^{\mathfrak{f}\left(L_\mathfrak{p}/K_\mathfrak{p}\right)}.$$

As a consequence, a finite prime is ramified in $L/K$ if, and only if, it divides $\mathfrak{f}(L/K)$.[^n14] An infinite prime $v$ occurs in the conductor if, and only if, $v$ is real and becomes complex in $L$.
