# Quasi-derivative

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasi-derivative"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **quasi-derivative** is one of several generalizations of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921) of a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) between two [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989). The quasi-derivative is a slightly stronger version of the [Gateaux derivative](wikipedia://en/2253139), though weaker than the [Fréchet derivative](wikipedia://en/2735727).

:::

Let *f* : *A* → *F* be a [continuous function](wikipedia://en/6122) from an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) *A* in a Banach space *E* to another Banach space *F*. Then the **quasi-derivative** of *f* at *x*<sub>0</sub> ∈ *A* is a [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) *u* : *E* → *F* with the following property: for every continuous function *g* : \[0,1\] → *A* with *g*(0)=*x*<sub>0</sub> such that *g*′(0) ∈ *E* exists,

$$\lim_{t\to 0^+}\frac{f(g(t))-f(x_0)}{t} = u(g'(0)).$$

If such a linear map *u* exists, then *f* is said to be *quasi-differentiable* at *x*<sub>0</sub>.

Continuity of *u* need not be assumed, but it follows instead from the definition of the quasi-derivative. If *f* is Fréchet differentiable at *x*<sub>0</sub>, then by the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113), *f* is also quasi-differentiable and its quasi-derivative is equal to its Fréchet derivative at *x*<sub>0</sub>. The converse is true provided *E* is finite-dimensional. Finally, if *f* is quasi-differentiable, then it is Gateaux differentiable and its Gateaux derivative is equal to its quasi-derivative.
