# R. P. Paranjpye

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="R. P. Paranjpye"}

**Sir Raghunath Purushottam Paranjpye** (16 February 1876 – 6 May 1966) also known as **Wrangler Paranjpye** was an [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) mathematician and diplomat who served as the [1st High Commissioner of India to Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_commissioners_of_India_to_Australia) from 1945 to 1947. He became the first Indian to achieve the coveted title of [Senior Wrangler](wikipedia://en/19355609) at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge). Later in life he also served as the Vice Chancellor of many Indian universities.

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Raghunath Paranjpye was born in Murdi near [Dapoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapoli) on the coastal [Ratnagiri district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratnagiri_district) of [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra) to a [Chitpavan Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitpavan_Brahmin) family. He was educated at Maratha high school, [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay), [Fergusson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergusson_College), [Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune) and [Bombay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_University) before entering [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) in 1896. He graduated B.A. as senior [Wrangler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrangler_%28University_of_Cambridge%29) in 1899. Paranjpye was elected a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow) of St John's College in November 1901[^o5] and stayed as such until 1907, but returned to India to become a professor of mathematics at Fergusson College in 1902.[^o4] One of the earliest Indian documentary film makers, [H. S. Bhatavdekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._S._Bhatavdekar), made silent documentary films, *Return of Wrangler Paranjpye* (1902) and *Delhi Durbar of Lord Curzon* (1903), featuring R. P.[^o6][^o8][^o9]

## Career {#Career}

In 1907, R. P. became the first librarian of the [Indian Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mathematical_Society) at Fergusson College.[^o11] He became the college's principal, and stayed in that position for two decades, until 1926.[^o12] Subsequently, he consecutively became the Vice-Chancellor of [Bombay University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_University)[^o13] and [University of Lucknow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Lucknow)[^o14] In 1921, the [University of Calcutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Calcutta) awarded him an honorary Doctor of Science degree.[^o15]

Sir Raghunath was elected to the [Bombay Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Legislative_Council) in 1912 representing the University of Bombay constituency. He was again elected to the enlarged Council as per the [Government of India Act 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1919). As part of [Diarchy in Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarchy_in_Bombay_Presidency), Paranjpye was appointed as the first Minister for Education and he served in the position till 1923. He was unable to get elected in the 1923 elections losing to [M. R. Jayakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._R._Jayakar) of the [Swaraj Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj_Party).

Paranjpye received a knighthood from the colonial government in 1942.[^o4] In the three years (1944–1947) preceding India's independence from the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), the British government appointed him India's High Commissioner to Australia.[^o16] In the days of the British Raj, there was some criticism that R. P. had often appeared on the side of British authorities at a time of nationalist ferment in India.

He was the founder of the [Indian Rationalist Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rationalist_Association) in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) (then [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras)) in 1949,[^o17] and remained its President for many years.[^o18] His autobiography, *84 Not Out*, appeared in 1961.

[Acharya Atre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acharya_Atre) has devoted one full chapter in his autobiography to Wrangler Paranjpye and has written about his fame all over the country and how because of him students from outside Maharashtra came to study at Ferguson College.

## Family {#Family}

His younger brother, Hari Purushottam Paranjpye was a well known agriculturist of his time. In 1991, the Government of India awarded R. P.'s daughter [Shakuntala Paranjpye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakuntala_Paranjpye) a [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan) title in recognition of her work in the field of family planning. She was also a nominated member of the [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha) in the 1960s.[^o20] In 2006, the Government of India awarded R. P.'s granddaughter [Sai Paranjpye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Paranjpye) a [Padma Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padma_Bhushan) title in recognition of her artistic talents. She is a film director and a scriptwriter.[^o21]
