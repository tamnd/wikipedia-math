# Subdivision bifiltration

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Subdivision bifiltration"}

In [topological data analysis](wikipedia://en/17740009), a **subdivision bifiltration** is a collection of [filtered](wikipedia://en/726014) [simplicial complexes](wikipedia://en/252686), typically built upon a set of data points in a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018), that captures shape and density information about the underlying data set. The subdivision bifiltration relies on a natural filtration of the [barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651) of a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686) by [flags](wikipedia://en/9352497) of minimum dimension, which encodes density information about the metric space upon which the complex is built. The subdivision bifiltration was first introduced by Donald Sheehy in 2011 as part of his doctoral thesis[^o2] (later subsumed by a conference paper in 2012[^o3]) as a [discrete model](wikipedia://en/1600294) of the [multicover bifiltration](wikipedia://en/73139878), a continuous construction whose underlying framework dates back to the 1970s.[^o4] In particular, Sheehy applied the construction to both the [Vietoris-Rips](wikipedia://en/13056778) and [Čech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Čech) filtrations, two common objects in the field of topological data analysis.[^o5][^o6][^o7] Whereas single parameter filtrations are not robust with respect to outliers in the data,[^o8] the subdivision-Rips and -Cech bifiltrations satisfy several desirable stability properties.[^o9]

:::

::: {#o11 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $T$ be a [simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/252686). Then a nested sequence of [simplices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplices) $\sigma_1 \subset \sigma_2 \subset \cdots \subset \sigma_k$ of $T$ is called a *flag* or *chain* of $T$. The set of all flags of $T$ comprises an [abstract simplicial complex](wikipedia://en/723105), known as the *[barycentric subdivision](wikipedia://en/583651)* of $T$, denoted by $\operatorname{Bary}(T)$. The barycentric subdivision is naturally identified with a geometric subdivision of $T$, created by *starring* the [geometric realization](wikipedia://en/1027784) of $T$ at the [barycenter](wikipedia://en/762954) of each simplex.[^o12]

There is a natural [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) on $\operatorname{Bary}(T)$ by considering for each [natural number](wikipedia://en/21474) $k$ the maximal subcomplex of $\operatorname{Bary}(T)$ spanned by [vertices](wikipedia://en/593693) of $\operatorname{Bary}(T)$ corresponding to simplices of $T$ of dimension at least $k-1$, which is denoted $\tilde \mathcal S (T)_k$. In particular, by this convention, then $\tilde \mathcal S (T)_1 = \operatorname{Bary}(T)$. Considering the sequence of nested subcomplexes given by varying the parameter $k$, we obtain a filtration on $\operatorname{Bary}(T)$ known as the *subdivision filtration.* Since the complexes in the subdivision filtration shrink as $k$ increases, we can regard it as a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $\tilde \mathcal S (-): \mathbb N^\operatorname{op} \to \mathbf{Simp}$ from the [opposite](wikipedia://en/372399) [posetal category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posetal_category) $\mathbb N^\operatorname{op}$ to the category $\mathbf{Simp}$ of simplicial complexes and [simplicial maps](wikipedia://en/25738537).

Let $P$ be a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572). Given a simplicial filtration $F: P \to \mathbf{Simp}$, regarded as a functor from the posetal category of $P$ to the category $\mathbf{Simp}$, by applying the subdivision filtration object-wise on $F$, we obtain a two-parameter filtration $\mathcal S (F): \mathbb N^\operatorname{op}\times P \to \mathbf{Simp}$, called the *subdivision bifiltration.*[^o13]

In particular, when we take $F$ to be the [Rips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rips_complex) or [Čech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Čech) filtration, we obtain bifiltrations $\mathcal S \operatorname{Rips}(-)$ and $\mathcal S \operatorname{\check{C}ech}(-)$, respectively.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

The subdivision-Čech bifiltration is weakly equivalent to the multicover bifiltration, implying that they have isomorphic persistent homology. A combinatorial proof of this statement was given in Sheehy's original conference paper, but a more algebraic version was presented in 2017 by Cavanna et al.[^o16] The ideas from Cavanna's proof were later generalized by Blumberg and Lesnick in a 2022 paper on 2-parameter persistent homology.[^o9]

By the *size* of a bifiltration, we mean the number of simplices in the largest complex. The subdivision-Čech bifiltration has exponential size as a function of the number of vertices.[^o17] This implies that its [homology](wikipedia://en/142432) cannot be directly computed in [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial-time). However, for points in Euclidean space, the homology of subdivision-Čech can be computed in polynomial time, up to [weak equivalence](wikipedia://en/34615176), via a construction known as the *[rhomboid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhomboid) bifiltration.* As a precursor to the rhomboid bifiltration, [Edelsbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Edelsbrunner) and Osang presented in 2021 a [polyhedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedral_surface) [cell complex](wikipedia://en/353022) called the *rhomboid tiling*, which they used to compute horizontal or vertices slices of the multicover bifiltration up to weak equivalence.[^o18] This was extended a year later by Corbet et al. to the rhomboid bifiltration, which is weakly equivalent to the multicover bifiltration, but has polynomial size.[^o17]
