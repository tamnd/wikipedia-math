# Yael Dowker

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Yael Dowker"}

**Yael Naim Dowker** (יעל נעים דוקר; born **Yael Naim**; 30 October 1919 – 28 January 2016)[^o3] was an Israeli-born [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), prominent especially due to her work in the fields of [measure theory](wikipedia://en/19873), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986) and [topological dynamics](wikipedia://en/16379713).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Yael Naim (later Dowker) was born in [Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv).[^o3] She left for the United States to study at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) in [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore), [Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland). In 1941, as a graduate student, she met [Clifford Hugh Dowker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Hugh_Dowker), a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada) [topologist](wikipedia://en/29954) working as an instructor there. The couple married in 1944. From 1943 to 1946 they worked together at the [Radiation Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_Laboratory_%28MIT%29) at [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). Clifford also worked as a civilian adviser for the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force) during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II).[^o5]

Dowker did her doctorate at [Radcliffe College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radcliffe_College) (in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts)) under [Witold Hurewicz](wikipedia://en/362385) (a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) mathematician known for the [Hurewicz theorem](wikipedia://en/591587)). She published her thesis *Invariant measure and the ergodic theorems* in 1947 and received her Ph.D. in 1948.[^o6] In the period between 1948 and 1949, she did post-doctoral work at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179), located in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). A few years after the war, [McCarthyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism) became a common phenomenon in the academic world, with several of the Dowker couple's friends in the mathematical community harassed and one arrested. In 1950, they emigrated to the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom).[^o5]

In 1951 Dowker was appointed as assistant lecturer at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Manchester),[^o7] and later went to the [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London), where she was the first female reader within the department.[^o3] While there, among the students she advised was [Bill Parry](wikipedia://en/7618438), who published his thesis in 1960.[^o6] She also cooperated on some of her work with the [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary) mathematician [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) (Erdős' number of one). She worked with her husband with gifted children who were having difficulties at school for the [National association for gifted children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_association_for_gifted_children).[^o3][^o5]

## Legacy {#Legacy}

The best PhD award at [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London) is given in her name each year.[^o9]

## Works {#Works}

- *Invariant measure and the ergodic theorems*, Duke Math. J. 14 (1947), 1051–1061
- *Finite and $\sigma$-finite measures*, Annals of Mathematics, 54 (1951), 595–608
- *The mean and transitive points of homeomorphisms*, Annals of Mathematics, 58 (1953), 123–133
- *On limit sets in dynamical systems*, Proc. London Math. Soc. 4 (1954), 168–176 (with Friedlander, F. G.)
- *On minimal sets in dynamical systems*, Quart. J. Math. Oxford Ser. (2) 7 (1956), 5–16
- *Some examples in ergodic theory*, Proc. London Math. Soc. 9 (1959), 227–241 (with Erdős, Paul)
