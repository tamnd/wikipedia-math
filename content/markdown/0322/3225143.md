# ECC patents

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="ECC patents"}

[Patent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent)-related uncertainty around [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography) (ECC), or **ECC patents**, is one of the main factors limiting its wide acceptance. For example, the [OpenSSL](wikipedia://en/438819) team accepted an ECC patch only in 2005 (in OpenSSL version 0.9.8), despite the fact that it was submitted in 2002.

:::

According to [Bruce Schneier](wikipedia://en/36732) as of May 31, 2007, "Certicom certainly can claim ownership of ECC. The algorithm was developed and patented by the company's founders, and the patents are well written and strong. I don't like it, but they can claim ownership."[^o2] Additionally, [NSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA) has licensed [MQV](wikipedia://en/1586447) and other ECC patents from [Certicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certicom) in a US\$25 million deal for [NSA Suite B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSA_Suite_B) algorithms.[^o3] (ECMQV is no longer part of Suite B.)

However, according to [RSA Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_Laboratories), "*in all of these cases, it is the implementation technique that is patented, not the prime or representation, and there are alternative, compatible implementation techniques that are not covered by the patents.*"[^o4] Additionally, [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983) has stated that he is "not aware of" patents that cover the [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444) [elliptic curve Diffie–Hellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_Diffie–Hellman) algorithm or its implementation.[^o5] 6090, published in February 2011, documents ECC techniques, some of which were published so long ago that even if they were patented, any such patents for these previously published techniques would now be expired.

## Known patents {#Known_patents}

- [Certicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certicom) holds a patent on efficient *GF(2<sup>n</sup>)* multiplication in normal basis representation; 5787028 expired in 2016.
- Certicom holds multiple patents which cover the MQV ([Menezes](wikipedia://en/4356319), Qu, and [Vanstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Vanstone)) key agreement technique:
  - 5761305 expired in 2015
  - 5889865 expired in 2015
  - 5896455 expired in 2015
  - 6122736 expired in 2015
  - 6785813 expired in 2017
  - EP/[EP0739105B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0739105B1) expired in 2016
- Certicom holds 6563928 on validating the key exchange messages using ECC to prevent a man-in-the-middle attack, which expired in 2016. Related 5933504, 8953787, 8229113 also expired in 2016 and 7567669 expired in 2018.
- Certicom holds 6704870 and 5999626 regarding digital signatures on a smartcard; these expired in 2017 and 2016 respectively.
- Certicom holds 6782100 on calculating the x-coordinate of the double of a point in binary curves via a Montgomery ladder in projective coordinates. The priority date is Jan 29, 1997, and the filing date is Oct 2, 2000. Claims disclosed in the original patent application have expired, but some claims kept going enforceable until 2020.
- US [National Security Agency](wikipedia://en/21939) holds 4567600, 4587627, 6212279, 6243467 on efficient *GF(2<sup>n</sup>)* calculations on a [normal basis](wikipedia://en/1028589) (all of these patents expired or lapsed (as Google shows) due to failure to pay fees)
- [RSA Data Security](wikipedia://en/301240) holds 5854759 on efficient [basis conversion](wikipedia://en/21170). It expired in 2017.
- [Hewlett-Packard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard) holds 6252960 on compression and decompression of data points on elliptic curves. It expired in 2018.

According to the NSA, Certicom holds over 130 patents relating to elliptic curves and public key cryptography in general.[^o7]

It is difficult to create a complete list of patents that are related to ECC. Still, a good starting point is [the Standards for Efficient Cryptography Group (SECG)](wikipedia://en/1582238) – a group devoted exclusively to developing standards based on ECC, however, https://www.secg.org/ the group's official website has an indicator that states "shut down for repairs" since 2014. It states that "The site is being restored" since then. There is controversy over the validity of some of the patent claims.[^o5]

## Certicom's lawsuit against Sony {#Certicom's_lawsuit_against_Sony}

On May 30, 2007, Certicom filed a lawsuit against [Sony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony) in [United States District Court for the Eastern District of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Texas) [Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall,_Texas) office, claiming that Sony's use of ECC in [Advanced Access Content System](wikipedia://en/11010249) and [Digital Transmission Content Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Transmission_Content_Protection) violates Certicom's patents for that cryptographic method. In particular, Certicom alleged violation of 6563928 and 6704870. The lawsuit was dismissed on May 27, 2009.[^o9] The stipulation states, "Whereas Certicom and Sony have entered into a settlement agreement according to which they have agreed to a dismissal without prejudice, these parties, therefore jointly move to dismiss all claims and counterclaims asserted in this suit, without prejudice to the right to pursue any such claims and counterclaims in the future."[^o10]

As for the [prior art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_art), Sony claimed:[^o11]

- For '870 patent: Alfred J. Menezes, Minghua Qu, and Scott A. Vanstone, *IEEE P1363 Standard, Standard for RSA, Diffie–Hellman and Related Public-Key Cryptography, Part 6: Elliptic Curve Systems (Draft 2)* (October 30, 1994)
- For '928 patent: Scott A. Vanstone, G. B. Agnew, and R. C. Mullin, *An implementation of elliptic curve cryptosystems over F<sub>2</sub>155*, IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications, Volume 11, Issue 5, Jun 1993 p. 804 - 813

## Modern curves {#Modern_curves}

According to [Daniel J. Bernstein](wikipedia://en/198983), the [Curve25519](wikipedia://en/17935444) and efficient implementations thereof can be free from patent encumbrance.[^o13]
