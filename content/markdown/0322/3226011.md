# Coadjoint representation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coadjoint representation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **coadjoint representation** $K$ of a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $G$ is the [dual](wikipedia://en/1442471) of the [adjoint representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_group). If $\mathfrak{g}$ denotes the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) of $G$, the corresponding action of $G$ on $\mathfrak{g}^*$, the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) to $\mathfrak{g}$, is called the **coadjoint action**. A geometrical interpretation is as the action by left-translation on the space of right-invariant [1-forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-form) on $G$.

:::

The importance of the coadjoint representation was emphasised by work of [Alexandre Kirillov](wikipedia://en/3772515), who showed that for [nilpotent Lie groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilpotent_Lie_group) $G$ a basic role in their [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) is played by **coadjoint orbits**. In the Kirillov method of orbits, representations of $G$ are constructed geometrically starting from the coadjoint orbits. In some sense those play a substitute role for the [conjugacy classes](wikipedia://en/49176) of $G$, which again may be complicated, while the orbits are relatively tractable.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $G$ be a Lie group and $\mathfrak{g}$ be its Lie algebra. Let $\mathrm{Ad} : G \rightarrow \mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})$ denote the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of $G$. Then the **coadjoint representation** $\mathrm{Ad}^*: G \rightarrow \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g}^*)$ is defined by

$\langle \mathrm{Ad}^*_g \, \mu, Y \rangle = \langle \mu, \mathrm{Ad}^{-1}_{g} Y \rangle = \langle \mu, \mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}} Y \rangle$ for $g \in G, Y \in \mathfrak{g}, \mu \in \mathfrak{g}^*,$

where $\langle \mu, Y \rangle$ denotes the value of the linear functional $\mu$ on the vector $Y$.

Let $\mathrm{ad}^*$ denote the representation of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ on $\mathfrak{g}^*$ induced by the coadjoint representation of the Lie group $G$. Then the infinitesimal version of the defining equation for $\mathrm{Ad}^*$ reads:

$\langle \mathrm{ad}^*_X \mu, Y \rangle = \langle \mu, - \mathrm{ad}_X Y \rangle = - \langle \mu, [X, Y] \rangle$ for $X,Y \in \mathfrak{g}, \mu \in \mathfrak{g}^*$

where $\mathrm{ad}$ is the [adjoint representation of the Lie algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_representation_of_a_Lie_algebra) $\mathfrak{g}$.

:::

## Coadjoint orbit {#Coadjoint_orbit}

A coadjoint orbit $\mathcal{O}_\mu$ for $\mu$ in the dual space $\mathfrak{g}^*$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ may be defined either extrinsically, as the actual [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_%28group_theory%29) $\mathrm{Ad}^*_G \mu$ inside $\mathfrak{g}^*$, or intrinsically as the [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325) $G/G_\mu$ where $G_\mu$ is the [stabilizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29#Orbits_and_stabilizers) of $\mu$ with respect to the coadjoint action; this distinction is worth making since the embedding of the orbit may be complicated.

The coadjoint orbits are submanifolds of $\mathfrak{g}^*$ and carry a natural symplectic structure. On each orbit $\mathcal{O}_\mu$, there is a closed non-degenerate $G$-invariant [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) $\omega \in \Omega^2(\mathcal{O}_\mu)$ inherited from $\mathfrak{g}$ in the following manner:

$$\omega_\nu(\mathrm{ad}^*_X \nu, \mathrm{ad}^*_Y \nu) := \langle \nu, [X, Y] \rangle , \nu \in \mathcal{O}_\mu, X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}.$$

The well-definedness, non-degeneracy, and $G$-invariance of $\omega$ follow from the following facts:

(i) The tangent space $\mathrm{T}_\nu \mathcal{O}_\mu = \{ -\mathrm{ad}^*_X \nu : X \in \mathfrak{g}\}$ may be identified with $\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_\nu$, where $\mathfrak{g}_\nu$ is the Lie algebra of $G_\nu$.

(ii) The kernel of the map $X \mapsto \langle \nu, [X, \cdot] \rangle$ is exactly $\mathfrak{g}_\nu$.

(iii) The bilinear form $\langle \nu, [\cdot, \cdot] \rangle$ on $\mathfrak{g}$ is invariant under $G_\nu$.

$\omega$ is also [closed](wikipedia://en/404181). The canonical [2-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2-form) $\omega$ is sometimes referred to as the *Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau symplectic form* or *KKS form* on the coadjoint orbit.

### Properties of coadjoint orbits {#Properties_of_coadjoint_orbits}

The coadjoint action on a coadjoint orbit $(\mathcal{O}_\mu, \omega)$ is a [Hamiltonian $G$-action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_action) with [momentum map](wikipedia://en/3701344) given by the inclusion $\mathcal{O}_\mu \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{g}^*$.
