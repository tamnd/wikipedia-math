# Landau prime ideal theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Landau prime ideal theorem"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), the **prime ideal theorem** is the [number field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) generalization of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692). It provides an asymptotic formula for counting the number of [prime ideals](wikipedia://en/24928) of a number field *K*, with [norm](wikipedia://en/450555) at most *X*.

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

What to expect can be seen already for the [Gaussian integers](wikipedia://en/48628). There for any prime number *p* of the form 4*n* + 1, *p* factors as a product of two [Gaussian primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_prime) of norm *p*. Primes of the form 4*n* + 3 remain prime, giving a Gaussian prime of norm *p*<sup>2</sup>. Therefore, we should estimate

$$2r(X)+r^\prime(\sqrt{X})$$

where *r* counts primes in the arithmetic progression 4*n* + 1, and *r*′ in the arithmetic progression 4*n* + 3. By the quantitative form of [Dirichlet's theorem on primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirichlet's_theorem_on_primes), each of *r*(*Y*) and *r*′(*Y*) is asymptotically

$$\frac{Y}{2\log Y}.$$

Therefore, the 2*r*(*X*) term dominates, and is asymptotically

$$\frac{X}{\log X}.$$

:::

## General number fields {#General_number_fields}

This general pattern holds for number fields in general, so that the prime ideal theorem is dominated by the ideals of norm a prime number. As [Edmund Landau](wikipedia://en/222230) proved in (Landau 1903), for norm at most *X* the same asymptotic formula

$$\frac{X}{\log X}$$

always holds. Heuristically this is because the [logarithmic derivative](wikipedia://en/470327) of the [Dedekind zeta-function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedekind_zeta-function) of *K* always has a simple pole with residue −1 at *s* = 1.

As with the Prime Number Theorem, a more precise estimate may be given in terms of the [logarithmic integral function](wikipedia://en/47137). The number of prime ideals of norm ≤ *X* is

$$\mathrm{Li}(X) + O_K(X \exp(-c_K \sqrt{\log(X)})), \,$$

where *c*<sub>*K*</sub> is a constant depending on *K*.
