# Rhonda Hatcher

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Rhonda Hatcher"}

**Rhonda Lee Hatcher** is an American mathematician whose research topics include [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513) and [L-functions](wikipedia://en/245283) as well as topics in [recreational mathematics](wikipedia://en/26446). She is an emeritus associate professor of mathematics at [Texas Christian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Christian_University).

:::

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Hatcher graduated [summa cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summa_cum_laude) from the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) in 1980. She earned her Ph.D. in 1988 from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University). Her dissertation, *Heights and L-Series*, was supervised by [Benedict Gross](wikipedia://en/4326863). After completing her doctorate, she taught at [St. Olaf College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Olaf_College) in [Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota) for three years before moving to Texas Christian.

At Texas Christian, Hatcher served as the Faculty Athletics Representative from 1999 until 2023. She has also represented the [Big 12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_12_Conference) in the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association) Committee on Academics. Within the committee, she took a minority position against a proposed rule change to allow students with high [grade point averages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_point_average) to transfer to other schools and maintain their athletic eligibility, arguing that the rule would disproportionately penalize African-American students.

## Book {#Book}

With George T. Gilbert, she is the author of *Mathematics Beyond the Numbers* (Wiley, 2000; 2nd ed., Kendall Hunt, 2012). This is a mathematics textbook designed for use in [liberal arts education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts_education). It covers topics mathematics through their applications, including [voting systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_system), [apportionment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apportionment), [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics), [opinion polls](wikipedia://en/277315), [scheduling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduling), and [cryptography](wikipedia://en/18934432). Its mathematical topics include [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401) and [combinatorial geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorial_geometry), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1998, Hatcher won the [Deborah and Franklin Haimo Award for Distinguished College or University Teaching of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_and_Franklin_Haimo_Awards_for_Distinguished_College_or_University_Teaching_of_Mathematics). The award announcement cited her excellent mathematics teaching at Texas Christian, her work helping high school teachers prepare to teach [AP Calculus](wikipedia://en/4234894), and her summer programs which attracted undergraduates from other parts of the country to work with her on number theory, [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), and [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401). Some of the research from this program ended up being published in professional journals.
