# Category of Markov kernels

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of Markov kernels"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **category of Markov kernels**, often denoted **Stoch**, is the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333) and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are [Markov kernels](wikipedia://en/21180014). [^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] It is analogous to the [category of sets and functions](wikipedia://en/26829), but where the [arrows](wikipedia://en/27716891) can be interpreted as being [stochastic](wikipedia://en/292222).

:::

Several variants of this category are used in the literature. For example, one can use subprobability kernels[^o6] instead of probability kernels, or more general [s-finite](wikipedia://en/16629890) kernels.[^o7] Also, one can take as morphisms [equivalence classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of Markov kernels under [almost sure equality](wikipedia://en/351908);[^o8] see below.

::: {#o10 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Recall that a [Markov kernel](wikipedia://en/21180014) between [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333) $(X,\mathcal{F})$ and $(Y,\mathcal{G})$ is an assignment $k:X\times\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}$ which is [measurable](wikipedia://en/44055) as a function on $X$ and which is a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on $\mathcal{G}$.[^o5] We denote its values by $k(B|x)$ for $x\in X$ and $B\in\mathcal{G}$, which suggests an interpretation as [conditional probability](wikipedia://en/24104134).

The category **Stoch** has:[^o5]

- As [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29), [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333);
- As [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891), [Markov kernels](wikipedia://en/21180014) between them;
- For each measurable space $(X,\mathcal{F})$, the [identity morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_morphism) is given by the kernel

$$\delta(A|x) = 1_A(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & x\in A ; \\ 0 & x\notin A \end{cases}$$

for all $x\in X$ and $A\in\mathcal{F}$;

- Given kernels $k:(X,\mathcal{F})\to(Y,\mathcal{G})$ and $h:(Y,\mathcal{G})\to(Z,\mathcal{H})$, the composite morphism $h\circ k:(X,\mathcal{F})\to(Z,\mathcal{H})$ is given by

$$(h\circ k) (C|x) = \int_Y h(C|y) \, k(dy|x)$$

for all $x\in X$ and $C\in\mathcal{H}$.

This composition formula is sometimes called the [Chapman-Kolmogorov equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman-Kolmogorov_equation).[^o5]

This composition is unital, and [associative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative) by the [monotone convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/146263), so that one indeed has a [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29).

:::

## Basic properties {#Basic_properties}

### Probability measures {#Probability_measures}

The [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object) of **Stoch** is the [one-point space](wikipedia://en/381798) $1$.[^o5] Morphisms in the form $1\to X$ can be equivalently seen as [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) on $X$, since they correspond to functions $1\to PX$, i.e. elements of $PX$.

Given kernels $p:1\to X$ and $k:X\to Y$, the composite kernel $k\circ p:1\to Y$ gives the probability measure on $Y$ with values

$$(k\circ p) (B) = \int_X k(B|x)\,p(dx) ,$$

for every measurable subset $B$ of $Y$.[^o8]

Given [probability spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_spaces) $(X,\mathcal{F},p)$ and $(Y,\mathcal{G},q)$, a **measure-preserving Markov kernel** $(X,\mathcal{F},p)\to(Y,\mathcal{G},q)$ is a Markov kernel $k:(X,\mathcal{F})\to(Y,\mathcal{G})$ such that for every measurable subset $B\in\mathcal{G}$,[^o8]

$$q(B) = \int_X k(B|x) \, p(dx) .$$

[Probability spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_spaces) and measure-preserving Markov kernels form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759), which can be seen as the [slice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slice_category) $(\mathrm{Hom}_\mathrm{Stoch}(1,-),\mathrm{Stoch})$.

### Measurable functions {#Measurable_functions}

Every measurable function $f:(X,\mathcal{F})\to(Y,\mathcal{G})$ defines canonically a Markov kernel $\delta_f:(X,\mathcal{F})\to(Y,\mathcal{G})$ as follows,

$$\delta_f(B|x) = 1_B(f(x)) = \begin{cases} 1 & f(x)\in B ; \\ 0 & f(x)\notin B \end{cases}$$

for every $x\in X$ and every $B\in\mathcal{G}$. This construction preserves identities and compositions, and is therefore a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from [**Meas**](wikipedia://en/75966255) to **Stoch**.

### Isomorphisms {#Isomorphisms}

By functoriality, every isomorphism of measurable spaces (in the category [**Meas**](wikipedia://en/75966255)) induces an isomorphism in **Stoch**. However, in **Stoch** there are more isomorphisms, and in particular, measurable spaces can be isomorphic in **Stoch** even when the underlying sets are not in bijection.

:::

## Relationship with other categories {#Relationship_with_other_categories}

- **Stoch** is the [Kleisli category](wikipedia://en/3957200) of the [Giry monad](wikipedia://en/75998118).[^o4][^o5] This in particular implies that there is an [adjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjunction_%28category_theory%29)

$$\mathrm{Hom}_\mathrm{Stoch}(X,Y) \cong \mathrm{Hom}_\mathrm{Meas}(X,PY)$$

between **Stoch** and the [category of measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/75966255).

- The [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint) $L:\mathrm{Meas}\to\mathrm{Stoch}$ of the adjunction above is the identity on objects, and on morphisms it gives the canonical [Markov kernel induced by a measurable function](wikipedia://en/21180014#Measurable_functions) described above.
- As mentioned above, one can construct a category of [probability spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_spaces) and measure-preserving Markov kernels as the [slice category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slice_category) $(\mathrm{Hom}_\mathrm{Stoch}(1,-),\mathrm{Stoch})$.
- Similarly, the [category of probability spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_probability_spaces) can be seen as the [comma category](wikipedia://en/840950) $(\mathrm{Hom}_\mathrm{Stoch}(1,-),L)$.

## Particular limits and colimits {#Particular_limits_and_colimits}

Since the functor $L:\mathrm{Meas}\to\mathrm{Stoch}$ is [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint), it preserves [colimits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit).[^o18] Because of this, all colimits in the [category of measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/75966255) are also colimits in **Stoch**. For example,

- The initial object is the empty set, with its trivial measurable structure;
- The coproduct is given by the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of measurable spaces, with its canonical [sigma-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra).
- The sequential [colimit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colimit) of a decreasing [filtration](wikipedia://en/57854817) is given by the intersection of sigma-algebras.

In general, the functor $L$ does not preserve [limits](wikipedia://en/39248). This in particular implies that the [product of measurable spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_sigma-algebra) is not a product in **Stoch** in general. Since the [Giry monad](wikipedia://en/75998118) is [monoidal](wikipedia://en/4585564), however, the product of measurable spaces still makes **Stoch** a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604).[^o5]

A limit of particular significance for [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) is [de Finetti's theorem](wikipedia://en/180835), which can be interpreted as the fact that the space of probability measures ([Giry monad](wikipedia://en/75998118)) is the limit in **Stoch** of the [diagram](wikipedia://en/7651625) formed by [finite permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchangeability) of sequences.

## Almost sure version {#Almost_sure_version}

Sometimes it is useful to consider Markov kernels only up to [almost sure equality](wikipedia://en/351908), for example when talking about [disintegrations](wikipedia://en/6845737) or about [regular conditional probability](wikipedia://en/18963326).

Given [probability spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_spaces) $(X,\mathcal{F},p)$ and $(Y,\mathcal{G},q)$, we say that two measure-preserving kernels $k,h:(X,\mathcal{F},p)\to(Y,\mathcal{G},q)$ are **almost surely equal** if and only if for every measurable subset $B\in\mathcal{G}$,

$$k(B|x) = h(B|x)$$

for $p$-almost all $x\in X$.[^o8] This defines an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) on the set of measure-preserving Markov kernels $k,h:(X,\mathcal{F},p)\to(Y,\mathcal{G},q)$.

Probability spaces and equivalence classes of Markov kernels under the relation defined above form a [category](wikipedia://en/53759). When restricted to [standard Borel](wikipedia://en/29819325) probability spaces, the category is often denoted by **Krn**.[^o8]
