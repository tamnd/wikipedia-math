# Category of measurable spaces

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of measurable spaces"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **category of measurable spaces**, often denoted **Meas**, is the [category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_%28category_theory%29) whose [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) are [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333) and whose [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) are [measurable maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurable_map).[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] This is a category because the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of two measurable maps is again measurable, and the identity function is measurable.

:::

N.B. Some authors reserve the name **Meas** for categories whose objects are [measure spaces](wikipedia://en/45270), and denote the category of [measurable spaces](wikipedia://en/43333) as **Mble**, or other notations. Some authors also restrict the category only to particular well-behaved measurable spaces, such as [standard Borel spaces](wikipedia://en/29819325).

## As a concrete category {#As_a_concrete_category}

Like many categories, the category **Meas** is a [concrete category](wikipedia://en/167001), meaning its objects are [sets](wikipedia://en/26691) with additional structure (i.e. [sigma-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebra)) and its morphisms are [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) preserving this structure. There is a natural [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783)

*U* : **Meas** → **Set**

to the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829) which assigns to each measurable space the underlying set and to each measurable map the underlying [function](wikipedia://en/185427).

The forgetful functor *U* has both a [left adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_adjoint)

*D* : **Set** → **Meas**

which equips a given set with the discrete sigma-algebra, and a [right adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_adjoint)

*I* : **Set** → **Meas**

which equips a given set with the indiscrete or trivial sigma-algebra. Both of these functors are, in fact, [right inverses](wikipedia://en/14907#Left_and_right_inverses) to *U* (meaning that *UD* and *UI* are equal to the [identity functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_functor) on **Set**). Moreover, since any function between discrete or between indiscrete spaces is measurable, both of these functors give [full embeddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_embedding) of **Set** into **Meas**.

## Limits and colimits {#Limits_and_colimits}

The category **Meas** is both [complete and cocomplete](wikipedia://en/62781), which means that all small [limits and colimits](wikipedia://en/39248) exist in **Meas**. In fact, the forgetful functor *U* : **Meas** → **Set** uniquely lifts both limits and colimits and preserves them as well. Therefore, (co)limits in **Meas** are given by placing particular sigma-algebras on the corresponding (co)limits in **Set**.

Examples of limits and colimits in **Meas** include:

- The [empty set](wikipedia://en/9566) (considered as a measurable space) is the [initial object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initial_object) of **Meas**; any [singleton](wikipedia://en/381798) measurable space is a [terminal object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_object). There are thus no [zero objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_object) in **Meas**.
- The [product](wikipedia://en/237868) in **Meas** is given by the [product sigma-algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_sigma-algebra) on the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095). The [coproduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coproduct_%28category_theory%29) is given by the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of measurable spaces.
- The [equalizer](wikipedia://en/310767#In_category_theory) of a pair of morphisms is given by placing the induced sigma-algebra on the subset given by the set-theoretic equalizer. Dually, the [coequalizer](wikipedia://en/431940) is given by placing the quotient sigma-algebra on the set-theoretic coequalizer.
- [Direct limits](wikipedia://en/211566) and [inverse limits](wikipedia://en/15109) are the set-theoretic limits with the final and initial sigma-algebra respectively. Canonical examples of direct and inverse systems are the ones arising from [filtrations in probability theory](wikipedia://en/57854817), and the limits and colimits of such systems are, respectively, the join and the intersection of [sigma-algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-algebras).

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

- The [monomorphisms](wikipedia://en/59538) in **Meas** are the [injective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injective) measurable maps, the [epimorphisms](wikipedia://en/59539) are the [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective) measurable maps, and the [isomorphisms](wikipedia://en/14828) are the isomorphisms of measurable spaces.
- The split monomorphisms are (essentially) the inclusions of measurable retracts into their ambient space.
- The split epimorphisms are (up to isomorphism) the measurable surjective maps of a measurable space onto one of its retracts.
- **Meas** is not [cartesian closed](wikipedia://en/237037) (and therefore also not a [topos](wikipedia://en/21391464)) since it does not have [exponential objects](wikipedia://en/2237858) for all spaces.
