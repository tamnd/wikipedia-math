# Airy process

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Airy process"}

The **Airy processes** are a family of [stationary](wikipedia://en/329898) [stochastic processes](wikipedia://en/47895) that appear as limit processes in the theory of [random growth models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_growth_model) and [random matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_matrix_theory). They are conjectured to be [universal limits](wikipedia://en/353890) describing the long time, large scale spatial fluctuations of the models in the [(1+1)-dimensional KPZ universality class](wikipedia://en/30724348#KPZ_universality_class) (Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation) for many initial conditions (see also [KPZ fixed point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPZ_fixed_point)).

:::

The original process Airy<sub>2</sub> was introduced in 2002 by the mathematicians [Michael Prähofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Prähofer) and [Herbert Spohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Spohn).[^o2] They proved that the height function of a model from the (1+1)-dimensional KPZ universality class - the [PNG droplet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNG_droplet) - converges under suitable scaling and initial condition to the Airy<sub>2</sub> process and that it is a stationary process with [almost surely](wikipedia://en/351908) continuous sample paths.

The Airy process is named after the [Airy function](wikipedia://en/406896). The process can be defined through its [finite-dimensional distribution](wikipedia://en/6594303) with a [Fredholm determinant](wikipedia://en/2685562) and the so-called **extended Airy kernel**. It turns out that the one-point marginal distribution of the Airy<sub>2</sub> process is the [Tracy-Widom distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy-Widom_distribution) of the [GUE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaussian_unitary_ensemble).

There are several Airy processes. The Airy<sub>1</sub> process was introduced by [Tomohiro Sasomoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomohiro_Sasomoto)[^o3] and the one-point marginal distribution of the Airy<sub>1</sub> is a scalar multiply of the Tracy-Widom distribution of the GOE.[^o4] Another Airy process is the Airy<sub>stat</sub> process.[^o5]

The Airy<sub>1</sub> resp. Airy<sub>2</sub> processes can be thought of as the limit behavior of [TASEP](wikipedia://en/37646664) fluctuations with flat resp. step initial condition.[^o6]

## Airy<sub>2</sub> process {#Airy2_process}

Let $t_1<t_2<\dots <t_n$ be in $\mathbb{R}$.

The **Airy<sub>2</sub> process** $A_2(t)$ has the following finite-dimensional distribution

$$P(A_2(t_{1})<\xi_1,\dots,A_2(t_{n})<\xi_n)=\det(1-f^{1/2}K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}f^{1/2})_{L^2(\{t_1,\dots,t_n\}\times \mathbb{R})}$$

where

$$f:=f(t_j,\xi)=1_{\{(\xi_j,\infty)\}}(\xi)$$

and $K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}:=K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}(t_i,x;t_j,y)$ is the *extended Airy kernel*

$$K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}(t_i,x;t_j,y):=\begin{cases}{\displaystyle \int_0^\infty e^{-z(t_i-t_j )}\operatorname{Ai}(x+z)\operatorname{Ai}(y+z)\mathrm{d}z}& \text{if }\;t_i\geq t_j,\\ {\displaystyle -\int_{-\infty}^0 e^{-z(t_i-t_j)}\operatorname{Ai}(x+z)\operatorname{Ai}(y+z)\mathrm{d}z}&\text{if }\;t_i< t_j.\end{cases}$$

### Explanations {#Explanations}

- If $t_i=t_j$ the extended Airy kernel reduces to the Airy kernel and hence

$$P(A_2(t)\leq \xi)=F_{2}(\xi),$$

where $F_{2}(\xi)$ is the Tracy-Widom distribution of the GUE.

- $f^{1/2}K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}f^{1/2}$ is a [trace class operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_class_operator) on $L^2(\{t_1,\dots,t_n\}\times \mathbb{R})$ with counting measure on $\{t_1,\dots,t_n\}$ and Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb{R}$, the kernel is $f^{1/2}K^{\operatorname{ext}}_{\operatorname{Ai}}(t_i,x;t_j,y)f^{1/2}$.[^o9]
