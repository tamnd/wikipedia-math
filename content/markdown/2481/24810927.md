# Demographics of Western Norway

*Image: Fish market, Bergen, Norway (LOC).jpg.* A picture from [Bergen Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_Municipality) [fish market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_market), taken between 1890 and 1905

*Image: Buoy.jpg.* [Buøy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buøy), a neighbourhood in [Stavanger Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_Municipality)

*Image: From the Stalheim.jpg.* Some parts of western Norway have small populations, such as here at Stalheim, [Voss Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voss_Municipality).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demographics of Western Norway"}

The **[Western Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Norway)** region of [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway) showed the highest population growth rate in Norway in 2010, at 1.44%. The fertility rate in this region is higher than in other parts of Norway. The population as of 1 January 2010 was 1,263,464, with 37.7% of the population living in [Hordaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hordaland), 33.8% in [Rogaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogaland), 19.8% in [Møre og Romsdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Møre_og_Romsdal), and 8.4% in [Sogn og Fjordane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogn_og_Fjordane). 60% of the population is under 40 years old, and 30% is under 20 years old. Many of the historical immigrants in Western Norway came from countries like [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden). Western Norway is the part of Norway which has the largest immigration from the western world.

## Religion {#Religion}

*Image: Bakka kyrkje 1.jpg.* [Bakka Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakka_Church) in [Aurland Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurland_Municipality), [Sogn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogn).

*Image: Stavanger Cathedral pulpit.jpg.* A liturgy in [Stavanger Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_Cathedral).

*Image: Saint Paul Church Bergen Norway 2009 1.JPG.* Saint Paul Church, a [Catholic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_church) in Western Norway.

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) is the largest religion. 1,054,573 people are members of the [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway). There is also 52,365 members in other Christian churches. Islam has 10,685 adherents in Western Norway, Buddhism has 2,082. 1,171 are of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baháʼí_Faith), [Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism), [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism) and other religions.

There are more than thousand [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_%28building%29) (in all municipalities), six [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_%28building%29) (in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29), [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29), [Haugesund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haugesund_%28town%29), [Ålesund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ålesund_%28town%29), [Molde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molde_%28town%29) and [Kristiansund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristiansund_%28town%29)), two [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque) (in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29)), two [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church) [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_%28building%29) (in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29)), and one [Hindu temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_temple) (in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29)).

The name "[Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt_%28Norway%29)" has been applied historically to the [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Norway) and Western parts of Norway. The region thus defined included most of Western Norway, especially [Rogaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogaland), [Møre og Romsdal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Møre_og_Romsdal) and some parts of [Hordaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hordaland). Notably absent from this belt are bigger cities like [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29) where many people identify themselves as non-religious or with other religions. In these areas the conservative branch of the [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway) has a stronghold and the members usually associate themselves to Indremisjonen (Inner Mission). There are also numerous [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostals) and members of the [Free Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Free_Church_of_Norway), but these movements are also strongly represented in the rest of the country. The Bible Belt in Norway traditionally reflects the support for the [Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_%28Norway%29). However, especially since the first decade of the 21st century, conservative bible belt Christians unhappy with the more liberal development of the party have increasingly turned to the [Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Party_%28Norway%29).[^o28][^o29]

### Christianity {#Christianity}

[Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) is the largest religious group. Although more and more ethnic [Norwegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) are less religious, Christian immigrants have increased the population. Many known Christian missionaries came from Western Norway, for example [Torill Selsvold Nyborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torill_Selsvold_Nyborg), who is now the county mayor of [Hordaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hordaland).

*Image: StSunniva.jpg.* Statue of Saint [Sunniva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunniva), the [patron saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron_saint) of the Norwegian [Diocese of Bjørgvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Bjørgvin). According to legend, Sunniva was the heir of an [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland) kingdom, but had to flee when a heathen king, who wanted to marry her, invaded. At the Norwegian island of [Selja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selja,_Stad), in the present-day [Stad Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stad_Municipality), she and her followers took refuge in a cave. The locals suspected the foreigners of stealing their sheep, and the ruler [Håkon Jarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haakon_Sigurdsson) was sent for. Sunniva and her followers prayed to God that they should not fall into the hands of the heathens, upon which rocks fell down blocking the entrance to the cave.

[Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) is the majority religion in Norway. Most Norwegians are [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran) and members of the [Church of Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Norway). Over 85% of the population is member here. Every municipalities have Protestant churches. Many municipalities, for example [Austevoll Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austevoll_Municipality), a small municipality with around 4,000 inhabitants, have over 13 church buildings. Most Norwegians are not active in the church. Nevertheless, there are many who participate to worship at [Christmas Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Eve). People outside the cities are often more religious than the population in the cities, although there are many religious Christians immigrants in the cities.

#### Catholicism {#Catholicism}

[Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) increased its members in Western Norway due to immigration from [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines), [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile), and other [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American) and [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe) countries. There are six Catholic churches (as of 2004), in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) (4,925 members), Stavanger (2,863 members), [Haugesund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haugesund_%28town%29) (822 members), [Ålesund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ålesund_%28town%29) (595 members), [Molde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molde_%28town%29) (289) and [Kristiansund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristiansund_%28town%29) (212). The largest church is St. Paul church in Bergen. The number of Catholics increased rapidly from 2005, because of immigration (mainly [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people)). The capacity in the region is too small for all Catholics, and it is therefore planned to build several Catholic churches in the region, in addition to Orthodox churches.[^o37]

#### Orthodoxy {#Orthodoxy}

There are only two Orthodox congregations in Western Norway, in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29). 368 people are members in Stavanger, but not all of them live in Rogaland. Congregations in some churches in Bergen have Orthodox worship in different languages. The numbers of Orthodox Christians in the region due to increased immigration from countries such as [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia) and the rest of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^o37]

#### Smaller denominations {#Smaller_denominations}

- 9,803 people are [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostals).[^o37]
- 3,876 people are [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses),[^o37] up from 3,872 in 2003.
- 3,692 people are [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodists).[^o37]
- 1,949 people are [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists),[^o37] up from 1,880 in 2003.
- 1,314 people are members in the Norwegian Mission Association.[^o37]
- 1,086 people are members in the [Seventh-day Adventist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist_Church).[^o37]

### Islam {#Islam}

*Image: Moske i Bergen.JPG.* The mosque of The Islamic Association of Bergen (*Det Islamske Forbundet i Bergen*), like most Norwegian mosques situated in a regular town house.

While as many as 7.2% of [Oslo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo)'s population are [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims), this figure is only 0.85% in Western Norway. There are two mosques, one in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger_%28city%29). In total, 10,685 people members of this religious community.[^o43]

#### Judaism {#Judaism}

[Western Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Norway) has a small [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) community. There has never been a [synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagogue) in this part of the country, but before [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), there was one congregation in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen_%28city%29) and one in [Kristiansund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristiansund_%28town%29).
