# Robert Sauer (mathematician)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Robert Sauer"}

**Robert Max Friedrich Sauer** (16 September 1898 – 22 August 1970) was a German mathematician. He was rector of the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) from 1954 to 1956 and president of the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) from 1964 to 1970.[^o4]

:::

## Early life {#Early_life}

After graduating from high school in [Bamberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamberg) and completing his military service in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I) as a non-commissioned officer in the artillery, Sauer studied [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) from 1919. In 1925 he received his doctorate.

## Career {#Career}

After his [habilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation) in 1926 he taught at the university as lecturer for [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891). In 1932 he became associate professor and in 1937 full professor of applied mathematics and descriptive geometry at the [TH Aachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RWTH_Aachen_University).

During this time, Sauer joined the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) and became involved in the [National Socialist German Lecturers League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_German_Lecturers_League). Sauer was appointed dean, in part because of his party affiliation.

During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II) he worked on ballistics and supersonic gas dynamics, constructing analog computers to solve the differential equations involved. He was intensively supported by his friend, the mathematician Franz Krauß, who also taught in Aachen. These calculations were related to the calculation of the trajectories of [V-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) and [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket) rockets sent to London. In 1944, he became a professor at the [TH Karlsruhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsruhe_Institute_of_Technology).

After the end of the war, he was removed from office by the US occupying forces. In return, the French made him an offer to work for the French government in ballistic research. Sauer was soon also employed by the U.S. government in the field of ballistic research.

In 1948, he became a professor at the [Technical University of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Munich) and director of the [Mathematical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUM_Department_of_Mathematics) - especially at the instigation of his friend [Josef Lense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Lense). From 1954 to 1956, he was rector of university.

In 1950, he became a member of the [Bavarian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Humanities) and was its president from 1964 to 1970. It endowed the Robert Sauer Prize in his honor. In 1962, he was elected a member of the [German National Academy of Sciences Leopoldina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_National_Academy_of_Sciences_Leopoldina).
