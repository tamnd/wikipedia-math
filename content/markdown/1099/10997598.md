# Binary octahedral group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binary octahedral group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **binary octahedral group**, name as 2O or $\langle 2,3,4\rangle$[^o2] is a certain [nonabelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonabelian_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 48. It is an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of the [chiral octahedral group](wikipedia://en/2900805#Chiral_octahedral_symmetry) *O* or (2,3,4) of order 24 by a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 2, and is the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of the octahedral group under the 2:1 [covering homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_homomorphism) $\operatorname{Spin}(3) \to \operatorname{SO}(3)$ of the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) by the [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231). It follows that the binary octahedral group is a [discrete subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_subgroup) of Spin(3) of order 48.

:::

The binary octahedral group is most easily described concretely as a discrete subgroup of the unit [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440), under the isomorphism $\operatorname{Spin}(3) \cong \operatorname{Sp}(1)$ where [Sp(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp%281%29) is the multiplicative group of unit quaternions. (For a description of this homomorphism see the article on [quaternions and spatial rotations](wikipedia://en/186057).)

## Elements {#Elements}

*Image: Binary octahedral group elements 12-fold.svg.* 48 elements seen in projection: 1 order-1: 1

Explicitly, the binary octahedral group is given as the union of the 24 [Hurwitz units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_unit)

$$\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k,\tfrac{1}{2}(\pm 1 \pm i \pm j \pm k)\}$$

with all 24 quaternions obtained from

$$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\pm 1 \pm 1i + 0j + 0k)$$

by a [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) of coordinates and all possible sign combinations. All 48 elements have absolute value 1 and therefore lie in the unit quaternion group Sp(1).

Their convex hull as a polychoron is the [disphenoidal 288-cell](wikipedia://en/4000168#Disphenoidal_288-cell).[^o4]

## Properties {#Properties}

The binary octahedral group, denoted by 2*O*, fits into the [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

$$1\to\{\pm 1\}\to 2O\to O \to 1.\,$$

This sequence does not [split](wikipedia://en/1531404), meaning that 2*O* is *not* a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of {±1} by *O*. In fact, there is no subgroup of 2*O* isomorphic to *O*.

The [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_group) of 2*O* is the subgroup {±1}, so that the [inner automorphism group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_automorphism_group) is isomorphic to *O*. The full [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) is isomorphic to *O* × **Z**<sub>2</sub>.

### Presentation {#Presentation}

The group 2*O* has a [presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) given by

$$\langle r,s,t \mid r^2 = s^3 = t^4 = rst \rangle$$

or equivalently,

$$\langle s,t \mid (st)^2 = s^3 = t^4 \rangle.$$

Quaternion generators with these relations are given by

$$r = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(i+j) \qquad s = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+i+j+k) \qquad t = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}(1+i),$$

with $r^2 = s^3 = t^4 = rst = -1.$

### Subgroups {#Subgroups}

*Image: Binary octahedral group subgroups.png.* The *binary octahedral group* 2*O*=⟨2,3,4⟩ order 48, has 3 primary subgroups: • 2*T*=⟨2,3,3⟩, index 2, • *Q*16=⟨2,2,4⟩ index 3, and • *Q*12=⟨2,2,3⟩ index 4. • ⟨*l*,*m*,*n*⟩=[binary polyhedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_polyhedral_group) • ⟨*p*⟩≃Z<sub>2*p*</sub>, (*p*)≃Z<sub>*p*</sub> ([cyclic groups](wikipedia://en/52327))

The [binary tetrahedral group](wikipedia://en/10997586), 2*T*, consisting of the 24 [Hurwitz units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_unit), forms a normal subgroup of index 2. The [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130), *Q*8, consisting of the 8 [Lipschitz units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_unit) forms a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of 2*O* of [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29) 6. The [quotient group](wikipedia://en/11526) is isomorphic to *S*<sub>3</sub> (the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on 3 letters). These two groups, together with the center {±1}, are the only nontrivial normal subgroups of 2*O*.

The [generalized quaternion group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_quaternion_group), *Q*16, also forms a subgroup of 2*O*, index 3. This subgroup is [self-normalizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-normalizing) so its [conjugacy class](wikipedia://en/49176) has 3 members. There are also isomorphic copies of the [binary dihedral groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_dihedral_group) *Q*8 and *Q*12 in 2*O*.

All other subgroups are [cyclic groups](wikipedia://en/52327) generated by the various elements (with orders 3, 4, 6, and 8).[^o9]

:::

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

The *binary octahedral group* generalizes to higher dimensions: just as the [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635) generalizes to the [orthoplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex), the [octahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedral_group) in SO(3) generalizes to the [hyperoctahedral group](wikipedia://en/24268961) in SO(*n*), which has a binary cover under the map $\operatorname{Spin}(n) \to SO(n).$
