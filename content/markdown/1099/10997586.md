# Binary tetrahedral group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Binary tetrahedral group"}

*Image: Complex polygon 3-6-2.svg.* The regular [complex polytope](wikipedia://en/8581934), 3{6}2 or or , represents the [Cayley diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley_diagram) for the binary tetrahedral group, with each red and blue triangle a directed subgraph.[^o2]

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **binary tetrahedral group**, denoted 2T or $\langle 2,3,3\rangle$,[^o3] is a certain [nonabelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonabelian_group) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 24. It is an [extension](wikipedia://en/345807) of the [tetrahedral group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrahedral_group) T or (2,3,3) of order 12 by a [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 2, and is the [preimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preimage) of the tetrahedral group under the 2:1 [covering homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covering_homomorphism) Spin(3) → SO(3) of the [special orthogonal group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_orthogonal_group) by the [spin group](wikipedia://en/411231). It follows that the binary tetrahedral group is a [discrete subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_subgroup) of Spin(3) of order 24. The [complex reflection group](wikipedia://en/5381096) named 3(24)3 by [G.C. Shephard](wikipedia://en/33354532) or 3\[3\]3 and by [Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter), is isomorphic to the binary tetrahedral group.

:::

The binary tetrahedral group is most easily described concretely as a discrete subgroup of the [unit quaternions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_quaternion), under the isomorphism Spin(3) ≅ Sp(1), where [Sp(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sp%281%29) is the multiplicative group of unit quaternions. (For a description of this homomorphism see the article on [quaternions and spatial rotations](wikipedia://en/186057).)

Explicitly, the binary tetrahedral group is given as the [group of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_units) in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) of [Hurwitz integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_integer). There are 24 such units given by

$$\{\pm 1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k,\tfrac{1}{2}(\pm 1 \pm i \pm j \pm k)\}$$

with all possible sign combinations.

All 24 units have absolute value 1 and therefore lie in the unit quaternion group Sp(1). The [convex hull](wikipedia://en/40634) of these 24 elements in 4-dimensional space form a [convex regular 4-polytope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_regular_4-polytope) called the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485).

## Properties {#Properties}

The binary tetrahedral group, denoted by 2T, fits into the [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

$$1\to\{\pm 1\}\to 2\mathrm{T}\to \mathrm{T} \to 1.$$

This sequence does not [split](wikipedia://en/1531404), meaning that 2T is *not* a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246) of {±1} by T. In fact, there is no subgroup of 2T isomorphic to T.

The binary tetrahedral group is the [covering group](wikipedia://en/25118486) of the tetrahedral group. Thinking of the tetrahedral group as the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) on four letters, T ≅ A<sub>4</sub>, we thus have the binary tetrahedral group as the covering group, 2T ≅ $\widehat{\mathrm{A}_4}$.

The [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_a_group) of 2T is the subgroup {±1}. The [inner automorphism group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_automorphism_group) is isomorphic to A<sub>4</sub>, and the full [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) is isomorphic to S<sub>4</sub>.[^o8]

*Image: Versor action on Hurwitz quaternions.svg.* Left multiplication by −*ω*, an [order](wikipedia://en/493527)-6 element: look at gray, blue, purple, and orange balls and arrows that constitute 4 [orbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) (two arrows are not depicted). *ω* itself is the bottommost ball: *ω* = (−*ω*)(−1) = (−*ω*)<sup>4</sup>

The binary tetrahedral group can be written as a [semidirect product](wikipedia://en/48246)

$$2\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{Q}\rtimes\mathrm{C}_3$$

where Q is the [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130) consisting of the 8 [Lipschitz units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_unit) and C<sub>3</sub> is the [cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) of order 3 generated by $\omega = -\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + i + j + k)$. The group C<sub>3</sub> acts on the normal subgroup Q by [conjugation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_theory%29). Conjugation by $\omega$ is the automorphism of Q that cyclically rotates $i$, $j$, and $k$.

One can show that the binary tetrahedral group is isomorphic to the [special linear group](wikipedia://en/144823) SL(2,3) – the group of all 2 × 2 matrices over the [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615) **F**<sub>3</sub> with unit determinant, with this isomorphism covering the isomorphism of the [projective special linear group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projective_special_linear_group) PSL(2,3) with the alternating group A<sub>4</sub>.

### Presentation {#Presentation}

The group 2T has a [presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) given by

$$\langle r,s,t \mid r^2 = s^3 = t^3 = rst \rangle$$

or equivalently,

$\langle s,t \mid (st)^2 = s^3 = t^3 \rangle.$[^o10]

Generators with these relations are given by

$$r = i \qquad s = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+i+j+k) \qquad t = \tfrac{1}{2}(1+i-j+k),$$

with $r^2 = s^3 = t^3 = -1$.

### Subgroups {#Subgroups}

*Image: Binary tetrahedral group subgroups.png.* The *binary tetrahedral group*, 2T=\<3,3,2>, has 2 primary subgroups: • [Quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130), Q=\<2,2,2>, index 3 • [Cyclic group](wikipedia://en/52327) Z6=\<3>, index 4.

The [quaternion group](wikipedia://en/153130#Propertie) consisting of the 8 [Lipschitz units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_unit) forms a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of 2T of [index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_%28group_theory%29) 3. This group and the center {±1} are the only nontrivial normal subgroups.

All other subgroups of 2T are [cyclic groups](wikipedia://en/52327) generated by the various elements, with orders 3, 4, and 6.[^o12]

## Higher dimensions {#Higher_dimensions}

Just as the tetrahedral group generalizes to the rotational symmetry group of the *n*-[simplex](wikipedia://en/39781) (as a subgroup of SO(*n*)), there is a corresponding higher binary group which is a 2-fold cover, coming from the cover Spin(*n*) → SO(*n*).

The rotational symmetry group of the *n*-simplex can be considered as the [alternating group](wikipedia://en/49726) on *n* + 1 points, A<sub>*n*+1</sub>, and the corresponding binary group is a 2-fold [covering group](wikipedia://en/25118486). For all higher dimensions except A<sub>6</sub> and A<sub>7</sub> (corresponding to the 5-dimensional and 6-dimensional simplexes), this binary group is the [covering group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_perfect_central_extension) (maximal cover) and is [superperfect](wikipedia://en/9915961), but for dimensional 5 and 6 there is an additional exceptional 3-fold cover, and the binary groups are not superperfect.

## Usage in theoretical physics {#Usage_in_theoretical_physics}

The binary tetrahedral group was used in the context of [Yang–Mills theory](wikipedia://en/672202) in 1956 by [Chen Ning Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Ning_Yang) and others.[^o15] It was first used in flavor physics model building by [Paul Frampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Frampton) and Thomas Kephart in 1994.[^o16] In 2012 it was shown[^o17] that a relation between two neutrino mixing angles, derived[^o18] by using this binary tetrahedral flavor symmetry, agrees with experiment.
