# Horton graph

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Horton graph"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Horton graph** or **Horton 96-graph** is a 3-[regular graph](wikipedia://en/85821) with 96 vertices and 144 edges discovered by Joseph Horton.[^o3] Published by Bondy and Murty in 1976, it provides a counterexample to the Tutte conjecture that every [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) 3-connected [bipartite graph](wikipedia://en/244431) is [Hamiltonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_graph).[^o4][^o5]

:::

After the Horton graph, a number of smaller counterexamples to the Tutte conjecture were found. Among them are a 92 vertex graph by Horton published in 1982, a 78 vertex graph by Owens published in 1983, and the two [Ellingham-Horton graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellingham-Horton_graph) (54 and 78 vertices).[^o6][^o7]

The first Ellingham-Horton graph was published by Ellingham in 1981 and was of order 78.[^o8] At that time, it was the smallest known counterexample to the Tutte conjecture. The second one was published by Ellingham and Horton in 1983 and was of order 54.[^o9] In 1989, Georges' graph, the smallest currently-known non-Hamiltonian 3-connected cubic bipartite graph was discovered, containing 50 vertices.[^o10]

As a non-Hamiltonian cubic graph with many long cycles, the Horton graph provides good benchmark for programs that search for Hamiltonian cycles.[^o11]

The Horton graph has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) 2, [chromatic index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_index) 3, radius 10, diameter 10, [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 6, [book thickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_thickness) 3[^o12] and [queue number](wikipedia://en/46183215) 2.[^o12] It is also a 3-[edge-connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph).

::: {#o14 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Algebraic properties"}

## Algebraic properties {#Algebraic_properties}

The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/15094186) of the Horton graph is of order 96 and is isomorphic to **Z**/2**Z**×**Z**/2**Z**×*S*<sub>4</sub>, the [direct product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_direct_product) of the [Klein four-group](wikipedia://en/41928) and the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) *S*<sub>4</sub>.

The [characteristic polynomial](wikipedia://en/218268) of the Horton graph is : $(x-3) (x-1)^{14} x^4 (x+1)^{14} (x+3) (x^2-5)^3 (x^2-3)^{11}(x^2-x-3) (x^2+x-3)$ $(x^{10}-23 x^8+188 x^6-644 x^4+803 x^2-101)^2$ $(x^{10}-20 x^8+143 x^6-437 x^4+500 x^2-59)$.

:::
