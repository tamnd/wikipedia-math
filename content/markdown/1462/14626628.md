# Polynomial Diophantine equation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Polynomial Diophantine equation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **polynomial Diophantine equation** is an [indeterminate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indeterminate_equation) [polynomial](wikipedia://en/23000) [equation](wikipedia://en/9284) for which one seeks solutions restricted to be [polynomials](wikipedia://en/23000) in the indeterminate. A [Diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109), in general, is one where the solutions are restricted to some algebraic system, typically integers. (In another usage ) *Diophantine* refers to the [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_civilization) mathematician of the 3rd century, [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), who made initial studies of integer Diophantine equations.

:::

An important type of polynomial Diophantine equations takes the form:

$$sa+tb=c$$

where *a*, *b*, and *c* are known polynomials, and we wish to solve for *s* and *t*.

A simple example (and a solution) is:

$$s(x^2+1)+t(x^3+1)=2x$$

$$s=-x^3-x^2+x$$

$$t=x^2+x.$$

A necessary and sufficient condition for a polynomial Diophantine equation to have a solution is for *c* to be a multiple of the [GCD](wikipedia://en/12354) of *a* and *b*. In the example above, the GCD of *a* and *b* was 1, so solutions would exist for any value of c.

Solutions to polynomial Diophantine equations are not unique. Any multiple of $ab$ (say $rab$) can be used to transform $s$ and $t$ into another solution $s'=s+rb$ $t'=t-ra$:

$$(s+rb)a+(t-ra)b=c.$$

Some polynomial Diophantine equations can be solved using the [extended Euclidean algorithm](wikipedia://en/99438), which works as well with polynomials as it does with integers.
