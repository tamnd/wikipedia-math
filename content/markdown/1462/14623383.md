# Henselian ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Henselian ring"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Henselian ring** (or **Hensel ring**) is a [local ring](wikipedia://en/153391) in which [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368) holds. They were introduced by (Azumaya 1951), who named them after [Kurt Hensel](wikipedia://en/2242565). Azumaya originally allowed Henselian rings to be [non-commutative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_ring), but most authors now restrict them to be [commutative](wikipedia://en/61346).

:::

Some standard references for Hensel rings are (Nagata 1975, Chapter VII), (Raynaud 1970), and (Grothendieck 1967, Chapter 18).

::: {#o7 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

In this article [rings](wikipedia://en/48404) will be assumed to be commutative, though there is also a theory of non-commutative Henselian rings.

- A local ring *R* with [maximal ideal](wikipedia://en/48164) *m* is called **Henselian** if Hensel's lemma holds. This means that if *P* is a [monic polynomial](wikipedia://en/314730) in *R*\[*x*\], then any factorization of its image *P* in (*R*/*m*)\[*x*\] into a product of [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime_polynomials) monic polynomials can be lifted to a factorization in *R*\[*x*\].
- A local ring is Henselian if and only if every finite ring extension is a [product](wikipedia://en/25063) of local rings.
- A Henselian local ring is called **strictly Henselian** if its [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680) is [separably closed](wikipedia://en/363340).
- By [abuse of terminology](wikipedia://en/1698066), a **[field](wikipedia://en/10603)** $K$ with [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) $v$ is said to be Henselian if its [valuation ring](wikipedia://en/966856) is Henselian. That is the case if and only if $v$ extends uniquely to every [finite extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) of $K$ (resp. to every finite separable extension of $K$, resp. to $K^{alg}$, resp. to $K^{sep}$).
- A ring is called Henselian if it is a direct product of a finite number of Henselian local rings.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

### Properties {#Properties}

- Assume that $(K, v)$ is a Henselian field. Then every [algebraic extension](wikipedia://en/2125) of $K$ is henselian (by the fourth definition above).
- If $(K, v)$ is a Henselian field and $\alpha$ is algebraic over $K$, then for every [conjugate](wikipedia://en/458467) $\alpha'$ of $\alpha$ over $K$, $v(\alpha') = v(\alpha)$. This follows from the fourth definition, and from the fact that for every K-automorphism $\sigma$ of $K^{alg}$, $v\circ \sigma$ is an extension of $v|_K$. The converse of this assertion also holds, because for a [normal field extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_field_extension) $L/K$, the extensions of $v$ to $L$ are known to be conjugated.[^o10]

## Henselian rings in algebraic geometry {#Henselian_rings_in_algebraic_geometry}

Henselian rings are the local rings with respect to the [Nisnevich topology](wikipedia://en/14627005) in the sense that if $R$ is a Henselian local ring, and $\{U_i \to X\}$ is a Nisnevich covering of $X = Spec(R)$, then one of the $U_i \to X$ is an isomorphism. This should be compared to the fact that for any [Zariski](wikipedia://en/241744) open covering $\{U_i \to X\}$ of the spectrum $X = Spec(R)$ of a local ring $R$, one of the $U_i \to X$ is an isomorphism. In fact, this property characterises Henselian rings, resp. local rings.

Likewise strict Henselian rings are the local rings of geometric points in the [étale topology](wikipedia://en/5707590).

::: {#o13 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Henselisation"}

## Henselization {#Henselization}

For any local ring *A* there is a universal Henselian ring *B* generated by *A*, called the **Henselization** of *A*, introduced by (Nagata 1953), such that any [local homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_homomorphism) from *A* to a Henselian ring can be extended uniquely to *B*. The Henselization of *A* is unique [up to](wikipedia://en/44787) unique [isomorphism](wikipedia://en/14828). The Henselization of *A* is an algebraic substitute for the [completion](wikipedia://en/9477975) of *A*. The Henselization of *A* has the same completion and residue field as *A* and is a [flat module](wikipedia://en/492616) over *A*. If *A* is [Noetherian](wikipedia://en/56129), [reduced](wikipedia://en/539749), normal, regular, or [excellent](wikipedia://en/10016360) then so is its Henselization. For example, the Henselization of the [ring of polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_polynomials) *k*\[*x*,*y*,...\] [localized](wikipedia://en/252216) at the point (0,0,...) is the ring of algebraic [formal power series](wikipedia://en/60012) (the formal [power series](wikipedia://en/59958) satisfying an algebraic equation). This can be thought of as the "algebraic" part of the completion.

Similarly there is a strictly Henselian ring generated by *A*, called the **strict Henselization** of *A*. The strict Henselization is not quite universal: it is unique, but only up to *non-unique* isomorphism. More precisely it depends on the choice of a separable algebraic closure of the residue field of *A*, and [automorphisms](wikipedia://en/1160) of this separable algebraic closure correspond to automorphisms of the corresponding strict Henselization. For example, a strict Henselization of the field of [*p*-adic numbers](wikipedia://en/51423) is given by the maximal unramified extension, generated by all [roots of unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roots_of_unity) of order prime to *p*. It is not "universal" as it has [non-trivial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_automorphism) automorphisms.

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every field is a Henselian local ring. (But not every field with valuation is "Henselian" in the sense of the fourth definition above.)
- Complete [Hausdorff](wikipedia://en/13637) local rings, such as the ring of [*p*-adic integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-adic_integer) and rings of formal power series over a field, are Henselian.
- The rings of convergent power series over the [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) are Henselian.
- Rings of algebraic power series over a field are Henselian.
- A local ring that is [integral](wikipedia://en/9478630) over a Henselian ring is Henselian.
- The Henselization of a local ring is a Henselian local ring.
- Every [quotient](wikipedia://en/184635) of a Henselian ring is Henselian.
- A ring *A* is Henselian if and only if the associated [reduced ring](wikipedia://en/539749) *A*<sub>red</sub> is Henselian (this is the quotient of *A* by the [ideal of nilpotent elements](wikipedia://en/289636)).
- If *A* has only one [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) then it is Henselian since *A*<sub>red</sub> is a field.
