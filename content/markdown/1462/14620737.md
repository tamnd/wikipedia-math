# Ostomachion

*Image: Ostomachion.svg.* Ostomachion (after Suter)

*Image: Stomachion.JPG.* Ostomachion (after Suter): square reformed with some pieces turned over

*Image: OstomachionFigures.jpg.* Ostomachion figures mentioned by Ausonius and others (Bibliotheca Augustana)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ostomachion"}

In [ancient Greek geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_geometry), the ***Ostomachion***, also known as **loculus Archimedius** (la) or ***syntomachion***, is a mathematical treatise attributed to [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844). This work has survived fragmentarily in an [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) version and a copy, the *[Archimedes Palimpsest](wikipedia://en/167417)*, of the original [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) text made in [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) times.[^o2]

:::

The word *Ostomachion* (Ὀστομάχιον)[^o3] comes el.[^o4][^o5] The manuscripts refer to the word as "**Stomachion**", an apparent corruption of the original Greek. [Ausonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausonius) gives us the correct name "Ostomachion" (quod Graeci ostomachion vocavere, "which the Greeks called ostomachion").

The *Ostomachion* which he describes was a puzzle similar to [tangrams](wikipedia://en/54419) and was played perhaps by several persons with pieces made of bone.[^o6] It is not known which is older, Archimedes' geometrical investigation of the figure, or the game. [Victorinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Marius_Victorinus),[^o7] [Bassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesius_Bassus),[^o8] [Ennodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Felix_Ennodius)[^o9] and [Lucretius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucretius)[^o10] have also discussed the game.

## Game {#Game}

The game is a 14-piece [dissection puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection_puzzle) forming a square. One form of play to which classical texts attest is the creation of different objects, animals, plants etc. by rearranging the pieces: an elephant, a tree, a barking dog, a ship, a sword, a tower etc. Another suggestion is that it exercised and developed memory skills in the young. James Gow, in his *Short History of Greek Mathematics* (1884), footnotes that the purpose was to put the pieces back in their box,[^o12] and this was also a view expressed by W. W. Rouse Ball in some intermediate editions of *Mathematical Essays and Recreations*.[^o13]

*Image: Archimedes' ostomachion, Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, Athens.jpg.* A reconstruction of Archimedes' ostomachion in Kotsanas Museum of Ancient Greek Technology, Athens

The number of different ways to arrange the parts of the Ostomachion within a square were determined to be 17,152 by [Fan Chung](wikipedia://en/246562), [Persi Diaconis](wikipedia://en/65467), [Susan P. Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_P._Holmes), and [Ronald Graham](wikipedia://en/240694), and confirmed by a computer search by William H. Cutler.[^o14] However, this count has been disputed because surviving images of the puzzle show it in a rectangle, not a square, and rotations or reflections of pieces may not have been allowed.[^o15]
