# Category of representations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Category of representations"}

In [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), the **category of representations** of some [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) $A$ has the representations of $A$ as [objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Object_%28category_theory%29) and [equivariant maps](wikipedia://en/19378200#Equivariant_maps_and_isomorphisms) as [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) between them. One of the basic thrusts of representation theory is to understand the conditions under which this [category](wikipedia://en/53759) is [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_category); i.e., whether an object decomposes into [simple objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_object) (see [Maschke's theorem](wikipedia://en/30876007) for the case of [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707)).

:::

The [Tannakian formalism](wikipedia://en/7221237) gives conditions under which a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G* may be recovered from the category of representations of it together with the [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) to the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces).[^o2]

The [Grothendieck ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck_ring) of the category of finite-dimensional representations of a group *G* is called the [representation ring](wikipedia://en/16673816) of *G*.

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

Depending on the types of representations one wants to consider, it is typical to use slightly different definitions.

For a finite group $G$ and a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) $F$, the **category of representations of $G$ over $F$** has

- **Objects:** [Pairs](wikipedia://en/132729) ($V$, $f$) of [vector spaces](wikipedia://en/32370) $V$ over $F$ and [representations](wikipedia://en/12695) $f$ of $G$ on that vector space
- **Morphisms:** Equivariant maps
- **Composition:** The [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of equivariant maps
- **Identities:** The [identity function](wikipedia://en/15069) (which is an equivariant map).

The category is denoted by $\operatorname{Rep}_F(G)$ or $\operatorname{Rep}(G)$.

For a [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945), one typically requires the representations to be [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_representation) or [admissible](wikipedia://en/11055227). For the case of a [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), see [Lie algebra representation](wikipedia://en/318712). See also: [category O](wikipedia://en/32961781).

### The category of modules over the group ring {#The_category_of_modules_over_the_group_ring}

There is an [isomorphism of categories](wikipedia://en/489935) between the category of representations of a group $G$ over a field $F$ (described above) and the [category of modules](wikipedia://en/11231812) over the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) $F$\[$G$\], denoted **$F$\[$G$\]-Mod**.

### Category-theoretic definition {#Category-theoretic_definition}

Every group $G$ can be viewed as a category with a single object, where [morphisms](wikipedia://en/27716891) in this category are the elements of $G$ and composition is given by the group operation; so $G$ is the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) of the unique object. Given an arbitrary category **$C$**, a *representation* of $G$ in **$C$** is a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) from $G$ to **$C$**. Such a functor sends the unique object to an object say *$X$* in **$C$** and induces a [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) $G \to \operatorname{Aut}(X)$; see [Automorphism group#In category theory](wikipedia://en/56661772#In_category_theory) for more. For example, a [$G$-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) is equivalent to a functor from $G$ to **Set**, the [category of sets](wikipedia://en/26829), and a linear representation is equivalent to a functor to **Vect**<sub>$F$</sub>, the [category of vector spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_of_vector_spaces) over a field $F$.[^o7]

In this setting, the **category of linear representations of $G$ over $F$** is the functor category $G$ → **Vect**<sub>$F$</sub>, which has [natural transformations](wikipedia://en/45022) as its morphisms.

## Properties {#Properties}

The category of linear representations of a group has a [monoidal structure](wikipedia://en/330604) given by the [tensor product of representations](wikipedia://en/43079145), which is an important ingredient in Tannaka-Krein duality (see below).

[Maschke's theorem](wikipedia://en/30876007) states that when the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of $F$ doesn't divide the [order](wikipedia://en/493527) of $G$, the category of representations of $G$ over $F$ is [semisimple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semisimple_category).

:::

## Restriction and induction {#Restriction_and_induction}

Given a group $G$ with a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) $H$, there are two fundamental functors between the categories of representations of $G$ and $H$ (over a fixed field): one is a [forgetful functor](wikipedia://en/844783) called the [restriction functor](wikipedia://en/880754)

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Res}_H^G : \operatorname{Rep}(G) &\longrightarrow \operatorname{Rep}(H) \\ \pi &\longmapsto \pi|_H \end{aligned}$$

and the other, the [induction functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induction_functor)

$$\operatorname{Ind}_H^G : \operatorname{Rep}(H) \to \operatorname{Rep}(G).$$

When $G$ and $H$ are finite groups, they are [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_functor) to each other

$$\operatorname{Hom}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W, U) \cong \operatorname{Hom}_H(W, \operatorname{Res}_H^G U),$$

a theorem called [Frobenius reciprocity](wikipedia://en/55690071).

The basic question is whether the decomposition into irreducible representations (simple objects of the category) behaves under restriction or induction. The question may be attacked for instance by the [Mackey theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackey_theory).

## Tannaka-Krein duality {#Tannaka-Krein_duality}

**Tannaka–Krein duality** concerns the interaction of a [compact](wikipedia://en/750326) [topological group](wikipedia://en/42315) and its category of [linear representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_representation). Tannaka's theorem describes the converse passage from the category of finite-dimensional representations of a group $G$ back to the group $G$, allowing one to recover the group from its category of representations. Krein's theorem in effect completely characterizes all categories that can arise from a group in this fashion. These concepts can be applied to representations of several different structures, see the main article for details.
