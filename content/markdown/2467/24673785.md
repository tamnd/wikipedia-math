# Schreier coset graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Schreier coset graph"}

In the area of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) called [combinatorial group theory](wikipedia://en/979834), the **Schreier coset graph** is a [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) associated with a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) *G*, a [generating set](wikipedia://en/99945) of *G*, and a [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G*. The Schreier graph encodes the abstract structure of the group modulo an [equivalence relation](wikipedia://en/9259) formed by the [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) of the subgroup.

:::

The graph is named after [Otto Schreier](wikipedia://en/4355201), who used the term "Nebengruppenbild".[^o2] An equivalent definition [was made](wikipedia://en/894779) in an early paper of Todd and Coxeter.[^o3]

## Description {#Description}

Given a group *G*, a subgroup *H* ≤ *G*, and a generating set *S* = {*s*<sub>*i*</sub>: *i* in *I*} of *G*, the Schreier graph Sch(*G*, *H*, *S*) is a graph whose [vertices](wikipedia://en/638899) are the right [cosets](wikipedia://en/98759) *Hg* = {*hg*: *h* in *H*} for *g* in *G* and whose [edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_%28graph_theory%29) are of the form (*Hg*, *Hgs*) for *g* in *G* and *s* in *S*.

More generally, if *X* is any [*G*-set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-set), one can define a Schreier graph Sch(*G*, *X*, *S*) of the [action](wikipedia://en/12781) of *G* on *X* (with respect to the generating set *S*): its vertices are the elements of *X*, and its edges are of the form (*x*, *xs*) for *x* in *X* and *s* in *S*. This includes the original Schreier coset graph definition, as *H*\\*G* is a naturally a *G*-set with respect to multiplication from the right. From an [algebraic-topological](wikipedia://en/38801) perspective, the graph Sch(*G*, *X*, *S*) has no distinguished vertex, whereas Sch(*G*, *H*, *S*) has the distinguished vertex *H*, and is thus a [pointed graph](wikipedia://en/1236666).

The [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of the group *G* itself is the Schreier coset graph for *H* = {1<sub>*G*</sub>}.[^o5]

A [spanning tree](wikipedia://en/455770) of a Schreier coset graph corresponds to a Schreier transversal, as in [Schreier's subgroup lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schreier's_subgroup_lemma).[^o6]

The book "Categories and Groupoids" listed below relates this to the theory of covering morphisms of [groupoids](wikipedia://en/12543). A subgroup *H* of a group *G* determines a covering morphism of groupoids $p: K \rightarrow G$ and if *S* is a generating set for *G* then its [inverse image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_image) under *p* is the Schreier graph of (*G*, *S*).

::: {#o8 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

The graph is useful to understand [coset enumeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coset_enumeration) and the [Todd–Coxeter algorithm](wikipedia://en/894779).

Coset graphs can be used to form large [permutation representations](wikipedia://en/59487764) of groups and were used by [Graham Higman](wikipedia://en/2600152) to show that the [alternating groups](wikipedia://en/49726) of large enough degree are [Hurwitz groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurwitz_group).[^o6]

[Stallings](wikipedia://en/18729795)' core graphs[^o9] are [retracts](wikipedia://en/2120001) of Schreier graphs of free groups, and are an essential tool for computing with subgroups of a free group.

Every [vertex-transitive graph](wikipedia://en/581175) is a coset graph.
