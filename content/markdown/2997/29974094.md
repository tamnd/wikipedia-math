# Spherical sector

*Image: Kugel-sektor.png.* A spherical sector (blue)

*Image: Spherical sector.png.* A spherical sector

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Spherical sector"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **spherical sector**, also known as a **spherical cone**, is a portion of a [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) that is bounded by a [spherical cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap) and the [cone](wikipedia://en/782427) that connects the centre of the sphere to the boundary of the cap. It is the three-dimensional analogue of the [sector](wikipedia://en/1967249) of a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220).

:::

## Volume {#Volume}

If the radius of the sphere is denoted by $r$ and the height of the cap by $h$, the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of the spherical sector is

$$V = \frac{2\pi r^2 h}{3}\,.$$

This may also be written as

$$V = \frac{2\pi r^3}{3} (1-\cos\varphi)\,,$$

where $\varphi$ is half the [cone aperture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_aperture) angle, i.e., $\varphi$ is the angle between the rim of the cap and the axis direction to the middle of the cap as seen from the sphere center. The limiting case is for $\varphi$ approaching 180 degrees, which then describes a complete sphere.

The height, $h$ is given by

$$h = r (1-\cos\varphi)\,.$$

The volume $V$ of the sector is related to the area $A$ of the cap by:

$$V = \frac{rA}{3}\,.$$

## Area {#Area}

The curved [surface area](wikipedia://en/27725) of the spherical cap (on the surface of the sphere, excluding the cone surface) is

$$A = 2\pi rh\,.$$

It is also

$$A = \Omega r^2$$

where $\Omega$ is the [solid angle](wikipedia://en/94102) of the spherical sector in [steradians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steradian), the SI unit of solid angle. One steradian is defined as the solid angle subtended by a cap area of $A = r^{2}$.

## Derivation {#Derivation}

The volume can be calculated by integrating the [differential volume element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_volume_element)

$$dV = \rho^2 \sin \phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

over the volume of the spherical sector,

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\varphi\int_0^r\rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^\varphi \sin\phi \, d\phi \int_0^r \rho^2 d\rho = \frac{2\pi r^3}{3} (1-\cos\varphi) \, ,$$

where the integrals have been separated, because the integrand can be separated into a product of functions each with one dummy variable.

The area can be similarly calculated by integrating the differential spherical area element

$$dA = r^2 \sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta$$

over the spherical sector, giving

$$A = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\varphi r^2 \sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta = r^2 \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^\varphi \sin\phi \, d\phi = 2\pi r^2(1-\cos\varphi) \, ,$$

where $\varphi$ is inclination (or elevation) and $\theta$ is [azimuth](wikipedia://en/47487) (right). Notice $r$ is a constant. Again, the integrals can be separated.
