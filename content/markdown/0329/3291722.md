# Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket** is an extension of the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799) of [vector fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_fields) to [vector-valued differential forms](wikipedia://en/3772116) on a [differentiable manifold](wikipedia://en/2119219).

:::

It is useful in the study of [connections](wikipedia://en/308815), notably the [Ehresmann connection](wikipedia://en/5731754), as well as in the more general study of [projections](wikipedia://en/519182) in the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794). It was introduced by [Alfred Frölicher](wikipedia://en/35026800) and [Albert Nijenhuis](wikipedia://en/11222925) (1956) and is related to the work of [Schouten](wikipedia://en/2862062) (1940).

It is related to but not the same as the [Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket](wikipedia://en/3291647) and the [Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/11216402).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let Ω\*(*M*) be the [sheaf](wikipedia://en/245466) of [exterior algebras](wikipedia://en/221537) of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) on a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M*. This is a [graded algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_algebra) in which forms are graded by degree:

$$\Omega^*(M) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^\infty \Omega^k(M).$$

A [graded derivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graded_derivation) of degree ℓ is a mapping

$$D:\Omega^*(M)\to\Omega^{*+l}(M)$$

which is linear with respect to constants and satisfies

$$D(\alpha\wedge\beta) = D(\alpha)\wedge\beta + (-1)^{\ell\deg(\alpha)}\alpha\wedge D(\beta).$$

Thus, in particular, the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963) with a vector defines a graded derivation of degree ℓ = −1, whereas the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782) is a graded derivation of degree ℓ = 1.

The vector space of all derivations of degree ℓ is denoted by Der<sub>ℓ</sub>Ω\*(*M*). The direct sum of these spaces is a [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734) whose homogeneous components consist of all graded derivations of a given degree; it is denoted

$$\mathrm{Der}\, \Omega^*(M) = \bigoplus_{k=-\infty}^\infty \mathrm{Der}_k\, \Omega^*(M).$$

This forms a [graded Lie superalgebra](wikipedia://en/5475402#graded_Lie_superalgebras) under the anticommutator of derivations defined on homogeneous derivations *D*<sub>1</sub> and *D*<sub>2</sub> of degrees *d*<sub>1</sub> and *d*<sub>2</sub>, respectively, by

$$[D_1,D_2] = D_1\circ D_2 - (-1)^{d_1d_2}D_2\circ D_1.$$

Any [vector-valued differential form](wikipedia://en/3772116) *K* in Ω<sup>*k*</sup>(*M*, T*M*) with values in the [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) of *M* defines a graded derivation of degree *k* − 1, denoted by *i*<sub>*K*</sub>, and called the insertion operator. For ω ∈ Ω<sup>ℓ</sup>(*M*),

$$i_K\,\omega(X_1,\dots,X_{k+\ell-1})=\frac{1}{k!(\ell-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in{S}_{k+\ell-1}}\textrm{sign}\,\sigma \cdot \omega(K(X_{\sigma(1)},\dots,X_{\sigma(k)}),X_{\sigma(k+1)},\dots,X_{\sigma(k+\ell-1)})$$

The [Nijenhuis–Lie derivative](wikipedia://en/294437#Nijenhuis–Lie_derivative) along *K* ∈ Ω<sup>k</sup>(*M*, T*M*) is defined by

$$\mathcal{L}_K = [i_K, d] =i_K\,{\circ}\,d-(-1)^{k-1} d\,{\circ}\, i_K$$

where *d* is the exterior derivative and *i*<sub>K</sub> is the insertion operator.

The Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket is defined to be the unique vector-valued differential form

$$[\cdot, \cdot]_{FN} : \Omega^k(M,\mathrm{T}M) \times \Omega^\ell(M,\mathrm{T}M) \to \Omega^{k+\ell}(M,\mathrm{T}M) : (K, L) \mapsto [K, L]_{FN}$$

such that

$$\mathcal{L}_{[K, L]_{FN}} = [\mathcal{L}_K, \mathcal{L}_L].$$

Hence,

$$[K, L]_{FN}=-(-1)^{kl}[L,K]_{FN}.$$

If *k* = 0, so that *K* ∈ Ω<sup>0</sup>(*M*, T*M*) is a vector field, the usual homotopy formula for the Lie derivative is recovered

$$\mathcal{L}_K = [i_K,d] =i_K \,{\circ}\, d+d \,{\circ}\, i_K.$$

If *k*=*ℓ*=1, so that *K,L* ∈ Ω<sup>1</sup>(*M*, T*M*), one has for any vector fields *X* and *Y*

$$[K, L]_{FN}(X,Y) = [KX, LY]+[LX, KY]+(KL+LK)[X,Y]-K([LX,Y]+[X, LY])-L([KX,Y]+[X, KY]).$$

If *k*=0 and *ℓ*=1, so that *K=Z*∈ Ω<sup>0</sup>(*M*, T*M*) is a vector field and *L* ∈ Ω<sup>1</sup>(*M*, T*M*), one has for any vector field *X*

$$[Z, L]_{FN}(X) = [Z, LX]-L[Z,X].$$

An explicit formula for the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket of $\phi\otimes X$ and $\psi\otimes Y$ (for forms φ and ψ and vector fields *X* and *Y*) is given by

$$\left.\right.[\phi \otimes X,\psi \otimes Y]_{FN} = \phi\wedge\psi\otimes [X,Y] + \phi\wedge\mathcal{L}_X \psi\otimes Y - \mathcal{L}_Y \phi\wedge\psi \otimes X +(-1)^{\deg(\phi)}(d\phi \wedge i_X(\psi)\otimes Y +i_Y(\phi) \wedge d\psi \otimes X).$$

:::

## Derivations of the ring of forms {#Derivations_of_the_ring_of_forms}

Every derivation of Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*) can be written as

$$i_L + \mathcal{L}_K$$

for unique elements *K* and *L* of Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*, T*M*). The Lie bracket of these derivations is given as follows.

- The derivations of the form $\mathcal{L}_K$ form the Lie superalgebra of all derivations commuting with *d*. The bracket is given by

$$[\mathcal{L}_{K_1},\mathcal{L}_{K_2}]= \mathcal{L}_{[K_1,K_2]}$$

where the bracket on the right is the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket. In particular the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket defines a [graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) structure on $\Omega(M,\mathrm{T}M)$, which extends the [Lie bracket](wikipedia://en/10282799) of [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641).

- The derivations of the form $i_L$ form the Lie superalgebra of all derivations vanishing on functions Ω<sup>0</sup>(*M*). The bracket is given by

$$[i_{L_1},i_{L_2}]= i_{[L_1,L_2]^\land}$$

where the bracket on the right is the [Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket](wikipedia://en/3291647).

- The bracket of derivations of different types is given by

$$[\mathcal{L}_{K}, i_L]= i_{[K,L]} - (-1)^{kl}\mathcal{L}_{i_LK}$$

for *K* in Ω<sup>k</sup>(*M*, T*M*), *L* in Ω<sup>l+1</sup>(*M*, T*M*).

## Applications {#Applications}

The [Nijenhuis tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijenhuis_tensor) of an [almost complex structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_complex_structure) *J*, is half the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket of *J* with itself. An almost complex structure is a complex structure if and only if the Nijenhuis tensor is zero.

With the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket it is possible to define the [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) and [cocurvature](wikipedia://en/3772062) of a vector-valued 1-form which is a [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097). This generalizes the concept of the curvature of a [connection](wikipedia://en/308815).

There is a common generalization of the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket and the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket; for details see the article on the [Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/11216402).
