# Adam Logan

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Adam Logan"}

**Adam Logan** is a Canadian-American mathematician and tournament *[Scrabble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrabble)* player. His work is primarily in [arithmetic geometry](wikipedia://en/1973177) and [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997). In *Scrabble*, he has won the [World *Scrabble* Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scrabble_Championship) in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scrabble_Championship_2005) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scrabble_Championship_2025).

:::

## Early life, education, and personal life {#Early_life,_education,_and_personal_life}

Logan is the son of George Logan and Michal Ben-Gera, who both described Logan as mathematically gifted as a child. He attended the [Lisgar Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisgar_Collegiate_Institute).[^o4] He was a [Putnam Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow) in 1992 and 1993.[^o5] He completed his first degree, in mathematics, at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1995, graduating at the top of his class and received a PhD from [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) in 1999.[^o4][^o7][^o12]

## *Scrabble* {#Scrabble}

Logan competed in his first *Scrabble* tournament in 1985, at age 10, in which he placed fourth; he was only entered into the tournament due to an odd player count. He first played using the wordlist of the *[Official Scrabble Players Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Scrabble_Players_Dictionary)*, first playing with the [Collins *Scrabble* Words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_Scrabble_Words) in the early 2000s, while working in England.[^o4]

Logan is one of three players who have won the [World *Scrabble* Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Scrabble_Championship) more than once.[^o7] In 2005, he won the championship against [Pakorn Nemitrmansuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakorn_Nemitrmansuk) of [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand), 3–0 in the final,[^o8] and in 2025, won against [Nigel Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Richards) of [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand), 4–2 in the final.[^o9]

Since his competitive career began in 1985, Logan has played nearly 2700 tournament games, compiling a winning percentage of 69.5%, and earning around \$103,000 in prize money.[^o10]

## Mathematics {#Mathematics}

He completed his postdoctoral work at [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University) between 2002 through 2003.[^o7][^o12] From 2003 to 2005, he worked in England.[^o4] From 2008 to 2009, he was employed as a Quantitative Analyst at [D. E. Shaw & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._E._Shaw_&_Co.) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). He also worked for the [Tutte Institute for Mathematics and Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte_Institute_for_Mathematics_and_Computing) in [Ottawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario), Canada.[^o12][^o7]
