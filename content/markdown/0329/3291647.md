# Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **algebraic bracket** or **Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket** is a [graded Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/5475402) structure on the space of [alternating multilinear forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_product) of a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) to itself, introduced by [A. Nijenhuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._Nijenhuis) and [R. W. Richardson, Jr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._W._Richardson,_Jr) (1966, 1967). It is related to but not the same as the [Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/3291722) and the [Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/11216402).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The primary motivation for introducing the bracket was to develop a uniform framework for discussing all possible [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) structures on a vector space, and subsequently the [deformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_theory) of these structures. If *V* is a vector space and *p* ≥ −1 is an integer, let

$$\operatorname{Alt}^p(V) = ({\bigwedge}^{p+1} V^*)\otimes V$$

be the space of all skew-symmetric (*p* + 1)-multilinear mappings of *V* to itself. The direct sum Alt(*V*) is a [graded vector space](wikipedia://en/1155734). A **Lie algebra** structure on *V* is determined by a skew-symmetric bilinear map *μ* : *V* × *V* → *V*. That is to say, *μ* is an element of Alt<sup>1</sup>(*V*). Furthermore, *μ* must obey the [Jacobi identity](wikipedia://en/294370). The Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket supplies a systematic manner for expressing this identity in the form \[*μ*, *μ*\] = 0.

In detail, the bracket is a bilinear bracket operation defined on Alt(*V*) as follows. On homogeneous elements *P* ∈ Alt<sup>*p*</sup>(*V*) and *Q* ∈ Alt<sup>*q*</sup>(*V*), the Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket \[*P*, *Q*\]<sup>∧</sup> ∈ Alt<sup>*p*+*q*</sup>(*V*) is given by

$$[P,Q]^\land = i_P Q - (-1)^{pq}i_Q P .$$

Here the [interior product](wikipedia://en/2154963) *i*<sub>*P*</sub> is defined by

$$(i_P Q)(X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_{p+q}) = \sum_{\sigma\in \text{Sh}_{q+1,p}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma) P(Q(X_{\sigma(0)},X_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,X_{\sigma(q)}),X_{{\sigma(q+1)}},\ldots,X_{{\sigma(p+q)}})$$

where $\text{Sh}_{q+1,p}$ denotes [(*q+1*, *p*)-shuffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%28p,q%29_shuffle) of the indices, i.e. permutations $\sigma$ of $\{0,\ldots,p+q\}$ such that $\sigma(0) < \cdots < \sigma(q)$ and $\sigma(q+1) < \cdots < \sigma(p+q)$.

On non-homogeneous elements, the bracket is extended by bilinearity.

:::

## Derivations of the ring of forms {#Derivations_of_the_ring_of_forms}

The Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket can be defined on the vector valued forms Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*, *T*(*M*)) on a smooth manifold *M* in a similar way. Vector valued forms act as derivations on the supercommutative ring Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*) of forms on *M* by taking *K* to the derivation *i<sub>K</sub>*, and the Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket then corresponds to the commutator of two derivations. This identifies Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*, *T*(*M*)) with the algebra of derivations that vanish on smooth functions. Not all derivations are of this form; for the structure of the full ring of all derivations see the article [Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket](wikipedia://en/3291722).

The Nijenhuis–Richardson bracket and the Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket both make Ω<sup>\*</sup>(*M*, *T*(*M*)) into a graded superalgebra, but have different degrees.
