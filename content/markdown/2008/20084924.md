# Pseudoreflection

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pseudoreflection"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **pseudoreflection** is an invertible [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of a finite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) such that it is not the [identity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_transformation), has a finite (multiplicative) [order](wikipedia://en/493527), and fixes a [hyperplane](wikipedia://en/99862). The concept of pseudoreflection generalizes the concepts of [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829) and [complex reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_reflection) and is simply called **reflection** by some mathematicians. It plays an important role in [Invariant theory of finite groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariant_theory_of_finite_groups), including the [Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevalley-Shephard-Todd_theorem).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Suppose that *V* is [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) over a field *K*, whose [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398) is a finite number *n*. A **pseudoreflection** is an invertible [linear transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) $g: V\to V$ such that the order of *g* is finite and the **fixed subspace** $V^g = \{ v\in V:\ gv=v \}$ of all vectors in *V* fixed by *g* has dimension *n-1*.

:::

## Eigenvalues {#Eigenvalues}

A pseudoreflection *g* has an eigenvalue 1 of multiplicity *n-1* and another eigenvalue *r* of multiplicity 1. Since *g* has finite order, the eigenvalue *r* must be a [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950) in the field *K*. It is possible that *r* = 1 (see Transvections).

## Diagonalizable pseudoreflections {#Diagonalizable_pseudoreflections}

Let *p* be the [characteristic](wikipedia://en/1066621) of the field *K*. If the order of *g* is [coprime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprime) to *p* then *g* is [diagonalizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonalizable) and represented by a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080)

diag(1, ... , 1, *r* ) = $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & r \\ \end{bmatrix}$

where *r* is a root of unity not equal to 1. This includes the case when *K* is a field of characteristic zero, such as the field of real numbers and the field of [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826).

A diagonalizable pseudoreflection is sometimes called a **semisimple reflection**.

## Real reflections {#Real_reflections}

When *K* is the field of real numbers, a pseudoreflection has matrix form diag(1, ... , 1, -1). A pseudoreflection with such matrix form is called a **real reflection**. If the space on which this transformation acts admits a [symmetric bilinear form](wikipedia://en/1803375) so that [orthogonality](wikipedia://en/102221) of vectors can be defined, then the transformation is a true [reflection](wikipedia://en/295829).

## Complex reflections {#Complex_reflections}

When *K* is the field of complex numbers, a pseudoreflection is called a [complex reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_reflection), which can be represented by a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080) diag(1, ... , 1, r) where r is a complex root of unity unequal to 1.

## Transvections {#Transvections}

If the pseudoreflection *g* is not diagonalizable then *r* = 1 and *g* has [Jordan normal form](wikipedia://en/312877)

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 \\ \end{bmatrix}$$

In such case *g* is called a **transvection**. A pseudoreflection *g* is a transvection if and only if the characteristic *p* of the field *K* is positive and the order of *g* is *p*. Transvections are useful in the study of finite geometries and the classification of their groups of motions. [^o10]
