# Uniform boundedness

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Uniform boundedness"}

the result in functional analysis In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **uniformly bounded** [family](wikipedia://en/5726601) of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) is a family of [bounded functions](wikipedia://en/311509) that can all be bounded by the same constant. This constant is larger than or equal to the [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) of any value of any of the functions in the family.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

### Real line and complex plane {#Real_line_and_complex_plane}

Let

$$\mathcal F=\{f_i: X \to \mathbb{K}, i \in I\}$$

be a family of functions [indexed](wikipedia://en/393055) by $I$, where $X$ is an arbitrary set and $\mathbb{K}$ is either the set of [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) $\mathbb{R}$ or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) $\mathbb{C}$. We call $\mathcal F$ **uniformly bounded** if there exists a real number $M>0$ such that

$$|f_i(x)|\le M \ , \qquad \forall i \in I \ , \quad \forall x \in X.$$

Another way of stating this would be the following:

$$\exists M>0: \quad \sup\limits_{i \in I} \sup\limits_{x \in X} |f_i(x)|\le M.$$

### Metric space {#Metric_space}

In general let $Y$ be a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) with metric $d$, then the set

$$\mathcal F=\{f_i: X \to Y, i\in I\}$$

is called **uniformly bounded** if there exists an element $a\in Y$ and $M\in\mathbb{R}^+$ such that

$$d(f_i(x), a) \le M \qquad \forall i \in I \quad \forall x \in X.$$

Another way of stating this would be the following:

$$\exists a\in Y\,, \exists M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}: \quad \sup\limits_{i \in I} \sup\limits_{x \in X} d(f_i(x), a) \le M.$$

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Every [uniformly convergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_convergent) sequence of bounded functions is uniformly bounded.
- The family of functions $f_n(x)=\sin nx$ defined for [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) $x$ with $n$ traveling through the [integers](wikipedia://en/14563), is uniformly bounded by 1.
- The family of [derivatives](wikipedia://en/7921) of the above family, $f'_n(x)=n\, \cos nx,$ is *not* uniformly bounded. Each $f'_n$ is bounded by $|n|,$ but there is no real number $M$ such that $|n|\le M$ for all integers $n.$

:::
