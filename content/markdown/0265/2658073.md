# Hyperbolic set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperbolic set"}

In [dynamical systems theory](wikipedia://en/990632), a subset Λ of a [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold) *M* is said to be **hyperbolic** or to have a **hyperbolic structure** with respect to a [smooth map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_map) *f* if its [tangent bundle](wikipedia://en/211794) may be split into two invariant [subbundles](wikipedia://en/2517151), one of which is contracting and the other is expanding under *f*, with respect to some [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on *M*. An analogous definition applies to the case of [flows](wikipedia://en/2619735).

:::

In the special case when the entire manifold *M* is hyperbolic, the map *f* is called an [Anosov diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/2320078). The dynamics of *f* on a hyperbolic set, or **hyperbolic dynamics**, exhibits features of local [structural stability](wikipedia://en/2515807) and has been much studied, cf. [Axiom A](wikipedia://en/5471083).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let *M* be a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [smooth manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold), *f*: *M* → *M* a [diffeomorphism](wikipedia://en/8564), and *Df*: *TM* → *TM* the [differential](wikipedia://en/1558510) of *f*. An *f*-invariant subset Λ of *M* is said to be *hyperbolic*, or to have a *hyperbolic structure*, if the restriction to Λ of the tangent bundle of *M* admits a splitting into a [Whitney sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_sum) of two *Df*-invariant subbundles, called the [stable bundle](wikipedia://en/5466649) and the [unstable bundle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unstable_manifold) and denoted *E*<sup>*s*</sup> and *E*<sup>*u*</sup>. With respect to some [Riemannian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemannian_metric) on *M*, the restriction of *Df* to *E*<sup>*s*</sup> must be a contraction and the restriction of *Df* to *E*<sup>*u*</sup> must be an expansion. Thus, there exist constants 0\<*λ*\<1 and *c*>0 such that

$$T_\Lambda M = E^s\oplus E^u$$

and

$(Df)_x E^s_x = E^s_{f(x)}$ and $(Df)_x E^u_x = E^u_{f(x)}$ for all $x\in \Lambda$

and

$\|Df^nv\| \le c\lambda^n\|v\|$ for all $v\in E^s$ and $n> 0$

and

$\|Df^{-n}v\| \le c\lambda^{n} \|v\|$ for all $v\in E^u$ and $n>0$.

If Λ is hyperbolic then there exists a Riemannian metric for which *c* = 1 — such a metric is called **adapted**.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- [Hyperbolic equilibrium point](wikipedia://en/3948604) *p* is a [fixed point](wikipedia://en/449738), or equilibrium point, of *f*, such that (*Df*)<sub>*p*</sub> has no [eigenvalue](wikipedia://en/2161429) with [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) 1. In this case, Λ = {*p*}.
- More generally, a [periodic orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_orbit) of *f* with period *n* is hyperbolic if and only if *Df*<sup>*n*</sup> at any point of the orbit has no eigenvalue with absolute value 1, and it is enough to check this condition at a single point of the orbit.

:::
