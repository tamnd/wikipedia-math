# Raised cosine distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Raised cosine distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **raised cosine distribution** is a continuous [probability distribution](wikipedia://en/23543) [supported](wikipedia://en/381013) on the interval $[\mu-s,\mu+s]$. The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) (PDF) is

:::

$$\begin{aligned} f(x;\mu,s) &=\frac{1}{2s} \left[1+\cos\left(\frac{x-\mu}{s}\,\pi\right)\right]\\ &= \frac{1}{s}\operatorname{hvc}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{s}\,\pi\right) & \text{ for } \mu-s\le x\le\mu+s \end{aligned}$$

and zero otherwise. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) is

$$F(x;\mu,s)=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+\frac{x-\mu}{s} + \frac{1}{\pi} \sin\left(\frac{x-\mu}{s} \, \pi \right) \right]$$

for $\mu-s \le x \le \mu+s$ and zero for $x<\mu-s$ and unity for $x>\mu+s$.

The [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the raised cosine distribution are somewhat complicated in the general case, but are considerably simplified for the standard raised cosine distribution. The standard raised cosine distribution is just the raised cosine distribution with $\mu=0$ and $s=1$. Because the standard raised cosine distribution is an [even function](wikipedia://en/592151), the odd moments are zero. The even moments are given by:

$$\begin{aligned} \operatorname E(x^{2n}) & = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^1 [1+\cos(x\pi)]x^{2n}\,dx = \int_{-1}^1 x^{2n} \operatorname{hvc}(x\pi)\,dx \\[5pt] & = \frac{1}{n+1}+\frac{1}{1+2n}\,_1F_2 \left(n+\frac{1}{2}; \frac{1}{2}, n+\frac{3}{2}; \frac{-\pi^2}{4} \right) \end{aligned}$$

where $\,_1F_2$ is a [generalized hypergeometric function](wikipedia://en/361184).
