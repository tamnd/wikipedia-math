# Formation (group theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Formation"}

In [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **formation** is a [class of groups](wikipedia://en/48438158) closed under taking images and such that if *G*/*M* and *G*/*N* are in the formation then so is *G*/*M*∩*N*. (Gaschütz 1962) introduced formations to unify the theory of [Hall subgroups](wikipedia://en/1735335) and [Carter subgroups](wikipedia://en/5456815) of [finite](wikipedia://en/323707) [solvable groups](wikipedia://en/105012).

:::

Some examples of formations are the formation of [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) for a [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) *p*, the formation of π-groups for a set of primes π, and the formation of [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

A **Melnikov formation** is closed under taking [quotients](wikipedia://en/11526), [normal subgroups](wikipedia://en/21918) and [group extensions](wikipedia://en/345807). Thus a Melnikov formation *M* has the property that for every [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

$$1 \rightarrow A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow 1\$$

*A* and *C* are in *M* [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) *B* is in *M*.[^n1]

A **full formation** is a Melnikov formation which is also closed under taking [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011).[^n1]

An **almost full formation** is one which is closed under quotients, [direct products](wikipedia://en/3706015) and subgroups, but not necessarily extensions. The families of finite [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) and finite [nilpotent groups](wikipedia://en/144569) are almost full, but neither full nor Melnikov.[^n2]

:::

::: {#o9 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Schunck class"}

## Schunck classes {#Schunck_classes}

A Schunck class, introduced by (Schunck 1967), is a generalization of a formation, consisting of a class of groups such that a group is in the class if and only if every primitive factor group is in the class. Here a group is called primitive if it has a self-[centralizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralizer) normal abelian subgroup.[^n3]

:::
