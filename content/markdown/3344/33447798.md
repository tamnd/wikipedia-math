# Canberra distance

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Canberra distance"}

The **Canberra distance** is a numerical measure of the distance between pairs of points in a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370), introduced in 1966[^o2] and refined in 1967[^o3] by Godfrey N. Lance and [William T. Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._T._Williams). It is a weighted version of [*L*₁ (Manhattan) distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_distance).[^o4] The Canberra distance has been used as a metric for comparing [ranked lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_list)[^o4] and for [intrusion detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrusion_detection) in [computer security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_security).[^o5] It has also been used to analyze the [gut microbiome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_microbiome) in different disease states.[^o6]

:::

::: {#o8 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The Canberra distance *d* between vectors **p** and **q** in an *n*-dimensional [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) is given as follows:

$$d(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q}) = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{|p_i-q_i|}{|p_i|+|q_i|}$$

where

$$\mathbf{p}=(p_1,p_2,\dots,p_n)\text{ and }\mathbf{q}=(q_1,q_2,\dots,q_n)$$

are [vectors](wikipedia://en/32533).

The Canberra metric, Adkins form, divides the distance d by (n-Z) where Z is the number of attributes that are 0 for p and q.[^o3][^o9]
