# Covariance operator

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Covariance operator"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542), for a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) **P** on a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) *H* with [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) $\langle \cdot,\cdot\rangle$, the **covariance** of **P** is the [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) Cov: *H* × *H* → **R** given by

:::

$$\mathrm{Cov}(x, y) = \int_{H} \langle x, z \rangle \langle y, z \rangle \, \mathrm{d} \mathbf{P} (z)$$

for all *x* and *y* in *H*. The **covariance operator** *C* is then defined by

$$\mathrm{Cov}(x, y) = \langle Cx, y \rangle$$

(from the [Riesz representation theorem](wikipedia://en/26452), such operator exists if Cov is [bounded](wikipedia://en/449477#On_normed_vector_spaces)). Since Cov is symmetric in its arguments, the covariance operator is [self-adjoint](wikipedia://en/187408).

Even more generally, for a [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) **P** on a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *B*, the covariance of **P** is the [bilinear form](wikipedia://en/449477) on the [algebraic dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_dual) *B*<sup>\#</sup>, defined by

$$\mathrm{Cov}(x, y) = \int_{B} \langle x, z \rangle \langle y, z \rangle \, \mathrm{d} \mathbf{P} (z)$$

where $\langle x, z \rangle$ is now the value of the linear functional *x* on the element *z*.

Quite similarly, the [covariance function](wikipedia://en/9793263) of a function-valued [random element](wikipedia://en/5215393) (in special cases is called [random process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_process) or [random field](wikipedia://en/540838)) *z* is

$$\mathrm{Cov}(x, y) = \int z(x) z(y) \, \mathrm{d} \mathbf{P} (z) = E(z(x) z(y))$$

where *z*(*x*) is now the value of the function *z* at the point *x*, i.e., the value of the [linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_functional) $u \mapsto u(x)$ evaluated at *z*.
