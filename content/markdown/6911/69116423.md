# Reducing subspace

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Reducing subspace"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a **reducing subspace** $W$ of a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102) $T:V\to V$ from a [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932) $V$ to itself is an [invariant subspace](wikipedia://en/585826) of $T$ whose [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) $W^\perp$ is also an invariant subspace of $T.$ That is, $T(W) \subseteq W$ and $T(W^\perp) \subseteq W^\perp.$ One says that the [subspace](wikipedia://en/56357) $W$ **reduces** the map $T.$

:::

One says that a linear map is **reducible** if it has a [nontrivial](wikipedia://en/56357#Definition) reducing subspace. Otherwise one says it is **irreducible**.

If $V$ is of finite [dimension](wikipedia://en/38267) $r$ and $W$ is a reducing subspace of the map $T:V\to V$ represented under [basis](wikipedia://en/18420) $B$ by matrix $M \in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$ then $M$ can be expressed as the sum

$$M = P_W M P_W + P_{W^\perp} M P_{W^\perp}$$

where $P_W \in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$ is the matrix of the [orthogonal projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) from $V$ to $W$ and $P_{W^\perp} = I - P_{W}$ is the matrix of the projection onto $W^\perp.$[^o2] (Here $I \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$ is the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718).)

Furthermore, $V$ has an [orthonormal basis](wikipedia://en/195063) $B'$ with a subset that is an orthonormal basis of $W$. If $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$ is the [transition matrix](wikipedia://en/1179451) from $B$ to $B'$ then with respect to $B'$ the matrix $Q^{-1}MQ$ representing $T$ is a [block-diagonal matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block-diagonal_matrix)

$$Q^{-1}MQ = \left[ \begin{array}{cc} A & 0 \\ 0 & B \end{array} \right]$$

with $A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d},$ where $d= \dim W$, and $B\in\mathbb{R}^{(r-d)\times(r-d)}.$
