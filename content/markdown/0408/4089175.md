# Logical constant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Logical constant"}

In [logic](wikipedia://en/46426065), a **logical constant** or **constant symbol** of a [language](wikipedia://en/10939) $\mathcal{L}$ is a [symbol](wikipedia://en/24028642) that has the same [semantic value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formal_semantics_%28logic%29) under every [interpretation](wikipedia://en/14511671) of $\mathcal{L}$. Two important types of logical constants are [logical connectives](wikipedia://en/18153) and [quantifiers](wikipedia://en/43507260). The [equality](wikipedia://en/90446) predicate (usually written '=') is also treated as a logical constant in many [systems of logic](wikipedia://en/396102).

:::

One of the fundamental questions in the [philosophy of logic](wikipedia://en/21368888) is "What is a logical constant?";[^o2] that is, what special feature of certain constants makes them *logical* in nature?[^o3]

Some symbols that are commonly treated as logical constants are:

::: {#o4 .table cue=table}

| Symbol | Meaning in English |
|---|---|
| T | "true" |
| F, ⊥ | "[false](wikipedia://en/229823)" |
| ¬ | "[not](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_negation)" |
| ∧ | "[and](wikipedia://en/18152)" |
| ∨ | "[or](wikipedia://en/7962)" |
| → | "[implies](wikipedia://en/658808)", "if...then" |
| ∀ | "[for all](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_all)" |
| ∃ | "[there exists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_exists)", "for some" |
| \= | "[equals](wikipedia://en/90446)" |
| $\Box$ | "necessarily" |
| $\Diamond$ | "possibly" |

:::

Many of these logical constants are sometimes denoted by alternative symbols (for instance, the use of the symbol "&" rather than "∧" to denote the [logical and](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_and)).

Defining logical constants is a major part of the work of [Gottlob Frege](wikipedia://en/48416) and [Bertrand Russell](wikipedia://en/4163). Russell returned to the subject of logical constants in the preface to the second edition (1937) of *[The Principles of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Principles_of_Mathematics)* noting that logic becomes linguistic: "If we are to say anything definite about them, \[they\] must be treated as part of the language, not as part of what the language speaks about."[^o5] The text of this book uses [relations](wikipedia://en/22517558) *R*, their [converses](wikipedia://en/3941020) and [complements](wikipedia://en/54347#Complementary_relation) as [primitive notions](wikipedia://en/23706), also taken as logical constants in the form *aRb*.
