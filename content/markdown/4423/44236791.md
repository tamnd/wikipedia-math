# Orthocentroidal circle

*Image: Orthocentroidal disk.svg.* A triangle (black), its [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) (blue), its [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) (red), and its orthocentroidal disk (yellow)

*Image: Orthocentroidal circle3.svg.* solid red 2px solid #87CEFA 2px \#8B4513 indigo orange \#FF00FF 00FF00

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthocentroidal circle"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), the **orthocentroidal circle** of a non-equilateral [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) is the circle that has the triangle's [orthocenter](wikipedia://en/161244) and [centroid](wikipedia://en/187926) at opposite ends of its [diameter](wikipedia://en/8007). This diameter also contains the triangle's [nine-point center](wikipedia://en/4517570) and is a subset of the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622), which also contains the [circumcenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumcenter) outside the orthocentroidal circle.

:::

[Andrew Guinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Guinand) showed in 1984 that the triangle's [incenter](wikipedia://en/316837) must lie in the interior of the orthocentroidal circle, but not coinciding with the nine-point center; that is, it must fall in the open **orthocentroidal disk** punctured at the nine-point center.[^o2][^o3][^o4][^o5] [^o6] The incenter could be any such point, depending on the specific triangle having that particular orthocentroidal disk.[^o4]

Furthermore,[^o3] the [Fermat point](wikipedia://en/2666205), the [Gergonne point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gergonne_point), and the [symmedian point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmedian_point) are in the open orthocentroidal disk punctured at its own center (and could be at any point therein), while the [second Fermat point](wikipedia://en/2666205#Aliases) and [Feuerbach point](wikipedia://en/547762) are in the exterior of the orthocentroidal circle. The [set of potential locations](wikipedia://en/542465) of one or the other of the [Brocard points](wikipedia://en/7029586) is also the open orthocentroidal disk.[^o7]

The square of the diameter of the orthocentroidal circle is[^o8] $D^2-\tfrac{4}{9}(a^2+b^2+c^2),$ where *a, b,* and *c* are the triangle's side lengths and *D* is the diameter of its [circumcircle](wikipedia://en/1926432).

The orthocentroidal circle, circumcircle, [nine-point circle](wikipedia://en/161243), [polar circle](wikipedia://en/45079434) and circumcircle of the [tangential triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_triangle) are [coaxal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaxal_circles), and all meet on the common radical axis if the triangle is not acute. The radical axis is the [orthic axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthic_axis).[^o9][^o10]
