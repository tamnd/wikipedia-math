# Nitin Saxena

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Nitin Saxena"}

**Nitin Saxena** (born 3 May 1981[^o3]) is an [Indian scientist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_the_Republic_of_India) in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). His research focuses on [computational complexity](wikipedia://en/7543).

:::

He attracted international attention for proposing the [AKS Primality Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AKS_Primality_Test) in 2002 in a joint work with [Manindra Agrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manindra_Agrawal) and [Neeraj Kayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neeraj_Kayal), for which the trio won the 2006 [Fulkerson Prize](wikipedia://en/1425916), and the 2006 [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize). They provided the first unconditional deterministic algorithm to test an *n*-digit number for primality in a time that has been proven to be polynomial in *n*.[^o4] This research work came out as a part of his [undergraduate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduate) study.

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

He is an alumnus of [Boys' High School And College, Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys'_High_School_&_College_%28Allahabad,_Uttar_Pradesh%29). He graduated with his [B.Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.Tech) in Computer Science and Engineering from [Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Kanpur) in 2002. He received his PhD from the Department of [Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Science) and [Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Engineering) of the same institute in 2006 with the Dissertation titled "Morphisms of Rings and Applications to Complexity".[^o6]

## Career {#Career}

He was awarded the Distinguished [Alumnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumnus) Award of the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur in 2003 for his work in [computational complexity theory](wikipedia://en/7543). He was a postdoctoral researcher at the [Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica](wikipedia://en/506063) (CWI) in the Netherlands from 2006 to 2008, and a Bonn Junior Fellow at the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn) from Summer 2008 onwards.[^o3] He joined the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at [IIT Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Kanpur) as faculty in April 2013.[^o8]

Saxena was awarded the 2018 Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize for his work in Algebraic Complexity Theory. One of the youngest awardees, Saxena’s research interests include Computational Complexity and Algebraic Geometry.[^o9]
