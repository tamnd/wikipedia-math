# Sequence transformation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Sequence transformation"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **sequence transformation** is an [operator](wikipedia://en/22213) acting on a given space of [sequences](wikipedia://en/27838) (a [sequence space](wikipedia://en/1701075)). Sequence transformations include [linear mappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_mapping) such as [discrete convolution](wikipedia://en/7519) with another sequence and [resummation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resummation) of a sequence and nonlinear mappings, more generally. They are commonly used for [series acceleration](wikipedia://en/8737421), that is, for improving the [rate of convergence](wikipedia://en/999701) of a slowly convergent sequence or [series](wikipedia://en/15287). Sequence transformations are also commonly used to compute the [antilimit](wikipedia://en/72396541) of a [divergent series](wikipedia://en/876428) numerically, and are used in conjunction with [extrapolation methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrapolation_methods).

:::

Classical examples for sequence transformations include the [binomial transform](wikipedia://en/3877474), [Möbius transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_transform), and [Stirling transform](wikipedia://en/1782555).

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definitions"}

## Definitions {#Definitions}

For a given sequence

$$(s_n)_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\,$$

and a sequence transformation $\mathbf{T},$ the sequence resulting from transformation by **$\mathbf{T}$** is

$$\mathbf{T}( ( s_n ) ) = ( s'_n )_{n\in\mathbb{N}},$$

where the elements of the transformed sequence are usually computed from some finite number of members of the original sequence, for instance

$$s_n' = T_n(s_n,s_{n+1},\dots,s_{n+k_n})$$

for some natural number $k_n$ for each $n$ and a [multivariate function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_function) $T_n$ of $k_n + 1$ variables for each $n.$ See for instance the [binomial transform](wikipedia://en/3877474) and [Aitken's delta-squared process](wikipedia://en/7411694). In the simplest case the elements of the sequences, the $s_n$ and $s'_n$, are [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) or [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826). More generally, they may be elements of some [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) or [algebra](wikipedia://en/191788).

If the multivariate functions $T_n$ are [linear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_mapping) in each of their arguments for each value of $n,$ for instance if

$$s'_n=\sum_{m=0}^{k_n} c_{n,m} s_{n+m}$$

for some constants $k_n$ and $c_{n,0},\dots,c_{n,k_n}$ for each $n,$ then the sequence transformation $\mathbf{T}$ is called a **linear sequence transformation**. Sequence transformations that are not linear are called nonlinear sequence transformations.

In the context of [series acceleration](wikipedia://en/8737421), when the original sequence $(s_n)$ and the transformed sequence $(s'_n)$ share the same limit $\ell$ as $n \rightarrow \infty,$ the transformed sequence is said to have a faster [rate of convergence](wikipedia://en/999701) than the original sequence if

$$\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{s'_n-\ell}{s_n-\ell} = 0.$$

If the original sequence is [divergent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divergent_sequence), the sequence transformation may act as an [extrapolation method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrapolation_method) to an [antilimit](wikipedia://en/72396541) $\ell$.

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The simplest examples of sequence transformations include shifting all elements by an integer $k$ that does not depend on $n,$ $s'_n = s_{n+k}$ if $n + k \geq 0$ and 0 otherwise, and [scalar multiplication](wikipedia://en/244373) of the sequence some constant $c$ that does not depend on $n,$ $s'_n = c s_{n}.$ These are both examples of linear sequence transformations.

Less trivial examples include the [discrete convolution](wikipedia://en/7519#Discrete_convolution) of sequences with another reference sequence. A particularly basic example is the [difference operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difference_operator), which is convolution with the sequence $(-1,1,0,\ldots)$ and is a discrete analog of the [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921); technically the shift operator and scalar multiplication can also be written as trivial discrete convolutions. The [binomial transform](wikipedia://en/3877474) and the [Stirling transform](wikipedia://en/1782555) are two linear transformations of a more general type.

An example of a nonlinear sequence transformation is [Aitken's delta-squared process](wikipedia://en/7411694), used to improve the [rate of convergence](wikipedia://en/999701) of a slowly convergent sequence. An extended form of this is the [Shanks transformation](wikipedia://en/21581860). The [Möbius transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Möbius_transform) is also a nonlinear transformation, only possible for [integer sequences](wikipedia://en/51421).
