# Anti-diagonal matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anti-diagonal matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **anti-diagonal matrix** is a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) where all the entries are zero except those on the [diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagonal_of_a_matrix) going from the lower left corner to the upper right corner (↗), known as the [anti-diagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-diagonal) (sometimes Harrison diagonal, secondary diagonal, trailing diagonal, minor diagonal, off diagonal or bad diagonal).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is an anti-diagonal matrix if the $(i, j)$th element $a_{ij}$ is zero [for all](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_all) rows $i$ and columns $j$ whose indices do not sum to $n + 1$. Symbolically,

$$a_{ij} = 0 \ \forall i,j \in \left\{1, \ldots, n\right\},\ (i+j \ne n+1).$$

:::

::: {#o5 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

## Example {#Example}

An example of an anti-diagonal matrix is

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.$$

Another example is

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix},$$

which can be used to reverse the elements of an array (as a column matrix) by multiplying on the left.

:::

::: {#o7 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

All anti-diagonal matrices are also [persymmetric](wikipedia://en/10535463).

The product of two anti-diagonal matrices is a [diagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/174080). Furthermore, the product of an anti-diagonal matrix with a diagonal matrix is anti-diagonal, as is the product of a diagonal matrix with an anti-diagonal matrix.

An anti-diagonal matrix is [invertible](wikipedia://en/217122) if and only if the entries on the diagonal from the lower left corner to the upper right corner are nonzero. The inverse of an invertible anti-diagonal matrix is also anti-diagonal, as can be seen from the paragraph above. The [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of an anti-diagonal matrix has [absolute value](wikipedia://en/991) given by the [product](wikipedia://en/24977) of the entries on the diagonal from the lower left corner to the upper right corner. However, the sign of this determinant varies because the one nonzero signed elementary product from an anti-diagonal matrix has a different sign depending on whether the [permutation](wikipedia://en/44027) related to it is odd or even:

::: {#o8 .table cue=table}

| Matrix size | Permutation for nonzero elementary product of anti-diagonal matrix | Even or odd | Sign of elementary product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 × 2 | {2, 1} | Odd | − |
| 3 × 3 | {3, 2, 1} | Odd | − |
| 4 × 4 | {4, 3, 2, 1} | Even | \+ |
| 5 × 5 | {5, 4, 3, 2, 1} | Even | \+ |
| 6 × 6 | {6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1} | Odd | − |

:::

More precisely, the sign of the elementary product needed to calculate the determinant of an anti-diagonal matrix is related to whether the corresponding [triangular number](wikipedia://en/213915) is even or odd. This is because the number of inversions in the permutation for the only nonzero signed elementary product of an $n \times n$ anti-diagonal matrix is equal to the $n$th such number.

:::
