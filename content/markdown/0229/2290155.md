# Daniel Kan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Daniel Kan"}

**Daniel Marinus Kan** (or simply **Dan Kan**) (August 4, 1927 – August 4, 2013) was a Dutch [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) working in [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869) and [homotopy theory](wikipedia://en/61994831). He was a prolific contributor to both fields for six decades, having authored or coauthored several dozen research papers and monographs.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Daniel Kan was born into a Jewish family. During World War II, Kan and his family were interned at the [Bergen-Belsen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen-Belsen_concentration_camp). Although the family survived the internment, his parents died of dysentery shortly after the camp's liberation, leaving Kan orphaned.[^o5] He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at [Hebrew University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University) in 1955, under the direction of [Samuel Eilenberg](wikipedia://en/362501). His students include [Aldridge K. Bousfield](wikipedia://en/65553242), [William Dwyer](wikipedia://en/57424279), [Stewart Priddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Priddy), [Emmanuel Dror Farjoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Dror_Farjoun), and [Jeffrey H. Smith](wikipedia://en/2350459). He was an emeritus professor at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology) where he taught from 1959, formally retiring in 1993.

## Work {#Work}

He played a role in the beginnings of modern homotopy theory similar to that of [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574) in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), namely the adroit and persistent application of [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) methods. His most famous work is the abstract formulation of the discovery of [adjoint functors](wikipedia://en/53991), which dates from 1958.[^o7] The [Kan extension](wikipedia://en/2502518) is one of the broadest descriptions of a useful general class of adjunctions.

From the mid-1950s he made distinguished contributions to the theory of [simplicial sets](wikipedia://en/1027784) and simplicial methods in topology in general. In recognition of this, the usual [closed model category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_model_category) structure on the category of simplicial sets is known as [Kan–Quillen model structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan–Quillen_model_structure), while its fibrations and fibrant objects are known as [Kan fibrations](wikipedia://en/8992759) and [Kan complexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan_complex) respectively.

Some of Kan's later work concerned [model categories](wikipedia://en/2895304) and other homotopical categories. Especially noteworthy are his work with Aldridge Bousfield on completions and [homotopy limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_colimit), and his work with [William Dwyer](wikipedia://en/57424279) on simplicial localizations of relative categories.
