# Differential-linear attack

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Differential-linear attack"}

Introduced by [Martin Hellman](wikipedia://en/422387) and [Susan K. Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_K._Langford) in 1994, the **differential-linear** attack is a mix of both [linear cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/59469) and [differential cryptanalysis](wikipedia://en/8536).

:::

The attack utilises a differential characteristic over part of the cipher with a probability of 1 (for a few rounds—this probability would be much lower for the whole cipher). The rounds immediately following the differential characteristic have a linear approximation defined, and we expect that for each [chosen plaintext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chosen_plaintext) pair, the probability of the linear approximation holding for one chosen plaintext but not the other will be lower for the correct key. Hellman and Langford have shown that this attack can recover 10 key bits of an 8-round [DES](wikipedia://en/7978) with only 512 chosen plaintexts and an 80% chance of success.

The attack was generalised by [Eli Biham](wikipedia://en/348920) et al. to use differential characteristics with probability less than 1. Besides DES, it has been applied to [FEAL](wikipedia://en/592532), [IDEA](wikipedia://en/15076), [Serpent](wikipedia://en/385154), [Camellia](wikipedia://en/532475), and even the [stream cipher](wikipedia://en/49007) [Phelix](wikipedia://en/883596).
