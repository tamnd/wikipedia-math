# Tietze's graph

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tietze's graph"}

*Image: Tietze-Moebius.svg.* Tietze's subdivision of a [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) into six mutually-adjacent regions. The vertices and edges of the subdivision form an embedding of Tietze's graph onto the strip.

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), **Tietze's graph** is an [undirected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) [cubic](wikipedia://en/980508) [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) with 12 vertices and 18 edges. It is named after [Heinrich Franz Friedrich Tietze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Franz_Friedrich_Tietze), who showed in 1910 that the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817) can be subdivided into six regions that all touch each other – three along the boundary of the strip and three along its center line – and therefore that graphs that are [embedded](wikipedia://en/8149170) onto the Möbius strip may require six [colors](wikipedia://en/426743).[^o3] The boundary segments of the regions of Tietze's subdivision (including the segments along the boundary of the Möbius strip itself) form an embedding of Tietze's graph.

:::

## Relation to Petersen graph {#Relation_to_Petersen_graph}

Tietze's graph may be formed from the [Petersen graph](wikipedia://en/194926) by replacing one of its vertices with a triangle.[^o5] Like the Tietze graph, the Petersen graph forms the boundary of six mutually touching regions, but on the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350) rather than on the Möbius strip. If one cuts a hole from this subdivision of the projective plane, surrounding a single vertex, the surrounded vertex is replaced by a triangle of region boundaries around the hole, giving the previously described construction of the Tietze graph.

## Hamiltonicity {#Hamiltonicity}

Both Tietze's graph and the Petersen graph are *maximally nonhamiltonian*: they have no [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), but any two non-adjacent vertices can be connected by a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437).[^o5] Tietze's graph and the Petersen graph are the only [2-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) cubic non-Hamiltonian graphs with 12 or fewer vertices.[^o8]

Unlike the Petersen graph, Tietze's graph is not [hypohamiltonian](wikipedia://en/18301304): removing one of its three triangle vertices forms a smaller graph that remains non-Hamiltonian.

## Edge coloring and perfect matchings {#Edge_coloring_and_perfect_matchings}

[Edge coloring](wikipedia://en/690647) Tietze's graph requires four colors; that is, its chromatic index is 4. Equivalently, the edges of Tietze's graph can be partitioned into four [matchings](wikipedia://en/581797), but no fewer.

Tietze's graph matches part of the definition of a [snark](wikipedia://en/727811): it is a cubic [bridgeless graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeless_graph) that is not 3-edge-colorable. However, most authors restrict snarks to graphs without 3-cycles, so Tietze's graph is not generally considered to be a snark. Nevertheless, it is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) to the graph J<sub>3</sub>, part of an infinite family of [flower snarks](wikipedia://en/23759337) introduced by [R. Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Isaacs_%28game_theorist%29) in 1975.[^o10]

Unlike the Petersen graph, the Tietze graph can be covered by four [perfect matchings](wikipedia://en/44031). This property plays a key role in a [proof](wikipedia://en/82285) that testing whether a graph can be covered by four perfect matchings is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete).[^o11]

## Additional properties {#Additional_properties}

Tietze's graph has chromatic number 3, chromatic index 4, [girth](wikipedia://en/12013) 3 and [diameter](wikipedia://en/2979341) 3. The [independence number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_number) is 5. Its [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) has [order](wikipedia://en/493527) 12, and is [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/12397) to the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267) D<sub>6</sub>, the group of symmetries of a [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) (including both rotations and reflections). This group has two orbits of size 3 and one of size 6 on vertices, and thus this graph is not [vertex-transitive](wikipedia://en/581175).
