# William P. Byers

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="William P. Byers"}

**William Paul Byers** (born 1943) is a [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian) mathematician and philosopher; professor emeritus in mathematics and statistics at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University_%28Quebec%29) in [Montreal, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal), Canada.

:::

He completed a BSc ('64), and an MSc ('65) from [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University), and obtained his PhD ('69) from the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley). His dissertation, *Anosov Flows*, was supervised by [Stephen Smale](wikipedia://en/40294).

His area of interests include [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087) and the [philosophy of mathematics](wikipedia://en/46439).

## Books {#Books}

Byers is the author of three books on mathematics:

- *How Mathematicians Think: Using Ambiguity, Contradiction, and Paradox to Create Mathematics* (Princeton University Press, 2007)
- *The Blind Spot: Science and the Crisis of Uncertainty* (Princeton University Press, 2011)
- *Deep Thinking: What Mathematics Can Teach Us About the Mind* (World Scientific, 2015)
