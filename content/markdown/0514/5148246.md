# Dunce hat (topology)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dunce hat"}

*Image: DunceHatSpace.png.* To get a dunce hat, take a solid triangle and successively glue together all three sides with the indicated orientation.

In [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), the **dunce hat** is a [compact](wikipedia://en/6042) [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) formed by taking a solid [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654) and [gluing](wikipedia://en/237213) all three sides together, with the orientation of one side reversed. Simply gluing two sides oriented in the opposite direction would yield a cone much like the [dunce cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunce#Dunce_cap), but the gluing of the third side results in identifying the base of the cap with a line joining the base to the point.

:::

*Image: Dunce hat animated.gif.* Dunce hat Folding. The blue hole is only for better view: it may be filled by a [spherical cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_cap). The (green) triangle border folds on a circle.

## Name {#Name}

The name is due to [E. C. Zeeman](wikipedia://en/871415), who observed that any [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414) 2-complex (such as the dunce hat) after taking the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/24104095) with the [closed unit interval](wikipedia://en/49324) seemed to be collapsible. This observation became known as the [Zeeman conjecture](wikipedia://en/38271866) and was shown by Zeeman to imply the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798).

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

The dunce hat is [contractible](wikipedia://en/1752414), but not [collapsible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise-linear_collapse). Contractibility can be easily seen by noting that the dunce hat embeds in the 3-ball and the 3-ball [deformation retracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_retract) onto the dunce hat. Alternatively, note that the dunce hat is the [CW-complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-complex) obtained by gluing the boundary of a 2-cell onto the circle. The gluing map is [homotopic](wikipedia://en/212250) to the identity map on the circle and so the complex is [homotopy equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_equivalent) to the disc. By contrast, it is not [collapsible](wikipedia://en/20814602) because it does not have a free face.
