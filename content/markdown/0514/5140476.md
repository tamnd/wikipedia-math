# Nagata's conjecture on curves

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Nagata's conjecture on curves"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Nagata conjecture** on curves, named after [Masayoshi Nagata](wikipedia://en/3086117), governs the minimal degree required for a [plane algebraic curve](wikipedia://en/253260) to pass through a collection of very general points with prescribed [multiplicities](wikipedia://en/19211790).

:::

## History {#History}

Nagata arrived at the conjecture via work on the [14th problem of Hilbert](wikipedia://en/154584), which asks whether the invariant ring of a linear group action on the polynomial ring $k[x_{1}, ..., x_{n}]$ over some field $k$ is [finitely generated](wikipedia://en/963042). Nagata published the conjecture in a 1959 paper in the [American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics), in which he presented a counterexample to Hilbert's 14th problem.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

**Nagata Conjecture.** Suppose $p_{1}, ..., p_{r}$ are very general points in $\mathbf{P}^{2}$ and that $m_{1}, ..., m_{r}$ are given positive integers. Then for $r > 9$ any curve $C$ in $\mathbf{P}^{2}$ that passes through each of the points $p_{i}$ with multiplicity $m_{i}$ must satisfy

$$\deg C > \frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{i=1}^r m_i.$$

The condition $r > 9$ is necessary: The cases $r > 9$ and $r \le 9$ are distinguished by whether or not the [anti-canonical bundle](wikipedia://en/734893) on the [blowup](wikipedia://en/1817790) of $\mathbf{P}^{2}$ at a collection of $r$ points is [nef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerically_effective). In the case where $r \le 9$, the [cone theorem](wikipedia://en/15098681#A_structure_theorem) essentially gives a complete description of the [cone of curves](wikipedia://en/15098681) of the blow-up of the plane.

:::

## Current status {#Current_status}

The only case when this is known to hold is when $r$ is a perfect square, which was proved by [Nagata](wikipedia://en/3086117). Despite much interest, the other cases remain open. A more modern formulation of this conjecture is often given in terms of [Seshadri constants](wikipedia://en/3086195) and has been generalised to other surfaces under the name of the [Nagata–Biran conjecture](wikipedia://en/3086255).
