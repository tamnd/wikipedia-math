# Jean-Louis Koszul

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Jean-Louis Koszul"}

**Jean-Louis Koszul** (; 3 January 1921 – 12 January 2018) was a French mathematician, best known for studying [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and discovering the [Koszul complex](wikipedia://en/519992). He was a second generation member of [Bourbaki](wikipedia://en/167394).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Koszul was educated at the Lycée Fustel-de-Coulanges (Strasbourg) in Strasbourg before studying at the Faculty of Science [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) and the Faculty of Science of the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris). His [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) thesis, titled *Homologie et cohomologie des algèbres de Lie*, was written in 1950 under the direction of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536).

He lectured at many universities and was appointed in 1963 professor in the Faculty of Science at the [University of Grenoble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Fourier_University). He was a member of the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).

Koszul was the cousin of the French composer [Henri Dutilleux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Dutilleux), and the grandchild of the composer [Julien Koszul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Koszul).

Koszul married Denise Reyss-Brion on 17 July 1948. They had three children: Michel, Bertrand, and Anne.

He died on 12 January 2018, at the age of 97, nine days after his 97th birthday.[^o4]
