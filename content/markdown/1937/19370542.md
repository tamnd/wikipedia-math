# Standard normal deviate

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Standard normal deviate"}

A **standard normal deviate** is a [deviate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deviate_%28statistics%29) that is [normally distributed](wikipedia://en/21462). It is a [realization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realization_%28statistics%29) of a **standard normal random variable**, defined as a [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) with [expected value](wikipedia://en/9653) 0 and [variance](wikipedia://en/32344) 1.[^o2] Where collections of such random variables are used, there is often an associated (possibly unstated) assumption that members of such collections are [statistically independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistically_independent).

:::

Standard normal variables play a major role in theoretical statistics in the description of many types of models, particularly in [regression analysis](wikipedia://en/826997), the [analysis of variance](wikipedia://en/634) and [time series analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series_analysis).

When the term "deviate" is used, rather than "variable", there is a connotation that the value concerned is treated as the no-longer-random outcome of a standard normal random variable. The terminology here is the same as that for [random variable](wikipedia://en/25685) and [random variate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_variate). Standard normal deviates arise in practical [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685) in two ways.

- Given a model for a set of observed data, a set of manipulations of the data can result in a derived quantity which, assuming that the model is a true representation of reality, is a standard normal deviate (perhaps in an approximate sense). This enables a [significance test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Significance_test) to be made for the validity of the model.
- In the computer generation of a [pseudorandom number sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudorandom_number_sequence), the aim may be to generate random numbers having a [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462): these can be obtained from standard normal deviates (themselves the output of a pseudorandom number sequence) by multiplying by the [scale parameter](wikipedia://en/769503) and adding the [location parameter](wikipedia://en/17516). More generally, the generation of pseudorandom number sequence having other [marginal distributions](wikipedia://en/506077) may involve manipulating sequences of standard normal deviates: an example here is the [chi-squared distribution](wikipedia://en/113424), random values of which can be obtained by adding the squares of standard normal deviates (although this would seldom be the fastest method of generating such values).
