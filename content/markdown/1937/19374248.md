# Timeline of geometry

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of geometry"}

The following is a timeline of key developments of [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446):

:::

## Before 1000 BC {#Before_1000_BC}

- ca. 2000 BC – [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland), [carved stone balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carved_stone_balls) exhibit a variety of symmetries including all of the symmetries of [Platonic solids](wikipedia://en/23905).
- 1800 BC – [Moscow Mathematical Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Mathematical_Papyrus), findings volume of a frustum
- 1800 BC – [Plimpton 322](wikipedia://en/2983547) contains the oldest reference to the Pythagorean triplets.[^o3]
- 1650 BC – [Rhind Mathematical Papyrus](wikipedia://en/9210114), copy of a lost scroll from around 1850 BC, the scribe [Ahmes](wikipedia://en/386234) presents one of the first known approximate values of [π](wikipedia://en/23601) at 3.16, the first attempt at [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359), earliest known use of a sort of [cotangent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotangent), and knowledge of solving first order linear equations

## 1st millennium BC {#1st_millennium_BC}

- 800 BC – Baudhayana, author of the Baudhayana [Sulba Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulba_Sutras), a [Vedic Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_Sanskrit) geometric text, contains [quadratic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_equations), and calculates the [square root of 2](wikipedia://en/341682) correct to five decimal places
- ca. 600 BC – the other [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_civilization) "[Sulba Sutras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulba_Sutras)" ("rule of chords" in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit)) use [Pythagorean triples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_triples), contain a number of geometrical proofs, and approximate [π](wikipedia://en/23601) at 3.16
- 530 BC – [Pythagoras](wikipedia://en/23275) studies propositional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) and vibrating lyre strings; his group also discover the [irrationality](wikipedia://en/20647689) of the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of [two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two),
- 5th century BC – [Hippocrates of Chios](wikipedia://en/3632522) utilizes [lunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lune_%28mathematics%29) in an attempt to [square the circle](wikipedia://en/201359)
- 5th century BC – [Apastamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apastamba), author of the Apastamba [Sulba Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulba_Sutras), another [Vedic Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_Sanskrit) geometric text, makes an attempt at [squaring the circle](wikipedia://en/201359) and also calculates the [square root](wikipedia://en/29208) of 2 correct to five decimal places
- 370 BC – [Eudoxus](wikipedia://en/48388) states the [method of exhaustion](wikipedia://en/1721284) for [area](wikipedia://en/1209) determination
- 300 BC – [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) in his *[Elements](wikipedia://en/244107)* studies [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) as an [axiomatic system](wikipedia://en/188401), proves the [infinitude](wikipedia://en/142679) of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and presents the [Euclidean algorithm](wikipedia://en/10377); he states the law of reflection in *Catoptrics*, and he proves the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/11556)
- 260 BC – [Archimedes](wikipedia://en/1844) [proved](wikipedia://en/1721284) that the value of [π](wikipedia://en/23601) lies between 3 + 1/7 (approx. 3.1429) and 3 + 10/71 (approx. 3.1408), that the area of a circle was equal to π multiplied by the square of the radius of the circle and that the area enclosed by a parabola and a straight line is 4/3 multiplied by the area of a triangle with equal base and height. He also gave a very accurate estimate of the value of the square root of 3.
- 225 BC – [Apollonius of Perga](wikipedia://en/257242) writes *On [Conic Sections](wikipedia://en/19008673)* and names the [ellipse](wikipedia://en/9277), [parabola](wikipedia://en/23231), and [hyperbola](wikipedia://en/14052),
- 150 BC – [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) mathematicians in [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India) write the "Sthananga Sutra", which contains work on the theory of numbers, arithmetical operations, [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), operations with [fractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractions), simple equations, [cubic equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_equations), quartic equations, and [permutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutations) and [combinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinations)
- 140 BC – [Hipparchus](wikipedia://en/13600) develops the bases of [trigonometry](wikipedia://en/18717261).

## 1st millennium {#1st_millennium}

- ca 340 – [Pappus of Alexandria](wikipedia://en/509300) states his [hexagon theorem](wikipedia://en/1493395) and his [centroid theorem](wikipedia://en/509331)
- 50 – [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204) writes the "Aryabhata-Siddhanta", which first introduces the trigonometric functions and methods of calculating their approximate numerical values. It defines the concepts of [sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sine) and [cosine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine), and also contains the [earliest tables of sine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryabhata's_sine_table) and cosine values (in 3.75-degree intervals from 0 to 90 degrees)
- 7th century – [Bhaskara I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_I) gives a rational approximation of the sine function
- 8th century – [Virasena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virasena) gives explicit rules for the [Fibonacci sequence](wikipedia://en/10918), gives the derivation of the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498) of a [frustum](wikipedia://en/89405) using an [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) procedure.
- 8th century – [Shridhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shridhara) gives the rule for finding the volume of a sphere and also the formula for solving quadratic equations
- 820 – [Al-Mahani](wikipedia://en/1782729) conceived the idea of reducing [geometrical](wikipedia://en/18973446) problems such as [doubling the cube](wikipedia://en/91110) to problems in algebra.
- ca. 900 – [Abu Kamil](wikipedia://en/2479369) of Egypt had begun to understand what we would write in symbols as $x^n \cdot x^m = x^{m+n}$
- 975 – [Al-Batani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Batani) – Extended the Indian concepts of sine and cosine to other trigonometrical ratios, like tangent, secant and their inverse functions. Derived the formula: $\sin \alpha = \tan \alpha / \sqrt{1+\tan^2 \alpha}$ and $\cos \alpha = 1 / \sqrt{1 + \tan^2 \alpha}$.

## 1000–1500 {#1000–1500}

- ca. 1000 – [Law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222) is discovered by [Muslim mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_mathematics), but it is uncertain who discovers it first between [Abu-Mahmud al-Khujandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu-Mahmud_al-Khujandi), [Abu Nasr Mansur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nasr_Mansur), and [Abu al-Wafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abū_al-Wafā'_al-Būzjānī).
- ca. 1100 – [Omar Khayyám](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Khayyám) "gave a complete classification of [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787) with geometric solutions found by means of intersecting [conic sections](wikipedia://en/19008673)." He became the first to find general [geometric](wikipedia://en/18973446) solutions of [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787) and laid the foundations for the development of [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) and [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610). He also extracted [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_of_a_function) using the [decimal](wikipedia://en/8214) system ([Hindu–Arabic numeral system](wikipedia://en/3393371)).
- 1135 – [Sharafeddin Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharafeddin_Tusi) followed al-Khayyam's application of algebra to geometry, and wrote a treatise on [cubic equations](wikipedia://en/180787) which "represents an essential contribution to another [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923) which aimed to study [curves](wikipedia://en/89246) by means of [equations](wikipedia://en/9284), thus inaugurating the beginning of [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997)."[^o7]
- ca. 1250 – [Nasir Al-Din Al-Tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_Al-Din_Al-Tusi) attempts to develop a form of [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610).
- 15th century – [Nilakantha Somayaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilakantha_Somayaji), a [Kerala school](wikipedia://en/3099132) mathematician, writes the "Aryabhatiya Bhasya", which contains work on infinite-series expansions, problems of algebra, and spherical geometry

## 17th century {#17th_century}

- 17th century – Putumana Somayaji writes the "Paddhati", which presents a detailed discussion of various trigonometric series
- 1619 – [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) discovers two of the [Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot_polyhedra).
- 1637 - [René Descartes](wikipedia://en/25525) publishes [La Géométrie](wikipedia://en/239981) which introduces [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202), which involves reducing geometry to a form of arithmetic and algebra and translating geometric shapes into algebraic equations.

## 18th century {#18th_century}

- 1722 – [Abraham de Moivre](wikipedia://en/57327) states [de Moivre's formula](wikipedia://en/57326) connecting [trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_function) and [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826),
- 1733 – [Giovanni Gerolamo Saccheri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gerolamo_Saccheri) studies what geometry would be like if [Euclid's fifth postulate](wikipedia://en/33731493) were false,
- 1796 – [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125) proves that the [regular 17-gon](wikipedia://en/196890) can be constructed using only a [compass and straightedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_and_straightedge)
- 1797 – [Caspar Wessel](wikipedia://en/21418268) associates vectors with [complex numbers](wikipedia://en/5826) and studies complex number operations in geometrical terms,
- 1799 – [Gaspard Monge](wikipedia://en/77840) publishes Géométrie descriptive, in which he introduces [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891).

::: {#o11 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="19th century in geometry"}

## 19th century {#19th_century}

- 1806 – [Louis Poinsot](wikipedia://en/764639) discovers the two remaining [Kepler-Poinsot polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler-Poinsot_polyhedra).
- 1829 – [Bolyai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolyai), [Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), and [Lobachevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Ivanovich_Lobachevsky) invent hyperbolic [non-Euclidean geometry](wikipedia://en/58610),
- 1837 – [Pierre Wantzel](wikipedia://en/2610953) proves that doubling the cube and [trisecting the angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisecting_the_angle) are impossible with only a compass and straightedge, as well as the full completion of the problem of [constructibility](wikipedia://en/523430) of regular polygons
- 1843 – [William Hamilton](wikipedia://en/39817) discovers the calculus of [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) and deduces that they are non-commutative,
- 1854 – [Bernhard Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980) introduces [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243),
- 1854 – [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) shows that [quaternions](wikipedia://en/51440) can be used to represent rotations in four-dimensional [space](wikipedia://en/27667),
- 1858 – [August Ferdinand Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875) invents the [Möbius strip](wikipedia://en/37817),
- 1870 – [Felix Klein](wikipedia://en/41887) constructs an analytic geometry for Lobachevski's geometry thereby establishing its self-consistency and the logical independence of Euclid's fifth postulate,
- 1873 – [Charles Hermite](wikipedia://en/22870422) proves that [e](wikipedia://en/9633) is transcendental,
- 1878 – Charles Hermite solves the general quintic equation by means of elliptic and modular functions
- 1882 – [Ferdinand von Lindemann](wikipedia://en/335736) proves that π is transcendental and that therefore the circle cannot be squared with a compass and straightedge,
- 1882 – Felix Klein discovers the [Klein bottle](wikipedia://en/17412),
- 1899 – [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) presents a set of self-consistent geometric axioms in *Foundations of Geometry*

:::

## 20th century {#20th_century}

- 1901 – [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) develops the [exterior derivative](wikipedia://en/220782),
- 1912 – [Luitzen Egbertus Jan Brouwer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitzen_Egbertus_Jan_Brouwer) presents the [Brouwer fixed-point theorem](wikipedia://en/4101),
- 1916 – [Einstein's](wikipedia://en/736) theory of [general relativity](wikipedia://en/12024).
- 1930 – [Casimir Kuratowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_Kuratowski) shows that the [three-cottage problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-cottage_problem) has no solution,
- 1931 – [Georges de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699) develops theorems in [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) and [characteristic classes](wikipedia://en/363530),
- 1933 – [Karol Borsuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Borsuk) and [Stanislaw Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam) present the [Borsuk-Ulam antipodal-point theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borsuk-Ulam_Theorem),
- 1955 – [H. S. M. Coxeter](wikipedia://en/216328) et al. publish the complete list of [uniform polyhedron](wikipedia://en/2537223),
- 1975 – [Benoit Mandelbrot](wikipedia://en/3999), [fractals](wikipedia://en/10913) theory,
- 1981 – [Mikhail Gromov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gromov_%28mathematician%29) develops the theory of [hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817), revolutionizing both infinite group theory and global differential geometry,
- 1983 – the [classification of finite simple groups](wikipedia://en/7445), a collaborative work involving some hundred mathematicians and spanning thirty years, is completed,
- 1991 – [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340) and [John Lott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lott_%28mathematician%29) develop [non-commutative geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_geometry),
- 1998 – [Thomas Callister Hales](wikipedia://en/2388356) proves the [Kepler conjecture](wikipedia://en/368311),

## 21st century {#21st_century}

- 2003 – [Grigori Perelman](wikipedia://en/225266) proves the [Poincaré conjecture](wikipedia://en/23798),
- 2007 – a team of researchers throughout North America and Europe used networks of computers to map [E8 (mathematics)](wikipedia://en/647994).[^o14]
