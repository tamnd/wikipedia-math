# Timeline of number theory

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of number theory"}

A timeline of **[number theory](wikipedia://en/21527)**.

:::

## Before 1000 BCE {#Before_1000_BCE}

- ca. [20,000 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Paleolithic) — [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley), [Ishango Bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishango_Bone): possibly the earliest reference to [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) and [Egyptian multiplication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_multiplication) although this is disputed.[^o3]

## About 300 BCE {#About_300_BCE}

- 300 BCE — [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331) proves [the number of prime numbers is infinite](wikipedia://en/3143591).

## 1st millennium AD {#1st_millennium_AD}

- 250 — [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110) writes *[Arithmetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetica)*, one of the earliest treatises on [algebra](wikipedia://en/18716923).
- 500 — [Aryabhata](wikipedia://en/297204) solves the general linear [diophantine equation](wikipedia://en/9109).
- 628 – [Brahmagupta](wikipedia://en/297206) gives [Brahmagupta's identity](wikipedia://en/5161105) and solves the so-called [Pell's equation](wikipedia://en/24877) using his composition method.
- ca. 650 — Mathematicians in India create the [Hindu–Arabic numeral system](wikipedia://en/3393371) we use, including the zero, the decimals and negative numbers.

## 1000–1500 {#1000–1500}

- ca. 1000 — [Abu-Mahmud al-Khujandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu-Mahmud_al-Khujandi) first states a special case of [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).
- 895 — [Thabit ibn Qurra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabit_ibn_Qurra) gives a [theorem](wikipedia://en/1817053) by which pairs of [amicable numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amicable_number) can be found, (i.e., two numbers such that each is the sum of the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisor) of the other).
- 975 — The earliest triangle of [binomial coefficients](wikipedia://en/4668) (Pascal triangle) occur in the 10th century in commentaries on the Chandas Shastra.
- 1150 — [Bhaskara II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhaskara_II) gives first general method for solving [Pell's equation](wikipedia://en/24877)
- 1260 — [Kamāl al-Dīn al-Fārisī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamāl_al-Dīn_al-Fārisī) gave a new proof of [Thābit ibn Qurra](wikipedia://en/207547)'s theorem, introducing important new ideas concerning [factorization](wikipedia://en/82341) and [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) methods. He also gave the pair of amicable numbers 17296 and 18416 which have also been jointly attributed to [Fermat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat) as well as Thabit ibn Qurra.[^o7]

## 17th century {#17th_century}

- 1637 — Pierre de Fermat claims to have proven [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953) in his copy of [Diophantus](wikipedia://en/9110)' *Arithmetica*.

## 18th century {#18th_century}

- 1742 — [Christian Goldbach](wikipedia://en/6290) conjectures that every [even](wikipedia://en/143135) number greater than two can be expressed as the sum of two primes, now known as [Goldbach's conjecture](wikipedia://en/44758).
- 1770 — [Joseph Louis Lagrange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Louis_Lagrange) proves the [four-square theorem](wikipedia://en/326395), that every positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) is the sum of four [squares](wikipedia://en/213919) of integers. In the same year, [Edward Waring](wikipedia://en/9723) conjectures [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569), that for any positive integer *k*, every positive integer is the sum of a fixed number of *k*<sup>th</sup> powers.
- 1796 — [Adrien-Marie Legendre](wikipedia://en/49695) conjectures the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).

## 19th century {#19th_century}

- 1801 — *[Disquisitiones Arithmeticae](wikipedia://en/736713)*, [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527) treatise, is published in Latin.
- 1825 — [Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet](wikipedia://en/240002) and Adrien-Marie Legendre prove Fermat's Last Theorem for *n* = 5.
- 1832 — Lejeune Dirichlet proves Fermat's Last Theorem for *n* = 14.
- 1835 — Lejeune Dirichlet proves [Dirichlet's theorem](wikipedia://en/101453) about prime numbers in [arithmetic progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_progressions).
- 1859 — Bernhard Riemann formulates the [Riemann hypothesis](wikipedia://en/19344125) which has strong implications about the distribution of prime numbers.
- 1896 — [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) and [Charles Jean de la Vallée-Poussin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Jean_de_la_Vallée-Poussin) independently prove the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).
- 1896 — [Hermann Minkowski](wikipedia://en/193537) presents *Geometry of numbers*.

## 20th century {#20th_century}

- 1903 — [Edmund Georg Hermann Landau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Georg_Hermann_Landau) gives considerably simpler proof of the prime number theorem.
- 1909 — [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302) proves [Waring's problem](wikipedia://en/33569).
- 1912 — Josip Plemelj publishes simplified proof for the Fermat's Last Theorem for exponent *n* = 5.
- 1913 — [Srinivasa Aaiyangar Ramanujan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srinivasa_Aaiyangar_Ramanujan) sends a long list of complex theorems without proofs to [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599).
- 1914 — Srinivasa Aaiyangar Ramanujan publishes *Modular Equations and Approximations to π*.
- 1910s — Srinivasa Aaiyangar Ramanujan develops over 3000 theorems, including properties of [highly composite numbers](wikipedia://en/208732), the [partition function](wikipedia://en/312250) and its [asymptotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotics), and [mock theta functions](wikipedia://en/5954264). He also makes major breakthroughs and discoveries in the areas of [gamma functions](wikipedia://en/12316), [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000), [divergent series](wikipedia://en/876428), [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_hypergeometric_series) and prime number theory.
- 1919 — [Viggo Brun](wikipedia://en/40647) defines [Brun's constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brun's_constant) *B*<sub>2</sub> for [twin primes](wikipedia://en/41997).
- 1937 — [I. M. Vinogradov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Vinogradov) proves [Vinogradov's theorem](wikipedia://en/4633030) that every sufficiently large [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) integer is the sum of three primes, a close approach to proving [Goldbach's weak conjecture](wikipedia://en/147164).
- 1949 — [Atle Selberg](wikipedia://en/2021) and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) give the first elementary proof of the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).
- 1966 — [Chen Jingrun](wikipedia://en/352985) proves [Chen's theorem](wikipedia://en/1400840), a close approach to proving the [Goldbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldbach_conjecture).
- 1967 — [Robert Langlands](wikipedia://en/25986) formulates the influential [Langlands program](wikipedia://en/171905) of conjectures relating number theory and [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200).
- 1983 — [Gerd Faltings](wikipedia://en/862226) proves the [Mordell conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordell_conjecture) and thereby shows that there are only finitely many whole number solutions for each exponent of Fermat's Last Theorem.
- 1994 — [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027) proves part of the [Taniyama–Shimura conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taniyama–Shimura_conjecture) and thereby proves [Fermat's Last Theorem](wikipedia://en/19021953).
- 1999 — the full Taniyama–Shimura conjecture is proved.

## 21st century {#21st_century}

- 2002 — [Manindra Agrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manindra_Agrawal), [Nitin Saxena](wikipedia://en/2297722), and [Neeraj Kayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neeraj_Kayal) of [IIT Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT_Kanpur) present an unconditional deterministic [polynomial time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_time) algorithm to determine whether a given number is [prime](wikipedia://en/23666).
- 2002 — [Preda Mihăilescu](wikipedia://en/639556) proves [Catalan's conjecture](wikipedia://en/62382).
- 2004 — [Ben Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_J._Green) and [Terence Tao](wikipedia://en/1118498) prove the [Green–Tao theorem](wikipedia://en/7006166), which states that the [sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) of prime numbers contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions.
