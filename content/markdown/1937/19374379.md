# Timeline of mathematical logic

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Timeline of mathematical logic"}

A [timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline) of **[mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636)**; see also [history of logic](wikipedia://en/59945).

:::

## 19th century {#19th_century}

- 1847 – [George Boole](wikipedia://en/38298) proposes symbolic logic in *The Mathematical Analysis of Logic*, defining what is now called [Boolean algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boolean_algebra_%28logic%29).[^o3]
- 1854 – George Boole perfects his ideas, with the publication of [An Investigation of the Laws of Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Investigation_of_the_Laws_of_Thought).
- 1874 – [Georg Cantor](wikipedia://en/12216) proves that the set of all [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) is [uncountably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) but the set of all real [algebraic numbers](wikipedia://en/1158) is [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable). [His proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor's_first_uncountability_proof) does not use his famous [diagonal argument](wikipedia://en/51426), which he published in 1891.
- 1895 – Georg Cantor publishes a book about set theory containing the arithmetic of infinite [cardinal numbers](wikipedia://en/6173) and the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705).
- 1899 – Georg Cantor discovers a contradiction in his set theory.

## 20th century {#20th_century}

- 1904 - [Edward Vermilye Huntington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Vermilye_Huntington) develops the [back-and-forth method](wikipedia://en/2033586) to prove Cantor's result that countable dense linear orders (without endpoints) are isomorphic.
- 1908 – [Ernst Zermelo](wikipedia://en/178655) axiomatizes [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), thus avoiding Cantor's contradictions.
- 1915 - [Leopold Löwenheim](wikipedia://en/755668) publishes a proof of the (downward) [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Löwenheim-Skolem_theorem), implicitly using the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840).
- 1918 - [C. I. Lewis](wikipedia://en/433707) writes *A Survey of Symbolic Logic*, introducing the modal logic system later called S3.
- 1920 - [Thoralf Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765) proves the (downward) [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Löwenheim-Skolem_theorem) using the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) explicitly.
- 1922 - [Thoralf Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765) proves a weaker version of the [Löwenheim-Skolem theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Löwenheim-Skolem_theorem) without the axiom of choice.
- 1929 - [Mojżesz Presburger](wikipedia://en/3968176) introduces [Presburger arithmetic](wikipedia://en/23756) and proving its decidability and completeness.
- 1928 - [Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert) and [Wilhelm Ackermann](wikipedia://en/62727) propose the [Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672): to determine, for a statement of [first-order logic](wikipedia://en/10983) whether it is universally valid (in all models).
- 1930 - [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) proves the [completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Completeness_theorem) and [countable compactness](wikipedia://en/152207) of first-order logic for countable languages.
- 1930 - [Oskar Becker](wikipedia://en/1798192) introduces the modal logic systems now called S4 and S5 as variations of Lewis's system.
- 1930 - [Arend Heyting](wikipedia://en/1180911) develops an [intuitionistic propositional calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitionistic_propositional_calculus).
- 1931 – [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736) proves [his incompleteness theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel's_incompleteness_theorem) which shows that every axiomatic system for mathematics is either incomplete or inconsistent.
- 1932 - [C. I. Lewis](wikipedia://en/433707) and [C. H. Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Langford)'s *Symbolic Logic* contains descriptions of the [modal logic](wikipedia://en/333365) systems S1-5.
- 1933 - Kurt Gödel develops two interpretations of intuitionistic logic in terms of a [provability logic](wikipedia://en/618119), which would become the standard axiomatization of S4.
- 1934 - [Thoralf Skolem](wikipedia://en/453765) constructs a [non-standard model of arithmetic](wikipedia://en/6251420).
- 1936 - [Alonzo Church](wikipedia://en/49731) develops the [lambda calculus](wikipedia://en/18203). [Alan Turing](wikipedia://en/1208) introduces the [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) model proves the existence of [universal Turing machines](wikipedia://en/71435), and uses these results to settle the [Entscheidungsproblem](wikipedia://en/9672) by proving it equivalent to (what is now called) the [halting problem](wikipedia://en/21391870).
- 1936 - [Anatoly Maltsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Maltsev) proves the full compactness theorem for first-order logic, and the "upwards" version of the [Löwenheim–Skolem theorem](wikipedia://en/341482).
- 1940 – Kurt Gödel shows that neither the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) nor the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) can be disproven from the standard axioms of set theory.
- 1943 - [Stephen Kleene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Kleene) introduces the assertion he calls "[Church's Thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church-Turing_Thesis)" asserting the identity of [general recursive functions](wikipedia://en/26469) with effective calculable ones.
- 1944 - [McKinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey) and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) study the relationship between [topological closure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_closure) and Boolean [closure algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closure_algebra).
- 1944 - [Emil Leon Post](wikipedia://en/362382) introduces the [partial order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_order) of the [Turing degrees](wikipedia://en/764405), and also introduces Post's problem: to determine if there are [computably enumerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computably_enumerable) degrees lying in between the degree of computable functions and the degree of the halting problem.
- 1947 - [Andrey Markov Jr.](wikipedia://en/14457637) and [Emil Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Post) independently prove the undecidability of the [word problem for semigroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_problem_for_semigroups).
- 1948 - [McKinsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey) and [Alfred Tarski](wikipedia://en/59920) study closure algebras for S4 and intuitionistic logic.

### 1950-1999 {#1950-1999}

- 1950 - [Boris Trakhtenbrot](wikipedia://en/2815679) [proves that](wikipedia://en/4232656) validity in all finite models (the finite-model version of the Entscheidungsproblem) is also undecidable; here validity corresponds to non-halting, rather than halting as in the usual case.
- 1952 - Kleene presents "Turing's Thesis", asserting the identity of computability in general with computability by Turing machines, as an equivalent form of Church's Thesis.
- 1954 - [Jerzy Łoś](wikipedia://en/3055456) and [Robert Lawson Vaught](wikipedia://en/3550212) [independently proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Łoś-Vaught_test) that a first-order theory which has only infinite models and is [categorical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morley's_categoricity_theorem) in any infinite cardinal at least equal to the language cardinality is [complete](wikipedia://en/10086335). Łoś further conjectures that, in the case where the language is countable, if the theory is categorical in an uncountable cardinal, it is categorical in all uncountable cardinals.
- 1955 - [Jerzy Łoś](wikipedia://en/3055456) uses the [ultraproduct](wikipedia://en/273642) construction to construct the [hyperreals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperreals) and prove the [transfer principle](wikipedia://en/637138).
- 1955 - [Pyotr Novikov](wikipedia://en/1139033) finds a ([finitely presented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finitely_presented_group)) group whose [word problem](wikipedia://en/33563) is undecidable.
- 1955 - [Evertt William Beth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evertt_William_Beth) develops [semantic tableaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantic_tableau).
- 1958 - [William Boone](wikipedia://en/34399699) independently proves the undecidability of the uniform word problem for groups.
- 1959 - [Saul Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039) develops a semantics for quantified S5 based on multiple models.
- 1959 - [Stanley Tennenbaum](wikipedia://en/33807699) [proves that](wikipedia://en/13392068) all countable nonstandard models of [Peano arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peano_arithmetic) are nonrecursive.
- 1960 - [Ray Solomonoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Solomonoff) develops the concept of what would come to be called [Kolmogorov complexity](wikipedia://en/1635) as part of his theory of [Solomonoff induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomonoff_induction).
- 1961 – [Abraham Robinson](wikipedia://en/46024) creates [non-standard analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-standard_analysis).
- 1963 – [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen_%28mathematician%29) uses his technique of [forcing](wikipedia://en/152205) to show that neither the [continuum hypothesis](wikipedia://en/5705) nor the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) can be proven from the standard axioms of set theory.
- 1963 - [Saul Kripke](wikipedia://en/48039) extends his possible-world semantics to [normal modal logics](wikipedia://en/911820).
- 1965 - [Michael D. Morley](wikipedia://en/2921675) introduces the beginnings of [stable theory](wikipedia://en/15828771) in order to prove [Morley's categoricity theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morley's_categoricity_theorem) confirming Łoś' conjecture.
- 1965 - [Andrei Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Kolmogorov) independently develops the theory of [Kolmogorov complexity](wikipedia://en/1635) and uses it to analyze the concept of randomness.
- 1966 - [Grothendieck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grothendieck) proves the [Ax-Grothendieck theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ax-Grothendieck_theorem): any injective polynomial self-map of [algebraic varieties](wikipedia://en/248808) over algebraically closed fields is bijective.
- 1968 - [James Ax](wikipedia://en/6165715) independently proves the Ax-Grothendieck theorem.
- 1969 - [Saharon Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) introduces the concept of [stable](wikipedia://en/15828771) and [superstable theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstable_theory).
- 1970 - [Yuri Matiyasevich](wikipedia://en/2627712) proves that the existence of solutions to [Diophantine equations](wikipedia://en/9109) is [undecidable](wikipedia://en/15631055)
- 1975 - [Harvey Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108) introduces the [Reverse Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_Mathematics) program.
