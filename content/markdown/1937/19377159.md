# Wild knot

*Image: Wild knot.svg.* A wild knot

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wild knot"}

In the [mathematical theory of knots](wikipedia://en/153008), a knot is **tame** if it can be "thickened", that is, if there exists an extension to an [embedding](wikipedia://en/52119) of the [solid torus](wikipedia://en/1251473) $S^1\times D^2$ into the [3-sphere](wikipedia://en/39792). A knot is tame if and only if it can be represented as a finite [closed polygonal chain](wikipedia://en/12056032). In knot theory and [3-manifold](wikipedia://en/1018257) theory, often the adjective "tame" is omitted. Smooth knots, for example, are always tame.

:::

Knots that are not tame are called **wild** and can have [pathological](wikipedia://en/372742) behavior. Every closed curve containing a [wild arc](wikipedia://en/25851959) is a wild knot. It has been conjectured that every **wild knot** has infinitely many [quadrisecants](wikipedia://en/40087321).[^o3]

As well as their mathematical study, wild knots have also been studied for their potential for decorative purposes in [Celtic-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_knot) [ornamental knotwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_decorative_knots).[^o4]
