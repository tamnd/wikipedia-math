# Fred Diamond

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Fred Diamond"}

**Fred Irvin Diamond** (born November 19, 1964)[^o9] is a [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his role in proving the [modularity theorem](wikipedia://en/174475) for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o10] His research interest is in [modular forms](wikipedia://en/286000) and [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_module).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Diamond received his [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) in 1984,[^o11] and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in mathematics from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1988 as a [doctoral student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_student) of [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027).[^o11][^o12] He has held positions at [Brandeis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis_University) and [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University), and is currently a professor at [King's College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_College_London).[^o11]

Diamond is the author of several research papers, and is also a coauthor along with [Jerry Shurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Shurman) of *A First Course in Modular Forms*, in the [Graduate Texts in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/7392980) series published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer-Verlag).[^o4][^o5][^o6]
