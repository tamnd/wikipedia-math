# Hyperelliptic surface

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperelliptic surface"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **hyperelliptic surface**, or **bi-elliptic surface**, is a minimal [surface](wikipedia://en/4342970) whose [Albanese morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanese_morphism) is an [elliptic fibration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_fibration) without singular fibres. Any such surface can be written as the [quotient](wikipedia://en/11526) of a [product](wikipedia://en/8887) of two elliptic curves by a [finite abelian group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_abelian_group). Hyperelliptic surfaces form one of the classes of surfaces of [Kodaira dimension](wikipedia://en/1866873) 0 in the [Enriques–Kodaira classification](wikipedia://en/3029260).

:::

## Classification {#Classification}

Any hyperelliptic surface is a quotient (*E*×*F*)/*G*, where *E* = **C**/Λ and *F* are elliptic curves, and *G* is a subgroup of *F* ([acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) on *F* by translations), which acts on *E* not only by translations. There are seven families of hyperelliptic surfaces as in the following table.

::: {#o4 .table cue=table}

| order of K | Λ | G | Action of G on *E* |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Any | **Z**/2**Z** | *e* → −*e* |
| 2 | Any | **Z**/2**Z** ⊕ **Z**/2**Z** | *e* → −*e*, *e* → *e*+*c*, −*c*=*c* |
| 3 | **Z** ⊕ **Z**ω | **Z**/3**Z** | *e* → ω*e* |
| 3 | **Z** ⊕ **Z**ω | **Z**/3**Z** ⊕ **Z**/3**Z** | *e* → ω*e*, *e* → *e*+*c*, ω*c*=*c* |
| 4 | **Z** ⊕ **Z**i; | **Z**/4**Z** | *e* → i*e* |
| 4 | **Z** ⊕ **Z**i | **Z**/4**Z** ⊕ **Z**/2**Z** | *e* → i*e*, *e* → *e*+*c*, i*c*=*c* |
| 6 | **Z** ⊕ **Z**ω | **Z**/6**Z** | *e* → −ω*e* |

:::

Here ω is a [primitive cube root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_nth_root_of_unity) of 1 and i is a primitive 4th root of 1.

## Quasi hyperelliptic surfaces {#Quasi_hyperelliptic_surfaces}

A **quasi-hyperelliptic surface** is a surface whose [canonical divisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_divisor) is numerically equivalent to zero, the [Albanese mapping](wikipedia://en/1814828) maps to an elliptic curve, and all its [fibers](wikipedia://en/6603892) are [rational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve) with a [cusp](wikipedia://en/6851758). They only exist in [characteristics](wikipedia://en/1066621) 2 or 3. Their second [Betti number](wikipedia://en/346262) is 2, the second [Chern number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chern_number) vanishes, and the [holomorphic Euler characteristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holomorphic_Euler_characteristic) vanishes. They were classified by (Bombieri Mumford 1976), who found six cases in characteristic 3 (in which case 6*K*= 0) and eight in characteristic 2 (in which case 6*K* or 4*K* vanishes). Any quasi-hyperelliptic surface is a quotient (*E*×*F*)/*G*, where *E* is a [rational curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_curve) with one cusp, *F* is an elliptic curve, and *G* is a finite [subgroup scheme](wikipedia://en/510523) of *F* (acting on *F* by translations).
