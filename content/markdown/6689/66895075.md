# Pestov–Ionin theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pestov–Ionin theorem"}

*Image: Moon-in-puddle-green.svg.* A smooth simple closed curve of curvature at most one, and a unit disk enclosed by it

The **Pestov–Ionin theorem** in the [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) of [plane curves](wikipedia://en/971691) states that every [simple closed curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_closed_curve) of [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770) at most one encloses a [unit disk](wikipedia://en/252738). It has also been called the **moon in a puddle theorem**.

:::

## History and generalizations {#History_and_generalizations}

Although a version of this was published for [convex curves](wikipedia://en/29747989) by [Wilhelm Blaschke](wikipedia://en/728998) in 1916, it is named for German Gavrilovich Pestov and Vladimir Kuzmich Ionin, who published a version of this theorem in 1959 for non-convex [doubly differentiable](wikipedia://en/1408000) ($C^2$) curves, the curves for which the curvature is well-defined at every point. The theorem has been generalized further, to curves of bounded average curvature (singly differentiable, and satisfying a [Lipschitz condition](wikipedia://en/51731) on the derivative), to curves of bounded convex curvature (each point of the curve touches a unit disk that, within some small neighborhood of the point, remains interior to the curve), and to curves whose curvature is bounded in a viscosity sense.

It does not generalize to higher dimensions: there exist surfaces whose [principal curvatures](wikipedia://en/2072472) are lower bounded by a constant, [topologically equivalent](wikipedia://en/13660) to embedded spheres, that do not contain a sphere with that constant curvature. One construction for this is based on the [house with two rooms](wikipedia://en/4446017), a topological complex that is [contractible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractible) but not [collapsible](wikipedia://en/20814602).

## Applications {#Applications}

The theorem has been applied in [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) for [motion planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_planning). In particular it has been used for finding [Dubins paths](wikipedia://en/35938288), shortest routes for vehicles that can move only in a forwards direction and that can turn left or right with a bounded [turning radius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_radius). It has also been used for planning the motion of the cutter in a milling machine for [pocket machining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milling_%28machining%29), and in reconstructing curves from scattered data points.
