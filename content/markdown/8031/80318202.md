# Grünbaum–Nash-Williams conjecture

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Grünbaum–Nash-Williams conjecture"}

::: {#o2 .problem cue=unsolved}

Does every [4-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057) have a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle)?

:::

*Image: Toroidal Hamiltonian.svg.* A [4-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057) with a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle) in red

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Grünbaum–Nash-Williams conjecture** states that every [4-vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph) [toroidal graph](wikipedia://en/1633057) has a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle). It is a generalization of [Tutte's theorem on Hamiltonian cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutte's_theorem_on_Hamiltonian_cycles), according to which every 4-vertex-connected [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314) has a Hamiltonian cycle. An analogous theorem of Thomas and Yu holds for graphs on the [projective plane](wikipedia://en/24350).

:::

For 4-vertex-connected planar and projective planar graphs, more strongly, every edge belongs to a Hamiltonian cycle. However, this stronger property is not true of toroidal graphs. For instance, the [Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/2856852) of two even cycles, with a diagonal added to one of its quadrilateral faces, is a 4-vertex-connected toroidal graph none of whose Hamiltonian cycles contains the added diagonal.

The conjecture was formulated in the early 1970s by [Branko Grünbaum](wikipedia://en/1731976) and [Crispin Nash-Williams](wikipedia://en/16550517). As partial progress toward the conjecture, [Robin Thomas](wikipedia://en/24641679) and X. Yu proved that every 5-vertex-connected toroidal graph has a Hamiltonian cycle, and (with W. Zang) that every 4-vertex-connected toroidal graph has a [Hamiltonian path](wikipedia://en/244437).
