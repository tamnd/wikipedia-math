# Wirtinger inequality (2-forms)

*For other inequalities named after Wirtinger, see [Wirtinger's inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtinger's_inequality_%28disambiguation%29).*

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wirtinger inequality"}

In mathematics, the **Wirtinger inequality**, named after [Wilhelm Wirtinger](wikipedia://en/2044921), is a fundamental result in [complex linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422) which relates the symplectic and volume forms of a [hermitian inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_inner_product). It has important consequences in [complex geometry](wikipedia://en/186101), such as showing that the normalized [exterior powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_power) of the [Kähler form](wikipedia://en/390538) of a [Kähler manifold](wikipedia://en/390538) are [calibrations](wikipedia://en/19364294).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

## Statement {#Statement}

Consider a [real vector space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_vector_space) with positive-definite [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) $g$, symplectic form $\omega$, and almost-complex structure $J$, linked by $\omega(u, v) = g(J(u), v)$ for any vectors $u$ and $v$. Then for any orthonormal vectors $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$ there is

$$(\underbrace{\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega}_{k\text{ times}})(v_1,\ldots,v_{2k}) \leq k !.$$

There is equality if and only if the span of $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$ is closed under the operation of $J$.

In the language of the [comass](wikipedia://en/3021207) of a form, the Wirtinger theorem (although without precision about when equality is achieved) can also be phrased as saying that the comass of the form $\omega \land \cdot\cdot\cdot \land \omega$ is equal to $k!$.

:::

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof"}

## Proof {#Proof}

### $k = 1$

In the special case $k = 1$, the Wirtinger inequality is a special case of the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](wikipedia://en/38128):

$$\omega(v_1,v_2)=g(J(v_1),v_2)\leq \|J(v_1)\|_g\|v_2\|_g=1.$$

According to the equality case of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, equality occurs if and only if $J(v_{1})$ and $v_{2}$ are collinear, which is equivalent to the span of $v_{1}, v_{2}$ being closed under $J$.

### $k > 1$

Let $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$ be fixed, and let $T$ denote their span. Then there is an orthonormal basis $e_{1}, ..., e_{2k}$ of $T$ with dual basis $w_{1}, ..., w_{2k}$ such that

$$\iota^\ast\omega=\sum_{j=1}^k\omega(e_{2j-1},e_{2j})w_{2j-1}\wedge w_{2j},$$

where $\iota$ denotes the inclusion map from $T$ into $V$. This implies

$$\underbrace{\iota^\ast\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge \iota^\ast\omega}_{k\text{ times}}=k!\prod_{i=1}^k\omega(e_{2i-1},e_{2i})w_1\wedge \cdots\wedge w_{2k},$$

which in turn implies

$$(\underbrace{\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega}_{k\text{ times}})(e_1,\ldots,e_{2k})=k!\prod_{i=1}^k\omega(e_{2i-1},e_{2i})\leq k!,$$

where the inequality follows from the previously-established $k = 1$ case. If equality holds, then according to the $k = 1$ equality case, it must be the case that $\omega(e_{2i - 1}, e_{2i}) = \pm1$ for each $i$. This is equivalent to either $\omega(e_{2i - 1}, e_{2i}) = 1$ or $\omega(e_{2i}, e_{2i - 1}) = 1$, which in either case (from the $k = 1$ case) implies that the span of $e_{2i - 1}, e_{2i}$ is closed under $J$, and hence that the span of $e_{1}, ..., e_{2k}$ is closed under $J$.

Finally, the dependence of the quantity

$$(\underbrace{\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega}_{k\text{ times}})(v_1,\ldots,v_{2k})$$

on $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$ is only on the quantity $v_{1} \land \cdot\cdot\cdot \land v_{2k}$, and from the orthonormality condition on $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$, this wedge product is well-determined up to a sign. This relates the above work with $e_{1}, ..., e_{2k}$ to the desired statement in terms of $v_{1}, ..., v_{2k}$.

:::

## Consequences {#Consequences}

Given a [complex manifold](wikipedia://en/509742) with [hermitian metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_metric), the Wirtinger theorem immediately implies that for any $2k$-dimensional [embedded submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_submanifold) $M$, there is

$$\operatorname{vol}(M)\geq\frac{1}{k!}\int_M \omega^k,$$

where $\omega$ is the [Kähler form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_form) of the metric. Furthermore, equality is achieved if and only if $M$ is a [complex submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_submanifold). In the special case that the hermitian metric satisfies the [Kähler condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kähler_metric), this says that $\tfrac{1}{k!}\omega^{k}$ is a [calibration](wikipedia://en/19364294) for the underlying Riemannian metric, and that the corresponding [calibrated submanifolds](wikipedia://en/19364294) are the complex submanifolds of complex dimension $k$. This says in particular that every complex submanifold of a Kähler manifold is a [minimal submanifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_submanifold), and is even volume-minimizing among all submanifolds in its [homology class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_class).

Using the Wirtinger inequality, these facts even extend to the more sophisticated context of [currents](wikipedia://en/3021207) in Kähler manifolds.
