# Adriaan Cornelis Zaanen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Adriaan Cornelis Zaanen"}

**Adriaan Cornelis "Aad" Zaanen** (14 June 1913 in [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam) – 1 April 2003 in [Wassenaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassenaar)) was a Dutch mathematician working in analysis. He is known for his books on [Riesz spaces](wikipedia://en/8099018) (together with [Wim Luxemburg](wikipedia://en/26233152)).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Zaanen was born in [Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam), where he attended the [Hogere Burgerschool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogereburgerschool). He graduated in 1930 with excellent marks, and started his studies in mathematics at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University). Having obtained his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) in 1935, he did research under the guidance of his [doctoral advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_advisor) Johannes Droste,[^o4] and was awarded a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1938. His [doctoral thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctoral_thesis) dealt with the convergence of series of [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) of the [Sturm–Liouville type](wikipedia://en/490990).[^o5] The same year he was appointed a mathematics teacher at the Hogere Burgerschool in Rotterdam, a profession that he continued until 1947.[^o6]

In the next years and also in the period of the [German occupation of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_the_Netherlands), Zaanen continued to do mathematical research in his spare time. He studied [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646)'s *Théorie des Opérations Linéaires*, the book that laid the foundations of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), and [Marshall H. Stone](wikipedia://en/758331)'s *Linear Transformations in Hilbert Space*. During this period he wrote nine scientific papers on [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234) with symmetrisable kernels that were published in the Proceedings of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1946–47.[^o7]

In parallel to his job as a secondary-school teacher, Zaanen was appointed in 1946 as a mathematics teacher for three hours per week at the [Technische Hogeschool Delft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technische_Hogeschool_Delft), and as an unpaid [*privaatdocent*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatdozent) at Leiden University where he taught a course on [Lebesgue integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebesgue_integration).[^o7]

In 1947 Zaanen accepted the position of Professor of Mathematics at the [Technische Hogeschool Bandoeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung_Institute_of_Technology). In 1950 he returned to the Netherlands where he was appointed Professor of Mathematics at the Technische Hogeschool Delft. In these years he continued his work on the book *Linear Analysis*, which was published in 1953 and for years was a prominent work on functional analysis and the theory of integral equations.[^o8]

In 1956 Zaanen was appointed Professor of Mathematics at Leiden University. There he started a large research programme into the theory of [Riesz spaces](wikipedia://en/8099018), together with his first doctoral student [Wim Luxemburg](wikipedia://en/26233152), Professor of Mathematics at the [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology). Most of their results were published in a series of papers in the Proceedings of the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences. Unusually for mathematics research in the Netherlands at the time, Zaanen pursued a long-term research programme involving a number of collaborators and doctoral students. Eight doctoral theses on various topics in the theory of Riesz spaces were produced in this school.[^o9]

Zaanen took retirement in 1982.

## Publications {#Publications}

Zaanen published almost 70 papers in scientific journals and reviewed conference proceedings.[^o11] He is however best known for four large books that each took a prominent place in scientific literature:

- A.C. Zaanen, *Linear Analysis*, North-Holland Publishing Company, Amsterdam and P. Noordhoff, Groningen (1953, 1957, 1960), 600 pages
- Adriaan Cornelis Zaanen, *Integration*, North-Holland Publishing Company, Amsterdam (1967) 604 pages. Revised and enlarged edition of *An Introduction to the Theory of Integration* (1958, 1961, 1965).
- W.A.J. Luxemburg and A.C. Zaanen, *Riesz Spaces Volume I*, North-Holland Publishing Company, Amsterdam London (1971), 514 pages
- A.C. Zaanen, *Riesz Spaces II*, North-Holland Publishing Company, Amsterdam New York Oxford (1983), 720 pages

## Other functions and honours {#Other_functions_and_honours}

Zaanen was elected a member of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) in 1960.[^o6]

He served as President of the Dutch Mathematical Society from 1970 until 1972. He was an editor of the Society's journal *Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde* from 1953 until 1982. In 1988 he was appointed an honorary member of the Society.

He served as a member of the *Curatorium* of the [Mathematisch Centrum](wikipedia://en/506063) from 1965 until 1979.

On his retirement in 1982 Zaanen was appointed Knight of the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).[^o7]
