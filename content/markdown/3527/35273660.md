# Albert Charles Schaeffer

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Albert Charles Schaeffer"}

**Albert Charles Schaeffer** (13 August 1907, [Belvidere, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belvidere,_Illinois) – 2 February 1957) was an American mathematician who worked on [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Schaeffer was the son of Albert John and Mary Plane Schaeffer (*née* Herrick). He studied [civil engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) at the [University of Wisconsin, Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin,_Madison) (bachelor's degree 1930) and was, from 1930 to 1933, employed as a highway engineer. In 1936, he received a PhD in mathematics under [Eberhard Hopf](wikipedia://en/6021730) at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology). From 1936 to 1939, he was an instructor at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University). In 1939, he became an instructor at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) where he became, in 1941, assistant professor, in 1943 associate professor and in 1946 professor. From 1947 to 1950, he was a professor at Purdue University. From 1950 to 1957, he was a professor at the University of Wisconsin, Madison, and in the academic year 1956/57, the chair of the mathematics department.[^o3]

Schaeffer worked with [Donald Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_C._Spencer) at Stanford University on variational problems of [conformal mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_mapping), for example, coefficient ranges for [schlicht functions](wikipedia://en/252075#Schlicht_functions) (functions analytic and one-to-one). Specifically, they worked on special cases of the [Bieberbach conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieberbach_conjecture), for which they gave a proof that the third coefficient satisfied the conjectured estimate (a result already proved by [Charles Loewner](wikipedia://en/9337483)). Their goal was to give a proof for the fourth coefficient, but their approach would have required the numerical integration of about one million [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). A little later, [Paul Garabedian](wikipedia://en/22618676) and [Max Schiffer](wikipedia://en/48545257), then at Stanford, improved the Schaeffer–Spencer method and greatly reduced the number of necessary integrations. With this improvement, Garabedian and Schiffer were able in 1955 to prove the conjectured estimate for the fourth coefficient. In 1948, Schaeffer shared the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) with Spencer for their joint work on schlicht functions.[^o4]

In 1941, Schaeffer and [R. J. Duffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Duffin) put forward[^o5] a conjecture in metric [diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558) which was resolved in 2020 by [James Maynard](wikipedia://en/42512102) and [Dimitris Koukoulopoulos](wikipedia://en/69797343).[^o6]

In 1931, he married Caroline Juliette Marsh. They had two sons and a daughter.
