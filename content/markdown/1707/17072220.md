# Affine Grassmannian (manifold)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Affine Grassmannian"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), there are two distinct meanings of the term **affine Grassmannian**. In one it is the manifold of all *k*-dimensional [affine subspaces](wikipedia://en/298834) of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> (described on this page), while in the other the [affine Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/16896827) is a quotient of a group-ring based on formal [Laurent series](wikipedia://en/61213).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Given a finite-dimensional [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) *V* and a non-negative integer *k*, then Graff<sub>*k*</sub>(*V*) is the [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) of all [affine](wikipedia://en/298834) *k*-dimensional subspaces of *V*.

It has a natural projection *p*:Graff<sub>*k*</sub>(*V*) → Gr<sub>*k*</sub>(*V*), the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of all linear *k*-dimensional subspaces of *V* by defining *p*(*U*) to be the translation of *U* to a subspace through the origin. This projection is a fibration, and if *V* is given an inner product, the fibre containing *U* can be identified with $p(U)^\perp$, the [orthogonal complement](wikipedia://en/1058833) to *p*(*U*). The fibres are therefore vector spaces, and the projection *p* is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) over the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810), which defines the [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) structure on Graff<sub>*k*</sub>(*V*).

As a [homogeneous space](wikipedia://en/363325), the affine Grassmannian of an *n*-dimensional vector space *V* can be identified with

$$\mathrm{Graff}_k(V) \simeq \frac{E(n)}{E(k)\times O(n-k)}$$

where *E*(*n*) is the [Euclidean group](wikipedia://en/652164) of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> and O(*m*) is the [orthogonal group](wikipedia://en/173954) on **R**<sup>*m*</sup>. It follows that the dimension is given by

$$\dim\left[ \mathrm{Graff}_k(V) \right] = (n-k)(k+1) \, .$$

(This relation is easier to deduce from the identification of next section, as the difference between the number of coefficients, (*n*−*k*)(*n*+1) and the dimension of the linear group acting on the equations, (*n*−*k*)<sup>2</sup>.)

:::

## Relationship with ordinary Grassmannian {#Relationship_with_ordinary_Grassmannian}

Let (*x*<sub>1</sub>,...,*x*<sub>*n*</sub>) be the usual linear coordinates on **R**<sup>*n*</sup>. Then **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is embedded into **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup> as the affine hyperplane *x*<sub>*n*+1</sub> = 1. The *k*-dimensional affine subspaces of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> are in one-to-one correspondence with the (*k*+*1*)-dimensional linear subspaces of **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup> that are in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736) with respect to the plane *x*<sub>*n*+1</sub> = 1. Indeed, a *k*-dimensional affine subspace of **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is the locus of solutions of a rank *n* − *k* system of affine equations

$$\begin{aligned} a_{11}x_1 + \cdots + a_{1n}x_n &= a_{1,n+1}\\ &\vdots&\\ a_{n-k,1}x_1 + \cdots + a_{n-k,n}x_n &= a_{n-k,n+1}. \end{aligned}$$

These determine a rank *n*−*k* system of *linear* equations on **R**<sup>*n*+1</sup>

$$\begin{aligned} a_{11}x_1 + \cdots + a_{1n}x_n &= a_{1,n+1}x_{n+1}\\ &\vdots&\\ a_{n-k,1}x_1 + \cdots + a_{n-k,n}x_n &= a_{n-k,n+1}x_{n+1}. \end{aligned}$$

whose solution is a (*k* + 1)-plane that, when intersected with *x*<sub>*n*+1</sub> = 1, is the original *k*-plane.

Because of this identification, Graff(*k*,*n*) is a [Zariski open set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski_open_set) in Gr(*k* + 1, *n* + 1).
