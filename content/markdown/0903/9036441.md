# Anatoly Vershik

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anatoly Vershik"}

**Anatoly Moiseevich Vershik** (Анато́лий Моисе́евич Ве́ршик; 28 December 1933 – 14 February 2024) was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and Russian mathematician. He is most famous for his joint work with [Sergei V. Kerov](wikipedia://en/72290961) on [representations](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [infinite](wikipedia://en/20110824) [symmetric groups](wikipedia://en/28901) and applications to the [longest increasing subsequences](wikipedia://en/4587078).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Vershik studied at Leningrad State University (later renamed to [Saint Petersburg State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University)), receiving his doctoral degree in 1974; his advisor was [Vladimir Rokhlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Rokhlin_%28Soviet_mathematician%29).

Vershik worked at the [St. Petersburg Department of Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/16031996) and at Saint Petersburg State University. In 1998–2008, he was the president of the [St. Petersburg Mathematical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg_Mathematical_Society).

In 2012, Vershik became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o5] In 2015, he was elected a member of [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea). [^o6]

His doctoral students include [Alexander Barvinok](wikipedia://en/47548981), [Dmitri Burago](wikipedia://en/57995304), [Anna Erschler](wikipedia://en/48303774), [Sergey Fomin](wikipedia://en/33046302), Vadim Kaimanovich, [Sergei Kerov](wikipedia://en/72290961), Alexander N. Livshits, Andrei Lodkin, Nikolai Mnev, and Natalia Tsilevich.

He had a daughter, Estonian linguist [Anna Verschik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Verschik).

Anatoly Vershik died on 14 February 2024, at the age of 90.[^o7][^o8]
