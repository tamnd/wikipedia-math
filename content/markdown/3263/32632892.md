# Noncentral beta distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Noncentral beta distribution"}

In [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **noncentral beta distribution** is a [continuous probability distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_probability_distribution) that is a [noncentral generalization](wikipedia://en/20829683) of the (central) [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074).

:::

The noncentral beta distribution (Type I) is the distribution of the ratio

$$X = \frac{\chi^2_m(\lambda)}{\chi^2_m(\lambda) + \chi^2_n},$$

where $\chi^2_m(\lambda)$ is a [noncentral chi-squared](wikipedia://en/2601749) random variable with degrees of freedom *m* and noncentrality parameter $\lambda$, and $\chi^2_n$ is a central [chi-squared](wikipedia://en/113424) random variable with degrees of freedom *n*, independent of $\chi^2_m(\lambda)$.[^o2] In this case, $X \sim \mbox{Beta}\left(\frac{m}{2},\frac{n}{2},\lambda\right)$

A Type II noncentral beta distribution is the distribution of the ratio

$$Y = \frac{\chi^2_n}{\chi^2_n + \chi^2_m(\lambda)},$$

where the noncentral chi-squared variable is in the denominator only.[^o2] If $Y$ follows the type II distribution, then $X = 1 - Y$ follows a type I distribution.

## Cumulative distribution function {#Cumulative_distribution_function}

The Type I [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) is usually represented as a [Poisson](wikipedia://en/23009144) mixture of central [beta](wikipedia://en/207074) random variables:[^o2]

$$F(x) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty P(j) I_x(\alpha+j,\beta),$$

where λ is the noncentrality parameter, *P*(.) is the Poisson(λ/2) probability mass function, *\\alpha=m/2* and *\\beta=n/2* are shape parameters, and $I_x(a,b)$ is the [incomplete beta function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regularized_incomplete_beta_function). That is,

$$F(x) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^je^{-\lambda/2}I_x(\alpha+j,\beta).$$

The Type II [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) in mixture form is

$$F(x) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty P(j) I_x(\alpha,\beta+j).$$

Algorithms for evaluating the noncentral beta distribution functions are given by Posten[^o4] and Chattamvelli.[^o2]

## Probability density function {#Probability_density_function}

The (Type I) [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) for the noncentral beta distribution is:

$$f(x) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty \frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)^je^{-\lambda/2}\frac{x^{\alpha+j-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}}{B(\alpha+j,\beta)}.$$

where $B$ is the [beta function](wikipedia://en/245660), $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the shape parameters, and $\lambda$ is the [noncentrality parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noncentrality_parameter). The density of *Y* is the same as that of *1-X* with the degrees of freedom reversed.[^o2]

## Related distributions {#Related_distributions}

### Transformations {#Transformations}

If $X\sim\mbox{Beta}\left(\alpha,\beta,\lambda\right)$, then $\frac{\beta X}{\alpha (1-X)}$ follows a [noncentral F-distribution](wikipedia://en/1368010) with $2\alpha, 2\beta$ degrees of freedom, and non-centrality parameter $\lambda$.

If $X$ follows a [noncentral F-distribution](wikipedia://en/1368010) $F_{\mu_{1}, \mu_{2}}\left( \lambda \right)$ with $\mu_{1}$ numerator degrees of freedom and $\mu_{2}$ denominator degrees of freedom, then

$$Z = \cfrac{\cfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}}{\cfrac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}} + X^{-1} }$$

follows a noncentral Beta distribution:

$$Z \sim \mbox{Beta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1},\frac{1}{2}\mu_{2},\lambda\right).$$

This is derived from making a straightforward transformation.

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

### Special cases {#Special_cases}

When $\lambda = 0$, the noncentral beta distribution is equivalent to the (central) [beta distribution](wikipedia://en/207074).

:::
