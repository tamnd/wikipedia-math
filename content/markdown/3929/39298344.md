# Mathieu Lewin

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mathieu Lewin"}

**Mathieu Lewin** (born 14 November 1977 in [Senlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senlis), [Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oise), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France)) is a French mathematician and [mathematical physicist](wikipedia://en/173416) who deals with [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564), mathematical [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267), and mathematics of [quantum mechanical](wikipedia://en/25202) [many-body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Many-body_theory) systems.[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Lewin studied mathematics at the [École normale supérieure de Cachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_normale_supérieure_de_Cachan), receiving his master's degree in 2000. He then received his PhD in 2004 at the [Paris Dauphine University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Dauphine_University) (Dauphine-Paris) PhD under the direction of Éric Séré. His dissertation was titled *Some Nonlinear Models in quantum mechanics*. From 2004 to 2005 he was a postdoctoral fellow at the [University of Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen) under Jan Philip Solovej. From 2005, he conducted research for the Centre national de la recherche scientifique (CNRS) at the [University of Cergy-Pontoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cergy-Pontoise), then at the Paris-Dauphine university.[^o2]

In July 2012, he was awarded an [EMS Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMS_Prize) "for his ground breaking work in rigorous aspects of quantum chemistry, mean field approximations to relativistic quantum field theory and statistical mechanics".[^o2]

## Works {#Works}

His works concern the mathematical properties of matter at the microscopic scale, and they are mostly based on quantum mechanics. He uses tools from the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882), [nonlinear functional analysis](wikipedia://en/8546912), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), and [spectral theory](wikipedia://en/506713). For instance, he studied several nonlinear models for atoms and molecules (e.g. the [Multi-configurational self-consistent field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-configurational_self-consistent_field) and [Hartree–Fock methods](wikipedia://en/475393)), or for infinite quantum systems (e.g. in [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) and [condensed matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensed_matter)).
