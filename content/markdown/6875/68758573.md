# Jeb Willenbring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Jeb Willenbring"}

**Jeb F. Willenbring** is a [Full Professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor) and Associate Chair for Graduate Mathematics at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee).[^o3] Most of his research falls within the categories of [Representation Theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), [Discrete Mathematics](wikipedia://en/8492), and [Mathematical Physics](wikipedia://en/173416). His current research consists of several collaborations concerning [algebraic combinatorics](wikipedia://en/14835582) and various aspects of Representation Theory. Willenbring has published multiple papers concerning the nature of [Littlewood-Richardson coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littlewood-Richardson_coefficient), a particular focus of his, in collaboration with Professors [Pamela Harris](wikipedia://en/62858002), Mark Colorusso, and William Erickson.[^o4]

:::

## Education/Academic Career {#Education/Academic_Career}

Willenbring attended [North Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_University) during his undergraduate years and graduated with a bachelor's degree in 1995.[^o6] After graduating, he went on to pursue a PhD degree in mathematics from the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego). He worked with his doctoral advisor, [Nolan Russell Wallach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolan_Wallach), to produce his thesis *Stability Properties for q-Mulitplicities and Branching Formulas for Representations of the Classical Groups*.[^o7]

After receiving his PhD in mathematics in 2000, he accepted an Assistant Professor position at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee) in 2003. He was promoted to an Associate Professor position in 2008 and to a Full Professor position in 2013. During his time at the [University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin–Milwaukee), Willenbring has taught numerous classes including Advanced Calculus, Number Theory, and Graduate Algebra and supervised 9 Graduate Student theses to the point of completion.[^o8] As a legally blind professor, he is a particularly zealous advocate for students with disabilities.[^o9] As a member of his university's ADA advisory committee, Willenbring successfully advocated to bring an app for visually impaired student to campus,[^o10] making UWM the first university in its state to offer a free app for the blind and visually impaired.[^o11][^o12][^o13]

## Awards {#Awards}

Willenbring has received numerous awards and grants. After graduating from his PhD Program, he taught at [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) from 2000–2003 and was granted NSF VIGRE funding.[^o27] In 2013, Willenbring was named as a fellow of the [American Mathematics Society](wikipedia://en/198822) as part of its inaugural class of fellows.[^o28] Recognizing his work towards increasing campus accessibility, Willenbring also received a UWM Accessibility Resource Center Excellence Award in 2016.[^o29]
