# Mackey space

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mackey space"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly in [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180), a **Mackey space** is a [locally convex topological vector space](wikipedia://en/518844) *X* such that the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of *X* coincides with the [Mackey topology](wikipedia://en/1790627) τ(*X*,*X′*), the [finest topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finer_topology) which still preserves the [continuous dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual). They are named after [George Mackey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mackey).

:::

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

Examples of locally convex spaces that are Mackey spaces include:

- All [barrelled spaces](wikipedia://en/1733592) [^n1] and more generally all [infrabarreled spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrabarreled_space) [^n2]
  - Hence in particular all [bornological spaces](wikipedia://en/1804457) [^n1] and [reflexive spaces](wikipedia://en/210091)
- All [metrizable spaces](wikipedia://en/19738).[^n1]
  - In particular, all [Fréchet spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fréchet_spaces), including all [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) and specifically [Hilbert spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_spaces), are Mackey spaces.
- The product, locally convex direct sum, and the inductive limit of a family of Mackey spaces is a Mackey space.[^o8]

:::

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A locally convex space $X$ with continuous dual $X'$ is a Mackey space if and only if each convex and $\sigma(X', X)$-relatively compact subset of $X'$ is equicontinuous.
- The [completion](wikipedia://en/53695856#Completions) of a Mackey space is again a Mackey space.[^o11]
- A separated quotient of a Mackey space is again a Mackey space.
- A Mackey space need not be [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), [complete](wikipedia://en/53695856), [quasi-barrelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-barrelled_space), nor $\sigma$-quasi-barrelled.

:::
