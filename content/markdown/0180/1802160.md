# Markov chain geostatistics

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Markov chain geostatistics"}

**Markov chain geostatistics** uses [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) spatial models, [simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulation) [algorithms](wikipedia://en/775) and associated spatial [correlation](wikipedia://en/157057) measures (e.g., transiogram) based on the Markov chain random field theory, which extends a single [Markov chain](wikipedia://en/60876) into a multi-dimensional random field for [geostatistical modeling](wikipedia://en/51531). A Markov chain random field is still a single spatial Markov chain. The spatial Markov chain moves or jumps in a space and decides its state at any unobserved location through interactions with its nearest known neighbors in different directions. The data interaction process can be well explained as a local sequential Bayesian updating process within a neighborhood. Because single-step transition probability [matrices](wikipedia://en/20556859) are difficult to estimate from sparse [sample](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_%28statistics%29) data and are impractical in representing the complex spatial [heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterogeneity) of states, the **transiogram**, which is defined as a [transition probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_probability) [function](wikipedia://en/185427) over the distance lag, is proposed as the accompanying spatial measure of Markov chain random fields.

:::
