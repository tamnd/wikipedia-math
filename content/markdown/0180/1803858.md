# Gaussian random field

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian random field"}

In [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), a **Gaussian random field** (**GRF**) is a [random field](wikipedia://en/540838) involving [Gaussian probability density functions](wikipedia://en/50347) of the variables. A one-dimensional GRF is also called a [Gaussian process](wikipedia://en/302944). An important special case of a GRF is the [Gaussian free field](wikipedia://en/9200590).

:::

With regard to applications of GRFs, the initial conditions of [physical cosmology](wikipedia://en/5378) generated by [quantum mechanical fluctuations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_fluctuation) during [cosmic inflation](wikipedia://en/5382) are thought to be a GRF with a nearly [scale invariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_invariant) spectrum.[^o2]

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

One way of constructing a GRF is by assuming that the field is the sum of a large number of plane, cylindrical or spherical waves with uniformly distributed random phase. Where applicable, the [central limit theorem](wikipedia://en/39406) dictates that at any point, the sum of these individual plane-wave contributions will exhibit a Gaussian distribution. This type of GRF is completely described by its [power spectral density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_spectral_density), and hence, through the [Wiener–Khinchin theorem](wikipedia://en/3350111), by its two-point [autocorrelation function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocorrelation_function), which is related to the power spectral density through a Fourier transformation.

Suppose *f*(*x*) is the value of a GRF at a point *x* in some *D*-dimensional space. If we make a vector of the values of *f* at *N* points, *x*<sub>1</sub>, ..., *x*<sub>*N*</sub>, in the *D*-dimensional space, then the vector (*f*(*x*<sub>1</sub>), ..., *f*(*x*<sub>*N*</sub>)) will always be distributed as a multivariate Gaussian.

:::
