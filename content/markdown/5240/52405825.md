# Graded-commutative ring

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Graded-commutative ring"}

In [algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **graded-commutative ring** (also called a **skew-commutative ring**) is a [graded ring](wikipedia://en/103094) that is commutative in the graded sense; that is, [homogeneous elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homogeneous_element) *x*, *y* satisfy

:::

$$xy = (-1)^{|x||y|} yx,$$

where |*x*| and |*y*| denote the degrees of *x* and *y*.

A [commutative (non-graded) ring](wikipedia://en/61346), with trivial grading, is a basic example. For a nontrivial example, an [exterior algebra](wikipedia://en/221537) is generally not a [commutative ring](wikipedia://en/61346) but is a *graded*-commutative ring.

A [cup product](wikipedia://en/848684) on [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) satisfies the skew-commutative relation; hence, a [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) is graded-commutative. In fact, many examples of graded-commutative rings come from [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801) and [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410).
