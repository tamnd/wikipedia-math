# Samuel C. Bradford

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Samuel C. Bradford"}

**Samuel Clement Bradford** (10 January 1878 in London – 13 November 1948)[^o3] was a British mathematician, [librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Librarian) and [documentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentalist) at the [Science Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum_%28London%29) in London. He developed "[Bradford's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford's_law)" (or the "law of scattering") regarding differences in demand for scientific journals.[^o4][^o5] This work influences [bibliometrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliometrics) and [citation analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_analysis) of scientific publications. Bradford founded the British Society for International Bibliography (BSIB) (est. 1927) and he was elected president of [International Federation for Information and Documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Information_and_Documentation) (FID) in 1945. Bradford was a strong proponent of [Universal Decimal Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Decimal_Classification) and of establishing [abstracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_%28summary%29) of the [scientific literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_literature).[^o6]

:::

He became head of the [Science Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_Museum_Library) in 1925 and became Keeper of the Science Library in 1930.[^o7] He was succeeded in this role by [Ernest Lancaster-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Lancaster-Jones) in 1935.[^o8]
