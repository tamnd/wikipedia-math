# Adherent point

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Adherent point"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), an **adherent point** (also **closure point** or **point of closure** or **contact point**)[^o2] of a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $A$ of a [topological space](wikipedia://en/30450) $X,$ is a point $x$ in $X$ such that every [neighbourhood](wikipedia://en/1529485) of $x$ (or equivalently, every [open neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_neighborhood) of $x$) contains at least one point of $A.$ A point $x \in X$ is an adherent point for $A$ if and only if $x$ is in the [closure](wikipedia://en/42309) of $A,$ thus

:::

$x \in \operatorname{Cl}_X A$ if and only if for all open subsets $U \subseteq X,$ if $x \in U \text{ then } U \cap A \neq \varnothing.$

This definition differs from that of a [limit point of a set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limit_point_of_a_set), in that for a limit point it is required that every neighborhood of $x$ contains at least one point of $A$ different from $x.$ Thus every limit point is an adherent point, but the converse is not true. An adherent point of $A$ is either a limit point of $A$ or an element of $A$ (or both). An adherent point which is not a limit point is an [isolated point](wikipedia://en/390557).

Intuitively, having an open set $A$ defined as the area within (but not including) some boundary, the adherent points of $A$ are those of $A$ including the boundary.

## Examples and sufficient conditions {#Examples_and_sufficient_conditions}

If $S$ is a [non-empty](wikipedia://en/9566) subset of $\mathbb{R}$ which is bounded above, then the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) $\sup S$ is adherent to $S.$ In the [interval](wikipedia://en/49172) $(a, b],$ $a$ is an adherent point that is not in the interval, with usual [topology](wikipedia://en/30450) of $\mathbb{R}.$

A subset $S$ of a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) $M$ contains all of its adherent points if and only if $S$ is ([sequentially](wikipedia://en/4668395#Definitions)) [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) in $M.$

### Adherent points and subspaces {#Adherent_points_and_subspaces}

Suppose $x \in X$ and $S \subseteq X \subseteq Y,$ where $X$ is a [topological subspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_subspace) of $Y$ (that is, $X$ is endowed with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) induced on it by $Y$). Then $x$ is an adherent point of $S$ in $X$ if and only if $x$ is an adherent point of $S$ in $Y.$

::: {#o5 .proof cue=collapse name="Proof"}

By assumption, $S \subseteq X \subseteq Y$ and $x \in X.$ Assuming that $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_X S,$ let $V$ be a neighborhood of $x$ in $Y$ so that $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_Y S$ will follow once it is shown that $V \cap S \neq \varnothing.$ The set $U := V \cap X$ is a neighborhood of $x$ in $X$ (by definition of the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926)) so that $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_X S$ implies that $\varnothing \neq U \cap S.$ Thus $\varnothing \neq U \cap S = (V \cap X) \cap S \subseteq V \cap S,$ as desired. For the converse, assume that $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_Y S$ and let $U$ be a neighborhood of $x$ in $X$ so that $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_X S$ will follow once it is shown that $U \cap S \neq \varnothing.$ By definition of the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926), there exists a neighborhood $V$ of $x$ in $Y$ such that $U = V \cap X.$ Now $x \in \operatorname{Cl}_Y S$ implies that $\varnothing \neq V \cap S.$ From $S \subseteq X$ it follows that $S = X \cap S$ and so $\varnothing \neq V \cap S = V \cap (X \cap S) = (V \cap X) \cap S = U \cap S,$ as desired. $\blacksquare$

:::

Consequently, $x$ is an adherent point of $S$ in $X$ if and only if this is true of $x$ in every (or alternatively, in some) topological superspace of $X.$

### Adherent points and sequences {#Adherent_points_and_sequences}

If $S$ is a subset of a topological space then the [limit](wikipedia://en/285773) of a convergent sequence in $S$ does not necessarily belong to $S,$ however it is always an adherent point of $S.$ Let $\left(x_n\right)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ be such a sequence and let $x$ be its limit. Then by definition of limit, for all [neighbourhoods](wikipedia://en/1529485) $U$ of $x$ there exists $n \in \mathbb{N}$ such that $x_n \in U$ for all $n \geq N.$ In particular, $x_N \in U$ and also $x_N \in S,$ so $x$ is an adherent point of $S.$ In contrast to the previous example, the limit of a convergent sequence in $S$ is not necessarily a limit point of $S$; for example consider $S = \{ 0 \}$ as a subset of $\mathbb{R}.$ Then the only sequence in $S$ is the constant sequence $0, 0, \ldots$ whose limit is $0,$ but $0$ is not a limit point of $S;$ it is only an adherent point of $S.$
