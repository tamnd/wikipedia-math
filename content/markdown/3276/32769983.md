# Finite extensions of local fields

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Finite extensions of local fields"}

In [algebraic number theory](wikipedia://en/174705), through completion, the study of **ramification** of a [prime ideal](wikipedia://en/24928) can often be reduced to the case of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375) where a more detailed analysis can be carried out with the aid of tools such as [ramification groups](wikipedia://en/501614).

:::

In this article, a local field is non-archimedean and has finite [residue field](wikipedia://en/10789680).

## Unramified extension {#Unramified_extension}

Let $L/K$ be a [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of nonarchimedean local fields with finite residue fields $\ell/k$ and [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) $G$. Then the following are equivalent.

- (i) $L/K$ is **unramified**.
- (ii) $\mathcal{O}_L / \mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_L$ is a field, where $\mathfrak{p}$ is the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_K$.
- (iii) $[L : K] = [\ell : k]$
- (iv) The [inertia subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia_subgroup) of $G$ is trivial.
- (v) If $\pi$ is a [uniformizing element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformizing_element) of $K$, then $\pi$ is also a uniformizing element of $L$.

When $L/K$ is unramified, by (iv) (or (iii)), *G* can be identified with $\operatorname{Gal}(\ell/k)$, which is finite [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327).

The above implies that there is an [equivalence of categories](wikipedia://en/379051) between the finite unramified extensions of a local field *K* and finite [separable extensions](wikipedia://en/363340) of the residue field of *K*.

## Totally ramified extension {#Totally_ramified_extension}

Again, let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension of nonarchimedean local fields with finite residue fields $l/k$ and Galois group $G$. The following are equivalent.

- $L/K$ is **totally ramified**.
- $G$ coincides with its inertia subgroup.
- $L = K[\pi]$ where $\pi$ is a root of an [Eisenstein polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenstein_polynomial).
- The norm $N(L/K)$ contains a uniformizer of $K$.
