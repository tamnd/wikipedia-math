# Anderson impurity model

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Anderson impurity model"}

The **Anderson impurity model**, named after [Philip Warren Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Warren_Anderson), is a [Hamiltonian](wikipedia://en/14381) that is used to describe [magnetic impurities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_impurity) embedded in [metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal).[^o2] It is often applied to the description of [Kondo effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondo_effect)-type problems,[^o3] such as [heavy fermion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_fermion) systems[^o4] and [Kondo insulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondo_insulator).[^o5] In its simplest form, the model contains a term describing the kinetic energy of the conduction electrons, a two-level term with an on-site Coulomb repulsion that models the impurity energy levels, and a hybridization term that couples conduction and impurity orbitals. For a single impurity, the Hamiltonian takes the form[^o2][^o3]

:::

$$H = \sum_{k,\sigma}\epsilon_k c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + \sum_{\sigma}\epsilon_d d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}d_{\sigma} + Ud^{\dagger}_{\uparrow}d_{\uparrow}d^{\dagger}_{\downarrow}d_{\downarrow} + \sum_{k,\sigma}V_k(d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}d_{\sigma}),$$

where the $c$ operator is the [annihilation operator](wikipedia://en/701991) of a conduction electron, and $d$ is the annihilation operator for the impurity, $k$ is the conduction electron [wavevector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavevector), $\sigma$ labels the [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829). The unperturbed single-electron energy, relative to the [Fermi level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_level), is $\epsilon_k$ for the conduction electrons and $\epsilon_d$ for impurity electrons. The The on–site Coulomb repulsion is $U$, and $V$ gives the hybridization.

## Regimes {#Regimes}

The model yields several regimes that depend on the relationship of the impurity energy levels to the [Fermi level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermi_level) $E_{\rm F}$:[^o7]

- The *empty orbital* regime for $\epsilon_d \gg E_{\rm F}$, and the *full orbital* regime for $\epsilon_d+U \ll E_{\rm F}$, which have no local moment.
- The *intermediate* regime for $\epsilon_d\approx E_{\rm F}$ or $\epsilon_d+U\approx E_{\rm F}$.
- The *local moment* regime for $\epsilon_d \ll E_{\rm F} \ll \epsilon_d+U$, which yields a magnetic moment at the impurity.

In the local moment regime, the magnetic moment is present at the impurity site. However, for low enough temperature, the moment is [Kondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondo_effect) screened to give non-magnetic many-body singlet state.[^o3][^o4]

## Heavy-fermion systems {#Heavy-fermion_systems}

For heavy-fermion systems, a lattice of impurities is described by the periodic Anderson model.[^o4] The one-dimensional model is

$$H = \sum_{k,\sigma}\epsilon_k c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + \sum_{j,\sigma}\epsilon_f f^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}f_{j\sigma} + U\sum_{j}f^{\dagger}_{j\uparrow}f_{j\uparrow}f^{\dagger}_{j\downarrow}f_{j\downarrow} + \sum_{j,k,\sigma}V_{jk}(e^{ikx_j}f^{\dagger}_{j\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + e^{-ikx_j}c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}f_{j\sigma}),$$

where $x_j$ is the position of impurity site $j$, and $f$ is the impurity creation operator (used instead of $d$ by convention for heavy-fermion systems). The hybridization term allows *f*-[orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_orbital) electrons in heavy fermion systems to interact, although they are separated by a distance greater than the [Hill limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_limit_%28solid-state%29).

## Other variants {#Other_variants}

There are other variants of the Anderson model, such as the [SU(4)](wikipedia://en/173997) Anderson model, which is used to describe impurities which have an orbital, as well as a spin, degree of freedom. This is relevant in [carbon nanotube quantum dot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_nanotube_quantum_dot) systems. The SU(4) Anderson model Hamiltonian is

$$H = \sum_{k,\sigma}\epsilon_k c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + \sum_{i,\sigma}\epsilon_d d^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}d_{i\sigma} + \sum_{i,\sigma,i'\sigma '} \frac{U}{2}n_{i\sigma}n_{i'\sigma '} + \sum_{i,k,\sigma}V_k(d^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{k\sigma} + c^{\dagger}_{k\sigma}d_{i\sigma}),$$

where $i$ and *$i'$* label the orbital degree of freedom (which can take one of two values), and *$n$* represents the [number operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_operator) for the impurity.
