# Generalized Verma module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Generalized Verma module"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **generalized Verma modules** are a generalization of a (true) [Verma module](wikipedia://en/2975234),[^o2] and are objects in the [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200) of [Lie algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lie_algebras). They were studied originally by [James Lepowsky](wikipedia://en/5033025) in the 1970s. The motivation for their study is that their homomorphisms correspond to [invariant differential operators](wikipedia://en/6935363) over [generalized flag manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_flag_manifold). The study of these operators is an important part of the theory of parabolic geometries.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587) and $\mathfrak{p}$ a [parabolic subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_subalgebra) of $\mathfrak{g}$. For any [irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreducible_%28representation_theory%29) finite-dimensional [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_a_Lie_algebra) $V$ of $\mathfrak{p}$ we define the generalized Verma module to be the [relative tensor product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_tensor_product)

$$M_{\mathfrak{p}}(V):=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{p})} V.$$

The action of $\mathfrak{g}$ is left multiplication in $\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})$.

If λ is the highest weight of V, we sometimes denote the Verma module by $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$.

Note that $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ makes sense only for $\mathfrak{p}$-dominant and $\mathfrak{p}$-integral weights (see [weight](wikipedia://en/277179)) $\lambda$.

It is well known that a [parabolic subalgebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabolic_subalgebra) $\mathfrak{p}$ of $\mathfrak{g}$ determines a unique grading $\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=-k}^k \mathfrak{g}_j$ so that $\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{j \geq 0} \mathfrak{g}_j$. Let $\mathfrak{g}_-:=\oplus_{j<0} \mathfrak{g}_j$. It follows from the [Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem](wikipedia://en/762696) that, as a [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) (and even as a $\mathfrak{g}_-$-[module](wikipedia://en/276410) and as a $\mathfrak{g}_0$-module),

$$M_{\mathfrak{p}}(V)\simeq \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_-)\otimes V.$$

In further text, we will denote a generalized Verma module simply by GVM.

:::

## Properties of GVMs {#Properties_of_GVMs}

GVM's are [highest weight modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight_module) and their [highest weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight) λ is the highest weight of the representation V. If $v_\lambda$ is the highest weight vector in V, then $1\otimes v_\lambda$ is the highest weight vector in $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$.

GVM's are [weight modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_module), i.e. they are direct sum of its [weight spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight_space_%28representation_theory%29) and these weight spaces are finite-dimensional.

As all [highest weight modules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highest_weight_module), GVM's are quotients of Verma modules. The [kernel](wikipedia://en/45240) of the [projection](wikipedia://en/3909097) $M_\lambda\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ is

$$(1)\quad K_\lambda:=\sum_{\alpha\in S} M_{s_\alpha\cdot \lambda}\subset M_\lambda$$

where $S\subset\Delta$ is the set of those [simple roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_root_%28root_system%29) α such that the negative root spaces of root $-\alpha$ are in $\mathfrak{p}$ (the set S determines uniquely the subalgebra $\mathfrak{p}$), $s_\alpha$ is the [root reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_reflection) with respect to the root α and $s_\alpha\cdot \lambda$ is the [affine action](wikipedia://en/7020888) of $s_\alpha$ on λ. It follows from the theory of (true) [Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234) that $M_{s_\alpha\cdot\lambda}$ is isomorphic to a unique submodule of $M_\lambda$. In (1), we identified $M_{s_\alpha\cdot\lambda}\subset M_\lambda$. The sum in (1) is not [direct](wikipedia://en/58899).

In the special case when $S=\emptyset$, the parabolic subalgebra $\mathfrak{p}$ is the [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925) and the GVM coincides with (true) Verma module. In the other extremal case when $S=\Delta$, $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}$ and the GVM is isomorphic to the inducing representation V.

The GVM $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ is called *regular*, if its highest weight λ is on the affine Weyl orbit of a dominant weight $\tilde\lambda$. In other word, there exist an element w of the Weyl group W such that

$$\lambda=w\cdot\tilde\lambda$$

where $\cdot$ is the [affine action](wikipedia://en/7020888) of the Weyl group.

The Verma module $M_\lambda$ is called *singular*, if there is no dominant weight on the affine orbit of λ. In this case, there exists a weight $\tilde\lambda$ so that $\tilde\lambda+\delta$ is on the wall of the [fundamental Weyl chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Weyl_chamber) (δ is the sum of all [fundamental weights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_weight)).

## Homomorphisms of GVMs {#Homomorphisms_of_GVMs}

By a homomorphism of GVMs we mean $\mathfrak{g}$-homomorphism.

For any two weights $\lambda, \mu$ a [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564)

$$M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\rightarrow M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$$

may exist only if $\mu$ and $\lambda$ are linked with an [affine action](wikipedia://en/7020888) of the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) $W$ of the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$. This follows easily from the [Harish-Chandra theorem](wikipedia://en/11277644) on [infinitesimal central characters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinitesimal_central_character).

Unlike in the case of (true) [Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234), the homomorphisms of GVM's are in general not injective and the [dimension](wikipedia://en/8398)

$$dim(Hom(M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu), M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)))$$

may be larger than one in some specific cases.

If $f: M_\mu\to M_\lambda$ is a homomorphism of (true) Verma modules, $K_\mu$ resp. $K_\lambda$ is the kernels of the projection $M_\mu\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)$, resp. $M_\lambda\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$, then there exists a homomorphism $K_\mu\to K_\lambda$ and f factors to a homomorphism of generalized Verma modules $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$. Such a homomorphism (that is a factor of a homomorphism of Verma modules) is called **standard**. However, the standard homomorphism may be zero in some cases.

### Standard {#Standard}

Let us suppose that there exists a nontrivial homomorphism of true Verma modules $M_\mu \to M_\lambda$. Let $S\subset\Delta$ be the set of those [simple roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_root_%28root_system%29) α such that the negative root spaces of root $-\alpha$ are in $\mathfrak{p}$ (like in section [Properties](wikipedia://en/17114413#Properties_of_GVM's)). The following theorem is proved by [Lepowsky](wikipedia://en/5033025):[^o8]

> The standard homomorphism $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ is zero if and only if there exists $\alpha\in S$ such that $M_\mu$ is isomorphic to a submodule of $M_{s_\alpha\cdot \lambda}$ ($s_\alpha$ is the corresponding [root reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_reflection) and $\cdot$ is the [affine action](wikipedia://en/7020888)).

The structure of GVMs on the affine orbit of a $\mathfrak{g}$-dominant and $\mathfrak{g}$-integral [weight](wikipedia://en/277179) $\tilde\lambda$ can be described explicitly. If W is the [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) of $\mathfrak{g}$, there exists a subset $W^{\mathfrak{p}}\subset W$ of such elements, so that $w\in W^{\mathfrak{p}}\Leftrightarrow w(\tilde\lambda)$ is $\mathfrak{p}$-dominant. It can be shown that $W^{\mathfrak{p}}\simeq W_{\mathfrak{p}}\backslash W$ where $W_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the Weyl group of $\mathfrak{p}$ (in particular, $W^{\mathfrak{p}}$ does not depend on the choice of $\tilde\lambda$). The map $w\in W^{\mathfrak{p}} \mapsto M_{\mathfrak{p}}(w\cdot\tilde\lambda)$ is a [bijection](wikipedia://en/3942) between $W^{\mathfrak{p}}$ and the set of GVM's with highest weights on the [affine orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_orbit) of $\tilde\lambda$. Let as suppose that $\mu=w'\cdot\tilde\lambda$, $\lambda=w\cdot\tilde\lambda$ and $w\leq w'$ in the [Bruhat ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat_ordering) (otherwise, there is no homomorphism of (true) Verma modules $M_\mu\to M_\lambda$ and the standard homomorphism does not make sense, see [Homomorphisms of Verma modules](wikipedia://en/2975234#Homomorphisms_of_Verma_modules)).

The following statements follow from the above theorem and the structure of $W^{\mathfrak{p}}$:

> *Theorem.* If $w'=s_\gamma w$ for some [positive root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_root) $\gamma$ and the length (see [Bruhat ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruhat_ordering)) l(w')=l(w)+1, then there exists a nonzero standard homomorphism $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$.

> *Theorem*. The standard homomorphism $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ is zero if and only if there exists $w''\in W$ such that $w\leq w''\leq w'$ and $w''\notin W^{\mathfrak{p}}$.

However, if $\tilde\lambda$ is only dominant but not integral, there may still exist $\mathfrak{p}$-dominant and $\mathfrak{p}$-integral weights on its affine orbit.

The situation is even more complicated if the GVM's have singular character, i.e. there $\mu$ and $\lambda$ are on the affine orbit of some $\tilde\lambda$ such that $\tilde\lambda+\delta$ is on the wall of the [fundamental Weyl chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Weyl_chamber).

### Nonstandard {#Nonstandard}

A homomorphism $M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mu)\to M_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda)$ is called **nonstandard**, if it is not standard. It may happen that the standard homomorphism of GVMs is zero but there still exists a nonstandard homomorphism.
