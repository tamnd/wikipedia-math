# Marc Lackenby

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marc Lackenby"}

**Marc Lackenby** is a professor of mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford) whose research concerns [knot theory](wikipedia://en/153008), [low-dimensional topology](wikipedia://en/621774), and [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890).

:::

Lackenby studied mathematics at the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge) beginning in 1990, and earned his Ph.D. in 1997, with a dissertation on *Dehn Surgery and Unknotting Operations* supervised by [W. B. R. Lickorish](wikipedia://en/11177096). After positions as Miller Research Fellow at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) and as Research Fellow at Cambridge, he joined Oxford as a Lecturer and Fellow of [St Catherine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Catherine's_College,_Oxford) in 1999. He was promoted to Professor at Oxford in 2006.

Lackenby's research contributions include a proof of a strengthened version of the [2$\pi$ theorem](wikipedia://en/11945645) on sufficient conditions for [Dehn surgery](wikipedia://en/1240699) to produce a [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135),L00 a bound on the [hyperbolic volume](wikipedia://en/8158628) of a [knot complement](wikipedia://en/1350120) of an [alternating knot](wikipedia://en/1236143),L04 and a proof that every diagram of the [unknot](wikipedia://en/60075) can be transformed into a diagram without crossings by only a polynomial number of [Reidemeister moves](wikipedia://en/1152374).L15 In February 2021 he announced a new unknot recognition algorithm that runs in [quasi-polynomial time](wikipedia://en/405944).

Lackenby won the [Whitehead Prize](wikipedia://en/10681913) of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 2003. In 2006, he won the [Philip Leverhulme Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Leverhulme_Prize) in mathematics and statistics. He was an [invited speaker at the International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/47738065) in 2010.
