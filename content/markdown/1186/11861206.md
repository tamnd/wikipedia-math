# C. Louis Kincannon

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="C. Louis Kincannon"}

**Charles Louis Kincannon** (December 9, 1940 – December 15, 2012) was an American [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who served as the Director of the [United States Census Bureau](wikipedia://en/57070) from 2002 to 2008.[^o3] Kincannon had joined the Census Bureau in 1963.[^o4] Kincannon took the Director's office on March 13, 2002, after being nominated by President [George W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Bush) and confirmed unanimously by the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate). He served for six years until his retirement on January 3, 2008.[^o5]

:::

Kincannon was born in [Waco, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waco,_Texas), on December 12, 1940.[^o3] He earned a [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) from the [University of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas) in 1963.[^o6]

He joined the United States Census Bureau in 1963 as a statistician.[^o3] Kincannon worked at the Census Bureau for the majority of his federal career. His two exceptions were stints in the 1970s, when he joined the staff of the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget), and a tenure as the chief statistician of the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development), based in [Paris](wikipedia://en/22989) during the 1990s.[^o3]

Kincannon became the Director of the United States Census Bureau in 2002.[^o3] He is credited with expanding the [linguistic diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_diversity) of the Bureau's staff during his six-year tenure.[^o3] Kincannon purposely hired new census enumerators (or [census takers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_takers)) for the then-forthcoming [2010 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_U.S._census) from diverse ethnic and linguistic backgrounds.[^o3] He argued that employees who were familiar with different languages and ethnicities would produce a more accurate census count.[^o3]

Kincannon expressed pride of serving as Director of the Census Bureau, noting that past directors included [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson), [John Quincy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams) and [Martin Van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Van_Buren), who had overseen the census as [United States Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State).[^o3][^o6]

Kincannon died from cancer at [Washington Hospital Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Hospital_Center) in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_D.C.), on December 15, 2012, at the age of 72.[^o3][^o6] Survivors included his wife of forty-four years, Lois Claire Green Kincannon, and his two daughters, Alexandra Kincannon and Indya Kincannon, then a [Knox County, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_County,_Tennessee), [school board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_board) member, and current mayor of [Knoxville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoxville), [Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee).[^o3][^o6]
