# Václav Jeřábek

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Václav Jeřábek"}

**Václav Jeřábek** (1845–1931) was a Czech [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), specialized in constructive [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Jeřábek studied at the lower school of [Pardubice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardubice) and at the higher school of [Písek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Písek), then he was to [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866) and studied at Imperial and Royal Polytechnic Institute where he graduated. Although he participated in several leading intellectual circles of Vienna, he remained a Czech with a clear view of patriotism.[^n1] He began his teaching at the *Realschule* of [Litomyšl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litomyšl) (1870), being transferred two years after to the *Realschule* of Telč. In 1881, he was appointed professor of the *Czech Realschule* in Brno, and became its director in 1901. He retired in 1907, and suffering of a cataract, he died almost completely blind[^o5] in 1931.

Jeřábek was one of the men who kept the Czech geometry at the scientific level.[^n2] He published scientific articles in Czech, German and French, and longer lectures. He is well remembered by the [Jerabek hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerabek_hyperbola),[^n3] the locus of the isogonal conjugate of a point that traverses the [Euler line](wikipedia://en/203622) of a triangle.[^n4]

He was honorary member of the [Union of Czech mathematicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Czech_mathematicians_and_physicists) and member of the scientific societies of [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia) and [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia).[^n5]
