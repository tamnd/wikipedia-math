# Cecilia Krieger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Cecilia Krieger"}

*Image: CeciliaKrieger.jpg.* Cecilia Krieger

**Cypra Cecilia Krieger-Dunaij** (9 April 1894 – 17 August 1974) was an [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) (more specifically, [Galician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_%28Central_Europe%29))-born [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish) ancestry who lived and worked in [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada). Krieger has the distinction of being the third person in Canadian history to complete from any Canadian university a PhD in any discipline. She was the first woman PhD candidate to complete her degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831). She did it in 1930 at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).[^o2] Krieger is well known for having translated two works of [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122) in [general topology](wikipedia://en/178649). She and [Evelyn Nelson](wikipedia://en/11664159) have been commemorated since 1995 by the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829), awarded annually by the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) to recognize outstanding research by a female mathematician.[^o2]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Krieger was born on 9 April 1894 in [Jasło](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasło) in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_%28Spain%29). The town was then part of Austria-Hungary, now in modern-day [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland).[^o3] Her parents, Moses and Sarah Krieger, had two sons and two daughters besides Cecilia.[^o3] Krieger began in 1919 the study of mathematics and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) at the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna)[^o3] When the family moved to [Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto), [Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario), Canada in 1920, Krieger would complete a BA (1924) and an MA (1925) at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), followed by a PhD (1930). Her thesis, under the supervision of W.J. Webber, was entitled "On the summability of trigonometric series with localized parameters—on Fourier constants and convergence factors of double [Fourier series](wikipedia://en/59038)".[^o3]

## At the University of Toronto {#At_the_University_of_Toronto}

During her PhD programme, Krieger was appointed as an instructor (1928), until she completed the PhD when she was promoted a [lecturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecturer) (1930).[^o3] She continued as a lecturer until 1942 when she was promoted a [assistant professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_professor) teaching in the departments of engineering and mathematics.[^o3] Marriage would follow in 1953 Dr. Zygmund Dunaij.

## Retirement {#Retirement}

Krieger retired as an assistant professor in 1962[^o3] but she continued teaching there for another five years, leaving in 1968 upon the death of her husband.[^o3] She then taught for six years at [Upper Canada College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada_College), a [private school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_school) in Toronto, until her own death in 1974.[^o7]

## Publications {#Publications}

In 1934, Krieger published an English translation of Sierpinski's book *Introduction to General [Topology](wikipedia://en/29954)*. She also translated *General Topology* by Sierpinski in 1952, adding a 30-page appendix on [infinite cardinals](wikipedia://en/6173) and [ordinals](wikipedia://en/26547932).[^o3]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Krieger was a strong supporter of women in mathematics. In honour of the contributions of Krieger and [Evelyn Nelson](wikipedia://en/11664159), the [Canadian Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/906055) created the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) in 1995. It is awarded to an outstanding woman in mathematics.[^o2]
