# Roger Paman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Roger Paman"}

**Roger Paman** (c. 1700 - 1748) was an 18th-century English [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

Very little is known about the life of Roger Paman. He cited a professor of [St John's College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge) as mentor, so it is possible that he studied at [Cambridge University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_University).[^o4]

As he explains in the preface of his only published book, he participated in [George Anson's voyage around the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Anson's_voyage_around_the_world) (1740–1744), but he returned to England in 1742 with one of two ships which returned before the end of the expedition.[^o5] Before embarking on the expedition, he left his mathematical papers to philosopher [David Hartley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hartley_%28philosopher%29).[^o6]

His papers were presented to the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1742 and, as a result, he was named *fellow* of the institution in 1743.[^o4]

In 1745 his book "The Harmony of the Ancient and Modern Geometry Asserted" was published in London. The book is a reply to the mathematical concepts of [George Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Berkeley) in his book *[The Analyst](wikipedia://en/341394)* (1734).[^o7] The originality of this book lies in the development of all the calculus in finite differences, in order to avoid the paradoxes of infinity explained by Berkeley.[^o8] Paman presents in his book the concepts of *minimaius* and *maximinus*, a far antecedent of the mathematical concepts of [infimum and supremum](wikipedia://en/39382).[^o9][^o10]
