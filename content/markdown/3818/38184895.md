# Gluon field strength tensor

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gluon field strength tensor"}

In [theoretical](wikipedia://en/19594028) [particle physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particle_physics), the **gluon field strength tensor** is a second-order [tensor field](wikipedia://en/256738) characterizing the [gluon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluon) interaction between [quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark).

:::

The [strong interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong_interaction) is one of the [fundamental interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_interaction) of nature, and the [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) (QFT) to describe it is called *[quantum chromodynamics](wikipedia://en/25264)* (QCD). [Quarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quark) interact with each other by the strong force due to their [color charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge), mediated by gluons. Gluons themselves possess color charge and can mutually interact.

The gluon field strength tensor is a [rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_rank)-2 tensor field on the [spacetime](wikipedia://en/28758) with values in the [adjoint bundle](wikipedia://en/9943932) of the chromodynamical SU(3) [gauge group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_group) (see [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) for necessary definitions).

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/notation cue=heading name="Convention"}

## Convention {#Convention}

Throughout this article, Latin indices (typically $a, b, c, n$) take values 1, 2, ..., 8 for the eight gluon [color charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge), while Greek indices (typically $\alpha, \beta, \mu, \nu$) take values 0 for timelike components and 1, 2, 3 for spacelike components of [four-vectors](wikipedia://en/226424) and four-dimensional spacetime tensors. In all equations, the [summation convention](wikipedia://en/195407) is used on all color and tensor indices, unless the text explicitly states that there is no sum to be taken (e.g. "no sum").

:::

## Definition {#Definition}

Below the definitions (and most of the notation) follow K. Yagi, T. Hatsuda, Y. Miake[^o6] and Greiner, Schäfer.[^o7]

### Tensor components {#Tensor_components}

The tensor is denoted $G$, (or $F$, $\overline{F}$, or some variant), and has components defined [proportional](wikipedia://en/81863) to the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) of the quark [covariant derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauge_covariant_derivative) $D_{\mu}$:[^o7][^o9]

$$G_{\alpha\beta} = \pm \frac{1}{i g_\text{s}} [D_\alpha, D_\beta],$$

where

$$D_\mu =\partial_\mu \pm ig_\text{s} t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\mu,$$

in which

$i$ is the [imaginary unit](wikipedia://en/52358);

$g_{s}$ is the [coupling constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupling_constant#QCD_and_asymptotic_freedom) of the strong force;

$t_{a} = \lambda_{a}/2$ are the [Gell-Mann matrices](wikipedia://en/563694) $\lambda_{a}$ divided by 2;

$a$ is a color index in the [adjoint representation](wikipedia://en/302188) of [SU(3)](wikipedia://en/173997) which take values 1, 2, ..., 8 for the eight generators of the group, namely the [Gell-Mann matrices](wikipedia://en/563694);

$\mu$ is a spacetime index, 0 for timelike components and 1, 2, 3 for spacelike components;

$\mathcal{A}_\mu = t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\mu$ expresses the [gluon field](wikipedia://en/40812493), a [spin](wikipedia://en/19593829)-1 gauge field or, in differential-geometric parlance, a [connection](wikipedia://en/470632) in the SU(3) [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950);

$\mathcal{A}_\mu$ are its four (coordinate-system-dependent) components, that in a fixed gauge are 3 traceless [Hermitian matrix](wikipedia://en/189682)-valued functions, while $\mathcal{A}^a_\mu$ are 32 [real-valued functions](wikipedia://en/22284647), the four components for each of the eight four-vector fields.

Note that different authors choose different signs.

Expanding the commutator gives

$$G_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}_\beta - \partial_\beta\mathcal{A}_\alpha \pm ig_\text{s}[\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}, \mathcal{A}_{\beta}].$$

Substituting $t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\alpha = \mathcal{A}_\alpha$ and using the [commutation relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_relation) $[t_a, t_b ] = i f_{ab}{}^{c} t_c$ for the Gell-Mann matrices (with a relabeling of indices), in which $f^{abc}$ are the [structure constants](wikipedia://en/20285142) of SU(3), each of the gluon field strength components can be expressed as a [linear combination](wikipedia://en/55632) of the Gell-Mann matrices as follows:

$$\begin{aligned} G_{\alpha \beta} &= \partial_\alpha t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\beta - \partial_\beta t_a \mathcal{A}^a_\alpha \pm i g_\text{s} [t_b, t_c] \mathcal{A}^b_\alpha \mathcal{A}^c_\beta \\ &= t_a \big(\partial_\alpha \mathcal{A}^a_\beta - \partial_\beta \mathcal{A}^a_\alpha \pm i^2 f_{bc}{}^ag_\text{s} \mathcal{A}^b_\alpha \mathcal{A}^c_\beta\big) \\ &= t_a G^a_{\alpha\beta}, \\ \end{aligned}$$

so that[^o10][^o11]

$$G^a_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_\alpha \mathcal{A}^a_\beta - \partial_\beta \mathcal{A}^a_\alpha \mp g_\text{s} f^a{}_{bc} \mathcal{A}^b_\alpha \mathcal{A}^c_\beta,$$

where again $a, b, c = 1, 2, ..., 8$ are color indices. As with the gluon field, in a specific coordinate system and fixed gauge $G_{\alpha\beta}$ are 3 traceless Hermitian matrix-valued functions, while $G^{a}_{\alpha\beta}$ are real-valued functions, the components of eight four-dimensional second-order tensor fields.

### Differential forms {#Differential_forms}

The gluon color field can be described using the language of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519), specifically as an adjoint bundle-valued [curvature 2-form](wikipedia://en/489440) (note that fibers of the adjoint bundle are the **su**(3) [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944));

$$\mathbf{G} = \mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}} \mp g_\text{s}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}} \wedge \boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}},$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}$ is the gluon field, a [vector potential](wikipedia://en/422936) 1-form corresponding to $\mathbf{G}$, and $\land$ is the (antisymmetric) [wedge product](wikipedia://en/221537) of this algebra, producing the structure constants $f\,^{abc}$. The [Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274)-derivative of the field form (i.e. essentially the divergence of the field) would be zero in the absence of the "gluon terms", i.e. those $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}$ which represent the non-abelian character of the SU(3).

A more mathematically formal derivation of these same ideas (but a slightly altered setting) can be found in the article on [metric connections](wikipedia://en/4794503).

### Comparison with the electromagnetic tensor {#Comparison_with_the_electromagnetic_tensor}

This almost parallels the [electromagnetic field tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_field_tensor) (also denoted $F\,$) in [quantum electrodynamics](wikipedia://en/25268), given by the [electromagnetic four-potential](wikipedia://en/731884) $A$ describing a spin-1 [photon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon):

$$F_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_{\alpha}A_{\beta} - \partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha},$$

or in the language of differential forms,

$$\mathbf{F} = \mathrm{d}\mathbf{A}.$$

The key difference between quantum electrodynamics and quantum chromodynamics is that the gluon field strength has extra terms which lead to [self-interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-interaction) between the gluons and [asymptotic freedom](wikipedia://en/648008). This is a complication of the strong force making it inherently [non-linear](wikipedia://en/146103), contrary to the linear theory of the electromagnetic force. QCD is a [non-abelian gauge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-abelian_gauge_theory). The word *non-abelian* in [group-theoretical](wikipedia://en/41890) language means that the group operation is not [commutative](wikipedia://en/294390), making the corresponding Lie algebra non-trivial.

:::

## QCD Lagrangian density {#QCD_Lagrangian_density}

Characteristic of field theories, the dynamics of the field strength are summarized by a suitable [Lagrangian density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrangian_density) and substitution into the [Euler–Lagrange equation](wikipedia://en/294995) (for fields) obtains the [equation of motion for the field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equation_of_motion#Analogues_for_waves_and_fields). The Lagrangian density for massless quarks, bound by gluons, is[^o7]

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{tr}(G_{\alpha\beta} G^{\alpha\beta}) + \bar{\psi}(iD_\mu) \gamma^\mu\psi,$$

where "tr" denotes [trace](wikipedia://en/43270) of the 3 matrix $G_{\alpha\beta}G^{\alpha\beta}$, and $\gamma^{\mu}$ are the 4 [gamma matrices](wikipedia://en/2907966). In the fermionic term $i\bar{\psi}(iD_\mu) \gamma^\mu \psi$, both color and spinor indices are suppressed. With indices explicit, $\psi_{i,\alpha}$, where $i = 1, \ldots, 3$ are color indices, and $\alpha = 1, \ldots, 4$ are Dirac spinor indices.

## Gauge transformations {#Gauge_transformations}

In contrast to QED, the gluon field strength tensor is not gauge invariant by itself. Only the product of two contracted over all indices is gauge invariant.

## Equations of motion {#Equations_of_motion}

Treated as a classical field theory, the equations of motion for the quark fields are[^o6]

$$(i\hbar \gamma^\mu D_\mu - mc) \psi = 0,$$

which is like the [Dirac equation](wikipedia://en/39407), and the equations of motion for the gluon (gauge) fields are

$$[D_\mu, G^{\mu\nu}] = g_\text{s} j^\nu,$$

which are similar to the [Maxwell equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell_equations) (when written in tensor notation). More specifically, these are the [Yang–Mills equations](wikipedia://en/672202) for quark and gluon fields. The [color charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_charge) [four-current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-current) is the source of the gluon field strength tensor, analogous to the electromagnetic four-current as the source of the electromagnetic tensor. It is given by

$$j^\nu = t^b j_b^\nu, \quad j_b^\nu = \bar{\psi} \gamma^\nu t^b \psi,$$

which is a conserved current, since color charge is conserved. In other words, the color four-current must satisfy the [continuity equation](wikipedia://en/410793)

$$D_\nu j^\nu = 0.$$
