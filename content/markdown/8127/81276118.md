# Amir Mohammadi

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Amir Mohammadi"}

**Amir Mohammadi** is an Iranian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor at the [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley).[^o2] His research focuses on [homogeneous dynamics](wikipedia://en/9087#Construction_of_dynamical_systems), [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), and [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Mohammadi earned his [B.Sc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) from [Sharif University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_University_of_Technology) in 2002 and his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) in 2009.[^o2] He has held academic positions at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin), and [UC San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) before joining the University of California, Berkeley, in 2025.[^o2]

He was a [von Neumann Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_Fellowship) at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) during 2015–2016.[^o4]

## Research {#Research}

Mohammadi’s research is primarily in homogeneous dynamics and [ergodic theory](wikipedia://en/258986), with connections to [arithmetic groups](wikipedia://en/630741) and [Diophantine approximation](wikipedia://en/251558). He has contributed to results on orbit closures, equidistribution, and measure classification, including work on the [Oppenheim conjecture](wikipedia://en/5007342) and effective methods in homogeneous spaces.[^o6]
