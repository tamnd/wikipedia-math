# Durfee square

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Durfee square"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), a **Durfee square** is an attribute of an [integer partition](wikipedia://en/104790). A partition of *n* has a Durfee square of size *s* if *s* is the largest number such that the partition contains at least *s* parts with values ≥ *s*.[^o2] An equivalent, but more visual, definition is that the Durfee square is the largest square that is contained within a partition's [Ferrers diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrers_diagram).[^o3] The side-length of the Durfee square is known as the *rank* of the partition.[^o4]

:::

The **Durfee symbol** consists of the two partitions represented by the points to the right or below the Durfee square.

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## History {#History}

Durfee squares are named after [William Pitt Durfee](wikipedia://en/34063236), a student of English mathematician [James Joseph Sylvester](wikipedia://en/234861). In a letter to [Arthur Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034) in 1883, Sylvester wrote:[^o8]

"*Durfee's square is a great invention of the importance of which its author has no conception.*"

## Generating function {#Generating_function}

The Durfee square method leads to this [generating function](wikipedia://en/160993) for the integer partitions:

$$P(x) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{x^{k^2}}{\prod_{i=1}^k (1-x^i)^2}$$

where $x^{k^2}$ is the size of the Durfee square, and $(1-x^i)^2$ represents the two sections to the right and below a Durfee square of size *k* (being two partitions into parts of size at most *k*, equivalently partitions with at most *k* parts).[^o10]

::: {#o12 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

It is clear from the visual definition that the Durfee square of a partition and its conjugate partition have the same size. The partitions of an integer *n* contain Durfee squares with sides up to and including $\lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor$.

:::
