# Bernard Dwork

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bernard Dwork"}

**Bernard Morris Dwork** (May 27, 1923 – May 9, 1998) was an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known for his application of [*p*-adic analysis](wikipedia://en/291327) to [local zeta functions](wikipedia://en/396014), and in particular for a proof of the first part of the [Weil conjectures](wikipedia://en/244705): the rationality of the zeta function of a [variety](wikipedia://en/248808) over a [finite field](wikipedia://en/11615). The general theme of Dwork's research was *p*-adic [cohomology](wikipedia://en/194060) and *p*-adic [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309). He published two papers under the pseudonym Maurizio Boyarsky.

:::

## Career {#Career}

Dwork studied electrical engineering at the [City College of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_College_of_New_York) and [Brooklyn Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Polytechnic_Institute).[^o4] He served in the [Pacific theater of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War).[^o4]

He received his Ph.D. at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in 1954 under direction of [Emil Artin](wikipedia://en/245351) (his formal advisor was [John Tate](wikipedia://en/245933)); [Nick Katz](wikipedia://en/1451427) was one of his students.[^o7][^o5]

He spent 3 years at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) and 7 years at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) before joining [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) as a faculty member in 1964.[^o4] He became Eugene Higgins Professor of Mathematics in 1978 and became emeritus in 1993.[^o4] He was named a Professore di Chiara Fama by the Italian government and held a special chair at the [University of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Padua) from 1992 onwards.[^o4]

## Awards and honors {#Awards_and_honors}

For his proof of the first part of the Weil conjectures, Dwork received (together with [Kenkichi Iwasawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkichi_Iwasawa)) the [Cole Prize](wikipedia://en/1372177) in 1962.[^o7] He received a [Guggenheim Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship) in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Guggenheim_Fellowships_awarded_in_1964).[^o7]

## Personal life {#Personal_life}

Dwork was married to Shirley Dwork and is the father of computer scientist [Cynthia Dwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynthia_Dwork), historian [Deborah Dwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Dwork), and Andrew Dwork.[^o4]
