# Stationary set

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Stationary set"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), specifically [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553) and [model theory](wikipedia://en/19858), a **stationary set** is a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) that is not too small in the sense that it intersects all [club sets](wikipedia://en/1251559) and is analogous to a set of non-zero measure in [measure theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measure_theory). There are at least three closely related notions of stationary set, depending on whether one is looking at subsets of an [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932), or [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of something of given [cardinality](wikipedia://en/6174), or a [powerset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerset).

:::

## Classical notion {#Classical_notion}

If $\kappa$ is a [cardinal](wikipedia://en/6173) of [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) [cofinality](wikipedia://en/6693), $S \subseteq \kappa,$ and $S$ [intersects](wikipedia://en/23476429) every [club set](wikipedia://en/1251559) in $\kappa,$ then $S$ is called a **stationary set**.[^o3] If a set is not stationary, then it is called a **thin set**. This notion should not be confused with the notion of a [thin set in number theory](wikipedia://en/3143411).

If $S$ is a stationary set and $C$ is a club set, then their intersection $S \cap C$ is also stationary. This is because if $D$ is any club set, then $C \cap D$ is a club set, thus $(S \cap C) \cap D = S \cap (C \cap D)$ is nonempty. Therefore, $(S \cap C)$ must be stationary.

*See also*: [Fodor's lemma](wikipedia://en/1679022)

The restriction to uncountable cofinality is in order to avoid trivialities: Suppose $\kappa$ has countable cofinality. Then $S \subseteq \kappa$ is stationary in $\kappa$ if and only if $\kappa\setminus S$ is bounded in $\kappa$. In particular, if the cofinality of $\kappa$ is $\omega=\aleph_0$, then any two stationary subsets of $\kappa$ have stationary intersection.

This is no longer the case if the cofinality of $\kappa$ is uncountable. In fact, suppose $\kappa$ is moreover [regular](wikipedia://en/374128) and $S \subseteq \kappa$ is stationary. Then $S$ can be partitioned into $\kappa$ many disjoint stationary sets. This result is due to [Solovay](wikipedia://en/3160110). If $\kappa$ is a [successor cardinal](wikipedia://en/375210), this result is due to [Ulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaw_Ulam) and is easily shown by means of what is called an **Ulam matrix**.

[H. Friedman](wikipedia://en/3038108) has shown that for every countable successor ordinal $\beta$, every stationary subset of $\omega_1$ contains a [closed](wikipedia://en/1251559#Formal_definition) subset of order type $\beta$.

## Jech's notion {#Jech's_notion}

There is also a notion of stationary subset of $[X]^\lambda$, for $\lambda$ a cardinal and $X$ a set such that $|X|\ge\lambda$, where $[X]^\lambda$ is the set of subsets of $X$ of cardinality $\lambda$: $[X]^\lambda=\{Y\subseteq X:|Y|=\lambda\}$. This notion is due to [Thomas Jech](wikipedia://en/6207903). As before, $S\subseteq[X]^\lambda$ is stationary if and only if it meets every club, where a club subset of $[X]^\lambda$ is a set unbounded under $\subseteq$ and closed under union of chains of length at most $\lambda$. These notions are in general different, although for $X = \omega_1$ and $\lambda = \aleph_0$ they coincide in the sense that $S\subseteq[\omega_1]^\omega$ is stationary if and only if $S\cap\omega_1$ is stationary in $\omega_1$.

The appropriate version of Fodor's lemma also holds for this notion.

## Generalized notion {#Generalized_notion}

There is yet a third notion, model theoretic in nature and sometimes referred to as **generalized** stationarity. This notion is probably due to [Magidor](wikipedia://en/17115328), [Foreman](wikipedia://en/3332703) and [Shelah](wikipedia://en/331211) and has also been used prominently by [Woodin](wikipedia://en/2275071).

Now let $X$ be a nonempty set. A set $C\subseteq{\mathcal P}(X)$ is club (closed and unbounded) if and only if there is a function $F:[X]^{<\omega}\to X$ such that $C=\{z:F[[z]^{<\omega}]\subseteq z\}$. Here, $[y]^{<\omega}$ is the collection of finite subsets of $y$.

$S\subseteq{\mathcal P}(X)$ is stationary in ${\mathcal P}(X)$ if and only if it meets every club subset of ${\mathcal P}(X)$.

To see the connection with model theory, notice that if $M$ is a [structure](wikipedia://en/4055928) with [universe](wikipedia://en/296838) $X$ in a countable language and $F$ is a [Skolem function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skolem_function) for $M$, then a stationary $S$ must contain an elementary substructure of $M$. In fact, $S\subseteq{\mathcal P}(X)$ is stationary if and only if for any such structure $M$ there is an elementary substructure of $M$ that belongs to $S$.
