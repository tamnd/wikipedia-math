# Édouard Goursat

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Édouard Goursat"}

**Édouard Jean-Baptiste Goursat** (21 May 1858 – 25 November 1936) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), now remembered principally as an expositor for his *Cours d'analyse mathématique*, which appeared in the first decade of the twentieth century. It set a standard for the high-level teaching of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), especially [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759). This text was reviewed by [William Fogg Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Fogg_Osgood) for the Bulletin of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o3][^o4] This led to its translation into English by [Earle Raymond Hedrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_Raymond_Hedrick) published by Ginn and Company. Goursat also published texts on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [hypergeometric series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_series).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Edouard Goursat was born in [Lanzac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzac), [Lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lot_%28département%29). He was a graduate of the [École Normale Supérieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/École_Normale_Supérieure), where he later taught and developed his *Cours*. At that time the [topological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological) foundations of complex analysis were still not clarified, with the [Jordan curve theorem](wikipedia://en/267444) considered a challenge to [mathematical rigour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigour) (as it would remain until [L. E. J. Brouwer](wikipedia://en/172896) took in hand the approach from [combinatorial topology](wikipedia://en/363400)). Goursat's work was considered by his contemporaries, including [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599), to be exemplary in facing up to the difficulties inherent in stating the fundamental [Cauchy integral theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy_integral_theorem) properly. For that reason it is sometimes called the [Cauchy–Goursat theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy–Goursat_theorem).

## Work {#Work}

Goursat, along with [Möbius](wikipedia://en/37875), [Schläfli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schläfli), [Cayley](wikipedia://en/311034), [Riemann](wikipedia://en/41980), [Clifford](wikipedia://en/250820) and others, was one of the 19th century mathematicians who envisioned and explored a [geometry of more than three dimensions](wikipedia://en/1364622).[^o7]

He was the first to enumerate the [finite groups generated by reflections](wikipedia://en/297004) in four-dimensional space, in 1889.[^n1] The [Goursat tetrahedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goursat_tetrahedra) are the fundamental domains which generate, by repeated reflections of their faces, uniform polyhedra and their honeycombs which fill three-dimensional space. Goursat recognized that the honeycombs are *four*-dimensional Euclidean polytopes.

He derived a formula for the general displacement in four dimensions preserving the origin, which he recognized as a [double rotation](wikipedia://en/2307854#Double_rotations) in two completely orthogonal planes.[^n2]

Goursat was the first to note that the [generalized Stokes theorem](wikipedia://en/29040) can be written in the simple form

$$\int_S \omega = \int_T d \omega$$

where $\omega$ is a *p*-form in *n*-space and *S* is the *p*-dimensional boundary of the (*p* + 1)-dimensional region *T*. Goursat also used [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519) to state the [Poincaré lemma](wikipedia://en/600030) and its converse, namely, that if $\omega$ is a *p*-form, then $d\omega=0$ if and only if there is a (*p* − 1)-form $\eta$ with $d \eta=\omega$. However Goursat did not notice that the "only if" part of the result depends on the domain of $\omega$ and is not true in general. [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) himself in 1922 gave a counterexample, which provided one of the impulses in the next decade for the development of the [De Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of a [differential manifold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_manifold).
