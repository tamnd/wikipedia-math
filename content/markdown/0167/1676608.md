# Space partitioning

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Space partitioning"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), **space partitioning** is the process of dividing an entire [space](wikipedia://en/27667) (usually a [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697)) into two or more [disjoint](wikipedia://en/39359) [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) (see also [partition of a set](wikipedia://en/340240)). In other words, space partitioning divides a space into non-overlapping regions. Any point in the space can then be identified to lie in exactly one of the regions.

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Space-partitioning systems are often [hierarchical](wikipedia://en/13998), meaning that a space (or a region of space) is divided into several regions, and then the same space-partitioning system is [recursively](wikipedia://en/25407) applied to each of the regions thus created. The regions can be organized into a [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_data_structure), called a **space-partitioning tree**.

Most space-partitioning systems use [planes](wikipedia://en/19469852) (or, in higher dimensions, [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862)) to divide space: points on one side of the plane form one region, and points on the other side form another. Points exactly on the plane are usually arbitrarily assigned to one or the other side. Recursively partitioning space using planes in this way produces a [BSP tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSP_tree), one of the most common forms of space partitioning.

::: {#o4 .application cue=heading name="Uses"}

## Uses {#Uses}

### In computer graphics {#In_computer_graphics}

Space partitioning is particularly important in [computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_graphics), especially heavily used in [ray tracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_tracing_%28graphics%29), where it is frequently used to organize the objects in a virtual scene. A typical scene may contain millions of polygons. Performing a ray/polygon [intersection test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_test) with each would be a very computationally expensive task.

Storing objects in a space-partitioning [data structure](wikipedia://en/8519) ([*k*-d tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-d_tree) or [BSP tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSP_tree) for example) makes it easy and fast to perform certain kinds of geometry queries—for example in determining whether a ray intersects an object, space partitioning can reduce the number of intersection test to just a few per primary ray, yielding a logarithmic [time complexity](wikipedia://en/405944) with respect to the number of polygons.[^o6][^o7][^o8]

Space partitioning is also often used in scanline algorithms to eliminate the polygons out of the camera's [viewing frustum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viewing_frustum), limiting the number of polygons processed by the pipeline. There is also a usage in [collision detection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_detection): determining whether two objects are close to each other can be much faster using space partitioning.

### In integrated circuit design {#In_integrated_circuit_design}

In [integrated circuit design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_circuit_design), an important step is [design rule check](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_rule_check). This step ensures that the completed design is manufacturable. The check involves rules that specify widths and spacings and other geometry patterns. A modern design can have billions of polygons that represent wires and transistors. Efficient checking relies heavily on geometry query. For example, a rule may specify that any polygon must be at least *n* nanometers from any other polygon. This is converted into a geometry query by enlarging a polygon by *n/2* at all sides and query to find all intersecting polygons.

### In probability and statistical learning theory {#In_probability_and_statistical_learning_theory}

The number of components in a space partition plays a central role in some results in probability theory. See [Growth function](wikipedia://en/52651586) for more details.

### In geography and GIS {#In_geography_and_GIS}

There are many studies and applications where Geographical Spatial Reality is partitioned by [hydrological criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin), [administrative criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division), [mathematical criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_global_grid) or many others.

In the context of [cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartography) and [GIS - Geographic Information System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_system), is common to identify cells of the partition by [standard codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocode). For example the for [HUC code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrological_code) identifying hydrographical basins and sub-basins, [ISO 3166-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-2) codes identifying countries and its subdivisions, or arbitrary [DGGs - discrete global grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_global_grid) identifying quadrants or locations.

## Number of components {#Number_of_components}

Suppose the n-dimensional [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) is partitioned by $r$ [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862) that are $(n-1)$-dimensional. What is the number of components in the partition? The largest number of components is attained when the hyperplanes are in [general position](wikipedia://en/383736), i.e., no two are parallel and no three have the same intersection. Denote this maximum number of components by $Comp(n,r)$. Then, the following recurrence relation holds: [^o14] [^o15]

$$Comp(n,r) = Comp(n, r-1) + Comp(n-1,r-1)$$

$Comp(0,r) = 1$ - when there are no dimensions, there is a single point.

$Comp(n,0) = 1$ - when there are no hyperplanes, all the space is a single component.

And its solution is:

$Comp(n,r) = \sum_{k=0}^n {r\choose k}$ if $r\geq n$

$Comp(n,r) = 2^r$ if $r\leq n$

(consider e.g. $r$ perpendicular hyperplanes; each additional hyperplane divides each existing component to 2).

which is upper-bounded as:

$$Comp(n,r) \leq r^n+1$$
