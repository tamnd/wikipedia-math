# Lehmer matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lehmer matrix"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [matrix theory](wikipedia://en/20556859), the *n×n* **Lehmer matrix** (named after [Derrick Henry Lehmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Henry_Lehmer)) is the constant [symmetric matrix](wikipedia://en/126474) defined by

:::

$$A_{ij} = \begin{cases} i/j, & j\ge i \\ j/i, & j<i. \end{cases}$$

Alternatively, this may be written as

$$A_{ij} = \frac{\mbox{min}(i,j)}{\mbox{max}(i,j)}.$$

::: {#o3 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

As can be seen in the examples section, if *A* is an *n×n* Lehmer matrix and *B* is an *m×m* Lehmer matrix, then *A* is a [submatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submatrix) of *B* whenever *m*>*n*. The values of elements diminish toward zero away from the diagonal, where all elements have value 1.

The [inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_inverse) of a Lehmer matrix is a [tridiagonal matrix](wikipedia://en/519218), where the [superdiagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superdiagonal) and [subdiagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdiagonal) have strictly negative entries. Consider again the *n×n* *A* and *m×m* *B* Lehmer matrices, where *m*>*n*. A rather peculiar property of their inverses is that *A<sup>−1</sup>* is *nearly* a submatrix of *B<sup>−1</sup>*, except for the *A<sup>−1</sup><sub>n,n</sub>* element, which is not equal to *B<sup>−1</sup><sub>n,n</sub>*.

A Lehmer matrix of order *n* has [trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_of_a_matrix) *n*.

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

The 2×2, 3×3 and 4×4 Lehmer matrices and their inverses are shown below.

$$\begin{array}{lllll} A_2=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1/2 \\ 1/2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}; & A_2^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 4/3 & -2/3 \\ -2/3 & {\color{Brown}{\mathbf{4/3}}} \end{pmatrix}; \\ \\ A_3=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1/2 & 1/3 \\ 1/2 & 1 & 2/3 \\ 1/3 & 2/3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}; & A_3^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 4/3 & -2/3 & \\ -2/3 & 32/15 & -6/5 \\ & -6/5 & {\color{Brown}{\mathbf{9/5}}} \end{pmatrix}; \\ \\ A_4=\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1/2 & 1/3 & 1/4 \\ 1/2 & 1 & 2/3 & 1/2 \\ 1/3 & 2/3 & 1 & 3/4 \\ 1/4 & 1/2 & 3/4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}; & A_4^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix} 4/3 & -2/3 & & \\ -2/3 & 32/15 & -6/5 & \\ & -6/5 & 108/35 & -12/7 \\ & & -12/7 & {\color{Brown}{\mathbf{16/7}}} \end{pmatrix}. \\ \end{array}$$

:::
