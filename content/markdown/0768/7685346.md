# Mycielskian

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mycielskian"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) area of [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Mycielskian** or **Mycielski graph** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is a larger graph formed from it by a construction of (txt). The construction preserves the property of being [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996) but increases the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number); by applying the construction repeatedly to a triangle-free starting graph, Mycielski showed that there exist triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number.

:::

::: {#o4 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

*Image: Grötzsch graph as a Mycielskian.svg.* Mycielskian construction applied to a 5-[cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962), producing the [Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783) with 11 vertices and 20 edges, the smallest triangle-free 4-chromatic graph (Chvátal 1974).

Let the *n* vertices of the given graph *G* be *v*<sub>1</sub>, *v*<sub>2</sub>, . . . , *v*<sub>n</sub>. The Mycielski graph μ(*G*) contains *G* itself as a subgraph, together with *n*+1 additional vertices: a vertex *u*<sub>*i*</sub> corresponding to each vertex *v*<sub>*i*</sub> of *G*, and an extra vertex *w*. Each vertex *u*<sub>*i*</sub> is connected by an edge to *w*, so that these vertices form a subgraph in the form of a star *K*<sub>1,*n*</sub>. In addition, for each edge *v*<sub>*i*</sub>*v*<sub>*j*</sub> of *G*, the Mycielski graph includes two edges, *u*<sub>*i*</sub>*v*<sub>*j*</sub> and *v*<sub>*i*</sub>*u*<sub>*j*</sub>.

Thus, if *G* has *n* vertices and *m* edges, μ(*G*) has 2*n*+1 vertices and 3*m*+*n* edges.

The only new triangles in μ(*G*) are of the form *v*<sub>*i*</sub>*v*<sub>*j*</sub>*u*<sub>*k*</sub>, where *v*<sub>*i*</sub>*v*<sub>*j*</sub>*v*<sub>*k*</sub> is a triangle in *G*. Thus, if *G* is triangle-free, so is μ(*G*).

To see that the construction increases the chromatic number $\chi(G)=k$, consider a proper *k*-coloring of $\mu(G){-}\{w\}$; that is, a mapping $c : \{v_1,\ldots,v_n,u_1,\ldots,u_n\}\to \{1,2,\ldots,k\}$ with $c(x)\neq c(y)$ for adjacent vertices *x*,*y*. If we had $c(u_i)\in \{1,2,\ldots,k{-}1\}$ for all *i*, then we could define a proper (*k*−1)-coloring of *G* by $c'\!(v_i) = c(u_i)$ when $c(v_i) = k$, and $c'\!(v_i) = c(v_i)$ otherwise. But this is impossible for $\chi(G)=k$, so *c* must use all *k* colors for $\{u_1,\ldots,u_n\}$, and any proper coloring of the last vertex *w* must use an extra color. That is, $\chi(\mu(G))=k{+}1$.

:::

## Iterated Mycielskians {#Iterated_Mycielskians}

*Image: Mycielski graphs.svg.* *M*<sub>2</sub>, *M*<sub>3</sub> and *M*<sub>4</sub> Mycielski graphs

Applying the Mycielskian repeatedly, starting with the one-edge graph, produces a sequence of graphs *M*<sub>*i*</sub> = μ(*M*<sub>*i*−1</sub>), sometimes called the Mycielski graphs. The first few graphs in this sequence are the graph *M*<sub>2</sub> = *K*<sub>2</sub> with two vertices connected by an edge, the [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) *M*<sub>3</sub> = *C*<sub>5</sub>, and the [Grötzsch graph](wikipedia://en/7685783) *M*<sub>4</sub> with 11 vertices and 20 edges.

In general, the graph *M*<sub>*i*</sub> is [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996), (*i*−1)-[vertex-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-vertex-connected_graph), and *i*-[chromatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number). The number of vertices in *M*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* ≥ 2 is 3 × 2<sup>*i*−2</sup> − 1 , while the number of edges for *i ≥ 2* is $\frac{1}{2}\left(7 \times 3^{i-2} + 1\right) - 3 \times 2^{i-2}$, which begins as:

1, 5, 20, 71, 236, 755, 2360, 7271, 22196, 67355, ... .

## Properties {#Properties}

*Image: Mycielski graph k4 hamiltonian path.svg.* Hamiltonian cycle in *M*<sub>4</sub> (Grötzsch graph)

- If *G* has [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) *k*, then μ(*G*) has chromatic number *k* + 1 (Mycielski 1955).
- If *G* is [triangle-free](wikipedia://en/7667996), then so is μ(*G*) (Mycielski 1955).
- More generally, if *G* has [clique number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clique_number) ω(*G*), then μ(*G*) has clique number of the maximum among 2 and ω(*G*). (Mycielski 1955)
- If *G* is a [factor-critical graph](wikipedia://en/7684634), then so is μ(*G*) (Došlić 2005). In particular, every graph *M*<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* ≥ 2 is factor-critical.
- If *G* has a [Hamiltonian cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamiltonian_cycle), then so does μ(*G*) (Fisher McKenna Boyer 1998).
- If *G* has [domination number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domination_number) γ(*G*), then μ(*G*) has domination number γ(*G*)+1 (Fisher McKenna Boyer 1998).

:::

## Cones over graphs {#Cones_over_graphs}

*Image: Generalized Mycielskian.svg.* A generalized Mycielskian, formed as a cone over the 5-cycle, Δ<sub>3</sub>(C<sub>5</sub>) = Δ<sub>3</sub>(Δ<sub>2</sub>(*K*<sub>2</sub>)).

A generalization of the Mycielskian, called a cone over a graph, was introduced by (Stiebitz 1985) and further studied by (Tardif 2001) and (Lin Wu Lam Gu 2006). In this construction, one forms a graph $\Delta_i(G)$ from a given graph *G* by taking the [tensor product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_product_of_graphs) *G* × *H*, where *H* is a path of length *i* with a self-loop at one end, and then collapsing into a single supervertex all of the vertices associated with the vertex of *H* at the non-loop end of the path. The Mycielskian itself can be formed in this way as μ(*G*) = Δ<sub>2</sub>(*G*).

While the cone construction does not always increase the chromatic number, (Stiebitz 1985) proved that it does so when applied iteratively to *K*<sub>2</sub>. That is, define a sequence of families of graphs, called generalized Mycielskians, as

ℳ(2) = {*K*<sub>2</sub>} and ℳ(*k*+1) = {$\Delta_i(G)$ | *G* ∈ ℳ(*k*), i ∈ $\mathbb{N}$}.

For example, ℳ(3) is the family of odd cycles. Then each graph in ℳ(*k*) is *k*-chromatic. The proof uses methods of [topological combinatorics](wikipedia://en/17633579) developed by [László Lovász](wikipedia://en/1623938) to compute the chromatic number of [Kneser graphs](wikipedia://en/2724154). The triangle-free property is then strengthened as follows: if one only applies the cone construction Δ<sub>*i*</sub> for *i* ≥ *r*, then the resulting graph has [odd girth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_girth) at least 2*r* + 1, that is, it contains no odd cycles of length less than 2*r* + 1. Thus generalized Mycielskians provide a simple construction of graphs with high chromatic number and high odd girth.
