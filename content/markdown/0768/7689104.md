# Leonidas Alaoglu

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Leonidas Alaoglu"}

**Leonidas** (*Leon*) **Alaoglu** (Λεωνίδας Αλάογλου; 1914–1981)[^o6] was a Canadian-American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [operations researcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_researcher). During his six-year stint as a mathematician from 1938 to 1944, Alaoglu studied several topics, including [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), and the geometry of [polyhedra](wikipedia://en/23470). His best known result, which he proved during this period, was [Alaoglu's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaoglu's_theorem) on the [weak-star](wikipedia://en/33662) compactness of the closed [unit ball](wikipedia://en/27033090) in the [dual](wikipedia://en/7988) of a [normed space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_space).[^o3] After 1944, he left academia for the world of operations research.

:::

## Life and work {#Life_and_work}

### Early life (1914–1938) {#Early_life_(1914–1938)}

Alaoglu was born in 1914 in [Red Deer, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Deer,_Alberta), Canada, to [Greek Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Canadians) parents. He studied mathematics at the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta).[^o6]

### Education and mathematical career (1938–1944) {#Education_and_mathematical_career_(1938–1944)}

In 1938, Alaoglu received his PhD from the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o8] His dissertation was on *Weak topologies of normed linear spaces* and establishes [Alaoglu's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaoglu's_theorem).[^o17] He went on to spend one year at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University),[^o9] then went on to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) between 1939 and 1941 and to [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) between 1942 and 1944.[^o6][^o9][^o17]

### Operations research career (1944–1981) {#Operations_research_career_(1944–1981)}

In 1944, in the midst of World War II, Alaoglu left academia to become an [operations analyst](wikipedia://en/43476) for the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force).[^o9] In 1946, he gained [U.S. citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_United_States).[^o6]

On August 21, 1947, Alaoglu married teacher Cleo Alaoglu (1915–2016).[^o11] The couple would go on to have three children, raising them in the [Encino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encino,_Los_Angeles) district of Los Angeles as well as in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C.)[^o11]

In 1952, Anaoglu attended the founding meeting of the [Operations Research Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operations_Research_Society_of_America).[^o12] In 1953, he joined the Operations Research Division of the [Lockheed Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Corporation) as a mathematician, where he worked ever since, until he eventually died in 1981.[^o6]

### Legacy {#Legacy}

Beginning in 1983, [Caltech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) instituted the annual "Leonidas Alaoglu Memorial Lecture in Mathematics" in Alaoglu's honor.[^o14]

## Research {#Research}

### Topology and analysis {#Topology_and_analysis}

In 1938, Alaoglu proved in his PhD thesis that, in the [dual space](wikipedia://en/7988) of a [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) under the [weak-star topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak-star_topology), the [closed unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_unit_ball) is [compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28mathematics%29).[^o17] His thesis was at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) with [Lawrence M. Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_M._Graves).[^o17]

In 1940, Alaoglu gave a general theory of [weak convergence](wikipedia://en/2119266) in a [normed linear space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normed_linear_space) in his PhD thesis using the notion of a [directed set](wikipedia://en/9165).[^o18] In particular, he constructed a [universal Banach space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Banach_space), and also described how to integrate and differentiate functions which take values in an [adjoint space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_space).[^o18]

In 1940, Alaoglu and [Garrett Birkhoff](wikipedia://en/1180916) proved two [ergodic theorems](wikipedia://en/258986) (i.e., statements that sums of the form $\sum_g \lambda_g xT_g$ for some [group](wikipedia://en/19447) or [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) *G* of linear operators $T_g$ on a Banach space *E* converge). The first one covers the case when $|xT_g|\leq |x|$ and *E* is [uniformly convex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformly_convex).[^o19] The second covers the case when the group is an "[ergodic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergodic_group)", in the sense that there is an infinite series of [measures](wikipedia://en/19873) on the group that is asymptotically invariant under both left-multiplication and right-multiplication.[^o19] (This class includes *n*-parameter [abelian groups](wikipedia://en/2974) and all [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945) which correspond to a [nilpotent Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/3358867).)[^o19]

### Number theory {#Number_theory}

In 1944, Alaoglu and [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284) studied the prime factorizations of [superabundant numbers](wikipedia://en/1759187) and [highly composite numbers](wikipedia://en/208732). In particular, for a highly [abundant number](wikipedia://en/321831) $n = \prod_i p^{k_p}$, they gave the estimate $\log(1 + 1/k_p) > \log q\log 2/\log p + O(\delta)$, where the error term behaves as $(\log\log p)^3/(\log p)^3$ for small *q* and $1/q^{1 - \theta}\log p$ for larger *q*.[^o21] In doing so, they made use of [Albert Ingham](wikipedia://en/5380138) and [Guido Hoheisel](wikipedia://en/5384175)'s result that the density of the prime numbers is the same in intervals $(q, q + cq^{\theta})$ for some $\theta < 1$.[^o21] The same year, Alaoglu and Erdős discussed a 1932 conjecture of [Paul Poulet](wikipedia://en/40157186) that iterating the function $\phi(\sigma(n))$ where $\phi$ is the [totient function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totient_function) and $\sigma$ is the [sum-of-divisors function](wikipedia://en/407249) eventually leads to a cycle. Using tables originally provided by [James Whitbread Lee Glaisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whitbread_Lee_Glaisher), they verified the conjecture up to $n < 10,000$.[^o22]

### Geometry {#Geometry}

In 1946, Alaoglu and [John H. Giese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_H._Giese) constructed [uniform](wikipedia://en/2537223), [isohedral](wikipedia://en/6286120) polyhedra that were topologically equivalent to a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800).[^o24]
