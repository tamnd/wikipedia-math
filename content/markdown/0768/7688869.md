# Geoffrey Watson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Geoffrey Watson"}

**Geoffrey Stuart Watson** (3 December 1921 – 3 January 1998) was an Australian [statistician](wikipedia://en/26685).

:::

Watson was born in [Bendigo, Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendigo,_Victoria) in 1921. He studied at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), and received his PhD at the [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) in 1951. After taking positions at the [University of Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Melbourne), the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) and [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University), he became chair of the Department of Statistics of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) in 1970. He remained there until his death.

Watson developed the [Durbin–Watson statistic](wikipedia://en/4864529) for detecting [autocorrelation](wikipedia://en/2724) with [James Durbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Durbin) of the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) in 1950. Watson was especially interested in applications of statistics. He used statistical methods to support the theory of [continental drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift). He estimated the size of the [penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin) population in [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica), and the effect of repealing the [motorcycle helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_helmet) law in the United States.

In 1966, he was elected as a [Fellow of the American Statistical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow_of_the_American_Statistical_Association).[^o3]

He is sometimes confused with the mathematician [G. L. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Leo_Watson), who worked on quadratic forms, and [G. N. Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._N._Watson), a mathematical analyst.
