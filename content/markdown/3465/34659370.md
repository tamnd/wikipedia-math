# 1 − 1 + 2 − 6 + 24 − 120 + ⋯

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="1 − 1 + 2 − 6 + 24 − 120 + ⋯"}

In mathematics,

:::

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k k!$$

is a [divergent series](wikipedia://en/876428), first considered by [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), that sums the [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) with alternating signs. Despite being divergent, it can be assigned a value of approximately 0.596347 by [Borel summation](wikipedia://en/2357705).

## Euler and Borel summation {#Euler_and_Borel_summation}

This series was first considered by [Euler](wikipedia://en/17902), who applied [summability methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summability_method) to assign a finite value to the series.[^o3] The series is a sum of [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) that are alternately added or subtracted. One way to assign a value to this divergent series is by using [Borel summation](wikipedia://en/2357705), where one formally writes

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k k! = \sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k \int_0^\infty x^k e^{-x} \, dx.$$

If summation and integration are interchanged (ignoring that neither side converges), one obtains:

$$\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k k! = \int_0^\infty \left[\sum_{k=0}^\infty (-x)^k \right]e^{-x} \, dx.$$

The summation in the square brackets converges when $|x| < 1$, and for those values equals $\tfrac{1}{1+x}$. The [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of $\tfrac{1}{1+x}$ to all positive real $x$ leads to a convergent integral for the summation:

$$\begin{aligned} \sum_{k=0}^\infty (-1)^k k! & = \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-x}}{1+x} \, dx \\[4pt] & = e E_1 (1) \approx 0.596\,347\,362\,323\,194\,074\,341\,078\,499\,369\ldots \end{aligned}$$

where *E*<sub>1</sub>(*z*) is the [exponential integral](wikipedia://en/648614). This is by definition the [Borel sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sum) of the series, and is equal to the [Gompertz constant](wikipedia://en/42809296).

## Connection to differential equations {#Connection_to_differential_equations}

Consider the coupled system of differential equations

$$\dot{x}(t) = x(t) - y(t),\qquad \dot{y}(t)=-y(t)^{2}$$

where dots denote derivatives with respect to *t*.

The solution with stable equilibrium at (*x*,*y*) = (0,0) as *t* → ∞ has *y*(*t*) = $\tfrac{1}{t}$, and substituting it into the first equation gives a [formal series solution](wikipedia://en/1151206)

$$x(t) = \sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{(n-1)!}{t^{n}}$$

Observe *x*(1) is precisely Euler's series.

On the other hand, the system of differential equations has a solution

$$x(t) = e^{t}\int^{\infty}_{t}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,du.$$

By successively [integrating by parts](wikipedia://en/147252), the formal power series is recovered as an [asymptotic approximation](wikipedia://en/642090) to this expression for *x*(*t*). Euler argues (more or less) that since the formal series and the integral both describe the same solution to the differential equations, they should equal each other at $t=1$, giving

$$\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(-1)^{n+1}(n-1)! = e\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,du.$$
