# Nicole Tomczak-Jaegermann

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Nicole Tomczak-Jaegermann"}

**Nicole Tomczak-Jaegermann** [FRSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada) (8 June 1945 – 17 June 2022) was a [Polish-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Canadians) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), a professor of mathematics at the [University of Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Alberta), and the holder of the [Canada Research Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Research_Chair) in Geometric Analysis.[^o4]

:::

## Contributions {#Contributions}

Her research is in [geometric](wikipedia://en/4325474) [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180),[^o4] and is unusual in combining [asymptotic analysis](wikipedia://en/641995) with the theory of [Banach spaces](wikipedia://en/3989) and infinite-dimensional [convex bodies](wikipedia://en/8652020). It formed a key component of [Fields medalist](wikipedia://en/10859) [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715)' solution to [Stefan Banach](wikipedia://en/29646)'s homogeneous space problem, posed in 1932.[^o6] Her 1989 monograph on [Banach–Mazur distances](wikipedia://en/20021256) is also highly cited.[^o7]

## Education and career {#Education_and_career}

Tomczak-Jaegermann earned her M.S. in 1968 from the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw),[^o6] and her Ph.D. from the same university in 1974, under the supervision of [Aleksander Pełczyński](wikipedia://en/56285756).[^o9] She remained on the faculty at the University of Warsaw from 1975 until 1983, when she moved to Alberta.[^o6]

## Recognition {#Recognition}

In 1996, Tomczak-Jaegermann was elected to the [Royal Society of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Canada),[^o11] and in 1999 she won the [Krieger–Nelson Prize](wikipedia://en/11851829) for an outstanding female Canadian mathematician.[^o6] In 1998 she was an Invited Speaker of the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o12] She was the winner of the 2006 [CRM-Fields-PIMS prize](wikipedia://en/19284033) for exceptional research in mathematics.[^o6]

## Death {#Death}

Tomczak-Jaegermann died on 17 June 2022 at the age 77 in [Edmonton, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton,_Alberta), Canada.[^o14]
