# Annibale Giordano

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Annibale Giordano"}

**Annibale Giuseppe Nicolò Giordano** ([Ottaviano - San Giuseppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Giuseppe_Vesuviano), 20 November 1769 – [Troyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troyes), 13 March 1835) was an Italian-French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [revolutionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary).

:::

## Life {#Life}

Annibale Giordano was born 20 September 1769 in Ottaviano – [San Giuseppe Vesuviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Giuseppe_Vesuviano), to an educated middle-class family. His father Michele was a doctor who served both the king [Ferdinand I of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_the_Two_Sicilies), and the Medici [princes of Ottaviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princes_of_Ottajano). As a teenager, Annibale Giordano attended the school of [Nicolò Fergola](wikipedia://en/47472521), a brilliant mathematician from Naples.[^o3] In 1789, the year of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), he was appointed professor at the [Nunziatella Military School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunziatella_Military_School), thus becoming a colleague of the chemist Carlo Lauberg, a [freemason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry).[^o4] In 1790, Giordano and Lauberg established an *Accademia di chimica e matematica* in Naples, which became a club for Neapolitan progressives and Freemasons; among the members were [Mario Pagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Pagano), Emanuele De Deo, [Francesco Lomonaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Lomonaco), Vincenzo De Filippis and [Luigi de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_de'_Medici) di Ottajano, then regent of the *Gran Corte della Vicaria* court. In 1792 Giordano and Lauberg wrote the *Principi analitici delle Matematiche*, in which they theorized the political commitment of mathematicians;[^o5] this essay was Annibale Giordano's last scientific work.

In December 1792, Giordano was one of the scholars who met the French admiral [Latouche-Tréville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-René-Madeleine_de_Latouche-Tréville); starting from those meetings, a conspiracy began, sketched in the birth in August 1793 of the *Società Patriottica Napoletana*, a [Jacobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobin_%28politics%29) association, but structured on the model of [Masonic lodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge), with a hierarchy such that some secrets were known only by high-ranking members.[^o6] In February 1794, the *Società Patriottica Napoletana* split into two *clubs*. The *ROMO* (an acronym for *Repubblica o Morte*, i.e. "Republic or Death" was more radical and led by Andrea Vitaliani, among whose members were also Emanuele De Deo, Vincenzo Galiani and Vincenzo Vitaliani). The *LOMO* (acronym for "Libertà o Morte", i.e. "Freedom or Death"), was more moderate and willing to accept a [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy), and was led by [Rocco Lentini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Lentini), and joined by Annibale Giordano).

On 21 March 1794, authorities discovered the organization through a report by a certain Donato Froncillo; in the subsequent trial, some adherents of the *ROMO* (De Deo, Galiani and Vincenzo Vitaliani) were sentenced to death and executed, while Giordano was sentenced to twenty years[^o6] and transferred to the [Forte spagnolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forte_Spagnolo,_L'Aquila) prison. Many sources state that Annibale Giordano told investigators the secrets of the *Società Patriottica Napoletana*[^o7] and that he gave the names of over 250 members,[^o8] including [Luigi de' Medici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_de'_Medici), who was incarcerated.[^o16]

Back in Naples together with the general [Championnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Étienne_Championnet) on 5 December 1798, a few days after being released from [L'Aquila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Aquila), Annibale Giordano actively joined the short-lived [Neapolitan Republic of 1799](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_Republic_of_1799) as a member of the military committee and then head of the Navy's accounting service. When the Republic fell (in June 1799), he was again imprisoned by the [Bourbon king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbons) in [Castel Nuovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Nuovo) together with eighteen other revolutionaries including [Mario Pagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Pagano), [Domenico Cirillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Cirillo) and Giuseppe Leonardo Albanese. On 27 January 1800, he was sentenced to death by the junta; but the sentence was commuted to captivity on [Favignana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favignana) island; in July 1801, he left the island together with other political prisoners thanks to the [Treaty of Lunéville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lunéville). The non-execution was explained by many as a reward for Giordano's denunciation;[^o7] others state that it was due to intercession by his father or [Fergola](wikipedia://en/47472521) at the Bourbon court.[^o9] Giordano fled to France where he worked as [cadastral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadastral_surveying) [surveyor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveyor) in the French department of [Aube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aube); in 1824, he became a naturalized French citizen and changed his surname to Jourdan.

## Mathematical advancements {#Mathematical_advancements}

In 1786, Giordano already presented to the *Royal Academy of Sciences of Naples* a memoir entitled *Continuazione del medesimo argomento*,[^o11] which opened the doors of the Academy to him. Shortly thereafter, in 1788, he became famous for solving the following problem: "Given a circle and *n* points of its plane, inscribe in this circle a polygon whose sides, possibly prolonged, pass, according to a certain order, through the given points";[^o12] this problem was a generalization of the "problem of [Pappus](wikipedia://en/509300)", which had been already solved for the case of *n*=3 aligned points,[^o13] and the "problem of [Castillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Castillon)", solved by the latter in 1776, proposed to him by [Cramer](wikipedia://en/499331), for *n*=3 points but still arranged in the plane.[^o14] [Carnot](wikipedia://en/26532764) thought that "Ottajano", the birthplace of Giordano, was a noble predicate rather than a town, and he called the young mathematician "Ottajano" in his publications;[^o15] after this, he began to be referred to as "Ottajano" in subsequent scientific publications.[^o16]
