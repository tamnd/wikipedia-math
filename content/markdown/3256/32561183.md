# Demazure module

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Demazure module"}

In mathematics, a **Demazure module**, introduced by (txt), is a [submodule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submodule) of a finite-dimensional representation generated by an extremal [weight](wikipedia://en/277179) space under the action of a [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925). The **Demazure character formula**, introduced by (txt, theorem 2), gives the characters of Demazure modules, and is a generalization of the [Weyl character formula](wikipedia://en/5011916). The dimension of a Demazure module is a polynomial in the highest weight, called a **Demazure polynomial**.

:::

## Demazure modules {#Demazure_modules}

Suppose that *g* is a complex [semisimple Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/1337587), with a [Borel subalgebra](wikipedia://en/62074925) *b* containing a [Cartan subalgebra](wikipedia://en/1336000) *h*. An irreducible finite-dimensional representation *V* of *g* splits as a sum of eigenspaces of *h*, and the highest weight space is 1-dimensional and is an eigenspace of *b*. The [Weyl group](wikipedia://en/296332) *W* acts on the weights of *V*, and the conjugates *w*λ of the highest weight vector λ under this action are the extremal weights, whose weight spaces are all 1-dimensional.

A Demazure module is the *b*-submodule of *V* generated by the weight space of an extremal vector *w*λ, so the Demazure submodules of *V* are parametrized by the Weyl group *W*.

There are two extreme cases: if *w* is trivial the Demazure module is just 1-dimensional, and if *w* is the element of maximal length of *W* then the Demazure module is the whole of the irreducible representation *V*.

Demazure modules can be defined in a similar way for highest weight representations of [Kac–Moody algebras](wikipedia://en/1146292), except that one now has 2 cases as one can consider the submodules generated by either the Borel subalgebra *b* or its opposite subalgebra. In the finite-dimensional these are exchanged by the longest element of the Weyl group, but this is no longer the case in infinite dimensions as there is no longest element.

## Demazure character formula {#Demazure_character_formula}

### History {#History}

The Demazure character formula was introduced by (Demazure 1974b, theorem 2). [Victor Kac](wikipedia://en/4697697) pointed out that Demazure's proof has a serious gap, as it depends on (Demazure 1974a, Proposition 11, section 2), which is false; see (Joseph 1985, section 4) for Kac's counterexample. (Andersen 1985) gave a proof of Demazure's character formula using the work on the geometry of [Schubert varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_varieties) by (Ramanan Ramanathan 1985) and (Mehta Ramanathan 1985). (Joseph 1985) gave a proof for sufficiently large dominant highest weight modules using Lie algebra techniques. (Kashiwara 1993) proved a refined version of the Demazure character formula that (Littelmann 1995) conjectured (and proved in many cases).

::: {#o18 .statement cue=heading name="Statement"}

### Statement {#Statement}

The Demazure character formula is

$$\text{Ch}(F(w\lambda)) = \Delta_1\Delta_2\cdots\Delta_ne^\lambda$$

Here:

- *w* is an element of the Weyl group, with reduced decomposition *w* = *s*<sub>1</sub>...*s*<sub>*n*</sub> as a product of reflections of simple roots.
- λ is a lowest weight, and *e*<sup>λ</sup> the corresponding element of the [group ring](wikipedia://en/349223) of the weight lattice.
- Ch(*F*(*w*λ)) is the character of the Demazure module *F*(*w*λ).
- *P* is the weight lattice, and **Z**\[*P*\] is its group ring.
- $\rho$ is the sum of fundamental weights and the dot action is defined by $w\cdot u=w(u+\rho)-\rho$.
- Δ<sub>α</sub> for α a root is the [endomorphism](wikipedia://en/9569) of the **Z**-module **Z**\[*P*\] defined by

$$\Delta_\alpha(u) = \frac{u-s_\alpha \cdot u}{1-e^{-\alpha}}$$

and Δ<sub>*j*</sub> is Δ<sub>α</sub> for α the root of *s*<sub>*j*</sub>
