# Patrick J. Curran

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Patrick J. Curran"}

**Patrick James Curran** (born May 9, 1965) is an American psychologist and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661). He is a professor of [quantitative psychology](wikipedia://en/3067624) at the [University of North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), where he is also a faculty member at the Center for Developmental Science.

:::

He is the coauthor of *Latent Curve Models: A Structural Equation Perspective* (with [Ken Bollen](wikipedia://en/40229697)) and is known broadly in the [social sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences) for teaching and research on the measurement and analysis of [longitudinal data](wikipedia://en/2499813).[^o4]

He is co-host, along with [Gregory R. Hancock](wikipedia://en/26675086), of *Quantitude*, a podcast focusing on quantitative methodology.

## Career {#Career}

Curran earned a bachelor's degree [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude) from the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Colorado_Boulder) in 1987 and a PhD in [clinical psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychology) with an emphasis in [quantitative methodology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_methods) from [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) in 1994. He completed a postdoctoral fellowship in [applied statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_statistics) at the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) under [Bengt O. Muthén](wikipedia://en/53411130), the creator of the Mplus statistical program.[^o6]

He became an assistant professor of psychology at [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University) in 1996. He moved three years later to [UNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), where he was promoted to associate professor in 2002 and full professor in 2006. He was Director of the L. L. Thurstone Psychometric Laboratory from 2010 to 2017.

### Extramural service {#Extramural_service}

Curran serves on the editorial boards or as a consulting editor of several prominent journals in the field of psychology, including *[Multivariate Behavioral Research](wikipedia://en/26785977)* and *[Journal of Abnormal Psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Abnormal_Psychology)*.

In 2008, he cofounded Curran–[Bauer](wikipedia://en/53557180) Analytics, a consulting firm based in [Durham, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina). He has taught doctoral-level quantitative workshops to more than 1,000 students and research scientists across six countries.[^o8]

## Research {#Research}

Curran's program of methodological research encompasses [structural equation modeling](wikipedia://en/2007748), [multilevel modeling](wikipedia://en/4521038), and [latent growth curve modeling](wikipedia://en/6244696) of [longitudinal data](wikipedia://en/2499813). In addition, as a [principal investigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_investigator) on several grants from the [National Institute on Drug Abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_on_Drug_Abuse), he conducts applied research in [developmental psychopathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_psychopathology) in general and on the [risk factors](wikipedia://en/909227) of [adolescent substance use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse) in particular.

He has published extensively in books and peer-reviewed journals and has been cited in the scholarly literature more than 40,000 times.[^o10]
