# Bernoulli's triangle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Bernoulli's triangle"}

*Image: Bernoulli triangle derivation.svg.* Derivation of Bernoulli's triangle (blue bold text) from Pascal's triangle (pink italics)

**Bernoulli's triangle** is an [array](wikipedia://en/20556859) of [partial sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_sums) of the [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficients). For any non-negative integer *n* and for any integer *k* included between 0 and *n*, the component in row *n* and column *k* is given by:

:::

$$\sum_{p=0}^k {n \choose p},$$

*Image: pascal triangle compositions.svg.* As the numbers of [com­po­si­tions](wikipedia://en/550741) of *n*+1 into *k*+1 ordered partitions form [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497), the numbers of [compositions](wikipedia://en/550741) of *n*+1 into *k*+1 ***or fewer*** ordered partitions form Bernoulli's triangle

i.e., the sum of the first *k* *n*th-order binomial coefficients.[^o2] The first rows of Bernoulli's triangle are:

$$\begin{array}{cc|cccccc} & k & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5\\ n & & \\ \hline 0 & & 1 \\ 1 & & 1 & 2 \\ 2 & & 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 3 & & 1 & 4 & 7 & 8 \\ 4 & & 1 & 5 & 11 & 15 & 16 \\ 5 & & 1 & 6 & 16 & 26 & 31 & 32 \end{array}$$

Similarly to [Pascal's triangle](wikipedia://en/49497), each component of Bernoulli's triangle is the sum of two components of the previous row, except for the last number of each row, which is double the last number of the previous row. For example, if $B_{n,k}$ denotes the component in row *n* and column *k*, then:

$$B_{n,k}=\begin{cases} 1 & \mbox{if } n=0 \\ B_{n-1,k}+B_{n-1,k-1} & \mbox{if } k<n \\ 2B_{n-1,k-1} = 2^n & \mbox{if } k=n \end{cases}$$

## Sequences derived from Bernoulli's triangle {#Sequences_derived_from_Bernoulli's_triangle}

*Image: Bernoulli triangle columns.svg.* Sequences from the [On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences](wikipedia://en/500004) in Bernoulli's triangle

As in Pascal's triangle and other similarly constructed triangles,[^o4] sums of components along diagonal paths in Bernoulli's triangle result in the [Fibonacci numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_number).[^o5]

As the third column of Bernoulli's triangle (*k* = 2) is a triangular number plus one, it forms the [lazy caterer's sequence](wikipedia://en/2038304) for *n* cuts, where *n* ≥ 2. The fourth column (*k* = 3) is the three-dimensional analogue, known as the [cake numbers](wikipedia://en/3826308), for *n* cuts, where *n* ≥ 3.

The fifth column (*k* = 4) gives the maximum number of regions in the problem of [dividing a circle into areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividing_a_circle_into_areas) for *n* + 1 points, where *n* ≥ 4.

In general, the (*k* + 1)th column gives the maximum number of regions in [*k*-dimensional space](wikipedia://en/5308894) formed by *n* − 1 (*k* − 1)-dimensional [hyperplanes](wikipedia://en/99862), for *n* ≥ *k*. It also gives the number of [compositions](wikipedia://en/550741) (ordered partitions) of *n* + 1 into *k* + 1 or fewer parts.
