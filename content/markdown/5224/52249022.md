# Ellice Horsburgh

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ellice Horsburgh"}

**Ellice Martin Horsburgh** [FRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRSE) AMICE (1870 – 28 December 1935) was a Scottish mathematician and engineer. He was an expert on [numismatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numismatics) and a skilled photographer.

:::

## Life {#Life}

He was born in [Kelso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelso,_Scottish_Borders) in 1870, the son of Ellen Sarah Vost and the Rev Andrew Horsburgh, a preacher and missionary in India.

He was educated at the Collegiate School, 27/28 [Charlotte Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Square)[^o4] in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh). He then trained for the Indian Civil Service with [Wren and Gurney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wren_and_Gurney) but, falling very ill, was instructed to go to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia) to improve his health. During this sea journey he became interested in navigational mathematics. He arrived at [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne) during a financial panic and then got mixed up in the [Coolgardie Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolgardie_Gold_Mine) of 1892. After two years of little success as a gold miner he returned to Scotland in 1894.[^o5]

He then went to the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) where he graduated MA BSc in engineering in 1897. He gained a further BSc in mathematics and natural philosophy (physics) in 1899. He began lecturing in electrical engineering at Leith Technical College around 1900. In 1903 he moved to the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh).[^o3]

In 1904 he was elected a Fellow of the [Royal Society of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_of_Edinburgh). His proposers were [George Chrystal](wikipedia://en/14008803), [Charles Tweedie](wikipedia://en/59536038), [Sir Thomas Hudson Beare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hudson_Beare) and [Cargill Gilston Knott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargill_Gilston_Knott).[^o6] The [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) awarded him an honorary doctorate (DSc) in 1912. From 1920 to 1935 he was a Reader in Technical Mathematics. He also worked as a consultant to Brunton’s Wire Mills in [Musselburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musselburgh).

He died at his home, 11 Granville Terrace[^o7] in [Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinburgh) on 28 December 1935. He is buried in [Dirleton Kirkyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirleton_Kirk) in [East Lothian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Lothian), east of Edinburgh.

## Family {#Family}

He married Helen Howden Ferme in 1920.

## Publications {#Publications}

- *Calculating Machines* (1919)
- *The Fracture of Wire in Steel Ropes* (1921)
- *Modern Instruments and Methods of Calculation* (1914)
