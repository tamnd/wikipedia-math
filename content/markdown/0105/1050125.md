# Laver table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Laver table"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Laver tables** (named after [Richard Laver](wikipedia://en/22280145), who discovered them towards the end of the 1980s in connection with his works on [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553)) are tables of numbers that have certain properties of [algebraic](wikipedia://en/18716923) and [combinatorial](wikipedia://en/5170) interest. They occur in the study of [racks and quandles](wikipedia://en/1140981).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

For any nonnegative [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n*, the *n*-th *Laver table* is the 2<sup>*n*</sup> × 2<sup>*n*</sup> table whose entry in the cell at row *p* and column *q* (1 ≤ *p*,*q* ≤ 2<sup>*n*</sup>) is defined as[^o4]

$$L_n(p, q) := p \star_n q$$

where $\star_n$ is the unique [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948) on {1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>} that satisfies the following two equations for all *p*, *q*:

::: {#o5 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$p \star_n 1 = p+1 \mod{2^n} \tag{1}$$

:::

and

::: {#o6 .equation cue=numblk name="2"}

$$p \star_n (q \star_n r) = (p \star_n q) \star_n (p \star_n r). \tag{2}$$

:::

Note: Equation ((1)) uses the notation $x \bmod 2^n$ to mean the unique member of {1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>} [congruent](wikipedia://en/20087) to *x* [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) 2<sup>*n*</sup>.

Equation ((2)) is known as the *(left) self-distributive law*, and a set endowed with *any* binary operation satisfying this law is called a [shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelf_%28mathematics%29). Thus, the *n*-th Laver table is just the [multiplication table](wikipedia://en/57122) for the unique shelf ({1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>}, $\star_n$) that satisfies Equation ((1)).

There is no known [closed-form expression](wikipedia://en/585143) to calculate the entries of a Laver table directly,[^o8] but [Patrick Dehornoy](wikipedia://en/25223557) provides a simple [algorithm](wikipedia://en/775) for filling out Laver tables.[^o9]

::: {#o11 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

1. For all *p*, *q* in {1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>}: $\ \ 2^n \star_n q = q;\ \ p \star_n 2^n = 2^n;\ \ (2^n-1)\star_n q = 2^n;\ \ p\star_n 2^{n-1}=2^n\text{ if }p\ne 2^n$.
1. For all *p* in {1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>}: $\ \ (p \star_n q)_{q=1,2,3,...}$ is periodic with period π<sub>n</sub>(p) equal to a [power of two](wikipedia://en/376948).
1. For all *p* in {1,...,2<sup>*n*</sup>}: $\ \ (p \star_n q)_{q=1,2,3,...,\pi_n(p)}$ is strictly increasing from $p \star_n 1 = p+1\$ to $\ p \star_n \pi_n(p) = 2^n$.
1. For all *p*,*q*: $\ p \star_n q = (p+1)^{(q)}, \text{ where } x^{(1)}=x,\ x^{(k+1)}=x^{(k)} \star_n x.$[^o4]

:::

## Are the first-row periods unbounded? {#Are_the_first-row_periods_unbounded?}

::: {#o13 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is ZFC set theory able to prove that the periods of the first rows of Laver tables are unbounded?

:::

Looking at just the first row in the *n*-th Laver table, for *n* = 0, 1, 2, ..., the entries in each first row are seen to be periodic with a period that's always a power of two, as mentioned in Property 2 above. The first few periods are 1, 1, 2, 4, 4, 8, 8, 8, 8, 16, 16, ... . This sequence is nondecreasing, and in 1995 Richard Laver [proved](wikipedia://en/82285), *under the assumption that there exists a [rank-into-rank](wikipedia://en/311017) (a [large cardinal](wikipedia://en/383644) property)*, that it actually increases without bound. (It is not known whether this is also provable in [ZFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZFC) without the additional large-cardinal axiom.)[^o14] In any case, it grows extremely slowly; [Randall Dougherty](wikipedia://en/28140491) showed that 32 cannot appear in this sequence (if it ever does) until *n* > A(9, A(8, A(8, 254))), where A denotes the [Ackermann–Péter function](wikipedia://en/2925).[^o15]
