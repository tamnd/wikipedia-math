# Hao Wang (academic)

[Wang (王)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_%28surname%29)

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Hao Wang"}

**Hao Wang** (; 20 May 1921 – 13 May 1995) was a Chinese-American [logician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logician), philosopher, mathematician, and commentator on [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Born in [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan), Shandong, in the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_%281912–1949%29) (today in the People's Republic of China), Wang received his early education in China. He obtained a B.S. degree in mathematics from the [National Southwestern Associated University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Southwestern_Associated_University) in 1943 and an M.A. in philosophy from [Tsinghua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsinghua_University) in 1945, where his teachers included [Feng Youlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_Youlan) and [Jin Yuelin](wikipedia://en/5484556), after which he moved to the United States for further graduate studies. He studied logic under [W. V. O. Quine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._V._O._Quine) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), culminating in a Ph.D. in 1948. He was appointed to an assistant professorship at Harvard the same year.

During the early 1950s, Wang studied with [Paul Bernays](wikipedia://en/2556580) in [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zürich). In 1956, he was appointed Reader in the Philosophy of Mathematics at the [University of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oxford). In 1959, Wang wrote on an [IBM 704](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_704) computer a program that in only 9 minutes mechanically proved several hundred [mathematical logic](wikipedia://en/19636) theorems in [Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395) and [Russell](wikipedia://en/4163)'s *[Principia Mathematica](wikipedia://en/24133)*.[^o4] In 1961, he was appointed [Gordon McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_McKay) Professor of Mathematical Logic and Applied Mathematics at Harvard.[^o5] From 1967 until 1991, he headed the logic research group at [Rockefeller University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_University) in New York City, where he was professor of logic. In 1972, Wang joined in a group of Chinese American scientists led by [Chih-Kung Jen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chih-Kung_Jen) as the first such delegation from the U.S. to the People's Republic of China.

In the 1950s, Hao's work used [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) and Western [analytical philosophy](wikipedia://en/159211) to critique the then-prevailing view of cognitive processes as mechanical computation.[^o6] Hao contended instead that human reason is not influenced by universal rules, but rather is personal and influenced by historical and material conditions.[^o6]

One of Wang's most important contributions was the [Wang tile](wikipedia://en/65798).[^o7] He showed that any [Turing machine](wikipedia://en/30403) can be turned into a set of Wang tiles. The domino problem is to find an algorithm that uses a set of Wang tiles to tile the plane. The first noted example of [aperiodic tiling](wikipedia://en/868145) is a set of Wang tiles, whose nonexistence Wang had once conjectured, discovered by his student [Robert Berger](wikipedia://en/2012183) in 1966. Wang also had a significant influence on theory of computational complexity.[^o8]

A philosopher in his own right,[^o9] Wang also developed a penetrating interpretation of [Ludwig Wittgenstein](wikipedia://en/17741)'s later philosophy of mathematics, which he called "anthropologism." Later he broadened this reading in the foundations of mathematics. He chronicled [Kurt Gödel](wikipedia://en/16736)'s philosophical ideas and authored several books on the subject,[^o10] thereby providing contemporary scholars many insights elucidating Gödel's later philosophical thought. He saw his own philosophy of "substantial factualism" as a middle ground that includes both abstract theoretical formulations and the ordinary language of everyday discourse.

In 1983 he was presented with the first Milestone Prize for [Automated Theorem-Proving](wikipedia://en/2546), sponsored by the [International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Joint_Conference_on_Artificial_Intelligence).[^o11]

On 13 May 1995, Wang died at [New York Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weill_Cornell_Medical_Center) one week from his 74th birthday. According to his wife Hanne Tierney, Wang's cause of death was from [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma).[^o12][^o13] In addition to Tierney, Wang was survived by a daughter and two sons.[^o12]

## Books {#Books}

- *Les Systèmes axiomatiques de la Théorie des Ensembles*, Gauthier-Villars; Paris, 1953. \[Wang 1953a, with Robert McNaughton\].[^o15][^o16][^o17]
- *A Survey of Mathematical Logic*. Peking: Science Press; Amsterdam: North-Holland, 1962. \[Wang 1962a\].
- *From Mathematics to Philosophy*. London: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1974. \[Wang 1974a\].
- *Popular Lectures on Mathematical Logic*. New York: Van Nostrand, 1981. \[Wang 1981a\]. . Dover reprint 2014. [^o18]

- *Reflections on Kurt Gödel*. Cambridge, Massachusetts: MIT Press, 1987. \[Wang 1987a\]. .
- *Computation, Logic, Philosophy. A Collection of Essays*. Beijing: Science Press; Dordrecht: Kluwer Academic, 1990. \[Wang 1990a\]. .
- *A Logical Journey: From Gödel to Philosophy*. Cambridge, Massachusetts: MIT Press, 1996. \[Wang 1996a\]. .[^o19]
