# Quasidihedral group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasidihedral group"}

*Image: Cayley graph of the quasidihedral group of order 16.svg.* [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of the quasidihedral group of order 16

*Image: Cayley graph of the modular maximal-cyclic group of order 16.svg.* Cayley graph of the modular maximal-cyclic group of order 16

*Image: Cayley graph of the dihedral group of order 16.svg.* Cayley graph of the dihedral group of order 16

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **quasi-dihedral groups**, also called **semi-dihedral groups**, are certain [non-abelian groups](wikipedia://en/4791400) of [order](wikipedia://en/493527) a power of 2. For every positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) *n* greater than or equal to 4, there are exactly four [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of non-abelian [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of order 2<sup>*n*</sup> which have a [cyclic](wikipedia://en/52327) [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of [index](wikipedia://en/230581) 2. Two are well known, the [generalized quaternion group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_quaternion_group) and the [dihedral group](wikipedia://en/100267). One of the remaining two groups is often considered particularly important, since it is an example of a 2-group of [maximal nilpotency class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_class). In [Bertram Huppert](wikipedia://en/17639456)'s text *Endliche Gruppen*, this group is called a "Quasidiedergruppe". In [Daniel Gorenstein](wikipedia://en/1569478)'s text, *Finite Groups*, this group is called the "semidihedral group". Dummit and Foote refer to it as the "quasidihedral group"; we adopt that name in this article. All give the same [presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_presentation) for this group:

:::

$$\langle r,s \mid r^{2^{n-1}} = s^2 = 1,\ srs = r^{2^{n-2}-1}\rangle\,\!.$$

The other non-abelian 2-group with cyclic subgroup of index 2 is not given a special name in either text, but referred to as just *G* or M<sub>*m*</sub>(2). When this group has order 16, Dummit and Foote refer to this group as the "modular group of order 16", as its [lattice of subgroups is modular](wikipedia://en/16355158). In this article this group will be called the modular maximal-cyclic group of order $2^n$. Its presentation is:

$$\langle r,s \mid r^{2^{n-1}} = s^2 = 1,\ srs = r^{2^{n-2}+1}\rangle\,\!.$$

Both these two groups and the dihedral group are [semidirect products](wikipedia://en/48246) of a cyclic group \<*r*> of order 2<sup>*n*−1</sup> with a cyclic group \<*s*> of order 2. Such a non-abelian semidirect product is uniquely determined by an element of order 2 in the [group of units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_units) of the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $\mathbb{Z}/2^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}$ and there are precisely three such elements, $2^{n-1}-1$, $2^{n-2}-1$, and $2^{n-2}+1$, corresponding to the dihedral group, the quasidihedral, and the modular maximal-cyclic group.

The generalized quaternion group, the dihedral group, and the quasidihedral group of order 2<sup>*n*</sup> all have nilpotency class *n* − 1, and are the only isomorphism classes of groups of order 2<sup>*n*</sup> with nilpotency class *n* − 1. The groups of order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> and nilpotency class *n* − 1 were the beginning of the classification of all [*p*-groups](wikipedia://en/24664) via [coclass](wikipedia://en/24664#Classification). The modular maximal-cyclic group of order 2<sup>*n*</sup> always has nilpotency class 2. This makes the modular maximal-cyclic group less interesting, since most groups of order *p*<sup>*n*</sup> for large *n* have nilpotency class 2 and have proven difficult to understand directly.

The generalized quaternion, the dihedral, and the quasidihedral group are the only 2-groups whose [derived subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derived_subgroup) has index 4. The [Alperin–Brauer–Gorenstein theorem](wikipedia://en/8918642) classifies the [simple groups](wikipedia://en/47600), and to a degree the [finite groups](wikipedia://en/323707), with quasidihedral Sylow 2-subgroups.

## Examples {#Examples}

The Sylow 2-subgroups of the following groups are quasidihedral:

- PSL<sub>3</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) for *q* ≡ 3 mod 4,
- PSU<sub>3</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) for *q* ≡ 1 mod 4,
- the [Mathieu group](wikipedia://en/380207) M<sub>11</sub>,
- GL<sub>2</sub>(**F**<sub>*q*</sub>) for *q* ≡ 3 mod 4.

:::
