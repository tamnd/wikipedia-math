# Quantum field theory in curved spacetime

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quantum field theory in curved spacetime"}

In [theoretical physics](wikipedia://en/19594028), **quantum field theory in curved spacetime (QFTCS)**[^o2] is an extension of [quantum field theory](wikipedia://en/25267) from [Minkowski spacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space) to a general [curved spacetime](wikipedia://en/1016422). This theory uses a semi-classical approach; it treats spacetime as a fixed, classical background, while giving a quantum-mechanical description of the matter and energy propagating through that spacetime. A general prediction of this theory is that particles can be created by time-dependent [gravitational fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_field) (multi[graviton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviton) [pair production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_production)), or by time-independent gravitational fields that contain horizons. The most famous example of the latter is the phenomenon of [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724) emitted by [black holes](wikipedia://en/4650).

:::

## Overview {#Overview}

Ordinary [quantum field theories](wikipedia://en/25267), which form the basis of [Standard Model](wikipedia://en/47641), are defined in flat [Minkowski space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minkowski_space), which is an excellent approximation when it comes to describing the behavior of microscopic particles in weak gravitational fields like those found on Earth. In order to describe situations in which gravity is strong enough to influence (quantum) matter, yet not strong enough to require quantization itself, physicists have formulated quantum field theories in curved spacetime. These theories rely on general relativity to describe a curved background spacetime, and define a generalized quantum field theory to describe the behavior of quantum matter within that spacetime.

For non-zero [cosmological constants](wikipedia://en/38992), on curved spacetimes quantum fields lose their interpretation as asymptotic [particles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_particle).[^o4] Only in certain situations, such as in asymptotically flat spacetimes (zero [cosmological](wikipedia://en/5378) [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770#Curvature_of_space)), can the notion of incoming and outgoing particle be recovered, thus enabling one to define an [*S*-matrix](wikipedia://en/622053). Even then, as in flat spacetime, the asymptotic particle interpretation depends on the observer (i.e., different observers may measure different numbers of asymptotic particles on a given spacetime).

Another observation is that unless the background [metric tensor](wikipedia://en/195795) has a global timelike [Killing vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_vector), there is no way to define a [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) or ground state canonically. The concept of a vacuum is not invariant under [diffeomorphisms](wikipedia://en/8564). This is because a mode decomposition of a field into positive and negative frequency modes is not invariant under diffeomorphisms. If $t'(t)$ is a diffeomorphism, then, in general, the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) of $e^{i k t'(t)}$ will contain negative frequencies even if $k > 0$. [Creation operators](wikipedia://en/701991) correspond to positive frequencies, while [annihilation operators](wikipedia://en/701991) correspond to negative frequencies. This is why a state that looks like a vacuum to one observer cannot look like a vacuum state to another observer; it could even appear as a [heat bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_bath) under suitable hypotheses.

Since the end of the 1980s, the [local quantum field theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_quantum_field_theory) approach due to [Rudolf Haag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Haag) and [Daniel Kastler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kastler) has been implemented in order to include an algebraic version of quantum field theory in curved spacetime. Indeed, the viewpoint of local quantum physics is suitable to generalize the [renormalization](wikipedia://en/291453) procedure to the theory of quantum fields developed on curved backgrounds. Several rigorous results concerning QFT in the presence of a black hole have been obtained. In particular the algebraic approach allows one to deal with the problems mentioned above arising from the absence of a preferred reference vacuum state, the absence of a natural notion of particle and the appearance of unitarily inequivalent representations of the algebra of observables.[^o5][^o6]

## Applications {#Applications}

Using [perturbation theory](wikipedia://en/297069) in quantum field theory in curved spacetime geometry is known as the [semiclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiclassical_physics) approach to [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312). This approach studies the interaction of [quantum fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_fields) in a fixed classical spacetime and among other things predicts the creation of particles by time-varying spacetimes[^o9] and [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724).[^o10] The latter can be understood as a manifestation of the [Unruh effect](wikipedia://en/691838) where an accelerating observer observes black-body radiation.[^o11] Other predictions of quantum fields in curved spaces include,[^o12] for example, the radiation emitted by a particle moving along a [geodesic](wikipedia://en/91096)[^o13][^o14][^o15][^o16] and the interaction of [Hawking radiation](wikipedia://en/173724) with particles outside black holes.[^o17][^o18][^o19][^o20]

This formalism is also used to predict the primordial density [perturbation](wikipedia://en/102140) spectrum arising in different models of [cosmic inflation](wikipedia://en/5382). These predictions are calculated using the [Bunch&ndash;Davies vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunch&ndash;Davies_vacuum) or modifications thereto.[^o21]

:::

## Approximation to quantum gravity {#Approximation_to_quantum_gravity}

The theory of quantum field theory in curved spacetime may be considered as an intermediate step towards [quantum gravity](wikipedia://en/25312).[^o23] QFT in curved spacetime is expected to be a viable approximation to the theory of quantum gravity when spacetime curvature is not significant on the Planck scale.[^o24][^o25][^o26] Nonetheless, the fact that the true theory of quantum gravity remains unknown means that the precise criteria for when QFT on curved spacetime is a good approximation are also unknown.[^o4]

Gravity is not [renormalizable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renormalizable) in QFT, so merely formulating QFT in curved spacetime is not a true theory of quantum gravity.
