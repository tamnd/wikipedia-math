# Euler system

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euler system"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), particularly [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), an **Euler system** is a collection of compatible elements of [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315) groups indexed by [fields](wikipedia://en/11634). They were introduced by (txt) in his work on [Heegner points](wikipedia://en/5828654) on [modular elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/4559850), which was motivated by his earlier paper (Kolyvagin 1988) and the work of (Thaine 1988). Euler systems are named after [Leonhard Euler](wikipedia://en/17902) because the factors relating different elements of an Euler system resemble the [Euler factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_factor) of an [Euler product](wikipedia://en/398540).

:::

Euler systems can be used to construct annihilators of [ideal class groups](wikipedia://en/150907) or [Selmer groups](wikipedia://en/1970862), thus giving bounds on their orders, which in turn has led to deep theorems such as the finiteness of some [Tate-Shafarevich groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate-Shafarevich_group). This led to [Karl Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rubin)'s new proof of the [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332), considered simpler than the original proof due to [Barry Mazur](wikipedia://en/23087679) and [Andrew Wiles](wikipedia://en/2027).

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Although there are several definitions of special sorts of Euler system, there seems to be no published definition of an Euler system that covers all known cases. But it is possible to say roughly what an Euler system is, as follows:

- An Euler system is given by collection of elements *c*<sub>*F*</sub>. These elements are often indexed by certain [number fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_field) *F* containing some fixed number field *K*, or by something closely related such as square-free integers. The elements *c*<sub>*F*</sub> are typically elements of some Galois cohomology group such as H<sup>1</sup>(*F*, *T*) where *T* is a *p*-adic representation of the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of *K*.
- The most important condition is that the elements *c*<sub>*F*</sub> and *c*<sub>*G*</sub> for two different fields *F* ⊆ *G* are related by a simple formula, such as

$${\rm cor}_{G/F}(c_G) = \prod_{q\in \Sigma(G/F)}P(\mathrm{Fr}_q^{-1}|{\rm Hom}_O(T,O(1));\mathrm{Fr}_q^{-1})c_F$$

Here the "Euler factor" *P*(τ|*B*;*x*) is defined to be the element det(1-τ*x*|*B*) considered as an element of O\[*x*\], which when *x* happens to act on *B* is not the same as det(1-τ*x*|*B*) considered as an element of O.

- There may be other conditions that the *c*<sub>*F*</sub> have to satisfy, such as congruence conditions.

[Kazuya Kato](wikipedia://en/4616818) refers to the elements in an Euler system as "arithmetic incarnations of zeta" and describes the property of being an Euler system as "an arithmetic reflection of the fact that these incarnations are related to special values of Euler products".[^o7]

## Examples {#Examples}

### Cyclotomic units {#Cyclotomic_units}

For every square-free positive integer *n* pick an *n*-th root ζ<sub>*n*</sub> of 1, with ζ<sub>*mn*</sub> = ζ<sub>*m*</sub>ζ<sub>*n*</sub> for *m*,*n* coprime. Then the cyclotomic Euler system is the set of numbers α<sub>*n*</sub> = 1 − ζ<sub>*n*</sub>. These satisfy the relations

$$N_{Q(\zeta_{nl})/Q(\zeta_l)}(\alpha_{nl}) = \alpha_n^{F_l-1}$$

$\alpha_{nl}\equiv\alpha_n$ modulo all primes above *l*

where *l* is a prime not dividing *n* and *F*<sub>*l*</sub> is a Frobenius automorphism with *F*<sub>*l*</sub>(ζ<sub>*n*</sub>) = ζ${}^{l}_{n}$. Kolyvagin used this Euler system to give an elementary proof of the [Gras conjecture](wikipedia://en/28337935).

### Heegner points {#Heegner_points}

Kolyvagin constructed an Euler system from the [Heegner points](wikipedia://en/5828654) of an elliptic curve, and used this to show that in some cases the [Tate-Shafarevich group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate-Shafarevich_group) is finite.

### Kato's Euler system {#Kato's_Euler_system}

**Kato's Euler system** consists of certain elements occurring in the [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) of [modular curves](wikipedia://en/669552). These elements—named **Beilinson elements** after [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613) who introduced them in (Beilinson 1984)—were used by Kazuya Kato in (Kato 2004) to prove one divisibility in Barry Mazur's [main conjecture of Iwasawa theory](wikipedia://en/22091332) for [elliptic curves](wikipedia://en/10225).[^o17]
