# Fundamental theorem of curves

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Fundamental theorem of curves"}

In [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), the **fundamental theorem of space curves** states that every regular [curve](wikipedia://en/89246) in three-dimensional space, with non-zero [curvature](wikipedia://en/60770), has its [shape](wikipedia://en/169191) (and size or [scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_%28mathematics%29)) completely determined by its curvature and [torsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsion_of_curves).[^o2][^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Use"}

## Use {#Use}

A curve can be described, and thereby defined, by a pair of [scalar fields](wikipedia://en/161879): curvature $\kappa$ and torsion $\tau$, both of which depend on some parameter which [parametrizes](wikipedia://en/576108) the curve but which can ideally be the [arc length](wikipedia://en/1898401) of the curve. From just the curvature and torsion, the [vector fields](wikipedia://en/62641) for the tangent, normal, and binormal vectors can be derived using the [Frenet–Serret formulas](wikipedia://en/666987). Then, [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) of the tangent field (done numerically, if not analytically) yields the curve.

:::

## Congruence {#Congruence}

If a pair of curves are in different positions but have the same curvature and torsion, then they are [congruent](wikipedia://en/39330) to each other.
