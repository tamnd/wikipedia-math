# Paul Gordan

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Paul Gordan"}

**Paul Albert Gordan** (27 April 1837 – 21 December 1912) was a German mathematician known for work in [invariant theory](wikipedia://en/363762) and for the [Clebsch–Gordan coefficients](wikipedia://en/1074990) and [Gordan's lemma](wikipedia://en/23835696).[^o22] He was called "the king of invariant theory".[^o11][^o12] His most famous result is that the ring of [invariants of binary forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invariants_of_binary_forms) of fixed degree is finitely generated.[^o12] Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are named after him and [Alfred Clebsch](wikipedia://en/1369957). Gordan also served as the thesis advisor for [Emmy Noether](wikipedia://en/149896).[^o14][^o21]

:::

## Life and career {#Life_and_career}

Gordan was born to Jewish parents in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau), Germany (now [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), Poland), and died in [Erlangen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erlangen), Germany.[^o13]

He received his Dr. phil. at the [University of Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Breslau) with the thesis *De Linea Geodetica*, (On [Geodesics of Spheroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geodesics_on_spheroids)) in 1862.[^o19] He moved to Erlangen in 1874 to become professor of mathematics at the [University of Erlangen-Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Erlangen-Nuremberg).[^o14]

A famous quote attributed to Gordan about [David Hilbert](wikipedia://en/8302)'s proof of [Hilbert's basis theorem](wikipedia://en/13733), a result which vastly generalized his result on invariants, is "This is not mathematics; this is [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology)."[^o11][^o15] The proof in question was the (non-constructive) existence of a finite basis for invariants. It is not clear if Gordan really said this since the earliest reference to it is 25 years after the events and after his death. Nor is it clear whether the quote was intended as criticism, or praise, or a subtle joke. Gordan himself encouraged Hilbert and used Hilbert's results and methods, and the widespread story that he opposed Hilbert's work on invariant theory is a myth (though he did correctly point out in a referee's report that some of the reasoning in Hilbert's paper was incomplete).[^o16][^o20]

He later said "I have convinced myself that even theology has its merits". He also published a simplified version of the proof.[^o17][^o18]
