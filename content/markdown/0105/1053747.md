# Vladimir Drinfeld

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Vladimir Drinfeld"}

**Vladimir Gershonovich Drinfeld** (Володи́мир Ге́ршонович Дрінфельд; born February 14, 1954), surname also romanized as **Drinfel'd**, is a mathematician from Ukraine, who immigrated to the US and works at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).

:::

Drinfeld's work connected [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997) over [finite fields](wikipedia://en/11615) with [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), especially the theory of [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146), through the notions of [elliptic module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_module) and the theory of the [geometric Langlands correspondence](wikipedia://en/10500178). Drinfeld introduced the notion of a [quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678) (independently discovered by [Michio Jimbo](wikipedia://en/22363355) at the same time) and made important contributions to [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416), including the [ADHM construction](wikipedia://en/5165589) of [instantons](wikipedia://en/296077), algebraic formalism of the [quantum inverse scattering method](wikipedia://en/25672752), and the Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction in the theory of [solitons](wikipedia://en/51654).

He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1990.[^o3] In 2016, he was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o4] In 2018 he received the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278).[^o5] In 2023 he was awarded the [Shaw Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_Prize) in Mathematical Sciences.[^o6]

## Early life, family and education {#Early_life,_family_and_education}

Drinfeld was born into a [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish)[^o8] family, in [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv), [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1954. The family was well-steeped in mathematics.

In 1969, at the age of 15, Drinfeld represented the Soviet Union at the [International Mathematics Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mathematics_Olympiad) in [Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania), and won a gold medal with the full score of 40 points. He was, at the time, the [youngest participant to achieve a perfect score](wikipedia://en/15712699#Exceptionally_young_participants), a record that has since been surpassed by only four others including [Sergei Konyagin](wikipedia://en/29007447) and [Noam Elkies](wikipedia://en/1136999).

Drinfeld entered [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) in the same year and graduated from it in 1974. Drinfeld was awarded the [Candidate of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidate_of_Sciences) degree in 1978 and the [Doctor of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Sciences) degree from the [Steklov Institute of Mathematics](wikipedia://en/984494) in 1988.

## Career {#Career}

From 1981 until 1999, he worked at the [Verkin Institute for Low Temperature Physics and Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkin_Institute_for_Low_Temperature_Physics_and_Engineering) (Department of Mathematical Physics). Drinfeld moved to the US in January 1999 where he began working at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

In 1974, at the age of twenty, Drinfeld announced a proof of the [Langlands conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_conjectures) for [GL<sub>2</sub>](wikipedia://en/113564) over a [global field](wikipedia://en/288358) of positive characteristic. In the course of proving the conjectures, Drinfeld introduced a new class of objects that he called "elliptic modules" (now known as [Drinfeld modules](wikipedia://en/5578871)). Later, in 1983, Drinfeld published a short article that expanded the scope of the Langlands conjectures. The Langlands conjectures, when published in 1967, could be seen as a sort of [non-abelian class field theory](wikipedia://en/8354260). It postulated the existence of a natural one-to-one correspondence between [Galois representations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galois_representations) and some [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146). The "naturalness" is guaranteed by the essential coincidence of [L-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-functions). However, this condition is purely arithmetic and cannot be considered for a general one-dimensional function field in a straightforward way. Drinfeld pointed out that instead of automorphic forms one can consider automorphic [perverse sheaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perverse_sheaves) or automorphic [D-modules](wikipedia://en/3440755). "Automorphicity" of these modules and the Langlands correspondence could be then understood in terms of the action of [Hecke operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecke_operators).

Drinfeld has also worked in [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416). In collaboration with his advisor [Yuri Manin](wikipedia://en/1692086), he constructed the [moduli space](wikipedia://en/361609) of [Yang–Mills](wikipedia://en/672202) [instantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instantons), a result that was proved independently by [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Nigel Hitchin](wikipedia://en/1034164). Drinfeld coined the term "[quantum group](wikipedia://en/340678)" in reference to [Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/325714) that are deformations of [simple Lie algebras](wikipedia://en/59500649), and connected them to the study of the [Yang–Baxter equation](wikipedia://en/3720810), which is a necessary condition for the solvability of statistical mechanical models. He also generalized Hopf algebras to [quasi-Hopf algebras](wikipedia://en/4899676) and introduced the study of [Drinfeld twists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinfeld_twist), which can be used to factorize the [R-matrix](wikipedia://en/8498996) corresponding to the solution of the Yang–Baxter equation associated with a [quasitriangular Hopf algebra](wikipedia://en/3727020).

Drinfeld has also collaborated with [Alexander Beilinson](wikipedia://en/10230613) to rebuild the theory of [vertex algebras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex_algebras) in a coordinate-free form, which have become increasingly important to [two-dimensional conformal field theory](wikipedia://en/52183913), [string theory](wikipedia://en/28305), and the [geometric Langlands program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Langlands_program). Drinfeld and Beilinson published their work in 2004 in a book titled "Chiral Algebras."[^o11]
