# Coarea formula

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Coarea formula"}

In the [mathematical](wikipedia://en/18831) field of [geometric measure theory](wikipedia://en/12169694), the **coarea formula** expresses the [integral](wikipedia://en/15532) of a function over an [open set](wikipedia://en/39358) in [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) in terms of integrals over the [level sets](wikipedia://en/559622) of another function. A special case is [Fubini's theorem](wikipedia://en/255245), which says under suitable hypotheses that the integral of a function over the region enclosed by a rectangular box can be written as the [iterated integral](wikipedia://en/349053) over the level sets of the coordinate functions. Another special case is integration in [spherical coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_coordinates), in which the integral of a function on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is related to the integral of the function over spherical shells: level sets of the radial function. The formula plays a decisive role in the modern study of [isoperimetric problems](wikipedia://en/326182).

:::

For [smooth functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) the formula is a result in [multivariate calculus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivariate_calculus) which follows from a [change of variables](wikipedia://en/2146848). More general forms of the formula for [Lipschitz functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) were first established by [Herbert Federer](wikipedia://en/12185345) (Federer 1959), and for [*$\operatorname{BV}$* functions](wikipedia://en/330673) by (Fleming Rishel 1960).

A precise statement of the formula is as follows. Suppose that Ω is an open set in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and *u* is a real-valued [Lipschitz function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipschitz_function) on Ω. Then, for an [L<sup>1</sup>](wikipedia://en/45194) function *g*,

$$\int_\Omega g(x) |\nabla u(x)|\, dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \left(\int_{u^{-1}(t)}g(x)\,dH_{n-1}(x)\right)\,dt$$

where *H*<sub>*n*−1</sub> is the (*n* − 1)-dimensional [Hausdorff measure](wikipedia://en/504109). In particular, by taking *g* to be one, this implies

$$\int_\Omega |\nabla u| = \int_{-\infty}^\infty H_{n-1}(u^{-1}(t))\,dt,$$

and conversely the latter equality implies the former by standard techniques in [Lebesgue integration](wikipedia://en/26064288).

More generally, the coarea formula can be applied to Lipschitz functions *u* defined in $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^n,$ taking on values in $\mathbb{R}^k$ where *k* ≤ *n*. In this case, the following identity holds

$$\int_\Omega g(x) |J_k u(x)|\, dx = \int_{\mathbb{R}^k} \left(\int_{u^{-1}(t)}g(x)\,dH_{n-k}(x)\right)\,dt$$

where *J<sub>k</sub>u* is the *k*-dimensional [Jacobian](wikipedia://en/195351) of *u* whose determinant is given by

$$|J_k u(x)| = \left({\det\left(J u(x) J u(x)^\intercal\right)}\right)^{1/2}.$$

::: {#o5 .application cue=heading name="Applications"}

## Applications {#Applications}

- Taking *u*(*x*) = |*x* − *x*<sub>0</sub>| gives the formula for integration in spherical coordinates of an integrable function *f*:

$$\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}f\,dx = \int_0^\infty\left\{\int_{\partial B(x_0;r)} f\,dS\right\}\,dr.$$

- Combining the coarea formula with the [isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/326182) gives a proof of the [Sobolev inequality](wikipedia://en/4665038) for *W*<sup>1,1</sup> with best constant:

$$\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} |u|^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\le n^{-1}\omega_n^{-\frac{1}{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^n}|\nabla u|$$

where $\omega_n$ is the volume of the [unit ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_ball) in $\mathbb{R}^n.$

:::
