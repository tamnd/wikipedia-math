# Antoni Przeborski

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Antoni Przeborski"}

**Antoni Bonifacy Przeborski** (27 May 1871 – 24 May 1941) was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902).[^o3] He was a professor at [Kharkiv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_University) in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) and was elected [rector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rector_%28academia%29) of that institution. From 1922 until 1939 he occupied the chair of [theoretical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_mechanics) at the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw) in the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic).[^o4][^o3][^o5]

:::

## Education and early career {#Education_and_early_career}

Przeborski was born on 27 May 1871 in Chorosza (near Lipovets), which was part of the Russian Empire at the time and is now located in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine).[^o3] After completing his secondary education and earning a gold medal from the gymnasium in [Nikolaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykolaiv), where his father worked as a physician for the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet), he attended the Faculty of Physics and Mathematics at [Saint Vladimir University in Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vladimir_Imperial_University_of_Kiev).[^o4] In 1895 he won the Professor I. I. Rachmaninoff Prize for his work titled *On the methods of Abel, Jacobi, Liouville and Weierstrass in the theory of elliptic functions*.[^o4] Additionally, he studied at the universities of Moscow, [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen) and [Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University).[^o3]

## Kharkiv {#Kharkiv}

In 1905 Przeborski became a professor at Kharkiv University; in 1908 he became an [ordinary professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_professor) of mathematics there. He was elected rector of Kharkiv University, and during the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish–Soviet_War) he was taken into custody by the Soviet authorities as a "Polish hostage".[^o4] In August 1921 Przeborski submitted an application to the Polish Ministry of Religious Denominations and Public Education for help obtaining permission to move permanently to Poland.[^o4]

## Warsaw {#Warsaw}

Przeborski was one of the mathematicians who left the former Russian Empire for Poland after the [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution).[^o5] In 1921 he was for a short time an ordinary professor at [Stefan Batory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University) in [Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius); in 1922 he moved to Warsaw with the help of [Wacław Sierpiński](wikipedia://en/34122), where he was elected ordinary professor in the Department of Theoretical Mechanics at the University of Warsaw.[^o4] He held the theoretical mechanics chair until 1939.[^o3]

Since 1923 he was a member of the [Polish Academy of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Academy_of_Learning), and since 1930 of the [Warsaw Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Scientific_Society).[^o3] Przeborski died in Warsaw on 24 May 1941.[^o3]

## Scientific work {#Scientific_work}

Przeborski's research was done in fields such as analytic and real functions, [integral equations](wikipedia://en/474234), [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), and applications of mathematics.[^o3] The research he conducted in Poland was mostly related to [classical mechanics](wikipedia://en/19555586).[^o4]

## Selected works {#Selected_works}

- *Wykłady mechaniki teoretycznej* \[Lectures on Theoretical Mechanics\], vols. I–II. Warsaw, 1930–1935.[^o3]
