# Relatively hyperbolic group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Relatively hyperbolic group"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **relatively hyperbolic groups** form an important class of [groups](wikipedia://en/19447) of interest for [geometric group theory](wikipedia://en/955164). The main purpose in their study is to extend the theory of [Gromov-hyperbolic groups](wikipedia://en/3172817) to groups $G$ that may be regarded as [hyperbolic](wikipedia://en/1285375) assemblies of subgroups $H_i$, called peripheral subgroups, in a way that enables "hyperbolic reduction" of problems for $G$ to problems for the $H_i$s.

:::

Illustrative examples of relatively hyperbolic groups are provided by the [fundamental groups](wikipedia://en/11004) of [complete](wikipedia://en/6875557) [noncompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_%28topology%29) [hyperbolic manifolds](wikipedia://en/1589135) of finite volume. Further generalizations such as [acylindrical hyperbolicity](wikipedia://en/55866076) are also explored by current research.

## Intuition and History {#Intuition_and_History}

Just like Gromov-hyperbolic groups or spaces can be thought of as thickened [free groups](wikipedia://en/59735) or [trees](wikipedia://en/48560), the idea of a group $G$ being *hyperbolic relative* to a collection of subgroups $H_i$ (called *peripheral subgroups*) is that $G$ looks like a "thickened tree-like patchwork" of the [conjugates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugation_%28group_action%29) of the $H_i$s, so that it is "hyperbolic-away" from them.

From there, different approaches exist and find relevance in different contexts.

The original insight by [Gromov](wikipedia://en/771562), motivated by examples from [Riemannian geometry](wikipedia://en/195243) and later elaborated by [Bowditch](wikipedia://en/13677348), is to say that $G$ acts properly, but not cocompactly, on a Gromov-hyperbolic space in such a way that the conjugates of the $H_i$s fix points at infinity and that the action becomes cocompact after truncating [horoballs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horoball) around them. For this reason, the conjugates of the $H_i$s are called the *parabolic subgroups*.[^o3][^o4]

Yaman later gave a fully dynamical characterization, no longer involving a hyperbolic space but only its boundary (called the *Bowditch boundary*).[^o5]

The second kind of definition, first due to [Farb](wikipedia://en/32518776), roughly says that after contracting the [left-cosets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_coset) of the $H_i$s to bounded sets, the [Cayley graph](wikipedia://en/358277) of $G$ becomes a (non-proper) Gromov-hyperbolic space.[^o6] The resulting notion, known today as *weak hyperbolicity*, turns out to require extra assumptions on the behavior of quasi-geodesics in order to match the Gromov-Bowditch one.[^o7] Bowditch elaborated Farb's definition by only requiring $G$ to act on a hyperbolic graph with certain additional properties, including that the conjugates of the $H_i$s are the infinite vertex stabilizers.[^o4]

[Osin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Osin) later characterized relative hyperbolicity in terms of relative linear isoperimetric inequalities.[^o8] [Druțu](wikipedia://en/29421438) and [Sapir](wikipedia://en/58965999) gave a characterization in terms of [asymptotic cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic_cone) being [tree-graded metric spaces](wikipedia://en/8234367), a relative version of [real trees](wikipedia://en/371914). This allows for a notion of relative hyperbolicity that makes sense for more general metric spaces than Cayley graphs, and which is invariant by [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259).[^o9]

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Given a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* with Cayley graph *Γ*(*G*) equipped with the path metric and a subgroup *H* of *G*, one can construct the **coned off Cayley graph** $\hat{\Gamma}(G,H)$ as follows: For each left coset *gH*, add a vertex *v*(*gH*) to the Cayley graph *Γ*(*G*) and for each element *x* of *gH*, add an edge *e*(*x*) of length 1/2 from *x* to the vertex *v*(*gH*). This results in a metric space that may not be [proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_metric_space) (i.e. closed balls need not be compact).

The definition of a relatively hyperbolic group, as formulated by [Bowditch](wikipedia://en/13677348) goes as follows. A group *G* is said to be **hyperbolic relative to a subgroup *H*** if the coned off Cayley graph $\hat{\Gamma}(G,H)$ has the properties:

- It is [δ-hyperbolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Δ-hyperbolic_space) and
- it is *fine*: for each integer L, every edge belongs to only finitely many simple cycles of length L.

If only the first condition holds then the group *G* is said to be weakly relatively hyperbolic with respect to *H*.

The definition of the coned off Cayley graph can be generalized to the case of a collection of subgroups and yields the corresponding notion of relative hyperbolicity. A group *G* which contains no collection of subgroups with respect to which it is relatively hyperbolic is said to be a non relatively hyperbolic group.

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- If a group *G* is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a hyperbolic group *H*, then *G* itself is hyperbolic.
- If a group *G* is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a group *H* then it acts as a geometrically finite [convergence group](wikipedia://en/52297304) on a compact space, its Bowditch boundary
- If a group *G* is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a group *H* that has solvable [word problem](wikipedia://en/3852079), then *G* has solvable word problem (Farb), and if *H* has solvable [conjugacy problem](wikipedia://en/4942384), then *G* has solvable conjugacy problem (Bumagin)
- If a group *G* is torsion-free relatively hyperbolic with respect to a group *H*, and *H* has a finite [classifying space](wikipedia://en/923556), then so does *G* (Dahmani)
- If a group *G* is relatively hyperbolic with respect to a group *H* that satisfies the [Farrell-Jones conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrell-Jones_conjecture), then *G* satisfies the Farrell-jones conjecture (Bartels).
- More generally, in many cases (but not all, and not easily or systematically), a property satisfied by all hyperbolic groups and by*H* can be suspected to be satisfied by *G*
- The [isomorphism problem](wikipedia://en/18054026) for virtually torsion-free relatively hyperbolic groups when the peripheral subgroups are finitely generated nilpotent (Dahmani, Touikan)

:::

## Examples {#Examples}

- Any [hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/3172817), such as a [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) of finite rank or the fundamental group of a hyperbolic surface, is hyperbolic relative to the trivial subgroup.
- The fundamental group of a [complete](wikipedia://en/6875557) [hyperbolic manifold](wikipedia://en/1589135) of finite volume is hyperbolic relative to its [cusp subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusp_subgroup). A similar result holds for any complete finite volume [Riemannian manifold](wikipedia://en/144652) with pinched negative [sectional curvature](wikipedia://en/285618).
- The [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) **Z**<sup>2</sup> of rank 2 is weakly hyperbolic, but not hyperbolic, relative to the cyclic subgroup **Z**: even though the graph $\hat{\Gamma}(\mathbb{Z}^2,\mathbb{Z})$ is hyperbolic, it is not fine.
- The free product of a group *H* with any hyperbolic group, is relatively hyperbolic, relative to *H*
- Non-abelian [limit groups](wikipedia://en/78938535) appearing as limits of free groups are relatively hyperbolic, relative to some free abelian subgroups.[^o16]
- Given a semi-direct product of a free group by an infinite cyclic group, it is relatively hyperbolic, relative to some canonical subgroups, if the action of the infinite cyclic group is expanding.
- Combination theorems and small cancellation techniques allow to construct new examples from previous ones.
- The [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) of an orientable finite type [surface](wikipedia://en/27865) is either hyperbolic (when 3*g*+*n*\<5, where *g* is the [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_%28topology%29) and *n* is the number of punctures) or is not relatively hyperbolic with respect to any subgroup.
- The [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772) and the [outer automorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_automorphism) group of a free group of finite rank at least 3 are not relatively hyperbolic.
