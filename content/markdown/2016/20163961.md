# Friedhelm Waldhausen

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Friedhelm Waldhausen"}

**Friedhelm Waldhausen** (born 1938 in Millich, [Hückelhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hückelhoven), [Rhine Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Province), died November 21, 2024[^o3]) was a German [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) known for his work in [algebraic topology](wikipedia://en/38801). He made fundamental contributions in the fields of [3-manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-manifolds) and (algebraic) [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748).

:::

## Career {#Career}

Waldhausen studied mathematics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), [LMU Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich), and the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn). He obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in 1966 from the [University of Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bonn); his advisor was [Friedrich Hirzebruch](wikipedia://en/987495) and his thesis was entitled "Eine Klasse von 3-dimensionalen Mannigfaltigkeiten" (A class of 3-dimensional manifolds).

After visits to [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois), and the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) he moved in 1968 to [Kiel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel_University), where he completed his habilitation (qualified to assume a professorship).

In 1969, he was appointed professor at the [Ruhr University Bochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_University_Bochum) before in 1971 becoming a professor at [Bielefeld University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld_University), an appointment he held until his retirement in 2004.[^o6][^o7]

## Academic work {#Academic_work}

His early work was mainly on the theory of [3-manifolds](wikipedia://en/1018257). He dealt mainly with [Haken manifolds](wikipedia://en/236567) and [Heegaard splitting](wikipedia://en/864418). Among other things, he proved that, roughly speaking, any homotopy equivalence of Haken manifolds is homotopic to a homeomorphism, i.e. that closed Haken manifolds are topologically rigid. He put forward the [Waldhausen conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldhausen_conjecture) about Heegaard splitting.

In the mid-seventies, he extended the connection between geometric topology and [algebraic K-theory](wikipedia://en/598500) by introducing A-theory, a kind of algebraic K-theory for topological spaces. This led to new foundations for algebraic K-theory (using what are now called [Waldhausen categories](wikipedia://en/12727767)) and also gave new impetus to the study of highly structured [ring spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_spectrum_%28homotopy_theory%29).

## Recognition {#Recognition}

Today, Waldhausen is seen, together with [Daniel Quillen](wikipedia://en/435519), as one of the pioneers of algebraic [K-theory](wikipedia://en/246748). Among others, he was awarded the von [Staudt Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staudt_Prize) in 2004 along with [Günter Harder](wikipedia://en/50792386), and an honorary doctorate from [Osnabrück University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabrück_University).[^o10]
