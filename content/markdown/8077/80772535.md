# Random band matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Random band matrix"}

**Random band matrices** are [random matrices](wikipedia://en/1648765) that are also [band matrices](wikipedia://en/1451476), meaning that their non-zero entries are confined to a diagonal *band*, comprising the [main diagonal](wikipedia://en/194990) and zero or more diagonals on each side.[^o2] They can be used to roughly model systems of interacting particles arranged roughly in a grid such that each particle is only allowed to interact with its neighbors, which is an improvement on the [mean field model](wikipedia://en/555768), corresponding to random Gaussian matrices, where no entries are necessarily zero and every particle interacts with every other.[^o2]

:::

In one dimension, this means that $H_{ij} = 0$ if $|i - j| > W$, where W is the band width.[^o2] Physically, this means that the amount by which particles *i* and *j* interact is 0 if their separation is over W.[^o2] In more than one dimension, i and j are no longer integers but *n*d vectors with integer components, and $H_{ij} = 0$ if $|i - j|_{L^1}$, where $|\cdot|_{L^1}$ indicates the [taxicab distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab_distance) between the two locations.[^o2]

## Anderson's model {#Anderson's_model}

Random band matrices are relevant to the study of [Anderson's model of electron localization](wikipedia://en/2213942), which attempts to explain why metals with small amounts of impurities added hit a sudden transition where they cease to be conductive and start to insulate.[^o4] The interaction potentials of certain particles *i*, *j*, in the model form a matrix; specifically a band matrix, as particles only interact with their neighbors.[^o4] Anderson's matrices always have a thin band.[^o4] However, as only some of the entries in Anderson's model are random, it is not a random band matrix.[^o4] The study of random band matrices, however, is related.[^o4]
