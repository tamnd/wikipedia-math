# Orientation (graph theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orientation"}

*Image: Oriented graph.svg.* The [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986) (or digraph) on the right is an orientation of the undirected graph on the left

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **orientation** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is an assignment of a direction to each edge, turning the initial graph into a [directed graph](wikipedia://en/19721986).

:::

## Oriented graphs {#Oriented_graphs}

A directed graph is called an **oriented graph** if none of its pairs of vertices is linked by two mutually symmetric edges. Among directed graphs, the oriented graphs are the ones that have no 2-cycles (that is at most one of $(x, y)$ and $(y, x)$ may be arrows of the graph).[^o3]

A [tournament](wikipedia://en/1427502) is an orientation of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816). A [polytree](wikipedia://en/1731488) is an orientation of an undirected [tree](wikipedia://en/48560).[^o4] [Sumner's conjecture](wikipedia://en/28866227) states that every tournament with $2n - 2$ vertices contains every polytree with $n$ vertices.[^o5]

The number of [non-isomorphic](wikipedia://en/247577) oriented graphs with $n$ vertices (for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$) is

1, 2, 7, 42, 582, 21480, 2142288, 575016219, 415939243032, … .

Tournaments are in one-to-one correspondence with complete directed graphs (graphs in which there is a directed edge in one or both directions between every pair of distinct vertices). A complete directed graph can be converted to an oriented graph by removing every 2-cycle, and conversely an oriented graph can be converted to a complete directed graph by adding a 2-cycle between every pair of vertices that are not endpoints of an edge; these correspondences are [bijective](wikipedia://en/3942). Therefore, the same sequence of numbers also solves the [graph enumeration](wikipedia://en/9982439) problem for complete digraphs. There is an explicit but complicated formula for the numbers in this sequence.[^o6]

## Constrained orientations {#Constrained_orientations}

A [strong orientation](wikipedia://en/16678715) is an orientation that results in a [strongly connected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongly_connected_graph). The closely related totally cyclic orientations are orientations in which every edge belongs to at least one simple cycle. An orientation of an undirected graph $G$ is totally cyclic if and only if it is a strong orientation of every [connected component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_component_%28graph_theory%29) of $G$. [Robbins' theorem](wikipedia://en/36633800) states that a graph has a strong orientation if and only if it is [2-edge-connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-edge-connected_graph); disconnected graphs may have totally cyclic orientations, but only if they have no [bridges](wikipedia://en/1305071).[^o8]

An [acyclic orientation](wikipedia://en/36619881) is an orientation that results in a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002). Every graph has an acyclic orientation; all acyclic orientations may be obtained by placing the vertices into a sequence, and then directing each edge from the earlier of its endpoints in the sequence to the later endpoint. The [Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem](wikipedia://en/36620043) states that a graph has an acyclic orientation in which the longest path has at most $k$ vertices if and only if it can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with at most $k$ colors.[^o9] Acyclic orientations and totally cyclic orientations are related to each other by [planar duality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_dual). An acyclic orientation with a single source and a single sink is called a [bipolar orientation](wikipedia://en/40147217).[^o10]

A [transitive orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_orientation) is an orientation such that the resulting directed graph is its own [transitive closure](wikipedia://en/292239). The graphs with transitive orientations are called [comparability graphs](wikipedia://en/6978672); they may be defined from a [partially ordered set](wikipedia://en/23572) by making two elements adjacent whenever they are comparable in the partial order.[^o11] A transitive orientation, if one exists, can be found in linear time.[^o12] However, testing whether the resulting orientation (or any given orientation) is actually transitive requires more time, as it is equivalent in complexity to [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).

An [Eulerian orientation](wikipedia://en/333219) of an undirected graph is an orientation in which each vertex has equal in-degree and out-degree. Eulerian orientations of [grid graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_graph) arise in [statistical mechanics](wikipedia://en/28481) in the theory of [ice-type models](wikipedia://en/20096022).[^o13]

A [Pfaffian orientation](wikipedia://en/56281176) has the property that certain even-length cycles in the graph have an odd number of edges oriented in each of the two directions around the cycle. They always exist for [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), but not for certain other graphs. They are used in the [FKT algorithm](wikipedia://en/30159370) for counting perfect matchings.[^o14]
