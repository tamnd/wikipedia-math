# Acyclic orientation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Acyclic orientation"}

*Image: Acyclic orientations of C4.svg.* The 14 different acyclic orientations of a 4-vertex [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962), assignments of a direction to each cycle edge that make the resulting directed graph [acyclic](wikipedia://en/204002). Two orientations are shown as adjacent when they differ in the direction of a single edge.

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), an **acyclic orientation** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) is an assignment of a direction to each edge (an [orientation](wikipedia://en/36619818)) that does not form any [directed cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_cycle) and therefore makes it into a [directed acyclic graph](wikipedia://en/204002). Every graph has an acyclic orientation.

:::

The [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of any graph equals one more than the length of the [longest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_path) in an acyclic orientation chosen to minimize this path length. Acyclic orientations are also related to colorings through the [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126), which counts both acyclic orientations and colorings. The [planar dual](wikipedia://en/2536864) of an acyclic orientation is a [totally cyclic orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totally_cyclic_orientation), and vice versa. The family of all acyclic orientations can be given the structure of a [partial cube](wikipedia://en/30697444) by making two orientations adjacent when they differ in the direction of a single edge.

Orientations of [trees](wikipedia://en/48560) are always acyclic, and give rise to [polytrees](wikipedia://en/1731488). Acyclic orientations of [complete graphs](wikipedia://en/85816) are called [transitive tournaments](wikipedia://en/1427502). The [bipolar orientations](wikipedia://en/40147217) are a special case of the acyclic orientations in which there is exactly one source and one sink; every transitive tournament is bipolar.

::: {#o3 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

## Construction {#Construction}

Every graph has an acyclic orientation. One way to generate an acyclic orientation is to place the vertices into a sequence, and then direct each edge from the earlier of its endpoints in the sequence to the later endpoint. The vertex sequence then becomes a [topological ordering](wikipedia://en/897064) of the resulting directed acyclic graph (DAG), and every topological ordering of this DAG generates the same orientation.

Because every DAG has a topological ordering, every acyclic orientation can be constructed in this way. However, it is possible for different vertex sequences to give rise to the same acyclic orientation, when the resulting DAG has multiple topological orderings. For instance, for a four-vertex [cycle graph](wikipedia://en/878962) (shown), there are 24 different vertex sequences, but only 14 possible acyclic orientations.

:::

## Relation to coloring {#Relation_to_coloring}

The [Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver theorem](wikipedia://en/36620043) states that a graph has an acyclic orientation in which the [longest path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longest_path) has at most $k$ vertices if and only if it can be [colored](wikipedia://en/426743) with at most $k$ colors.[^o5]

The number of acyclic orientations may be counted using the [chromatic polynomial](wikipedia://en/1460126) $\chi_G$, whose value at a positive integer $k$ is the number of $k$-colorings of the graph. Every graph $G$ has exactly $|\chi_G(-1)|$ different acyclic orientations,[^o6] so in this sense an acyclic orientation can be interpreted as a coloring with $-1$ colors.

## Duality {#Duality}

For [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), acyclic orientations are dual to [totally cyclic orientations](wikipedia://en/16678715), orientations in which each edge belongs to a directed cycle: if $G$ is a [planar graph](wikipedia://en/24314), and orientations of $G$ are transferred to orientations of the [planar dual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planar_dual) graph of $G$ by turning each edge 90 degrees clockwise, then a totally cyclic orientation of $G$ corresponds in this way to an acyclic orientation of the dual graph and vice versa.[^o8]

Like the chromatic polynomial, the [Tutte polynomial](wikipedia://en/3892303) $T_G$ of a graph $G$, can be used to count the number of acyclic orientations of $G$ as $T_G(2,0)$. The duality between acyclic and totally cyclic orientations of planar graphs extends in this form to nonplanar graphs as well: the Tutte polynomial of the dual graph of a planar graph is obtained by swapping the arguments of $T_G$, and the number of totally cyclic orientations of a graph $G$ is $T_G(0,2)$, also obtained by swapping the arguments of the formula for the number of acyclic orientations.[^o9]

## Edge flipping {#Edge_flipping}

The set of all acyclic orientations of a given graph may be given the structure of a [partial cube](wikipedia://en/30697444), in which two acyclic orientations are adjacent whenever they differ in the direction of a single edge.[^o11] This implies that when two different acyclic orientations of the same graph differ in the directions of $k$ edges, it is possible to transform one of the orientations into the other one by a sequence of $k$ changes of orientation of a single edge, such that each of the intermediate states of this sequence of transformations is also acyclic.

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/generalization cue=heading name="Special cases"}

## Special cases {#Special_cases}

*Image: Polytree.svg.* A polytree, the result of acyclically orienting a tree

Every orientation of a [tree](wikipedia://en/48560) is acyclic. The directed acyclic graph resulting from such an orientation is called a [polytree](wikipedia://en/1731488).[^o14]

An acyclic orientation of a [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) is called a [transitive tournament](wikipedia://en/1427502), and is equivalent to a [total ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_ordering) of the graph's vertices. In such an orientation there is in particular exactly one source and exactly one sink.

More generally, an acyclic orientation of an arbitrary graph that has a unique source and a unique sink is called a [bipolar orientation](wikipedia://en/40147217).[^o15]

A [transitive orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_orientation) of a graph is an acyclic orientation that equals its own [transitive closure](wikipedia://en/292239). Not every graph has a transitive orientation; the graphs that do are the [comparability graphs](wikipedia://en/6978672).[^o16] Complete graphs are special cases of comparability graphs, and transitive tournaments are special cases of transitive orientations.

:::
