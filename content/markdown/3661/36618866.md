# Katydid sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Katydid sequence"}

The **Katydid sequence** is a [sequence](wikipedia://en/51421) of numbers first defined in [Clifford A. Pickover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_A._Pickover)'s book *Wonders of Numbers* (2001).

:::

## Description {#Description}

A Katydid sequence is the smallest sequence of integers that can be reached from 1 by a sequence of the two operations *n* ↦ 2*n* + 2 and 7*n* + 7 (in any order).[^o3] For instance, applying the first operation to 1 produces the number 4, and applying the second operation to 4 produces the number 35, both of which are in the sequence.

The first 10 elements of the sequence are:

1, 4, 10, 14, 22, 30, 35, 46, 62, 72.

## Repetitions {#Repetitions}

Pickover asked whether there exist numbers that can be reached by more than one sequence of operations.[^o3] The answer is yes. For instance, 1814526 can be reached by the two sequences **1, 4, 10, 22, 46, 329, 660, 4627, 9256, 18514, 37030, 259217, 1814526** and **1, 14, 30, 62, 441, 884, 1770, 3542, 7086, 14174, 28350, 56702, 113406, 226814, 453630, 907262, 1814526**.
