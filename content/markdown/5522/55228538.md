# Co-Hopfian group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Co-Hopfian group"}

In the mathematical subject of [group theory](wikipedia://en/41890), a **co-Hopfian group** is a [group](wikipedia://en/19447) that is not [isomorphic](wikipedia://en/14828) to any of its proper [subgroups](wikipedia://en/28011). The notion is dual to that of a [Hopfian group](wikipedia://en/11486001), named after [Heinz Hopf](wikipedia://en/543082).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A group *G* is called **co-Hopfian** if whenever $\varphi:G\to G$ is an [injective](wikipedia://en/45196) [group homomorphism](wikipedia://en/12396) then $\varphi$ is [surjective](wikipedia://en/27873), that is $\varphi(G)=G$.[^o5]

:::

## Examples and non-examples {#Examples_and_non-examples}

- Every finite group *G* is co-Hopfian.
- The infinite cyclic group $\mathbb Z$ is not co-Hopfian since $f:\mathbb Z\to \mathbb Z, f(n)=2n$ is an injective but non-surjective homomorphism.
- The additive group of real numbers $\mathbb R$ is not co-Hopfian, since $\mathbb R$ is an infinite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb Q$ and therefore, as a group $\mathbb R\cong \mathbb R\times \mathbb R$.[^o5]
- The additive group of rational numbers $\mathbb Q$ and the quotient group $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ are co-Hopfian.[^o5]
- The multiplicative group $\mathbb Q^\ast$ of nonzero rational numbers is not co-Hopfian, since the map $\mathbb Q^\ast\to\mathbb Q^\ast, q\mapsto \operatorname{sign}(q)\,q^2$ is an injective but non-surjective homomorphism.[^o5] In the same way, the group $\mathbb Q^{\ast}_+$ of positive rational numbers is not co-Hopfian.
- The multiplicative group $\mathbb C^\ast$ of nonzero complex numbers is not co-Hopfian.[^o5]
- For every $n\ge 1$ the [free abelian group](wikipedia://en/247296) $\mathbb Z^n$ is not co-Hopfian.[^o5]
- For every $n\ge 1$ the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) $F_n$ is not co-Hopfian.[^o5]
- There exists a finitely generated non-elementary (that is, not virtually cyclic) [virtually free group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtually_free_group) which is co-Hopfian. Thus a subgroup of finite index in a finitely generated co-Hopfian group need not be co-Hopfian, and being co-Hopfian is not a [quasi-isometry](wikipedia://en/12747259) invariant for finitely generated groups.[^o16]
- [Baumslag–Solitar groups](wikipedia://en/11485892) $BS(1,m)$, where $m\ge 1$, are not co-Hopfian.[^o24]
- If *G* is the [fundamental group](wikipedia://en/11004) of a closed aspherical manifold with nonzero [Euler characteristic](wikipedia://en/148420) (or with nonzero [simplicial volume](wikipedia://en/11197892) or nonzero [L<sup>2</sup>-Betti number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L2-Betti_number)), then *G* is co-Hopfian.[^o15]
- If *G* is the fundamental group of a closed connected oriented irreducible 3-manifold *M* then *G* is co-Hopfian if and only if no finite cover of *M* is a torus bundle over the circle or the product of a circle and a closed surface.[^o7]
- If *G* is an irreducible lattice in a real [semi-simple Lie group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-simple_Lie_group) and *G* is not a [virtually free group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtually_free_group) then *G* is co-Hopfian.[^o8] E.g. this fact applies to the group $SL(n,\mathbb Z)$ for $n\ge 3$.
- If *G* is a one-ended torsion-free [word-hyperbolic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word-hyperbolic_group) then *G* is co-Hopfian, by a result of [Sela](wikipedia://en/19309289).[^o9]
- If *G* is the fundamental group of a complete finite volume smooth Riemannian *n*-manifold (where *n* > 2) of pinched negative curvature then *G* is co-Hopfian.[^o10]
- The [mapping class group](wikipedia://en/793295) of a closed hyperbolic surface is co-Hopfian.[^o11]
- The group [Out(*F<sub>n</sub>*)](wikipedia://en/12747184) (where *n*>2) is co-Hopfian.[^o12]
- Delzant and Polyagailo gave a characterization of co-Hopficity for geometrically finite [Kleinian groups](wikipedia://en/1362652) of isometries of $\mathbb H^n$ without 2-torsion.[^o13]
- A [right-angled Artin group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-angled_Artin_group) $A(\Gamma)$ (where $\Gamma$ is a finite nonempty graph) is not co-Hopfian; sending every standard generator of $A(\Gamma)$ to a power $>1$ defines and endomorphism of $A(\Gamma)$ which is injective but not surjective.[^o14]
- A finitely generated torsion-free [nilpotent group](wikipedia://en/144569) *G* may be either co-Hopfian or not co-Hopfian, depending on the properties of its associated rational [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944).[^o15][^o16]
- If *G* is a [relatively hyperbolic group](wikipedia://en/21993809) and $\varphi:G\to G$ is an injective but non-surjective endomorphism of *G* then either $\varphi^k(G)$ is parabolic for some *k* >1 or *G* splits over a virtually cyclic or a parabolic subgroup.[^o17]
- [Grigorchuk group](wikipedia://en/18707371) *G* of intermediate growth is not co-Hopfian.[^o18]
- [Thompson group](wikipedia://en/612245) *F* is not co-Hopfian.[^o19]
- There exists a [finitely generated group](wikipedia://en/963042) *G* which is not co-Hopfian but has [Kazhdan's property (T)](wikipedia://en/1362724).[^o20]
- If *G* is Higman's [universal finitely presented group](wikipedia://en/4061200) then *G* is not co-Hopfian, and *G* cannot be embedded in a finitely generated recursively presented co-Hopfian group.[^o21]

## Generalizations and related notions {#Generalizations_and_related_notions}

- A group *G* is called **finitely co-Hopfian**[^o23] if whenever $\varphi:G\to G$ is an injective endomorphism whose image has finite index in *G* then $\varphi(G)=G$. For example, for $n\ge 2$ the [free group](wikipedia://en/59735) $F_n$ is not co-Hopfian but it is finitely co-Hopfian.
- A finitely generated group *G* is called **scale-invariant** if there exists a nested sequence of subgroups of finite index of *G*, each isomorphic to *G*, and whose intersection is a finite group.[^o24]
- A group *G* is called **dis-cohopfian**[^o16] if there exists an injective endomorphism $\varphi:G\to G$ such that $\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty \varphi^n(G)=\{1\}$.
- In [coarse geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarse_geometry), a metric space *X* is called **quasi-isometrically co-Hopf** if every [quasi-isometric embedding](wikipedia://en/12747259) $f:X\to X$ is coarsely surjective (that is, is a quasi-isometry). Similarly, *X* is called **coarsely co-Hopf** if every [coarse embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coarse_embedding) $f:X\to X$ is coarsely surjective.[^o25]
- In [metric geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_geometry), a metric space *K* is called **quasisymmetrically co-Hopf** if every [quasisymmetric embedding](wikipedia://en/30220726) $K\to K$ is onto.[^o26]
