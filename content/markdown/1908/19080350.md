# Lambert summation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lambert summation"}

In [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [analytic number theory](wikipedia://en/251513), **Lambert summation** is a summability method for summing infinite series related to [Lambert series](wikipedia://en/1367040) specially relevant in analytic number theory.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Define the Lambert kernel by $L(x)=\log(1/x)\frac{x}{1-x}$ with $L(1)=1$. Note that $L(x^n)>0$ is decreasing as a function of $n$ when $0<x<1$. A sum $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n$ is Lambert summable to $A$ if $\lim_{x\to 1^-}\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n L(x^n)=A$, written $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n=A\,\,(\mathrm{L})$.

:::

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Abelian and Tauberian theorem"}

## Abelian and Tauberian theorem {#Abelian_and_Tauberian_theorem}

[Abelian theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_theorem): If a series is convergent to $A$ then it is Lambert summable to $A$.

[Tauberian theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauberian_theorem): Suppose that $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ is Lambert summable to $A$. Then it is Abel summable to $A$. In particular, if $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n$ is Lambert summable to $A$ and $na_n\geq -C$ then $\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n$ converges to $A$.

The Tauberian theorem was first proven by [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599) and [John Edensor Littlewood](wikipedia://en/158371) but was not independent of number theory, in fact they used a number-theoretic estimate which is somewhat stronger than the prime number theorem itself. The unsatisfactory situation around the Lambert Tauberian theorem was resolved by [Norbert Wiener](wikipedia://en/63185).

:::

::: {#o7 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mu(n)}{n} = 0 \,(\mathrm{L})$, where μ is the [Möbius function](wikipedia://en/20961). Hence if this series converges at all, it converges to zero. Note that the sequence $\frac{\mu(n)}{n}$ satisfies the Tauberian condition, therefore the Tauberian theorem implies $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\mu(n)}{n}=0$ in the ordinary sense. This is equivalent to the [prime number theorem](wikipedia://en/23692).
- $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\Lambda(n)-1}{n}=-2\gamma\,\,(\mathrm{L})$ where $\Lambda$ is [von Mangoldt function](wikipedia://en/1928862) and $\gamma$ is [Euler's constant](wikipedia://en/148594). By the Tauberian theorem, the ordinary sum converges and in particular converges to $-2\gamma$. This is equivalent to $\psi(x)\sim x$ where $\psi$ is the second [Chebyshev function](wikipedia://en/5793598).

:::
