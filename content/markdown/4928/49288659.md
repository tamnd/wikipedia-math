# The Archimedeans

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="The Archimedeans"}

**The Archimedeans** are the mathematical [society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_social_activities_at_the_University_of_Cambridge) of the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge), founded in 1935.[^o4] It currently has over 2000 active members,[^o3] many of them alumni, making it one of the largest student societies in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge). The society hosts regular talks at the [Centre for Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Mathematical_Sciences_%28Cambridge%29), including in the past by many well-known speakers in the field of mathematics. It publishes two magazines, [*Eureka*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_%28University_of_Cambridge_magazine%29) and *QARCH*.[^o4]

:::

One of several aims of the society, as laid down in its constitution, is to encourage co-operation between the existing mathematical societies of individual [Cambridge colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleges_of_the_University_of_Cambridge), which at present are just the Adams Society of [St John's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John's_College,_Cambridge), Emmanuel Mathematical Society, the Hyperbolics (St Catherine's College), Queens' College Mathematics Society and the [Trinity Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/21274363), but in the past have included many more.

The society is mentioned in [G. H. Hardy](wikipedia://en/87599)'s essay [A Mathematician's Apology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematician's_Apology).

Past presidents of The Archimedeans include [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698) and [Richard Taylor](wikipedia://en/646931).

## Activity {#Activity}

The main focus of the society's activities are the regular talks, which generally concern topics from mathematics or theoretical physics, and are accessible to students on an undergraduate level. Among the list of recent speakers are [Fields medalists](wikipedia://en/10859) [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698), [Wendelin Werner](wikipedia://en/6607797) and [Alain Connes](wikipedia://en/340), as well as authors [Ian Stewart](wikipedia://en/332183) and [Simon Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Singh). Many of the speakers are international, and are hosted by The Archimedeans during their visit.

After exams and University-wide project deadlines, the society is also known to organise social events.

## Publications {#Publications}

*[Eureka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eureka_%28University_of_Cambridge_magazine%29)* is a mathematical journal that is published annually by The Archimedeans. It includes articles on a variety of topics in mathematics, written by students and academics from all over the world, as well as a short summary of the activities of the society, [problem sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problem_set), puzzles, artwork and book reviews. The magazine has been published 65 times since 1939,[^o7] and authors include many famous mathematicians and scientists such as [Paul Erdős](wikipedia://en/601284), [Martin Gardner](wikipedia://en/20435), [Douglas Hofstadter](wikipedia://en/8758), [Godfrey Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Godfrey_Hardy), [Béla Bollobás](wikipedia://en/614956), [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807), [Stephen Hawking](wikipedia://en/19376148), [Roger Penrose](wikipedia://en/26193), [Ian Stewart](wikipedia://en/332183), [Chris Budd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Budd_%28mathematician%29), Fields Medallist [Timothy Gowers](wikipedia://en/365715) and [Nobel laureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize) [Paul Dirac](wikipedia://en/24742).

The Archimedeans also publish *QARCH*, a magazine containing problem sets and solutions or partial solutions submitted by readers. It is published on an irregular basis and distributed free of charge.
