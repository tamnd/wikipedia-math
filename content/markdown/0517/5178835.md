# PROP (category theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="PROP"}

In [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), a branch of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **PROP** is a [symmetric](wikipedia://en/3865249) strict [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) whose objects are the natural numbers *n* identified with the finite sets $\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}$ and whose tensor product is given on objects by the addition on numbers.[^o2] Because of “symmetric”, for each *n*, the [symmetric group](wikipedia://en/28901) on *n* letters is given as a subgroup of the [automorphism group](wikipedia://en/56661772#In_category_theory) of *n*. The name PROP is an abbreviation of "PROduct and [Permutation category](wikipedia://en/59512190)".

:::

The notion was introduced by [Frank Adams](wikipedia://en/1223959) and [Saunders Mac Lane](wikipedia://en/336574); the topological version of it was later given by [Michael Boardman](wikipedia://en/8770110) and Rainer Vogt.[^o3] Following them, [J. P. May](wikipedia://en/14644098) then introduced the term “[operad](wikipedia://en/3126358)”, which is a particular kind of PROP, for the object that Boardman and Vogt called the "category of operators in standard form".

There are the following inclusions of [full subcategories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_subcategories):[^o4]

$$\mathsf{Operads} \subset \tfrac{1}{2}\mathsf{PROP} \subset \mathsf{PROP}$$

where the first category is the category of (symmetric) operads.

## Examples and variants {#Examples_and_variants}

An important *elementary* class of PROPs are the sets $\mathcal{R}^{\bullet\times\bullet}$ of *all* matrices (regardless of number of rows and columns) over some fixed ring $\mathcal{R}$. More concretely, these matrices are the *morphisms* of the PROP; the objects can be taken as either $\{\mathcal{R}^n\}_{n=0}^\infty$ (sets of vectors) or just as the plain natural numbers (since [objects](wikipedia://en/5869) *do not have to* be sets with some structure). In this example:

- **Composition** $\circ$ of morphisms is ordinary [matrix multiplication](wikipedia://en/125280).
- The **identity morphism** of an object $n$ (or $\mathcal{R}^n$) is the [identity matrix](wikipedia://en/59718) with size $n$.
- The **product** $\otimes$ acts on objects like addition ($m \otimes n = m+n$ or $\mathcal{R}^m \otimes \mathcal{R}^n = \mathcal{R}^{m+n}$) and on morphisms like an operation of constructing [block diagonal matrices](wikipedia://en/457464#Block_diagonal_matrices): $\alpha \otimes \beta = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha & 0 \\ 0 & \beta \end{bmatrix}$.
  - The compatibility of composition and product thus boils down to

$$(A \otimes B) \circ (C \otimes D) = \begin{bmatrix} A & 0 \\ 0 & B \end{bmatrix} \circ \begin{bmatrix} C & 0 \\ 0 & D \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} AC & 0 \\ 0 & BD \end{bmatrix} = (A \circ C) \otimes (B \circ D).$$

  - As an [edge case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_case), matrices with no rows ($0 \times n$ matrices) or no columns ($m \times 0$ matrices) are allowed, and with respect to multiplication count as being zero matrices. The $\otimes$ identity is the $0 \times 0$ matrix.
- The **permutations** in the PROP are the [permutation matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_matrices). Thus the **left action** of a permutation on a matrix (morphism of this PROP) is to permute the rows, whereas the **right action** is to permute the columns.

There are also PROPs of matrices where the product $\otimes$ is the [Kronecker product](wikipedia://en/712430), but in that class of PROPs the matrices must all be of the form $k^m \times k^n$ (sides are all powers of some common [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_%28exponentiation%29) $k$); these are the coordinate counterparts of appropriate symmetric monoidal categories of vector spaces under tensor product.

Further examples of PROPs:

- the [discrete category](wikipedia://en/808519) $\mathbb{N}$ of natural numbers,
- the category **[FinSet](wikipedia://en/25044975)** of natural numbers and functions between them,
- the category **Bij** of natural numbers and bijections,
- the category **Inj** of natural numbers and injections.

If the requirement “symmetric” is dropped, then one gets the notion of **PRO** category. If “symmetric” is replaced by [**b**raided](wikipedia://en/1470466), then one gets the notion of **PROB** category.

- the category **Bij<sub>Braid</sub>**of natural numbers, equipped with the [braid group](wikipedia://en/550138) *B<sub>n</sub>*as the automorphisms of each *n*(and no other morphisms).

is a PROB but not a PROP.

- the [augmented simplex category](wikipedia://en/1028841#Augmented_simplex_category) $\Delta_+$ of natural numbers and [order-preserving functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order-preserving_function).

is an example of PRO that is not even a PROB.

## Algebras of a PRO {#Algebras_of_a_PRO}

An algebra of a PRO $P$ in a [monoidal category](wikipedia://en/330604) $C$ is a strict [monoidal functor](wikipedia://en/4278182) from $P$ to $C$. Every PRO $P$ and category $C$ give rise to a category $\mathrm{Alg}_P^C$ of algebras whose objects are the algebras of $P$ in $C$ and whose morphisms are the natural transformations between them.

For example:

- an algebra of $\mathbb{N}$ is just an object of $C$,
- an algebra of **FinSet** is a commutative [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) of $C$,
- an algebra of $\Delta$ is a [monoid object](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoid_object) in $C$.

More precisely, what we mean here by "the algebras of $\Delta$ in $C$ are the monoid objects in $C$" for example is that the category of algebras of $P$ in $C$ is [equivalent](wikipedia://en/379051) to the category of monoids in $C$.
