# Leray spectral sequence

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Leray spectral sequence"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Leray spectral sequence** was a pioneering example in [homological algebra](wikipedia://en/139410), introduced in 1946[^o2][^o3] by [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430). It is usually seen nowadays as a special case of the [Grothendieck spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3017363).

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map of topological spaces, which in particular gives a [functor](wikipedia://en/10987) $f_*$ from [sheaves of abelian groups](wikipedia://en/245466) on $X$ to sheaves of abelian groups on $Y$. Composing this with the functor $\Gamma$ of taking sections on $\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(Y)$ is the same as taking sections on $\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(X)$, by the definition of the direct image functor $f_*$:

$$\mathrm{Sh_{Ab}} (X) \xrightarrow{f_*} \mathrm{Sh_{Ab}}(Y) \xrightarrow{\Gamma} \mathrm{Ab}.$$

Thus the [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875) of $\Gamma \circ f_*$ compute the [sheaf cohomology](wikipedia://en/1055357) for $X$:

$$R^i (\Gamma \cdot f_*)(\mathcal{F})=H^i(X,\mathcal{F}).$$

But because $f_*$ and $\Gamma$ send [injective objects](wikipedia://en/473779) in $\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(X)$ to $\Gamma$-[acyclic objects](wikipedia://en/5818194) in $\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(Y)$, there is a [spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/803894)[^o6]<sup>pg 33,19</sup> whose second page is

$$E^{pq}_2=(R^p\Gamma \cdot R^q f_*)(\mathcal{F})=H^p(Y,R^qf_*(\mathcal{F})) ,$$

and which converges to

$$E^{p+q} = R^{p+q}(\Gamma \circ f_*)(\mathcal{F})= H^{p+q}(X,\mathcal{F}) .$$

This is called the **Leray spectral sequence**.

### Generalizing to other sheaves and complexes of sheaves {#Generalizing_to_other_sheaves_and_complexes_of_sheaves}

Note this result can be generalized by instead considering sheaves of modules over a [locally constant sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locally_constant_sheaf) of rings $\underline{A}$ for a fixed commutative ring $A$. Then, the sheaves will be sheaves of $\underline{A}$-modules, where for an open set $U \subset X$, such a sheaf $\mathcal{F} \in \text{Sh}_{\underline{A}}(X)$ is an $\underline{A}(U)$-module for $\mathcal{F}(U)$. In addition, instead of sheaves, we could consider complexes of sheaves bounded below $\mathcal{F}^\bullet \in D^+_{\underline{A}}(X)$ for the [derived category](wikipedia://en/985897) of $\text{Sh}_{\underline{A}}(X)$. Then, one replaces sheaf cohomology with [sheaf hypercohomology](wikipedia://en/10324484).

::: {#o9 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

The existence of the Leray spectral sequence is a direct application of the [Grothendieck spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3017363)[^o6]<sup>pg 19</sup>. This states that given additive functors

$$\mathcal{A} \xrightarrow{G}\mathcal{B} \xrightarrow{F} \mathcal{C}$$

between [Abelian categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abelian_categories) having [enough injectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enough_injectives), $F$ a [left-exact functor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_exact_functor), and $G$ sending injective objects to $F$-acyclic objects, then there is an isomorphism of [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875)

$$R^+(F\circ G) \simeq R^+F\circ R^+G$$

for the derived categories $D^+(\mathcal{A}),D^+(\mathcal{B}), D^+(\mathcal{C})$. In the example above, we have the composition of derived functors

$$D^+(\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(X)) \xrightarrow{Rf_*} D^+(\text{Sh}_\text{Ab}(Y)) \xrightarrow{\Gamma} D^+(\text{Ab}).$$

:::

## Classical definition {#Classical_definition}

Let $f\colon X\to Y$ be a continuous map of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifolds). If $\mathcal{U} = \{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ is an open cover of $Y$, form the [Čech complex](wikipedia://en/53325526) of a sheaf $\mathcal{F} \in \text{Sh}(X)$ with respect to cover $f^{-1}(U)$ of $X$:

$$\text{C}^p(f^{-1}\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{F})$$

The boundary maps $d^p\colon C^p \to C^{p+1}$ and maps $\delta^q\colon \Omega^q_X \to \Omega_X^{q+1}$ of sheaves on $X$ together give a boundary map on the double complex $\text{C}^p(f^{-1}\mathcal{U}, \Omega_X^q)$

$$D=d+\delta \colon C^\bullet(f^{-1}\mathcal{U},\Omega_X^\bullet)\longrightarrow C^\bullet(f^{-1}\mathcal{U},\Omega_X^\bullet) .$$

This [double complex](wikipedia://en/11797854) is also a single complex graded by $n=p+q$, with respect to which $D$ is a boundary map. If each finite intersection of the $U_i$ is [diffeomorphic](wikipedia://en/8564) to $\mathbb{R}^n$, one can show that the cohomology

$$H_D^n( C^\bullet(f^{-1}\mathcal{U},\Omega_X^\bullet)) = H_\text{dR}^n(X,\mathbb{R})$$

of this complex is the [de Rham cohomology](wikipedia://en/221530) of $X$.[^o11] Moreover,[^o11][^o12] any double complex has a spectral sequence *E* with

$$E_\infty^{n-p,p} = \text{the }p\text{th graded part of } H^n_{dR}( C^\bullet(f^{-1}\mathcal{U},\Omega_X^\bullet))$$

(so that the sum of these is $H^n_{dR}$), and

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(f^{-1}\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{H}^q),$$

where $\mathcal{H}^q$ is the presheaf on *Y* sending $U \mapsto H^q(f^{-1}(U), F)$. In this context, this is called the Leray spectral sequence.

The modern definition subsumes this, because the higher direct image functor $R^pf_*(F)$ is the sheafification of the presheaf $U \mapsto H^q(f^{-1}(U), F)$.

::: {#o14 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

- Let $X,F$ be [smooth manifolds](wikipedia://en/2119219), and $X$ be [simply connected](wikipedia://en/523879), so $\pi_1(X) = 0$. We calculate the Leray spectral sequence of the projection $f\colon X\times F \to X$. If the cover $\mathcal{U} = \{U_i\}_{i \in I}$ is good (finite intersections are $\mathbb{R}^n$) then

$$\mathcal{H}^p(f^{-1}U_i)\simeq H^q(F)$$

Since $X$ is simply connected, any locally constant presheaf is constant, so this is the constant presheaf $H^q(F) = \underline{\mathbb{R}}^{n_q}$. So the second page of the Leray spectral sequence is

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(f^{-1}\mathcal{U}, H^q(F))=H^p(f^{-1}\mathcal{U}, \mathbb{R})\otimes H^q(F)$$

As the cover $\{f^{-1}(U_i)\}_{i \in I}$ of $X\times F$ is also good, $H^p(f^{-1}(U_i);\mathbb{R}) \cong H^p(f;\mathbb{R})$. So

$$E_2^{p,q} = H^p(X)\otimes H^q(F) \ \Longrightarrow \ H^{p+q}(X\times F,\mathbb{R})$$

Here is the first place we use that $f$ is a projection and not just a fibre bundle: every element of $E_2$ is an actual closed [differential form](wikipedia://en/221519) on all of $X\times F$, so applying both *d* and $\delta$ to them gives zero. Thus $E_\infty = E_2$. This proves the [Künneth theorem](wikipedia://en/918047) for $X$ simply connected:

$$H^\bullet(X\times Y,\mathbb{R})\simeq H^\bullet(X)\otimes H^\bullet(Y)$$

- If $f\colon X \to Y$ is a general [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) with fibre $F$, the above applies, except that $V^p \to H^p(f^{-1}V,H^q)$ is only a locally constant presheaf, not constant.
- All example computations with the [Serre spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3085825) are the Leray sequence for the constant sheaf.

:::

## Degeneration theorem {#Degeneration_theorem}

In the category of [quasi-projective varieties](wikipedia://en/1321785) over $\mathbb{C}$, there is a degeneration theorem proved by [Pierre Deligne](wikipedia://en/394544) and Blanchard for the Leray spectral sequence, which states that a smooth projective morphism of varieties $f\colon X \to Y$ gives us that the $E_2$-page of the spectral sequence for $\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X$ degenerates, hence

$$H^k(X;\mathbb{Q}) \cong \bigoplus_{p + q = k} H^{p}(Y;\mathbf{R}^qf_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)).$$

Easy examples can be computed if $Y$ is simply connected; for example a complete intersection of dimension $\geq 2$ (this is because of the [Hurewicz homomorphism](wikipedia://en/591587) and the [Lefschetz hyperplane theorem](wikipedia://en/3459657)). In this case the local systems $\mathbf{R}^qf_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X)$ will have trivial monodromy, hence $\mathbf{R}^qf_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X) \cong \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y^{\oplus l_q}$. For example, consider a smooth family $f\colon X\to Y$ of genus 3 curves over a smooth [K3 surface](wikipedia://en/646116). Then, we have that

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{R}^0f_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X) &\cong \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y \\ \mathbf{R}^1f_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X) &\cong \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y^{\oplus 6} \\ \mathbf{R}^2f_*(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_X) &\cong \underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y \end{aligned}$$

giving us the $E_2$-page

$$E_2 = E_\infty = \begin{bmatrix} H^0(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) & 0 & H^2(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) & 0 & H^4(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) \\ H^0(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y^{\oplus 6}) & 0 & H^2(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y^{\oplus 6}) & 0 & H^4(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y^{\oplus 6}) \\ H^0(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) & 0 & H^2(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) & 0 & H^4(Y;\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_Y) \end{bmatrix}$$

### Example with monodromy {#Example_with_monodromy}

Another important example of a smooth projective family is the family associated to the elliptic curves

$$y^2 = x(x-1)(x-t)$$

over $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty \}$. Here the monodromy around 0 and 1 can be computed using [Picard–Lefschetz theory](wikipedia://en/34794974), giving the monodromy around $\infty$ by composing local monodromies.

:::

## History and connection to other spectral sequences {#History_and_connection_to_other_spectral_sequences}

At the time of Leray's work, neither of the two concepts involved (spectral sequence, sheaf cohomology) had reached anything like a definitive state. Therefore it is rarely the case that Leray's result is quoted in its original form. After much work, in the seminar of [Henri Cartan](wikipedia://en/362536) in particular, the modern statement was obtained, though not the general Grothendieck spectral sequence.

Earlier (1948/9) the implications for [fiber bundles](wikipedia://en/276526) were extracted in a form formally identical to that of the [Serre spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3085825), which makes no use of sheaves. This treatment, however, applied to [Alexander–Spanier cohomology](wikipedia://en/694843) with [compact supports](wikipedia://en/381013), as applied to [proper maps](wikipedia://en/1190262) of locally compact Hausdorff spaces, as the derivation of the spectral sequence required a [fine sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_sheaf) of real [differential graded algebras](wikipedia://en/2415863) on the total space, which was obtained by pulling back the [de Rham complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Rham_complex) along an embedding into a sphere. [Jean-Pierre Serre](wikipedia://en/205483), who needed a spectral sequence in *homology* that applied to [path space fibrations](wikipedia://en/48842115), whose total spaces are almost never locally compact, thus was unable to use the original Leray spectral sequence and so derived a related spectral sequence whose cohomological variant agrees, for a compact fiber bundle on a well-behaved space with the sequence above.

In the formulation achieved by [Alexander Grothendieck](wikipedia://en/2042) by about 1957, the Leray spectral sequence is the [Grothendieck spectral sequence](wikipedia://en/3017363) for the composition of two [derived functors](wikipedia://en/433875).
