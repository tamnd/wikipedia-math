# Piston motion equations

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Piston motion equations"}

The [reciprocating motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocating_motion) of a non-offset [piston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston) connected to a rotating [crank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crank_%28mechanism%29) through a [connecting rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecting_rod) (common in [internal combustion engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion_engine)) can be expressed by [equations of motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equations_of_motion). This article shows how these equations of motion can be derived using [calculus](wikipedia://en/7921) as [functions](wikipedia://en/185427) of angle *([angle](wikipedia://en/1196) [domain](wikipedia://en/50263))* and of time *([time domain](wikipedia://en/173419))*.

:::

### Definitions {#Definitions}

From the geometry shown in the diagram above, the following variables are defined:

$l$ [rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecting_rod) length (distance between [piston pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudgeon_pin) and [crank pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crank_pin))

$r$ [crank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crank_%28mechanism%29) [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) (distance between crank center and crank pin, i.e. half [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke_%28engine%29))

$A$ crank [angle](wikipedia://en/1196) (from [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28engine%29) [bore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bore_%28engine%29) centerline at [TDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_centre_%28engineering%29))

$x$ piston pin [position](wikipedia://en/859275) (distance upward from crank center along cylinder bore centerline)

The following variables are also defined:

$v$ piston pin [velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) (upward from crank center along cylinder bore centerline)

$a$ piston pin [acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) (upward from crank center along cylinder bore centerline)

$\omega$ crank [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) (in the same direction/sense as crank angle $A$)

:::

### Angular velocity {#Angular_velocity}

The [frequency](wikipedia://en/10779) ([Hz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hz)) of the [crankshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crankshaft)'s rotation is related to the engine's speed ([revolutions per minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_per_minute)) as follows:

$$\nu= \frac{\mathrm{RPM}}{60}$$

So the [angular velocity](wikipedia://en/65927) ([radians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radians)/s) of the crankshaft is:

$$\omega= 2\pi\cdot\nu= 2\pi\cdot \frac{\mathrm{RPM}}{60}$$

### Triangle relation {#Triangle_relation}

As shown in the diagram, the [crank pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crank_pin), crank center and piston pin form triangle NOP. By the [cosine law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosine_law) it is seen that:

$$l^2 = r^2 + x^2 - 2\cdot r\cdot x\cdot\cos A$$

where $l$ and $r$ are constant and $x$ varies as $A$ changes.

## Equations with respect to angular position ([angle](wikipedia://en/1196) [domain](wikipedia://en/50263)) {#Equations_with_respect_to_angular_position_(angle_domain)}

Angle domain equations are expressed as functions of angle.

### Deriving angle domain equations {#Deriving_angle_domain_equations}

The angle domain equations of the piston's reciprocating motion are derived from the system's geometry equations as follows.

### [Position (geometry)](wikipedia://en/1345771) {#Position_(geometry)}

Position with respect to crank angle (from the triangle relation, [completing the square](wikipedia://en/303500), utilizing the [Pythagorean identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_identity), and rearranging):

$$\begin{array}{lcl} l^2 = r^2 + x^2 - 2\cdot r\cdot x\cdot\cos A \\ l^2 - r^2 = (x - r\cdot\cos A)^2 - r^2\cdot\cos^2 A \\ l^2 - r^2 + r^2\cdot\cos^2 A = (x - r\cdot\cos A)^2 \\ l^2 - r^2\cdot(1 - \cos^2 A) = (x - r\cdot\cos A)^2 \\ l^2 - r^2\cdot\sin^2 A = (x - r \cdot \cos A)^2 \\ x = r\cdot \cos A + \sqrt{l^2 - r^2\cdot\sin^2 A} \\ \end{array}$$

### [Velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) {#Velocity}

Velocity with respect to crank angle (take first [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921), using the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113)):

$$\begin{array}{lcl} x' & = & \frac{dx}{dA} \\ & = & - r\cdot\sin A + \frac{(\frac{1}{2})\cdot(-2)\cdot r^2\cdot\sin A\cdot\cos A}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}} \\ & = & -r\cdot\sin A - \frac{r^2\cdot\sin A\cdot\cos A}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}} \\ \end{array}$$

### [Acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) {#Acceleration}

Acceleration with respect to crank angle (take second [derivative](wikipedia://en/7921), using the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113) and the [quotient rule](wikipedia://en/209128)):

$$\begin{array}{lcl} x'' &= & \frac{d^2x}{dA^2} \\ &= & -r\cdot\cos A - \frac{r^2\cdot\cos^2 A}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}}-\frac{-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}} - \frac{r^2\cdot\sin A\cdot\cos A \cdot\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot(-2)\cdot r^2\cdot\sin A\cdot\cos A}{\left (\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A} \right )^3} \\ &= & -r\cdot\cos A - \frac{r^2\cdot\left(\cos^2 A - \sin^2 A\right)}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}}-\frac{r^4\cdot\sin^2 A\cdot\cos^2 A}{\left(\sqrt{l^2-r^2\cdot\sin^2 A}\right)^3} \\ \end{array}$$

### Non Simple Harmonic Motion {#Non_Simple_Harmonic_Motion}

The angle domain equations above show that the motion of the piston (connected to rod and crank) is **not** [simple harmonic motion](wikipedia://en/28437), but is modified by the motion of the rod as it swings with the rotation of the crank. This is in contrast to the [Scotch Yoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch_Yoke) which directly produces simple harmonic motion.

## Equations with respect to time ([time domain](wikipedia://en/173419)) {#Equations_with_respect_to_time_(time_domain)}

Time domain equations are expressed as functions of time.

### Angular velocity derivatives {#Angular_velocity_derivatives}

Angle is related to time by angular velocity $\omega$ as follows:

$$A = \omega t \,$$

If angular velocity $\omega$ is constant, then:

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \omega$$

and:

$$\frac{d^2 A}{dt^2} = 0$$

### Deriving time domain equations {#Deriving_time_domain_equations}

The time domain equations of the piston's reciprocating motion are derived from the angle domain equations as follows.

### Position {#Position}

Position with respect to time is simply:

$$x \,$$

### Velocity {#Velocity}

[Velocity](wikipedia://en/20491903) with respect to time (using the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113)):

$$\begin{array}{lcl} v & = & \frac{dx}{dt} \\ & = & \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot \frac{dA}{dt} \\ & = & \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot\ \omega \\ & = & x' \cdot \omega \\ \end{array}$$

### Acceleration {#Acceleration}

[Acceleration](wikipedia://en/2443) with respect to time (using the [chain rule](wikipedia://en/6113) and [product rule](wikipedia://en/248988), and the angular velocity [derivatives](wikipedia://en/3832917)):

$$\begin{array}{lcl} a & = & \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} \\ & = & \frac{d}{dt} \frac{dx}{dt} \\ & = & \frac{d}{dt} (\frac{dx}{dA} \cdot \frac{dA}{dt}) \\ & = & \frac{d}{dt} (\frac{dx}{dA}) \cdot \frac{dA}{dt} + \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot \frac{d}{dt} (\frac{dA}{dt}) \\ & = & \frac{d}{dA} (\frac{dx}{dA}) \cdot (\frac{dA}{dt})^2 + \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot \frac{d^2A}{dt^2} \\ & = & \frac{d^2x}{dA^2} \cdot (\frac{dA}{dt})^2 + \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot \frac{d^2A}{dt^2} \\ & = & \frac{d^2x}{dA^2} \cdot \omega^2 + \frac{dx}{dA} \cdot 0 \\ & = & x'' \cdot \omega^2 \\ \end{array}$$

### Scaling for angular velocity {#Scaling_for_angular_velocity}

From the foregoing, you can see that the time domain equations are simply *scaled* forms of the angle domain equations: $x$ is unscaled, $x'$ is scaled by *ω*, and $x''$ is scaled by *ω²*.

To convert the angle domain equations to time domain, first replace *A* with *ωt*, and then [scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_factor) for angular velocity as follows: multiply $x'$ by *ω*, and multiply $x''$ by *ω²*.

## Velocity maxima and minima {#Velocity_maxima_and_minima}

By definition, the velocity [maxima and minima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) occur at the acceleration zeros *(crossings of the horizontal axis)*.

### Crank angle not right-angled {#Crank_angle_not_right-angled}

The velocity maxima and minima *(see the acceleration zero crossings in the graphs below)* depend on rod length $l$ and half stroke $r$ and do **not** occur when the crank angle $A$ is right angled.

### Crank-rod angle not right angled {#Crank-rod_angle_not_right_angled}

The velocity maxima and minima **do not necessarily occur** when the crank makes a right angle with the rod. Counter-examples exist to **disprove** the statement *"velocity maxima and minima only occur when the crank-rod angle is right angled"*.

::: {#o25 .example cue=heading name="Example"}

#### Example {#Example}

For rod length 6" and crank radius 2" (as shown in the example graph below), numerically solving the acceleration zero-crossings finds the velocity maxima/minima to be at crank angles of ±73.1753°. Then, using the triangle [law of sines](wikipedia://en/113222), it is found that the rod-vertical angle is 18.6064° and the crank-rod angle is 88.2183°. Clearly, in this example, the angle between the crank and the rod is not a right angle. Summing the angles of the triangle 88.2183° + 18.6064° + 73.1753° gives 180.0000°. A single counter-example is sufficient to **disprove** the statement *"velocity maxima/minima occur when crank makes a right angle with rod"*.

:::
