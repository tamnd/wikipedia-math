# Trirectangular tetrahedron

*Image: 2D-simplex.svg.* A trirectangular tetrahedron with its base shown in green and its apex as a solid black disk. It can be constructed by a coordinate [octant](wikipedia://en/36160226) and a plane crossing all 3 axes away from the origin (*x*>0; *y*>0; *z*>0) and *x/a+y/b+z/c*\<1

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Trirectangular tetrahedron"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **trirectangular tetrahedron** is a [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) where all three [face](wikipedia://en/149926) [angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_angle) at one [vertex](wikipedia://en/11308417) are [right angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_angles). That vertex is called the *right angle* or [*apex*](wikipedia://en/6022079) of the trirectangular tetrahedron and the face opposite it is called the *[base](wikipedia://en/9579379)*. The three [edges](wikipedia://en/12158034) that meet at the right angle are called the *legs* and the perpendicular from the right angle to the base is called the *altitude* of the tetrahedron (analogous to the [altitude](wikipedia://en/161241) of a triangle).

:::

*Image: Kepler's tetrahedron in cube.png.* Kepler's drawing of a [regular tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/5242521) inscribed in a cube (on the left), and one of the four trirectangular tetrahedra that surround it (on the right), filling the cube.

An example of a trirectangular tetrahedron is a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [solid figure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_figure) near the corner of a [cube](wikipedia://en/6285) or an [octant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octant_%28geometry%29) at the origin of [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697). [Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) discovered the relationship between the cube, regular tetrahedron and trirectangular tetrahedron.[^o2]

Only the bifurcating graph of the $B_3$ [affine Coxeter group](wikipedia://en/297004#Affine_Coxeter_groups) has a Trirectangular tetrahedron fundamental domain.

::: {#o4 .statement cue=named-heading name="Metric formulas"}

## Metric formulas {#Metric_formulas}

If the legs have lengths x*, y, z* then the trirectangular tetrahedron has the [volume](wikipedia://en/32498)[^o5]

$$V=\frac{xyz}{6}.$$

The altitude *h* satisfies[^o6]

$$\frac{1}{h^2}=\frac{1}{x^2}+\frac{1}{y^2}+\frac{1}{z^2}.$$

The area $T_0$ of the base is given by[^o7]

$$T_0=\frac{xyz}{2h}.$$

The [solid angle](wikipedia://en/94102) at the right-angled vertex, from which the opposite face (the base) subtends an [octant](wikipedia://en/74955167), has measure $\pi$/2 [steradians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steradian), one eighth of the surface area of a [unit sphere](wikipedia://en/27033090).

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="De Gua's theorem"}

## De Gua's theorem {#De_Gua's_theorem}

If the [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of the base is $T_0$ and the areas of the three other (right-angled) faces are $T_1$, $T_2$ and $T_3$, then

$$T_0^2=T_1^2+T_2^2+T_3^2.$$

This is a generalization of the [Pythagorean theorem](wikipedia://en/26513034) to a tetrahedron.

:::

## Integer solution {#Integer_solution}

### Integer edges {#Integer_edges}

Trirectangular tetrahedra with integer legs $a,b,c$ and sides $d=\sqrt{b^2+c^2}, e=\sqrt{a^2+c^2}, f=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ of the base triangle exist, e.g. $a=240,b=117,c=44,d=125,e=244,f=267$ (discovered 1719 by Halcke). Here are a few more examples with integer legs and sides.

### Integer faces {#Integer_faces}

Trirectangular tetrahedra with integer faces $T_c, T_a, T_b, T_0$ and altitude *h* exist, e.g. $a=42,b=28,c=14,T_c=588,T_a=196,T_b=294,T_0=686,h=12$ without or $a=156,b=80,c=65,T_c=6240,T_a=2600,T_b=5070,T_0=8450,h=48$ with coprime $a,b,c$.
