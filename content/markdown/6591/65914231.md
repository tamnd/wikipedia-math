# Thomas J. Osler

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Thomas J. Osler"}

**Thomas Joseph Osler** (April 26, 1940 – March 26, 2023) was an American mathematician, national champion [distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_runner), and author.[^o3]

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Born in 1940 in [Camden, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden,_New_Jersey),[^o5] Osler was a graduate of [Camden High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden_High_School_%28New_Jersey%29) in 1957 and then studied physics at [Drexel University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drexel_University), graduating in 1962.[^o6][^o7] He completed his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) of [New York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University),[^o11] in 1970. His dissertation, *Leibniz Rule, the Chain Rule, and Taylor's Theorem for Fractional Derivatives*, was supervised by Samuel Karp.

## Career {#Career}

Osler taught at [Saint Joseph's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_University) and the [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute)[^o18] before joining the mathematics department at [Rowan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan_University) in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey) in 1972;[^o10] he was a full professor at Rowan University until his death.[^o11]

In mathematics, Osler is best known for his work on [fractional calculus](wikipedia://en/229939).[^o12][^o13][^o14] He also gave a series of product formulas for $\pi$ that interpolate between the formula of [Viète](wikipedia://en/1609504) and that of [Wallis](wikipedia://en/1301687).[^o15]

In 2009, the New Jersey Section of the [Mathematical Association of America](wikipedia://en/198772) gave him their Distinguished Teaching Award.[^o16][^o17] A mathematics conference was held at Rowan University in honor of his 70th birthday in 2010.[^o18]

### Running {#Running}

Osler won three national [Amateur Athletic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_Athletic_Union) championships at 25 km (1965), 30 km and 50 mi (1967).[^o20][^o21] Osler won the 1965 [Philadelphia Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Marathon), finishing the race in freezing-cold weather in a time of 2:34:07.[^o22]

*Image: Tom Osler at Fort Meade 50 mile track race (August 9 1975), later in race (solo profile shot).jpg.* Osler during 50-mile track race at Fort Meade in 1975

Osler was involved in the creation of the [Road Runners Club of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Runners_Club_of_America) with Olympian [Browning Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browning_Ross); together they were elected as co-secretaries in 1959[^o23] and were among the four first official elected officers of the newly formed club.[^o24] He served on the Amateur Athletic Union Standards Committee in 1979.[^o25] He has been credited with helping to popularize the idea of [walk breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_slow_distance) among US [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon) runners.[^o5][^o7]

In 1980, Osler was inducted into the [Road Runners Club of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Runners_Club_of_America) Hall of fame.[^o23][^o26]

### Running publications {#Running_publications}

Osler was the author of several books and booklets on running:

- *Guide to Long Distance Running* (a 20-page booklet coauthored with Edward Dodd) was published in 1965 by the South Jersey Track Club.[^o28]
- *The Conditioning of Distance Runners* (a 29-page booklet) was published in 1967 by the *[Long Distance Log](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Distance_Log)*.[^o5][^o7][^o28] It was reprinted in 1984–1985 in *[Runner's World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runner's_World)* magazine[^o29][^o30] and reprinted with a new foreword by [Amby Burfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amby_Burfoot) in 2019.[^o31]
- *Serious Runner's Handbook: Answers to Hundreds of your Running Questions* (187 pages) was published by World Publications in 1978.[^o32]
- *Ultramarathoning: The Next Challenge* (299 pages, coauthored with Edward Dodd) was also published by World Publications, in 1979.[^o33]

## Personal life and death {#Personal_life_and_death}

Osler was a resident of [Glassboro, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glassboro,_New_Jersey).[^o16]

Osler died on March 26, 2023, at the age of 82.[^o35]
