# Maillet's determinant

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maillet's determinant"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Maillet's determinant** *D*<sub>*p*</sub> is the [determinant](wikipedia://en/8468) of the [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) introduced by (Maillet 1913) whose entries are *R*(*s/r*) for *s*,*r* = 1, 2, ..., (*p* – 1)/2 ∈ **Z**/*p***Z** for an odd prime *p*, where and *R*(*a*) is the least positive residue of *a* [modulo](wikipedia://en/1352428) *p* (Muir 1930, pages 340–342).

:::

## Determinant value {#Determinant_value}

(Malo 1914) calculated the determinant *D*<sub>*p*</sub> for *p* = 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 and found that in these cases it is given by (–*p*)<sup>(*p* – 3)/2</sup>, and conjectured that it is given by this formula in general. (Carlitz Olson 1955) showed that this conjecture is incorrect; the determinant in general is given by *D*<sub>*p*</sub> = (–*p*)<sup>(*p* – 3)/2</sup>*h*<sup>−</sup>, where *h*<sup>−</sup> is the first factor of the [class number](wikipedia://en/150907) of the [cyclotomic field](wikipedia://en/30872462) generated by *p*th roots of 1, which happens to be 1 for *p* less than 23. In particular, this verifies Maillet's conjecture that the determinant is always non-zero. Chowla and Weil had previously found the same formula but did not publish it. Their results have been extended to all non-prime odd numbers by (Wang 1984).
