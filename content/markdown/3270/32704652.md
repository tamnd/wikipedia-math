# Orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle"}

In mathematics, **orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle** are families of [polynomials that are orthogonal with respect to integration](wikipedia://en/32811718) over the [unit circle](wikipedia://en/27072071) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628), for some [probability measure](wikipedia://en/45437) on the unit circle. They were introduced by (txt).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on the unit circle $\mathbb{T} =\{z\in\mathbb{C} :|z|=1\}$ and assume $\mu$ is nontrivial, i.e., its [support](wikipedia://en/381013) is an [infinite set](wikipedia://en/142679). By a combination of the [Radon-Nikodym](wikipedia://en/338746) and [Lebesgue decomposition](wikipedia://en/4316238) theorems, any such measure can be uniquely decomposed into

$$d\mu =w(\theta) \frac{d\theta}{2\pi} + d\mu_s,$$

where $d\mu_s$ is *singular* with respect to $d\theta/2\pi$ and $w \in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})$ with $wd\theta/2\pi$ the *absolutely continuous part* of $d\mu$.[^n1]

The orthogonal polynomials associated with $\mu$ are defined as

$$\Phi_n(z)=z^n + \text{lower order},$$

such that

$$\int \bar{z}^j\Phi_n(z)\,d\mu(z) =0, \quad j = 0,1,\dots,n-1.$$

:::

## The Szegő recurrence {#The_Szegő_recurrence}

The monic orthogonal Szegő polynomials satisfy a recurrence relation of the form

$$\Phi_{n+1}(z)=z\Phi_n(z)-\overline\alpha_n\Phi_n^*(z)$$

$$\Phi_{n+1}^{\ast}(z)=\Phi_n^{\ast}(z)-\alpha_n z\Phi_n(z)$$

for $n \geq 0$ and initial condition $\Phi_0=1$, with

$$\Phi_n^*(z)=z^n\overline{\Phi_n(1/\overline{z})}$$

and constants $\alpha_n$ in the open unit disk $\mathbb{D} = \{ z\in \mathbb{C} : |z|<1\}$ given by

$$\alpha_n = -\overline{\Phi_{n+1}(0)}$$

called the **Verblunsky coefficients**. [^n2] Moreover,

$$\|\Phi_{n+1}\|^{2} = \prod_{j=0}^{n} (1-|\alpha_j|^2) = (1-|\alpha_n|^2)\|\Phi_n\|^2.$$

Geronimus' theorem states that the Verblunsky coefficients associated with $d\mu$ are the [Schur parameters](wikipedia://en/41513534):[^n3]

$$\alpha_n(d\mu) = \gamma_n$$

::: {#o10 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Verblunsky's theorem"}

### Verblunsky's theorem {#Verblunsky's_theorem}

[Verblunsky](wikipedia://en/33163790)'s theorem states that for any sequence of numbers $\{\alpha_{j}^{(0)}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}$ in $\mathbb{D}$ there is a unique nontrivial probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb{T}$ with $\alpha_j(d\mu)=\alpha_j^{(0)}$.[^n4]

:::

::: {#o13 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Baxter's theorem"}

### Baxter's theorem {#Baxter's_theorem}

Baxter's theorem states that the Verblunsky coefficients form an absolutely convergent series [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) the moments of $\mu$ form an absolutely convergent series and the weight function $w$ is strictly positive everywhere.[^n5]

:::

::: {#o16 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Szegő's theorem"}

## Szegő's theorem {#Szegő's_theorem}

For any nontrivial probability measure $d\mu$ on $\mathbb{T}$, Verblunsky's form of [Szegő's theorem](wikipedia://en/32905373) states that

$$\prod_{n = 0}^\infty(1-|\alpha_n|^2) = \exp\big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log w(\theta)\,d\theta\big).$$

The left-hand side is independent of $d\mu_s$ but unlike Szegő's original version, where $d\mu = d\mu_{ac}$, Verblunsky's form does allow $d\mu_s \neq 0$.[^n6] Subsequently,

$$\sum_{n = 0}^\infty |\alpha_n|^2 < \infty \;\iff\; \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log w(\theta)\,d\theta > -\infty.$$

One of the consequences is the existence of a mixed spectrum for discretized Schrödinger operators.[^n7]

:::

::: {#o20 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Rakhmanov's theorem"}

## Rakhmanov's theorem {#Rakhmanov's_theorem}

Rakhmanov's theorem states that if the absolutely continuous part $w$ of the measure $\mu$ is positive [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) then the Verblunsky coefficients $\alpha_n$ tend to 0.

:::

::: {#o22 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

The [Rogers–Szegő polynomials](wikipedia://en/32745764) are an example of orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle.

:::
