# Alternation (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Alternation"}

In geometry, an **alternation** or **partial truncation** is an operation on a [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621), [polyhedron](wikipedia://en/23470), [tiling](wikipedia://en/321671), or higher dimensional [polytope](wikipedia://en/23471) that removes alternate vertices.[^o2]

:::

Coxeter labels an *alternation* by a prefixed *h*, standing for *hemi* or *half*. Because alternation reduces all polygon faces to half as many sides, it can only be applied to polytopes with all even-sided faces. An alternated square face becomes a [digon](wikipedia://en/5442728), and being degenerate, is usually reduced to a single edge.

More generally any [vertex-uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-uniform) polyhedron or tiling with a [vertex configuration](wikipedia://en/3603745) consisting of all even-numbered elements can be *alternated*. For example, the alternation of a vertex figure with *2a.2b.2c* is *a.3.b.3.c.3* where the three is the number of elements in this vertex figure. A special case is square faces whose order divides in half into degenerate [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728). So for example, the cube *\4.4.4* is alternated as *\2.3.2.3.2.3* which is reduced to 3.3.3, being the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606), and all the 6 edges of the tetrahedra can also be seen as the degenerate faces of the original cube.

::: {#o4 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Simiation"}

## Snub {#Snub}

A **snub** (in [Coxeter's terminology](wikipedia://en/9428917#Coxeter's_snubs,_regular_and_quasiregular)) can be seen as an **alternation** of a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) regular or truncated [quasiregular](wikipedia://en/8810651) polyhedron. In general a polyhedron can be snubbed if its truncation has only even-sided faces. All [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [rectified](wikipedia://en/2192366) polyhedra can be snubbed, not just from regular polyhedra.

The [snub square antiprism](wikipedia://en/1133918) is an example of a general snub, and can be represented by ss{2,4}, with the [square antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860045), s{2,4}.

:::

## Alternated polytopes {#Alternated_polytopes}

This *alternation* operation applies to higher-dimensional polytopes and honeycombs as well, but in general most of the results of this operation will not be uniform. The voids created by the deleted vertices will not in general create uniform facets, and there are typically not enough degrees of freedom to allow an appropriate rescaling of the new edges. Exceptions do exist, however, such as the derivation of the [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756) from the [truncated 24-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_24-cell).

Examples:

- [Honeycombs](wikipedia://en/6008654)
  1. An alternated [cubic honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3733318) is the [tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb](wikipedia://en/3735260).
  1. An alternated [hexagonal prismatic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_prismatic_honeycomb) is the [gyrated alternated cubic honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyrated_alternated_cubic_honeycomb).
- [4-polytope](wikipedia://en/24979)
  1. An alternated [truncated 24-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_24-cell) is the [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756).
- 4-honeycombs:
  1. An alternated [truncated 24-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31983684) is the [snub 24-cell honeycomb](wikipedia://en/31806890).
- A [hypercube](wikipedia://en/39783) can always be alternated into a uniform [demihypercube](wikipedia://en/6833695).
  1. [Cube](wikipedia://en/6285) → [Tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) (regular)

  1. *Tesseract* ([8-cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8-cell)) → [16-cell](wikipedia://en/716422) (regular)
     - *Image: Schlegel wireframe 8-cell.png.* → *Image: Schlegel wireframe 16-cell.png.*
  1. [Penteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penteract) → [demipenteract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demipenteract) (semiregular)
  1. [Hexeract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexeract) → [demihexeract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demihexeract) (uniform)
  1. ...

## Altered polyhedra {#Altered_polyhedra}

Coxeter also used the operator *a*, which contains both halves, so retains the original symmetry. For even-sided regular polyhedra, a{2p,q} represents a [compound polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_polyhedron) with two opposite copies of h{2p,q}. For odd-sided, greater than 3, regular polyhedra a{p,q}, becomes a [star polyhedron](wikipedia://en/7087334).

[Norman Johnson](wikipedia://en/715527) extended the use of the **altered** operator *a*{p,q}, *b*{p,q} for **blended**, and *c*{p,q} for **converted**, as , , and respectively.
