# Oleg D. Jefimenko

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Oleg D. Jefimenko"}

**Oleg Dmitrovich Jefimenko** (October 14, 1922, [Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv), [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) – May 14, 2009, [Morgantown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgantown,_West_Virginia), [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia), United States) was a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at [West Virginia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_University), best known for his work on the [Jefimenko's equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefimenko's_equations).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Jefimenko received his [bachelor's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor's_degree) at [Lewis and Clark College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_College) in 1952 and his [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) in 1954. He received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon) in 1956. Jefimenko worked for the development of the theory of electromagnetic retardation and relativity. In 1956, he was awarded the [Sigma Xi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Xi) Prize. In 1971 and 1973, he won awards in the [AAPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_of_Physics_Teachers) Apparatus Competition. Jefimenko constructed and operated electrostatic generators run by [atmospheric electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_electricity).

Jefimenko worked on the generalization of [Newton's gravitational theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_theory_of_gravitation) to [time-dependent systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-dependent_density_functional_theory). In his opinion, there is no objective reason for abandoning Newton's [force-field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_%28physics%29) gravitational theory (in favor of a [metric gravitational theory](wikipedia://en/12024)). He was trying to develop and expand Newton's theory, making it compatible with the [principle of causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality_%28physics%29) and making it applicable to time-dependent gravitational interactions.

Jefimenko's expansion, or generalization, is based on the existence of the second gravitational force field, the "[cogravitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogravitational_field), or [Heaviside's field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaviside's_field)".[^o4] This might also be called a [gravimagnetic field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitomagnetism). It represents a physical approach profoundly different from the time-space geometry approach of Einstein's [general theory of relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_theory_of_relativity). [Oliver Heaviside](wikipedia://en/22831) first predicted this field in the article *A Gravitational and Electromagnetic Analogy* (1893).

## Electromagnetic analogy of gravitational and cogravitational fields {#Electromagnetic_analogy_of_gravitational_and_cogravitational_fields}

Jefimenko suggests that electromagnetic equations can be converted to their gravitational-cogravitational equivalent by replacing electromagnetic symbols and constants with their corresponding gravitational-cogravitational symbols and constants,[^n1] given in the table below.

*Corresponding symbols and constants*

::: {#o7 .table cue=table name="Corresponding symbols and constants"}

| Electric | Gravitational |
|---|---|
| $q$ (charge) | $m$ (mass) |
| $\rho$ (volume charge density) | $\rho$ (volume mass density) |
| $\sigma$ (surface charge density) | $\sigma$ (surface mass density) |
| $\lambda$ (line charge density) | $\lambda$ (line mass density) |
| $\varphi$ (scalar potential) | $\varphi$ (scalar potential) |
| $\mathbf{A}$ (vector potential) | $\mathbf{A}$ (vector potential) |
| $\mathbf{J}$ (convection current density) | $\mathbf{J}$ (mass-current density) |
| $I$ (electric current) | $I$ (mass current) |
| $\mathbf{m}$ (magnetic dipole moment) | $\mathbf{d}$ (cogravitational moment) |
| $\mathbf{E}$ (electric field) | $\mathbf{g}$ (gravitational field) |
| $\mathbf{B}$ (magnetic field) | $\mathbf{K}$ (cogravitational field) |
| $\varepsilon_{0}$ (permittivity of space) | $-\tfrac{1}{4\pi G}$ |
| $\mu_{0}$ (permeability of space) | $-\tfrac{4\pi G}{c^2}$ |
| $-\tfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$ or $-\tfrac{\mu_0 c^2}{4\pi}$ | $G$ (gravitational constant) |

:::

## Generalized theory of gravitation {#Generalized_theory_of_gravitation}

Jefimenko posits the following generalized theory of gravitation.[^n2]

$$\begin{aligned} & \mathbf{g} = -G \int \left[\dfrac{[\rho]}{r^3} + \dfrac{1}{r^2c}\left[\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}\right]\right]\mathbf(r)dV' + \dfrac{G}{c^2} \int\dfrac{1}{r}\left[\dfrac{\partial(\rho\mathbf{v})}{\partial t}\right]dV',\\ & \mathbf{K} = -\dfrac{G}{c^2} \int \left[\dfrac{[\rho\mathbf{v}]}{r^3} + \dfrac{1}{r^2c}\dfrac{\partial[\rho\mathbf{v}]}{\partial t}\right] \times \mathbf{r}dV', \end{aligned}$$
