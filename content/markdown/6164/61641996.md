# Maxwell's theorem (geometry)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Maxwell's theorem"}

*Image: Maxwell theorem2.svg.* Line segments with identical markings are parallel. If the sides of the triangle$A'B'C'$ are parallel to the according cevians of triangle $ABC$, which are intersecting in a common point $V'$, then the cevians of triangle $A'B'C'$, which are parallel to the according sides of triangle $ABC$ intersect in a common point $V'$ as well

**Maxwell's theorem** is the following statement about triangles in the plane.

:::

For a given triangle $ABC$ and a point $V$ not on the sides of that triangle construct a second triangle $A'B'C'$, such that the side $A'B'$ is parallel to the line segment $CV$, the side $A'C'$ is parallel to the line segment $BV$ and the side $B'C'$ is parallel to the line segment $AV$. Then the parallel to $AB$ through $C'$, the parallel to $BC$ through $A'$ and the parallel to $AC$ through $B'$ intersect in a common point $V'$.

The theorem is named after the physicist [James Clerk Maxwell](wikipedia://en/28989696) (1831–1879), who proved it in his work on [reciprocal figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_figures), which are of importance in [statics](wikipedia://en/28767).
