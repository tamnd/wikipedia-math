# Gaussian q-distribution

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaussian q-distribution"}

In [mathematical physics](wikipedia://en/173416) and [probability](wikipedia://en/22934) and [statistics](wikipedia://en/26685), the **Gaussian *q*-distribution** is a family of [probability distributions](wikipedia://en/23543) that includes, as [limiting cases](wikipedia://en/40400729), the [uniform distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_distribution_%28continuous%29) and the [normal (Gaussian) distribution](wikipedia://en/21462). It was introduced by Diaz and Teruel. It is a [q-analog](wikipedia://en/1619396) of the Gaussian or [normal distribution](wikipedia://en/21462).

:::

The distribution is symmetric about zero and is bounded, except for the limiting case of the normal distribution. The limiting uniform distribution is on the range -1 to +1.

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

*Image: Gaussianq-density2.jpg.* The Gaussian q-density.

Let *q* be a [real number](wikipedia://en/20646438) in the interval \[0, 1). The [probability density function](wikipedia://en/43487) of the Gaussian *q*-distribution is given by

$$s_q(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < -\nu \\ \frac{1}{c(q)}E_{q^2}^{\frac{-q^2x^2}{[2]_q}} & \text{if } -\nu \leq x \leq \nu \\ 0 & \mbox{if } x >\nu. \end{cases}$$

where

$$\nu = \nu(q) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q}} ,$$

$$c(q)=2(1-q)^{1/2}\sum_{m=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^m q^{m(m+1)}}{(1-q^{2m+1})(1-q^2)_{q^2}^m} .$$

The *q*-analogue \[*t*\]<sub>*q*</sub> of the real number $t$ is given by

$$[t]_q=\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}.$$

The *q*-analogue of the [exponential function](wikipedia://en/9678) is the [q-exponential](wikipedia://en/4120642), *E*${}^{x}_{q}$, which is given by

$$E_q^{x}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j(j-1)/2}\frac{x^{j}}{[j]!}$$

where the *q*-analogue of the [factorial](wikipedia://en/10606) is the [q-factorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-factorial), \[*n*\]<sub>*q*</sub>!, which is in turn given by

$$[n]_q!=[n]_q[n-1]_q\cdots [2]_q \,$$

for an integer *n* > 2 and \[1\]<sub>*q*</sub>! = \[0\]<sub>*q*</sub>! = 1.

*Image: CumulativeGaussianq-distribution2.jpg.* The Cumulative Gaussian q-distribution.

The [cumulative distribution function](wikipedia://en/5793) of the Gaussian *q*-distribution is given by

$$G_q(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < -\nu \\[12pt] \displaystyle \frac{1}{c(q)}\int_{-\nu}^{x} E_{q^2}^{-q^2 t^2/[2]} \, d_qt & \text{if } -\nu \leq x \leq \nu \\[12pt] 1 & \text{if } x>\nu \end{cases}$$

where the [integration](wikipedia://en/15532) symbol denotes the [Jackson integral](wikipedia://en/10335099).

The function *G*<sub>*q*</sub> is given explicitly by

$$G_q(x)= \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } x < -\nu, \\ \displaystyle \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1-q}{c(q)} \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{q^{n(n+1)}(q-1)^n}{(1-q^{2n+1})(1-q^2)_{q^2}^{n}}x^{2n+1} & \text{if } -\nu \leq x \leq \nu \\ 1 & \text{if}\ x > \nu \end{cases}$$

where

$$(a+b)_q^n=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(a+q^ib) .$$

:::

## Moments {#Moments}

The [moments](wikipedia://en/368684) of the Gaussian *q*-distribution are given by

$$\frac{1}{c(q)}\int_{-\nu}^\nu E_{q^2}^{-q^2 x^2/[2]} \, x^{2n} \, d_qx =[2n-1]!! ,$$

$$\frac{1}{c(q)}\int_{-\nu}^\nu E_{q^{2}}^{-q^2 x^2/[2]} \, x^{2n+1} \, d_qx=0 ,$$

where the symbol \[2*n* − 1\]!! is the *q*-analogue of the [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208) given by

$$[2n-1][2n-3]\cdots[1]= [2n-1]!!. \,$$
