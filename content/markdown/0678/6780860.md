# Kepler–Bouwkamp constant

*Image: Bouwkamp1.svg.* A sequence of inscribed circles and [regular polygons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_polygons) with increasing numbers of sides, which converge towards a circle at the [limit](wikipedia://en/285759).

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kepler–Bouwkamp constant"}

In [plane geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_geometry), the **Kepler–Bouwkamp constant** (or **polygon inscribing constant**) is obtained as a [limit](wikipedia://en/285773) of the following [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838). Take a [circle](wikipedia://en/6220) of radius 1. [Inscribe](wikipedia://en/4470203) a [regular triangle](wikipedia://en/173285) in this circle. Inscribe a circle in this triangle. Inscribe a [square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_%28geometry%29) in it. Inscribe a circle, [regular pentagon](wikipedia://en/20741014), circle, [regular hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733) and so forth. The [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815) of the limiting circle is called the Kepler–Bouwkamp constant.[^o2] It is named after [Johannes Kepler](wikipedia://en/15736) and Christoffel Jacob Bouwkamp, and is the inverse of the [polygon circumscribing constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon_circumscribing_constant).

:::

## Numerical value {#Numerical_value}

::: {#o4 .problem cue=unsolved}

Is the Kepler–Bouwkamp constant irrational?

:::

The decimal expansion of the Kepler–Bouwkamp constant is

$$\prod_{k=3}^\infty \cos \frac\pi k = 0.1149420448\dots.$$

The natural logarithm of the Kepler-Bouwkamp constant is given by

$$-2\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{2^{2k}-1}{2k}\zeta(2k)\left(\zeta(2k)-1-\frac{1}{2^{2k}}\right)$$

where $\zeta(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^s}$ is the [Riemann zeta function](wikipedia://en/25809).

If the product is taken over the [odd primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_prime), the constant

$$\prod_{{k\,=\,3,\,5,\,7,\atop 11,\,13,\,17,\,\ldots}} \!\!\!\cos \frac\pi k = 0.312832\ldots$$

is obtained .
