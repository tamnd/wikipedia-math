# Lévy–Prokhorov metric

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Lévy–Prokhorov metric"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Lévy–Prokhorov metric** (sometimes known just as the **Prokhorov metric**) is a [metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_%28mathematics%29) (i.e., a definition of distance) on the collection of [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) on a given [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018). It is named after the French mathematician [Paul Lévy](wikipedia://en/556462) and the Soviet mathematician [Yuri Vasilyevich Prokhorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Vasilyevich_Prokhorov); Prokhorov introduced it in 1956 as a generalization of the earlier [Lévy metric](wikipedia://en/6790044).

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

Let $(M, d)$ be a [metric space](wikipedia://en/20018) with its [Borel sigma algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borel_sigma_algebra) $\mathcal{B} (M)$. Let $\mathcal{P} (M)$ denote the collection of all [probability measures](wikipedia://en/45437) on the [measurable space](wikipedia://en/43333) $(M, \mathcal{B} (M))$.

For a [subset](wikipedia://en/27631) $A \subseteq M$, define the [ε-neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epsilon-neighborhood) of $A$ by

$$A^{\varepsilon} := \{ p \in M ~|~ \exists q \in A, \ d(p, q) < \varepsilon \} = \bigcup_{p \in A} B_{\varepsilon} (p).$$

where $B_{\varepsilon} (p)$ is the [open ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_ball) of radius $\varepsilon$ centered at $p$.

The **Lévy–Prokhorov metric** $\pi : \mathcal{P} (M)^{2} \to [0, + \infty)$ is defined by setting the distance between two probability measures $\mu$ and $\nu$ to be

$$\pi (\mu, \nu) := \inf \left\{ \varepsilon > 0 ~|~ \mu(A) \leq \nu (A^{\varepsilon}) + \varepsilon \ \text{and} \ \nu (A) \leq \mu (A^{\varepsilon}) + \varepsilon \ \text{for all} \ A \in \mathcal{B}(M) \right\}.$$

For probability measures clearly $\pi (\mu, \nu) \le 1$.

Some authors omit one of the two inequalities or choose only [open](wikipedia://en/39358) or [closed](wikipedia://en/47279) $A$; either inequality implies the other, and $(\bar{A})^\varepsilon = A^\varepsilon$, but restricting to open sets may change the metric so defined (if $M$ is not [Polish](wikipedia://en/396622)).

:::

## Properties {#Properties}

- If $(M, d)$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), convergence of measures in the Lévy–Prokhorov metric is equivalent to [weak convergence of measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weak_convergence_of_measures). Thus, $\pi$ is a [metrization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrization_theorem) of the topology of weak convergence on $\mathcal{P} (M)$.
- The metric space $\left( \mathcal{P} (M), \pi \right)$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $(M, d)$ is separable.
- If $\left( \mathcal{P} (M), \pi \right)$ is [complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_space) then $(M, d)$ is complete. If all the measures in $\mathcal{P} (M)$ have separable [support](wikipedia://en/6591796), then the converse implication also holds: if $(M, d)$ is complete then $\left( \mathcal{P} (M), \pi \right)$ is complete. In particular, this is the case if $(M, d)$ is separable.
- If $(M, d)$ is separable and complete, a subset $\mathcal{K} \subseteq \mathcal{P} (M)$ is [relatively compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relatively_compact) if and only if its $\pi$-closure is $\pi$-compact.
- If $(M,d)$ is [separable](wikipedia://en/27855), then $\pi (\mu , \nu ) = \inf \{ \alpha (X,Y) : \text{Law}(X) = \mu , \text{Law}(Y) = \nu \}$, where $\alpha (X,Y) = \inf\{ \varepsilon > 0 : \mathbb{P} ( d( X ,Y ) > \varepsilon ) \leq \varepsilon \}$ is the *[Ky Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky_Fan) metric*.[^o6][^o7]

:::

## Relation to other distances {#Relation_to_other_distances}

Let $(M,d)$ be separable. Then

- $\pi (\mu , \nu ) \leq \delta (\mu , \nu)$, where $\delta (\mu,\nu)$ is the [total variation distance of probability measures](wikipedia://en/683570)[^o9]
- $\pi (\mu , \nu)^2 \leq W_p (\mu, \nu)^p$, where $W_p$ is the [Wasserstein metric](wikipedia://en/6704603) with $p\geq 1$ and $\mu, \nu$ have finite $p$th moment.[^o10]
