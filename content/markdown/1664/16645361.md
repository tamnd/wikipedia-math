# Concorde TSP Solver

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Concorde TSP Solver"}

The **Concorde TSP Solver** is a program for solving the [travelling salesman problem](wikipedia://en/31248). It was written by [David Applegate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Applegate), [Robert E. Bixby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Bixby), [Vašek Chvátal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vašek_Chvátal), and [William J. Cook](wikipedia://en/38308915), in [ANSI C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANSI_C), and is freely available for academic use.

:::

Concorde has been applied to problems of [gene mapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_mapping),[^o3] [protein function prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_function_prediction),[^o4] [vehicle routing](wikipedia://en/7799668),[^o5] conversion of bitmap images to continuous line drawings,[^o6] scheduling ship movements for seismic surveys,[^o7] and in studying the scaling properties of combinatorial optimization problems.[^o8]

According to (Mulder Wunsch 2003), Concorde “is widely regarded as the fastest TSP solver, for large instances, currently in existence.” In 2001, Concorde won a 5000 [guilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_guilder) prize from [CMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMG_%28company%29) for solving a vehicle routing problem the company had posed in 1996.[^o10]

Concorde requires a [linear programming](wikipedia://en/43730) solver and only supports QSopt[^o11] and [CPLEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPLEX) 8.0.
