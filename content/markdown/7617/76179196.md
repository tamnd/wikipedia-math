# Neil Hindman

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Neil Hindman"}

**Neil Hindman** (born April 14, 1943) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and professor emeritus[^o3] at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University). His research focuses on various areas within [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), including [topology](wikipedia://en/29954), [Stone-Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897), [discrete systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_system), and [Ramsey theory](wikipedia://en/157175).[^o4]

:::

## Life and education {#Life_and_education}

Neil Hindman actively participated in [civil rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_rights_movement) work during his college years. In the summer of 1964, he served as a [freedom school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Schools) coordinator in [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi).[^o6]

Hindman completed his Bachelor of Arts degree in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [physics](wikipedia://en/22939) in 1965 at [Westmar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmar_University).[^o7][^o8] He then pursued a graduate degree, earning a Master of Arts in mathematics from the [University of Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts) in 1967.[^o8] Subsequently, Hindman continued his academic journey at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University), where he received his Ph.D. in 1969. Under the supervision of W. W. Comfort, Hindman wrote his doctoral thesis on "P-like spaces and their product with P-spaces.[^o8]"

## Academic career {#Academic_career}

Neil Hindman began his academic career as a visiting assistant professor at [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University), serving from September 1969 to June 1970. Following this, he joined [California State University, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Los_Angeles),[^o10] as an assistant professor in September 1970. From September 1975 to August 1976, Hindman held a visiting associate professorship at [SUNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York) (The State University of New York) at [Binghamton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton,_New_York). By December 1979, he had risen to the rank of Professor at [California State University, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Los_Angeles).

In January 1980, Hindman transitioned to [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University), where he assumed the role of associate professor, continuing to impart knowledge in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831).[^o3] He dedicated several decades to teaching and research at [Howard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_University), ultimately retiring as a professor of Mathematics[^o3] in June 2017.[^o8]

## Mathematical work {#Mathematical_work}

One of Hindman's early contributions was his dissertation for his Ph.D. thesis,[^o12] conducted in collaboration with W. W. Comfort and S. Negrepontis. Their research explored conditions for defining F'-spaces and investigated concepts such as weakly Lindelöf spaces and [P-spaces](wikipedia://en/41775284), shedding light on the structure of [F-spaces](wikipedia://en/200440) in [topology](wikipedia://en/29954). This pioneering work significantly advanced theoretical models and analytical techniques within the field.

[Hindman's Theorem](wikipedia://en/3999498), formulated and proven by Neil Hindman,[^o13] addresses a [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) originally proposed by [Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) and [Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee_Rothschild). The theorem asserts that any partition of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) $\mathbb{N}$ into a finite number of classes contains at least one class with a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) such that all [finite sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_sum) of distinct elements from this [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) also belong to the same class. [Hindman's Theorem](wikipedia://en/3999498) confirms the [conjecture](wikipedia://en/6138) by [Graham](wikipedia://en/240694) and [Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee_Rothschild) and establishes its equivalence with the existence of an [ultrafilter](wikipedia://en/31911) on $\mathbb{N}$. This theorem highlights the relationship between the [partition regularity](wikipedia://en/3999801) of the [natural numbers](wikipedia://en/21474) and [ultrafilters](wikipedia://en/31911), offering a fundamental result with broad implications across various mathematical domains.

Hindman remains active in the fields of [Ramsey Theory](wikipedia://en/157175) and [Topology](wikipedia://en/29954), with a particular focus on the [Stone–Čech compactification](wikipedia://en/52897).
