# Quasitransitive relation

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Quasitransitive relation"}

*Image: Quasitransitive 25 percent margin.gif.* The quasitransitive relation *x*≤$\tfrac{5}{4}$*y*. Its symmetric and transitive part is shown in blue and green, respectively.

The mathematical notion of **quasitransitivity** is a weakened version of [transitivity](wikipedia://en/200463) that is used in [social choice theory](wikipedia://en/1745670) and [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics). Informally, a relation is quasitransitive if it is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/200459) for some values and transitive elsewhere. The concept was introduced by (Sen 1969) to study the consequences of [Arrow's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow's_theorem).

:::

::: {#o4 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

A [binary relation](wikipedia://en/3931) T over a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) *X* is **quasitransitive** if for all *a*, *b*, and *c* in *X* the following holds:

$$(a\operatorname{T}b) \wedge \neg(b\operatorname{T}a) \wedge (b\operatorname{T}c) \wedge \neg(c\operatorname{T}b) \Rightarrow (a\operatorname{T}c) \wedge \neg(c\operatorname{T}a).$$

If the relation is also [antisymmetric](wikipedia://en/1176), T is transitive.

Alternately, for a relation T, define the [asymmetric](wikipedia://en/1229368) or "strict" part P:

$$(a\operatorname{P}b) \Leftrightarrow (a\operatorname{T}b) \wedge \neg(b\operatorname{T}a).$$

Then T is quasitransitive if and only if P is transitive.

:::

::: {#o6 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

[Preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preference) are assumed to be quasitransitive (rather than transitive) in some economic contexts. The classic example is a person indifferent between 7 and 8 grams of sugar and indifferent between 8 and 9 grams of sugar, but who prefers 9 grams of sugar to 7.[^o7] Similarly, the [Sorites paradox](wikipedia://en/231687#Resolutions_in_utility_theory) can be resolved by weakening assumed transitivity of certain relations to quasitransitivity.

:::

::: {#o9 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

- A relation *R* is quasitransitive if, and only if, it is the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of a symmetric relation *J* and a transitive relation *P*.[^o10] *J* and *P* are not uniquely determined by a given *R*;[^o11] however, the *P* from the *only-if* part is minimal.[^o12]
- As a consequence, each symmetric relation is quasitransitive, and so is each transitive relation.[^o13] Moreover, an antisymmetric and quasitransitive relation is always transitive.[^o14]
- The relation from the above sugar example, {(7,7), (7,8), (7,9), (8,7), (8,8), (8,9), (9,8), (9,9)}, is quasitransitive, but not transitive.
- A quasitransitive relation needn't be [acyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acyclic_relation): for every non-empty set *A*, the [universal relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_relation) *A*[×](wikipedia://en/24104095)*A* is both cyclic and quasitransitive.
- A relation is quasitransitive if, and only if, its [complement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementary_relation) is.
- Similarly, a relation is quasitransitive if, and only if, its [converse](wikipedia://en/3941020) is.

:::
