# Gabriel Judah Lichtenfeld

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gabriel Judah Lichtenfeld"}

**Gabriel Judah Lichtenfeld** (גַּבְרִיאֵל יְהוּדָה ליכטענפעלד; 1811, [Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin) — 22 March 1887, [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw)) was a [Jewish-Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish-Polish) [maskilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maskilic) mathematician, poet, and author. He wrote for *[Ha-Shachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha-Shaḥar)*, *[Ha-Tzefirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha-Tsfira)*, *Izraelita*, and Polish newspapers, mostly on mathematical topics.[^o2]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

A descendant of [Moses Isserles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Isserles), Lichtenfeld showed early ability as a [Talmudic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud) scholar. He later became familiar with [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), German, French, and [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), and made a special study of philosophy and mathematics.

In the Hebrew periodical *[Ha-Shachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ha-Shaḥar)*, there appeared a series of Hebrew articles by Lichtenfeld which attracted attention. His reputation was enhanced by his series of articles, in the Polish periodical *Izraelita*, on Jewish mathematicians. Lichtenfeld is known also by his polemics with [Hayyim Selig Slonimski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayyim_Selig_Slonimski) on mathematical subjects. Among other works, Lichtenfeld was the author of *Yedi'ot ha-Shi'urim* (1865, "Science of Measurement"), *Tzofnat Pa'neach* (1874), a critical review of Slonimski's *Yesode Ḥokmat ha-Shi'ur*, *Tosefot* (1875), a polemic against Slonimski, *Kohen Lelo Elohim* (1876), a book of mathematical criticisms, and *Sippurim be-Shir ve'Shirim Shonim* (1877, "Stories in Verse and Selected Poems"), a collection of poems and rimed prose by himself and by his son-in-law [I. L. Peretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._L._Peretz). Lichtenfeld's main book on mathematics, *Bo'u Ḥeshbon*, was published posthumously in 1895.
