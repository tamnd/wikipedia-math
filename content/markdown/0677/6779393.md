# Riesz potential

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Riesz potential"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Riesz potential** is a [potential](wikipedia://en/1431342) named after its discoverer, the Hungarian mathematician [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz). In a sense, the Riesz potential defines a fractional inverse power $(-\Delta)^{-\alpha/2}$ of the [Laplace operator](wikipedia://en/174706) on Euclidean space. It generalizes to several variables the [Riemann–Liouville integrals](wikipedia://en/567743) of one variable.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

If 0 \< *α* \< *n*, then the Riesz potential *I*<sub>α</sub>*f* of a [locally integrable function](wikipedia://en/4258398) *f* on **R**<sup>*n*</sup> is the function defined by

::: {#o4 .equation cue=numblk name="1"}

$$(I_{\alpha}f) (x)= \frac{1}{c_\alpha} \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} \frac{f(y)}{| x - y |^{n-\alpha}} \, \mathrm{d}y \tag{1}$$

:::

where the constant is given by

$$c_\alpha = \pi^{n/2}2^\alpha\frac{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}{\Gamma((n-\alpha)/2)}.$$

This [singular integral](wikipedia://en/12791220) is well-defined provided *f* decays sufficiently rapidly at infinity, specifically if *f* ∈ [L<sup>*p*</sup>(**R**<sup>*n*</sup>)](wikipedia://en/45194) with 1 ≤ *p* \< *n*/*α*. The classical result due to Sobolev states that the rate of decay of *f* and that of *I*<sub>*α*</sub>*f* are related in the form of an inequality (the [Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev_inequality))

$$\|I_\alpha f\|_{p^*} \le C_p \|f\|_p, \quad p^*=\frac{np}{n-\alpha p}, \quad \forall 1 < p < \frac{n}{\alpha}$$

For *p*=1 the result was extended by (Schikorra Spector Van Schaftingen 2014),

$$\|I_\alpha f\|_{1^*} \le C_p \|Rf\|_1.$$

where $Rf=DI_1f$ is the vector-valued [Riesz transform](wikipedia://en/18673900). More generally, the operators *I*<sub>*α*</sub> are well-defined for [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) α such that 0 \< Re *α* \< *n*.

The Riesz potential can be defined more generally in a [weak sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_%28mathematics%29) as the [convolution](wikipedia://en/7519)

$$I_\alpha f = f*K_\alpha$$

where *K*<sub>α</sub> is the locally integrable function:

$$K_\alpha(x) = \frac{1}{c_\alpha}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-\alpha}}.$$

The Riesz potential can therefore be defined whenever *f* is a compactly supported distribution. In this connection, the Riesz potential of a positive [Borel measure](wikipedia://en/4322) μ with [compact support](wikipedia://en/6591796) is chiefly of interest in [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) because *I*<sub>*α*</sub>μ is then a (continuous) [subharmonic function](wikipedia://en/3237359) off the support of μ, and is [lower semicontinuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semicontinuous) on all of **R**<sup>*n*</sup>.

Consideration of the [Fourier transform](wikipedia://en/52247) reveals that the Riesz potential is a [Fourier multiplier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_multiplier).[^o6] In fact, one has

$$\widehat{K_\alpha}(\xi) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^n} K_{\alpha}(x) e^{-2\pi i x \xi }\, \mathrm{d}x = |2\pi\xi|^{-\alpha}$$

and so, by the [convolution theorem](wikipedia://en/53268),

$$\widehat{I_\alpha f}(\xi) = |2\pi\xi|^{-\alpha} \hat{f}(\xi).$$

The Riesz potentials satisfy the following [semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) property on, for instance, [rapidly decreasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapidly_decreasing) [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122)

$$I_\alpha I_\beta = I_{\alpha+\beta}$$

provided

$$0 < \operatorname{Re} \alpha, \operatorname{Re} \beta < n,\quad 0 < \operatorname{Re} (\alpha+\beta) < n.$$

Furthermore, if 0 \< Re *α* \< *n*–2, then

$$\Delta I_{\alpha+2} = I_{\alpha+2} \Delta=-I_\alpha.$$

One also has, for this class of functions,

$$\lim_{\alpha\to 0^+} (I_\alpha f)(x) = f(x).$$
