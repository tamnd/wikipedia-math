# Veblen's theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Veblen's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Veblen's theorem**, introduced by (txt), states that the set of edges of a finite [graph](wikipedia://en/325806) can be written as a union of disjoint [simple cycles](wikipedia://en/168609) if and only if every vertex has even [degree](wikipedia://en/1374448). Thus, it is closely related to the theorem of (Euler 1736) that a finite graph has an [Euler tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler_tour) (a single non-simple cycle that covers the edges of the graph) if and only if it is [connected](wikipedia://en/1614492) and every vertex has even degree. Indeed, a representation of a graph as a union of simple cycles may be obtained from an Euler tour by repeatedly splitting the tour into smaller cycles whenever there is a repeated vertex. However, Veblen's theorem applies also to disconnected graphs, and can be generalized to [infinite graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_graph) in which every vertex has finite degree.[^n1]

:::

If a [countably infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countably_infinite) graph *G* has no odd-degree vertices, then it may be written as a union of disjoint (finite) simple cycles if and only if every finite subgraph of *G* can be extended (by including more edges and vertices from *G*) to a finite Eulerian graph. In particular, every countably infinite graph with only one [end](wikipedia://en/33989041) and with no odd vertices can be written as a union of disjoint cycles.[^n1]
