# Hadamard gap theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadamard gap theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Hadamard gap theorem** is a result about the [analytic continuation](wikipedia://en/80842) of [lacunary](wikipedia://en/8969021) [power series](wikipedia://en/59958). Such a power series is "badly behaved" in the sense that it cannot be extended to be an [analytic function](wikipedia://en/61478) anywhere on the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of its [disk of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476). The result was proved by [Jacques Hadamard](wikipedia://en/312675) in his influencial 1892 [thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis).[^o2]

:::

::: {#o4 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $0<n_0<n_1<\dots$ be a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) such that the $n_{k+1}/n_k \ge \lambda > 1$ for $k \ge 0$, or equivalently

$$\liminf_{k\to\infty} \frac{n_{k+1}}{n_k} > 1.$$

Let $(a_k)_{k\ge0}$ be a sequence of complex numbers such that the power series

$$f(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k z^{n_k}$$

has [radius of convergence](wikipedia://en/61476) $1$. Then the unit circle is a [natural boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_boundary) for the series *$f$*.[^o5][^o6]

This theorem is a special case of both the [Fabry gap theorem](wikipedia://en/39697327) and [Ostrowski's overconvergence theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrowski's_overconvergence_theorem).
