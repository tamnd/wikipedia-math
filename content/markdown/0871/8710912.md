# W. Allen Wallis

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="W. Allen Wallis"}

**Wilson Allen Wallis** (November 5, 1912 – October 12, 1998) was an American [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661) who served as president of the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester).[^o5] He is best known for the [Kruskal–Wallis one-way analysis of variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruskal–Wallis_one-way_analysis_of_variance), which is named after him and [William Kruskal](wikipedia://en/3613880).

:::

## Early years {#Early_years}

Born in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia), he attended the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), Class of 1932, where he was a member of [Chi Phi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_Phi) fraternity. After receiving his degree in psychology and a year of graduate work at the University of Minnesota, he began graduate studies in economics at [The University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Chicago) in 1933, where he began what would prove to be lifelong friendships with [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman), [Aaron Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Director) and [George Stigler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stigler).

In 1936–37, he served as an economist and statistician for the National Resources Committee. During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II), Wallis was the director of research of the U.S. [Office of Scientific Research and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Scientific_Research_and_Development)'s [Statistical Research Group](wikipedia://en/67262774) (1942–46) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University); he recruited a team of bright young economists, including Friedman and Stigler, to the Statistical Research Group.

From 1948 to 1954, Wallis served as the treasurer of the [Mont Pèlerin Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mont_Pèlerin_Society).[^o7]

## University administration {#University_administration}

*Image: WallisHall.jpg.* Wallis Hall on the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) campus

Wallis served as [dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_%28education%29) of [The University of Chicago Graduate School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Chicago_Booth_School_of_Business) from 1956 to 1962. During his time as dean he established the "Chicago Approach to Business Education," which involved the application of statistical methodology to business.[^o9]

He became [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_president) of the [University of Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rochester) in 1962, a position he held until 1970, when he became the University of Rochester's [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_%28education%29) and chief executive. In 1975, he relinquished the job of chief executive, but remained chancellor of the university until his retirement in 1982.[^o10]

In December 1992, the University of Rochester named a joint program of its Departments of Economics and Political Science in honor of Wallis: the W. Allen Wallis Institute of Political Economy at the University of Rochester.[^o11] He died in 1998 in [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_%28state%29).

*Image: Preparations for the 1988 Economic Summit - NARA - 198389.jpg.* January 1988 memo identifying Wallis as President Reagan's "personal representative" for policy matters during the administration's preparations for attending the [14th G7 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_G7_summit) in June.

## Presidential advisor {#Presidential_advisor}

In addition to his role as an academic and academic administrator, Wallis served as an advisor to [U.S. presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_United_States) [Dwight Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Eisenhower), [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon), [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford), and [Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan). Under Eisenhower, he collaborated with Vice President Nixon on the report of the Cabinet Committee on Price Stability for Economic Growth (1959–61). Under Nixon and Ford, he served on the President's Commission on Federal Statistics and on the Advisory Council on [Social Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_%28United_States%29). Nixon also appointed Wallis as chairman of the [Corporation for Public Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation_for_Public_Broadcasting), a post he held 1975–78. Under Reagan, he served as Under Secretary of State for Economic Affairs (1982–85), and then, after Congress changed the job description and title, as [Under Secretary of State for Economic, Business, and Agricultural Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_State_for_Economic,_Business,_and_Agricultural_Affairs) (1985–89).
