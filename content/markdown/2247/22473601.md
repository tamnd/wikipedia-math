# Wilfried Schmid

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Wilfried Schmid"}

**Wilfried Schmid** (born May 28, 1943) is a German-born American mathematician who works in [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), [representation theory](wikipedia://en/19378200), and [automorphic forms](wikipedia://en/782146).

:::

Schmid was raised in [Bonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonn) where his father Wolfgang Schmid (classicist) (1913–1980) served as a professor of [classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classics). In 1960, Schmid followed his father to [Princeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey) when the latter was a fellow at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179). After graduating as valedictorian[^o3] of [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University)'s class of 1964, Schmid earned his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) at [University of California, Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley) in 1967 under the direction of [Phillip Griffiths](wikipedia://en/3660414), and then taught at Berkeley and [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University), becoming a full professor at Columbia at age 27. In 1978, he moved to [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he served as the Dwight Parker Robinson Professor of Mathematics until his retirement in 2019.[^o4]

Schmid's early work concerns the construction of [discrete series representations](wikipedia://en/7205312) of semi-simple [Lie groups](wikipedia://en/17945). Notable accomplishments here include a proof of the [Langlands conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_conjecture) on the [discrete series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrete_series), along with a later proof (joint with [Michael Atiyah](wikipedia://en/20698)) constructing all such discrete series representations on spaces of harmonic spinors. Schmid, along with his student Henryk Hecht, proved [Blattner's conjecture](wikipedia://en/2964127) in 1975. In the 1970s, he described the singularities of the Griffith's period map by applying [Lie-theoretic methods](wikipedia://en/1595681) to problems in [algebraic geometry](wikipedia://en/1997).[^o5]

Schmid has been very involved in K–12 mathematics education in his home state, and both nationally and internationally. His interest arose in 1999 after being disturbed by the experiences of his 2nd-grade daughter, Sabina, in her mathematics class.[^o6] He was heavily involved in the drafting of the Massachusetts Mathematics Curriculum Framework in 2000. Later, he served on the National Mathematics Advisory Panel of the [U.S. Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Education).[^o4] He has opposed new ways of teaching children that would neglect basic math skills.[^o7]

In 2012, he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822)[^o8] and in 2020 he was elected as a member of the U.S. [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o5]
