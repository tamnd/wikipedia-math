::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="D5 polytope"}

In 5-dimensional [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), there are 23 [uniform polytopes](wikipedia://en/14415338) with D<sub>5</sub> symmetry, 8 are unique, and 15 are shared with the B<sub>5</sub> symmetry. There are two special forms, the [5-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/7594615), and [5-demicube](wikipedia://en/7186232) with 10 and 16 vertices respectively.

:::

They can be visualized as symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) in [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane) of the D<sub>5</sub> Coxeter group, and other subgroups.

## Graphs {#Graphs}

Symmetric [orthographic projections](wikipedia://en/203052) of these 8 polytopes can be made in the D<sub>5</sub>, D<sub>4</sub>, D<sub>3</sub>, A<sub>3</sub>, [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). A<sub>k</sub> has *\[k+1\]* symmetry, D<sub>k</sub> has *\[2(k-1)\]* symmetry. The B<sub>5</sub> plane is included, with only half the \[10\] symmetry displayed.

These 8 polytopes are each shown in these 5 symmetry planes, with vertices and edges drawn, and vertices colored by the number of overlapping vertices in each projective position.
