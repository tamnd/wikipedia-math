# Gaetano Fichera

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Gaetano Fichera"}

**Gaetano Fichera** (8 February 1922 – 1 June 1996) was an Italian [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), working in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429), [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). He was born in [Acireale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acireale), and died in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born in [Acireale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acireale), a town near [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania) in Sicily, the elder of the four sons of Giuseppe Fichera and Marianna Abate.[^o4] His father Giuseppe was a professor of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and influenced the young Gaetano starting his lifelong passion. In his young years he was a talented [football player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football). On 1 February 1943 he was in the [Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Army) and during the [events of September 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_between_Italy_and_Allied_armed_forces) he was taken prisoner by the [Nazist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazist) troops, kept imprisoned in [Teramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teramo) and then sent to [Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verona): he succeeded in escaping from there and reached the Italian region of [Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilia-Romagna), spending with partisans the last year of war. After the war he was first in Rome and then in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), where he met Matelda Colautti, who became his wife in 1952.

### Education and academic career {#Education_and_academic_career}

After graduating from the [liceo classico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liceo_classico) in only two years, he entered the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania) at the age of 16, being there from 1937 to 1939 and studying under [Pia Nalli](wikipedia://en/48115731). Then he went to the [university of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rome_La_Sapienza), where in 1941 he earned his [laurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurea) with [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_honors#Types) under the direction of [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), when he was only 19. He was immediately appointed by Picone as an assistant professor to his chair and as a researcher at the [Istituto Nazionale per le Applicazioni del Calcolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_per_le_Applicazioni_del_Calcolo), becoming his pupil. After the war he went back to Rome working with [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009): in 1948 he became "Libero Docente" (free professor) of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and in 1949 he was appointed as full professor at the [University of Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Trieste). As he remembers in (Fichera 1991b, 14), in both cases one of the members of the judging commission was [Renato Caccioppoli](wikipedia://en/4262313), which become a close friend of him. From 1956 onward he was full professor at the [Sapienza University of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapienza_University_of_Rome) in the chair of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and then at the [Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Alta_Matematica) in the chair of higher analysis, succeeding to [Luigi Fantappiè](wikipedia://en/7255073). He retired from university teaching in 1992,[^o7] but was professionally active until his death in 1996: particularly, as a member of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei) and first director of the journal *Rendiconti Lincei – Matematica e Applicazioni*,[^o8] he succeeded in reviving its reputation.[^o9]

### Honours {#Honours}

He was member of several [academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy), notably of the [Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_dei_Lincei), the [Accademia Nazionale delle Scienze detta dei XL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_delle_Scienze_detta_dei_XL) and of the [Russian Academy of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Science).

### Teachers {#Teachers}

His lifelong friendship with his teacher [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) is remembered by him in several occasions. As recalled by (Colautti Fichera 2006, 13–14), his father Giuseppe was an assistant professor to the chair of Picone while he was teaching at the [University of Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Catania): they become friends and their friendship lasted even when Giuseppe was forced to leave the academic career for economic reasons, being already the father of two sons, until Giuseppe's death. The young, in effect child, Gaetano, was kept by Picone in his arms. From 1939 to 1941 the young Fichera developed his research directly under the supervision of Picone: as he remembers, it was a time of intense work. But also, when he was back from the front in April 1945[^o13] he met Picone while he was in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome) in his way back to [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicily), and his advisor was so happy to see him as a father can be seeing its living child. Another [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) Fichera was influenced by and acknowledged as one of his teachers and inspirators was [Pia Nalli](wikipedia://en/48115731): she was an outstanding [analyst](wikipedia://en/48396), teaching for several years at the University of [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), being his teacher of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) from 1937 to 1939. [Antonio Signorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Signorini_%28physicist%29) and [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) were two of Fichera's teachers of the Roman period: the first one introduced him and inspired his research in the field of [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429) while the second inspired his research in the field he taught him i.e. the [theory of analytic functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). Signorini had a strong long-time friendship with Picone: on a wall of the [apartment building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartment_building) where they lived, in Via delle Tre Madonne, 18 in Rome, a memorial tablet which commemorates the two friends is placed, as (Fichera 1995b, 47) recalls. The two great mathematicians extended their friendship to the young Fichera, and as a consequence this led to the solution of the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510) and the foundation of the theory of [variational inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_inequalities). Fichera's relations with Severi were not as friendly as with Signorini and Picone: nevertheless, Severi, which was one of the most influential Italian mathematicians of the first half of the 20th century, esteemed the young mathematician. During a course on the [theory of analytic functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) taught at the [Istituto Nazionale di Alta Matematica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_di_Alta_Matematica) from the fall of 1956 and the beginning of the 1957, whose lectures were collected in the book (Severi 1958), Severi posed the problem of generalizing his theorem on the [Dirichlet problem](wikipedia://en/571109) for [holomorphic function of several variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), as (Fichera 1957, 707) recalls: the result was the paper (Fichera 1957), which is a masterpiece, although not generally acknowledged for various reasons described by (Range 2002, 6–11). Other scientists he had as teachers during the period 1939–1941 were [Enrico Bompiani](wikipedia://en/48385220), [Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991) and [Giuseppe Armellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Armellini): he remembered them with great respect and admiration, even if he did not share all their opinions and ideas, as (Colautti Fichera 2006, 16) recalls.

### Friends {#Friends}

A complete list of Fichera's friends includes some of the best scientists and [mathematicians](wikipedia://en/18902) of the 20th century: [Olga Oleinik](wikipedia://en/3033629), [Olga Ladyzhenskaya](wikipedia://en/1914268), [Israel Gel'fand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Gel'fand), [Ivan Petrovsky](wikipedia://en/4598657), [Vladimir Maz'ya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Gilelevich_Maz'ya), [Nikoloz Muskhelishvili](wikipedia://en/977804), [Ilia Vekua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilia_Vekua), [Richard Courant](wikipedia://en/715238), [Fritz John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_John), [Kurt Friedrichs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Friedrichs), [Peter Lax](wikipedia://en/486365), [Louis Nirenberg](wikipedia://en/1814725), [Ronald Rivlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Rivlin), [Hans Lewy](wikipedia://en/1752415), [Clifford Truesdell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Truesdell), [Edmund Hlawka](wikipedia://en/5442007), [Ian Sneddon](wikipedia://en/34942133), [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430), [Alexander Weinstein](wikipedia://en/33986254), [Alexander Ostrowski](wikipedia://en/5827091), [Renato Caccioppoli](wikipedia://en/4262313), [Solomon Mikhlin](wikipedia://en/22994055), [Paul Naghdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_M._Naghdi), [Marston Morse](wikipedia://en/856523) were among his friends, scientific collaborators and correspondents, just to name a few. He built up such a network of contacts being invited several times to lecture on his research by various universities and research institutions, and also participating to several [academic conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_conference), always upon invitation. This long series of scientific journeys started in 1951, when he went to the USA together with his master and friend [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) and [Bruno de Finetti](wikipedia://en/180825) in order to examine the capabilities and characteristics of the first [electronic computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_computer) and purchase one for the [Istituto Nazionale per le Applicazioni del Calcolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Nazionale_per_le_Applicazioni_del_Calcolo): the machine they advised to purchase was the first computer ever working in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy). The most complete source about his friends and collaborators is the book (Colautti Fichera 2006) by his wife Matelda: in those reference it is also possible to find a fairly complete description of Gaetano Fichera's scientific journeys.

The close friendship between [Angelo Pescarini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Pescarini) and Fichera has not his roots in their scientific interests: it is another war story. As (Oleinik 1997, 12) recalls, Gaetano, being escaped from [Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verona) and hidden in a [convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent) in [Alfonsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonsine), tried to get in touch with the local group of partisans in order to help the people of that town who had been so helpful with him: they were informed about an assistant professor to the chair of higher analysis in Rome who was trying to reach them. Angelo, which was a student of mathematics at the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) under [Gianfranco Cimmino](wikipedia://en/50215912), a former pupil of [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), was charged of the task of testing the truth of Gaetano's assertions, examining him in mathematics: his question was:– "Mi sai dire una condizione sufficiente per scambiare un limite con un integrale (Can you give me a sufficient condition for interchanging limit and integration)?"–. Gaetano quickly answered:– "Non solo ti darò la condizione sufficiente, ma ti darò anche la condizione necessaria e pure per insiemi non-limitati (I can give you not only a sufficient condition, but also a necessary condition, and not only for bounded domains, but also for unbounded domains)"–. In effect, Fichera proved such a theorem in the paper (Fichera 1943), his latest paper written in while he was in Rome before joining the army: from that moment on he often used to joke saying that good mathematicians can always have a good application, even for saving one's life.

One of his best friends and appreciated scientific collaborator was [Olga Arsenievna Oleinik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Arsenievna_Oleinik): she cured the redaction of his last posthumous paper (Fichera 1997), as (Colautti Fichera 2006, 202–204) recalls. Also, she used to discuss his work with Gaetano, as he did with her: sometimes their discussion become lively, but nothing more, since they were extremely good friends and estimators of each one's work.

## Work {#Work}

### Research activity {#Research_activity}

He is the author of more than 250 papers and 18 books (monographs and course notes): his work concerns mainly the fields of [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969) listed below. A common characteristic to all of his research is the use of the methods of [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) to prove [existence](wikipedia://en/251177), [uniqueness](wikipedia://en/276894) and [approximation theorems](wikipedia://en/1092713) for the various problems he studied, and also a high consideration of the [analytic problems](wikipedia://en/48396) related to problems in [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969).

#### Mathematical theory of elasticity {#Mathematical_theory_of_elasticity}

His work in [elasticity theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_theory) includes the paper (Fichera 1961c), where Fichera proves the "[Fichera's maximum principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fichera's_maximum_principle)", his work on [variational inequalities](wikipedia://en/1468817). The work on this last topic started with the paper (Fichera 1963), where he announced the existence and [uniqueness theorem](wikipedia://en/276894) for the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510), and ended with the following one (Fichera 1964a),[^o32] where the full proof was published: those papers are the founding works of the field of variational inequalities, as remarked by [Stuart Antman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Antman) in (Antman 1983, 282–284).[^o34] Concerning the [Saint-Venant's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Venant's_principle), he was able to prove it using a [variational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variation) approach and a slight variation of a technique employed by [Richard Toupin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Toupin) to study the same problem: in the paper (Fichera 1979a)[^o36] there is a complete proof of the principle under the [hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothesis) that the base of the [cylinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29) is a set with [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise) [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824). Also he is known for his researches in the theory of [hereditary elasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_elasticity): the paper (Fichera 1979b) emphasizes the necessity of analyzing the [constitutive equations](wikipedia://en/1034699) of materials with memory in order to introduce [models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_modelling) where an existence **and** [uniqueness theorems](wikipedia://en/276894) can be proved in such a way that the proof does not rely on an implicit choice of the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of the [function space](wikipedia://en/340630) where the problem is studied. At last, it is worth to mention that Clifford Truesdell invited him to write the contributions (Fichera 1972a) and (Fichera 1972b) for [Siegfried Flügge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegfried_Flügge)'s *Handbuch der Physik*.

#### Partial differential equations {#Partial_differential_equations}

He was one of the pioneers in the development of the abstract approach through [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) in order to study general [boundary value problems](wikipedia://en/436779) for [linear partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_partial_differential_equation) proving in the paper (Fichera 1955a) a theorem similar in spirit to the [Lax–Milgram theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lax–Milgram_theorem). He studied deeply the [mixed boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/2784886) i.e. a [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) where the boundary has to satisfy a [mixed boundary condition](wikipedia://en/2784886): in his first paper on the topic, (Fichera 1949), he proves the first existence theorem for the mixed boundary problem for [self-adjoint operators](wikipedia://en/187408) of $n > 2$ [variables](wikipedia://en/3728109), while in the paper (Fichera 1955a, 22–29) he proves the same theorem dropping the hypothesis of [self-adjointness](wikipedia://en/187408). He is, according to (Oleinik 1997), the founder of the theory of [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) of [non-positive characteristics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristics_of_PDE): in the paper (Fichera 1956) he introduced the now called [Fichera's function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fichera's_function), in order to identify [subsets](wikipedia://en/27631) of the boundary of the [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) where the [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) for such kind of equations is posed, where it is necessary or not to specify the [boundary condition](wikipedia://en/436779): another account of the theory can be found in the paper (Fichera 1960), which is written in English and was later translated in Russian and [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language).[^o47]

#### Calculus of variation {#Calculus_of_variation}

His contributions to the [calculus of variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculus_of_variation) are mainly devoted to the proof of existence and [uniqueness theorems](wikipedia://en/276894) for [maxima and minima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxima_and_minima) of [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948) of particular form, in conjunction with his studies on [variational inequalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variational_inequalities) and [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429) in theoretical and applied problems: in the paper (Fichera 1964a) a [semicontinuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semicontinuity) [theorem](wikipedia://en/30977) for a functional introduced in the same paper is proved in order to solve the [Signorini problem](wikipedia://en/22194510), and this theorem was extended in (Fichera 1964c) to the case where the given [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948) has general [linear operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_operator) as [arguments](wikipedia://en/13018310), not necessarily [partial differential operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_operator).

#### Functional analysis and eigenvalue theory {#Functional_analysis_and_eigenvalue_theory}

It is difficult to single out his contributions to functional analysis since, as stated at the beginning of this section, the methods of functional analysis are ubiquitous in his research: however, it is worth to remember paper (Fichera 1955a), where an important existence theorem is proved.[^o53]

His contributions in the field of eigenvalue theory began with the paper (Fichera 1955b), where he formalizes a method developed by [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) for the approximation of eigenvalues of [operators](wikipedia://en/22213) subject only to the condition that their [inverse](wikipedia://en/14907) is [compact](wikipedia://en/577441): however, as he admits in (Fichera 1974a, 13–14), this method does not give any estimate on the approximation error on the value of the calculated (approximated) eigenvalues.

He contributed also to the classical [eigenvalue problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalue) for [symmetric operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_operator), introducing the [method of orthogonal invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_orthogonal_invariants).[^o56]

#### Approximation theory {#Approximation_theory}

His work in this field is mainly related to the study of systems of [functions](wikipedia://en/185427), possibly being particular solutions of a given [partial differential equation](wikipedia://en/52564) or system of such equations, in order to prove their [completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_set) on the boundary of a given [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643). The interest of this research is obvious: given such a system of functions, every solution of a [boundary value problem](wikipedia://en/436779) can be approximated by an [infinite series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_series) or [Fourier type integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourier_integral) in the [topology](wikipedia://en/29954) of a given [function space](wikipedia://en/340630). One of the most famous examples of this kind of theorem is [Mergelyan's theorem](wikipedia://en/3277014), which completely solves the problem in the class of [holomorphic functions](wikipedia://en/14110) for a [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) in the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628). In his paper (Fichera 1948), Fichera studies this problem for [harmonic functions](wikipedia://en/55762),[^o59] relaxing the [smoothness requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) on the boundary in the already cited work (Fichera 1955a): a survey on his and others' work in this area, including contributions of [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), [Bernard Malgrange](wikipedia://en/9170142), [Felix Browder](wikipedia://en/10201445) and a number of other mathematicians, is contained in the paper (Fichera 1979c). Another branch of his studies on [approximation theory](wikipedia://en/1092713) is strictly tied to [complex analysis in one variable](wikipedia://en/5759), and to the already cited [Mergelyan's theorem](wikipedia://en/3277014): he studied the problem of approximating [continuous functions](wikipedia://en/6122) on a [compact set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_set) (and analytic on its [interior](wikipedia://en/55610) if this is non-void) of the [complex plane](wikipedia://en/217628) by [rational functions](wikipedia://en/361210) with prescribed [poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_%28complex_analysis%29), simple or not. The paper (Fichera 1974b) surveys the contribution to the solution of this and related problems by [Sergey Mergelyan](wikipedia://en/5661318), [Lennart Carleson](wikipedia://en/1669541), [Gábor Szegő](wikipedia://en/46065) as well as others, including his own.

#### Potential theory {#Potential_theory}

His contributions to [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342) are important. The results of his paper (Fichera 1948) occupy paragraph 24 of chapter II of the textbook (Günther 1967, 108–117), as remarked by in (Oleinik 1997, 11). Also, his researches (Fichera 1975) and (Fichera 1976) on the [asymptotic behaviour](wikipedia://en/641995) of the [electric field](wikipedia://en/41092) near [singular points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) of the conducting surface, widely known among the specialists (as several works of [V.G. Maz'ya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Gilelevich_Maz'ya), [S.A. Nazarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.A._Nazarov), [B.A. Plamenevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.A._Plamenevsky), [B.W. Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.W._Schulze) and others testify) can be included in between his works in potential theory.

#### Measure and integration theory {#Measure_and_integration_theory}

His main contributions to those topics and are the papers (Fichera 1943) and (Fichera 1954). In the first one he proves that a condition on a [sequence](wikipedia://en/27838) of [integrable functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrable_function) previously introduced by [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009) is both necessary and sufficient in order to assure that [limit process](wikipedia://en/26551602) and the [integration process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integration_%28mathematics%29) commute, both in [bounded and unbounded](wikipedia://en/48258) [domains](wikipedia://en/16796643): the theorem is similar in spirit to the [dominated convergence theorem](wikipedia://en/351853), which however only states a sufficient condition. The second paper contains an extension of the [Lebesgue's decomposition theorem](wikipedia://en/4316238) to [finitely additive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_additivity#Additive_%28or_finitely_additive%29_set_functions) [measures](wikipedia://en/19873): this extension required him to generalize the [Radon–Nikodym derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon–Nikodym_derivative), requiring it to be a [set function](wikipedia://en/25911217) belonging to a given class and [minimizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum) a particular [functional](wikipedia://en/1374948).

#### Complex analysis of functions of one and several variables {#Complex_analysis_of_functions_of_one_and_several_variables}

He contributed to both the classical topic of [complex analysis](wikipedia://en/5759) in one variable and the more recent one of [complex analysis in several variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables). His contributions to complex analysis in one variable are essentially [approximation results](wikipedia://en/1092713), well described in the survey paper (Fichera 1974b).[^o74] In the field of functions of several complex variables, his contributions were outstanding, but also not generally acknowledged.[^o75] Precisely, in the paper (Fichera 1957) he solved the Dirichlet problem for [holomorphic function of several variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) under the hypothesis that the [boundary](wikipedia://en/72824) of the [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) $\partial\Omega$ has a [Hölder continuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hölder_continuous) [normal vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_vector) (i.e. it belongs to the $C^{\{1,\alpha\}}$ class) and the [Dirichlet boundary condition](wikipedia://en/423504) is a [function](wikipedia://en/185427) belonging to the [Sobolev space](wikipedia://en/611964) $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$ satisfying the [weak form](wikipedia://en/3368981) of the [tangential Cauchy–Riemann condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_Cauchy–Riemann_condition),[^o77][^o78] extending a previous result of [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716): this theorem and the [Lewy–Kneser theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy–Kneser_theorem) on the [local](wikipedia://en/1462457) [Cauchy problem](wikipedia://en/1725027) for holomorphic functions of several variables, laid the foundations of the theory of [CR-functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR-function). Another important result is his proof in (Fichera 1983) of an extension of [Morera's theorem](wikipedia://en/407047) to [functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), under the hypothesis that the given [function](wikipedia://en/185427) $f$ is only [locally integrable](wikipedia://en/4258398): previous proofs under more restrictive assumptions were given by [Francesco Severi](wikipedia://en/1565716) in (Severi 1931) and [Salomon Bochner](wikipedia://en/987847) in (Bochner 1953). He also studied the properties of the [real part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_part) and [imaginary part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaginary_part) of [functions of several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables), i.e. [pluriharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/2519049): starting from the paper (Amoroso 1912) he gives a [trace condition](wikipedia://en/11199199) analogous to the [tangential Cauchy–Riemann condition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangential_Cauchy–Riemann_condition) for the solvability of the Dirichlet problem for [pluriharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/2519049) in the paper (Fichera 1982a), and generalizes a theorem of [Luigi Amoroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Amoroso) to the [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) $\mathbb{C}^n \equiv \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ for $n \ge 2$ [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) in the paper (Fichera 1982b). Also he was able to prove that an [integro-differential equation](wikipedia://en/11095324) defined on the boundary of a [smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_function) [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) by Luigi Amoroso in his cited paper, the [Amoroso integro-differential equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoroso_integro-differential_equation), is a necessary and sufficient condition for the solvability of the Dirichlet problem for [pluriharmonic functions](wikipedia://en/2519049) when this domain is the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) in $\mathbb{C}^2 \equiv \mathbb{R}^4$.[^o85]

#### Exterior differential forms {#Exterior_differential_forms}

His contributions to the theory of [exterior differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_differential_form) started as a war story:[^o87] having read a famous memoir of [Enrico Betti](wikipedia://en/979158) (where [Betti numbers](wikipedia://en/346262) were introduced) just before joining the army, he used this knowledge in order to develop a theory of [exterior differential forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exterior_differential_form) while he was kept prisoner in [Teramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teramo) jail.[^o88] When he was back in Rome in 1945, he discussed his discovery with [Enzo Martinelli](wikipedia://en/30265224), who informed him that the idea was already developed by mathematicians [Élie Cartan](wikipedia://en/386274) and [Georges de Rham](wikipedia://en/398699). However, he continued work on this theory, contributing with several papers, and also advised all of his students to study it, despite from the fact of being an [analyst](wikipedia://en/48396), as he remarks: his main results are collected in the papers (Fichera 1961a) and (Fichera 1961b). In the first one he introduced $k$-measures, a concept less general than [currents](wikipedia://en/3021207) but easier to work with: his aim was to clarify the [analytic structure](wikipedia://en/48396) of currents and to prove all relevant results of the theory i.e. the [three theorems of de Rham](wikipedia://en/221530) and [Hodge theorem on harmonic forms](wikipedia://en/501590) in a simpler, more analytic way. In the second one he developed an abstract [Hodge theory](wikipedia://en/501590), following the [axiomatic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_method), proving an abstract form of Hodge theorem.

#### Numerical analysis {#Numerical_analysis}

As noted in the "Functional analysis and eigenvalue theory" section, his main *direct* contribution to the field of [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) is the introduction of the [method of orthogonal invariants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_of_orthogonal_invariants) for the calculus of [eigenvalues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues) of [symmetric operators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symmetric_operator): however, as already remarked, it is hard to find something in his works which is not related to applications. His works on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) and [linear elasticity](wikipedia://en/301429) have always a constructive aim: for example, the results of paper (Fichera 1975), which deals with the [asymptotic analysis](wikipedia://en/641995) of the [potential](wikipedia://en/1431342), were included in the book (Fichera 1978a) and led to the definition of the [Fichera corner problem](wikipedia://en/19719776#Example:_the_Fichera_problem) as a standard [benchmark problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmark_%28computing%29) for [numerical methods](wikipedia://en/1678715).[^o94] Another example of his work on quantitative problems is the interdisciplinary study (Fichera Sneider Wyman 1977), surveyed in (Fichera 1978b), where methods of [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396) and [numerical analysis](wikipedia://en/21506) are applied to a problem posed by [biological sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_sciences).[^o97][^o98]

#### History of mathematics {#History_of_mathematics}

his work in this field occupy all the volume (Fichera 2002). He wrote bibliographical sketches for a number of mathematicians, both teachers, friends and collaborators, including [Mauro Picone](wikipedia://en/10162009), [Luigi Fantappiè](wikipedia://en/7255073), [Pia Nalli](wikipedia://en/48115731), [Maria Adelaide Sneider](wikipedia://en/41373980), [Renato Caccioppoli](wikipedia://en/4262313), [Solomon Mikhlin](wikipedia://en/22994055), [Francesco Tricomi](wikipedia://en/19830487), [Alexander Weinstein](wikipedia://en/33986254), [Aldo Ghizzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Ghizzetti). His [historical](wikipedia://en/14220) works contain several observations against the so-called **historical revisitation**: the meaning of this concept is clearly stated in the paper (Fichera 1996). He identifies with the word **revisitation** the analysis of historical facts basing only on modern conceptions and points of view: this kind of analysis differs from the "true" historical one since it is heavily affected by the historian's point of view. The historian applying this kind of methodology to [history of mathematics](wikipedia://en/14220), and more generally to the [history of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_science), emphasizes the sources that have led a field to its modern shape, neglecting the efforts of the pioneers.
