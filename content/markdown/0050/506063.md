# Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica"}

The **Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica** (abbr. **CWI**; English: "National Research Institute for Mathematics and Computer Science") is a research centre in the field of [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831) and [theoretical computer science](wikipedia://en/323392). It is part of the institutes organization of the [Dutch Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Research_Council) (NWO) and is located at the [Amsterdam Science Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Science_Park). This institute is famous as the creation site of the programming language [Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29). It was a founding member of the European Research Consortium for Informatics and Mathematics (ERCIM).

:::

## Early history {#Early_history}

The institute was founded in 1946 by [Johannes van der Corput](wikipedia://en/3791412), [David van Dantzig](wikipedia://en/528853), [Jurjen Koksma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurjen_Koksma), [Hendrik Anthony Kramers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Anthony_Kramers), [Marcel Minnaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Minnaert) and [Jan Arnoldus Schouten](wikipedia://en/2862062). It was originally called *Mathematical Centre* (in Dutch: *Mathematisch Centrum*). One early mission was to develop mathematical prediction models to assist large Dutch engineering projects, such as the [Delta Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Works). During this early period, the Mathematics Institute also helped with designing the wings of the [Fokker F27 Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27_Friendship) airplane, voted in 2006 as the most beautiful Dutch design of the 20th century.[^o4][^o5]

The computer science component developed soon after. [Adriaan van Wijngaarden](wikipedia://en/943404), considered the founder of computer science (or *informatica*) in the Netherlands, was the director of the institute for almost 20 years. [Edsger Dijkstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsger_Dijkstra) did most of his early influential work on algorithms and formal methods at CWI. The first Dutch computers, the [Electrologica X1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrologica_X1) and [Electrologica X8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrologica_X8), were both designed at the centre, and [Electrologica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrologica) was created as a spinoff to manufacture the machines.

In 1983, the name of the institute was changed to Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica (CWI) to reflect a governmental push for emphasizing computer science research in the Netherlands.[^o6]

## Recent research {#Recent_research}

The institute is known for its work in fields such as [operations research](wikipedia://en/43476), [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineering), information processing, and mathematical applications in [life sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_sciences) and [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics). More recent examples of research results from CWI include the development of scheduling algorithms for the Dutch railway system (the [Nederlandse Spoorwegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen), one of the busiest rail networks in the world) and the development of the [Python programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Python_%28programming_language%29) by [Guido van Rossum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_van_Rossum). Python has played an important role in the development of the [Google search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_search) platform from the beginning, and it continues to do so as the system grows and evolves.[^o8] Many information retrieval techniques used by packages such as [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088) were initially developed by Data Distilleries, a CWI [spinoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_spin-off).[^o9][^o10]

Work at the institute was recognized by national or international research awards, such as the [Lanchester Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanchester_Prize) (awarded yearly by [INFORMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INFORMS)), the [Gödel Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gödel_Prize) (awarded by [ACM SIGACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACM_SIGACT)) and the [Spinoza Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinoza_Prize). Most of its senior researchers hold part-time professorships at other Dutch universities, with the institute producing over 170 full professors during the course of its history. Several CWI researchers have been recognized as members of the [Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea), or as knights in the [Order of the Netherlands Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Netherlands_Lion).[^o11]

In February 2017, CWI in association with [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google) announced a successful [collision attack](wikipedia://en/969624) on [SHA 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHA_1) encryption algorithm.[^o12]

## Early European Internet {#Early_European_Internet}

CWI was a pioneer of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) in Europe through the implementation of a [TCP/IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_protocol_suite) connection to [NSFNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_Foundation_Network) in 1988.[^o14] [Piet Beertema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Beertema) at CWI established the first sustained connection outside the United States to the NSFNET for [EUnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUnet) on 17 November 1988.[^o15]

The first Dutch [country code top-level domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_code_top-level_domain) (ccTLD) issued was cwi.nl.[^o15][^o16][^o17] When this domain was registered on 1 May 1986, [.nl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.nl) became the first active ccTLD outside the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States).[^o18] For the first ten years CWI, or rather Beertema, managed the .nl administration, until in 1996 this task was transferred to its spin-off SIDN.[^o15]

The [Amsterdam Internet Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Internet_Exchange) (one of the largest Internet Exchanges in the world, in terms of both members and throughput traffic) is located at the neighbouring [SARA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stichting_Academisch_Rekencentrum_Amsterdam) (an early CWI spin-off) and [Nikhef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikhef) institutes. The [World Wide Web Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Web_Consortium) (W3C) office for the Benelux countries is located at CWI.[^o19]

## Spin-off companies {#Spin-off_companies}

CWI has demonstrated a continuing effort to put the work of its researchers at the disposal of society, mainly by collaborating with commercial companies and creating spin-off businesses. In 2000 CWI established "CWI Incubator BV", a dedicated company with the aim to generate high tech spin-off companies.[^o21] Some of the CWI spinoffs include:[^o22]

- 1956: [Electrologica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrologica), a pioneering Dutch computer manufacturer.
- 1971: [SARA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stichting_Academisch_Rekencentrum_Amsterdam) (now called [SURF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SURFnet)), founded as a center for data processing activities for [Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrije_Universiteit_Amsterdam), [Universiteit van Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiteit_van_Amsterdam), and the CWI.
- 1990: [DigiCash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigiCash), an electronic money corporation founded by [David Chaum](wikipedia://en/239365).
- 1994: [NLnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLnet_Foundation), an [Internet service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_service_provider).
- 1994: General Design / Satama Amsterdam, a design company, acquired by [LBi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LBi) (then Lost Boys international).
- 1995: Data Distilleries, developer of analytical database software aimed at information retrieval, eventually becoming part of [SPSS](wikipedia://en/179088) and acquired by [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM).
- 1996: [Stichting Internet Domeinregistratie Nederland (SIDN)](https://sidn.nl), the [.nl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.nl) top-level [domain registrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domain_registrar).
- 2000: [Software Improvement Group (SIG)](https://www.softwareimprovementgroup.com), a software improvement and legacy code analysis company.
- 2008: [MonetDB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MonetDB), a high-tech database technology company, developer of the MonetDB [column-store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column-oriented_DBMS).
- 2008: [Vectorwise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vectorwise), an analytical database technology company, founded in cooperation with the [Ingres Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingres_Corporation) (now [Actian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actian)) and eventually acquired by it.
- 2010: [Spinque](http://www.spinque.com), a company providing search technology for information retrieval specialists.
- 2013: [MonetDB Solutions](https://monetdbsolutions.com), a database services company.
- 2016: [Seita](http://seita.nl), a technology company providing demand response services for the energy sector.
- 2021: [DuckDB Labs](https://duckdblabs.com), a data processing company offering support and consulting services for DuckDB (a column-oriented RDBMS, designed for high performance on complex queries against large databases in embedded configurations).
