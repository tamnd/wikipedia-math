# Lars Hörmander

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Lars Hörmander"}

**Lars Valter Hörmander** (24 January 1931 – 25 November 2012) was a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_people) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who has been called "the foremost contributor to the modern theory of linear [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations)". Hörmander was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1962 and the [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) in 1988. In 2006 he was awarded the [Steele Prize for Mathematical Exposition](wikipedia://en/1252550) for his four-volume textbook *Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators*, which is considered a foundational work on the subject.[^o4]

:::

Hörmander completed his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1955 at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University). Hörmander then worked at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University), at [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), and at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in [Princeton, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton,_New_Jersey). He returned to Lund University as a professor from 1968 until 1996, when he retired with the title of [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus).

## Biography {#Biography}

### Education {#Education}

Hörmander was born in [Mjällby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mjällby), a village in [Blekinge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blekinge) in southern Sweden where his father was a teacher. Like his older brothers and sisters before him, he attended the [realskola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realskola) (secondary school), in a nearby town to which he commuted by train, and the [gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_%28school%29) (high school) in [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund) from which he graduated in 1948.

At the time when he entered the gymnasium, the principal had instituted an experiment of reducing the period of the education from three to two years, and the daily activities to three hours. This freedom to work on his own, "\[greater\] than the universities offer in Sweden today", suited Hörmander "very well". He was also positively influenced by his enthusiastic mathematics teacher, a [docent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Docent) at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) who encouraged him to study university-level mathematics.

After proceeding to receive a [Master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree) from Lund University in 1950, Hörmander began his graduate studies under [Marcel Riesz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Riesz) (who had also been the advisor for Hörmander's gymnasium teacher). He made his first research attempts in classical function theory and [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), which "did not amount to much" but were "an excellent preparation for working in the theory of partial differential equations." He turned to partial differential equations when Riesz retired and [Lars Gårding](wikipedia://en/8000370) who worked actively in that area was appointed professor.

Hörmander took a one-year break for [military service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_service) from 1953 to 1954, but due to his position in defense research was able to proceed with his studies even during that time. His [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) thesis *On the theory of general partial differential operators* was finished in 1955, inspired by the nearly concurrent Ph.D. work of Bernard Malgrange and techniques for hyperbolic differential operators developed by [Lars Gårding](wikipedia://en/8000370) and [Jean Leray](wikipedia://en/245430).

### Fields Medal and years in the U.S. {#Fields_Medal_and_years_in_the_U.S.}

Hörmander applied for a professorship at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University), but temporarily left for the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States) while the request was examined. He spent quarters from winter to fall in respective order at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago), the [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas), the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota), and finally at the [Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courant_Institute_of_Mathematical_Sciences) in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City). These locations offered "much to learn" in partial differential equations, with the exception of Chicago of which he however notes the [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477) seminar held by [Elias Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_Stein) and [Guido Weiss](wikipedia://en/36535208) to have strengthened his familiarity with harmonic analysis.

In the theory of linear differential operators, "many people have contributed but the deepest and most significant results are due to Hörmander", according to Hörmander's doctoral advisor, Lars Gårding. Hörmander won the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1962.[^o8]

Hörmander was given a position as a part-time professor at Stanford in 1963 but was soon thereafter offered a professorship at the [Institute for Advanced Study](wikipedia://en/184179) in Princeton, New Jersey. He first wished not to leave Sweden but attempts to find a research professorship in Sweden failed and "the opportunity to do research full time in a mathematically very active environment was hard to resist", so he accepted the offer and resigned from both Stanford and Stockholm and began at the Institute in the fall of 1964. Within two years of "hard work", he felt that the environment at the institute was too demanding, and in 1967 decided to return to Lund after one year. He later noted that his best work at the institute was done during the remaining year.

### Later years {#Later_years}

Hörmander mostly remained at [Lund University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lund_University) as a professor after 1968 but made several visits to the United States during the two next decades. He visited the Courant Institute in 1970, and also the Institute for Advanced Study in 1971 and during the academic year, 1977–1978 when a special year in [microlocal analysis](wikipedia://en/6108841) was held. He also visited Stanford in 1971, 1977, and 1982, and the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) in winter 1990. Hörmander was briefly director of the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) in Stockholm between 1984 and 1986 but only accepted a two-year appointment as he "suspected that the administrative duties would not agree well" with him, and found that "the hunch was right". He also served as vice president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868) between 1987 and 1990. Hörmander retired [emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) in Lund in January 1996. In 2006 he was honored with the [Steele Prize for Mathematical Exposition](wikipedia://en/1252550) from the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).

He was made a member of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1968. In 1970 he gave a plenary address (Linear Differential Operators) at the [ICM](wikipedia://en/627842) in [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice).

He received the 1988 [Wolf Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Prize) "for fundamental work in modern analysis, in particular, the application of [pseudo differential](wikipedia://en/973479) and [Fourier integral operators](wikipedia://en/10138003) to linear partial differential equations".

In 2012 he was selected as a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822), but died on 25 November 2012,[^o10] before the list of fellows was released.[^o11]

## Important publications {#Important_publications}

His book *Linear Partial Differential Operators*, which largely was the cause for his Fields Medal, has been described as "the first major account of this theory". It was published by [Springer-Verlag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springer_Science+Business_Media) in 1963 as part of the *[Grundlehren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grundlehren)* series. In the U.S., this book is now in the public domain and reprints are available under ISBN 978-1014977045.

Hörmander devoted five years to compiling the four-volume [monograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monograph), *The Analysis of Linear Partial Differential Operators*, first published between 1983 and 1985. A follow-up of his *Linear Partial Differential Operators*, "illustrate\[d\] the vast expansion of the subject" over the past 20 years, and is considered the "standard of the field". In addition to these works, he has written a recognised introduction to [several complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Several_complex_variables) based on his 1964 Stanford lectures, and wrote the entries on [differential equations](wikipedia://en/1424309) in [Nationalencyklopedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalencyklopedin).
