# Charles Fefferman

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Charles Fefferman"}

**Charles Louis Fefferman** (born April 18, 1949) is an American [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he is currently the Herbert E. Jones, Jr. '43 University Professor of Mathematics. He was awarded the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978 for his contributions to [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396).

:::

## Early life and education {#Early_life_and_education}

Fefferman was born to a Jewish family,[^o4][^o5] in Washington, DC. He was a [child prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_prodigy), entered the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) at age 14,[^o6][^o7] and had written his first scientific paper by the age of 15.[^o6] He graduated with degrees in math and physics at 17,[^o8] and earned his [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in mathematics three years later from [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), under [Elias Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_Stein). His doctoral dissertation was titled "Inequalities for strongly singular convolution operators".[^o9] Fefferman achieved a full professorship at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) at the age of 22, making him the youngest full professor ever appointed in the United States.[^n1]

## Career {#Career}

At the age of 25, he returned to Princeton as a full professor, becoming the youngest person to be promoted to the title.[^o11] He won the [Alan T. Waterman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_T._Waterman_Award) in 1976[^o7] (the first person to get the award) and the [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) in 1978 for his work in [mathematical analysis](wikipedia://en/48396), specifically convergence and divergence.[^o6] He was elected to the [National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Academy_of_Sciences) in 1979.[^o12] He was appointed the Herbert Jones Professor at Princeton in 1984.

In addition to the above, his honors include the [Salem Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Prize) in 1971, the [Bergman Prize](wikipedia://en/9395687#The_Bergman_Prize) in 1992,[^o13] the [Bôcher Memorial Prize](wikipedia://en/1099872) in 2008,[^o14] and the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) for 2017,[^o15] as well as election to the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences) and the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society).[^o16][^o17] In 2021 he was awarded the [BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBVA_Foundation_Frontiers_of_Knowledge_Award) in Basic Sciences.[^o18]

Fefferman contributed several innovations that revised the study of multidimensional complex analysis by finding fruitful generalisations of classical low-dimensional results. Fefferman's work on [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations), [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659), in particular convergence, multipliers, divergence, singular integrals and [Hardy spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardy_spaces) earned him a [Fields Medal](wikipedia://en/10859) at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) at [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) in 1978.[^o19] He was a Plenary Speaker of the ICM in 1974 in Vancouver.[^o20]

His early work included a study of the [asymptotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotics) of the [Bergman kernel](wikipedia://en/3205923) off the boundaries of [pseudoconvex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoconvex) domains in $\mathbb C^n$.[^o21] He has studied mathematical physics, [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147), [fluid dynamics](wikipedia://en/11034), [neural networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_network), [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), [mathematical finance](wikipedia://en/33589680) and [spectral analysis](wikipedia://en/506713), amongst others.

## Family {#Family}

Fefferman and his wife Julie have two daughters, Nina and Lainie. Lainie Fefferman is a composer, taught math at [Saint Ann's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Ann's_School_%28Brooklyn%29) and holds a degree in music from [Yale University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_University) and a Ph.D. in music composition from Princeton.[^o23] She has an interest in [Middle Eastern music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Middle_Eastern_and_North_African_folk_music_traditions).[^o24] [Nina Fefferman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Fefferman) is a computational biologist at the University of Tennessee who studies the application of mathematical models to biological systems.[^o25]

Fefferman's brother, [Robert Fefferman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Fefferman), is also a mathematician and former Dean of the Physical Sciences Division at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago).[^o26] Fefferman is also the nephew of mathematician [Abe Gelbart](wikipedia://en/38968041).[^o27]

- Fefferman, Charles. (2005). "A sharp form of Whitney's extension theorem". *Annals of Mathematics*. 161 (1). p. 509–577. doi:10.4007/annals.2005.161.509
