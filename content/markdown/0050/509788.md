# Skew-Hermitian matrix

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Skew-Hermitian matrix"}

In [linear algebra](wikipedia://en/18422), a [square matrix](wikipedia://en/166022) with [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) entries is said to be **skew-Hermitian** or **anti-Hermitian** if its [conjugate transpose](wikipedia://en/217548) is the negative of the original matrix.[^o2] That is, the matrix $A$ is skew-Hermitian if it satisfies the relation

Skew-Hermitian matrices can be understood as the complex versions of real [skew-symmetric matrices](wikipedia://en/174055), or as the matrix analogue of the purely imaginary numbers.[^o5] The set of all skew-Hermitian $n \times n$ matrices forms the $u(n)$ [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944), which corresponds to the Lie group [U(n)](wikipedia://en/173993). The concept can be generalized to include [linear transformations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_transformation) of any [complex](wikipedia://en/5826) [vector space](wikipedia://en/32370) with a [Hermitian product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermitian_product).

Note that the [adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjoint_operator) of an operator depends on the [scalar product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalar_product) considered on the $n$ dimensional complex or real space $K^n$. If $(\cdot\mid\cdot)$ denotes the scalar product on $K^n$, then saying $A$ is skew-adjoint means that for all $\mathbf u, \mathbf v \in K^n$ one has $(A \mathbf u \mid \mathbf v) = - (\mathbf u \mid A \mathbf v)$.

In dimension 1, the skew-adjoint operators are exactly the [imaginary numbers](wikipedia://en/14554), whereas [real numbers](wikipedia://en/20646438) correspond to [self-adjoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-adjoint) operators.

## Example {#Example}

For example, the following matrix is skew-Hermitian

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} -i & 2 + i \\ -2 + i & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

because

$$-A = \begin{bmatrix} i & -2 - i \\ 2 - i & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \overline{-i} & \overline{-2 + i} \\ \overline{2 + i} & \overline{0} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \overline{-i} & \overline{2 + i} \\ \overline{-2 + i} & \overline{0} \end{bmatrix}^\mathsf{T} = A^\mathsf{H}$$

## Properties {#Properties}

- The eigenvalues of a skew-Hermitian matrix are all purely imaginary (and possibly zero). Furthermore, skew-Hermitian matrices are [normal](wikipedia://en/170366). Hence they are diagonalizable and their eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues must be orthogonal.[^o10]
- All entries on the [main diagonal](wikipedia://en/194990) of a skew-Hermitian matrix have to be pure [imaginary](wikipedia://en/14554); i.e., on the imaginary axis (the number zero is also considered purely imaginary).[^o11]
- If $A$ and $B$ are skew-Hermitian, then $aA + bB$ is skew-Hermitian for all [real](wikipedia://en/20646438) [scalars](wikipedia://en/3588331) $a$ and $b$.[^o12]
- $A$ is skew-Hermitian *if and only if* $i A$ (or equivalently, $-i A$) is [Hermitian](wikipedia://en/189682).[^o12]
- $A$ is skew-Hermitian *if and only if* the real part $\Re{(A)}$ is [skew-symmetric](wikipedia://en/174055) and the imaginary part $\Im{(A)}$ is [symmetric](wikipedia://en/126474).
- If $A$ is skew-Hermitian, then $A^k$ is Hermitian if $k$ is an even integer and skew-Hermitian if $k$ is an odd integer.
- $A$ is skew-Hermitian if and only if $\mathbf{x}^\mathsf{H} A \mathbf{y} = -\overline{\mathbf{y}^\mathsf{H} A \mathbf{x}}$ for all vectors $\mathbf x, \mathbf y$.
- If $A$ is skew-Hermitian, then the [matrix exponential](wikipedia://en/672731) $e^A$ is [unitary](wikipedia://en/129222).
- The space of skew-Hermitian matrices forms the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $u(n)$ of the [Lie group](wikipedia://en/17945) $U(n)$.

:::

## Decomposition into Hermitian and skew-Hermitian {#Decomposition_into_Hermitian_and_skew-Hermitian}

- The sum of a square matrix and its conjugate transpose $\left(A + A^\mathsf{H}\right)$ is Hermitian.
- The difference of a square matrix and its conjugate transpose $\left(A - A^\mathsf{H}\right)$ is skew-Hermitian. This implies that the [commutator](wikipedia://en/7193) of two Hermitian matrices is skew-Hermitian.
- An arbitrary square matrix $C$ can be written as the sum of a Hermitian matrix $A$ and a skew-Hermitian matrix $B$: $$C = A + B \quad\mbox{with}\quad A = \frac{1}{2}\left(C + C^\mathsf{H}\right) \quad\mbox{and}\quad B = \frac{1}{2}\left(C - C^\mathsf{H}\right)$$
