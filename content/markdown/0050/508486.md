# Dodecagon

Regular polygon stat table

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Dodecagon"}

In [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446), a **dodecagon**, or **12-gon**, is any twelve-sided [polygon](wikipedia://en/23621).

:::

## Regular dodecagon {#Regular_dodecagon}

*Image: Fit\_3\_unit\_squares\_in\_unit\_circle.svg.* Three squares of sides *R* can be cut and rearranged into a dodecagon of [circumradius](wikipedia://en/161262) *R*, yielding a [proof without words](wikipedia://en/18045517) that its area is 3*R*<sup>2</sup>

A [regular](wikipedia://en/333306) dodecagon is a figure with sides of the same length and internal angles of the same size. It has twelve lines of reflective symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 12. A regular dodecagon is represented by the [Schläfli symbol](wikipedia://en/489394) {12} and can be constructed as a [truncated](wikipedia://en/4224429) [hexagon](wikipedia://en/59733), t{6}, or a twice-truncated [triangle](wikipedia://en/30654), tt{3}. The internal angle at each vertex of a regular dodecagon is 150°.

### Area {#Area}

The [area](wikipedia://en/1209) of a regular dodecagon of side length *a* is given by:

$$\begin{aligned} A & = 3 \cot\left(\frac{\pi}{12} \right) a^2 = 3 \left(2+\sqrt{3} \right) a^2 \\ & \simeq 11.19615242\,a^2 \end{aligned}$$

And in terms of the [apothem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apothem) *r* (see also [inscribed figure](wikipedia://en/4470203)), the area is:

$$\begin{aligned} A & = 12 \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) r^2 = 12 \left(2-\sqrt{3} \right) r^2 \\ & \simeq 3.2153903\,r^2 \end{aligned}$$

In terms of the [circumradius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumradius) *R*, the area is:[^o4]

$$A = 6 \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right) R^2 = 3 R^2$$

The span *S* of the dodecagon is the distance between two parallel sides and is equal to twice the apothem. A simple formula for area (given side length and span) is:

$$A = 3aS$$

This can be verified with the trigonometric relationship:

$$S = a(1+ 2\cos{30^{\circ}} + 2\cos{60^{\circ}})$$

### Perimeter {#Perimeter}

The [perimeter](wikipedia://en/23636) of a regular dodecagon in terms of circumradius is:[^o6]

$$\begin{aligned} p & = 24R \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) = 12R \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}\\ & \simeq 6.21165708246\,R \end{aligned}$$

The perimeter in terms of apothem is:

$$\begin{aligned} p & = 24r \tan\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right) = 24r(2-\sqrt{3})\\ & \simeq 6.43078061835\,r \end{aligned}$$

This coefficient is double the coefficient found in the apothem equation for area.[^o7]

*Image: Isotoxal 12-gon rhombic dissection-size2.svg.* Isotoxal dodecagon

[Coxeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter) states that every [zonogon](wikipedia://en/55312103) (a 2*m*-gon whose opposite sides are parallel and of equal length) can be dissected into *m*(*m*-1)/2 parallelograms.[^o12] In particular this is true for regular polygons with evenly many sides, in which case the parallelograms are all [rhombi](wikipedia://en/183884). For the *regular dodecagon*, *m*=6, and it can be divided into 15: 3 squares, 6 wide 30° rhombs and 6 narrow 15° rhombs. This decomposition is based on a [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) projection of a [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462), with 15 of 240 faces. The sequence OEIS sequence defines the number of solutions as 908, including up to 12-fold rotations and chiral forms in reflection.

## Symmetry {#Symmetry}

*Image: Regular\_dodecagon\_symmetries4.png.* The symmetries of a regular dodecagon as shown with colors on edges and vertices. [John Conway](wikipedia://en/15807) labels these lower symmetries with a letter and order of the symmetry follows the letter. He gives **d** (diagonal, diasymmetry) with mirror lines through vertices, **p** with mirror lines through edges (perpendicular, persymmetry) **i** with mirror lines through both vertices and edges (isosymmetry), and **g** for rotational (gyrosymmetry). **a1** labels asymmetry. These lower symmetries allows degrees of freedoms in defining irregular dodecagons.[^o19]

The *regular dodecagon* has Dih<sub>12</sub> symmetry, order 24. There are 15 distinct subgroup dihedral and cyclic symmetries. Each subgroup symmetry allows one or more degrees of freedom for irregular forms. Only the **g12** subgroup has no degrees of freedom but can be seen as [directed edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed_edge).

## Skew dodecagon {#Skew_dodecagon}

*Image: Regular skew polygon in hexagonal antiprism.svg.* A regular skew dodecagon seen as zig-zagging edges of a [hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125).

A **skew dodecagon** is a [skew polygon](wikipedia://en/6545461) with 12 vertices and edges but not existing on the same plane. The interior of such a dodecagon is not generally defined. A *skew zig-zag dodecagon* has vertices alternating between two parallel planes.

A **[regular skew dodecagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_skew_dodecagon)** is [vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive) with equal edge lengths. In 3-dimensions it will be a zig-zag skew dodecagon and can be seen in the vertices and side edges of a [hexagonal antiprism](wikipedia://en/2860125) with the same D<sub>5d</sub>, \[2<sup>\+</sup>,10\] symmetry, order 20. The dodecagrammic antiprism, s{2,24/5} and dodecagrammic crossed-antiprism, s{2,24/7} also have regular skew dodecagons.

### Petrie polygons {#Petrie_polygons}

The regular dodecagon is the [Petrie polygon](wikipedia://en/18571869) for many higher-dimensional polytopes, seen as [orthogonal projections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthogonal_projection) in [Coxeter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_plane). Examples in 4 dimensions are the [24-cell](wikipedia://en/293485), [snub 24-cell](wikipedia://en/3704756), [6-6 duoprism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-6_duoprism), [6-6 duopyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-6_duopyramid). In 6 dimensions [6-cube](wikipedia://en/10083462), [6-orthoplex](wikipedia://en/10083518), [2<sub>21</sub>](wikipedia://en/19232473), [1<sub>22</sub>](wikipedia://en/19070548). It is also the Petrie polygon for the [grand 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604599) and [great stellated 120-cell](wikipedia://en/8604610).

## Related figures {#Related_figures}

A [dodecagram](wikipedia://en/26173953) is a 12-sided star polygon, represented by symbol {12/n}. There is one regular [star polygon](wikipedia://en/313303): {12/5}, using the same vertices, but connecting every fifth point. There are also three compounds: {12/2} is reduced to 2{6} as two [hexagons](wikipedia://en/59733), and {12/3} is reduced to 3{4} as three [squares](wikipedia://en/659939), {12/4} is reduced to 4{3} as four triangles, and {12/6} is reduced to 6{2} as six degenerate [digons](wikipedia://en/5442728).

Deeper truncations of the regular dodecagon and dodecagrams can produce isogonal ([vertex-transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertex-transitive)) intermediate star polygon forms with equal spaced vertices and two edge lengths. A truncated hexagon is a dodecagon, t{6}={12}. A quasitruncated hexagon, inverted as {6/5}, is a dodecagram: t{6/5}={12/5}.[^o33]

## Examples in use {#Examples_in_use}

In [block capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_capitals), the letters [E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E), [H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H) and [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X) (and [I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I) in a [slab serif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slab_serif) font) have dodecagonal outlines. A [cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross) is a dodecagon, as is the logo for the [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet) automobile division.

*Image: Segovia Vera Cruz.jpg.* The Vera Cruz church in [Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segovia)

The regular dodecagon features prominently in many buildings. The [Torre del Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torre_del_Oro) is a dodecagonal military [watchtower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtower_%28fortification%29) in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville), southern [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain), built by the [Almohad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_dynasty). The early thirteenth century Vera Cruz church in [Segovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segovia), Spain is dodecagonal. Another example is the Porta di Venere (Venus' Gate), in [Spello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spello), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy), built in the 1st century BC has two dodecagonal towers, called "Propertius' Towers".

*Image: 1942 threepence reverse.jpg.* A 1942 [British threepence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threepence_%28British_coin%29), reverse

Regular [dodecagonal coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecagonal_coin) include:

- [British threepenny bit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threepence_%28British_coin%29) from 1937 to 1971, when it ceased to be legal tender.
- [British One Pound Coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_pound_%28British_coin%29), introduced in 2017.
- [Australian 50-cent coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_50-cent_coin)
- [Fijian 50 cents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_of_the_Fijian_dollar)
- [Tongan 50-seniti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongan_paʻanga), since 1974
- [Solomon Islands 50 cents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon_Islands_dollar)
- [Croatian 25 kuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_kuna)
- [Romanian 5000 lei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_leu), 2001–2005
- [Canadian penny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_%28Canadian_coin%29), 1982–1996
- [South Vietnamese 20 đồng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnamese_đồng), 1968–1975
- [Zambian 50 ngwee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambian_kwacha), 1969–1992
- [Malawian 50 tambala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawian_kwacha), 1986–1995
- [Mexican 20 centavos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_peso), 1992-2009
- [Israeli 5 new shekel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel)
