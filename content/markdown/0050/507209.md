# Hyperfactorial

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hyperfactorial"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), and more specifically [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), the **hyperfactorial** of a positive [integer](wikipedia://en/14563) $n$ is the product of the numbers of the form $x^x$ from $1^1$ to $n^n$.

:::

::: {#o3 .definition cue=heading name="Definition"}

## Definition {#Definition}

The **hyperfactorial** of a positive integer $n$ is the product of the numbers $1^1, 2^2, \dots, n^n$. That is,

$$H(n) = 1^1\cdot 2^2\cdot \cdots n^n = \prod_{i=1}^{n} i^i = n^n H(n-1).$$

## Interpolation and approximation {#Interpolation_and_approximation}

The hyperfactorials were studied beginning in the 19th century by [Hermann Kinkelin](wikipedia://en/38058761) and [James Whitbread Lee Glaisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Whitbread_Lee_Glaisher). As Kinkelin showed, just as the [factorials](wikipedia://en/10606) can be [continuously](wikipedia://en/6122) interpolated by the [gamma function](wikipedia://en/12316), the hyperfactorials can be continuously interpolated by the [K-function](wikipedia://en/2060130) as $K(n+1)= H(n)$.

Glaisher provided an [asymptotic](wikipedia://en/641995) formula for the hyperfactorials, analogous to [Stirling's formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling's_formula) for the factorials:

$$H(n) = An^{(6n^2+6n+1)/12}e^{-n^2/4}\left(1+\frac{1}{720n^2}-\frac{1433}{7257600n^4}+\cdots\right)\!,$$

where $A\approx 1.28243$ is the [Glaisher–Kinkelin constant](wikipedia://en/2076831).

## Other properties {#Other_properties}

According to an analogue of [Wilson's theorem](wikipedia://en/227323) on the behavior of factorials [modulo](wikipedia://en/20087) [prime](wikipedia://en/23666) numbers, when $p$ is an [odd](wikipedia://en/143135) prime number

$$H(p-1)\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}(p-1)!!\pmod{p},$$

where $!!$ is the notation for the [double factorial](wikipedia://en/507208).

The hyperfactorials give the sequence of [discriminants](wikipedia://en/55607) of [Hermite polynomials](wikipedia://en/195984) in their probabilistic formulation.
