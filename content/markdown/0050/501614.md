# Ramification group

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Ramification group"}

In [number theory](wikipedia://en/21527), more specifically in [local class field theory](wikipedia://en/669532), the **ramification groups** are a [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014) of the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of a [local field](wikipedia://en/145375) extension, which gives detailed information on the [ramification](wikipedia://en/384644) phenomena of the extension.

:::

::: {#o3 .pointer-item cue=pointer name="Decomposition group of an extension of valuations"}

## Ramification theory of valuations {#Ramification_theory_of_valuations}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **ramification theory of valuations** studies the set of [extensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extension_of_a_valuation) of a [valuation](wikipedia://en/347049) *v* of a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* to an [extension](wikipedia://en/11634) *L* of *K*. It is a generalization of the ramification theory of Dedekind domains.[^o4][^o5]

The structure of the set of extensions is known better when *L*/*K* is [Galois](wikipedia://en/457064).

### Decomposition group and inertia group {#Decomposition_group_and_inertia_group}

Let (*K*, *v*) be a [valued field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valued_field) and let *L* be a [finite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finite_extension) [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of *K*. Let *S<sub>v</sub>* be the set of [equivalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalence_of_valuations) [classes](wikipedia://en/9260) of extensions of *v* to *L* and let *G* be the [Galois group](wikipedia://en/12673) of *L* over *K*. Then *G* acts on *S<sub>v</sub>* by σ\[*w*\] = \[*w* ∘ σ\] (i.e. *w* is a [representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_%28mathematics%29) of the equivalence class \[*w*\] ∈ *S<sub>v</sub>* and \[*w*\] is sent to the equivalence class of the [composition](wikipedia://en/195947) of *w* with the [automorphism](wikipedia://en/1160) σ : *L* → *L*; this is independent of the choice of *w* in \[*w*\]). In fact, this action is [transitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_action).

Given a fixed extension *w* of *v* to *L*, the **decomposition group of *w*** is the [stabilizer subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilizer_subgroup) *G<sub>w</sub>* of \[*w*\], i.e. it is the [subgroup](wikipedia://en/28011) of *G* consisting of all elements that fix the equivalence class \[*w*\] ∈ *S<sub>v</sub>*.

Let *m<sub>w</sub>* denote the [maximal ideal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximal_ideal_of_a_valuation) of *w* inside the [valuation ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_ring_of_a_valuation) *R<sub>w</sub>* of *w*. The **inertia group of *w*** is the subgroup *I<sub>w</sub>* of *G<sub>w</sub>* consisting of elements *σ* such that σ*x* ≡ *x* (mod *m<sub>w</sub>*) for all *x* in *R<sub>w</sub>*. In other words, *I<sub>w</sub>* consists of the elements of the decomposition group that [act trivially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivial_action) on the [residue field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residue_field_of_a_valuation) of *w*. It is a [normal subgroup](wikipedia://en/21918) of *G<sub>w</sub>*.

The [reduced ramification index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduced_ramification_index_of_an_extension_of_valuations) *e*(*w*/*v*) is independent of *w* and is denoted *e*(*v*). Similarly, the [relative degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_degree_of_an_extension_of_valuations) *f*(*w*/*v*) is also independent of *w* and is denoted *f*(*v*).

## Ramification groups in lower numbering {#Ramification_groups_in_lower_numbering}

Ramification groups are a refinement of the Galois group $G$ of a finite $L/K$ [Galois extension](wikipedia://en/457064) of [local fields](wikipedia://en/145375). We shall write $w, \mathcal O_L, \mathfrak p$ for the valuation, the ring of integers and its maximal ideal for $L$. As a consequence of [Hensel's lemma](wikipedia://en/1633368), one can write $\mathcal O_L = \mathcal O_K[\alpha]$ for some $\alpha \in L$ where $\mathcal O_K$ is the ring of integers of $K$.[^o9] (This is stronger than the [primitive element theorem](wikipedia://en/448933).) Then, for each integer $i \ge -1$, we define $G_i$ to be the set of all $s \in G$ that satisfies the following equivalent conditions.

- (i) $s$ operates trivially on $\mathcal O_L / \mathfrak p^{i+1}.$
- (ii) $w(s(x) - x) \ge i+1$ for all $x \in \mathcal O_L$
- (iii) $w(s(\alpha) - \alpha) \ge i+1.$

The group $G_i$ is called *$i$-th ramification group*. They form a decreasing [filtration](wikipedia://en/726014),

$$G_{-1} = G \supset G_0 \supset G_1 \supset \dots \{*\}.$$

In fact, the $G_i$ are normal by (i) and [trivial](wikipedia://en/193059) for sufficiently large $i$ by (iii). For the lowest indices, it is customary to call $G_0$ the [inertia subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertia_subgroup) of $G$ because of its relation to [splitting of prime ideals](wikipedia://en/1190816), while $G_1$ the [wild inertia subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_inertia_subgroup) of $G$. The quotient $G_0 / G_1$ is called the tame quotient.

The Galois group $G$ and its subgroups $G_i$ are studied by employing the above filtration or, more specifically, the corresponding quotients. In particular,

- $G/G_0 = \operatorname{Gal}(l/k),$ where $l, k$ are the (finite) residue fields of $L, K$.[^o10]
- $G_0 = 1 \Leftrightarrow L/K$ is [unramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unramified_extension).
- $G_1 = 1 \Leftrightarrow L/K$ is [tamely ramified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamely_ramified) (i.e., the ramification index is prime to the residue characteristic.)

The study of ramification groups reduces to the totally ramified case since one has $G_i = (G_0)_i$ for $i \ge 0$.

One also defines the function $i_G(s) = w(s(\alpha) - \alpha), s \in G$. (ii) in the above shows $i_G$ is independent of choice of $\alpha$ and, moreover, the study of the filtration $G_i$ is essentially equivalent to that of $i_G$.[^o11] $i_G$ satisfies the following: for $s, t \in G$,

- $$i_G(s) \ge i + 1 \Leftrightarrow s \in G_i.$$
- $$i_G(t s t^{-1}) = i_G(s).$$
- $$i_G(st) \ge \min\{ i_G(s), i_G(t) \}.$$

Fix a uniformizer $\pi$ of $L$. Then $s \mapsto s(\pi)/\pi$ induces the injection $G_i/G_{i+1} \to U_{L, i}/U_{L, i+1}, i \ge 0$ where $U_{L, 0} = \mathcal{O}_L^\times, U_{L, i} = 1 + \mathfrak{p}^i$. (The map actually does not depend on the choice of the uniformizer.[^o12]) It follows from this[^o13]

- $G_0/G_1$ is cyclic of order prime to $p$
- $G_i/G_{i+1}$ is a product of cyclic groups of order $p$.

In particular, $G_1$ is a [*p*-group](wikipedia://en/24664) and $G_0$ is [solvable](wikipedia://en/105012).

The ramification groups can be used to compute the [different](wikipedia://en/2371783) $\mathfrak{D}_{L/K}$ of the extension $L/K$ and that of subextensions:[^o14]

$$w(\mathfrak{D}_{L/K}) = \sum_{s \ne 1} i_G(s) = \sum_{i=0}^\infty (|G_i| - 1).$$

If $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$, then, for $\sigma \in G$, $i_{G/H}(\sigma) = {1 \over e_{L/K}} \sum_{s \mapsto \sigma} i_G(s)$.[^o15]

Combining this with the above one obtains: for a subextension $F/K$ corresponding to $H$,

$$v_F(\mathfrak{D}_{F/K}) = {1 \over e_{L/F}} \sum_{s \not\in H} i_G(s).$$

If $s \in G_i, t \in G_j, i, j \ge 1$, then $sts^{-1}t^{-1} \in G_{i+j+1}$.[^o16] In the terminology of [Lazard](wikipedia://en/47432466), this can be understood to mean the [Lie algebra](wikipedia://en/17944) $\operatorname{gr}(G_1) = \sum_{i \ge 1} G_i/G_{i+1}$ is abelian.

### Example: the cyclotomic extension {#Example:_the_cyclotomic_extension}

The ramification groups for a [cyclotomic extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_extension) $K_n := \mathbf Q_p(\zeta)/\mathbf Q_p$, where $\zeta$ is a $p^n$-th primitive [root of unity](wikipedia://en/171950), can be described explicitly:[^o18]

$$G_s = \operatorname{Gal}(K_n / K_e),$$

where *e* is chosen such that $p^{e-1} \le s < p^e$.

### Example: a quartic extension {#Example:_a_quartic_extension}

Let *K* be the extension of $\mathbf{Q}_{2}$ generated by $x_1=\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2}}$. The conjugates of $x_1$ are $x_2 = \sqrt{2-\sqrt{2}}$, $x_3 = -x_1$, $x_4 = -x_2$.

A little computation shows that the quotient of any two of these is a [unit](wikipedia://en/289666). Hence they all generate the same ideal; call it $\pi$. $\sqrt{2}$ generates $\pi$<sup>2</sup>; (2)=$\pi$<sup>4</sup>.

Now $x_1-x_3=2x_1$, which is in $\pi$<sup>5</sup>.

and $x_1 - x_2 = \sqrt{4-2\sqrt{2}},$ which is in $\pi$<sup>3</sup>.

Various methods show that the Galois group of *K* is $C_4$, cyclic of order 4. Also:

$$G_0 = G_1 = G_2 = C_4.$$

and $G_3 = G_4=(13)(24).$

$w(\mathfrak{D}_{K/Q_2}) = 3+3+3+1+1 = 11,$ so that the different $\mathfrak{D}_{K/Q_2} = \pi^{11}$

$x_1$ satisfies *X*<sup>4</sup> − 4*X*<sup>2</sup> + 2, which has discriminant 2048 = 2<sup>11</sup>.

## Ramification groups in upper numbering {#Ramification_groups_in_upper_numbering}

If $u$ is a real number $\ge -1$, let $G_u$ denote $G_i$ where *i* the least integer $\ge u$. In other words, $s \in G_u \Leftrightarrow i_G(s) \ge u+1.$ Define $\phi$ by[^o21]

$$\phi(u) = \int_0^u {dt \over (G_0 : G_t)}$$

where, by convention, $(G_0 : G_t)$ is equal to $(G_{-1} : G_0)^{-1}$ if $t = -1$ and is equal to $1$ for $-1 < t \le 0$.[^o22] Then $\phi(u) = u$ for $-1 \le u \le 0$. It is immediate that $\phi$ is continuous and strictly increasing, and thus has the continuous inverse function $\psi$ defined on $[-1, \infty)$. Define $G^v = G_{\psi(v)}$. $G^v$ is then called the ***v*-th ramification group** in upper numbering. In other words, $G^{\phi(u)} = G_u$. Note $G^{-1} = G, G^0 = G_0$. The upper numbering is defined so as to be compatible with passage to quotients:[^o23] if $H$ is normal in $G$, then

$(G/H)^v = G^v H / H$ for all $v$

(whereas lower numbering is compatible with passage to subgroups.)

::: {#o25 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=named-heading name="Herbrand's theorem"}

### Herbrand's theorem {#Herbrand's_theorem}

**Herbrand's theorem** states that the ramification groups in the lower numbering satisfy $G_u H/H = (G/H)_v$ (for $v = \phi_{L/F}(u)$ where $L/F$ is the subextension corresponding to $H$), and that the ramification groups in the upper numbering satisfy $G^u H/H = (G/H)^u$.[^o26][^o27] This allows one to define ramification groups in the upper numbering for infinite Galois extensions (such as the [absolute Galois group](wikipedia://en/2996781) of a local field) from the inverse system of ramification groups for finite subextensions.

The upper numbering for an abelian extension is important because of the [Hasse–Arf theorem](wikipedia://en/22590101). It states that if $G$ is abelian, then the jumps in the filtration $G^v$ are integers; i.e., $G_i = G_{i+1}$ whenever $\phi(i)$ is not an integer.[^o28]

The upper numbering is compatible with the filtration of the norm residue group by the unit groups under the [Artin isomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artin_isomorphism). The image of $G^n(L/K)$ under the isomorphism

$$G(L/K)^{\mathrm{ab}} \leftrightarrow K^*/N_{L/K}(L^*)$$

is just[^o29]

$$U^n_K / (U^n_K \cap N_{L/K}(L^*)) \ .$$

:::
