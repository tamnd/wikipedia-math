# Solid geometry

*Image: Hyperboloid1.png.* [Hyperboloid](wikipedia://en/145844) of one sheet

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Solid geometry"}

**Solid geometry** or **stereometry** is the [geometry](wikipedia://en/18973446) of [three-dimensional](wikipedia://en/3054853) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) (3D space).[^o2] A **solid figure** is the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_%28mathematics%29) of 3D space bounded by a [two-dimensional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-dimensional) [closed surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_surface); for example, a solid [ball](wikipedia://en/160556) consists of a [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859) and its [interior](wikipedia://en/55610).

:::

Solid geometry deals with the [measurements](wikipedia://en/19022) of [volumes](wikipedia://en/32498) of various solids, including [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045), [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007), [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubes) (and other [polyhedrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedrons)), [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29), [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29) (including [truncated](wikipedia://en/89405)) and other [solids of revolution](wikipedia://en/329400).[^o3]

## History {#History}

The [Pythagoreans](wikipedia://en/291170) dealt with the [regular solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_solid), but the pyramid, prism, cone and cylinder were not studied until the [Platonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonism). [Eudoxus](wikipedia://en/48388) established their measurement, proving the pyramid and cone to have one-third the volume of a prism and cylinder on the same base and of the same height. He was probably also the discoverer of a proof that the volume enclosed by a sphere is proportional to the cube of its [radius](wikipedia://en/1780815).[^o5]

## Topics {#Topics}

Basic topics in solid geometry and stereometry include:

- [incidence](wikipedia://en/387224) of [planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_%28geometry%29) and [lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_%28mathematics%29)
- [dihedral angle](wikipedia://en/338046) and [solid angle](wikipedia://en/94102)
- the [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_%28geometry%29), [cuboid](wikipedia://en/156802), [parallelepiped](wikipedia://en/23975)
- the [tetrahedron](wikipedia://en/30606) and other [pyramids](wikipedia://en/3307045)
- [prisms](wikipedia://en/283007)
- [octahedron](wikipedia://en/80177635), [dodecahedron](wikipedia://en/8407), [icosahedron](wikipedia://en/43856084)
- [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_%28geometry%29) and [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_%28geometry%29)
- the [sphere](wikipedia://en/27859)
- other [quadrics](wikipedia://en/145570): [spheroid](wikipedia://en/82365), [ellipsoid](wikipedia://en/145381), [paraboloid](wikipedia://en/145845) and [hyperboloids](wikipedia://en/145844).

Advanced topics include:

- [projective geometry](wikipedia://en/243849) of three dimensions (leading to a proof of [Desargues' theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desargues'_theorem) by using an extra dimension)
- further [polyhedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhedra)
- [descriptive geometry](wikipedia://en/484891).

## Techniques {#Techniques}

Various techniques and tools are used in solid geometry. Among them, [analytic geometry](wikipedia://en/2202) and [vector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_%28geometric%29) techniques have a major impact by allowing the systematic use of [linear equations](wikipedia://en/113087) and [matrix](wikipedia://en/20556859) algebra, which are important for higher dimensions.

## Applications {#Applications}

A major application of solid geometry and stereometry is in [3D computer graphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics).
