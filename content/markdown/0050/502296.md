# Medial magma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Medial magma"}

In [abstract algebra](wikipedia://en/19616384), a **medial magma** or **medial groupoid** is a [magma](wikipedia://en/141916) or [groupoid](wikipedia://en/141916#History_and_terminology) (that is, a [set](wikipedia://en/26691) with a [binary operation](wikipedia://en/3948)) that satisfies the [identity](wikipedia://en/298428)

:::

$$(x \bullet y) \bullet (u \bullet v) = (x \bullet u) \bullet (y \bullet v),$$

or more simply,

$$xy \bullet uv = xu \bullet yv$$

for all $x$, $y$, $u$ and $v$, using the convention that juxtaposition denotes the same operation but has higher precedence. This identity has been variously called *medial*, *abelian*, *alternation*, *transposition*, *interchange*, *bi-commutative*, *bisymmetric*, *surcommutative*, *entropic*, etc.[^n1]

Any [commutative semigroup](wikipedia://en/27799) is a medial magma, and a medial magma has an [identity element](wikipedia://en/14962) if and only if it is a [commutative](wikipedia://en/19652#Commutative_monoid) [monoid](wikipedia://en/19652). The "only if" direction is the [Eckmann–Hilton argument](wikipedia://en/488809). Another class of semigroups forming medial magmas are [normal bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_%28mathematics%29).[^n2] Medial magmas need not be associative: for any nontrivial [abelian group](wikipedia://en/2974) with operation $+$ and [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) $m \ne n$, the new binary operation defined by $x \bullet y = mx + ny$ yields a medial magma that in general is neither associative nor commutative.

Using the [categorical](wikipedia://en/5869) definition of [product](wikipedia://en/237868), for a magma $M$, one may define the [Cartesian square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesian_square) magma $M\,\times\,M$ with the operation

$$(x,\,y) \bullet (u,\,v) = (x \bullet u,\,y \bullet v).$$

The binary operation $\bullet$ of $M$, considered as a mapping from $M \times M$ to $M$, maps $(x,\,y)$ to $x \bullet y$, $(u,\,v)$ to $u \bullet v$, and $(x \bullet u,\,y \bullet v)\,$ to $(x \bullet u) \bullet (y \bullet v)\,$. Hence, a magma $M$ is medial if and only if its binary operation is a magma [homomorphism](wikipedia://en/13564) from $M \times M$ to $M$. This can easily be expressed in terms of a [commutative diagram](wikipedia://en/138484), and thus leads to the notion of a **medial magma object** in a [category with a Cartesian product](wikipedia://en/237037). (See the discussion in auto magma object.)

If $f$ and $g$ are [endomorphisms](wikipedia://en/9569) of a medial magma, then the mapping $f \bullet g$ defined by pointwise multiplication

$$(f \bullet g)(x) = f(x) \bullet g(x)$$

is itself an endomorphism. It follows that the set $\operatorname{End}(M)$ of all endomorphisms of a medial magma $M$ is itself a medial magma.

## Bruck–Murdoch–Toyoda theorem {#Bruck–Murdoch–Toyoda_theorem}

The **Bruck–Murdoch–Toyoda theorem** provides the following characterization of medial [quasigroups](wikipedia://en/25223). Given an abelian group $A$ and two commuting [automorphisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_automorphism) $\varphi$ and $\psi$ of $A$, define an operation $\bullet$ on $A$ by

$$x \bullet y = \varphi(x) + \psi(y) + c,$$

where $c$ some fixed element of $A$. It is not hard to prove that $A$ forms a medial quasigroup under this operation. The Bruck–Murdoch-Toyoda theorem states that every medial quasigroup is of this form, i.e. is [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a quasigroup defined from an abelian group in this way.[^n3] In particular, every medial quasigroup is [isotopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotopy_of_loops) to an abelian group.

The result was obtained independently in 1941 by Murdoch and Toyoda.[^n4][^n5] It was then rediscovered by Bruck in 1944.[^n6]

:::

## Generalizations {#Generalizations}

The term *medial* or (more commonly) *entropic* is also used for a generalization to multiple operations. An [algebraic structure](wikipedia://en/106364) is an entropic algebra[^n7] if every two operations satisfy a generalization of the medial identity. Let $f$ and $g$ be operations of [arity](wikipedia://en/42301) $m$ and $n$, respectively. Then $f$ and $g$ are required to satisfy

$$f(g(x_{11}, \ldots, x_{1n}), \ldots, g(x_{m1}, \ldots, x_{mn})) = g(f(x_{11}, \ldots, x_{m1}), \ldots, f(x_{1n}, \ldots, x_{mn})).$$

:::

## Nonassociative examples {#Nonassociative_examples}

A particularly natural example of a nonassociative medial magma is given by collinear points on [elliptic curves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curves). The operation $x \bullet y = -(x + y)$ for points on the curve, corresponding to drawing a line between x and y and defining $x \bullet y$ as the third intersection point of the line with the elliptic curve, is a (commutative) medial magma which is isotopic to the operation of elliptic curve addition.

Unlike elliptic curve addition, $x \bullet y$ is independent of the choice of a neutral element on the curve, and further satisfies the identities $x \bullet (x \bullet y) = y$. This property is commonly used in purely geometric proofs that elliptic curve addition is associative.
