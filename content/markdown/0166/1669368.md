# Severi–Brauer variety

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Severi–Brauer variety"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **Severi–Brauer variety** over a [field](wikipedia://en/10603) *K* is an [algebraic variety](wikipedia://en/248808) *V* which becomes [isomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic) to a [projective space](wikipedia://en/242135) over an [algebraic closure](wikipedia://en/3129) of *K*. The varieties are associated to [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082) in such a way that the algebra splits over *K* if and only if the variety has a rational point over *K*.[^n1] (txt) studied these varieties, and they are also named after [Richard Brauer](wikipedia://en/605134) because of their close relation to the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078).

:::

In dimension one, the Severi–Brauer varieties are [conics](wikipedia://en/19008673). The corresponding central simple algebras are the [quaternion algebras](wikipedia://en/2776890). The quaternion algebra (*a*, *b*)<sub>*K*</sub>, with generators $i,j$ and relations $i^2=a, j^2=b$ and $ij = -ji$, corresponds to the conic *C*(*a*, *b*) with equation

$$z^2 = ax^2 + by^2$$

and the algebra (*a*, *b*)<sub>*K*</sub> *splits*, that is, (*a*, *b*)<sub>*K*</sub> is isomorphic to a [matrix algebra](wikipedia://en/487541) over *K*, if and only if *C*(*a*, *b*) has a point defined over *K*: this is in turn equivalent to *C*(*a*, *b*) being isomorphic to the [projective line](wikipedia://en/398578) over *K*.[^n1][^n2]

Such varieties are of interest not only in [diophantine geometry](wikipedia://en/502205), but also in [Galois cohomology](wikipedia://en/1111315). They represent (at least if *K* is a [perfect field](wikipedia://en/505088)) Galois cohomology classes in *H*<sup>1</sup>(G(K<sup>s</sup>/K),PGL<sub>*n*</sub>), where PGL<sub>*n*</sub> is the [projective linear group](wikipedia://en/382667), and *n* is one more than the [dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimension_of_a_variety) of the variety *V*. As usual in Galois cohomology, we often leave the $G(K^s/K)$ implied. There is a [short exact sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence)

1 → GL<sub>1</sub> → GL<sub>*n*</sub> → PGL<sub>*n*</sub> → 1

of [algebraic groups](wikipedia://en/247924). This implies a [connecting homomorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecting_homomorphism)

*H*<sup>1</sup>(PGL<sub>*n*</sub>) → *H*<sup>2</sup>(GL<sub>1</sub>)

at the level of cohomology. Here *H*<sup>2</sup>(GL<sub>1</sub>) is identified with the [Brauer group](wikipedia://en/404078) of *K*, while the kernel is trivial because *H*<sup>1</sup>(GL<sub>*n*</sub>) = $\{1\}$ by an extension of [Hilbert's Theorem 90](wikipedia://en/1099709).[^n3][^n4] Therefore, Severi–Brauer varieties can be faithfully represented by Brauer group elements, i.e. classes of [central simple algebras](wikipedia://en/404082).

Lichtenbaum showed that if *X* is a Severi–Brauer variety over *K* then there is an exact sequence

$$0 \rightarrow \mathrm{Pic}(X) \rightarrow \mathbb{Z} ~\stackrel{\delta}{\rightarrow}~ \mathrm{Br}(K) \rightarrow \mathrm{Br}(K)/(X) \rightarrow 0 \ .$$

Here the map *δ* sends 1 to the Brauer class corresponding to *X*.[^n2]

As a consequence, we see that if the class of *X* has order *d* in the Brauer group then there is a [divisor class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisor_class) of degree *d* on *X*. The associated [linear system](wikipedia://en/722503) defines the *d*-dimensional embedding of *X* over a splitting field *L*.[^n5]
