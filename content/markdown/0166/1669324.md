# Johann Friedrich Pfaff

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Johann Friedrich Pfaff"}

**Johann Friedrich Pfaff** (sometimes spelled **Friederich**; 22 December 1765 – 21 April 1825) was a German mathematician. He is best known for his work on [differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_equations) and as [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125)'s doctoral advisor.

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Johann Friedrich Pfaff was born 22 December 1765 to Friedrich Burkhard Pfaff (1738-1817) and Mary Magdalena Pfaff (née Brand) in Stuttgart. He was one of seven sons and five daughters.[^o19]

Pfaff attended the [Hohe Karlsschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohe_Karlsschule) from 1774 to 1785, where his classmates included [Carl Friedrich Kielmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Kielmeyer) and Ludwig Schubart, and where he met his lifelong friend, [Friedrich Schiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Schiller). His mathematical talent was noticed early on by his teachers and he came to the attention of [Duke Charles Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Eugene,_Duke_of_Württemberg), who sponsored his further studies. In 1785 he went to [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Göttingen), where he studied mathematics under [Abraham Gotthelf Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Gotthelf_Kästner) and completed his prize-winning essay on astronomy.[^n1] In 1787 he went to [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin) and studied astronomy under [J. E. Bode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Elert_Bode);[^o19][^o5] there he completed his first notable mathematical work, on [series](wikipedia://en/15287) summation.[^n2] While in Berlin, Pfaff joined [Friedrich Nicolai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Friedrich_Nicolai)'s circle of [enlighteners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Enlightenment).[^o7]

In 1788, on the recommendation of [Georg Christoph Lichtenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Christoph_Lichtenberg), Pfaff became professor of mathematics at the [University of Helmstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Helmstedt). His [inaugural dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habilitation)[^n3] investigates the calculation of [differentials](wikipedia://en/677191).[^o9] His subsequent mathematical work at Helmstedt included: nine articles in [Carl Hindenburg](wikipedia://en/545047)'s journals, notably two[^n4][^n5] linking arithmetic and analysis,[^o12][^o13] his treatise on analysis,[^n6] a paper[^n7] independently deriving [Vandermonde's identity](wikipedia://en/916157) and proving [Saalschütz's theorem](wikipedia://en/58097367),[^o16] and a paper[^n8] solving for the largest ellipse that can be inscribed in a quadrilateral, a problem posed by Gauss.

Pfaff was the official doctoral advisor to [Carl Friedrich Gauss](wikipedia://en/6125), who lived with him in Helmstedt in 1798, the one year of Gauss's residence at the university. Another notable acquaintance was [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt), whom Pfaff recommended to Göttingen.

While at Helmstedt, Pfaff was active outside of mathematics too, collaborating with [Gottfried Gabriel Bredow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Gabriel_Bredow) on historical works. When the university's finances were in trouble, he wrote an essay[^n9] defending it, preventing its closure. Pfaff served Helmstedt loyally, administering the university's pension fund for widows and refusing appointments at both Göttingen, for which he proposed Gauss, and [Dorpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tartu), for which he proposed his [brother](wikipedia://en/37099053); for his loyalty he was appointed [Hofrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofrat) by the [Duke of Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_William_Ferdinand,_Duke_of_Brunswick).[^o19]

On 8 November 1803, Pfaff married Sophie Wilhelmine Caroline Brand (b. 28 March 1784), his maternal cousin.

When Helmstedt was abolished in 1810, Pfaff moved to the [University of Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Halle), where he remained for the rest of his career. In 1812, on the death of [Georg Simon Klügel](wikipedia://en/19043679), Pfaff became director of the observatory at Halle. While at Halle, he had several students, most notably [August Möbius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Möbius).[^o19]

At Halle, Pfaff completed his most significant work[^n10], on [partial differential equations](wikipedia://en/52564) of the first order [Pfaffian systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfaffian_system), as they are now called, which became part of the theory of [differential forms](wikipedia://en/221519). Despite a favorable review from Gauss on publication, it was not until a later assessment[^o21] by [Jacobi](wikipedia://en/210340) in 1827 that his work was widely recognized.[^o5][^o22][^o7] Pfaff died 20 April 1825 in Halle of apoplexy.[^o19]

Over his career, Pfaff joined several major academic societies, including the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Göttingen Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Göttingen_Academy_of_Sciences), the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences), and the [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).[^o19]

### Family {#Family}

Pfaff and his wife had two children, Carl Pfaff (b. 1 February 1805), who became a professor of philosophy and history at Halle, and Ludwig Pfaff (1811-1829).

Two of Pfaff's brothers also became notable academics: [Johann Wilhelm Andreas Pfaff](wikipedia://en/37099053) was a professor of [pure](wikipedia://en/457210) and [applied mathematics](wikipedia://en/24295969), and [Christoph Heinrich Pfaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Heinrich_Pfaff) was a professor of medicine, physics and chemistry.[^o5]
