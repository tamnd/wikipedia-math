# Pullback bundle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Pullback bundle"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), a **pullback bundle** or **induced bundle**[^o2][^o3][^o4] is the [fiber bundle](wikipedia://en/276526) that is induced by a map of its base-space. Given a fiber bundle $\pi:E\rightarrow B$ and a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28topology%29) $f:B'\rightarrow B$ one can define a "pullback" of $E$ by $f$ as a bundle $f^*E$ over $B'$. The fiber of $f^*E$ over a point $b'$ in $B'$ is just the fiber of $E$ over $f(b')$. Thus $f^*E$ is the [disjoint union](wikipedia://en/188946) of all these fibers equipped with a suitable [topology](wikipedia://en/30450).

:::

::: {#o6 .definition cue=heading name="Formal definition"}

## Formal definition {#Formal_definition}

Let $\pi:E\rightarrow B$ be a fiber bundle with abstract fiber $F$ and let $f:B'\rightarrow B$ be a [continuous map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_%28topology%29). Define the **pullback bundle** by

$$f^{*}E = \{(b',e) \in B' \times E \mid f(b') = \pi(e)\}\subseteq B'\times E$$

and equip it with the [subspace topology](wikipedia://en/910926) and the [projection map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projection_map) $\pi':f^*E\rightarrow B'$ given by the projection onto the first factor, i.e.,

$$\pi'(b',e) = b'.\,$$

The projection onto the second factor gives a map

$$h \colon f^{*}E \to E$$

such that the following diagram [commutes](wikipedia://en/138484):

$$\begin{array} {ccc} f^{\ast}E & \stackrel {h} {\longrightarrow} & E\\ {\pi}' \downarrow & & \downarrow \pi\\ B' & \stackrel f {\longrightarrow} & B \end{array}$$

If $(U,\varphi)$ is a [local trivialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_trivialization) of $E$ then $(f^{-1}U,\psi)$ is a local trivialization of $f^*E$ where

$$\psi(b',e) = (b', \mbox{proj}_2(\varphi(e))).\,$$

It then follows that $f^*E$ is a fiber bundle over $B'$ with fiber $F$. The bundle $f^*E$ is called the **pullback of *E* by $f$** or the **bundle induced by $f$**. The map $h$ is then a [bundle morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundle_morphism) covering $f$.

:::

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Any [section](wikipedia://en/367621) $s$ of $E$ over $B$ induces a section of $f^*E$ over $B'$, called the **pullback section** $f^*s$, simply by defining

$f^*s(b'):=(b',s(f(b')))$ for all $b'\in B'$.

If the bundle $E\rightarrow B$ has [structure group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_group) $G$ with transition functions $t_{ij}$ (with respect to a family of local trivializations $\{(U_i,\varphi_i)\}$) then the pullback bundle $f^*E$ also has structure group $G$. The transition functions in $f^*E$ are given by

$$f^{*}t_{ij} = t_{ij} \circ f.$$

If $E\rightarrow B$ is a [vector bundle](wikipedia://en/276524) or [principal bundle](wikipedia://en/310950) then so is the pullback $f^*E$. In the case of a principal bundle the right [action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of $G$ on $f^*E$ is given by

$$(x,e)\cdot g = (x,e\cdot g)$$

It then follows that the map $h$ covering $f$ is [equivariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivariant) and so defines a morphism of principal bundles.

In the language of [category theory](wikipedia://en/5869), the pullback bundle construction is an example of the more general [categorical pullback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_pullback). As such it satisfies the corresponding [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245).

The construction of the pullback bundle can be carried out in subcategories of the category of [topological spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topological_spaces), such as the category of [smooth manifolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_manifold). The latter construction is useful in [differential geometry and topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_geometry_and_topology).

:::

## Bundles and sheaves {#Bundles_and_sheaves}

Bundles may also be described by their [sheaves of sections](wikipedia://en/245466). The pullback of bundles then corresponds to the [inverse image of sheaves](wikipedia://en/7086661), which is a [contravariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariance_and_contravariance_of_functors) functor. A sheaf, however, is more naturally a [covariant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covariance_and_contravariance_of_functors) object, since it has a [pushforward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_image_sheaf), called the [direct image of a sheaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_image_of_a_sheaf). The tension and interplay between bundles and sheaves, or inverse and direct image, can be advantageous in many areas of geometry. However, the direct image of a sheaf of sections of a bundle is *not* in general the sheaf of sections of some direct image bundle, so that although the notion of a 'pushforward of a bundle' is defined in some contexts (for example, the pushforward by a diffeomorphism), in general it is better understood in the category of sheaves, because the objects it creates cannot in general be bundles.
