# Kleptography

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Kleptography"}

**Kleptography** is the study of stealing information securely and subliminally. The ideas were introduced by Adam Young and [Moti Yung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Yung) in the Proceedings of Advances in Cryptology – Crypto '96[^o2] and the term was coined in their EUROCRYPT '97 paper.[^o3] Kleptography is a subfield of [cryptovirology](wikipedia://en/3852459) and is a natural extension of the theory of [subliminal channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subliminal_channels) that was pioneered by [Gus Simmons](wikipedia://en/7440474) while at [Sandia National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratory).[^o4][^o5][^o6] A kleptographic backdoor is synonymously referred to as an asymmetric backdoor. Kleptography encompasses secure and covert communications through cryptosystems and cryptographic protocols. This is reminiscent of, but not the same as [steganography](wikipedia://en/28733) that studies covert communications through graphics, video, digital audio data, and so forth.

:::

## Kleptographic attack {#Kleptographic_attack}

### Meaning {#Meaning}

A kleptographic attack is an attack which uses [asymmetric cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymmetric_cryptography) to implement a cryptographic [backdoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backdoor_%28computing%29).[^o9] For example, one such attack could be to subtly modify how the [public and private key pairs](wikipedia://en/24222) are generated by the cryptosystem so that the private key could be derived from the public key using the attacker's private key. In a well-designed attack, the outputs of the infected cryptosystem would be [computationally indistinguishable](wikipedia://en/1467815) from the outputs of the corresponding uninfected cryptosystem.[^o10][^o11] If the infected cryptosystem is a [black-box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box) implementation such as a [hardware security module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardware_security_module), a [smartcard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartcard), or a [Trusted Platform Module](wikipedia://en/2936835), a successful attack could go completely unnoticed.

A [reverse engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_engineering) might be able to uncover a backdoor inserted by an attacker, and when it is a symmetric backdoor, even use it themself.[^o9] However, by definition a kleptographic backdoor is asymmetric and the reverse-engineer cannot use it. A kleptographic attack (asymmetric backdoor) requires a private key known only to the attacker in order to use the backdoor. In this case, even if the reverse engineer was well-funded and gained complete knowledge of the backdoor, it would remain useless for them to extract the plaintext without the attacker's private key.[^o9]

::: {#o13 .definition kind=definition/construction cue=heading name="Construction"}

### Construction {#Construction}

Kleptographic attacks can be constructed as a cryptotrojan that infects a cryptosystem and opens a backdoor for the attacker, or can be implemented by the manufacturer of a cryptosystem. The attack does not necessarily have to reveal the entirety of the cryptosystem's output; a more complicated attack technique may alternate between producing uninfected output and insecure data with the backdoor present.[^o14]

:::

### Design {#Design}

Kleptographic attacks have been designed for [RSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_%28algorithm%29) key generation, the [Diffie–Hellman key exchange](wikipedia://en/7903), the [Digital Signature Algorithm](wikipedia://en/59470), and other cryptographic algorithms and protocols.[^o14] [SSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Sockets_Layer), [SSH](wikipedia://en/28814), and [IPsec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPsec) protocols are vulnerable to [kleptographic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptographic_attack).[^o16] In each case, the attacker is able to compromise the particular cryptographic algorithm or protocol by inspecting the information that the backdoor information is encoded in (e.g., the public key, the digital signature, the key exchange messages, etc.) and then exploiting the logic of the asymmetric backdoor using their secret key (usually a private key).

A. Juels and J. Guajardo[^o17] proposed a method ([KEGVER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KEGVER)) through which a third party can verify RSA key generation. This is devised as a form of distributed key generation in which the secret key is only known to the [black box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_box) itself. This assures that the key generation process was not modified and that the private key cannot be reproduced through a kleptographic attack.[^o17]

::: {#o19 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

### Examples {#Examples}

Four practical examples of kleptographic attacks (including a simplified SETUP attack against RSA) can be found in JCrypTool 1.0,[^o20] the platform-independent version of the open-source [CrypTool](wikipedia://en/3305892) project.[^o21] A demonstration of the prevention of [kleptographic attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptographic_attack) by means of the KEGVER method is also implemented in JCrypTool.

The [Dual\_EC\_DRBG](wikipedia://en/14259066) [cryptographic pseudo-random number generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptographic_pseudo-random_number_generator) from the [NIST&nbsp;SP&nbsp;800-90A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIST&nbsp;SP&nbsp;800-90A) is thought to contain a kleptographic backdoor. Dual\_EC\_DRBG utilizes [elliptic curve cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_curve_cryptography), and NSA is thought to hold a private key which, together with bias flaws in Dual\_EC\_DRBG, allows NSA to decrypt SSL traffic between computers using [Dual\_EC\_DRBG](wikipedia://en/14259066) for example.[^o22] The algebraic nature of the attack follows the structure of the repeated Dlog Kleptogram in the work of Young and [Yung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moti_Yung).

:::
