# Euclid number

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Euclid number"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Euclid numbers** are [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) of the form *E*<sub>*n*</sub> = *p*<sub>*n*</sub># + 1, where *p*<sub>*n*</sub># is the *n*th [primorial](wikipedia://en/422786) (the product of the first *n* [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666)). They are named after the [ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) mathematician [Euclid](wikipedia://en/9331), in connection with [Euclid's theorem](wikipedia://en/3143591) that there are infinitely many prime numbers.

:::

A **Euclid number of the second kind** (also called **Kummer number**) is an integer of the form *E<sub>n</sub>* = *p*<sub>*n*</sub># − 1, where *p*<sub>*n*</sub># is the *n*th primorial.

::: {#o3 .example cue=heading name="Examples"}

## Examples {#Examples}

For example, the first three primes are 2, 3, 5; their product is 30, and the corresponding Euclid number is 31.

The first few Euclid numbers are [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_%28number%29), [7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_%28number%29), [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_%28number%29), [211](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/211_%28number%29), 2311, 30031, 510511, 9699691, 223092871, 6469693231, 200560490131, ... .

The first few Kummer numbers are 1, 5, 29, 209, 2309, 30029, 510509, 9699689, 223092869, 6469693229, 200560490129, ... .

:::

## History {#History}

It is sometimes falsely stated that [Euclid's celebrated proof](wikipedia://en/3143591) of the infinitude of [prime numbers](wikipedia://en/23666) relied on these numbers.[^o5] Euclid did not begin with the assumption that the set of all primes is finite. Rather, he said: consider any finite set of primes (he did not assume that it contained only the first *n* primes) and reasoned from there to the conclusion that at least one prime exists that is not in that set.[^o6] Nevertheless, Euclid's argument, applied to the set of the first *n* primes, shows that the *n*th Euclid number has a [prime factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_factor) that is not in this set.

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/fact cue=heading name="Properties"}

## Properties {#Properties}

Not all Euclid or Kummer numbers are prime. *E*<sub>6</sub> = 13# + 1 = 30031 = 59 × 509 is the first [composite](wikipedia://en/82289) Euclid number, and *E*<sub>4</sub> = 7# − 1 = 209 = 11 × 19 is the first composite Kummer number.

For all *n* ≥ 3 the last digit of *E*<sub>*n*</sub> is 1, since *E*<sub>*n*</sub> − 1 is divisible by 2 and 5. In other words, since all primorial numbers greater than *E*<sub>2</sub> have 2 and 5 as prime factors, they are divisible by 10, thus all *E*<sub>*n* ≥ 3</sub> + 1 have a final digit of 1. Likewise, the last digit of every Kummer number is 9.

No Euclid or Kummer numbers are [perfect powers](wikipedia://en/4023591).[^o9]

It is not known whether there is an infinite number of prime Euclid numbers ([primorial primes](wikipedia://en/409348)) or prime Kummer numbers. It is also unknown whether every Euclid number is a [squarefree number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squarefree_number).[^o16]
