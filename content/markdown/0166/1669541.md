# Lennart Carleson

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lennart Carleson"}

**Lennart Axel Edvard Carleson** (born 18 March 1928) is a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902), known as a leader in the field of [harmonic analysis](wikipedia://en/14147). One of his most noted accomplishments is his proof of [Lusin's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusin's_conjecture). He was awarded the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2006 for "his profound and seminal contributions to harmonic analysis and the theory of smooth dynamical systems."[^o5][^o6]

:::

## Life {#Life}

Carleson was a student of [Arne Beurling](wikipedia://en/286530) and received his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) from [Uppsala University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala_University) in 1950. He did his post-doctoral work at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) where he met and discussed Fourier series and their convergence with [Antoni Zygmund](wikipedia://en/362477) and [Raphaël Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphaël_Salem) who were there in 1950 and 1951. He is a [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus) at Uppsala University, the [Royal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Institute_of_Technology) in [Stockholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm), and the [University of California, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles), and has served as director of the [Mittag-Leffler Institute](wikipedia://en/10096452) in [Djursholm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djursholm) outside Stockholm 1968–1984. Between 1978 and 1982 he served as president of the [International Mathematical Union](wikipedia://en/14868).

Carleson married Butte Jonsson in 1953, and they had two children: Caspar (born 1955) and Beatrice (born 1958).

Carleson has supervised 29 PhD students. They include [Svante Janson](wikipedia://en/27867508), [Kurt Johansson](wikipedia://en/62288113), [Warwick Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwick_Tucker), Bengt Rosén, Per Sjölin, Hans Wallin and Ingemar Wik.

## Work {#Work}

His work has included the solution of some [outstanding problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_problems), using techniques from [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170) and [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) (especially [stopping times](wikipedia://en/1202193)). In the theory of [Hardy spaces](wikipedia://en/314780), Carleson's contributions include the [corona theorem](wikipedia://en/4499209) (1962), and establishing the [almost everywhere](wikipedia://en/44688) [convergence of Fourier series](wikipedia://en/927236) for [square-integrable functions](wikipedia://en/28680558) (now known as [Carleson's theorem](wikipedia://en/24105681)). It was a famous old problem by [Joseph Fourier](wikipedia://en/25767401) when he invented [Fourier analysis](wikipedia://en/11659) in 1807 and formalised by [Nikolai Luzin](wikipedia://en/576228) in 1913 as the [Lusin's conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusin's_conjecture). [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov) proved a famous negative result of the conjecture for *L*<sup>1</sup> function in 1923 and stated that the conjecture must be false. It was so until 43 years later when Carleson gave his proof at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) at Moscow in 1966. But his proofs were very hard and only understood in the late 80s and early 90s when a general theory of operators arrived and brought mathematicians closer to using his striking ideas with ease.

He is also known for the theory of [Carleson measures](wikipedia://en/7755881). His tools and methods have been of fundamental importance to analysis as well as many areas of mathematics. The theorem for Fourier multipliers developed by Carleson and Per Sjölin has been standard in the study of the [Kakeya problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakeya_problem). In 1974 he solved the extension problem for [quasiconformal mappings](wikipedia://en/2749226), and gave important new results in the [Bochner–Riesz mean](wikipedia://en/20966805) in dimension two.

In the theory of [dynamical systems](wikipedia://en/9087), Carleson has worked in [complex dynamics](wikipedia://en/650518). His proof with [Michael Benedicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Benedicks) of the existence of [strange attractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_attractors) in the [Hénon map](wikipedia://en/485467) in 1991 led to a new field in dynamical systems.

In addition to publishing some landmark papers, Carleson has also published two books: First, an influential book on [potential theory](wikipedia://en/1431342), *Selected Problems on Exceptional Sets* (Van Nostrand, 1967), and second a book on the [iteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_system) of [analytic functions](wikipedia://en/61478), *Complex Dynamics* (Springer, 1993, in collaboration with [T. W. Gamelin](wikipedia://en/53092669)). He was the co-editor along with Paul Malliavin, J. Neuberger and J. Wermer who collected and published the unpublished works of his mentor Arne Beurling in 1989.

## Awards {#Awards}

He was awarded the [Wolf Prize in Mathematics](wikipedia://en/714278) in 1992, the [Lomonosov Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lomonosov_Gold_Medal) in 2002, the [Sylvester Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Medal) in 2003, and the [Abel Prize](wikipedia://en/159285) in 2006 for his profound and seminal contributions to harmonic analysis and the theory of smooth [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems).[^o5][^o10]

He is a member of the [Norwegian Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters).[^o11] In 2012 he became a fellow of the [American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/198822).[^o12] He became a Foreign Fellow of the [Royal Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society) in 1993, Honorary member of the [London Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/591097) in 1981, the [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences), [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences), [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences), [Finnish Society of Sciences and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Society_of_Sciences_and_Letters), [Finnish Academy of Science and Letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Academy_of_Science_and_Letters), Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters and the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. He has honorary doctorates from many universities such as Helsinki, Paris and KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm.
