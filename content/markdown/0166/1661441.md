# Boris Tsirelson

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Boris Tsirelson"}

**Boris Semyonovich Tsirelson** (בוריס סמיונוביץ' צירלסון, Борис Семёнович Цирельсон; May 4, 1950 – January 21, 2020) was a Russian–Israeli mathematician and Professor of Mathematics at [Tel Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_University) in Israel, as well as a [Wikipedia editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedian).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

*Image: BorisTsirelsonStudent.jpg.* Tsirelson while a first-year student, in 1967

Tsirelson was born in [Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg) to a [Russian Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia) family. From his father Simeon's side, he was the great-nephew of rabbi [Yehuda Leib Tsirelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehuda_Leib_Tsirelson), chief rabbi of [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia) from 1918 to 1941, and a prominent [posek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posek) and Jewish leader. He obtained his Master of Science from the [University of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_State_University) and remained there to pursue graduate studies. He obtained his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) in 1975, with thesis "General properties of bounded Gaussian processes and related questions" written under the direction of [Ildar Abdulovich Ibragimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ildar_Abdulovich_Ibragimov).[^o2]

Later, he participated in the [refusenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik_%28Soviet_Union%29) movement, but only received permission to immigrate to Israel in 1991. From then until 2017, he was a professor at [Tel-Aviv University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel-Aviv_University).

He also worked on fault-tolerant [cellular automaton](wikipedia://en/54342).[^o5]

In 1998 he was an Invited Speaker at the [International Congress of Mathematicians](wikipedia://en/627842) in Berlin.[^o6]

## Contributions to mathematics {#Contributions_to_mathematics}

Tsirelson made notable contributions to [probability theory](wikipedia://en/23542) and [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180). These include:

- [Tsirelson's bound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsirelson's_bound), in [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202), is an inequality, related to the issue of [quantum nonlocality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_nonlocality).[^o8]
- [Tsirelson space](wikipedia://en/628352) is an example of a [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) in which neither a [*l<sup>p</sup>* space](wikipedia://en/45194) nor a [*c*<sub>0</sub> space](wikipedia://en/45194) can be embedded.[^o9][^o10]
- The [Tsirelson's drift](wikipedia://en/74281844), a counterexample in the theory of [stochastic differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_differential_equations), is an SDE which has a weak solution but no strong solution.
- The [Gaussian isoperimetric inequality](wikipedia://en/9082215) (proved by [Vladimir Sudakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Sudakov) and Tsirelson, and independently by [Christer Borell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christer_Borell)), stating that [affine halfspaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affine_halfspace) are the isoperimetric sets for the [Gaussian measure](wikipedia://en/43325).

## Death {#Death}

Tsirelson died on January 21, 2020, at the age of 69.[^o12]
