# Hadwiger number

*Image: Hadwiger conjecture.svg.* A graph with four connected subgraphs that, when contracted, form a complete graph. It has no five-vertex complete minor by [Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130), so its Hadwiger number is exactly four.

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Hadwiger number"}

In [graph theory](wikipedia://en/12401), the **Hadwiger number** of an [undirected graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undirected_graph) $G$ is the size of the largest [complete graph](wikipedia://en/85816) that can be obtained by [contracting edges](wikipedia://en/3758115) of $G$. Equivalently, the Hadwiger number $h(G)$ is the largest number $n$ for which the complete graph $K_{n}$ is a [minor](wikipedia://en/353042) of $G$, a smaller graph obtained from $G$ by edge contractions and vertex and edge deletions. The Hadwiger number is also known as the **contraction clique number** of $G$[^n1] or the **homomorphism degree** of $G$.[^n2] It is named after [Hugo Hadwiger](wikipedia://en/630451), who introduced it in 1943 in conjunction with the [Hadwiger conjecture](wikipedia://en/1657860), which states that the Hadwiger number is always at least as large as the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of $G$.

:::

The graphs that have Hadwiger number at most four have been characterized by (Wagner 1937). The graphs with any finite bound on the Hadwiger number are sparse, and have small chromatic number. Determining the Hadwiger number of a graph is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard) but [fixed-parameter tractable](wikipedia://en/603026).

## Graphs with small Hadwiger number {#Graphs_with_small_Hadwiger_number}

A graph $G$ has Hadwiger number at most two [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) it is a [forest](wikipedia://en/48560), for a three-vertex complete minor can only be formed by contracting a [cycle](wikipedia://en/168609) in $G$.

A graph has Hadwiger number at most three if and only if its [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086) is at most two, which is true if and only if each of its [biconnected components](wikipedia://en/4842394) is a [series–parallel graph](wikipedia://en/9938559).

*Image: Clique-sum.svg.* A clique-sum of two planar graphs and the Wagner graph, forming a larger graph with Hadwiger number four.

[Wagner's theorem](wikipedia://en/3126130), which characterizes the [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314) by their [forbidden minors](wikipedia://en/13547826), implies that the planar graphs have Hadwiger number at most four. In the same paper that proved this theorem, (Wagner 1937) also characterized the graphs with Hadwiger number at most four more precisely: they are graphs that can be formed by [clique-sum](wikipedia://en/17598460) operations that combine planar graphs with the eight-vertex [Wagner graph](wikipedia://en/17598450).

The graphs with Hadwiger number at most five include the [apex graphs](wikipedia://en/28228942) and the [linklessly embeddable graphs](wikipedia://en/772031), both of which have the complete graph $K_{6}$ among their forbidden minors.[^n3]

## Sparsity {#Sparsity}

Every graph with $n$ vertices and Hadwiger number $k$ has $O(nk \sqrt{ \log k})$ edges. This bound is tight: for every $k$, there exist graphs with Hadwiger number $k$ that have $\Omega (nk \sqrt{ \log k})$ edges.[^o9] If a graph $G$ has Hadwiger number $k$, then all of its subgraphs also have Hadwiger number at most $k$, and it follows that $G$ must have [degeneracy](wikipedia://en/27545816) $O(k \sqrt{ \log k})$. Therefore, the graphs with bounded Hadwiger number are [sparse graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparse_graph).

## Coloring {#Coloring}

The [Hadwiger conjecture](wikipedia://en/1657860) states that the Hadwiger number is always at least as large as the [chromatic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_number) of $G$. That is, every graph with Hadwiger number $k$ should have a [graph coloring](wikipedia://en/426743) with at most $k$ colors. The case $k = 4$ is equivalent (by Wagner's characterization of the graphs with this Hadwiger number) to the [four color theorem](wikipedia://en/10949) on colorings of [planar graphs](wikipedia://en/24314), and the conjecture has also been proven for $k \le 5$, but remains unproven for larger values of $k$.[^n4]

Because of their low degeneracy, the graphs with Hadwiger number at most $k$ can be colored by a [greedy coloring](wikipedia://en/21051195) algorithm using $O(k \sqrt{ \log k})$ colors.

## Computational complexity {#Computational_complexity}

Testing whether the Hadwiger number of a given graph is at least a given value $k$ is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete),[^n5] from which it follows that determining the Hadwiger number is [NP-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-hard). However, the problem is [fixed-parameter tractable](wikipedia://en/603026): there is an algorithm for finding the largest clique minor in an amount of time that depends only polynomially on the size of the graph, but exponentially in $h(G)$.[^o14] Additionally, polynomial time algorithms can approximate the Hadwiger number to within an [approximation ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Approximation_ratio) of $O(\sqrt n)$, significantly more accurately than the best polynomial-time approximation (assuming [P ≠ NP](wikipedia://en/6115)) to the size of the largest [complete subgraph](wikipedia://en/524466).[^o14]

## Related concepts {#Related_concepts}

The [achromatic number](wikipedia://en/690736) of a graph $G$ is the size of the largest clique that can be formed by contracting a family of [independent sets](wikipedia://en/524501) in $G$.

[Uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable) clique minors in infinite graphs may be characterized in terms of [havens](wikipedia://en/5133456), which formalize the evasion strategies for certain [pursuit–evasion](wikipedia://en/3857398) games: if the Hadwiger number is uncountable, then it equals the largest order of a haven in the graph.[^n6]

Every graph with Hadwiger number $k$ has at most $n2^{O(k \log(\log k))}$ [cliques](wikipedia://en/524466) (complete subgraphs).[^n7]

(Halin 1976) defines a class of graph parameters that he calls $S$-functions, which include the Hadwiger number. These functions from graphs to integers are required to be zero on [graphs with no edges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empty_graph), to be [minor-monotone](wikipedia://en/353042), to increase by one when a new vertex is added that is adjacent to all previous vertices, and to take the larger value from the two subgraphs on either side of a [clique](wikipedia://en/524466) [separator](wikipedia://en/1499165). The set of all such functions forms a [complete lattice](wikipedia://en/44777) under the operations of elementwise minimization and maximization. The bottom element in this lattice is the Hadwiger number, and the top element is the [treewidth](wikipedia://en/3987086).
