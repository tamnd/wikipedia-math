# Lazarus Fuchs

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Lazarus Fuchs"}

**Lazarus Immanuel Fuchs** (5 May 1833 – 26 April 1902) was a [Jewish-German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish-German) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) who made important contributions to the field of [linear differential equations](wikipedia://en/379868).[^o4] He was born in [Moschin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosina) in the [Grand Duchy of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Posen) (modern-day Mosina, Poland) and died in [Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). He was buried in [Schöneberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schöneberg) in the [St. Matthew's Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_St.-Matthäus-Kirchhof). His grave in section H is preserved and listed as a grave of honour of the State of Berlin.

:::

## Contribution {#Contribution}

He is the eponym of [Fuchsian groups](wikipedia://en/471365) and functions, and the [Picard–Fuchs equation](wikipedia://en/2071385). A [singular point](wikipedia://en/54240) *a* of a [linear differential equation](wikipedia://en/379868)

$$y''+p(x)y'+q(x)y=0$$

is called Fuchsian if *p* and *q* are [meromorphic](wikipedia://en/80857) around the point *a*, and have poles of orders at most 1 and 2, respectively. According to a [theorem of Fuchs](wikipedia://en/18617497), this condition is necessary and sufficient for the [regularity](wikipedia://en/3166061) of the singular point, that is, to ensure the existence of two linearly independent solutions of the form

$$y_j=\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_{j,n}(x-x_0)^{n+\sigma_j},\quad a_0\ne0\,\quad j=1,2.$$

where the exponents $\sigma_j$ can be determined from the equation. In the case when $\sigma_1-\sigma_2$ is an integer this formula has to be [modified](wikipedia://en/18617497).

Another well-known result of Fuchs is the *Fuchs's conditions*, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the non-linear differential equation of the form

$$F\left(\frac{dy}{dz},y,z\right)=0$$

to be free of [movable singularities](wikipedia://en/7664719).

An interesting remark about him as a teacher during the period of his work at the [Heidelberg University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_University) pertains to his manner of lecturing: his knowledge of the mathematics he was assigned to teach was so deep that he would not prepare before giving a lecture — he would simply improvise on the spot, while exposing the students to the train of thought taken by mathematicians of the finest degree.

Lazarus Fuchs was the father of Richard Fuchs (mathematician), a German mathematician.
