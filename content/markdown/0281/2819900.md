# Classifying space for U(n)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Classifying space for U(n)"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **classifying space for the unitary group** U(*n*) is a space BU(*n*) together with a universal bundle EU(*n*) such that any hermitian bundle on a [paracompact space](wikipedia://en/48631) *X* is the pull-back of EU(*n*) by a map *X* → BU(*n*) unique up to homotopy. A particular application are [principal U(1)-bundles](wikipedia://en/80364893).

:::

This space with its universal fibration may be constructed as either

1. the [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) of *n*-planes in an infinite-dimensional complex [Hilbert space](wikipedia://en/20598932); or,
1. the direct limit, with the induced topology, of [Grassmannians](wikipedia://en/373810) of *n* planes.

Both constructions are detailed here.

## Construction as an infinite Grassmannian {#Construction_as_an_infinite_Grassmannian}

The [total space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_space) EU(*n*) of the [universal bundle](wikipedia://en/2179570) is given by

$$EU(n)=\left \{e_1,\ldots,e_n \ : \ (e_i,e_j)=\delta_{ij}, e_i\in H \right \}.$$

Here, *H* denotes an infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, the *e*<sub>*i*</sub> are vectors in *H*, and $\delta_{ij}$ is the [Kronecker delta](wikipedia://en/182890). The symbol $(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the [inner product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_product) on *H*. Thus, we have that EU(*n*) is the space of [orthonormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthonormal) *n*-frames in *H*.

The [group action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_action_%28mathematics%29) of U(*n*) on this space is the natural one. The [base space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_space) is then

$$BU(n)=EU(n)/U(n)$$

and is the set of [Grassmannian](wikipedia://en/373810) *n*-dimensional subspaces (or *n*-planes) in *H*. That is,

$$BU(n) = \{ V \subset H \ : \ \dim V = n \}$$

so that *V* is an *n*-dimensional vector space.

### Case of line bundles {#Case_of_line_bundles}

For *n* = 1, one has EU(1) = **S**<sup>∞</sup>, which is [known to be a contractible space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contractibility_of_unit_sphere_in_Hilbert_space). The base space is then BU(1) = **CP**<sup>∞</sup>, the infinite-dimensional [complex projective space](wikipedia://en/762977). Thus, the set of [isomorphism classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphism_class) of [circle bundles](wikipedia://en/2794417) over a [manifold](wikipedia://en/2073470) *M* are in one-to-one correspondence with the [homotopy classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_class) of maps from *M* to **CP**<sup>∞</sup>.

One also has the relation that

$$BU(1)= PU(H),$$

that is, BU(1) is the infinite-dimensional [projective unitary group](wikipedia://en/4402359). See that article for additional discussion and properties.

For a [torus](wikipedia://en/74800) *T*, which is abstractly isomorphic to U(1) × ... × U(1), but need not have a chosen identification, one writes B*T*.

The [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) *K*<sub>0</sub>(B*T*) is given by [numerical polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_polynomial); more details below.

## Construction as an inductive limit {#Construction_as_an_inductive_limit}

Let *F<sub>n</sub>*(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) be the space of orthonormal families of *n* vectors in **C**<sup>*k*</sup> and let *G<sub>n</sub>*(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) be the Grassmannian of *n*-dimensional subvector spaces of **C**<sup>*k*</sup>. The total space of the universal bundle can be taken to be the direct limit of the *F<sub>n</sub>*(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) as *k* → ∞, while the base space is the direct limit of the *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) as *k* → ∞.

### Validity of the construction {#Validity_of_the_construction}

In this section, we will define the topology on EU(*n*) and prove that EU(*n*) is indeed contractible.

The group U(*n*) acts freely on *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) and the quotient is the Grassmannian *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>). The map

$$\begin{aligned} F_n(\mathbf{C}^k) & \longrightarrow \mathbf{S}^{2k-1} \\ (e_1,\ldots,e_n) & \longmapsto e_n \end{aligned}$$

is a fibre bundle of fibre *F*<sub>*n*−1</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*−1</sup>). Thus because $\pi_p(\mathbf{S}^{2k-1})$ is trivial and because of the [long exact sequence of the fibration](wikipedia://en/297506), we have

$$\pi_p(F_n(\mathbf{C}^k))=\pi_p(F_{n-1}(\mathbf{C}^{k-1}))$$

whenever $p\leq 2k-2$. By taking *k* big enough, precisely for $k>\tfrac{1}{2}p+n-1$, we can repeat the process and get

$$\pi_p(F_n(\mathbf{C}^k)) = \pi_p(F_{n-1}(\mathbf{C}^{k-1})) = \cdots = \pi_p(F_1(\mathbf{C}^{k+1-n})) = \pi_p(\mathbf{S}^{k-n}).$$

This last group is trivial for *k* > *n* + *p*. Let

$$EU(n)={\lim_{\to}}\;_{k\to\infty}F_n(\mathbf{C}^k)$$

be the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of all the *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) (with the induced topology). Let

$$G_n(\mathbf{C}^\infty)={\lim_\to}\;_{k\to\infty}G_n(\mathbf{C}^k)$$

be the [direct limit](wikipedia://en/211566) of all the *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) (with the induced topology).

> **Lemma:** The group $\pi_p(EU(n))$ is trivial for all *p* ≥ 1.

::: {#o6 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof:** Let γ : **S**<sup>*p*</sup> → EU(*n*), since **S**<sup>*p*</sup> is [compact](wikipedia://en/6042), there exists *k* such that γ(**S**<sup>*p*</sup>) is included in *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>). By taking *k* big enough, we see that γ is homotopic, with respect to the base point, to the constant map.$\Box$

In addition, U(*n*) acts freely on EU(*n*). The spaces *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) and *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>) are [CW-complexes](wikipedia://en/353022). One can find a decomposition of these spaces into CW-complexes such that the decomposition of *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>), resp. *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*</sup>), is induced by restriction of the one for *F*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*+1</sup>), resp. *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>*k*+1</sup>). Thus EU(*n*) (and also *G*<sub>*n*</sub>(**C**<sup>∞</sup>)) is a CW-complex. By [Whitehead Theorem](wikipedia://en/976793) and the above Lemma, EU(*n*) is contractible.

:::

## Cohomology of BU(*n*) {#Cohomology_of_BU(n)}

::: {#o8 .statement kind=statement/proposition cue=inline-head name="Proposition"}

**Proposition**: The [cohomology ring](wikipedia://en/1069723) of $\operatorname{BU}(n)$ with coefficients in the [ring](wikipedia://en/48404) $\mathbb{Z}$ of [integers](wikipedia://en/14563) is generated by the [Chern classes](wikipedia://en/294349):[^o9][^o10]

$$H^*(\operatorname{BU}(n);\mathbb{Z}) =\mathbb{Z}[c_1,\ldots,c_n].$$

:::

::: {#o11 .proof cue=inline-head name="Proof"}

**Proof:** Let us first consider the case *n* = 1. In this case, U(1) is the circle **S**<sup>1</sup> and the universal bundle is **S**<sup>∞</sup> → **CP**<sup>∞</sup>. It is well known[^o12] that the cohomology of **CP**<sup>*k*</sup> is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}\lbrack c_1\rbrack/c_1^{k+1}$, where *c*<sub>1</sub> is the [Euler class](wikipedia://en/2932393) of the U(1)-bundle **S**<sup>2*k*+1</sup> → **CP**<sup>*k*</sup>, and that the injections **CP**<sup>*k*</sup> → **CP**<sup>*k*+1</sup>, for *k* ∈ **N**\*, are compatible with these presentations of the cohomology of the projective spaces. This proves the Proposition for *n* = 1.

There are homotopy fiber sequences

$$\mathbb{S}^{2n-1} \to B U(n-1) \to B U(n)$$

Concretely, a point of the total space $BU(n-1)$ is given by a point of the base space $BU(n)$ classifying a complex vector space $V$, together with a unit vector $u$ in $V$; together they classify $u^\perp < V$ while the splitting $V = (\mathbb{C} u) \oplus u^\perp$, trivialized by $u$, realizes the map $B U(n-1) \to B U(n)$ representing direct sum with $\mathbb{C}.$

Applying the [Gysin sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gysin_sequence), one has a long exact sequence

$$H^p ( BU(n) ) \overset{\smile d_{2n} \eta}{\longrightarrow} H^{p+2n} ( BU(n) ) \overset{j^*}{\longrightarrow} H^{p+2n} (BU(n-1)) \overset{\partial}{\longrightarrow} H^{p+1}(BU(n)) \longrightarrow \cdots$$

where $\eta$ is the [fundamental class](wikipedia://en/669440) of the fiber $\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}$. By properties of the Gysin Sequence, $j^*$ is a multiplicative homomorphism; by induction, $H^*BU(n-1)$ is generated by elements with $p < -1$, where $\partial$ must be zero, and hence where $j^*$ must be surjective. It follows that $j^*$ must **always** be surjective: by the [universal property](wikipedia://en/32245) of [polynomial rings](wikipedia://en/373065), a choice of preimage for each generator induces a multiplicative splitting. Hence, by exactness, $\smile d_{2n}\eta$ must always be **injective**. We therefore have [short exact sequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_exact_sequence) split by a ring homomorphism

$$0 \to H^p ( BU(n) ) \overset{\smile d_{2n} \eta}{\longrightarrow} H^{p+2n} ( BU(n) ) \overset{j^*}{\longrightarrow} H^{p+2n} (BU(n-1)) \to 0$$

Thus we conclude $H^*(BU(n)) = H^*(BU(n-1))[c_{2n}]$ where $c_{2n} = d_{2n} \eta$. This completes the induction.

:::

## K-theory of BU(*n*) {#K-theory_of_BU(n)}

Consider topological complex K-theory as the cohomology theory represented by the spectrum $KU$. In this case, $KU^*(BU(n))\cong \mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}][[c_1,...,c_n]]$,[^o14] and $KU_*(BU(n))$ is the free $\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]$ module on $\beta_0$ and $\beta_{i_1}\ldots\beta_{i_r}$ for $n\geq i_j > 0$ and $r\leq n$.[^o15] In this description, the product structure on $KU_*(BU(n))$ comes from the H-space structure of $BU$ given by Whitney sum of vector bundles. This product is called the [Pontryagin product](wikipedia://en/34640104).

The [topological K-theory](wikipedia://en/2912468) is known explicitly in terms of [numerical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_polynomial) [symmetric polynomials](wikipedia://en/1440207).

The K-theory reduces to computing *K*<sub>0</sub>, since K-theory is 2-periodic by the [Bott periodicity theorem](wikipedia://en/782099), and BU(*n*) is a limit of complex manifolds, so it has a [CW-structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CW-structure) with only cells in even dimensions, so odd K-theory vanishes.

Thus $K_*(X) = \pi_*(K) \otimes K_0(X)$, where $\pi_*(K)=\mathbf{Z}[t,t^{-1}]$, where *t* is the Bott generator.

*K*<sub>0</sub>(BU(1)) is the ring of [numerical polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_polynomial) in *w*, regarded as a subring of *H*<sub>∗</sub>(BU(1); **Q**) = **Q**\[*w*\], where *w* is element dual to tautological bundle.

For the *n*-torus, *K*<sub>0</sub>(B*T<sup>n</sup>*) is numerical polynomials in *n* variables. The map *K*<sub>0</sub>(B*T<sup>n</sup>*) → *K*<sub>0</sub>(BU(*n*)) is onto, via a [splitting principle](wikipedia://en/15958803), as *T<sup>n</sup>* is the [maximal torus](wikipedia://en/360136) of U(*n*). The map is the symmetrization map

$$f(w_1,\dots,w_n) \mapsto \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} f(x_{\sigma(1)}, \dots, x_{\sigma(n)})$$

and the image can be identified as the symmetric polynomials satisfying the integrality condition that

$${n \choose n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_r}f(k_1,\dots,k_n) \in \mathbf{Z}$$

where

$${n \choose k_1, k_2, \ldots, k_m} = \frac{n!}{k_1!\, k_2! \cdots k_m!}$$

is the [multinomial coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinomial_coefficient) and $k_1,\dots,k_n$ contains *r* distinct integers, repeated $n_1,\dots,n_r$ times, respectively.

## Infinite classifying space {#Infinite_classifying_space}

The canonical inclusions $\operatorname{U}(n)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{U}(n+1)$ induce canonical inclusions $\operatorname{BU}(n)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{BU}(n+1)$ on their respective classifying spaces. Their respective colimits are denoted as:

$$\operatorname{U} :=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{U}(n);$$

$$\operatorname{BU} :=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{BU}(n).$$

$\operatorname{BU}$ is indeed the classifying space of $\operatorname{U}$.

:::
