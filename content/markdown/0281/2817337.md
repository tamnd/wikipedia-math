# Marcel-Paul Schützenberger

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Marcel-Paul Schützenberger"}

**Marcel-Paul "Marco" Schützenberger** (24 October 1920 – 29 July 1996) was a French [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and Doctor of Medicine. He worked in the fields of [formal language](wikipedia://en/10939), [combinatorics](wikipedia://en/5170), and [information theory](wikipedia://en/14773).[^o3] In addition to his formal results in [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), he was "deeply involved in \[a\] struggle against the [votaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Votary) of [\[neo-\]Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Darwinism)",[^o4] a stance which has resulted in some mixed reactions from his peers and from critics of his stance on [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution). Several notable theorems and objects in mathematics as well as [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) bear his name (for example [Schutzenberger group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzenberger_group) or the [Chomsky–Schützenberger hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chomsky–Schützenberger_hierarchy)). [Paul Schützenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schützenberger) was his great-grandfather.

:::

In the late 1940s, he was briefly married to the psychologist [Anne Ancelin Schützenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Ancelin_Schützenberger).[^o5]

## Contributions to medicine and biology {#Contributions_to_medicine_and_biology}

Schützenberger's first doctorate, in medicine, was awarded in 1948 from the *Faculté de Médecine de Paris*.[^o7] His doctoral thesis, on the statistical study of biological sex at birth, was distinguished by the [Baron Larrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Jean_Larrey) Prize from the [French Academy of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Medicine).[^o8]

Biologist Jaques Besson, a co-author with Schützenberger on a biological topic,[^o9] while noting that Schützenberger is perhaps most remembered for work in pure mathematical fields, credits him[^o8] for likely being responsible for the introduction of statistical sequential analysis in French hospital practice.[^o10]

## Contributions to mathematics, computer science, and linguistics {#Contributions_to_mathematics,_computer_science,_and_linguistics}

Schützenberger's second doctorate was awarded in 1953 through the [Paris Institute of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Statistics).[^o12] This work, developed from earlier results[^o13][^o14] is counted amongst the early influential French academic work in information theory.[^o15] His later impact in both [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) and combinatorics is reflected by two theorems in formal linguistics (the [Chomsky–Schützenberger enumeration theorem](wikipedia://en/44695494)[^o16] and the [Chomsky–Schützenberger representation theorem](wikipedia://en/44695495)), and one in combinatorics (the [Schützenberger theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schützenberger_theorem)). With [Alain Lascoux](wikipedia://en/32555229), Schützenberger is credited with the foundation of the notion of the [plactic monoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plactic_monoid),[^o17][^o18] reflected in the name of the combinatorial structure called by some the Lascoux–Schützenberger tree.[^o19][^o20] Relatedly, they invented [Schubert polynomials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert_polynomials).

In [automata theory](wikipedia://en/103356), Schützenberger is credited with first defining (what later became known as) [weighted automata](wikipedia://en/43590026), the first studied model of automata which compute a quantitative output.[^o21]

The mathematician [Dominique Perrin](wikipedia://en/53543152) credited Schützenberger with "deeply \[influencing\] the theory of semigroups" and "deep results on rational functions and transducers", amongst other contributions to mathematics.[^o3]

## Offices, honors, and recognitions {#Offices,_honors,_and_recognitions}

**Professorships and other teaching[^o3]**

- Professor in the Faculty of Sciences at the [University of Poitiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Poitiers) (1957–1963)
- Lecturer in the Faculty of Medicine at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University) (1961–1962)
- Director of Research at the [CNRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNRS) (1963–1964)
- Professor at the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) (1964–1970)
- Professor in the Faculty of Sciences at the [University of Paris VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris_VII:_Denis_Diderot) (1970-until his death in 1996)

**National honors**

- In 1988, after having been a *Correspondant* since 1979, Schützenberger was made a full *Membre* of [French Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Academy_of_Sciences).

**Posthumous recognitions**

After his death, two journals in theoretical mathematics dedicated issues to Schützenberger's memory. He was commemorated in this manner by *[Theoretical Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_Computer_Science_%28journal%29)* in 1998[^o23] and again by the *[International Journal of Algebra and Computation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Journal_of_Algebra_and_Computation)* in 1999.[^o24]

The mathematician [David Berlinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Berlinski) provided this dedication in his 2000 book *The Advent of The Algorithm: The Idea that Rules the World*: À la mémoire de mon ami . . M. P. Schützenberger, 1921–1996.

## Trivia {#Trivia}

- The character "Dr. Schütz" in [Boris Vian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Vian)'s 1948 novel, *Et on tuera tous les affreux*, is said to have been inspired by Schützenberger.[^o26]
- Together with many of his students, Schützenberger is one of the contributors of the pseudonymous collective [M. Lothaire](wikipedia://en/32232474).
