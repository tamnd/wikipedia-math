# Theta (set theory)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Theta"}

In [set theory](wikipedia://en/27553), $\Theta$ (pronounced like the letter [theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theta)) is the least nonzero [ordinal](wikipedia://en/26547932) $\alpha$ such that there is no [surjection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjection) from the [reals](wikipedia://en/20646438) onto $\alpha$.

:::

$\Theta$ has been studied in connection with [strong partition cardinals](wikipedia://en/11006582) and the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300).[^o2] The axiom of determinacy is equivalent to the existence of unboundedly many strong partition cardinals below $\Theta$, in the sense that every cardinal below $\Theta$ has a strong partition cardinal above it.[^o3] This does not preclude the possibility that a single strong partition cardinal, above $\Theta$, suffices for all cardinals below $\Theta$, but the existence of such a cardinal would have additional consequences.[^o2]

If the reals can be [wellordered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellordered), then $\Theta$ is simply $(2^{\aleph_0})^+$, the [cardinal successor](wikipedia://en/375210) of the [cardinality of the continuum](wikipedia://en/1574901). Any set may be well-ordered assuming the [axiom of choice](wikipedia://en/840) (AC). However, Θ is often studied in contexts where the axiom of choice fails, such as [models](wikipedia://en/19858) of the [axiom of determinacy](wikipedia://en/1002300).

$\Theta$ is also the [supremum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremum) of the [order types](wikipedia://en/4499021) of all [prewellorderings](wikipedia://en/2137523) of the reals.

::: {#o5 .proof cue=heading name="Proof of existence"}

## Proof of existence {#Proof_of_existence}

It may not be obvious that it can be proven, without using AC, that there even exists a nonzero ordinal onto which there is no surjection from the reals (if there is such an ordinal, then there must be a least one because the ordinals are wellordered). However, suppose there were no such ordinal. Then to every ordinal α we could associate the [set](wikipedia://en/26691) of all prewellorderings of the reals having order type α. This would give an [injection](wikipedia://en/45196) from the [class](wikipedia://en/20696) of all ordinals into the set of all sets of orderings on the reals (which can to be seen to be a set via repeated application of the [powerset axiom](wikipedia://en/52552)). Now the [axiom of replacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiom_of_replacement) shows that the class of all ordinals is in fact a set. But that is impossible, by the [Burali-Forti paradox](wikipedia://en/51653).
