# Muzio Oddi

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Muzio Oddi"}

**Muzio** or **Mutio Oddi** (15 December 1569 – 15 December 1639) was an Italian mathematician and [Gnomonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnomon).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

He was born to Lisabetta Genga and Lattanzio Oddi. His initial training was in eloquence and philosophy, but he later trained under the painter [Federico Barocci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Barocci). He moved to [Pesaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesaro) to work under [Guidobaldo del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidobaldo_del_Monte), one of the main disciples of [Federico Commandino](wikipedia://en/2828269). He was hired to work in Spain and France as a [military engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_engineer), which required him also to help train in the use of artillery. He returned to the Duchy of Urbino to work as an engineer under the Duke [Francesco Maria II della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_II_della_Rovere).

In 1601, he was accused, perhaps unfairly, of being involved in a plot that also involved the Marchese [Ippolito della Rovere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ippolito_della_Rovere); the plot aimed to depose the Duke Francesco Maria. In 1609, Muzio Oddi was released from prison, and exiled to [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan).

In Milan, he won a competition to become professor of mathematics. He published his works, prepared in prison, including De gli horologi solari nelle superficie piane (1614, dedicated to Count and Cardinal [Teodoro Trivulzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodoro_Trivulzio), probably one of his patrons), and a treatise entitled *Dello squadro* (1625) regarding [surveyor's quadrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveyor's_quadrants).

In 1625, Oddi replaced his brother, Matteo, as chief fortifications engineer to the Republic of Lucca. In 1626, he helped design new city walls and was rewarded with a commemorative medal. In 1631, he became architect for the [Sanctuary of the Holy House of Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_of_the_Holy_House_of_Loreto), and in 1633 publishes his book on the [proportional or military compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_%28instrument%29), In 1636, he moves to [Lucca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucca) to work as a military engineer. Late in life, he returned to Urbino as a professor of mathematics. In 1638, he publishes a second book on [sundials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundials), De gli horologi solari, also composed while in prison. He was reappointed [Gonfaloniere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonfaloniere) in Urbino. A few letters to [Christopher Clavius](wikipedia://en/633645) and others exist.
