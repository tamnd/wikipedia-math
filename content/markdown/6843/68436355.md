# Limiting amplitude principle

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Limiting amplitude principle"}

In mathematics, the **limiting amplitude principle** is a concept from [operator theory](wikipedia://en/712675) and [scattering theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scattering_theory) used for choosing a particular solution to the [Helmholtz equation](wikipedia://en/1156215). The choice is made by considering a particular time-dependent problem of the [forced oscillations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_oscillations) due to the action of a periodic force. The principle was introduced by [Andrey Nikolayevich Tikhonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Nikolayevich_Tikhonov) and [Alexander Andreevich Samarskii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Andreevich_Samarskii).[^o2] It is closely related to the [limiting absorption principle](wikipedia://en/61148504) (1905) and the [Sommerfeld radiation condition](wikipedia://en/6226425) (1912). The terminology -- both the limiting absorption principle and the limiting amplitude principle -- was introduced by [Aleksei Sveshnikov](wikipedia://en/68087527).[^o3]

:::

::: {#o5 .definition cue=heading name="Formulation"}

## Formulation {#Formulation}

To find which solution to the Helmholz equation with nonzero right-hand side

$$\Delta v(x)+k^2 v(x)=-F(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^3,$$

with some fixed $k>0$, corresponds to the outgoing waves, one considers the [wave equation](wikipedia://en/33691) with the source term,

$$(\Delta-\partial_t^2)u(x,t)=-F(x)e^{-i k t},\quad t\ge 0, \quad x\in\mathbb{R}^3,$$

with zero initial data $u(x,0)=0,\,\partial_t u(x,0)=0$. A particular solution to the Helmholtz equation corresponding to outgoing waves is obtained as the limit

$$v(x)=\lim_{t\to +\infty}u(x,t)e^{i k t}$$

for large times.[^o2][^o6]

:::
