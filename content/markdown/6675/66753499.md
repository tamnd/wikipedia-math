# Zdzisław Józef Porosiński

::: {#o3 .subject cue=lead name="Zdzisław Józef Porosiński"}

**Zdzisław Józef Porosiński** (19 March 1955 in [Kłodzko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kłodzko), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) – 19 March 2016 in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland))[^o4] was a Polish [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) and [statistician](wikipedia://en/48661).[^o5]

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

In 1979, he graduated in mathematics from Faculty of Fundamental Problems of Technology, [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology) with a master's degree. After graduation, he started working at his alma mater at the Institute of Mathematics (later renamed Institute of Mathematics and Informatics of the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology), and from 2015 transformed into the Faculty of Pure and Applied Mathematics). On 17 March 1987, he defended his doctoral dissertation entitled S*elected problems of optimal stopping*.[^o7] The research supervisor and thesis supervisor was professor [Stanisław Trybuła](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanisław_Trybuła). On 20 November 2003, the habilitation colloquium was held. On 31 May 2004, he obtained a postdoctoral degree in mathematical sciences. In the years 2001–2008 he worked at the Institute of Basic Sciences State Higher Vocational School in [Nysa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nysa,_Poland). In the years 2006–2012 he was the deputy director for didactics at Institute of Mathematics and Computer Science of the [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology). From 2009 he was a professor [Wrocław University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrocław_University_of_Technology).[^o8]

## Contributions {#Contributions}

His research contributions include over 40 papers.[^o10][^o11] His work in probability theory included work on optimal stopping of the sequences of random variables and the statistics of stochastic processes. The most cited results concern the optimal strategies for the optimal stopping problem when the decision horizon is random.[^o12] He was the doctoral advisor of one student.
