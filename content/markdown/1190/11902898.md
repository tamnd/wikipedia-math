# Wolfgang Smith

::: {#o2 .subject cue=lead name="Wolfgang Smith"}

**Wolfgang Smith** (February 18, 1930 – July 19, 2024) was an Austrian mathematician, physicist, [philosopher of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosopher_of_science), [metaphysician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysician), and member of the [Traditionalist School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_School). He wrote extensively in the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625), as a critic of [scientism](wikipedia://en/167742) and as a proponent of a new [interpretation of quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretation_of_quantum_mechanics) that draws heavily from premodern [ontology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontology) and [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism).

:::

## Biography {#Biography}

Smith was born February 18, 1930, in [Vienna](wikipedia://en/55866). He graduated in 1948 from [Cornell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_University) with majors in philosophy, physics, and mathematics. Two years later he earned his [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M.S.) in physics at [Purdue University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdue_University) and ultimately his [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D.) in mathematics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University).

He worked as an aerodynamicist at [Bell Aircraft Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Aircraft_Corporation) in the early 1950s, and during this time researched and published on the problem of [atmospheric reentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_reentry).[^o4] He was a mathematics professor at [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Institute_of_Technology), [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles) and [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University), doing research in the field of [differential geometry](wikipedia://en/8625) and publishing in [academic journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_journal) such as the *[Transactions of the American Mathematical Society](wikipedia://en/1388937)*, the *[Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proceedings_of_the_National_Academy_of_Sciences)*, the *[American Journal of Mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Journal_of_Mathematics)*, and others. He retired from academic life in 1992.

In parallel with his academic duties, he developed philosophical inquiries in the fields of [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics) and the [philosophy of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_science), publishing in specialized journals such as *The Thomist*, *Sacred Web: A Journal of Tradition and Modernity*, and *Sophia: The Journal of Traditional Studies*.[^o4]

Smith died in [Ventura, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura,_California) at the age of 94.[^o5]

## Philosophical work {#Philosophical_work}

Smith was a member of the [Traditionalist School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_School) of [metaphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphysics), having contributed extensively to its criticism of modernity while exploring the philosophical underpinnings of the scientific method and emphasizing the idea of bringing science back into a [Platonist](wikipedia://en/22954) and [Aristotelian](wikipedia://en/308) framework of traditional ontological [realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_realism).

Identifying with [Alfred North Whitehead](wikipedia://en/43395)'s critique of the "bifurcationism" and "physical reductionism" of [scientism](wikipedia://en/167742) – i.e., the belief that, first, the qualitative properties of the objects of perception ("corporeal" objects) are ultimately distinct from their respective quantitative properties (the "physical" objects studied by the various sciences); and second, that physical objects are in fact all there is, meaning corporeal objects are reduced to their physical counterparts – Smith examines critically in his work *Cosmos and Transcendence* (1984) the [Cartesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartesianism) roots of modern science.

Proceeding with his critique of scientism in his monograph, *The Quantum Enigma* (1995), Smith raised the questions of whether the [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) is in fact dependent on the scientistic philosophy and, if it is not, whether linking it to other philosophical frameworks would provide better solutions to the way physical phenomena are interpreted. Demonstrating that neither the scientific method nor its results require adhering to a scientistic metaphysics, he answered in the negative to the first question, resulting in the conclusion that it is possible to link the scientific method to any underlying ontology, or to none at all. Working then into the second question, he proposed linking the scientific method – and thus the modern sciences – to a non-bifurcationist, non-reductionist metaphysics in the form of a modified [Thomistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomism) ontology, showing how such a move resolves the apparent incoherences of [quantum mechanics](wikipedia://en/25202).[^o7]

According to Smith, this [interpretation of quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpretation_of_quantum_mechanics) allows for the usage of the [hylomorphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylomorphism#Aristotle's_concept_of_matter) concepts of [potency and act](wikipedia://en/308#Potentiality_and_actuality) to properly understand [quantum superposition](wikipedia://en/82728). For example, instead of considering that a photon is "simultaneously a wave and a particle" or "a particle in two distinct positions," one may consider that the photon (or any other physical object) at first does not exist *in act*, but only *in potency*; i.e., as "[matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matter_%28philosophy%29)" in the hylomorphic meaning of the term, having the *potential* of becoming "a wave or a particle," or "of being here or there." Whether one of these outcomes will happen to this undifferentiated matter is dependent on the determination imposed upon it by the [macroscopic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroscopic) corporeal object that provides its *actualization*. A photon, thus, would be no more strange for having many potentials than, say, an individual who has the "superposed" potentials of learning French and/or Spanish and/or Greek, all the while reading and/or walking and/or stretching his arms. A further consequence of this interpretation is that a corporeal object and its "associated physical object" are not dichotomized or reduced one to the other anymore but, on the contrary, altogether constitute a whole of which different aspects are dealt with depending upon perspective.[^o8]

Smith's understanding of the relationship between corporeal and physical objects extends to his interpretation of [biology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology), where he became an opponent of [Darwinian evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution), as the fundamental element in a species would be its form, not its causal history, which evolutionists favor. This led him to be a supporter of the [intelligent design movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligent_design_movement), though his own hylomorphic approach is not widely adopted by mainstream intelligent design theorists (who, like evolutionists, also favor causal history, albeit differently).

Smith also took a stance towards a [relativistic](wikipedia://en/30001) rehabilitation of [geocentrism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocentrism). He did not support a Ptolemaic or medieval geocentrism unequivocally, nor assert that heliocentrism is absolutely false. Rather, he argued that, according to the theory of [relativity](wikipedia://en/30001), both heliocentrism and geocentrism have scientific merit, insofar as scientific observation depends upon the [reference frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_reference) of the observer. Consequently, any observations made from Earth (or any near-Earth satellites) are in effect geocentric.[^o9][^o10]

## Filmography {#Filmography}

Smith participated in *[Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_%282019_film%29)* (2019), a documentary by [Mauro Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Ventura), with the participation of Raphael De Paola and [Olavo de Carvalho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavo_de_Carvalho).[^o12][^o13]
