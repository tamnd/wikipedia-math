# Mazur's lemma

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Mazur's lemma"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Mazur's lemma** is a result in the theory of normed vector spaces introduced by Polish mathematician [Stanisław Mazur](wikipedia://en/1023162).[^o2] It shows that any [weakly convergent](wikipedia://en/33662) sequence in a [normed space](wikipedia://en/21538) has a sequence of [convex combinations](wikipedia://en/794534) of its members that converges strongly to the same limit. Mazur's lemma is used in the proof of [Tonelli's theorem](wikipedia://en/11905171).

:::

::: {#o4 .statement kind=statement/lemma cue=named-heading name="Statement of the lemma"}

## Statement of the lemma {#Statement_of_the_lemma}

::: {#o5 .statement kind=statement/theorem cue=math-theorem name="Mazur's theorem"}

**Mazur's theorem.**

Let $(X, \lVert\cdot\rVert)$ be a normed vector space and let $\left\{x_j\right\}_{j \in \mathbb{N}}\subset X$ be a sequence which [converges weakly](wikipedia://en/33662) to some $x\in X$.

Then there exists a sequence $\left\{y_k\right\}_{k \in \mathbb{N}}\subset X$ made up of finite convex combination of the $x_j$'s of the form

$$y_k=\sum_{j\ge k}\lambda_j^{(k)}x_j$$

such that $y_k\to x$ strongly that is $\lVert y_k-x\rVert\to 0$.

:::

For a proof see Ekeland & Temam (1974), p. 6.

:::
