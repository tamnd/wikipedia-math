# Browder–Minty theorem

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Browder–Minty theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), the **Browder–Minty theorem** (sometimes called the **Minty–Browder theorem**) states that a [bounded](wikipedia://en/455961), [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122), [coercive](wikipedia://en/8971904) and [monotone function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotone_function) *T* from a [real](wikipedia://en/20646438), [separable](wikipedia://en/27855) [reflexive](wikipedia://en/210091) [Banach space](wikipedia://en/3989) *X* into its [continuous dual space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_dual_space) *X*<sup>∗</sup> is automatically [surjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surjective). That is, for each [continuous linear functional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_linear_functional) *g* ∈ *X*<sup>∗</sup>, there exists a solution *u* ∈ *X* of the equation *T*(*u*) = *g*. (Note that *T* itself is not required to be a [linear map](wikipedia://en/18102).)

:::

The theorem is named in honor of [Felix Browder](wikipedia://en/10201445) and [George J. Minty](wikipedia://en/67693763), who independently proved it.[^o2]
