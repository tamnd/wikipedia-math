# Tonelli's theorem (functional analysis)

::: {#o1 .subject cue=lead name="Tonelli's theorem"}

In [mathematics](wikipedia://en/18831), **Tonelli's theorem in functional analysis** is a fundamental result on the [weak](wikipedia://en/33662) [lower semicontinuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_semicontinuous) of [nonlinear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonlinear) [functionals](wikipedia://en/1374948) on [*L*<sup>*p*</sup> spaces](wikipedia://en/45194). As such, it has major implications for [functional analysis](wikipedia://en/11180) and the [calculus of variations](wikipedia://en/171882). Roughly, it shows that weak lower semicontinuity for integral functionals is equivalent to [convexity](wikipedia://en/245568) of the integral kernel. The result is attributed to the [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy) [mathematician](wikipedia://en/18902) [Leonida Tonelli](wikipedia://en/10018991).

:::

::: {#o3 .statement cue=heading name="Statement of the theorem"}

## Statement of the theorem {#Statement_of_the_theorem}

Let $\Omega$ be a bounded [domain](wikipedia://en/16796643) in $n$-[dimensional](wikipedia://en/8398) [Euclidean space](wikipedia://en/9697) $\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $f : \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R} \cup \{\pm \infty\}$ be a [continuous](wikipedia://en/6122) [extended real-valued](wikipedia://en/51698) function. Define a nonlinear functional $F$ on functions $u : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}^m$by

$$F[u] = \int_{\Omega} f(u(x)) \, \mathrm{d} x.$$

Then $F$ is sequentially weakly lower [semicontinuous](wikipedia://en/57824) on the $L^p$ space $L^p(\Omega)$ for $1 < p < +\infty$ and weakly-∗ lower semicontinuous on $L^\infty(\Omega)$ [if and only if](wikipedia://en/14922) $f$ is [convex](wikipedia://en/245568).
